Duality of Codes over Non-unital Rings of Order Six
Altaf Alshuhail

Abstract
We present some basic theory on the duality of codes over two non-unital rings of order 6 6 , namely H 23 H_{23} and H 32 H_{32} . For a code 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} over these rings, we associate a binary code 𝒞 a {\mathcal{C}}_{a} and a ternary code 𝒞 b {\mathcal{C}}_{b} . We characterize self-orthogonal, self-dual and quasi self-dual (QSD) codes over these rings using the codes 𝒞 a {\mathcal{C}}_{a} and 𝒞 b {\mathcal{C}}_{b} . In addition, we present a building-up construction for self-orthogonal codes, introduce cyclic codes and linear complementary dual (LCD) codes. We also gave a classification of self-orthogonal codes for short lengths.



Keywords:
Non-unital rings, Self-orthogonal codes, Self-dual codes, Quasi self-dual codes, Linear complementary dual, Cyclic codes

Introduction

Coding theory was studied classically using finite fields as alphabets.
In the last thirty years, rings have been used along with finite fields, i.e., linear codes are defined as modules over a ring.
Most research on codes over rings take commutative rings as alphabet
[
9
]
, and in almost all cases, rings with unity (see
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
4
]
, for example). Recently, there has been interest in non-unitary rings. One ring in particular is the non-local ring
H
of order
4
(see
[
7
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
)
The goal of this paper is to lay down the foundations of the study of duality of linear codes over two non-unital rings of order 6, denoted by
H_{23}
and
H_{32}
(see
[
1
]
), whose properties are similar to the ring
H
. These rings are interesting to study because a lot of new codes can be constructed over these rings. They also are in close connection to widely studied binary and ternary codes. For every code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
H_{23}
or
H_{32}
, there is an associated binary code
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and a ternary code
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
. This allows us to determine the characteristics of the code
{\mathcal{C}}
by looking at the associated codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
. In particular, conditions for self-orthogonal, self-dual, quasi self-dual and LCD codes are studied. Also, a method to construct self-orthgonal codes of longer length from codes of shorter length is presented as well as introduce cyclic codes over these rings. We will also present a
classification of self-orthogonal codes for short lengths.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we define the rings
H_{23}
and
H_{32}
and give their properties.
In Section
3

H_{23}
and
H_{32}

This is followed by a build-up construction, i.e. a method to construct codes from codes of smaller length in Section
5
. In Section
6
, we give a classification of self-orthogonal codes of lengths up to 7.

From these tables, we see that both rings are commutative and without unity. They have two maximal ideals
J_{a}=\{0,a\}
and

.

We define a natural action of
\mathbb{F}_{2}

r0=0r=0
and
r1=1r=r
, for all
r\in J_{a}
. This action is distributive in the sense that
r(s\oplus t)=rs+rt
, where
\oplus
denotes the addition in
\mathbb{F}_{2}

3
Codes over finite fields

An
[n,k]
linear code or an
[n,k]
-code
{\mathcal{C}}
of length
n
and dimension
k
is a subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
where
p
is a prime. The elements of such a code are called codewords. If
p=2
, then
{\mathcal{C}}
is called binary code. If
p=3
, then
{\mathcal{C}}
is called ternary code. The Hamming weight
wt({\mathbf{x}})
of
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{C}}
is the number of nonzero coordinates in
{\mathbf{x}}
. We equip
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
with the standard inner product
({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})=\sum^{n}_{i=1}x_{i}y_{i}
for
{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,

{\mathbf{y}}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}.

The dual
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
of
{\mathcal{C}}
is an
[n,n-k]
-code defined as

(3.1)

{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=\{{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\text{ }|\text{ }({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})=0\text{ }\text{for all}\text{ }{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{C}}\}.

A linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
is self-orthogonal if
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
and self-dual if
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
. A linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
is linear with complementary dual (LCD) if
{\mathcal{C}}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
. A linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
is cyclic provided that for each codeword
{\mathbf{c}}=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n})
in
{\mathcal{C}}
, the vector
(c_{n},c_{1},\cdots,c_{n-1})
is also in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

4
Duality of Codes over
H_{23}
and
H_{32}

Let
z\in\{23,32\}
. A linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
H_{z}
or an
H_{z}
-code of length
n
is an
H_{z}
-submodule of
H_{z}^{n}
. Since
H_{z}=J_{a}\oplus J_{b},
a code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
H_{23}
or
H_{32}

can be written as the direct sum
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
,
where
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is a binary code and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is a ternary code.

The Hamming weight of a codeword
{\mathbf{c}}
in an
H_{z}
- code
{\mathcal{C}}
, denoted
wt({\mathbf{c}})
, is the number of nonzero coordinates of
{\mathbf{c}}
and the Hamming distance between codewords
{\mathbf{x}}
and
{\mathbf{y}}
in an
H_{z}
-code, denoted
d({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})
, is the number of coordinates where
{\mathbf{x}}
and
{\mathbf{y}}
are differ.

Define the standard inner product on
H_{z}
as

({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})=\sum^{n}_{i=1}x_{i}y_{i}

for all

{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}),{\mathbf{y}}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\in H_{z}^{n}
. The dual
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
of
{\mathcal{C}}
is the submodule of
H_{z}^{n}

defined by

(4.1)

{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=\{{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\text{ }|\text{ }({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})=0\text{ }\text{for all}\text{ }{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{C}}\}.

An
H_{z}
-code
{\mathcal{C}}
is self-orthogonal if
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
and self-dual if
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
. An
H_{z}
- code
{\mathcal{C}}
is said to be nice if
|{\mathcal{C}}||{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}|=6^{n}
. If an
H_{z}
-code
{\mathcal{C}}
is a self-orthogonal code and
|{\mathcal{C}}|=6^{\frac{n}{2}}
, we say
{\mathcal{C}}
is a quasi self-dual (QSD) code.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
z\in\{23,32\}
. If
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is an
H_{z}
-code where
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is a nonzero binary code of minimum distance
d_{1}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is a nonzero ternary code of minimum distance
d_{2}
, then the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{C}}
is
d=\min\{d_{1},d_{2}\}
.

Proof.
Let
{\mathbf{u}}_{1}
be a nonzero codeword of
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
of weight
d_{1}
and
{\mathbf{u}}_{2}
be a nonzero codeword of
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
of weight
d_{2}
. Since
a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
and
b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
, this implies that,
a{\mathbf{u}}_{1},b{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
wt(a{\mathbf{u}}_{1})=wt({\mathbf{u}}_{1})
and
wt(b{\mathbf{u}}_{2})=wt({\mathbf{u}}_{2})
. Therefore,
d\leq\min\{d_{1},d_{2}\}
. Conversely, let
{\mathbf{u}}
be a nonzero codeword of
{\mathcal{C}}
of weight
d
. Then,
{\mathbf{u}}=a{\mathbf{x}}+b{\mathbf{y}}
where
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and
{\mathbf{y}}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
. Since
{\mathbf{u}}\neq 0
, then one case of the following is true:

•

If
{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{0}}
and
{\mathbf{y}}\neq 0
, then

d=wt({\mathbf{u}})=wt(b{\mathbf{y}})=wt({\mathbf{y}})\geq d_{2}.

•

If
{\mathbf{x}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}
and
{\mathbf{y}}=0
, then

d=wt({\mathbf{u}})=wt(a{\mathbf{x}})=wt({\mathbf{x}})\geq d_{1}.

•

If
{\mathbf{x}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}
and
{\mathbf{y}}\neq 0
, then

d=wt({\mathbf{u}})\geq wt(a{\mathbf{y}})=wt({\mathbf{y}})\geq d_{2}
and

d=wt({\mathbf{u}})\geq wt(a{\mathbf{x}})=wt({\mathbf{x}})\geq d_{1}.

For all three cases, we have

d=wt({\mathbf{u}})\geq\min\{d_{1},d_{2}\},

and the result follows.

\Box

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
be an
H_{z}
-code of length
n
. Then

(i)

for
z=23
,
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp}\oplus b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
. Furthermore,
({\mathcal{C}}^{\perp})^{\perp}={\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
.

(ii)

for
z=32
,
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=a\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}^{\perp}
. Furthermore,
({\mathcal{C}}^{\perp})^{\perp}={\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.

Proof.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an
H_{23}
-code and suppose
{\mathbf{u}}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
. Write
{\mathbf{u}}=a{\mathbf{x}}+b{\mathbf{y}}
where
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}_{2}
and
{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}_{3}
. If
{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
, then
a{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathcal{C}}
. Thus, we have

0=({\mathbf{u}},a{\mathbf{v}})=(a{\mathbf{x}}+b{\mathbf{y}},a\,{\mathbf{v}})=a({\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{x}}).

Since
a\neq 0
,
({\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{x}})=0
, so it follows that
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp}
. Thus, we have
{\mathbf{u}}\in a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp}+b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
. Consequently, we have
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}\subseteq a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp}+b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
.

Now, suppose
{\mathbf{c}}\in a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp}+b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
, i.e.,
{\mathbf{c}}=a{\mathbf{x}}+b{\mathbf{y}}
, where
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp}
and
{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
.
For an arbitrary
{\mathbf{u}}=a{\mathbf{r}}+b{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathcal{C}}
, where
{\mathbf{r}}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a},{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
, we have

({\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{u}})=(a{\mathbf{x}}+b{\mathbf{y}},a{\mathbf{r}}+b{\mathbf{t}})=a({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{r}})=a\cdot 0=0.

Since
{\mathbf{u}}
is arbitrary, we see that
{\mathbf{c}}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
and hence,
a\,\mathcal{C}_{a}^{\perp}+b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
.
Then, we have
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp}+b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
. Since the intersection of the two vector spaces
a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and
b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
is trivial, the sum is direct. Finally, since
({\mathcal{C}}^{\perp})^{\perp}=(a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp}\oplus b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n})^{\perp}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
, we have
({\mathcal{C}}^{\perp})^{\perp}={\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
, so (i) follows. The case where
{\mathcal{C}}
is an
H_{32}
code is proved similarly.

\Box

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
be an
H_{z}
-code of length
n
.
Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is nice if and only if

(i)

{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
for
z=23
.

(ii)

{\mathcal{C}}_{a}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
for
z=32
.

Proof.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an
H_{23}
code and
k_{a}=\dim({\mathcal{C}}_{a})
and
k_{b}=\dim({\mathcal{C}}_{b})
. Then
|{\mathcal{C}}|=2^{k_{a}}3^{k_{b}}
.
By Theorem
4.2
(i),
|{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}|=2^{n-k_{a}}3^{n}
.
Thus,

|{\mathcal{C}}||{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}|=2^{n}\,3^{n+k_{b}}.

Therefore,
{\mathcal{C}}
is nice if and only if
k_{b}=0
.

Similarly, (ii) follows from Theorem
4.2
(ii).
\Box

4.1
Self-orthogonal, Self-dual and QSD codes

We now determine the conditions so that a code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
H_{23}
or
H_{32}
is self-orthognal and quasi self-dual.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
be an
H_{z}
-code.

(i)

If
z=23
, then
{\mathcal{C}}
is self-orthogonal if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is a binary self-orthogonal code.

(ii)

If
z=32
, then
{\mathcal{C}}
is self-orthogonal if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is a ternary self-orthogonal code.

Proof.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an
H_{23}
code and suppose
{\mathbf{c}}_{1}=a{\mathbf{x}}_{1}+by_{1}
and
{\mathbf{c}}_{2}=a{\mathbf{x}}_{2}+b{\mathbf{y}}_{2}
are arbitrary codewords in
{\mathcal{C}}
where
{\mathbf{x}}_{1}
,
{\mathbf{x}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and
{\mathbf{y}}_{1}
,
{\mathbf{y}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
.
We have

(4.2)

\begin{split}({\mathbf{c}}_{1},{\mathbf{c}}_{2})=&a^{2}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2})+b^{2}({\mathbf{y}}_{1},{\mathbf{y}}_{2})\\
=&a({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2}).\end{split}

Since
a\neq 0
, by (
4.2
),
{\mathcal{C}}
is a self-orthogonal if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is a binary self-orthogonal code.

On the other hand,
a^{2}=0
and
b^{2}=b
in
H_{32}
, so (ii) follows.

\Box

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
be a binary linear code and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
be a ternary linear code.
The code
{\mathcal{C}}
defined by

{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{2}

is a self-orthogonal
H_{z}
-code of length
n
where
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}={\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}={\mathcal{C}}_{2}
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

For
z=23
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
is a binary self-orthogonal code of length
n
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
is a ternary code of length
n
.

(ii)

For
z=32
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
is a binary code of length
n
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
is a ternary self-orthogonal code of length
n
.

Moreover, if
|{\mathcal{C}}_{1}||{\mathcal{C}}_{2}|=6^{n/2}
, then
{\mathcal{C}}
is QSD.

Proof.
Let
z=23
. By the linearity of
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
, the code
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
is closed under addition.
For the scalar multiplication, let
{\mathbf{c}}=a{\mathbf{x}}+b{\mathbf{y}}\in{\mathcal{C}}
where
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{y}}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
. Then we have the following:

•

a(a{\mathbf{x}}+b{\mathbf{y}})=a^{2}{\mathbf{x}}=a{\mathbf{x}}=a{\mathbf{x}}+b\mathbf{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}
;

•

b(a{\mathbf{x}}+b{\mathbf{y}})=\mathbf{0}=a\mathbf{0}+b\mathbf{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}
.

Since
{\mathcal{C}}
is closed under addition, for any
r=ax+by\in H_{23}
where
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
y\in\mathbb{F}_{3}
,
we have
r\,{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
. Hence,
{\mathcal{C}}
is a linear code over
H_{23}
.
To prove the self-orthogonality of
{\mathcal{C}}
, for all
a{\mathbf{x}}_{1}+b{\mathbf{y}}_{1},a{\mathbf{x}}_{2}+b{\mathbf{y}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}
where
{\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{y}}_{1},{\mathbf{y}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
, we have

\begin{split}(a{\mathbf{x}}_{1}+b{\mathbf{y}}_{1},a{\mathbf{x}}_{2}+b{\mathbf{y}}_{2})&=a^{2}({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2})+b^{2}({\mathbf{y}}_{1},{\mathbf{y}}_{2})\\
&=a({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2})\\
&=0,\end{split}

since
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
is a binary self-orthogonal code.

Statement (ii) is proved similarly.

Furthermore, since
|{\mathcal{C}}|=|{\mathcal{C}}_{1}||{\mathcal{C}}_{2}|
, the last statement holds.
\Box

{\mathcal{C}}

if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

For
z=23
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is a binary self-dual code and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is a ternary
[n,n/2]

(ii)

For

,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is a binary
[n,n/2]

{\mathcal{C}}_{b}

Proof.
The result follows from
[

, Lemma 1]
, Theorem
4.4
, Theorem
4.5

\Box

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
be an
H_{z}
-code of length
n
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is self-dual if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

For
z=23
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is a binary self-dual code and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
.

(ii)

For
z=32
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is a ternary self-dual code.

Proof.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an
H_{23}
-code. From the definition of self-dual codes and Theorem
4.2
(i), we have

a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}={\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp}\oplus b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}.

Therefore,
{\mathcal{C}}
is self-dual if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}={\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
. Similarly, (ii) follows from the definition of self-dual codes and Theorem
4.2
(ii).

\Box

Observe that by Corollary
4.6
and Theorem
4.7
, QSD and self-dual
H_{23}
-codes have even lengths while QSD and self-dual
H_{32}
-codes have length divisible by 4 since
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
must be ternary self-dual codes.

Now, we will show that any
H_{23}
-code cannot be both QSD and self-dual at the same time.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the set of all QSD codes of length
n
over
H_{23}
and
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all self-dual
codes of length
n
over
H_{23}
. Then
\mathcal{Q}\cap\mathcal{S}=\emptyset
.

Proof.
Suppose
{\mathcal{C}}
is an
H_{23}
-code of length
n
that is both QSD and self-dual.
Then
|{\mathcal{C}}|=|{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}|=6^{n/2}
. Therefore,
|{\mathcal{C}}||{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}|=6^{n}
,
so
{\mathcal{C}}
is a nice code. However, by Corollary
4.3
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\{\mathbf{0}\}

which contradicts Corollary
4.6
.
Thus,
\mathcal{Q}\cap\mathcal{S}=\emptyset
.

\Box

The same can be said for
H_{32}
codes.

4.2
Cyclic codes

A cyclic code
{\mathcal{C}}
of length
n
over
H_{z}
is a linear code such that if
(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n})\in{\mathcal{C}}
, then its cyclic shift
(c_{n},c_{1},\cdots,c_{n-1})
is also a codeword in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
be an
H_{z}
-code of length
n
, where
z\in\{23,32\}
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is a cyclic code if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
are both cyclic.

Proof.
Suppose that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a cyclic code over
H_{z}
. Let
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
. Then
(ax_{1}+by_{1},\ldots,ax_{n}+by_{n})
is a codeword in
{\mathcal{C}}
. Since
{\mathcal{C}}
is closed under cyclic shifts,

\begin{split}&a(x_{n},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})+b(y_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1})\\
&=(ax_{n}+by_{n},ax_{1}+by_{1},\ldots,ax_{n-1}+by_{n-1})\in{\mathcal{C}}.\end{split}

Therefore
(x_{n},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
and
(y_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1})
are codewords from
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
, respectively.
Thus,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
are both closed under cyclic shifts, hence they are cyclic codes. Now, we prove the reverse direction. Since
{\mathcal{C}}
is defined as
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,C_{a}\oplus b\,C_{b}
, any linear combination of codewords from
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
will be a codeword in
{\mathcal{C}}
. Since both
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
are cyclic codes, it follows that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a cyclic code.

\Box

Corollary 4.10
.

If
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is a cyclic code over
H_{z}
, where
z\in\{23,32\}
, then its dual is also cyclic.

Proof.
The result follows from Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
4.9
.

\Box

Corollary 4.11
.

A QSD code
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
of even length
n
over
H_{z}
is cyclic if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

For
z=23
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is a binary cyclic self-dual code and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is a ternary cyclic
[n,n/2]-
code.

(ii)

For
z=32
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is a binary cyclic
[n,n/2]-
code and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is a ternary cyclic self-dual code.

Proof.
The result follows from Corollary
4.6
and Theorem
4.9
.

\Box

4.3
LCD codes

An
H_{z}
-code
{\mathcal{C}}
is linear with complementary dual (LCD) if
{\mathcal{C}}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
. The following theorem gives a characterization of LCD codes over the ring
H_{z}
.

Theorem 4.12
.

A linear code
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}+b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
over
H_{z}
is LCD if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

For
z=23
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is LCD and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
iff
{\mathcal{C}}
is nice.

(ii)

For
z=32
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is LCD iff
{\mathcal{C}}
is nice.

Proof.
Let
z=23
and suppose
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}+b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is LCD, i.e.,
{\mathcal{C}}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
. By Theorem
4.2
,

{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}\cap\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
. This implies
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is LCD and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
. By Corollary
4.3
,
{\mathcal{C}}
is nice.

Suppose
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is LCD and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
. By definition,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
.
So we have
{\mathcal{C}}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=a({\mathcal{C}}_{a}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp})+b({\mathcal{C}}_{b}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{b}^{\perp})=\{\mathbf{0}\}
, which shows
{\mathcal{C}}
is LCD.

The case where
{\mathcal{C}}
is an
H_{32}
-code is proved similarly.

\Box

Corollary 4.13
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}+b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is a cyclic code over
H_{z}
.
Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is an LCD if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

For
z=23
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is an LCD cyclic code and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

(ii)

For
z=32
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is an LCD cyclic code.

Proof.
The result follows from Theorem
4.9
and Theorem
4.12
.

\Box

5
Build-up construction for self-orthogonal codes

In this chapter, we give several methods to construct self-orthogonal codes from codes of smaller length.
These are called the
building up constructions
.

Recall the action of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
on
J_{a}
and
\mathbb{F}_{3}
on
J_{b}
. If
{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, define
\alpha{\mathbf{x}}=(\alpha x_{1},\ldots,\alpha x_{n})\in H_{23}^{n}
for
\alpha\in J_{a}
. Similarly, if
{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
, define
\beta{\mathbf{x}}=(\beta x_{1},\ldots,\beta x_{n})\in H_{32}^{n}
for
\beta\in J_{b}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
be a self-orthogonal code over
H_{23}
of length
n

with generating set
\{{\mathbf{r}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{r}}_{k}\}
. Let
{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a fixed binary vector satisfying
({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}})=1
, that is,
{\mathbf{x}}
is a vector of odd Hamming weight. Denote
y_{i}=({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{r}}_{i}),

1\leq i\leq k
. Then the code
{\mathcal{C}}
with
k+1
generators given by

(5.1)

(\alpha,0,\alpha{\mathbf{x}}),\,(y_{1},y_{1},{\mathbf{r}}_{1}),\,\ldots,\,(y_{k},y_{k},{\mathbf{r}}_{k}),

where
\alpha\in J_{a}
is a self-orthogonal code over
H_{23}
of length
n+2
.

Proof.

•

The first vector is orthogonal to itself since
\alpha^{2}+({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}})\cdot\alpha^{2}=\alpha+\alpha=0
.

•

The first vector is orthogonal to the other
k
vectors since
\alpha y_{i}+(\alpha{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{r}}_{i})=2\alpha y_{i}=0.

•

The last
k
vectors are orthogonal to each other and to themselves by self-orthogonality of
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}

since
y_{i}y_{j}+y_{i}y_{j}+({\mathbf{r}}_{i},{\mathbf{r}}_{j})=2y_{i}y_{j}+0=0
,
where
y_{i}y_{j}\in J_{a}
in
H_{23}
.

Hence
{\mathcal{C}}
is a self-orthogonal code over
H_{23}
of length
n+2
.

\Box

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
be a self-orthogonal code over
H_{23}
of length
n
with generating set
\{{\mathbf{r}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{r}}_{k}\}
.
Let
{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a fixed ternary vector. Denote
y_{i}=({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{r}}_{i}),

1\leq i\leq k
.
Then the code
{\mathcal{C}}
with
k+3

generators given by

(5.2)

\begin{split}(\beta,0,0,\beta{\mathbf{x}}),(0,\beta,0,\beta{\mathbf{x}}),(0,0,\beta,\beta{\mathbf{x}}),\\
(2y_{1},2y_{1},2y_{1},{\mathbf{r}}_{1}),\ldots,(2y_{k},2y_{k},2y_{k},{\mathbf{r}}_{k}),\end{split}

where
\beta\in J_{b}=\{0,b,d\}
is a self-orthogonal code over
H_{23}
of length
n+3
.

Proof.

•

The first three vectors are orthogonal to itself since
\beta^{2}+\beta^{2}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}})=0+0({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}})=0
.

•

The first three vectors are orthogonal to each other as
\beta^{2}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}})=0
.

•

The first three vectors are orthogonal to any of the last
k
vectors since

2\beta y_{i}+(\beta{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{r}}_{i})=3\beta y_{i}=0.

•

The last
k
rows are orthogonal to each other and to themselves by self-orthogonality of
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}

since

4y_{i}y_{j}+4y_{i}y_{j}+4y_{i}y_{j}+({\mathbf{r}}_{i},{\mathbf{r}}_{j})=12y_{i}y_{j}+0=0
.

Hence
{\mathcal{C}}
is a self-orthogonal code over
H_{23}
of length
n+3
.

\Box

.

, we will construct a self-orthogonal code over

.

and

and

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
be a self-orthogonal code of length
n
over
H_{32}
with generating set
{\mathbf{r}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{r}}_{k}
.
Let
{\mathbf{x}}
be a fixed ternary vector of length
n
and
\alpha,\beta,
and
\gamma\in H_{32}
such that
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=0.
Denote
y_{i}=({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{r}}_{i}),

1\leq i\leq k
.
Then the code
{\mathcal{C}}
with
k+1

generators given by

(5.3)

(\alpha,\beta,0,\gamma{\mathbf{x}}),(y_{1},y_{1},y_{1},{\mathbf{r}}_{1}),\ldots,(y_{k},y_{k},y_{k},{\mathbf{r}}_{k}),

is a self-orthogonal code over
H_{32}
of length
n+3
, if one of the following hold:

(i)

({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}})=1
and
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}=0
,

(ii)

({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}})=-1
and
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}-\gamma^{2}=0.

Proof.

(i)

If
({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}})=1
and
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}=0
, then

•

The first vector is orthogonal to itself since
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}})=\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}=0
.

•

The first vector is orthogonal to any of the other
k
vectors since

\alpha y_{i}+\beta y_{i}+\gamma y_{i}=(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)y_{i}=0.

•

The last
k
vectors are orthogonal to each other and to themselves by self-orthogonality of
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
since

3y_{i}y_{j}+({\mathbf{r}}_{i},{\mathbf{r}}_{j})=0
,
where
y_{i}y_{j}\in J_{b}
in
H_{32}
.

(ii)

If
({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}})=-1
and
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}-\gamma^{2}=0,
then

•

The first vector is orthogonal to itself since
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}})=\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}-\gamma^{2}=0
.

•

The first vector is orthogonal to any of the other
k
vectors since
\alpha y_{i}+\beta y_{i}+\gamma y_{i}=(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)y_{i}=0.

•

The last
k
vectors are orthogonal to each other and to themselves by self-orthogonality of
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
since

3y_{i}y_{j}+({\mathbf{r}}_{i},{\mathbf{r}}_{j})=0
,
where
y_{i}y_{j}\in J_{b}
in
H_{32}
.

Hence
{\mathcal{C}}
is a self-orthogonal code over
H_{32}
of length
n+3
.
\Box

Numerical results

In this section, we give a classification of self-orthogonal codes over
H_{23}
and
H_{32}
of lengths up to 7.
We a method similar to
[
1
]
. For each pair
({\mathcal{C}}_{a},{\mathcal{C}}_{b})
, we find self-orthogonal codes that are permutation
equivalent to
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}+b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
. This can be accomplished by double coset decompositions of the symmetry group
S_{n}
by automorphism groups of codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
satisfying conditions given in Theorem
4.5
. Then we take the System of Distinct Representatives (SDR), i.e., a set of representative of the double cosets in the group
S_{n}
. Here, we have an analog of Theorem 2 in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
({\mathcal{C}}_{a},{\mathcal{C}}_{b})
be a pair of codes as defined in Theorem
4.5
.
Let
Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{a})
and
Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{b})
denote permutation groups of
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}

and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
, respectively. Then the set

S_{{\mathcal{C}}_{a},{\mathcal{C}}_{b}}:=\{a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}+b\,\sigma({\mathcal{C}}_{b})\,|\,\sigma\text{ runs over an SDR of }Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{a})\backslash S_{n}/Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{b})\}

forms a set of permutation inequivalent self-orthogonal
H_{z}
-codes. Moreover,
|S_{{\mathcal{C}}_{a},{\mathcal{C}}_{b}}|=|Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{a})\backslash S_{n}/Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{b})|
.

To carry out the classification, we note of the following consequence of Theorem
6.1
, similar to Corollary 1 and
Corollary 2 in
[
1
]
.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
L_{a}
be the set of all inequivalent binary codes of length
n
and
L_{b}
be
the set of all (permutation) inequivalent ternary codes that satisfies Theorem
4.5
.
Then the set of all self-orthogonal
H_{z}
-codes of length

n
is the disjoint union

\bigcup_{{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\in La,\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}\in L_{b}}S_{{\mathcal{C}}_{a},{\mathcal{C}}_{b}}.

Based on the above results, we have the classification algorithm for self-orthogonal
H_{z}
-codes of length
n
as follows:

1.

If
z=23
,

(a)

write a list
L_{a}
of inequivalent binary self-orthogonal
[n,k_{a}]
-codes;

(b)

write a list
L_{b}
of permutation inequivalent ternary
[n,k_{b}]
-codes.

2.

If
z=32
,

(a)

write a list
L_{a}
of inequivalent binary
[n,k_{a}]
-codes;

(b)

write a list
L_{b}
of permutation inequivalent ternary self-orthogonal
[n,k_{b}]
-codes.

3.

For every pair
({\mathcal{C}}_{a},{\mathcal{C}}_{b})\in L_{a}\times L_{b}
,

(a)

compute the automorphism groups
Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{a})
and
Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{b})
,

(b)

determine a list
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r}
of representatives of
Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{a})\backslash S_{n}/Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{b})
,

(c)

for
i=1,\ldots,r
, compute
a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}+b\,\sigma_{i}({\mathcal{C}}_{b})
.

Clearly, the zero code of length
n
is self-orthogonal.

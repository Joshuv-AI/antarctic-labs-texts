Autotrinet YOLO triple attention framework for robust traffic sign detection
Authors: Wei-Yi Dong, Ya-Xuan Chen, Guang-Yu Zou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Traffic sign detection (TSD) remains a critical challenge in intelligent transportation systems due to factors such as small target sizes, environmental variability, and real-time constraints. While deep learning-based methods like YOLO have advanced TSD performance, existing approaches often struggle to balance accuracy, computational efficiency, and robustness across diverse scenarios. This paper proposes AutoTriNet-YOLO, a novel framework that integrates triple-attention enhancement (local, global, and sequential pathways) with dynamic feature fusion and adaptive computation to address these limitations. The core innovation lies in the TriplePathBlock module, which parallelizes Convolutional Block Attention (CBAM), Non-local Blocks, and a Lite Transformer to capture multi-scale contextual dependencies efficiently. A Dynamic Fusion Gate adaptively weights attention paths, while a Selective Insert mechanism prunes redundant operations based on input complexity. Evaluated on a comprehensive traffic sign dataset, AutoTriNet-YOLO achieves state-of-the-art performance with 86.6% mAP@50 and 65.3% mAP@50–95, outperforming existing methods like TSD-YOLO and EDN-YOLO. Ablation studies validate the contributions of each component, particularly the CBAM pathway for local feature refinement. The framework maintains real-time efficiency, making it suitable for edge deployment in autonomous driving systems. This work advances robust TSD by unifying diverse attention mechanisms into a scalable, computationally optimized architecture.

Introduction

Traffic sign detection (TSD) is a critical component in intelligent transportation systems (ITS) and autonomous driving applications. As vehicles become increasingly intelligent, the ability to accurately detect and recognize traffic signs in real time is vital for ensuring safety, complying with road regulations, and enabling autonomous decision-making. However, reliable detection of traffic signs remains a challenging task due to several factors such as small target sizes, complex backgrounds, environmental variability, and limited computational resources, particularly in real-world driving scenarios.

Recent advancements in deep learning, especially convolutional neural networks (CNNs), have greatly improved object detection performance. Among these, YOLO (You Only Look Once) and Faster R-CNN architectures are widely employed due to their speed and accuracy. Many researchers have sought to enhance these frameworks specifically for traffic sign detection. For example, an improved YOLOv8 model named TSD-YOLO introduces a Space-to-Depth (SPD) module to handle multi-scale variations and a Select Kernel attention mechanism to focus on critical features. This design improves mAP scores on benchmark datasets while maintaining high speed, making it suitable for real-time applications1. Another approach integrates enhancements into Faster R-CNN by reconstructing the backbone and region proposal networks, incorporating multi-scale fusion modules such as scSE and GSConv, and updating anchor box sizes to better detect small targets. The result is a significant reduction in model weight and improved detection performance in complex scenes2.

However, existing attention-enhanced detection methods face fundamental architectural limitations. Sequential attention processing, commonly adopted in current approaches, suffers from three critical issues: (1) Information degradation: subsequent modules process pre-modified features rather than original representations, leading to cumulative information loss; (2) Fixed processing order: predetermined attention sequences cannot adapt to varying scene complexities; and (3) Suboptimal feature utilization: different attention mechanisms operate in distinct feature subspaces, but sequential processing prevents their simultaneous optimization. While individual attention components like CBAM, Non-local blocks, and Transformer modules have been extensively studied, their strategic integration for traffic sign detection presents unique theoretical and practical challenges. Existing methods typically employ these mechanisms sequentially or in isolation, which fundamentally limits their synergistic potential. Sequential processing creates a bottleneck where each attention module modifies the input features before passing to the next, resulting in compounding errors and suboptimal feature representations. In this arrangement, the Transformer module never sees the original features X, only the twice-modified representation. This sequential dependency prevents the optimization of complementary attention mechanisms and limits the model’s ability to capture multi-scale contextual relationships simultaneously.

Improving YOLO’s architecture has become a mainstream strategy in traffic sign detection research. An enhanced YOLO network optimized for 72 Chinese road signs modifies the backbone by removing the Conv3 module, reduces downsampling rates, and introduces a Hybrid Spatial Pyramid Pooling Fast (H-SPPF) module. Combined with a channel attention mechanism, this configuration achieves impressive precision and recall on large-scale datasets3. In the context of embedded edge computing, researchers have embedded YOLOv8 on Raspberry Pi by fusing Ghost and Efficient Multi-Scale Attention modules. This lightweight implementation demonstrates strong performance under poor weather conditions, with recognition accuracy exceeding 93%, and reduced inference latency, demonstrating its potential for deployment in real-world IoT and vehicular systems4.

To enhance detection under challenging visual conditions such as occlusions or low-resolution inputs, some researchers have proposed two-stage detection frameworks. One such study replaces traditional cascading classification with a fusion-based strategy that directly outputs categories in the first stage and then refines results through a second-stage prediction, further enhanced by a Surrounding-Aware Non-Maximum Suppression (SA-NMS) module. This method improves both robustness and accuracy across varied scenarios5. Similarly, EDN-YOLO employs a decoupled head for classification and regression, EfficientVit backbone for better feature extraction, and a hybrid loss combining CIoU and NWD to enhance small-object sensitivity, leading to noticeable improvements in multi-scale detection accuracy6.

Optimizing the receptive field has also proven effective. YOLO-TS leverages anchor-free fusion methods and redesigned receptive fields to align better with the typical size distribution of traffic signs. This helps boost both accuracy and speed on datasets such as TT100K and CCTSDB7. YOLOv5 has also been augmented with an Efficient Vision Transformer (EfficientViT) backbone and CBAM attention mechanism to enhance feature extraction, while the introduction of Wise-IoU loss improves bounding box prediction. This modified model achieves a high mAP and notable inference speed8.

There is also growing interest in methods that improve the detection of small or distant traffic signs. YOLOv7-TS addresses this by integrating sub-pixel convolution and a multi-feature interactive fusion network, effectively enhancing small object perception without inflating model complexity9. In snow-covered environments, MSGC-YOLOv8 replaces conventional modules with multi-scale group convolution and deformable attention to ensure accurate detection despite occlusions. It boosts mAP by over 17% while reducing parameter size significantly, showing superior adaptability to harsh weather10.

Low-light conditions present another real-world challenge. LLTH-YOLOv5 proposes a two-stage framework that first enhances images through a lightweight adjustment network and then applies a transformer-based head and GhostDarkNet backbone. This approach notably improves detection accuracy in poorly lit environments while maintaining real-time performance11. Similarly, YOLO-PPA introduces a Parallelized Patch Aware Attention mechanism that enhances multi-scale feature extraction for long-distance traffic signs, and a novel classification loss (APLoss) to address category imbalance. The model significantly improves both inference efficiency and accuracy, particularly on small-scale targets12.

To further reduce computational overhead, Sign-YOLO incorporates an attention-enhanced YOLOv7 framework with squeeze-and-excitation blocks, improving detection accuracy while reducing model size by 98%. It achieves a 99.10% mAP on the GTSDB dataset, positioning it as a robust and lightweight solution for real-time deployment13. Comparative studies confirm the superiority of YOLOv8 over conventional models such as ResNet50, with performance gains reflected in metrics such as precision, recall, and F1-score, highlighting YOLOv8’s strong adaptability to diverse environments14.

Weather conditions like haze or fog severely impact visibility, further complicating detection tasks. To address this, DPF-YOLOv8 introduces a dual-path fusion structure and simulates foggy conditions for data augmentation. This leads to measurable improvements in mAP under adverse environmental conditions15. ML_SAP mechanisms have also been integrated into YOLOv5s to enhance feature fusion and improve detection accuracy of small targets under varying environmental conditions, achieving consistent improvements on both CCTSDB and TT100K datasets16.

Transformer architectures have also been incorporated into traditional CNN-based detectors. TSDet, built upon YOLOv5 and Swin Transformer, combines shallow information enhancement, cross-stage attention, and adaptive fusion modules to improve real-time detection performance, achieving over 94% AP50 with high FPS on benchmark datasets17. Similarly, ETSR-YOLO improves YOLOv5’s PANet for better multi-scale fusion and introduces enhanced C3 modules to suppress background noise, along with a Wise-IoU loss for robust bounding box learning. This model improves mAP across several datasets and is optimized for embedded platforms18.

Zhang et al. introduced YOLO-BS, which incorporates a small object detection layer and bidirectional feature pyramid network (BiFPN) into YOLOv8 to enhance multi-scale object handling and improve small object detection performance19. This work demonstrates the continuing evolution of YOLO architectures specifically for traffic sign applications. Chen et al. proposed DEASF-YOLO, addressing adverse weather conditions through Detail-Enhanced Convolution, Attentional Scale Sequence Fusion, and Long-Short Term Spatial Attention Mechanism, achieving 11.7%-12.4% improvement in mAP@0.5:0.95 compared to YOLOv8n20. This work highlights the importance of environmental robustness in real-world deployments. Xing et al. developed FEBG-YOLOv8s, a lightweight model integrating FasterNet, Efficient Multi-scale Attention, and BiFPN for edge deployment in autonomous driving systems21. Im Choi and Tian addresses this through saliency and location-aware pruning of deep visual detectors for autonomous driving22. Their approach specifically tackles small-scale objects using attention mechanisms, demonstrating the importance of computational optimization in real-world autonomous driving systems.

Efforts to balance feature pyramid structures and improve context awareness include using global context blocks and enhanced feature extraction modules within YOLOv5, resulting in consistent improvements in both mAP@0.5 and mAP@0.5:0.9523. Beyond CNN-based architectures, hybrid models combining handcrafted features and extreme learning machines (ELMs) have demonstrated high accuracy under various conditions, even when using synthesized datasets for better generalization24. Few-shot detection approaches such as Meta-YOLO further aim to enable detection of unseen categories using minimal training data, with feature decorrelation modules and multi-scale prediction heads offering a 39.8% improvement in mAP over baseline algorithms25.

In addressing scale variation, CR-YOLOv8 enhances multi-scale detection using receptive field blocks (RFBs) and attention mechanisms, which improve the ability to recognize small traffic signs with minimal computational overhead26. Finally, novel neural network models like iELMNet and SegU-Net demonstrate that alternative architectures based on segmentation or anchor prediction can also achieve high detection precision, even under extreme or unusual conditions27,28. These findings underline the vast potential of diverse methodological frameworks in improving traffic sign detection for real-world deployment.

Collectively, these studies highlight the multifaceted nature of traffic sign detection challenges and the broad spectrum of solutions under exploration. From architecture enhancements and lightweight designs to attention mechanisms, transformer integration, and few-shot learning, the field continues to evolve rapidly. However, balancing accuracy, real-time performance, and generalization in varied environmental conditions remains an open research problem. As autonomous driving systems edge closer to large-scale adoption, advancing TSD algorithms with robustness, interpretability, and deployment efficiency will be crucial in ensuring safety and reliability on the road.

To address challenges in traffic sign detection—such as small-object sensitivity, environmental variation, and real-time requirements—this paper proposes AutoTriNet-YOLO, a lightweight yet powerful framework that integrates triple-attention enhancement, dynamic feature fusion, and adaptive computation. Unlike prior methods that rely on single-path attention (e.g., TSD-YOLO1 or computationally intensive transformers (e.g., TSDet17, AutoTriNet-YOLO introduces a TriplePathBlock module, which processes local (CBAM), global (Non-local), and sequential (Lite Transformer) attention in parallel. This design captures multi-scale contextual dependencies while preserving efficiency. To further optimize performance, a Dynamic Fusion Gate adaptively weights the contributions of each attention path, refining features across a wide range of traffic sign types—from large structured signs to small or occluded ones. Additionally, a Selective Insert mechanism reduces unnecessary computation by dynamically activating modules based on input complexity. AutoTriNet-YOLO achieves state-of-the-art results (mAP50: 0.866, mAP50–95: 0.653) with a strong balance between accuracy and speed, making it suitable for real-time, edge-deployed autonomous driving systems. To address these fundamental limitations, this paper proposes AutoTriNet-YOLO, which introduces three key innovations that provide synergies unachievable through conventional sequential combinations:

Parallel processing paradigm

Unlike sequential attention chains, our TriplePathBlock processes three complementary attention mechanisms (CBAM, Non-local, Lite Transformer) in parallel, ensuring each module operates on pristine input features. This eliminates information degradation and enables simultaneous optimization of different attention types.

Orthogonal Feature Space Exploitation: The three attention pathways operate in mathematically orthogonal feature spaces: CBAM focuses on spatial-channel refinement (local feature enhancement), Non-local captures pixel-wise global dependencies (contextual relationships), and Lite Transformer models sequential token interactions (structural patterns). Parallel processing prevents these mechanisms from interfering with each other’s optimization objectives.

Adaptive synergy via dynamic fusion

Rather than using fixed combination weights, our Dynamic Fusion Gate learns scene-adaptive attention weighting, automatically emphasizing the most relevant attention type based on input complexity. This creates emergent behaviors that cannot be replicated through static sequential combinations.

Methodology

Model framework

In this study, we propose AutoTriNet-YOLO, a modular enhancement of the YOLOv8 detection framework, tailored specifically for traffic sign detection tasks under constrained computational budgets. As shown in Fig. 1, the proposed architecture enhances both local and global feature representation while maintaining computational efficiency through the integration of triple attention paths, a Dynamic Fusion Gate, and a Selective Insert strategy. The pipeline begins with 640 × 640 input images processed by the YOLOv8 backbone, followed by a convolutional layer and the Selective Insert Module, which conditionally activates attention modules based on input complexity. The core component, TriplePathBlock, combines CBAM, Non-local, and Lite Transformer branches in parallel. Their outputs are adaptively fused via the Dynamic Fusion Gate to optimize feature quality. Finally, the enhanced features are passed to the YOLO Detection Head, which generates bounding box and class predictions.

The CBAM (Convolutional Block Attention Module) pathway serves as the local feature refinement component in our TriplePathBlock. Together, they form a channel-spatial attention pipeline that enhances discriminative feature learning across both dimensions. (c) Lite Transformer Path: Sequential Dynamics.

To further exploit spatial-token interactions while maintaining computational efficiency, we incorporate a Lite Transformer pathway within the TriplePathBlock. This component is inspired by the Transformer architecture but strategically streamlined to suit real-time traffic sign detection scenarios and resource-constrained edge devices.

Initially, the input feature map is bilinearly interpolated to a fixed resolution and reshaped into a sequence \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\text{S}\in\:{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times\:d}$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:N={H}^{{\prime\:}}\times\:{W}^{{\prime\:}}$$\end{document} represents the number of patches and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:d$$\end{document} denotes the embedding dimension per patch. The sequence is then passed through a single-layer Transformer Encoder to capture global sequential dependencies. To retain spatial structure, we employ fixed sinusoidal positional encodings, following the design used in the original Transformer architecture. Specifically, each patch embedding is added to a precomputed position vector based on its row-column location. This enables the Transformer to learn position-aware attention while keeping the encoding parameter-free and lightweight. The positional encoding \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:PE$$\end{document} for position \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:pos$$\end{document} and dimension \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:i$$\end{document} is defined as follows:3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:P{E}_{\left(pos,2i\right)}={sin}\left(\frac{pos}{{10000}^{\frac{2i}{d}}}\right)$$\end{document}4\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:P{E}_{\left(pos,2i+1\right)}=cos\left(\frac{pos}{{10000}^{\frac{2i}{d}}}\right)$$\end{document}

Where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:pos$$\end{document} is the position index within the token sequence, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:i$$\end{document} is the dimension index, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:d$$\end{document} is the total embedding dimension.

These encodings are precomputed and added to the patch embeddings before input to the Transformer Encoder. The final sequence input to the encoder is thus:5\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Y=\text{TransformerEncoder}\left({X}_{s}+PE\right)$$\end{document}

This encoding scheme allows the model to learn position-aware attention distributions while keeping the architecture lightweight and efficient—particularly suitable for edge deployment in real-time traffic sign detection.

The processed sequence is subsequently reshaped back into the original spatial dimensions and upsampled to match the resolution of the outputs from the CBAM and Non-local pathways. This harmonization ensures seamless integration within the Dynamic Fusion Gate.

Dynamic fusion gate

Upon obtaining outputs \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\text{X}}_{1},{\text{X}}_{2},{\text{X}}_{3}$$\end{document} from the three attention paths, we propose a Dynamic Fusion Gate (DFG) to adaptively combine them. First, a pooled summary is computed:6\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\text{P}=\text{Pool}\left(\right[{\text{X}}_{1},{\text{X}}_{2},{\text{X}}_{3}\left]\right)\in\:{\mathbb{R}}^{B\times\:3C\times\:1\times\:1\:}$$\end{document}

Next, soft weights \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\alpha\:\in\:{\mathbb{R}}^{B\times\:3}$$\end{document} are generated via a 1 × 1 convolution followed by a softmax:7\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\alpha\:=\text{Softmax}\left({\text{Conv}}_{1\times\:1}\left(\text{P}\right)\right)$$\end{document}

The final fused output is:8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\text{X}}_{\text{fused}}=\sum\:_{i=1}^{3}\:{\alpha\:}_{i}\cdot\:{\text{X}}_{i}$$\end{document}

This mechanism empowers the model to learn task-adaptive attention weighting, boosting flexibility across traffic sign variations.

Selective insert mechanism

To address the trade-off between model complexity and detection accuracy, we devise a Selective Insert (SI) mechanism. This mechanism controls the integration of enhanced blocks (like TriplePathBlock) via a learnable gate:9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\text{X}}_{\text{out}}=\text{X}+\sigma\:\left(\alpha\:\right)\cdot\:\left(\mathcal{M}\left(\text{X}\right)-\text{X}\right)$$\end{document}

Here, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mathcal{M}(\cdot\:)$$\end{document} denotes the enhanced module (e.g., TriplePathBlock), \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\alpha\:$$\end{document} is a learnable scalar, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\sigma\:\left(\alpha\:\right)\in\:\left[\text{0,1}\right]$$\end{document} modulates the contribution of the enhancement. During training, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\alpha\:$$\end{document} is optimized to either insert (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\sigma\:\left(\alpha\:\right)\approx\:1$$\end{document}) or bypass (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\sigma\:\left(\alpha\:\right)\approx\:0$$\end{document}) the module. This allows for network self-pruning and adaptability to various deployment constraints.

Network Self-Pruning Details: The mechanism uses learnable gate parameters αi ∼ N(0.5, 0.1) with three-phase training: (1) free learning (0-150 epochs), (2) sparsity regularization with L_sparse = λ∑|σ(αi)| (150–350 epochs), and (3) decision stabilization (350–500 epochs). Post-training, modules with σ(αi) ≤ 0.3 are pruned, typically removing 30–35% of modules with < 2% performance loss.

Overall architecture integration

AutoTriNet-YOLO is implemented by modifying the YOLOv8n backbone. During initialization, each convolutional block is traversed. If the block conforms to a standard convolution (Ultralytics Conv layer), it is wrapped as:10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\text{Conv}\to\:\text{Conv}+\text{SelectiveInsert}\left(\text{TriplePathBlock}\right)$$\end{document}

This design choice ensures that feature enhancement is seamlessly injected into the original network, preserving its efficiency and pretraining benefits while upgrading its representation power.

Experimental setup and evaluation

Dataset

Our study utilizes a publicly available road detection dataset, strategically selected to address the specific requirements of integrated traffic scene understanding rather than isolated traffic sign detection. The dataset choice reflects several critical considerations for autonomous driving applications.

Dataset selection rationale and statistical validation

The selected Road Detection Dataset contains 1,588 high-resolution images with comprehensive annotations for nine traffic-related categories. While some reviewers might question the dataset scale relative to larger repositories like GTSRB (43,000 + images) or Mapillary Traffic Sign Dataset, our selection serves distinct methodological purposes that align with the research objectives of architectural innovation validation.

Comprehensive scene context consideration

Unlike single-category datasets that focus exclusively on traffic signs, our chosen dataset provides complete traffic scenarios including traffic control devices, pedestrian elements, and vehicles. This multi-category approach is essential for validating the TriplePathBlock’s ability to handle diverse object scales and contextual relationships simultaneously. Traffic sign detection in real autonomous driving systems requires understanding signs within their environmental context, not in isolation.

Statistical significance validation

Although the total dataset contains 1,588 images, the statistical power for architectural comparison remains robust. With 1,270 training images and 159 test images, each of the nine categories maintains adequate representation (average 176 samples per class) for meaningful performance evaluation. For architectural innovation studies focusing on attention mechanism validation rather than large-scale deployment, this sample size provides sufficient statistical significance to demonstrate relative performance improvements.

We employ standard object detection evaluation metrics including Precision, Recall, F1-score, and Mean Average Precision (mAP). Based on the confusion matrix (True Positives, False Positives, False Negatives), we calculate:11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\text{Precision}=\frac{TP}{TP+FP}$$\end{document}12\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\text{Recall}=\frac{TP}{TP+FN}$$\end{document}13\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\text{F1}=\frac{2\times\:\text{Precision}\times\:\text{Recall}}{\text{Precision}+\text{Recall}}$$\end{document}

Performance on the test set

As presented in Table 3, the AutoTriNet-YOLO model exhibits strong and consistent detection performance across a wide spectrum of traffic-related object categories. On the overall test set, the model achieves a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 0.829 at IoU threshold 0.5 (mAP@50) and 0.609 across a range of IoU thresholds (mAP@50–95). These results indicate a solid balance between accurate classification and precise localization, affirming the effectiveness of the model’s triple attention architecture and dynamic fusion strategy. Notably, the model demonstrates outstanding precision-recall synergy for highly structured and visually salient traffic signs, such as Stop Signal*and Speedlimit Signal. Both classes yield perfect recall (1.00) and near-perfect mAP@50 scores (0.995), with corresponding F1 scores reaching 0.952. This confirms the model’s robustness in detecting standard traffic indicators, likely attributable to their consistent geometric features and high inter-class separability.

High performance is also maintained for Crosswalk Signal (F1 = 0.873, mAP@50 = 0.919, mAP@50–95 = 0.803) and Truck (F1 = 0.792, mAP@50 = 0.862), suggesting the model’s ability to generalize well to larger, structured objects that frequently appear in consistent contextual positions within road scenes. Moreover, Car detection achieves a high precision of 0.906, though recall is relatively lower at 0.694. This pattern implies that while the model is highly selective and accurate when positive identifications are made, certain instances may be missed, potentially due to occlusion or class imbalance.

In more challenging detection categories, such as Pedestrian and Traffic Light Signal, performance shows room for improvement, with F1 scores of 0.532 and 0.708, and mAP@50–95 scores of 0.242 and 0.489, respectively. These lower values can be reasonably attributed to the intrinsic variability and dynamic nature of these classes—Pedestrians often appear in varied poses, occluded scenarios, and lighting conditions, while Traffic Light Signals may suffer from small scale, color similarity with the background, and multi-instance ambiguity. Nonetheless, it is important to emphasize that the model still achieves baseline functionality in these classes, offering a solid platform for future refinement.

The relatively wider performance gap between mAP@50 and mAP@50–95 for certain categories (e.g., Car and Pedestrian) reflects the model’s sensitivity to stricter localization requirements. This suggests potential for further optimization in bounding box regression, especially under high IoU constraints. Addressing this could involve incorporating finer-resolution feature maps or enhanced anchor box priors during training.

In summary, the AutoTriNet-YOLO model achieves high-precision, high-recall detection across most traffic-relevant object classes while maintaining real-time efficiency. Its superior performance on structured and semantically stable classes validates the design of the triple-path attention mechanism and dynamic fusion gate. Meanwhile, the moderate results on certain complex categories are not limitations per se, but rather highlight the challenges inherent to real-world perception tasks. These areas offer promising directions for incremental improvement, such as advanced context modeling, motion-aware feature integration, or hybrid supervised-unsupervised learning for rare event detection. Collectively, these findings establish AutoTriNet-YOLO as a strong candidate for intelligent transportation systems requiring both accuracy and adaptability.

Comparative experiment

To ensure fair comparison, all baseline methods were re-implemented and trained under identical conditions: TSD-YOLO and DPF-YOLOv8 used official implementations, EDN-YOLO and TSDet were fully re-implemented based on paper specifications, and Sign-YOLO was re-trained using author-provided architecture. All models were trained for 500 epochs with identical hyperparameters (Table 2) and data augmentation protocols.

As illustrated in Table 4, the proposed AutoTriNet-YOLO model demonstrates superior performance in traffic sign detection, achieving an mAP50 of 0.86 and mAP50-95 of 0.653, which surpasses most existing methods. The model’s triple-attention mechanism, integrating local (CBAM), global (Non-local Blocks), and sequential (Lite Transformer) attention pathways, significantly enhances feature representation across diverse traffic scenarios. This architectural innovation enables AutoTriNet-YOLO to outperform EDN-YOLO (mAP50: 0.787, mAP50-95: 0.537) and TSD-YOLO (mAP50: 0.824, mAP50-95: 0.622), particularly in handling small and occluded objects, as evidenced by its higher mAP50-95 score.

While Sign-YOLO achieves a marginally higher mAP50 (0.887), AutoTriNet-YOLO excels in localization precision (mAP50-95: 0.653 vs. 0.642), underscoring its robustness under stricter IoU thresholds. This advantage stems from the dynamic fusion gate (DFG), which adaptively weights attention pathways to optimize feature aggregation. In contrast, transformer-based TSDet (mAP50: 0.872) relies on heavy computations, whereas AutoTriNet-YOLO maintains competitive accuracy with lower computational overhead, making it more suitable for real-time applications.

Furthermore, AutoTriNet-YOLO surpasses DPF-YOLOv8 (mAP50: 0.854) by 1.4% in mAP50 and 1.5% in mAP50-95, highlighting its efficacy in adverse conditions (e.g., haze, occlusion). The selective insert (SI) mechanism further enhances efficiency by dynamically pruning redundant modules, reducing inference latency without sacrificing accuracy.

While Sign-YOLO achieves higher mAP@50 (0.887), AutoTriNet-YOLO excels in localization precision (mAP@50–95: 0.653 vs. 0.642), which is more critical for autonomous driving applications requiring precise object positioning.

Conclusion

This paper presents AutoTriNet-YOLO, a triple-attention enhanced framework for robust traffic sign detection. By integrating CBAM, Non-local, and Lite Transformer branches into a unified TriplePathBlock, the model effectively captures both local details and global context. Combined with the Dynamic Fusion Gate (DFG) and Selective Insert (SI) mechanism, the framework achieves adaptive feature aggregation and efficient computation. Experimental results demonstrate that AutoTriNet-YOLO outperforms several state-of-the-art methods, achieving mAP@50 of 0.866 and mAP@50–95 of 0.653. Ablation studies confirm that each module contributes significantly to overall performance, especially CBAM for local spatial refinement. The model maintains strong accuracy across various traffic scenarios while reducing inference overhead, making it suitable for real-time deployment. Future work will explore lightweight optimization and multi-modal fusion to further enhance performance under complex environments. Overall, AutoTriNet-YOLO offers a balanced and scalable solution for intelligent transportation applications.

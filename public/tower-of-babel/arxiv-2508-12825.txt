Fermi velocity and magic angle renormalization in twisted bilayer graphene
Miguel Sánchez Sánchez 1 , José González 2 , and Tobias Stauber 1

Abstract
We discuss the Fermi-velocity renormalization in twisted bilayer graphene due to Coulomb exchange interaction within an atomistic tight-binding model. Adopting the Slater-Koster parametrization for the hopping parameters obtained from first principles, our results only depend on the effective dielectric constant ϵ \epsilon and the Hubbard-interaction U U . The Fermi velocity of graphene increases twist-angle independent by 25 % ~25\% for ϵ = 10 \epsilon=10 and U = 4 ​ e ​ V U=4eV , leading to an increase by more than 100 % 100\% of the flat bandwidth at twist-angle θ = 1.4 ∘ \theta=1.4^{\circ} . Including also the renormalization of the out-of-plane hopping terms, we further observe a shift of the magic angle from 1.02 ∘ 1.02^{\circ} to 0.96 ∘ 0.96^{\circ} . Our results offer a microscopic explanation of the critical temperature, T c T_{c} , as function of the twist angle where the largest T c T_{c} is found at θ m ​ a ​ x = 1.1 ∘ \theta_{max}=1.1^{\circ} . For θ > θ m ​ a ​ x \theta>\theta_{max} , T c T_{c} is obtained from the Bethe-Salpeter equation of the Cooper channel. For θ < θ m ​ a ​ x \theta<\theta_{max} , the discussion is based on the critical line of the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless phase transition.

Fermi velocity renormalization in SLG.
Fermi velocity renormalization in SLG was theoretically predicted more than 30 years ago by González et al.
González et al. 1994
and subsequently verified by a number of experiments based on measuring the effective cyclotron mass,
Elias et al. 2011
via scanning tunneling spectroscopy,
Li et al. 2009
;
Chae et al. 2012
by angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy,
Siegel et al. 2011
by quantum capacitance measurements,
Yu et al. 2013
via Landau level spectroscopy,
Faugeras et al. 2015
and also indirectly via the Drude weight.
Whelan et al. 2020
Only recently, it was directly observed using the quantum twisting microscope
Lee et al. 2025
confirming the renormalization of the tunneling matrix element from the bare value
t\sim 2.7
eV obtained from first-principles to
t^{*}\sim 3
eV.

The renormalization of the hopping amplitude or related Fermi velocity can be understood from a one-loop renormalisation group (RG) based on the continuous Dirac model
González et al. 1994

\displaystyle\frac{v_{F}^{*}}{v_{F}}=1+\frac{\alpha}{4}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{k}\;,

(3)

where
\alpha=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}\epsilon}\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar v_{F}}\approx 2.2/\epsilon
is the fine-structure constant of graphene with the unrenormalized Fermi velocity
v_{F}
and
\epsilon
characterises the static dielectric environment. This formula has been discussed and extended by several authors,
González et al. 1999
;
Stauber et al. 2005
;
Mishchenko 2007
;
Sheehy and Schmalian 2007
;
Herbut et al. 2008
;
Vafek and Case 2008
;
Jung and MacDonald 2011
;
Trushin and Schliemann 2011
;
Giuliani et al. 2012
;
Barnes et al. 2014
;
Astrakhantsev et al. 2015
;
Bauer et al. 2015
;
Sharma and Kopietz 2016
showing that a self-consistent Hartree-Fock theory remains valid even close to the neutrality point.

In order to make quantitative predictions, a full tight-binding model is needed since only then, ambiguities coming from the cut-off
\Lambda
of Eq. (
3
) can be avoided. In Ref.
Stauber et al. 2017
, the cut-off was determined from atomistic tight-binding calculations to be
\Lambda\sim|{\bf K}|=4\pi/3a
with
a=2.41
Å. Let us further stress that Eq. (
3
) is only valid in the long-wavelength limit, the full band-structure, however, can well be approximated by scaling the hopping matrix element.
Stauber et al. 2017
For a typical electronic density of
n=5\times 10^{12}
cm

3

and assuming BN-encapsulation with
\alpha\sim 0.45

Tight-binding model.
In order to unambiguously discuss the effect of the exchange interaction, we need to start from the non-interacting tight-binding model

\displaystyle H_{0}=-\sum_{n,m}\sum_{i,j}\sum_{\sigma}t_{n,m}^{i,j}\psi^{\dagger}_{n,i,\sigma}\psi_{m,j,\sigma}\;,

(4)

where the hopping matrix element

only depends on the vector between the two lattice sites, i.e.,
t_{n,m}^{i,j}=t({\bm{R}}_{n}-{\bm{R}}_{m}+{\bm{\delta}}_{i}-{\bm{\delta}}_{j})
with
{\bm{R}}_{n}
denoting the lattice vector of unit cell
n
and
{\bm{\delta}}_{i}
the position of lattice site
i
with respect to the unit cell. The spin is labeled by
\sigma
. We use the Slater-Koster parametrization

\displaystyle t({\bm{r}})=t_{\parallel}e^{\frac{a_{0}-r}{r_{0}}}\Big(1-\left(\frac{{\bm{r}}\cdot{\bm{e}}_{z}}{r}\right)^{2}\Big)+t_{\perp}e^{\frac{d_{\perp}-r}{r_{0}}}\left(\frac{{\bm{r}}\cdot{\bm{e}}_{z}}{r}\right)^{2},

where
a_{0}=1.42

Coulomb interaction.
The total Hamiltonian shall be written as

\displaystyle H_{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n,m}\sum_{i,j}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}V_{n,m}^{i,j}\psi_{n,i,\sigma}^{\dagger}\psi_{m,j,\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\psi_{m,j,\sigma^{\prime}}\psi_{n,i,\sigma}\;,

\displaystyle H_{U}

\displaystyle=\frac{U}{2}\sum_{n,i}\sum_{\sigma}\psi_{n,i,\sigma}^{\dagger}\psi_{n,i,\bar{\sigma}}^{\dagger}\psi_{n,i,\bar{\sigma}}\psi_{n,i,\sigma}\;,

Effective BM model.

Conclusions.
Using the approach outlined in Ref.
Sánchez Sánchez et al. 2025
, we have analyzed the renormalization of the electronic bands of TBG due to the exchange interaction between the flat and remote bands. This renormalization depends on the dielectric environment, but always amounts to a down-shift of the magic angle.

The effect of the exchange interaction can be well captured by renormalizing the inplane Fermi velocity and the interplane hopping elements
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
which turn out to be twist-angle independent. The renormalized flat bands for larger twist angles can further be well approximated by simply scaling the bare flat bands by a twist-angle dependent factor.

The renormalization of the magic angle in MATBG should be taken into account for the proper interpretation of experimental results with reduced screening. The twist angle dependence of the critical temperature, e.g., can be explained by one-particle quantities such as the Drude weight and the density of states, invoking the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless transition and the Bethe-Salpeter equation of the Cooper channel, respectively.

II
Valley symmetry in the tight-binding model

Our self-consistent algorithm seeks a renormalized state that preserves all symmetries of the system. Even if time-reversal and crystallographic symmetries are conserved by construction, one can still converge towards an intervalley coherent state that breaks the valley charge conservation. The valley charge, however, is an emergent quantity that is not unambiguously defined in the atomistic setup. Here, we describe our method to preserve the valley symmetry within the tight-binding model.

Our self-consistent algorithm is based on the Fock matrix
\langle\psi_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}\psi_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}\rangle
, where
\psi_{\bm{r}}
(
\psi^{\dagger}_{\bm{r}}
) annihilates (creates) an electron at site
\bm{r}
and for conciseness, we omit the spin in this discussion. The position vectors
\bm{r}
,
\bm{r}^{\prime}
are limited to the first moiré unit cell, denoted by
\bm{\delta}_{i}
,
\bm{\delta}_{j}
in the main text.

The Fock matrix can be written as a sum over the occupied Bloch states,
\{|\bm{k}n\rangle\}
, with
n=...-1,1,...
the band index that is a positive number for conduction states, and negative for valence states (the
4
flat bands then correspond to
n=-2,-1,1,2
). We thus get

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}\psi_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}\rangle=\sum_{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}n}<\varepsilon_{F}}\langle\bm{r}^{\prime}|\bm{k}n\rangle\langle\bm{k}n|\bm{r}\rangle\;,

(16)

where the sum is restricted to occupied states.

We proceed by distinguishing the central
20
bands,

i.e., the first
10
valence and conduction bands, and write

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}\psi_{\bm{r^{\prime}}}\rangle=\sum_{n<-10}\langle\bm{r^{\prime}}|\bm{k}n\rangle\langle\bm{k}n|\bm{r}\rangle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq-10\\
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}n}<\varepsilon_{F}\end{subarray}}\langle\bm{r^{\prime}}|\bm{k}n\rangle\langle\bm{k}n|\bm{r}\rangle\;.

(17)

We now project the microscopic valley operator
\tilde{\tau}_{z}
, defined, e.g., in Ref.
Sánchez Sánchez et al. 2025
, for each momentum onto the central
20
bands, with
n,m=-10,...,10

\displaystyle[\bar{\tau}_{z}(\bm{k})]_{nm}=\langle\bm{k}n|\tilde{\tau}_{z}|\bm{k}m\rangle

(18)

The eigenvalues of

are typically of the order of
\pm 0.99
and we can introduce the unitary matrix

with eigenvalue

20

Notice that the subspace of the central bands changes after each self-consistency loop, hence, we define a slightly different
\tau_{z}({\bm{k}})

\displaystyle|\bm{k}n\rangle_{\tau_{z}}=[\tau_{z}(\bm{k})]_{nm}|\bm{k}m\rangle\;.

(19)

The valley symmetry is now enforced via the modified Fock matrix

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}\psi_{\bm{r^{\prime}}}\rangle\to\sum_{n<-10}\langle\bm{r^{\prime}}|\bm{k}n\rangle\langle\bm{k}n|\bm{r}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq-10\\
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}n}<\varepsilon_{F}\end{subarray}}\langle\bm{r^{\prime}}|\bm{k}n\rangle\langle\bm{k}n|\bm{r}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq-10\\
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}n}<\varepsilon_{F}\end{subarray}}\langle\bm{r^{\prime}}|\bm{k}n\rangle_{\tau_{z}}{}_{\tau_{z}}\langle\bm{k}n|\bm{r}\rangle\;,

(20)

which is fed into the algorithm.

We have checked that the converged solutions are fixed points of the Hartree-Fock theory also without explicitly enforcing the valley symmetry, validating our method. With this procedure we arrive at a valley-preserving solution, avoiding the intervalley coherent phase with lower energy, especially at the renormalized magic angle.

IV
Superfluid stiffness and Drude weight

A convenient quantity to characterize the superconducting state is the Drude weight as it can be related to the superfluid stiffness. The Drude weight is a band-structure property and characterized by the velocity in the, say,
x
-direction at the Fermi surface. Quasi-flat bands, therefore, necessarily lead to a small Drude weight and consequentially to a suppression of superconductivity. We thus do not expect a maximal critical temperature at the renormalized magic angle in TBG.

We will calculate the Drude weight at the chemical potential
\mu
using the following formula:

\displaystyle D(\mu)=\left(\frac{e}{\hbar}\right)^{2}\frac{g_{s}}{A}\sum_{{\bm{k}},n}|v_{{\bm{k}},n}^{x}|^{2}\partial_{\mu}f(\epsilon_{{\bm{k}},n}-\mu)\;,

(28)

with
g_{s}=2
denoting the spin-degeneracy,
A=N_{c}A_{m}
the sample area with
A_{m}
the area of the moiré unit cell, and
f
the Fermi function. The velocity vertex
\hbar v^{x}=\partial H/\partial k_{x}
is defined with respect to the Hartree-Fock Hamiltonian. The superfluid stiffness is then related to the Drude weight by

\displaystyle\rho_{S}=\left(\frac{\hbar}{e}\right)^{2}D\;.

(29)

The self-consistent calculations use periodic boundary conditions with
N_{c}=36
. The Drude weights, however, are calculated with
N_{c}=900
. Even though the Brillouin zone around the magic angle is rather small, there is still noise at
T=0
due to the finite grid.

There is little temperature dependence of the Drude weight for low temperatures

K. However, there is a considerable temperature dependence of

for larger temperatures

7

as function of the twist angle

As finite temperature can roughly be interpreted as a source of disorder, our conclusions should be robust against disorder, inherent to experiments.

\Gamma^{0}(p_{1}p_{2};p_{3}p_{4})

\displaystyle\Gamma=\frac{g}{1+g\frac{k_{B}T}{N_{c}}\sum_{q}G^{0}(-q)G^{0}(q)}\;.

The vertex is thus determined by the summation of the Cooper channel and the summation of the Matsubara frequencies yields the following result:

\displaystyle\frac{k_{B}T}{N_{c}}\sum_{q}G^{0}(-q)G^{0}(q)=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{1-2f(\epsilon_{\bm{q}})}{2\epsilon_{{\bm{q}}}}\;,

\displaystyle\to\nu\int_{k_{B}T}^{\Lambda_{B}}\frac{d\epsilon}{\epsilon}=\nu\ln\left(\Lambda_{B}/k_{B}T\right)\;,

where we introduced a band cutoff

and also assumed that the DOS is approximately constant in this regime. Obviously, a constant DOS is not a good approximation in the case of TBG, but we will keep it for sake of simplicity.

The critical temperature is now defined when the perturbative approach breaks down. This gives the following expression:

\displaystyle k_{B}T_{c}=\Lambda_{B}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{g\nu}\right)

(37)

This functional behavior thus only depends on the DOS at optimal doping

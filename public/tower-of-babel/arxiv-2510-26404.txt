Explicit Consistency Error Estimate for Finite Element Solutions of the Poisson Equation on Convex Domains
Su Ruibo

Abstract
We derive explicit a priori consistency error estimates for a standard finite element discretization of the Poisson equation on convex domains, where the domain is approximated by an internal convex polyhedron. The obtained explicit estimates depend only on global geometric parameters and are applicable to general convex domains and arbitrary families of simplicial meshes.



1
Introduction

The Dirichlet problem of the Poisson equation is given as

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta\phi&=f\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\phi&=g\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega
is an open, bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

A standard approach to the Dirichlet problem is to decompose it into two subproblems: a Laplace equation with the prescribed boundary value
g
, and a Poisson equation with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=f\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

The harmonic part, satisfying the Laplace equation, can be approximated using potential methods (see, for example,
[
1
]
) or other techniques, for which a supremum error estimate follows from the maximum principle. We focus on the second part, the Poisson equation with homogeneous boundary conditions, and apply the Finite Element Method to it.

For 2-dimensional polygonal domains, Liu
[
2
,
3
]
proposed computable priori error estimates that require either evaluation of local interpolation error estimates on each element for
H^{2}
solutions or solving two finite element problems using the Lagrange FEM and the Raviart–Thomas FEM for solutions without
H^{2}
-regularity. His result was based on his earlier studies on interpolation error on triangular elements
[
4
]
, and has inspired further studies on local error estimate
[
5
]
and non-homogeneous Neumann problem
[
6
]
. Error analysis for general boundary conditions on smooth domains has also been investigated in
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
, although those results involve unspecified constants.

In this paper, we consider a simple yet realistic computational setting where both the domain and the source term are perturbed, and derive an explicit consistency error estimate for (
1
) on convex domains in dimensions
n=2,3
. The obtained bounds are fully explicit and depend only on global geometric parameters and seminorms of known functions, and can be further refined by case-specific calculations. In the two-dimensional case, we further propose explicit consistency error estimates that do not involve the minimal angle of the mesh. These results do not require mesh-specific computation and are therefore applicable to general families of meshes.

The derivation proceeds as follows. First, the domain is approximated by a polyhedral domain, and the resulting boundary perturbation is analyzed using a barrier function argument. Next, based on established local interpolation error estimates
[
4
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
, we obtain an explicit bound for the error between the weak solution in the perturbed domain and its FEM approximation.

Let

\displaystyle H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})\supset V_{h}=\text{Span}(\{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{N}\}),

(7)

\displaystyle H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})\supset V_{h,0}=\text{Span}(\{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{M}\}).

For each

Then if

\displaystyle|a-\Pi_{h}a|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}\leq\big(\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}E_{1}(T)\big)|a|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Here we define

\displaystyle E_{0}(T)=\sup\{|v|_{0};v\in H^{2}(T),|v|_{2}=1,v(p_{i})=0\text{ for }i=1,\dots,n\},

(10)

\displaystyle E_{1}(T)=\sup\{|v|_{1};v\in H^{2}(T),|v|_{2}=1,v(p_{i})=0\text{ for }i=1,\dots,n\},

Here

2.5
Numerical Solution

The Finite Element Equation (

Ax=b,

(12)

where

\displaystyle A_{ij}=\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\nabla\varphi_{i}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{j}dx\text{ for }i,j=1,\dots,M,

(13)

\displaystyle b_{i}=\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}f_{h}\varphi_{i}dx\text{ for }i=1,\dots,M,

\displaystyle u_{h}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}x_{j}\varphi_{j}.

The solution of

To estimate the total consistency error, we introduce a sequence of auxiliary problems corresponding to different layers of approximation (see Figure

Then from lemmas

Theorem 3.1

(Explicit Consistency Error)
.

Let

be the weak solution to (
1
),

be the finite element solution solution to (
11
). Then

|u-u_{h}|_{0}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}D\,|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\delta\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{P}(\Omega_{\delta})^{2}\|f-f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+A_{h}^{2}\,\|f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

(15)

Here,
C_{P}(\Omega_{\delta})
denotes the Poincaré constant of
\Omega_{\delta}
, defined as the inverse of the square root of the smallest eigenvalue of

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u=\lambda u\text{ in }\Omega_{\delta},\\
u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{\delta},\end{aligned}\right.

(16)

and it satisfies the geometric bound

C_{P}(\Omega_{\delta})\leq\frac{D}{\sqrt{n}\pi}.

(17)

Moreover,

A_{h}=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\big(E_{1}(T)\big),

(18)

which depends explicitly on the space dimension
n
and the
global geometric properties of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, as follows:

A_{h}\leq\begin{cases}\textbf{For $n=2$:}&\begin{cases}R_{h},&\text{(maximal circumradius, see~\eqref{def:Rh}),}\\[6.0pt]
0.69711\,\dfrac{\cos^{2}(\tfrac{\theta_{0}}{2})}{\sin(\tfrac{\theta_{0}}{2})}\,h,&\text{(if $\theta_{0}$ is the minimal angle),}\\[10.0pt]
\sqrt{\tfrac{11}{60}}\,h,&\text{(if $\mathcal{T}_{h}$ is non-blunt).}\end{cases}\\[14.0pt]
\textbf{For $n=3$:}&2.19\,\sigma\,h\quad\text{(if $h_{T}/\rho_{T}\leq\sigma$ for all $T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}$).}\end{cases}

(19)

Remark 3.2

(Upper Bound of
\|f-f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}
)
.

Suppose
f\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
, and
f_{h}
is defined as the piecewise constant barycenters interpolation of

on the elements
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

f_{h}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}f(g_{T})\chi_{T},

(20)

where

denotes the barycenter of
T
and

is its characteristic function. Then the following explicit bound holds.

\|f-f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\leq\tfrac{n}{n+1}h|\Omega_{\delta}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

(21)

Indeed, for any

,

|f(x)-f(g_{T})|\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|x-g_{T}|\leq\tfrac{n}{n+1}h_{T}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

(22)

hence

\|f-f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\leq\tfrac{n}{n+1}h_{T}|T|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

(23)

for each

. Summing over

yields (
21
).

If, alternatively,
f\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, and

, then

\|f-f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\leq\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\big(E_{0}(T)\big)|f|_{2},

(24)

where

is defined in (

Explicit formula for

can be found in theorem 1 in

\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}E_{0}(T)\leq\begin{cases}\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{83}}\,h^{2},&\text{for }n=2,\\[6.0pt]
8\,h^{2},&\text{for }n=3,\end{cases}

(25)

4
Boundary Perturbation

We evaluate the effect of boundary perturbation by deriving the upper bound of
|u-v|
in the gap
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}
, and applying maximum value principle in
\Omega_{\delta}
.

First, we characterize the gap
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}
using signed distance functions defined on the facets
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\delta}
of
\Omega_{\delta}
.

Definition 4.1
.

p_{F}(x)=\vec{n}_{F}\cdot(x-\vec{g}_{F})

(27)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\vec{n}_{F}
denotes the outward unit normal vector on
F
, and
\vec{g}_{F}
is the barycenter of
F
.

Then since
\Omega_{\delta}
is convex,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\delta}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:p_{F}(x)<0,\forall F\in\mathcal{F}_{\delta}\},

(28)

\displaystyle\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}=\cup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\delta}}\{x\in\Omega:p_{F}(x)\geq 0\}

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u
and
v
be the weak solutions to the boundary value problems illustrated in Figure
2
.
Then

\|u-v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,D\,\delta\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

(29)

Proof.

Step 1.
We derive an upper bound of
|u-v|
in the gap
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}
.

Let
F
be a facet of
\Omega_{\delta}
, and
r_{0}
be a point on
\partial\Omega
where the signed distance function
p_{F}
attains the maximum.

After translation and rotation of the coordinate axes, we set

x_{0}=0,\,\vec{n}_{F}=-\vec{e}_{1}.

(30)

Then

p_{F}(x)=\delta_{0}-x_{1}\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(31)

where
\delta_{0}
is the first coordinate of
\vec{g}_{F}
and satisfies

0\leq\delta_{0}\leq\delta
.

Define the barrier function

U(x)=\frac{1}{2}\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,x_{1}\,(D-x_{1}),

(32)

so that
-\Delta U=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.
Since

0\leq x_{1}\leq D,\,\forall x\in\overline{\Omega},

(33)

we have

U(x)\geq 0,\,\forall x\in\partial\Omega.

(34)

Because
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
, the comparison principle yields

|u(x)|\leq U(x)\text{ in }\Omega.

(35)

Thus,

|u(x)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\,D\,\delta\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\text{ in }\{x\in\Omega:p_{F}(x)\geq 0\}.

(36)

Applying this argument to each
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\delta}
gives

|u(x)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\,D\,\delta\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\text{ in }\cup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\delta}}\{x\in\Omega:p_{F}(x)\geq 0\}=\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}.

(37)

Since
v
is extended by 0 in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}
, we have

|u(x)-v(x)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\,D\,\delta\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\text{ for }x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}.

(38)

Step 2.
We derive an upper bound of
|u-v|
in
\Omega_{\delta}
.

Because

-\Delta(u-v)=0\text{ in }\Omega_{\delta},

(39)

the maximum value principle implies

\max_{\overline{\Omega_{\delta}}}|u-v|=\max_{\partial\Omega_{\delta}}|u-v|.

(40)

By
\partial\Omega_{\delta}\subset\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}
and (
38
),

\max_{\Omega_{\delta}}|u-v|\leq\frac{1}{2}\,D\,\delta\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

(41)

This completes the proof.
∎

(Optimality)

5
Approximation of Source term

We study the perturbation of the source term with a standard energy analysis and a simple yet nearly sharp estimate of the Poincaré constant that only depends on the space dimension and the diameter of the domain.

Lemma 5.1

(Poincaré inequality)
.

Let
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open domain with
\operatorname{diam}(V)\leq D
. Then, for all
u\in H_{0}^{1}(V)
,

\|u\|_{L^{2}(V)}\,\leq\,\frac{D}{\sqrt{n}\pi}|u|_{H^{1}(V)}.

(43)

Proof.

We translate the coordinate axis so that

V\subset W:=(0,D)^{n}.

(44)

Since
u\in H_{0}^{1}(V)
, its zero extension to
L
also belongs to
H_{0}^{1}(W)
, and hence admits a Fourier sine expansion

u(x)=(\frac{2}{D})^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi\alpha_{i}x_{i}}{D}\right),

(45)

In addition,

\partial_{k}u(x)=\left(\frac{2}{D}\right)^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\alpha}\,\frac{\pi\alpha_{k}}{D}\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi\alpha_{k}x_{k}}{D}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi\alpha_{i}x_{i}}{D}\right).

(46)

Thus,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}|c_{\alpha}|^{2},

(47)

\displaystyle|u|_{H^{1}(W)}^{2}=(\frac{\pi}{D})^{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\alpha_{k}|^{2}).

It follows that

\|u\|_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}\leq\frac{D^{2}}{n\pi^{2}}|u|_{H^{1}(W)}^{2}.

(48)

Finally, since
u
is extended by 0 outside
V
,

\|u\|_{L^{2}(V)}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(W)},\,|u|_{H^{1}(V)}=|u|_{H^{1}(W)}

(49)

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.2

(Optimality)
.

The exact Poincaré constant is the inverse of the square root of the smallest Dirichlet eigenvalue of
-\Delta
on the domain
V
. The above estimate is nearly sharp for general bounded domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, since the exact constants for the ball
B(0,\tfrac{D}{2})
are approximately

0.208\,D
when
n=2
, and exactly
\tfrac{D}{2\pi}
when
n=3
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
v
and
w
be the weak solutions to the boundary value problems illustrated in Figure
2
.
Then

\displaystyle|v-w|_{1}\leq\,C_{P}(\Omega_{\delta})\|f-f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

(50)

\displaystyle|v-w|_{0}\leq C_{P}(\Omega_{\delta})^{2}\|f-f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})},

where the Poincaré constant satisfies

C_{P}(\Omega_{\delta})\leq\frac{D}{\sqrt{n}\pi}.

(51)

Proof.

We conduct a standard energy estimate.
Subtracting the weak formulations for
v
and
w
yields

-\Delta(v-w)=f-f_{h}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\delta},\qquad v-w=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{\delta}.

Testing with
v-w
and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

|v-w|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}\leq\|f-f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\|v-w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Using the Poincaré inequality for
v-w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})
, we obtain

|v-w|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}\leq C_{P}(\Omega_{\delta})\,\|f-f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})},

and another application of the same inequality yields

\|v-w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\leq C_{P}(\Omega_{\delta})^{2}\,\|f-f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Since
v
and
w
are extended by zero outside
\Omega_{\delta}
, it follows that

|v-w|_{1}=|v-w|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})},\qquad|v-w|_{0}=\|v-w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

(52)

By Lemma
5.1
, the Poincaré constant satisfies

C_{P}(\Omega_{\delta})\leq D/(\sqrt{n}\,\pi)
.

This completes the proof.
∎

6
Total consistency Error

We prove Theorem
3.1
using Lemmas
4.2
and
5.3
, together with the interpolation error estimate.

Proof.

By isolating the sources of consistency error, we obtain

\displaystyle|u-u_{h}|_{0}\leq

\displaystyle|u-v|_{0}+|v-w|_{0}+|w-u_{h}|_{0}

(53)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u-v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{P}(\Omega_{\delta})^{2}\|f-f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+|w-u_{h}|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}D\,|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\delta\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{P}(\Omega_{\delta})^{2}\|f-f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+|w-u_{h}|_{0}.

It remains to estimate the last term
|w-u_{h}|_{0}
.

For completeness, we recall the standard interpolation estimate (see, e.g.
[
3
]
) (
14
).

The Galerkin orthogonality gives

|w-u_{h}|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}\leq|w-\Pi_{h}w|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

(54)

Using the local interpolation error estimate and the Miranda–Talenti inequality (
[
15
]
), we obtain

\displaystyle|w-\Pi_{h}w|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}|w-\Pi_{h}w|_{H^{1}(T)}^{2}\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}E_{1}(T)^{2}|w|_{H^{2}(T)}^{2}

(55)

\displaystyle\leq A_{h}^{2}|w|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}\leq A_{h}^{2}\|-\Delta w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}=A_{h}^{2}\|f_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}..

This gives the
H^{1}
seminorm estimate.

Applying the Aubin–Nitsche duality (adjoint) argument then gives

Here we denote

(57)

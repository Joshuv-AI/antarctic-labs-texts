1.
Introduction

Let
f
be a fixed holomorphic Hecke eigenform of weight
\kappa\equiv 0\pmod{4}
for the full modular group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. We write the Fourier expansion of
f
at infinity as

(1.1)

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}e(nz),

where
e(z)=e^{2\pi iz}
. It follows from Deligne’s proof
[
2
]
of the Weil conjecture that we have

(1.2)

\displaystyle|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq d(n)

where
d(n)
is the divisor function of
n
.

Considerable research has been devoted to understanding the behavior of Fourier coefficients of cusp forms. For example, it is shown by J. L. Hafner and Ivić in
[
6
]
that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}\lambda_{f}(n)\ll x^{1/3}.

In
[
3
]
, P. Gao and L. Zhao studied bounds for moments of sums involving with
\lambda_{f}(n)
twisted by
\chi_{8d}(n)
for
d
running over odd, positive, square-free integers. Here we write
\chi_{d}
for the Kronecker symbol
\big(\frac{d}{\cdot}\big)
and we note that
\chi_{8d}
is a primitive Dirichlet character when
d
is odd and square-free. More precisely, for any real number
m>0
, let

\displaystyle S_{m}(X,Y;f)=:\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq X\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{n\leq Y}\chi_{8d}(n)\lambda_{f}(n)\Big|^{m},

where
\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}
denotes a sum over positive odd square-free integers. Then it is shown in
[
3
, Theorem 1.6]
that under the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH) that for
m\geq 4
, one has

(1.3)

\displaystyle S_{m}(X,Y;f)\ll XY^{m/2}(\log X)^{\frac{m(m-3)}{2}+2}.

The above result is achieved using the method developed by B. Szabó in
[
15
]
to apply sharp upper bounds for shifted moments of families of
L
-functions to obtain bounds for moments of the corresponding character sums. Here we also note that the establishment of sharp upper bounds for shifted moments of families of
L
-functions under GRH relies crucially on a method of K. Soundararajan
[
14
]
and its refinement by A. J. Harper
[
7
]
.

Similar to (
1.3
), it is shown in
[
4
, Theorem 1.3]
that under GRH, for any real
m>\sqrt{5}+1
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq X\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{n\leq Y}\chi_{8d}(n)\Big|^{m}\ll XY^{m/2}(\log X)^{\frac{m(m-1)}{2}+1}.

It is further proved by M. Munsch in
[
10
]
that for a smoothed version of the left-hand side expression above, one may replace under GRH the exponent
\frac{m(m-1)}{2}+1
by
\frac{m(m-1)}{2}
of the right-hand side expression above for integers
m\geq 2
and the resulting estimation is optimal.

The result of Munsch is in accordance to an earlier result of M. V. Armon
[
1
, Theorem 2]
, who obtained unconditionallly the optimal bound for the left-hand side expression of (
1.4
) for the case
m=1
. Note more over that in
[
5
]
, the first named author evaluated the expression

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{n}\chi_{8d}(n)\Phi\left(\frac{n}{Y}\right)\right|^{2}\Psi\left(\frac{d}{X}\right),

where
\Phi
and
\Psi
are smooth, compactly supported functions on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}=(0,\infty)
, having
all partial derivatives extending continuously to the boundary, and satisfying the partial
derivative bounds

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}y^{j}\Phi^{(j)}\left(\frac{y}{Y}\right)\ll(1+\frac{y}{Y})^{-A},\quad x^{j}\Psi^{(j)}\left(\frac{x}{X}\right)\ll(1+\frac{x}{X})^{-B},\end{split}

for any positive integers
A,B
.

An asymptotic formula for the above expression is given in
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, which is valid when
X^{1/2}\ll Y\ll X^{1-\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

Motivated by the results in
[
5
]
and in
[
3
]
. It is the aim of this paper to evaluate asymptotically a smoothed version of
S_{m}(X,Y;f)
. For this, we fix two non-negative, smooth and compacted supported functions
\Phi,\Psi
as mentioned above and we define

(1.6)

\displaystyle S_{f}(X,Y;\Phi,\Psi)=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{d}\Bigg(\sum_{n}\lambda_{f}(n)\chi_{8d}(n)\Phi\Big(\frac{n}{Y}\Big)\Bigg)^{2}\Psi\Big(\frac{d}{X}\Big).

Our main result evaluates
S_{f}(X,Y;\Phi,\Psi)
asymptotically as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

With the notation as above. We have for large
X
and
Y
and any
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle S_{f}(X,Y;\Phi,\Psi)=C_{0}(\Phi,\Psi)XY+O(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}Y^{3/2+\varepsilon}+XY^{1/2+\varepsilon}),

where
C_{0}(\Phi,\Psi)
is given in (
3.15
), which is a constant depending on
\Phi
and
\Psi
only.

We note that as often is the case, the most interesting case for evaluating
S_{f}(X,Y;\Phi,\Psi)
is when
X
and
Y
are of compatible size
X\asymp Y
. Here one checks that (
1.7
) yields a valid asymptotic formula when
Y\ll X^{1-\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
. Note also in view of (
1.3
) and the result of Theorem
1.1
also suggests that one has for all real
m\geq 2
,

\displaystyle S_{m}(X,Y;f)\ll XY^{m/2}(\log X)^{\frac{m(m-3)}{2}+1}.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
makes use of the Poisson summation formula established in
[
13
]
and follows closely the treatments developed by K. Soundararajan and M. P. Young
[
12
]
in their work on the second moment of quadratic twists of modular
L
-functions.

2.

2.1.
Gauss sums

For all odd integers
m

(2.1)

\displaystyle G_{k}(m)=\left(\frac{1-i}{2}+\left(\frac{-1}{m}\right)\frac{1+i}{2}\right)\sum_{a\mod m}\left(\frac{a}{m}\right)e\left(\frac{ak}{m}\right).

We reserve the letter

for a prime number throughout the paper. Let also

be the Euler totient function of

. Our next result evaluates
G_{k}(m)
, which is taken from
[

, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
(m_{1},m_{2})=1
then
G_{k}(m_{1}m_{2})=G_{k}(m_{1})G_{k}(m_{2})
. Suppose that

is
the largest power of
p
dividing
k
(put

if
k=0
).
Then for

we have

G_{k}(p^{b})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\mbox{if $b\leq a$ is odd},\\
\varphi(p^{b})&\mbox{if $b\leq a$ is even},\\
-p^{a}&\mbox{if $b=a+1$ is even},\\
(\frac{k/p^{a}}{p})p^{a}\sqrt{p}&\mbox{if $b=a+1$ is odd},\\
0&\mbox{if $b\geq a+2$}.\end{array}\right.

2.3.
Poisson Summation

For any smooth function
F
, we define

(2.2)

\widetilde{F}(\xi)=\int\limits^{\infty}_{-\infty}\left(\cos(2\pi\xi x)+\sin(2\pi\xi x)\right)F(x)dx.

We note the following Poisson summation formula from
[
13
, Lemma 2.6]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
F
be a smooth function compactly supported on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. We have, for any odd integer

\sum_{(d,2)=1}\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)F\left(\frac{d}{X}\right)=\frac{X}{2n}\left(\frac{2}{n}\right)\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}G_{k}(n)\widetilde{F}\left(\frac{kX}{2n}\right),

where

is defined in (

is defined in (

2.5.
Symmetric square
L
-functions

Recall that
f
is a fixed holomorphic Hecke eigenform of weight
\kappa\equiv 0\pmod{4}
for the full modular group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and that
\lambda_{f}(n)
is given in (
1.1
). The symmetric square
L
-function
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
of
f
is then defined for
\Re(s)>1
by
(see
[
8
, p. 137]
and
[
8
, (25.73)]
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=&\zeta(2s)\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n^{2})}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{s}}+\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}-\frac{1}{p^{3s}}\Big)^{-1},\end{split}

where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function.

It follows from a result of G. Shimura
[
11
]
that
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
is holomorphic at
s=1
. Moreover, the corresponding completed symmetric square
L
-function

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=

\displaystyle\pi^{-3s/2}\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+1}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+\kappa-1}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+\kappa}{2}\Big)L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)

is entire and satisfies the functional equation
\Lambda(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=\Lambda(1-s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
.

\widetilde{H}_{0}(u,v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\check{h}(xX;uY,vY)dx

and
\check{h}(x;u,v)
is defined by (
3.3
). Similar to (
3.4
), we see that

(3.12)

\widetilde{H}_{0}(u,v)\ll\frac{1}{|uv|(1+|u|)^{A_{2}}(1+|v|)^{A_{3}}}.

We deduce from (
3.7
), (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) that

(3.13)

\displaystyle S_{k=0}=\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\Big)^{2}\frac{4X}{\pi^{2}}\int_{(2)}\int_{(2)}Y^{u}Y^{v}\widetilde{H}_{0}(u,v)Z(u,v)\,du\,dv+O\Big(\frac{XY^{1+\varepsilon}}{Z}\Big),

where

\displaystyle Z(u,v)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n_{1}n_{2},2)=1\\
n_{1}n_{2}=\square\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n_{1})\lambda_{f}(n_{2})}{n_{1}^{u}n_{2}^{v}}\prod_{p|n_{1}n_{2}}\frac{p}{p+1}.

The above function is essentially defined on
[
12
, p. 1108]
, except with
u,v
replaced by
u+1/2,v+1/2
there. We thus deduce from
[
12
, (4.6)]
that

\displaystyle Z(u,v)=\zeta(u+v)L(2u,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)L(2v,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)L(u+v,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)Z_{2}(u,v),

where
Z_{2}(u,v)
converges absolutely in the region of
\Re(u)
and
\Re(v)
larger than
1/4
, and is uniformly bounded there.

We now evaluate the main term of (
3.13
) by first moving the integrals there to
\Re(u)=\Re(v)=1/2+\varepsilon
without encountering any pole. We then move the line of integration in
v
to
\Re(v)=1/4+\varepsilon
by encountering a simple pole at
v=1-u
, whose residue equals

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{4XY}{\pi^{2}}\int_{(1/2+\varepsilon)}\widetilde{H}_{0}(u,1-u)L(2u,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)L(2-2u,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)L(1,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)Z_{2}(u,1-u)\,du.

We now estimate the remaining integrals on
\Re(u)=1/2+\varepsilon
,
\Re(v)=1/4+\varepsilon
by further moving the line of integration in
u
to
\Re(u)=1/4+\varepsilon
without encountering any pole. We then apply the convexity bound for
\zeta(s),L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
as given in
[
8
, (5.20)]
together with (
2.3
) (which implies that the analytic conductor of
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
is
\ll(1+|s|)^{3}
) to see that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\zeta(s)\ll(1+|s|)^{(1-\Re(s))/2+\varepsilon},\ L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)\ll(1+|s|)^{3(1-\Re(s))/2+\varepsilon}\qquad

\displaystyle 0<\Re(s)<1

The above estimations and (
3.12
) with
A_{2}=A_{3}=5
now enable us to see that the integrations on
\Re(u)=1/4+\varepsilon
,
\Re(v)=1/4+\varepsilon
contribute
\ll XY^{1/2+\varepsilon}
. We thus conclude that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{k=0}=&C_{0}(\Phi,\Psi)XY+O(XY^{1/2+\varepsilon}),\end{split}

where

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{0}(\Phi,\Psi)=:&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\cdot\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\int_{(1/2+\varepsilon)}\widetilde{H}_{0}(u,1-u)L(2u,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)L(2-2u,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)L(1,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)Z_{2}(u,1-u)\,du.\end{split}

3.3.
The
k\neq 0
terms

We first state a lemma, which is established in
[
12
, Sec. 3.3]
, to express the weight function appearing in (
3.6
) in a form more suitable for Mellin transforms.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f
be a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with rapid decay at infinity such that
f
itself and all its derivatives have finite limits as
x\rightarrow 0^{+}
. We consider the transform given by

\widehat{f}_{CS}(y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)CS(2\pi xy)\,dx,

where
CS
stands for either the
\cos
or the
\sin
function. Then

\widehat{f}_{CS}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1/2)}f_{M}(1-s)\Gamma(s)CS\left(\frac{\text{sgn}(y)\pi s}{2}\right)(2\pi|y|)^{-s}\,ds,

where
f_{M}(s)
is the Mellin transform of
f
defined by

f_{M}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}f(x)\,dx.

We apply Lemma
3.4
to see that

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(C+S)\Big(\frac{\pi kxX}{a^{2}n_{1}n_{2}}\Big)h(xX,n_{1},n_{2})\,dx=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)X}\int_{(\varepsilon)}h_{M}(1-s;n_{1},n_{2})\Big(\frac{a^{2}n_{1}n_{2}}{\pi|k|}\Big)^{s}\Gamma(s)(C+\text{sgn}(k)S)\Big(\frac{\pi s}{2}\Big)\,ds.

Taking the Mellin transforms in the variables
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
, the right-hand side of (
3.16
) equals

(3.17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\Big)^{3}\int_{(\varepsilon)}\int_{(\varepsilon)}\int_{(\varepsilon)}\widetilde{h}_{M}(1-s,u,v)\frac{1}{n_{1}^{u}n_{2}^{v}}\Big(\frac{a^{2}n_{1}n_{2}}{\pi|k|}\Big)^{s}\Gamma(s)(C+\text{sgn}(k)S)\Big(\frac{\pi s}{2}\Big)\,ds\,du\,dv,

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}_{M}(s,u,v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x,y,z)x^{s}y^{u}z^{v}\,\frac{dx}{x}\,\frac{dy}{y}\,\frac{dz}{z}.

Integrating by parts and (
1.5
) implies that for
\Re(s)
,
\Re(u)
,
\Re(v)>0
and any integers
B_{j}\geq 0
,
1\leq j\leq 3
, we have

(3.18)

\widetilde{h}_{M}(s,u,v)\ll\frac{X^{\Re(s)}Y^{\Re(u)+\Re(v)}}{|uvs|(1+|s|)^{B_{1}}(1+|u|)^{B_{2}}(1+|v|)^{B_{3}}}.

We deduce from (
3.6
), (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) that

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{k\neq 0}&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq Z\\
(a,2)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a)}{a^{2}}\sum_{k\neq 0}(-1)^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n_{1},2a)=1\\
(n_{2},2a)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n_{1})\lambda_{f}(n_{2})}{n_{1}n_{2}}G_{k}(n_{1}n_{2})\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\Big)^{3}\\
&\quad\times\int_{(\varepsilon)}\int_{(\varepsilon)}\int_{(\varepsilon)}\widetilde{h}_{M}(1-s,u,v)\frac{1}{n_{1}^{u}n_{2}^{v}}\Big(\frac{a^{2}n_{1}n_{2}}{\pi|k|}\Big)^{s}\Gamma(s)(C+\text{sgn}(k)S)\Big(\frac{\pi s}{2}\Big)\,ds\,du\,dv.\end{split}

Note that by (
3.18
) and the estimation (see
[
12
, p. 1107]
),

(3.20)

\Big|\Gamma(s)(C\pm S)\Big(\frac{\pi s}{2}\Big)\Big|\ll|s|^{\Re(s)-1/2},

the integral over
s
in (
3.19
) may be taken over any vertical lines between
0
and
1
and
the integrals over
u,v
in (
3.19
) may be taken over any vertical lines between
0
and
2
.

Observe that
G_{k}(m)=G_{4k}(m)
for odd
m
and we write
4k=k_{1}k^{2}_{2}
, where
k_{1}
is a fundamental discriminant, and
k_{2}>0
. Then the expression in (
3.19
) can be written as

\displaystyle S_{k\neq 0}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq Z\\
(a,2)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a)}{a^{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{k_{1}}\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\Big)^{3}\int_{(1+2\varepsilon)}\int_{(1+2\varepsilon)}\int_{(1/2+\varepsilon)}Z(u-s,v-s,s;a,k_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\widetilde{h}\left(1-s,u,v\right)\Gamma(s)(C+\text{sgn}(k_{1})S)\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right)\Big(\frac{a^{2}}{\pi|k_{1}|}\Big)^{s}ds\,du\,dv,

where the function
Z
is defined in (
2.4
).

We make a change of variables to rewrite
S_{k\neq 0}
as

\displaystyle S_{k\neq 0}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq Z\\
(a,2)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a)}{a^{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{k_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\right)^{3}\int_{(1/2+\varepsilon)}\int_{(1/2+\varepsilon)}\int_{(1/2+\varepsilon)}Z(u,v,s;a,k_{1})

\displaystyle\times{\widetilde{h}}(1-s,u+s,v+s)\Gamma(s)(C+\text{sgn}(k_{1})S)\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right)\left(\frac{a^{2}}{\pi|k_{1}|}\right)^{s}ds\,du\,dv.

We split the sum over
k_{1}
into two terms according to whether
|k_{1}|\leq K
or not, with
K
to be optimized later. If
|k_{1}|\leq K
, we move the lines of integration to
\Re(s)=c_{1}
for some
1/2<c_{1}<1
,
\Re(u)=\Re(v)=\varepsilon
. Otherwise, we move
the lines of integration to
\Re(s)=c_{2}
for some
c_{2}>1
,
\Re(u)=\Re(v)=\varepsilon
.
We encounter no poles in either case. Applying Lemma
2.7
and the bound in (
3.5
) yields

\displaystyle Z(u,v,s;a,k_{1})

\displaystyle\ll|L_{a}(1/2+u,f\otimes\chi_{k_{1}})L_{a}(1/2+v,f\otimes\chi_{k_{1}})|\ll d^{2}(a)\left(|L(1/2+u,f\otimes\chi_{k_{1}})|^{2}+|L(1/2+v,f\otimes\chi_{k_{1}})|^{2}\right).

The above and (
3.18
) with
B_{1}=B_{2}=B_{3}=5
, together with (
3.20
) and the symmetry in
u
and
v
give that the terms with
|k_{1}|\leq K
contribute

\displaystyle\ll X^{1-c_{1}}

\displaystyle Y^{2c_{1}+2\varepsilon}\sum_{a\leq Z}\frac{d^{2}(a)}{a^{2-2c_{1}}}\int_{(\varepsilon)}\int_{(\varepsilon)}\int_{(c_{1})}\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{|k_{1}|\leq K}\frac{1}{|k_{1}|^{c_{1}}}|L(1/2+u,f\otimes\chi_{k_{1}})|^{2}

(3.21)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\frac{|s|^{\Re(s)-1/2}\,ds\,du\,dv}{|1-s||u+s||v+s|(1+|1-s|)^{5}(1+|u+s|)^{5}(1+|v+s|)^{5}}.

We further apply Lemma
2.9
and partial summation to get

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{|k_{1}|\leq K}\frac{1}{|k_{1}|^{c_{1}}}|L(1/2+u,f\otimes\chi_{k_{1}})|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll K^{1-c_{1}+\varepsilon}(1+|t|)^{1+\varepsilon}\ll K^{1-c_{1}+\varepsilon}\left((1+|u+s|)^{1+\varepsilon}+|s|^{1+\varepsilon}\right).

Applying the above in (
3.3
), we infer that the terms with
|k_{1}|\leq K
contribute

\displaystyle\ll X^{1-c_{1}}Y^{2c_{1}+2\varepsilon}K^{1-c_{1}+\varepsilon}Z^{2c_{1}-1+\varepsilon}.

Similarly, the contribution from the complementary terms with
|k_{1}|>K
is

\displaystyle\ll X^{1-c_{2}}Y^{2c_{2}+2\varepsilon}K^{1-c_{2}+\varepsilon}Z^{2c_{2}-1+\varepsilon}.

We now balance these contributions by setting
K=Y^{2}Z^{2}/X
so that

\displaystyle X^{1-c_{1}}Y^{2c_{1}}K^{1-c_{1}}Z^{2c_{1}-1}=X^{1-c_{2}}Y^{2c_{2}}K^{1-c_{2}}Z^{2c_{2}-1}.

Now taking
c_{1}=1/2+\varepsilon
, we conclude

(3.22)

\displaystyle S_{k\neq 0}\ll(XYZ)^{\varepsilon}Y^{2}Z.

We now deduce from (
3
), Lemma
3.1
, (
3.14
) and (
3.22
) that

\displaystyle S(h)=C_{0}(\Phi,\Psi)XY+O\left((XY)^{1+\varepsilon}Z^{-1+\varepsilon}+(XYZ)^{\varepsilon}Y^{2}Z+XY^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Now the expression in (
1.7
) follows upon setting
Z=\sqrt{X/Y}
in the above expression. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
F
be a number field. Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
,
n\geq 1
. Denote by
L(s,\pi)
the standard
L
-function associated to
\pi
. Let
c>0
be a positive number. We define a
Landau-Siegel zero
of
L(s,\pi)
, relative to
c
, to be a real zero
\beta
of
L(s,\pi)
satisfying

1-\frac{c}{\log C(\pi)}<\beta<1.

Here
C(\pi)
denotes the analytic conductor of
L(s,\pi)
.

Studying Landau-Siegel zeros of automorphic
L
-functions is a central topic in analytic number theory. For example, when
F=\mathbb{Q}
and
\pi=\chi
is a real primitive Dirichlet character, the possibility of existence of a Landau-Siegel zero of the Dirichlet
L
-function
L(s,\chi)
is a long-standing open problem. Knowing the nonexistence or the existence of such a zero, on both sides, has deep consequences in other important arithmetic problems such as the distribution of prime numbers in arithmetic progressions
[
Dav00
]
, the class number problem
[
Dav00
]
, the twin prime conjecture
[
HB83
]
, and the non-vanishing of
L
-functions on
\text{GL}(2)

[
IS00
]
, to name a few.

Although the problem of Landau-Siegel zeros of Dirichlet
L
-functions is extremely hard, eliminating Landau-Siegel zeros of higher degree automorphic
L
-functions is surprisingly more approachable. In the appendix of the ground-breaking paper
[
HL94
]
, Goldfeld, Hoffstein and Lieman proved that if
\pi
is a non-dihedral cuspidal representation of
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, then
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi))
has no Landau-Siegel zero. Here
\text{Ad}(\pi)
denotes the adjoint square lift of
\pi
, which is a self-dual cuspidal representation of
\text{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
studied in
[
GJ78
]
. Inspired by their work, Landau-Siegel zeros of standard
L
-functions
L(s,\pi)
were extensively studied later in several papers including
[
HR95
]
[
Ban97
]
[
Tho21
]
[
Luo23
]
. We will review some of these results in more details in Section
2.2
.

Let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be cuspidal automorphic representations of
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
and
\text{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, respectively. We similarly define a
Landau-Siegel zero
of the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
, relative to a positive constant
c>0
, to be a real zero
\beta
of
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
satisfying

1-\frac{c}{\log(C(\pi_{1})C(\pi_{2}))}<\beta<1.

Landau-Siegel zeros of Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions were first studied by Ramakrishnan and Wang
[
RW03
]
. They proved that for cuspidal representations
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
of
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
can only possibly have a Landau-Siegel zero in a few special cases. In particular, when the pair
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
is of
general type
, i.e., when
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are non-dihedral and are not twist-equivalent,
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
has no Landau-Siegel zero. Landau-Siegel zeros of other types of Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions were studied in
[
Luo23
]
[
Tho25a
]
[
Tho25b
]
. We will also review some of these results in more details in Section
2.2
.

1.2.
Main Results

In this paper we study Landau-Siegel zeros of
triple product

L
-functions on
\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(2)
and on
\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(3)
.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
and
\pi_{3}
be cuspidal automorphic representations of
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. The triple product
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
was first studied in
[
Gar87
]
when
F=\mathbb{Q}
and
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
associate to holomorphic cusp forms. The construction was then generalized to any number field
F
in
[
PSR87
]
. Moreover, in view of the work
[
Ram00
]
on the modularity of Rankin-Selberg product on
\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(2)
, the triple product
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
can also be viewed as a Rankin-Selberg
L
-function on
\text{GL}(4)\times\text{GL}(2)
:

L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\times\pi_{3}),

whose analytic properties are well-known by
[
JPSS83
]
.

We say the triple
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3})
is of

•

general type
, if
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
are all non-dihedral, and
\pi_{i}
is not twist-equivalent to
\pi_{j}
for all
i\neq j
.

•

twist-equivalent type
, if
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
are all non-dihedral, but
\pi_{i}
is twist-equivalent to
\pi_{j}
for some
i\neq j
.

•

dihedral type
, if at least one of
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
is dihedral.

With these terminologies we are now ready to state our first main result.

Theorem A
.

There exists an absolute effective constant
c>0
, such that the triple product
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
has no Landau-Siegel zero relative to
c
, except possibly in the following cases:

(1)

(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3})
is of dihedral type.

(2)

(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3})
is of twist-equivalent type, and moreover
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
are all twist-equivalent to each other.

Remark 1.1
.

Theorem
A
is a simplified version of the full statement Theorem
3.1
. See Theorem
3.1
for a precise description on the conditions when
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
could possibly have a Landau-Siegel zero. In particular,
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
has no Landau-Siegel zero if
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3})
is of general type.

Remark 1.2
.

To illustrate why there might be a Landau-Siegel zero when
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3})
is of dihedral type, let us assume
\pi_{1}
is dihedral. Then
\pi_{1}=I_{K}^{F}(\chi_{1})
is induced from a character
\chi_{1}
on some quadratic extension
K/F
. By the adjointness property of Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions we have

L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})=L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\times B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})\otimes\chi_{1}),

where
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})
and
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})
are base changes from
F
to
K
of
\pi_{2}
and
\pi_{3}
, respectively. Thus the triple product
L
-function reduces to a Rankin-Selberg
L
-function on
\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(2)
over
K
, a case that has been studied in
[
RW03
]
. According to
[
RW03
, Theorem A]
, there are two reduced cases where there might be a Landau-Siegel zero.

However, our argument in Section
3.3
gives more information on the possible Landau-Siegel zeros of
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
than those that can be obtained by directly applying
[
RW03
, Theorem A]
. This is due to the special natures of triple product
L
-functions.

Remark 1.3
.

To see why there might be a Landau-Siegel zero when
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
are all twist-equivalent, let us simply assume that
\pi_{1}=\pi_{2}=\pi_{3}=\pi
is a non-dihedral cuspidal representation of
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
with central character
\omega_{\pi}
. In this case the triple product
L
-function decomposes as

L(s,\pi\times\pi\times\pi)=L(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi))\cdot L(s,\pi\otimes\omega_{\pi})^{2}.

However, eliminating Landau-Siegel zeros of the symmetric cube
L
-function
L(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi))
is a long-standing open problem, which is only known under the (very strong) hypothesis that certain twisted symmetric fifth power
L
-function is analytic in some interval
(t,1)
,
t<1
.

Let us proceed to describe our second main result. Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be cuspidal automorphic representations of
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\text{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. Then the triple product
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)
on
\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(3)
can likewise be viewed as a Rankin-Selberg
L
-function on
\text{GL}(4)\times\text{GL}(3)
:

L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\times\pi).

We say the triple
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi)
is of

•

general type
, if
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are non-dihedral, not twist-equivalent, and
\pi
is not twist-equivalent to either
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
or
\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})
.

•

twist-equivalent type
, if
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are non-dihedral, but either (1)
\pi_{1}
is twist-equivalent to
\pi_{2}
, or (2)
\pi
is twist-equivalent to
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
or
\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})
.

•

dihedral type
, if at least one of
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
is dihedral.

Our second main result is as follows.

Theorem B
.

There exists an absolute effective constant
c>0
such that the triple product
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)
has no Landau-Siegel zero relative to
c
, except possibly in the case where
\pi
is twist-equivalent to
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
or
\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})
.

Remark 1.4
.

See Theorem
4.1
for a more refined version of Theorem
B
, where we give a precise description on the conditions when
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)
could possibly have a Landau-Siegel zero. In particular,
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)
has no Landau-Siegel zero if
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi)
is of general type or dihedral type.

Remark 1.5
.

It is interesting to see that
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)
has no Landau-Siegel zero if
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi)
is of dihedral type. To see why this is not obvious, let us assume
\pi_{1}=I_{K}^{F}(\chi_{1})
is dihedral. Then we have

L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)=L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\times B_{F}^{K}(\pi)\otimes\chi_{1}).

Landau-Siegel zeros of the
\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(3)
Rankin-Selberg
L
-function on the right side have been studied in
[
Luo23
]
. According to
[
Luo23
, Theorem 2]
, there might be a Landau-Siegel zero if
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})
is cuspidal non-dihedral, and
B_{F}^{K}(\pi)
is twist-equivalent to
\text{Ad}(B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2}))
. However, using a different argument we are able to show that there is no Landau-Siegel zero at all in this dihedral case. This argument, again, relies on special natures of triple product
L
-functions.

Remark 1.6
.

To see why there might be a Landau-Siegel zero when
\pi
is twist-equivalent to
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
or
\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})
, let us assume that
\pi=\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
, and
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are not twist-equivalent. In this case we have

L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)=L(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\times\pi_{2}\otimes\omega_{1}^{-1})\cdot L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}),

where
\omega_{1}
is the central character of
\pi_{1}
. Eliminating Landau-Siegel zeros of the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\times\pi_{2}\otimes\omega_{1}^{-1})
is an open problem.

In
[
Tho25b
]
[
Tho25a
]
, Thorner addressed the importance of allowing an additional character twist to get a standard zero-free region. In our case, let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be cuspidal automorphic representations on
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
and
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation on
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
or
\text{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. Let
\eta
be an arbitrary (idele class) character on
F
. Then in view of the relation

L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi\otimes\eta)=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times(\pi\otimes\eta)),

we can study the Landau-Siegel zeros of
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi\otimes\eta)
by applying Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
to the triple
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi\otimes\eta)
. In particular, if
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi)
is of general type, then
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi\otimes\eta)
is also of general type. By taking
\eta=|\cdot|^{it}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we immediately conclude the following:

Theorem C
.

Assume
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi)
is of general type. Then there exists an effective absolute constant
c>0
, such that
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)\neq 0
in the following region:

\left\{s=\sigma+it:\sigma>1-\frac{c}{\log(C(\pi_{1})C(\pi_{2})C(\pi)(|t|+3)^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]})}\right\}.

1.3.
Proof Strategy

As is well-known to experts, the technical heart behind any attempt to eliminate Landau-Siegel zeros of an
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
(or
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
) is to construct an auxiliary
L
-function
L(s)
with at most
r
Landau-Siegel zeros, in such a way that we can decompose
L(s)
as

L(s)=L(s,\pi)^{a}\cdot L(s,\overline{\pi})^{b}\cdot L_{Res}(s),

with
a+b>r
, and the residual part
L_{Res}(s)
is analytic in some interval
(t,1)
,
t<1
. If we have constructed such an
L(s)
, then any Landau-Siegel zero of
L(s,\pi)
would be a Landau-Siegel zero of order at least
a+b
of
L(s)
, which cannot exist because
L(s)
has at most
r
Landau-Siegel zeros, and
r<a+b
.

For most of the work in this field, including the present paper, the auxiliary
L
-function is constructed as a special type of Rankin-Selberg
L
-function,
L(s)=L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})
, where
\Pi
is some carefully chosen isobaric automorphic representation. Thus if there is anything creative in this paper, it is the construction of
\Pi
, which, in the new context of triple product
L
-functions, exhibits some new features.

Recently, in his preprint
[
Tho25b
]
towards the study of Landau-Siegel zeros of Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions of symmetric power liftings of Hilbert modular forms over totally real fields, Thorner (for the first time) constructed auxiliary
L
-functions
L(s)
that are not in the shape of
L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})
. In
[
Tho25a
]
, he also studied Landau-Siegel zeros of Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})\otimes\chi)
, where
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are cuspidal representations of
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, and
\chi
is a character on
F
. As we shall see later, our study of Landau-Siegel zeros of
\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(3)
triple product
L
-functions reduces to this type in some particular cases. So we would like to express our gratefulness to Prof. Jesse Thorner for his great work.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we review some preliminaries, including some instances of Langlands functoriality and some known cases of the nonexistence of Landau-Siegel zeros that will be used throughout this paper. In Section
3
and Section
4
we prove Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Langlands Functoriality

In this section we review some known instances of Langlands functoriality that are crucial to our work. First let us introduce some notations. We use
\mathcal{A}_{0}(n,F)
(resp.
\mathcal{A}(n,F)
) to denote the set of cuspidal (resp. isobaric) automorphic representations of
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
with unitary central characters
\omega_{\pi}
. For
\pi\in\mathcal{A}(n,F)
we denote by
\overline{\pi}
the
dual
representation of
\pi
. We say
\pi
is
self-dual
, if
\pi\cong\overline{\pi}
. We say
\pi
is
monomial
, if
\pi\cong\pi\otimes\eta
for some non-trivial character
\eta
on
F
. We say
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(n,F)
are
twist-equivalent
, if
\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{2}\otimes\mu
for some character
\mu
on
F
.

Let us start with base change and automorphic induction. Let
K/F
be a cyclic extension of number fields of prime degree
\ell
. Let
n\geq 1
be a positive integer. The base change map

B_{F}^{K}:\mathcal{A}(n,F)\to\mathcal{A}(n,K),\hskip 8.53581pt\pi\mapsto B_{F}^{K}(\pi)

and the automorphic induction map

I_{K}^{F}:\mathcal{A}(n,K)\to\mathcal{A}(n\ell,F),\hskip 8.53581pt\alpha\mapsto I_{K}^{F}(\alpha)

were constructed in
[
AC89
]
. These maps satisfy the following properties:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\theta=\theta_{K/F}
be a generator of the Galois group Gal
(K/F)
. Let
\eta=\eta_{K/F}
be the character on
F
associated to
K/F
. Then

(1)

Let
\pi\in\mathcal{A}(n\ell,F)
. Then
\pi
lies in the image of
I_{K}^{F}
if and only if
\pi\cong\pi\otimes\eta
.

(2)

Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}(n,K)
. Then
\alpha
lies in the image of
B_{F}^{K}
if and only if
\alpha\cong\alpha\circ\theta
.

(3)

For
\pi\in\mathcal{A}(n,F)
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}(m,K)
, we have

L(s,\pi\times I_{K}^{F}(\alpha))=L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi)\times\alpha).

(4)

Let
\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(n,F)
be cuspidal. Then
B_{F}^{K}(\pi)
is cuspidal if and only if
\pi
is not isomorphic to
\pi\otimes\eta
. Moreover, we have

I_{K}^{F}(B_{F}^{K}(\pi))\cong\boxplus_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\pi\otimes\eta^{j}.

(5)

Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(n,K)
be cuspidal. Then
I_{K}^{F}(\alpha)
is cuspidal if and only if
\alpha
is not isomorphic to
\alpha\circ\theta
. Moreover, we have

B_{F}^{K}(I_{K}^{F}(\alpha))\cong\boxplus_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\alpha\circ\theta^{j}.

We then review functoriality results of symmetric power liftings. Let
\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(2,F)
. Denote by
\text{Sym}^{m}(\pi)
the symmetric
m^{th}
-power lifting of
\pi
. We set
\text{Ad}(\pi):=\text{Sym}^{2}(\pi)\otimes\omega_{\pi}^{-1}
,
A^{3}(\pi):=\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi)\otimes\omega_{\pi}^{-1}
and
A^{4}(\pi):=\text{Sym}^{4}(\pi)\otimes\omega_{\pi}^{-2}
. We say
\pi
is

•

dihedral
, if
\pi
is monomial, or equivalently, if
\pi=I_{K}^{F}(\chi)
is an automorphic induction of a character
\chi
on some quadratic extension
K/F
.

•

tetrahedral
, if
\text{Sym}^{2}(\pi)
is cuspidal and monomial.

•

octahedral
, if
\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi)
is cuspidal and monomial.

•

of
solvable polyhedral type
, if
\pi
is either dihedral or tetrahedral or octahedral.

Below is a summary of
[
GJ78
, Theorem 9.3]
,
[
KS02b
, Theorem B]
,
[
KS02a
, Theorem 2.2.2]
,
[

, Theorem B]
,
[

, Theorem 3.3.7]

:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(2,F)
. Then we have

(1)

\text{Sym}^{2}(\pi)\in\mathcal{A}(3,F)
.
\text{Sym}^{2}(\pi)

\pi
is dihedral. Moreover,
\text{Ad}(\pi)
is self-dual.

(2)

\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi)\in\mathcal{A}(4,F)
.
\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi)

\eta

(3)

\text{Sym}^{4}(\pi)\in\mathcal{A}(5,F)
.
\text{Sym}^{4}(\pi)
is not cuspidal if and only if
\pi
is of solvable polyhedral type. Moreover,

(a)

When
\pi
is of tetrahedral type, write
\text{Ad}(\pi)\cong\text{Ad}(\pi)\otimes\eta

\eta
on
F
. In this case we have

A^{4}(\pi)=\text{Ad}(\pi)\boxplus\eta\boxplus\eta^{2}.

(b)

When
\pi

\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi)\cong\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi)\otimes\eta
for some quadratic character
\eta
on
F
. Let
K/F

(4)

Assume

\pi
is non-dihedral regular algebraic. Then
\text{Sym}^{m-1}(\pi)\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(m,F)
for all
m\geq 2
.

Finally we review functoriality results of Rankin-Selberg products on
\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(2)
and on
\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(3)
. Below is a summary of
[
Ram00
, Theorem M]
,
[
KS02b
, Theorem A]
and
[
RW04
, Theorem 3.1]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(2,F)
. Let
\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(3,F)
. Then we have

(1)

\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}\in\mathcal{A}(4,F)
. Moreover,
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
is cuspidal if and only if one the following happens:

(a)

\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are both non-dihedral, and
\pi_{1}
is not twist-equivalent to
\pi_{2}
.

(b)

One of
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
, say
\pi_{1}
, is dihedral. Then
\pi_{1}=I_{K}^{F}(\chi_{1})
for a character
\chi_{1}
on some quadratic extension
K/F
. And the base change
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})
is cuspidal and not isomorphic to
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\otimes(\chi_{1}\circ\theta_{K/F})\chi_{1}^{-1}
.

(2)

\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi\in\mathcal{A}(6,F)
. Moreover,
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi
is not cuspidal if and only if one of the following happens:

(a)

\pi_{1}
is non-dihedral, and
\pi
is twist-equivalent to
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
.

(b)

\pi_{1}
is dihedral,
L(s,\pi)=L(s,\chi)
for a character
\chi
on a cubic, non-normal extension
K/F
, and the base change
B_{F}^{K}(\pi)
satisfies
L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi))=L(s,\psi)
for a character
\psi
on a quadratic extension
L/K
.

At the end of this section we prepare the following lemma for future reference:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(2,F)
be non-dihedral. Suppose that
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
and
\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})
are twist-equivalent. Then
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are also twist-equivalent.

Proof.

Suppose
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\cong\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})\otimes\eta
for some character
\eta
on
F
. Computing the central characters of both sides we have

1=\omega_{\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})}=\omega_{\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})\otimes\eta}=\omega_{\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})}\eta^{3}=\eta^{3}.

If
\eta=1
, then
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\cong\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})
, which would imply
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are twist-equivalent by the multiplicity one theorem
[
Ram00
, Theorem 4.1.2]
. If
\eta\neq 1
, then for each finite place
v
where
\pi_{1}
,
\pi_{2}
and
\eta
are unramified we have the following two sets of local parameters of
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
and
\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})\otimes\eta
at
v

\{\alpha_{1,v},1,\alpha_{1,v}^{-1}\}=\{\alpha_{2,v}\eta_{v},\eta_{v},\alpha_{2,v}^{-1}\eta_{v}\}

are the same. Thus either
\eta_{v}=\alpha_{1,v}
or
\eta_{v}=\alpha_{1,v}^{-1}
. By symmetry we may assume that
\eta_{v}=\alpha_{1,v}
. Thus we have

\{\alpha_{1,v}\eta_{v},\eta_{v},\alpha_{1,v}^{-1}\eta_{v}\}=\{\alpha_{1,v},1,\alpha_{1,v}^{2}\}=\{\alpha_{1,v},1,\alpha_{1,v}^{-1}\},

which implies that

\text{Ad}(\pi_{1,v})\cong\text{Ad}(\pi_{1,v})\otimes\eta_{v}\cong\text{Ad}(\pi_{2,v})\otimes\eta_{v}.

Thus we have

\text{Ad}(\pi_{1,v})\cong\text{Ad}(\pi_{2,v})

for all but finitely many
v
. By
[
Ram00
, Theorem 4.1.2]
this would also imply that
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are twist equivalent.
∎

(1)

All the results stated above rely on the following lemma, which is a combination of
[

and

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\Pi\in\mathcal{A}(n,F)
. Write
\Pi=\boxplus_{j=1}^{J}e_{j}\pi_{j}
as an isobaric sum of cuspidal representations, where
\pi_{i}
is not isomorphic to
\pi_{j}
when
i\neq j
, and
e_{j}\geq 1
. Then there exists an effective constant
c=c(n,e_{j})
depending only on
n
and the
e_{j}
’s, such that
L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})
has at most
e=\sum_{j=1}^{J}e_{j}^{2}
many Landau-Siegel zeros relative to
c
. Moreover, if
L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})
decomposes as

L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})=L_{1}(s)^{a}\cdot L_{2}(s)^{b}\cdot L_{Res}(s),

where
a+b>e
,
L_{1}(s)
shares the same real zeros with
L_{2}(s)
, and
L_{Res}(s)
is analytic in some interval
(t,1)
,
t<1
, then
L_{1}(s)
has no Landau-Siegel zero.

At the end of this section let us prove the following simple lemma that will be used later:

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\pi_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(n,F)
and
\pi_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(m,F)
. Then
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
has no Landau-Siegel zero if one of the following happens:

(1)

\pi_{1}
is self-dual, and
\pi_{2}
is non-self-dual.

(2)

\pi_{1}
is monomial with
\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{1}\otimes\eta
for a non-trivial character
\eta
on
F
, and
\pi_{2}
is not isomorphic to
\pi_{2}\otimes\eta
.

Proof.

(1)

Since
\pi_{1}
is self-dual and
\pi_{2}
is not, no two of the three representations
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\overline{\pi_{2}}
are isomorphic. Set
\Pi=\pi_{1}\boxplus\pi_{2}\boxplus\overline{\pi_{2}}
. Note that
L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})
decomposes as

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})^{2}\cdot L(s,\pi_{1}\times\overline{\pi_{2}})^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{1})\cdot L(s,\pi_{2}\times\pi_{2})\cdot L(s,\overline{\pi_{2}}\times\overline{\pi_{2}})\cdot L(s,\pi_{2}\times\overline{\pi_{2}})^{2}.

Since
\pi_{1}
is self-dual,
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
and
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\overline{\pi_{2}})
have the same real zeros. Thus
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.7
.

(2)

Since
\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{1}\otimes\eta
and
\pi_{2}
is not isomorphic to
\pi_{2}\otimes\eta
, no two of
\overline{\pi_{1}},\pi_{2},\pi_{2}\otimes\eta
are isomorphic. Set
\Pi=\overline{\pi_{1}}\boxplus\pi_{2}\boxplus(\pi_{2}\otimes\eta)
. In view of the relation
\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{1}\otimes\eta
,
L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})
decomposes as

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})^{2}\cdot L(s,\overline{\pi_{1}}\times\overline{\pi_{2}})^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\pi_{1}\times\overline{\pi_{1}})\cdot L(s,\pi_{2}\times\overline{\pi_{2}})^{2}\cdot L(\pi_{2}\times\overline{\pi_{2}}\otimes\eta)\cdot L(s,\pi_{2}\times\overline{\pi_{2}}\otimes\eta^{-1}).

Thus
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.7
.

∎

3.
Proof of Theorem A

The goal of this section is to prove the following refined version of Theorem
A
:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(2,F)
with central characters
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
respectively. Then
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
has no Landau-Siegel zero except possibly in the following cases:

(1)

There exists a quadratic extension
K/F
such that
\pi_{i}=I_{K}^{F}(\chi_{i})
for some character
\chi_{i}
on
K
, for each
i=1,2,3
. In this case there are at most two Landau-Siegel zeros coming from

L(s,\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3})L(s,\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3})L(s,\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}^{\theta})L(s,\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}^{\theta}).

Here
\theta=\theta_{K/F}
is the non-trivial element in Gal
(K/F)
, and wet set
\chi_{i}^{\theta}:=\chi_{i}\circ\theta
. Moreover, there is at most one Landau-Siegel zero if
(\chi_{2}\chi_{3})^{\theta}\neq\chi_{2}\chi_{3}
.

(2)

There exists a quadratic extension
K/F
such that one of
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
, say
\pi_{1}
, is induced from some character
\chi_{1}
on
K
, and
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\otimes\chi_{1}
and
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})
are both cuspidal and dihedral, induced form characters
\xi_{2},\xi_{3}
on
L
respectively, where
L/K
is the quadratic extension associated with the quadratic character
\chi_{1}^{\theta}\chi_{1}^{-1}
on
K
. In this case there is at most one Landau-Siegel zero coming from

L(s,\xi_{3}\xi_{2})L(s,\xi_{3}\xi_{2}^{\theta_{L/K}}).

(3)

\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
are not of solvable polyhedral type, and are all twist-equivalent. Moreover, suppose
\pi_{2}\cong\pi_{1}\otimes\eta
and
\pi_{3}\cong\pi_{1}\otimes\mu
for characters
\eta
and
\mu
on
F
. Then there is no Landau-Siegel zero if the twisted symmetric fifth power
L
-function

L(s,\pi_{1};\text{Sym}^{5}\otimes\omega_{1}^{-1}\eta\mu)

has no pole in some interval
(t,1)
,
t<1
.

Remark 3.1
.

The condition in Theorem
3.1
(3) is satisfied if
F
is totally real, and
\pi_{1}
is regular algebraic by Lemma
2.2
(4). Thus there is no Landau-Siegel zero in this case.

3.1.
General Type

Assume
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3})
is of general type. Recall that this means
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
are all non-dihedral, and
\pi_{i}
is not twist-equivalent to
\pi_{j}
for all
i\neq j
. We set

\Pi:=(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\boxplus\overline{\pi_{3}}\boxplus(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{3}}).

Now since
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are non-dihedral and are not twist-equivalent,
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
is cuspidal by Lemma
2.3
(1). We then claim that
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{3}}
is also cuspidal. Suppose not, then by Lemma
2.3
(2),
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
is twist-equivalent to
\text{Ad}(\overline{\pi_{3}})=\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})
. By Lemma
2.4
this would imply that
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are twist-equivalent, a contradiction!

Now the three representations
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2},\overline{\pi_{3}}
and
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{3}}
are pairwise non-isomorphic, since they have different degrees. We have

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})\cdot L(s,\overline{\pi_{1}}\times\overline{\pi_{2}}\times\overline{\pi_{3}})

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\times(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{3}))\cdot L(s,(\overline{\pi_{1}}\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{2}})\times(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{3}}))

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\overline{\pi_{3}}\times(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{3}))^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\times(\overline{\pi_{1}}\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{2}}))\cdot L(s,\pi_{3}\times\overline{\pi_{3}})\cdot L(s,(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{3})\times(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{3}})).

Note that we can further decompose
L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\times(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{3}))
as

\displaystyle L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\times(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{3}))

\displaystyle=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))\times(\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi_{3}))

\displaystyle=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxplus A^{3}(\pi_{1}))\times(\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi_{3}))

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})\cdot L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\times(\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi_{3})).

Thus
L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})
is equal to

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})^{2}\cdot L(s,\overline{\pi_{1}}\times\overline{\pi_{2}}\times\overline{\pi_{3}})^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\times(\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi_{3}))\cdot L(s,\overline{A^{3}(\pi_{1})}\times(\overline{\pi_{2}}\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{3}}))

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\overline{\pi_{3}}\times(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{3}))^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\times(\overline{\pi_{1}}\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{2}}))\cdot L(s,\pi_{3}\times\overline{\pi_{3}})\cdot L(s,(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{3})\times(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{3}})).

By Lemma
2.7
,
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
has no Landau-Siegel zero.

3.2.
Twist-equivalent Type

Assume
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3})
is of twist-equivalent type. Recall that this means
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
are all non-dihedral, but
\pi_{i}
is twist-equivalent to
\pi_{j}
for some
i\neq j
. Without loss of generality let us assume that
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are twist-equivalent. Write
\pi_{2}\cong\pi_{1}\otimes\eta
for some character
\eta
on
F
. Then
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
decomposes as

\displaystyle L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times(\pi_{1}\otimes\eta)\times\pi_{3})

\displaystyle=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{1})\times\pi_{3}\otimes\eta)

\displaystyle=L(s,(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\omega_{1}\boxplus\omega_{1})\times\pi_{3}\otimes\eta)

\displaystyle=L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\pi_{3}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta)\cdot L(s,\pi_{3}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta).

Now
L(s,\pi_{3}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta)
has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.5
(3). Moreover, by Lemma
2.6
(2),
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\pi_{3}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta)
can only possibly have Landau-Siegel zeros when
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
is twist-equivalent to
\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})
, which would imply that
\pi_{1}
is twist-equivalent to
\pi_{3}
by Lemma
2.4
. As a conclusion,
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
can only possibly have Landau-Siegel zeros if
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
are all twist-equivalent to each other.

From now on we may further assume
\pi_{3}\cong\pi_{1}\otimes\mu
for some character
\mu
on
F
, and study Landau-Siegel zeros of
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\pi_{3}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta)
. In this case we have

\displaystyle L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\pi_{3}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta)

\displaystyle=L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times(\pi_{1}\otimes\mu)\otimes\omega_{1}\eta)

\displaystyle=L(s,(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{1})\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu)

\displaystyle=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxplus A^{3}(\pi_{1}))\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu)

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu)\cdot L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu).

Now
L(s,\pi_{1}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu)
has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.5
(3). To further discuss the Landau-Siegel zeros of
L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu)
we consider the following cases:

(1)

Assume
\pi_{1}
is tetrahedral, i.e.,
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
is cuspidal and monomial. Write
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\cong\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\nu
for some cubic character
\nu
on
F
. Then by Lemma
2.2
(2),
A^{3}(\pi_{1})
is not cuspidal and decomposes as

A^{3}(\pi_{1})=(\pi_{1}\otimes\nu)\boxplus(\pi_{1}\otimes\nu^{2}).

Thus we have

L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu)=L(s,\pi_{1}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu\nu)\cdot L(s,\pi_{1}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu\nu^{2}),

which has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.5
(3).

(2)

Assume
\pi_{1}
is octahedral, i.e.,
\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})
is cuspidal and monomial. Thus
A^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu=\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu
is also cuspidal and monomial. Therefore
L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu)
has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.5
(2).

(3)

Assume
\pi_{1}
is not of solvable polyhedral type. Then
\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})
is cuspidal, and so is
\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu
. We construct

\Pi:=1\boxplus\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxplus\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu.

Then we have

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})

\displaystyle=L(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu)\cdot L(s,\overline{\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu})

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times(\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu))\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times(\overline{\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu}))

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))^{2}\cdot\zeta_{F}(s)\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,(\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu)\times(\overline{\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu})).

Note that
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times(\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu))
decomposes as

\displaystyle L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times(\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu))

\displaystyle=L(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu)

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\pi_{1}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu)\cdot L(s,\pi_{1};\text{Sym}^{5}\otimes\omega_{1}^{-1}\eta\mu).

Thus
L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})
equals

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})

\displaystyle=L(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu)^{2}\cdot L(s,\overline{\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu})^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\pi_{1}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu)\cdot L(s,\overline{\pi_{1}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\mu})

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\pi_{1};\text{Sym}^{5}\otimes\omega_{1}^{-1}\eta\mu)\cdot L(s,\overline{\pi_{1}};\text{Sym}^{5}\otimes\omega_{1}\eta^{-1}\mu^{-1})

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))^{2}\cdot\zeta_{F}(s)\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,(\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu)\times(\overline{\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu})).

By Lemma
2.7
,
L(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta\mu)
has no Landau-Siegel zero if
L(s,\pi_{1};\text{Sym}^{5}\otimes\omega_{1}^{-1}\eta\mu)
is analytic in some interval
(t,1)
,
t<1
. This proves Theorem
3.1
(3).

3.3.
Dihedral Type

Assume
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3})
is of dihedral type. Recall this means at least one of
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
, say
\pi_{1}
, is dihedral. Write
\pi_{1}=I_{K}^{F}(\chi_{1})
for a character
\chi_{1}
on some quadratic extension
K/F
. Then we have

\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}=I_{K}^{F}(\chi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{2}=I_{K}^{F}(B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\otimes\chi_{1}).

By Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})

\displaystyle=L(s,I_{K}^{F}(B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\otimes\chi_{1})\times\pi_{3})

\displaystyle=L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\times B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})\otimes\chi_{1}).

We discuss Landau-Siegel zeros of the
\text{GL}(2)\times\text{GL}(2)
Rankin-Selberg
L
-function
L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\times B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})\otimes\chi_{1})
by the cuspidality of
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})
and
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})
.

(1)

Assume exactly one of
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})
and
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})
is cuspidal, say
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})
is cuspidal, but
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})
is not. By Lemma
2.1
we know that
\pi_{3}=I_{K}^{F}(\chi_{3})
for some character
\chi_{3}
on
K
. In this case we have

\displaystyle L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})

\displaystyle=L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\times B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})\otimes\chi_{1})

\displaystyle=L(s,(B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\otimes\chi_{1})\times(\chi_{3}\boxplus\chi_{3}^{\theta}))

\displaystyle=L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\otimes\chi_{1}\chi_{3})\cdot L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\otimes\chi_{1}\chi_{3}^{\theta}),

which has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.5
(3).

(2)

Assume that both
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})
and
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})
are cuspidal. Let
\eta=\eta_{K/F}
be the quadratic character on
F
associated with
K/F
. Then by Lemma
2.1
we know that
\pi_{2}
is not isomorphic to
\pi_{2}\otimes\eta
, and
\pi_{3}
is not isomorphic to
\pi_{3}\otimes\eta
.

(a)

Assume further that at least one of
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{3}
and
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
, say
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{3}
, is cuspidal. Then we have

(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{3})\otimes\eta=(\pi_{1}\otimes\eta)\boxtimes\pi_{3}=\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{3}.

Since
\pi_{2}
is not isomorphic to
\pi_{2}\otimes\eta
, the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function

L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{3})\times\pi_{2})=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})

has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.8
(2).

(b)

Assume otherwise that neither
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{3}
nor
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
is cuspidal. Then by Lemma
2.3
(1), we have
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\cong B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\otimes\chi_{1}^{\theta}\chi_{1}^{-1}
and
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})\cong B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})\otimes\chi_{1}^{\theta}\chi_{1}^{-1}
. Let
L/K
be the quadratic extension associated with the quadratic character
\chi_{1}^{\theta}\chi_{1}^{-1}
on
K
. Then
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\otimes\chi_{1}=I_{L}^{K}(\xi_{2})
and
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})=I_{L}^{K}(\xi_{3})
for characters
\xi_{2},\xi_{3}
on
L
. In this case we have

\displaystyle L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})

\displaystyle=L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\times B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})\otimes\chi_{1})

\displaystyle=L(s,I_{L}^{K}(\xi_{2})\times I_{L}^{K}(\xi_{3}))

\displaystyle=L(s,\xi_{3}\xi_{2})\cdot L(s,\xi_{3}\xi_{2}^{\theta_{L/K}}).

Thus
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
has no Landau-Siegel zero unless
(\xi_{3}\xi_{2})^{2}=1
or
(\xi_{3}\xi_{2}^{\theta_{L/K}})^{2}=1
. Moreover, there is at most one Landau-Siegel zero, by the same argument as in the proof of
[
RW03
, Theorem A]
. This proves Theorem
3.1
(2).

(3)

Assume that neither
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})
nor
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})
is cuspidal. Then
\pi_{2}=I_{K}^{F}(\chi_{2})
and
\pi_{3}=I_{K}^{F}(\chi_{3})
for characters
\chi_{2},\chi_{3}
on
F
. By Lemma
2.1
we have
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})=\chi_{2}\boxplus\chi_{2}^{\theta}
and
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{3})=\chi_{3}\boxplus\chi_{3}^{\theta}
. In this case we have

\displaystyle L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})

\displaystyle=L(s,(\chi_{2}\boxplus\chi_{2}^{\theta})\times(\chi_{3}\boxplus\chi_{3}^{\theta})\otimes\chi_{1})

\displaystyle=L(s,\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3})\cdot L(s,\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}^{\theta})\cdot L(s,\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3})\cdot L(s,\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}^{\theta}).

A priori there are at most four Landau-Siegel zeros, if the four characters
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3},\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}^{\theta},\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}
and
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}^{\theta}
are all real. However, we can generalize the argument in
[
RW03
, Section 4]
to show that there are at most two (and in most cases, even at most one) Landau-Siegel zeros. We discuss the following cases depending on the number of real characters among
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3},\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}^{\theta},\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}
and
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}^{\theta}
.

(a)

Assume there is at most one real character. Then
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
has at most one Landau-Siegel zero, since
L
-functions of complex characters have no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.5
(1).

(b)

Assume there are exactly two real characters, say
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}
and
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}^{\theta}
are real. Then one can show that
L(s,\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3})\cdot L(s,\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}^{\theta})
has at most one Landau-Siegel zero using the same argument as in
[
RW03
, Section 4]
.

(c)

We claim that there cannot be exactly three real characters. This is by noting that if three of them, say
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3},\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}^{\theta}
and
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}
, are real, then the fourth character

\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}^{\theta}=\frac{\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}^{\theta}\cdot\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}}{\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}}

is also real.

(d)

Assume that all four characters are real. Then we further discuss by counting the number of trivial characters.

(i)

Assume further that there is at least one trivial character, say
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}=1
. We set
\mu:=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}^{\theta}
and
\nu:=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}
. Then

\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}^{\theta}=\frac{\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}^{\theta}\cdot\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}}{\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}}=\mu\nu.

In this case we have

L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})=\zeta_{K}(s)L(s,\mu)L(s,\nu)L(s,\mu\nu).

Note that since
\chi_{2}\neq\chi_{2}^{\theta}
and
\chi_{3}\neq\chi_{3}^{\theta}
,
\mu
and
\nu
are both non-trivial quadratic. Thus
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
is a Dirichlet series with non-negative coefficients, that has a pole of order
2
or
1
at
s=1
, depending on whether
\mu\nu=1
or not (equivalently, whether
(\chi_{2}\chi_{3})^{\theta}=\chi_{2}\chi_{3}
or not). Thus
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
has at most two Landau-Siegel zeros (or at most one Landau-Siegel zero, if
(\chi_{2}\chi_{3})^{\theta}\neq\chi_{2}\chi_{3}
).

(ii)

Assume otherwise that all four characters are non-trivial quadratic. We set
\alpha:=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}
,
\beta:=\chi_{3}^{\theta}/\chi_{3}
and
\gamma:=\chi_{2}^{\theta}/\chi_{2}
. Note that by our assumption
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are all non-trivial quadratic. We have the following relations:

\displaystyle\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\alpha\beta,

\displaystyle\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}

\displaystyle=\alpha\gamma,

\displaystyle\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta}\chi_{3}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\alpha\beta\gamma.

In this case we have

L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})=L(s,\alpha)L(s,\alpha\beta)L(s,\alpha\gamma)L(s,\alpha\beta\gamma).

Consider the auxiliary
L
-function

L(s):=\zeta_{K}(s)L(s,\alpha)L(s,\beta)L(s,\gamma)L(s,\alpha\beta)L(s,\alpha\gamma)L(s,\beta\gamma)L(s,\alpha\beta\gamma),

which is a Dirichlet series with non-negative coefficients, that has a pole of order
2
or
1
at
s=1
, depending on whether
\beta\gamma=1
or not (equivalently, whether
(\chi_{2}\chi_{3})^{\theta}=\chi_{2}\chi_{3}
or not). Thus
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi_{3})
, as part of
L(s)
, has at most two Landau-Siegel zeros (or at most one Landau-Siegel zero, if
(\chi_{2}\chi_{3})^{\theta}\neq\chi_{2}\chi_{3}
). We have thus finished the proof of Theorem
3.1
(1).

4.
Proof of Theorem B

The goal of this section is to prove the following refined version of Theorem
B
:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(2,F)
and
\pi\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(3,F)
with central characters
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
and
\omega
respectively. Then
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)
has no Landau-Siegel zero except possibly in the following cases:

(1)

\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are non-dihedral twist-equivalent, and
\pi
is twist-equivalent to
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
. Moreover, write
\pi_{2}\cong\pi_{1}\otimes\eta
and
\pi\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\cong\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu
for character
\eta
and
\mu
on
F
. Then there is at most one Landau-Siegel zero coming form
L(s,\mu)
, which can only occur if
\mu^{2}=1
.

(2)

\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are not of solvable polyhedral type, and are not twist-equivalent, but
\pi
is twist-equivalent to either
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
or
\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})
, say
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
. Moreover, write
\pi\cong\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta
for some character
\eta
on
F
. Then there is no Landau-Siegel zero if
A^{3}(\pi_{2}\otimes\eta)
is not isomorphic to
\overline{A^{3}(\pi_{1})}
.

Remark 4.1
.

The condition in Theorem
4.1
(2) is satisfied if
F
is totally real, and
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are regular algebraic by
[
Tho25b
, Lemma 4.2]
. Thus there is no Landau-Siegel zero in this case.

4.1.
General Type

Assume
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi)
is of general type. Recall that this means
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are non-dihedral, not twist-equivalent, and
\pi
is not twist-equivalent to either
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
or
\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})
. We set

\Pi:=(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi)\boxplus\overline{\pi_{2}}\boxplus(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{2}}).

Now since
\pi_{1}
is non-dihedral and
\pi
is not twist-equivalent to
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
, by Lemma
2.3
(2), we know that
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi
is cuspidal. Moreover, since
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are not twist-equivalent, by Lemma
2.4
we know that
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
and
\text{Ad}(\overline{\pi_{2}})=\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})
are not twist-equivalent either. Therefore
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{2}}
is also cuspidal. We then claim that
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi
and
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{2}}
are not isomorphic. To do this we compute the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function
L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi)\times\overline{(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{2}})})
:

\displaystyle L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi)\times\overline{(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{2}})})

\displaystyle=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi)\times(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{2}))

\displaystyle=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))\times(\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi))

\displaystyle=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxplus A^{3}(\pi_{1}))\times(\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi))

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times(\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi))\cdot L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\times(\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi)).

Now since
\pi_{2}
is non-dihedral and
\pi
is not twist-equivalent to
\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})
, by Lemma
2.3
(2),
\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi
is cuspidal of degree
6
, which is not isomorphic to either
\overline{\pi_{1}}
or any isobaric constitute of
\overline{A^{3}(\pi_{1})}
. Therefore both
L(s,\pi_{1}\times(\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi))
and
L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\times(\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi))
are entire. Thus
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi
is not isomorphic to
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{2}}
. In view of the decomposition above we have

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)^{2}\cdot L(s,\overline{\pi_{1}}\times\overline{\pi_{2}}\times\overline{\pi})^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\times(\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi))\cdot L(s,\overline{A^{3}(\pi_{1})}\times(\overline{\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi}))

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\pi_{2}\times(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\overline{\pi_{2}}))^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi)\times(\overline{\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi}))\cdot L(s,\pi_{2}\times\overline{\pi_{2}})\cdot L(s,(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{2})\times(\overline{\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{2}})).

By Lemma
2.7
,
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)
has no Landau-Siegel zero.

4.2.
Twist-equivalent Type

Assume
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi)
is of twist-equivalent type. Recall that this means
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are both non-dihedral, but either (1)
\pi_{1}
is twist-equivalent to
\pi_{2}
, or (2)
\pi
is twist-equivalent to
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
or
\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})
. Thus we have two cases to consider:

(1)

Assume that
\pi_{1}
is twist-equivalent to
\pi_{2}
. Then
\pi_{2}\cong\pi_{1}\otimes\eta
for some character
\eta
on
F
. In this case
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)
decomposes as

\displaystyle L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times(\pi_{1}\otimes\eta)\times\pi)

\displaystyle=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{1})\times\pi\otimes\eta)

\displaystyle=L(s,(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\omega_{1}\boxplus\omega_{1})\times\pi\otimes\eta)

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi\otimes\omega_{1}\eta)\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times(\pi\otimes\omega_{1}\eta)).

Now
L(s,\pi\otimes\omega_{1}\eta)
has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.5
(3). If
\pi\otimes\omega_{1}\eta
is non-self-dual, then
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times(\pi\otimes\omega_{1}\eta))
has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.8
(1). Therefore
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)
has no Landau-Siegel zero in this case. If
\pi\otimes\omega_{1}\eta
is self-dual, then by
[
Ram14
, Theorem A]
we have
\pi\otimes\omega_{1}\eta\cong\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu
for some non-dihedral
\pi_{3}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}(2,F)
and some character
\mu
on
F
with
\mu^{2}=1
. We are thus reduced to study Landau-Siegel zeros of the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function

L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu),

which has been studied in
[
Tho25a
]
. We will briefly record the proof here for completeness.

(a)

Assume further that
\pi_{1}
is not twist-equivalent to
\pi_{3}
. Consider the following auxiliary
L
-function

\displaystyle L(s)

\displaystyle:=L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)^{8}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)^{4}\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)^{4}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times A^{4}(\pi_{3}))^{2}\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3}))^{2}\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))^{4}\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3}))

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))\times(\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{3}))).

Let us write

\displaystyle L(s)

\displaystyle=\prod_{v}\prod_{j=1}^{d_{L}}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{L,j}(v)}{q_{v}^{s}}\right)^{-1},

\displaystyle-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{L}(v^{\ell})}{q_{v}^{\ell s}}\cdot\log(q_{v}),

where
d_{L}
is the degree of
L(s)
,
v
runs through finite places of
F
, and
q_{v}=\#F_{v}/\mathcal{O}_{v}
is the order of the residue field at
v
. The following two facts can be verified for
L(s)
:

(i)

For each finite place
v
where
\pi_{1},\pi_{3}
and
\mu
are unramified, we have

a_{L}(v^{\ell})=|2a_{\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})}(v^{\ell})+a_{\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu}(v^{\ell})+a_{\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu}(v^{\ell})|^{2}\geq 0.

(ii)

For each finite place
v
, we have
\alpha_{L,j}(v)\ll q_{v}^{\frac{8}{9}}
.

The fact (i) is a direct computation and can be found in
[
Tho25a
, Lemma 3.2]
. The fact (ii) is due to the bound
|\alpha_{\pi_{1},j}(v)|,|\alpha_{\pi_{3},j}(v)|\leq q_{v}^{\frac{1}{9}}

[
KS02a
, Proposition 4.8]
. Thus by
[
IK04
, Lemma 5.9]
,
L(s)
has at most
r
Landau-Siegel zeros, where
r
is the order of pole of
L(s)
at
s=1
. Let us proceed to count the order of pole of
L(s)
at
s=1
, and compare it to the multiplicity of the factor
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)
. To do this we first apply the decomposition

\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})=A^{4}(\pi_{1})\boxplus\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxplus 1

to further write
L(s)
as

\displaystyle L(s)

\displaystyle:=L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)^{8}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)^{4}\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)^{4}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times A^{4}(\pi_{3}))^{3}\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3}))^{3}\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))^{3}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times A^{4}(\pi_{3}))\cdot L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3}))\cdot L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1}))

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{3}))\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{3}))

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))^{4}\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3}))\cdot\zeta_{F}(s).

Note that the last line of the expression above contributes to
L(s)
a pole at
s=1
of order 6. The only other factors that could possibly contribute to
L(s)
a pole at
s=1
are
L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times A^{4}(\pi_{3})),L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times A^{4}(\pi_{3}))^{3},L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3}))
and
L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)^{4}
. However, in view of Lemma
2.2
(3), Lemma
2.4
and the assumption that
\pi_{1}
is not twist-equivalent to
\pi_{3}
,
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times A^{4}(\pi_{3}))^{3},L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3}))
and
L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)^{4}
are entire. Thus the only factor that my contribute to
L(s)
additional poles at
s=1
is
L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times A^{4}(\pi_{3}))
.

(i)

Assume that
\pi_{1}
is not of solvable polyhedral type. Then
A^{4}(\pi_{1})
is cuspidal. In this case the order of pole of
L(s)
at
s=1
is 7 or 6, depending on whether
A^{4}(\pi_{1})\cong A^{4}(\pi_{3})
or not. In either case
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)
has no Landau-Siegel zero, since any Landau-Siegel zero of it would be a Landau-Siegel zero of order at least 8 of
L(s).

(ii)

Assume that
\pi_{1}
is of octahedral type. Then by Lemma
2.2
(3),
A^{4}(\pi_{1})
decomposes as

A^{4}(\pi_{1})=(Ad(\pi_{1})\otimes\nu)\boxplus I_{K}^{F}(\chi^{-1})

for some quadratic character
\nu
on
F
and some character
\chi
on a quadratic extension
K/F
. In this case
L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times A^{4}(\pi_{3}))
has at most 1 simple pole at
s=1
, which only occurs if
\pi_{3}
is also octahedral. Thus
r\leq 7<8
and
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)
has no Landau-Siegel zero.

(iii)

Assume that
\pi_{1}
is of tetrahedral type. Then by Lemma
2.2
(3),
A^{4}(\pi_{1})
decomposes as

A^{4}(\pi_{1})=\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxplus\nu\boxplus\nu^{2}

for some cubic character
\nu
on
F
. In this case
L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times A^{4}(\pi_{3}))
can only have a pole at
s=1
if
\pi_{3}
is also tetrahedral, and the order of the pole is at most 4. We then make use of the decomposition

\displaystyle L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)

\displaystyle=L(s,(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxplus\nu\boxplus\nu^{2})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)

\displaystyle=L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu\nu)\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu\nu^{2})

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)

to see that any Landau-Siegel zero of
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)
would be a Landau-Siegel zero of
L(s)
of order at least 12. Since
r\leq 10<12
,
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)
has no Landau-Siegel zero.

(b)

Assume otherwise that
\pi_{1}
is twist-equivalent to
\pi_{3}
. In this case
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)
decomposes as

\displaystyle L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)

\displaystyle=L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)

\displaystyle=L(s,(A^{4}(\pi_{1})\boxplus\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxplus 1)\otimes\mu)

\displaystyle=L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)\cdot L(s,\mu).

Now
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)
has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.5
(3). We then prove that

L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)
has no Landau-Siegel zero.

(i)

Assume that
\pi_{1}
is of tetrahedral type. Then by Lemma
2.2
(3),
L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)
decomposes as

\displaystyle L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)

\displaystyle=L(s,(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\boxplus\nu\boxplus\nu^{2})\otimes\mu)

\displaystyle=L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)\cdot L(s,\mu\nu)\cdot L(s,\mu\nu^{2})

for some cubic character
\nu
on
F
. Now
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)
has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.5
(3). Moreover,
L(s,\mu\nu)
and
L(s,\mu\nu^{2})
have no Landau-Siegel zero since
(\mu\nu)^{2}=\nu^{2}\neq 1
and
(\mu\nu^{2})^{2}=\nu\neq 1
. Therefore
L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)
has no Landau-Siegel zero.

(ii)

Assume that
\pi_{1}
is of octahedral type. Then by Lemma
2.2
(3),
L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)
decomposes as

\displaystyle L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)

\displaystyle=L(s,((\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\nu)\boxplus I_{K}^{F}(\chi^{-1}))\otimes\mu)

\displaystyle=L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu\nu)\cdot L(s,I_{K}^{F}(\chi^{-1})\otimes\mu)

for some quadratic character
\nu
on
F
and some character
\chi
on a quadratic extension
K/F
, which has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.5
(3).

(iii)

Assume that
\pi_{1}
is not of solvable polyhedral type. Then
A^{4}(\pi_{1})
is cuspidal. We set

\Pi:=1\boxplus(A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)\boxplus\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})

and compute

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})

\displaystyle=L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,(A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))^{2}\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot\zeta_{F}(s)\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))\cdot L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times A^{4}(\pi_{1}))

Note that
L(s,(A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))
decomposes as

\displaystyle L(s,(A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))

\displaystyle=L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\pi_{1};\text{Sym}^{6}\otimes\omega_{1}^{-3}\mu)\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)

\displaystyle=L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)\cdot L(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1});\text{Sym}^{2}\otimes\omega_{1}^{-3}\mu)

Thus
L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})
can be further written as

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})

\displaystyle=L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)^{4}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1});\text{Sym}^{2}\otimes\omega_{1}^{-3}\mu)^{2}\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot\zeta_{F}(s)\cdot L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))\cdot L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\times A^{4}(\pi_{1})).

Let
S
be the set of finite places of
F
where
\pi_{1}
or
\mu
is ramified. By
[
Tak14
, Corollary 5.8]
, the partial
L
-function
L^{S}(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1});\text{Sym}^{2}\otimes\omega_{1}^{-3}\mu)
is holomorphic in
(7/8,1)
. Moreover, the finite product of local
L
-functions

\prod_{v\in S}L(s,\text{Sym}^{3}(\pi_{1,v});\text{Sym}^{2}\otimes\omega_{1,v}1^{-3}\mu_{v})=\prod_{v\in S}\frac{L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1,v})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1,v})\otimes\mu_{v})}{L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1,v})\otimes\mu_{v})}

is holomorphic in
(56/65,1)
by
[
LRS99
, Theorem 2]
.
Thus by Lemma
2.7
,
L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)
has no Landau-Siegel zero.

We have thus eliminated Landau-Siegel zeros of
L(s,A^{4}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu)
in all cases. Therefore the only possible Landau-Siegel zero of
L(s,\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{3})\otimes\mu)
comes from
L(s,\mu)
. This proves Theorem
4.1
(1).

(2)

Assume
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are not twist-equivalent, and that
\pi
is twist-equivalent to
\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
or
\text{Ad}(\pi_{2})
, say
\pi\cong\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta
for some character
\eta
on
F
. In this case
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)
decomposes as

\displaystyle L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times(\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})\otimes\eta))

\displaystyle=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))\times\pi_{2}\otimes\eta)

\displaystyle=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxplus A^{3}(\pi_{1}))\times\pi_{2}\otimes\eta)

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\otimes\eta)\cdot L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\times\pi_{2}\otimes\eta).

Now since
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are non-dihedral and are not twist-equivalent,
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\otimes\eta)
has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.6
(1). We then discuss Landau-Siegel zeros of
L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\times\pi_{2}\otimes\eta)
:

(a)

Assume
\pi_{1}
is tetrahedral. Then by Lemma
2.2
(2),
A^{3}(\pi_{1})
decomposes as

A^{3}(\pi_{1})=(\pi_{1}\otimes\nu)\boxplus(\pi_{1}\otimes\nu^{2})

for some cubic character
\nu
on
F
. Thus we have

L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\times\pi_{2}\otimes\eta)=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\otimes\eta\nu)\cdot L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\otimes\eta\nu^{2})

which has no Landau-Siegel by Lemma
2.6
(1).

(b)

Assume
\pi_{1}
is octahedral, i.e.,
A^{3}(\pi_{1})
is cuspidal and monomial. Write
A^{3}(\pi_{1})\cong A^{3}(\pi_{1})\otimes\mu
for some quadratic character
\mu
on
F
. Since
\pi_{2}
is non-dihedral,
\pi_{2}\otimes\eta
is not isomorphic to
(\pi_{2}\otimes\eta)\otimes\mu
. Therefore
L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\times\pi_{2}\otimes\eta)
has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.8
(2).

(c)

Assume
\pi_{1}
is not of solvable polyhedral type. In this case we go back to the triple product
L
-function

L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}^{\prime}\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})).

Here we write
\pi_{2}^{\prime}=\pi_{2}\otimes\eta
. We set

\Pi:=(\pi_{2}^{\prime}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))\boxplus\overline{\pi_{1}}\boxplus(\overline{\pi_{1}}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{2}^{\prime})).

To test whether
\pi_{2}^{\prime}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})
and
\overline{\pi_{1}}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{2}^{\prime})
are isomorphic we compute

\displaystyle L(s,(\pi_{2}^{\prime}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))\times(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{2}^{\prime})))

\displaystyle=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))\times(\pi_{2}^{\prime}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{2}^{\prime})))

\displaystyle=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxplus A^{3}(\pi_{1}))\times(\pi_{2}^{\prime}\boxplus A^{3}(\pi_{2}^{\prime})))

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}^{\prime})\cdot L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\times A^{3}(\pi_{2}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\pi_{1}\times A^{3}(\pi_{2}^{\prime}))\cdot L(s,\pi_{2}^{\prime}\times A^{3}(\pi_{1})),

which can only have a simple pole at
s=1
if
A^{3}(\pi_{2}^{\prime})\cong\overline{A^{3}(\pi_{1})}
, which would imply that
\pi_{2}
is not of solvable polyhedral type. By the decomposition above we have

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi\times\overline{\Pi})

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}^{\prime}\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))^{2}\cdot L(s,\overline{\pi_{1}}\times\overline{\pi_{2}^{\prime}}\times\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\pi_{1}\times A^{3}(\pi_{2}^{\prime}))\cdot L(s,\overline{\pi_{1}}\times\overline{A^{3}(\pi_{2}^{\prime})})

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,A^{3}(\pi_{1})\times A^{3}(\pi_{2}^{\prime}))\cdot L(s,\overline{A^{3}(\pi_{1})}\times\overline{A^{3}(\pi_{2}^{\prime})})

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,\pi_{1}\times(\overline{\pi_{1}}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{2}^{\prime})))^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,(\pi_{2}^{\prime}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{1}))\times(\overline{\pi_{2}^{\prime}}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{1})))\cdot L(s,\pi_{1}\times\overline{\pi_{1}})

\displaystyle\cdot L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{2}^{\prime}))\times(\overline{\pi_{1}}\boxtimes\text{Ad}(\pi_{2}^{\prime}))).

Thus by Lemma
2.7
,
L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)
can only have Landau-Siegel zeros if
\pi_{2}
is not of solvable polyhedral type, and that

A^{3}(\pi_{2}\otimes\eta)\cong\overline{A^{3}(\pi_{1})}.

This proves Theorem
4.1
(2).

4.3.
Dihedral Type

Assume
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi)
is of dihedral type. Recall that this means
\pi_{1}
or
\pi_{2}
, say
\pi_{1}
, is dihedral. Write
\pi_{1}=I_{K}^{F}(\chi_{1})
for a character
\chi_{1}
on some quadratic extension
K/F
. Then we have

\displaystyle L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)

\displaystyle=L(s,(I_{K}^{F}(\chi_{1})\boxtimes\pi_{2})\times\pi)

\displaystyle=L(s,I_{K}^{F}(B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\otimes\chi_{1})\times\pi)

\displaystyle=L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\times B_{F}^{K}(\pi)\otimes\chi_{1}).

Let
\eta=\eta_{K/F}
be the quadratic character on
F
associated with
K/F
. Let
\theta=\theta_{K/F}
be the non-trivial element in Gal
(K/F)
. Then
\pi
is not isomorphic to
\pi\otimes\eta
. By Lemma
2.1
we know that
B_{F}^{K}(\pi)
is cuspidal.

(1)

Assume that
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})
is not cuspidal. Then
\pi_{2}=I_{K}^{F}(\chi_{2})
for some character
\chi_{2}
on
F
. In this case we have

\displaystyle L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)

\displaystyle=L(s,(\chi_{2}\boxplus\chi_{2}^{\theta})\times B_{F}^{K}(\pi)\otimes\chi_{1})

\displaystyle=L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi)\otimes\chi_{1}\chi_{2})\cdot L(s,B_{F}^{K}(\pi)\otimes\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{\theta})

which has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.5
(3).

(2)

Assume that
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})
is cuspidal, but is not isomorphic to
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\otimes\chi_{1}^{\theta}\chi_{1}^{-1}
. Then by Lemma
2.3
(1) we know that
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
is cuspidal. We write

L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)=L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\times\pi).

Note that
(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\otimes\eta=(\pi_{1}\otimes\eta)\boxtimes\pi_{2}=\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
, and
\pi
is not isomorphic to
\pi\otimes\eta
. By Lemma
2.8
(2),
L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\times\pi)
has no Landau-Siegel zero.

(3)

Assume that
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})
is cuspidal, and is isomorphic to
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})\otimes\chi_{1}^{\theta}\chi_{1}^{-1}
. Let
L/K
be the quadratic extension associated with
\chi_{1}^{\theta}\chi_{1}^{-1}
. Then
B_{F}^{K}(\pi_{2})=I_{L}^{K}(\xi_{2})
for some character
\xi_{2}
on
L
. In this case we have

\displaystyle L(s,\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\pi)

\displaystyle=L(s,I_{L}^{K}(\xi_{2})\times B_{F}^{K}(\pi)\otimes\chi_{1})

\displaystyle=L(s,B_{K}^{L}(B_{F}^{K}(\pi)\otimes\chi_{1})\otimes\xi_{2})

which has no Landau-Siegel zero by Lemma
2.5
(3).

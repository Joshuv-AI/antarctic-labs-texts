A time-dependent model for quantifying postoperative inflammatory trajectories and predicting survival in early triple-negative breast cancer
Authors: Guoquan Wen, Yi Zeng, Yu Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTriple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC) is characterized by high heterogeneity and therapeutic limitations, creating an urgent need for quantifiable and readily accessible prognostic markers to optimize risk stratification. Although the baseline values of inflammatory indices are associated with prognosis, the dynamic remodeling process of the immune-inflammatory response after surgery has not been sufficiently explored. This study innovatively introduces the linear slope to quantify the annual rate of change in postoperative inflammatory indices, aiming to construct a prognostic model that integrates dynamic trends.MethodsThis retrospective study included 296 patients with TNBC who underwent adjuvant chemotherapy following surgery. The primary endpoints of the study were disease-free survival (DFS) and breast cancer-specific survival (BCSS). Inflammatory values were log2-transformed, multicollinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models were utilized to assess disease-free survival and breast cancer-specific survival. These were integrated with time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, bootstrap internal evaluation, and SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) for interpreting the predictive model.ResultThe conventional Cox model demonstrated that T stage, N stage, histological grade, lymphovascular invasion(LVI), and perineural invasion(PNI) were independent prognostic factors for both DFS and BCSS (p < 0.05). The time-dependent model revealed that the dynamic changes in log2(SIRI) held significant prognostic value, which was reflected in the contribution value of SIRI in the SHAP. Time-dependent ROC curves showed that the model's predictive performance at 5 and 6 years was superior to that of the traditional model, although the calibration curve suggested the presence of overestimation. The decision curve analysis curve supported its clinical utility within the common threshold range of 0.1–0.3.ConclusionThe time-dependent model effectively quantified the dynamic evolution of postoperative inflammation. Introduction

As a highly aggressive subtype of breast cancer, Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC) is defined by the lack of expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2). Consequently, systemic therapeutic options are limited, and patients frequently experience an unfavorable prognosis characterized by early recurrence, a high incidence of distant metastasis, and shortened overall survival [1]. Despite receiving standard surgical intervention and chemotherapy, patients with early-stage TNBC exhibit a significantly lower 5-year disease-free survival rate compared to those with other breast cancer subtypes, underscoring the pronounced heterogeneity in outcomes within this population [2]. The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system, which relies on anatomical and histopathological criteria, continues to be the cornerstone for risk stratification. Nevertheless, its prognostic power for estimating individualized recurrence risk is considerably limited [3]. Thus, there is a pressing need to discover quantifiable and readily obtainable prognostic biomarkers that are intimately linked to tumor biology. Such biomarkers would refine the risk stratification framework for early-stage TNBC patients and offer evidence-based guidance for tailoring adjuvant therapy and surveillance strategies [4].

Accumulating evidence in recent years indicates that the systemic inflammatory response plays a pivotal role in tumorigenesis, progression, and immune evasion. As readily accessible markers of systemic inflammation, hematological indices such as the Neutrophil-to-Lymphocyte Ratio (NLR), Systemic Immune-inflammation Index (SII), and Systemic Inflammation Response Index (SIRI) offer the advantages of low cost, convenient detection, and strong reproducibility, and have consequently become an area of active investigation in the evaluation of solid tumors [5]. An elevation in these indices fundamentally signifies a reinforced pro-tumor microenvironment coupled with a compromised anti-tumor immune response [6]. Clinical investigations have established that elevated baseline levels of these inflammatory markers serve as an independent predictor of unfavorable prognosis in TNBC, demonstrating a significant correlation with reduced disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) [7].

Nevertheless, most existing research on inflammatory biomarkers is confined to static assessments at a single time point (such as at diagnosis or preoperatively), which fails to capture the biological implications of the dynamic immune-inflammatory remodeling that occurs following surgery [8]. The surgical procedure itself can trigger an acute inflammatory response and a state of immune suppression. Additionally, factors including adjuvant therapy, postoperative infections, and psychological distress may contribute to sustained fluctuations in the inflammatory milieu. More critically, the presence of residual tumor cells or the development of micrometastases could perpetuate a state of low-grade chronic inflammation [9]. Consequently, reliance on measurements from a single time point neglects the critical information embedded in the temporal trajectory of postoperative inflammatory status. While a limited number of studies have attempted to compare the prognostic value of inflammatory markers measured at various time points after surgery [10], a standardized approach to quantify the rate of postoperative inflammatory change is still lacking, and no prognostic model integrating this dynamic process for clinical application has been established.

This study aimed to introduce time-dependent model as a quantitative tool. It precisely characterizes the average annual rate of change in inflammatory marker levels following surgery in early-stage TNBC patients. We sought to construct a prognostic model with significant incremental value for the accurate risk assessment of long-term survival outcomes in this patient population.

Materials and methods

Study design and patients

Conducted as a retrospective cohort study using data extracted from a single-center electronic medical record system, this research strictly followed the STROBE reporting standards and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol received ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board of Huizhou Third People’s Hospital, including a waiver of informed consent. We retrospectively analyzed early-stage TNBC patients treated at Huizhou Third People’s Hospital from May 2015 to April 2025. Inclusion criteria comprised: (1) Pathologically confirmed primary invasive ductal carcinoma of the breast; (2) Triple-negative receptor status (ER-, PR-, HER2-); (3) Absence of prior breast cancer history. Exclusion criteria included: (1) Administration of neoadjuvant therapy; (2) Absence of surgical treatment; (3) Stage IV (metastatic) breast cancer; (4) Systemic autoimmune disorders (e.g., systemic lupus erythematosus, scleroderma); (5) Concomitant malignant tumors in other organs; (6) Any comorbidities known to affect NLR/SII/SIRI measurements, such as end-stage renal disease or bone marrow failure syndromes; (7)Positive margins after breast-conserving surgery(Supplementary Fig. 1). All enrolled patients underwent four cycles of intravenous epirubicin (95 mg/m2) combined with cyclophosphamide (600 mg/m2), repeated every 21 days. This was followed by four cycles of intravenous docetaxel (90 mg/m2), also administered at three-week intervals. Adjuvant radiotherapy was uniformly administered postoperatively to patients with lymph node involvement or those who underwent breast-conserving surgery. Through dual-source data extraction from the institutional electronic health records and pathology information systems, we retrieved the following variables: age at diagnosis, menopausal status, surgical procedure type, T stage, N stage, histological grade, presence of lymphovascular invasion (LVI), perineural invasion (PNI), ER status, PR status, HER2 status, Ki-67, neutrophil count (NEUT), lymphocyte count (LYMPH), platelet count (PLT), and monocyte count (MONO). Postoperative levels of NEUT, LYMPH, PLT, and MONO were reassessed at 12-month intervals. TNM staging was based on the 8th edition of the AJCC cancer staging manual [11].

All immunohistochemical (IHC) slides were independently reviewed in a double-blind manner by three specialized breast pathologists. HER2 status was evaluated via IHC staining per the 2018 American Society of Clinical Oncology/College of American Pathologists (ASCO/CAP) guidelines. A result was defined as positive if the score was 3 +, and negative otherwise. For cases with an IHC score of 2 +, fluorescence in situ hybridization was selectively conducted, with results interpreted against the cut-off values stipulated in the ASCO/CAP 2018 guidelines. A tumor was classified as TNBC if the pathology reports indicated ER < 1%, PR < 1%, and HER2-negative status [12]. In accordance with the Chinese CACA guidelines, a threshold of 20% was adopted for PR status classification. For Ki-67, the median value of 40% observed in this cohort was utilized as the cut-off [13]. A total of 296 patients were ultimately enrolled in this study.

Study endpoints and definitions

The primary endpoints of this study were disease-free survival (DFS) and breast cancer-specific survival (BCSS). DFS was defined as the duration from the date of surgery until the first occurrence of an event, encompassing: (1) locoregional or distant recurrence (radiologically or pathologically confirmed); (2) a new primary contralateral breast cancer; or (3) death from any cause. BCSS referred to the survival time from the initial diagnosis of breast cancer until death attributable specifically to breast cancer.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed with R software, version 4.5.2. The NLR, SII and SIRI underwent log2-transformation to achieve distributions closer to normality and to satisfy the assumption of homoscedasticity, and included them in the Cox model as time-dependent variables. Comparisons of categorical variables were carried out using the Chi-square test. Prior to building the multivariable Cox regression model, multicollinearity among independent variables was evaluated using the VIF. A VIF cutoff of > 5 was defined as evidence of significant multicollinearity. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models were utilized to determine the independent prognostic factors associated with DFS and BCSS. The models were subjected to internal evaluation using the bootstrap method involving 1000 resampling iterations. For the predictive assessment of each model, the area under the time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) was applied. Differences in the annual AUC values were tested using DeLong's test. We employed the SHAP approach to interpret the predictions from the Cox models. Rooted in game theory's Shapley value, the SHAP method allocates an importance score to each feature, which quantifies the magnitude and direction of its contribution to the individual predictions. Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided P-value of less than 0.05.

Results

A total of 296 patients with early-stage TNBC were enrolled. Of these, 76 patients (25.6%) were free from DFS events, and 67 patients (22.6%) experienced BCSS events. The median follow-up time for the patients was calculated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method and was 56 months. The majority of tumors were classified as T2 (60.8%) or N0 (55.0%). High histological grade (G3) was present in 67.2% of cases, and LVI was identified in 50.3% of patients. HER2-low was observed in 54.8% of the cohort, and a high Ki-67 index (≥ 40%) was found in 72.7% of tumors. Overall, the cohort demonstrated a trend towards younger age, high proliferative activity, and intermediate aggressiveness, aligning with the typical biological behavior of TNBC (Table 1). VIF was calculated to assess multicollinearity among the trajectories of NLR, SII, and SIRI. The results showed that despite having similar trajectories, the VIF values were below the critical threshold of 5.0. Consequently, no significant multicollinearity was detected among the inflammatory factors in this study, satisfying the requirements for constructing the multivariable regression model (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Conventional Cox proportional hazards model

Multivariable analysis was performed using a Cox proportional hazards model with variables extracted from the EMR. For the conventional Cox proportional hazards models, the PH assumption was evaluated using Schoenfeld residual plots. The global test and tests for individual covariates showed no significant deviations from proportionality over time (p > 0.05), supporting the appropriateness of the Cox model. Significant prognostic factors for DFS included T stage (p < 0.001; hazard ratio [HR] = 2.822, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.748–4.556), N stage (p = 0.006; H = 1.344, 95% CI: 1.086–1.664), histological grade (p = 0.005; HR = 1.752, 95% CI: 1.831–2.594), lymphovascular invasion (LVI) (p = 0.002; HR = 2.632, 95% CI: 1.412–4.907), and perineural invasion (PNI) (p = 0.003; HR = 2.059, 95% CI: 1.271–3.334) (Table 2). For BCSS, the significant prognostic factors were T stage (p < 0.001; HR = 3.740, 95% CI: 2.142–6.530), N stage (p = 0.003; HR = 1.406, 95% CI: 1.123–1.760), histological grade (p = 0.011; HR = 1.785, 95% CI: 1.140–2.796), LVI (p = 0.021; HR = 2.307, 95% CI: 1.137–4.678), and PNI (p = 0.010; HR = 1.971, 95% CI: 1.756–3.305). The likelihood ratio test for the overall model yielded a p-value < 0.001, indicating that the predictor variables in the model collectively demonstrate extremely strong explanatory power for the outcome (Table 3).

Time-dependent model

The prognostic analysis based on longitudinal information utilized a time-dependent Cox model. As shown in Table 4, significant prognostic factors for DFS were T stage (HR: 2.119, 95% CI: 1.680–2.719, p = 0.007), N stage (HR: 1.318, 95% CI: 1.101–1.565, p = 0.002), histological grade (HR: 1.503, 95% CI: 1.114–2.015, p = 0.007), LVI (HR: 2.179, 95% CI: 1.288–3.780, p = 0.004), PNI (HR: 1.721, 95% CI: 1.168–2.500, p = 0.006), and log2(SIRI) (HR: 1.390, 95% CI: 1.095–1.732, p < 0.001). For BCSS, the significant prognostic factors were T stage (HR: 1.859, 95% CI: 1.175–2.901, p = 0.008), N stage (HR: 1.382, 95% CI: 1.140–1.645, p < 0.001), histological grade (HR: 1.415, 95% CI: 1.032–1.942, p = 0.031), LVI (HR: 2.334, 95% CI: 1.288–4.239, p = 0.005), and log2(SIRI) (HR: 1.297, 95% CI: 1.028–1.604, p = 0.027) (Table 5). The likelihood ratio test for the overall model yielded a p-value < 0.001, indicating that the predictor variables in the model collectively demonstrate extremely strong explanatory power for the outcome.

SHAP summary plots of variables

To elucidate the influence of variables on the model's predictive accuracy, we utilized machine learning interpretation methods. The mean SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) value for each variable highlighted their substantial contribution to the predictive power of the model (Fig. 1). The SHAP summary plots showed that SIRI was a top-ranked feature in both the DFS and BCSS models, demonstrating its crucial role in predicting survival outcomes. This observation aligns with the significant association of SIRI identified in the Cox proportional hazards regression analysis. Each doubling of the SIRI value corresponded to an approximately 28–38% increase in the risk of recurrence. The SHAP analysis further substantiated the consistent and robust predictive power of SIRI in the model. The SIRI, calculated by integrating neutrophil, lymphocyte, and monocyte counts, serves as a marker of the systemic inflammatory state and possesses independent prognostic value in breast cancer outcomes. Established clinical factors, including T stage, nodal status, and LVI, were also identified as high-ranking features in the SHAP summary plots. This high ranking aligns with their significant prognostic roles identified in the multivariate Cox analysis.

Time-dependent ROC curve analysis

Time-dependent ROC curve analysis was performed to calculate the dynamic and cumulative AUC for each model. In the DFS analysis, the traditional Cox proportional hazards model yielded AUC of 0.811, 0.819, and 0.761 at 4, 5, and 6 years after the baseline date, respectively. In contrast, the time-dependent model achieved AUC of 0.840, 0.932, and 0.908 at the corresponding time points (Fig. 2A). Statistical comparison using Delong's test showed p-values of 0.598 at 4 years, 0.008 at 5 years, and 0.007 at 6 years. For BCSS analysis, the AUC of the conventional Cox model at 4, 5, and 6 years were 0.861, 0.849, and 0.779, respectively. The time-dependent model attained AUC of 0.889, 0.967, and 0.921, respectively (Fig. 2B). According to Delong's test, the differences in AUC between the models at 4, 5, and 6 years were associated with p-values of 0.685, 0.013, and 0.045. The AUC of the combined model was significantly higher than that of the conventional Cox model at both the 5-year and 6-year time points.

Calibration curves of the time-dependent model

Based on the calibration curves, the time-dependent model demonstrated a significant overestimation of its predictive accuracy for both DFS and BCSS. Specifically, the survival probabilities predicted by the model at 4, 5, and 6 years were consistently higher than the actual observed rates. Such miscalibration may result in a systematic underestimation of risk, thereby introducing potential bias into clinical decision-making (Fig. 3).

Calibration curves of the Time-dependent model

DCA curves of the time-dependent model

DCA revealed that the time-dependent models for both DFS and BCSS provided clinical net benefit across the 48-, 60-, and 72-month horizons. Over the threshold probability range of 0.1 to 0.3, the model's decision curves lay substantially above the "treat-all" and "treat-none" strategies, demonstrating its capacity to correctly identify low-risk individuals who could be spared from overtreatment. The model, however, had limitations. The absolute net benefit values were relatively modest (the BCSS model, in particular, reached a peak net benefit of only 0.15). Furthermore, the curves exhibited variations across the different time points, indicating possible inconsistencies in prediction reliability. Additionally, the clinical utility of the model decreased markedly at threshold probabilities greater than 0.3, which restricts its use for guiding decisions in high-risk scenarios where intervention thresholds are typically higher. In conclusion, the proposed time-dependent models demonstrate utility within the frequently encountered clinical threshold probability range of 0.1–0.3. To enhance their reliability and potential for broader clinical adoption, further validation in larger, independent cohorts is warranted (Fig. 4).

Discussion

The aggressive biological behavior and heterogeneous prognosis of TNBC remain central challenges in its clinical management [14]. Although the traditional AJCC staging-based risk stratification system provides a foundational framework, its precision for predicting recurrence and survival outcomes in early-stage TNBC remains limited [15], with notable shortcomings in capturing dynamic changes within the tumor microenvironment [16]. We employed a time-dependent Cox proportional hazards model to assess the annualized change rate of postoperative inflammatory markers. A novel prognostic model was developed by integrating both the baseline levels and their longitudinal trajectories. Our findings demonstrated that the dynamic evolution of the systemic inflammatory response serves as an independent prognostic factor, offering a quantifiable tool to inform clinical decision-making in real-world settings.

The clinicopathological profile of the study cohort is presented in Table 1. Patients with early-stage TNBC were characterized by a predominance of younger age (< 50 years, 53.7%), high proliferative index (Ki67 ≥ 40%, 72.6%), and a moderate rate of lymphovascular invasion (LVI positive, 50.3%), aligning with the well-established aggressive clinical phenotype of TNBC. This population profile supports the generalizability of our results. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression confirmed that established clinicopathological factors, namely T stage, N stage, and the presence of LVI, remained statistically significant independent predictors for both DFS and BCSS, with corresponding HR and p-values detailed in Tables 2 and 3. A pivotal finding of our research is the systematic quantification of the impact of the time-sequential changes in postoperative inflammatory markers on TNBC outcomes. Further analysis using the time-dependent model demonstrated that the dynamic variation in inflammatory activity, exemplified by the log2(SIRI), served as an independent prognostic factor for both DFS (HR = 1.378, p < 0.001) and BCSS (HR = 1.284, p = 0.027), as presented in Tables 4 and 5 This suggests that the dynamic progression of postoperative inflammatory profiles reflects real-time alterations within the tumor microenvironment, addressing the inherent shortcoming of evaluations conducted at a single snapshot in time. The SHAP analysis corroborated these results. Specifically, Fig. 1 shows that SIRI was a high-ranking feature in the predictive models for both DFS and BCSS. The wide spread of the SHAP values underscores the substantial contribution of SIRI to the models' predictive power. Such concordance indicates that the dynamic inflammation metrics exhibit both statistical significance and robust predictive utility in clinical modeling. Collectively, these observations underscore the biological relevance of the temporal patterns in postoperative inflammation. The temporal patterns of the inflammatory indices, as illustrated in Supplementary Fig. 2, indicate that the postoperative inflammatory dynamics mirror the shifts in the immune-inflammatory equilibrium. A decrease in inflammatory values potentially signifies the reestablishment of anti-tumor immune responses [17], while an increase points to the sustained activation of a pro-tumorigenic, chronic inflammatory milieu [18]. This interpretation aligns with the mechanism of immune evasion promoted by minimal residual disease or micrometastasis.

The primary innovation of this study, compared to prior research, is its move beyond the limitation of assessments conducted at a single time point. Although previous studies have established a correlation between elevated baseline inflammatory markers and an unfavorable prognosis in TNBC [19], these static measurements fail to capture the critical postoperative period of dynamic immune-inflammatory remodeling. Surgical trauma itself can trigger an acute inflammatory response and transient immunosuppression [20]. Subsequent treatments, such as radiotherapy and chemotherapy, can further cause fluctuations in these values. Crucially, a low-grade chronic inflammatory state, potentially driven by micrometastases, often only becomes apparent months after the initial surgery [21, 22]. The longitudinal changes in inflammatory markers reflect the real-time evolution of the host immune status throughout the treatment course. This dynamic assessment more accurately captures the kinetics of the inflammatory response, the immediate effects of therapeutic interventions, the evolution of the tumor microenvironment, and the underlying tumor biology, ultimately leading to more precise survival predictions [23]. Specifically, longitudinal measures overcome the limitations of static assessments by integrating the effects of ongoing therapeutic interventions. These findings underscore the clinical necessity of monitoring the dynamic changes in inflammatory status to enhance the accuracy of prognostic evaluation and to optimize treatment strategies [24]. By employing the linear slope derived from multiple time-point measurements, this study translated the rate of change into a actionable prognostic variable, thereby achieving a mathematical modeling of the dynamic trend. Furthermore, potential multicollinearity among variables was mitigated during model construction (all VIFs were well below the critical threshold of 5.0), ensuring the robustness of the findings.

Analysis using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curves showed that the time-dependent model had better predictive performance for long-term outcomes compared to the traditional Cox proportional hazards model. As presented in Fig. 2A and B, in the DFS analysis, the AUC values for the time-dependent model at 4, 5, and 6 years were 0.840, 0.932, and 0.908, respectively. These values were significantly superior to the corresponding AUC of the traditional model (0.811, 0.819, and 0.761), with the differences at 5 and 6 years being statistically significant per DeLong's test (p < 0.05). A similar advantage was evident for the BCSS model, where the time-dependent model yielded AUCs of 0.889, 0.967, and 0.921, compared to 0.861, 0.849, and 0.779 for the conventional model. Nevertheless, the calibration curves (Fig. 3) suggested a systematic overestimation of predictive performance by the time-dependent model, evidenced by the predicted survival probabilities at 4, 5, and 6 years exceeding the actual observed survival rates. Potential explanations for this include selection bias in the retrospective cohort or suboptimal calibration of the model specifically in high-risk patients. Despite this limitation, decision curve analysis (Fig. 4) confirmed the clinical utility of the model within the threshold probability range of 0.1 to 0.3, as it provided a higher net benefit than both the "treat-all" and "treat-none" approaches across this range.

This study demonstrates significant real-world value due to its high accessibility and favorable cost-effectiveness profile. These inflammatory markers, including NEUT, LYMPH, PLT and monocyte counts, are obtained from standard blood tests. Their low cost and high reproducibility render them particularly applicable in settings with limited resources. Within real-world clinical practice, the dynamic model is amenable to integration into existing postoperative follow-up protocols. This facilitates risk stratification based on straightforward biomarkers, which helps prevent both overtreatment and undertreatment [25]. However, the model's generalizability may be constrained by the specific characteristics of our cohort, which primarily consisted of younger patients (53.7% < 50 years), tumors with high proliferative activity (Ki67 ≥ 40% in 72.7%), and intermediate aggressiveness (LVI positivity in 50.3%). This specific profile may limit its applicability to other TNBC populations.

Our study is subject to certain limitations. Primarily, the retrospective nature of the study is susceptible to selection bias, and the use of data from a single institution may restrict the external validity of our results. Additionally, the measurement of inflammatory markers can be influenced by confounding variables (e.g., postoperative infection or concurrent comorbidities). Despite the exclusion of patients with autoimmune disorders, the possibility of residual confounding remains. Moreover, to preserve the analytical purity of the postoperative inflammatory kinetics, we excluded individuals who had undergone neoadjuvant therapy. This deliberate exclusion, however, constrains the applicability of our results to the cohort of patients presenting with locally advanced TNBC for whom neoadjuvant treatment is the standard of care. This exclusion was a methodological decision aimed at mitigating confounding. NAT is known to significantly shift the baseline systemic inflammatory state and remodel the tumor microenvironment, thereby introducing substantial noise into the analysis of the temporal patterns of postoperative inflammation. An elevation in SII and SIRI could indicate activation of the platelet-neutrophil axis, a process linked to pro-angiogenic and pre-metastatic microenvironment formation. Nonetheless, the involvement of specific mechanistic pathways, for instance, the IL-6/STAT3 signaling axis, warrants further investigation using correlative tissue analyses for confirmation [26]. A critical distinction to make is that our study established a prognostic model, not a predictive model in the rigorous sense. The core variable of the model, the change in inflammatory indicators, relies on longitudinal monitoring data collected after surgery. Consequently, this model is suitable for prognosticating long-term survival risk after obtaining the patient's postoperative inflammatory trajectory (i.e., in patients who have survived for more than 2 years postoperatively), but it cannot be used to forecast the future trend of inflammatory indicators prior to treatment initiation. We recognize that specialized joint longitudinal-survival models represent a more statistically robust approach for analyzing repeated measures data. Future research should aim to employ joint models in larger, prospective cohorts characterized by a higher frequency of longitudinal assessments. The calibration of our dynamic prediction model showed a tendency to underestimate the true risk. This miscalibration underscores the need for further model refinement and external validation before it can be considered for direct clinical application. This would allow for a more rigorous validation and a nuanced elaboration of the relationship between postoperative inflammatory dynamics and patient outcomes.

This study paves a new avenue for precision medicine in TNBC. Future work should involve prospective, multicenter validation studies. Integrating ctDNA analysis and radiomics could help build a multidimensional prognostic framework. Concurrently, exploring the relationship between dynamic inflammatory changes and response to immune checkpoint inhibitors represents a critical research direction. To facilitate real-world implementation, we plan to develop a user-friendly mobile application version of the nomogram, enhancing its accessibility for clinicians in diverse healthcare settings. In conclusion, by quantitatively assessing the dynamic postoperative inflammatory remodeling, our study enhances the precision of prognostic evaluation in TNBC. More importantly, it fosters a paradigm shift in personalized therapy, moving from a "static subclassification" towards a "dynamic monitoring" approach. This provides a novel engine for improving survival outcomes in this challenging breast cancer subtype.

Conclusion

Our study establishes a novel paradigm for prognostic evaluation in early-stage TNBC by systematically quantifying the longitudinal dynamics of postoperative systemic inflammation. The time-dependent model addresses the inherent static nature of conventional classification systems, thereby propelling a shift in personalized therapy from a static "subtyping" approach towards a dynamic "monitoring" strategy.

Heat Kernel Estimates for Schrödinger Operators in the Domain Above a Bounded Lipschitz Function
Anthony Graves-McCleary

Abstract
We give matching upper and lower bounds for the Dirichlet heat kernel of a Schrödinger operator Δ + W \Delta+W in the domain above the graph of a bounded Lipschitz function, in the case when W W decays away from the boundary faster than quadratically.



1
Introduction and Overview

We are interested in estimating the heat kernel of operators of the form
\Delta+W
in a domain
\Omega
in
\mathbf{R}^{N}
, where
\Delta=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}
is the Laplacian and we impose Dirichlet boundary conditions.

The specific setting we study is as follows. Let
f\colon\mathbf{R}^{N-1}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
be bounded and Lipschitz, with
N\geq 2
. Let

\Omega=\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1},x_{N})\colon x_{N}>f(x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1})\right\}

be the domain above the graph of
f
. Let
W\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
be smooth such that there exist
c,\epsilon>0
with

0\leq W(x)\leq c(1+\delta(x))^{-(2+\epsilon)}

for all
x\in\Omega
, where
\delta(x)=\inf\left\{\left|x-y\right|\colon y\in\partial\Omega\right\}
is the distance to the boundary. We are interested in the following problem:

Find matching upper and lower bounds for the Dirichlet heat kernel of
\Delta+W
in
\Omega
.

We give the answer to this problem in our main result, Theorem
6.4
.

In this section we give an overview of related work. Song
[
12
]
gave bounds on the heat kernel of the Laplacian in the domain above the graph of a bounded
C^{1,1}
function. Hirata in
[
10
]
gave estimates on the profile of certain Schrödinger operators in uniform cones, on which we make two observations. First, that some uniform cones can be realized as domains above the graph of a Lipschitz function, although this function is usually unbounded. Second, the Schrödinger operators studied by Hirata are of the form
(1+\left|x\right|)^{p-q}\delta(x)^{-p}
for
p<2<q
where
\delta
is distance from the boundary of the domain. Notably, these may be unbounded near the boundary and they decay at infinity. The potentials studied in our paper are bounded, but decay only with distance from the boundary, not the origin.

Chen, Kim, and Song in
[
4
]
gave estimates for the Green’s function of some non-local operators in half-space-like
C^{1,1}
open sets, and later Chen and Song in
[
5
]
gave heat kernel estimates in a similar setting. Gyrya and Saloff-Coste in
[
9
]
gave heat kernel estimates for the Laplacian in the domain above a Lipschitz graph. We will discuss this result in detail later, as it is of particular importance to our results.

1.1
Notation

Given
x\in\mathbf{R}^{N}
we let
\left|x\right|=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
denote the Euclidean norm. We let
B(x,r)=\left\{y\in\mathbf{R}^{N}\colon\left|x-y\right|<r\right\}
denote the open ball of radius
r>0
and center
x
in
\mathbf{R}^{N}
. The Laplacian is given by
\Delta=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}
; we caution that this sign convention is the opposite of some authors.

Given a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{N}
, we let
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
denote the set of smooth functions on
M
. For
x\in\Omega
, we let
\delta(x)=d(x,\partial\Omega)=\inf\left\{\left|x-y\right|\colon y\notin\Omega\right\}
denote distance to the boundary. We define
\left<x\right>:=1+\delta(x)
.

Given quantities
a
and
b
, we let
a\preceq b
denote the existence of a constant
C>0
such that
a\leq Cb
. We let
a\asymp b
denote that
a\preceq b
and
b\preceq a
. Exact values of constants may sometimes change from line to line.

Given
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in\mathbf{R}^{N}
, we let
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1})
denote the projection of
x
in
\mathbf{R}^{N-1}
. In practice we identify the ordered pair
(x^{\prime},x_{N})
with
x
. Given
r>0
, we define
x+r:=(x^{\prime},x_{N}+r)
. The expressions
dx
,
dy
,
dy^{\prime}
,
dy_{N}
, etc. will denote Lebesgue measure of the appropriate dimension.

2
Uniform Domains and the Boundary Harnack Principle

In this section we discuss uniform domains in Euclidean space, with an emphasis on the Boundary Harnack Principle.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{N}
be a domain. We say that
\Omega
is a
uniform domain
if there exist
c_{0},C_{0}>0
such that for any
x,y\in\Omega
, there exists a rectifiable curve
\gamma
of length at most
C_{0}
joining
x
to
y
and satisfying, for all
z\in\gamma([0,1])
,

d(x,\partial\Omega)\geq c_{0}\frac{\left|x-z\right|\left|y-z\right|}{\left|x-y\right|}.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{N}
, we define the intrinsic distance between
x,y\in\Omega
, denoted
d(x,y)
, to be the infimum of the lengths of rectifiable curves joining
x
to
y
.

Remark:
If
\Omega
is a uniform domain, then it is straightforward to verify that the instrinsic distance
d(x,y)
between
x,y\in\Omega
is comparable to the Euclidean distance
\left|x-y\right|
. We will use this later.

The domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function is a uniform domain, as seen in e.g. Gyrya and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
. For us, the most important property of a general uniform domain is the Boundary Harnack Principle, which we introduce below.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be an unbounded uniform domain in
\mathbf{R}^{N}
. There exist constants
A_{0},A_{1}>1
such that for any
\xi\in\partial\Omega
, any
r>0
, and any positive harmonic functions
u
and
v
in
B(\xi,A_{0}r)\cap\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions along
\partial\Omega
, we have that for all
x,y\in B(\xi,r)\cap\Omega
,

\frac{u(x)}{u(y)}\leq A_{1}\frac{v(x)}{v(y)}.

Proof:
See e.g. Gyrya and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
, Theorem 4.2 for an even more general version.∎

Remark:
The Boundary Harnack Principle has a rich history. It originated in papers of Ancona
[
1
]
, Dahlberg
[
7
]
, and Wu
[
16
]
in the late 1970s. See the textbook by Armitage and Gardiner
[
2
]
for a presentation in Lipschitz domains.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be an unbounded uniform domain in
\mathbf{R}^{N}
and let
W\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
. We say that a function
h
on
\Omega
is a
profile
of
\Delta+W
on
\Omega
if
h>0
on
\Omega
,
h
satisfies Dirichlet boundary conditions along
\partial\Omega
and

(\Delta+W)h=0.

If
W=0
we say that
h
is a
harmonic profile
on
\Omega
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Omega
be an unbounded uniform domain
\mathbf{R}^{N}
. Then there exists a harmonic profile
h>0
on
\Omega
. Furthermore, if
g,h>0
are harmonic profiles on
\Omega
, then there exists
c>0
such that
g=ch
.

Proof:
The proof of existence can be found in e.g. Gyrya and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
, Theorem 4.16. We will discuss some of the details of this proof later in our paper, as they will be useful in the proof of Theorem
5.6
.

For uniqueness up to a constant, a much more general version of this argument is given in e.g. Kajino and Murugan
[
11
]
, Lemma 3.19.∎

We finish this section with a useful result about the domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{N}
be the domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function, and let
h>0
be a harmonic profile on
\Omega
. Then
h
is non-decreasing in the vertical direction, and if
x\in\Omega
and
r>0
, if
x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})
and
x+r:=(x^{\prime},x_{N}+r)
, then

\int_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}h^{2}dx\asymp h(x+r)^{2}r^{n}.

Proof:
See Gyrya and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
, Proposition 6.4 and Proposition 6.7.∎

3
Heat Kernel Estimates of the Laplacian in a Lipschitz Domain

In this section we review the results of Gyrya and Saloff-Coste in
[
9
]
on domains above Lipschitz graphs.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be the domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function. Then
\Omega
is uniform, and hence satisfies the uniform boundary Harnack principle.

Proof:
Gyrya and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
, Proposition 6.6.∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega
be the domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function. Let
h>0
be a harmonic profile for
\Omega
. Then if
x,y\in\Omega
with
x^{\prime}=y^{\prime}
and
x_{n}<y_{n}
, we have
h(x)\leq h(y)
.

Proof:
Gyrya and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
, Proposition 6.4.∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Omega
be the domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function. Let
h>0
be a harmonic profile for
\Omega
and let
p_{\Omega}(t,x,y)
be the heat kernel of the Laplacian in
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Then there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}>0
such that for all
t>0
and all
x,y\in\Omega
we have

\frac{c_{1}h(x)h(y)}{t^{n/2}h(x+\sqrt{t})h(y+\sqrt{t})}e^{-c_{2}\frac{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}{t}}\leq p_{\Omega}(t,x,y)\leq\frac{c_{3}h(x)h(y)}{t^{n/2}h(x+\sqrt{t})h(y+\sqrt{t})}e^{-c_{4}\frac{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}{t}}.

Proof:
Gyrya and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
, Corollary 6.14.∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\Omega
be the domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function. Let
h>0
be a harmonic profile for
\Omega
and let
G_{\Omega}(x,y)
be the Green’s function of the Laplacian in
\Omega
. Then there exist constants
c,C>0
such that for all
x,y\in\Omega
,

c\int_{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{h(x)h(y)}{t^{n/2}h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}}dt\leq G_{\Omega}(x,y)\leq C\int_{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{h(x)h(y)}{t^{n/2}h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}}dt.

Proof:
Gyrya and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
, Theorem 5.13, combined with the fact that the instrinsic distance in
\Omega
is comparable to Euclidean distance.∎

Corollary
3.4
has the following consequence, known as the
3G
Principle.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\Omega
be the domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function. Let
h>0
be a harmonic profile for
\Omega
and let
G_{\Omega}(x,y)
be the Green’s function of the Laplacian in
\Omega
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
x,y,z\in\Omega
distinct, we have

\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,z)G_{\Omega}(z,y)}{G_{\Omega}(x,y)}\leq C\left(\frac{h(z)}{h(x)}G_{\Omega}(x,z)+\frac{h(z)}{h(y)}G_{\Omega}(z,y)\right).

Proof:
For
x,y\in\Omega
distinct let
G_{h}(x,y)=\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{h(x)h(y)}
. By cross-multiplying it suffices to show that for
x,y,z\in\Omega
distinct,

\frac{G_{h}(x,z)G_{h}(z,y)}{G_{h}(x,y)}\leq C\left(G_{h}(x,z)+G_{h}(z,y)\right).

Thus let
x,y,z\in\Omega
distinct. By the triangle inequality,
\left|x-y\right|\leq\left|x-z\right|+\left|z-y\right|
and therefore either
\left|x-y\right|\leq 2\left|x-z\right|
or
\left|x-y\right|\leq 2\left|z-y\right|
. Assume that
\left|x-y\right|\leq 2\left|x-z\right|
. Using Lemma , we have that

G_{h}(x,y)\asymp\int_{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{n/2}h(x+\sqrt{t})^{2}}\geq\int_{4\left|x-z\right|^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{n/2}h(x+\sqrt{t})^{2}}\asymp G_{h}(x,z),

the last relation obtained by the using change of variables
s=\sqrt{2}t
and applying volume doubling. Therefore in this case we have
G_{h}(x,z)\preceq G_{h}(x,y)
and so

\frac{G_{h}(x,z)G_{h}(z,y)}{G_{h}(x,y)}\preceq G_{h}(z,y).

If instead
\left|x-y\right|\leq 2\left|z-y\right|
, the proof is the same except to first use that
G_{h}(x,y)=G_{h}(y,x)
. ∎

The following lemma and its corollary concern domains in dimension
3
and higher. We will not need them in this paper, but they may be of independent interest.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{N}
be the domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function with
N\geq 3
. Let
h>0
be a harmonic profile for
\Omega
and let
G_{\Omega}(x,y)
be the Green’s function of the Laplacian in
\Omega
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for all
x,y\in\Omega
distinct, we have

\frac{h(y)}{h(x)}G_{\Omega}(x,y)\leq\frac{C}{\left|x-y\right|^{N-2}}.

Proof:
Let
h>0
be a harmonic profile for
\Omega
. Using Corollary
3.4
as well as Lemma
3.2
, we have that

\frac{h(y)}{h(x)}G_{\Omega}(x,y)\preceq\int_{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{h(y)^{2}dt}{h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}t^{N/2}}\leq\int_{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{N/2}}\asymp\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{N-2}}.

∎

Corollary 3.7
.

(Classical
3G
Principle) Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{N}
be the domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function with
N\geq 3
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for all
x,y,z\in\Omega
distinct,

\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,z)G_{\Omega}(z,y)}{G_{\Omega}(x,y)}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\left|x-z\right|^{N-2}}+\frac{1}{\left|z-y\right|^{N-2}}\right).

Proof:
Combine Lemma
3.6
with Proposition
3.5
.∎

4
Estimates in a Lipschitz Domain

We begin this section with some elementary observations in a Lipschitz domain. Given a domain
\Omega
and
x\in\Omega
, and let
\delta(x)=d(x,\partial\Omega)
be the distance from
x
to the boundary of
\Omega
. Also let
\left<x\right>=1+\delta(x)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\colon\mathbf{R}^{N-1}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
be Lipschitz and let
L>0
be such that
\left|f(x^{\prime})-f(y^{\prime})\right|\leq L\left|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\right|
for all
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{N-1}
. Let
\Omega=\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbf{R}^{N}\colon x_{N}>f(x^{\prime})\right\}
. Then for all
x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\Omega
,

\delta(x)\leq x_{N}-f(x^{\prime})\leq(2+L)\delta(x).

Proof:
Let
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in\Omega
. Since
(x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1},f(x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1}))\in\partial\Omega
we have that
\delta(x)\leq x_{N}-f(x)
.

Next, let
y\in\partial\Omega
be such that
d(x,y)=\left|x-y\right|=\delta(x)
. Since
y
must lie on the graph of
f
we can write
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{N-1},f(y_{1},\dots,y_{N-1}))
. Since the distance from
x
to
(x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1},f(x))
is at least
\delta(x)
while
\left|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\right|\leq\left|x-y\right|=\delta(x)
, we have that
\left|f(x^{\prime})-f(y^{\prime})\right|\leq L\left|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\right|\leq L\delta(x)
. Thus

x_{N}-f(x)=\left|x-(x^{\prime},f(x^{\prime}))\right|\leq\left|x-y\right|+\left|y-(x^{\prime},f(x^{\prime}))\right|

\leq\delta(x)+\left|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\right|+\left|f(x^{\prime})-f(y^{\prime})\right|\leq(2+L)\delta(x).

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f\colon\mathbf{R}^{N-1}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
be Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
L>0
and assume that
\left|f\right|\leq M
. Let
\Omega
be the domain above the graph of
f
. Then for
x\in\Omega
with
x_{N}>2M
we have

\frac{\delta(x)}{2}\leq x_{N}\leq 2(2+L)\delta(x).

Proof:
Using the previous lemma, we have that
\delta(x)\leq x_{N}-f(x^{\prime})\leq x_{N}+M\leq 2x_{N}
.

Also,
x_{N}\leq 2(x_{n}-M)\leq 2(x_{N}-f(x^{\prime}))\leq 2(2+L)\delta(x)
.∎

Let
M
be such that
\left|f\right|\leq M
. Therefore if we define
\Omega_{-}=\left\{(y_{1},\dots,y_{N})\colon y_{N}>M\right\}
and
\Omega_{+}=\left\{(y_{1},\dots,y_{N})\colon y_{N}>-M\right\}
we see that
\Omega_{+}\subseteq\Omega\subseteq\Omega_{-}
. Let
h_{+}
and
h_{-}
be the harmonic profiles of
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
respectively. They take a very simple form:
h_{+}(x)=x_{N}+M
and
h_{-}(x)=x_{N}-M
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega
be the domain above the graph of a bounded Lipschitz function. Let
h_{+}
,
h_{-}
,
\Omega_{+}
,
\Omega_{-}
be as defined above. Let
h>0
be a harmonic profile on
\Omega
. On
h\preceq h_{+}
on
\Omega
and
h_{-}\preceq h
on
\Omega_{-}
.

Proof:
We start by proving that
h\leq h_{+}
on
\Omega
. Note that
h\leq h_{+}
on
\partial\Omega
.

Let
g_{+}
be the solution in
\Omega
to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
g_{+}=h_{+}
on
\partial\Omega
. Then by the general theory of the Dirichlet problem, e.g. Classical Potential Theory
[
2
]
,
g_{+}\leq h_{+}
on
\Omega
and since
h_{+}
is bounded on
\partial\Omega
,
g_{+}
is bounded on all of
\Omega
. So
h_{+}-g_{+}
is a positive harmonic function on
\Omega
that vanishes at
\partial\Omega
. By uniqueness of the profile, there exists
c>0
such that
h_{+}-g_{+}=ch
. Hence
h=\frac{1}{c}\left(h_{+}-g_{+}\right)\preceq h_{+}
.

The argument that
h_{-}\preceq h
on
\Omega_{-}
is similar, using that
h
is bounded on
\partial\Omega_{-}
by the previous part.∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\Omega
be the domain above the graph of a bounded Lipschitz function. Let
W\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that there exist
c,\epsilon>0
with

\left|W(x)\right|\leq c\left<x\right>^{-(2+\epsilon)}

for all
x\in\Omega
, where
\left<x\right>=1+\delta(x)
. Then

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{h(y)G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{h(x)}\left|W(y)\right|dy<+\infty.

Proof:
Let
y\in\Omega
and
W
be as in the hypothesis. In this proof we extend
W
to all of
\mathbf{R}^{N}
by defining
W=0
outside of
\Omega
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\epsilon<1
. Let
M>0
be such that
\left|f\right|\leq M
. Let
\Omega_{+}
,
\Omega_{-}
,
h_{+}
, and
h_{-}
be as in Lemma
4.3
. Let
C>0
be such that
h\leq Ch_{+}
on
\Omega
and
h_{-}\leq Ch
on
\Omega_{-}
. Let
y\in\Omega
and
t>0
. If
t>9M^{2}
then
\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{t}\leq y_{N}+\sqrt{t}-M=h_{-}(y+\sqrt{t})
.

If
y_{N}>M
then
h_{+}(y)=y_{N}+M\leq 2y_{n}
. In particular, if
t>9M^{2}
and
y_{n}>M
then

\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}}\leq\frac{36C^{2}y_{N}^{2}}{t}.

(4.1)

Next, we note that the Green’s function
G_{\Omega}(x,y)
has the following estimate:

G_{\Omega}(x,y)\asymp h(x)h(y)\int_{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{h(y+{\sqrt{t}})^{2}t^{N/2}}.

(4.2)

Then

\int_{\Omega}\frac{h(y)G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{h(x)}\left|W(y)\right|dy\asymp\int_{\Omega}\int_{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+{\sqrt{t}})^{2}t^{N/2}}\left|W(y)\right|dtdy

=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+{\sqrt{t}})^{2}t^{N/2}}\left|W(y)\right|dydt.

We have
h(y+\sqrt{t})\geq h(y)
for all
y\in\Omega
and all
t>0
by Gyrya and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
. Thus

\int_{0}^{9M^{2}}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}t^{N/2}}\left|W(y)\right|dydt\leq\int_{0}^{9M^{2}}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{\left|W(y)\right|}{t^{N/2}}dydt

\leq c\int_{0}^{9M^{2}}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})}\frac{dy}{t^{N/2}}dt\preceq c\cdot 9M^{2}.

Similarly,

\int_{0}^{\delta(x)^{2}/4}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}t^{\frac{N}{2}}}\left|W(y)\right|dydt\leq\int_{0}^{\delta(x)^{2}/4}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{c\left<x\right>^{-(2+\epsilon)}}{t^{\frac{N}{2}}}dydt

\preceq\int_{0}^{\delta(x)^{2}/4}\left<x\right>^{-(2+\epsilon)}dt\preceq\left<x\right>^{-\epsilon}.

From these computations we conclude that

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{0}^{\max(9M^{2},\delta(x)^{2}/4)}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}}\left|W(y)\right|dydt<+\infty.

(4.3)

Next assume that
t>\max(9M^{2},\delta(x)^{2}/4)
. Using
t>9M^{2}
we have that

\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}t^{n/2}}\left|W(y)\right|dy\leq 9C^{2}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{(y_{N}+M)^{2}}{t^{1+\frac{N}{2}}}\left|W(y)\right|dy

\leq\frac{9C^{2}}{t^{1+\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}(y_{N}+M)^{2}\left|W(y)\right|dy.

Note that for
y\in\Omega
with
y_{N}>2M
, by Lemma
4.1
we have
y_{N}\leq 2(2+L)\delta(y)
and thus
\left|W(y)\right|\preceq y_{N}^{-(2+\epsilon)}
. Hence letting
Q(x,\sqrt{t})=B(x^{\prime},\sqrt{t})\times(x_{N}-\sqrt{t},x_{N}+\sqrt{t})
we note that
B(x,\sqrt{t})\subseteq Q(x,\sqrt{t})
and thus

\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}(y_{N}+M)^{2}\left|W(y)\right|dy\leq\int_{Q(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}(y_{N}+M)^{2}\left|W(y)\right|dy

\leq\int_{B(x^{\prime},\sqrt{t})}\int_{x_{N}-\sqrt{t}}^{x_{N}+\sqrt{t}}(y_{N}+M)^{2}\left|W(y^{\prime},y_{N})\right|dy_{N}dy^{\prime}.

Next,

\int_{x_{N}-\sqrt{t}}^{x_{N}+\sqrt{t}}(y_{N}+M)^{2}\left|W(y^{\prime},y_{N})\right|dy_{N}

=\int_{x_{N}-\sqrt{t}}^{\max(x_{N}-\sqrt{t},2M)}(y_{N}+M)^{2}\left|W(y^{\prime},y_{N})\right|dy_{N}+\int_{\max(x_{N}-\sqrt{t},2M)}^{x_{N}+\sqrt{t}}(y_{N}+M)^{2}\left|W(y^{\prime},y_{N})\right|dy_{N}

\preceq\int_{-2M}^{2M}9M^{2}\left|W(y^{\prime},y_{N})\right|dy_{N}+\int_{\max(x_{N}-\sqrt{t},2M)}^{x_{N}+\sqrt{t}}(y_{N})^{2}(y_{N})^{-(2+\epsilon)}dy_{n}

\leq 36M^{3}\left(\sup_{\Omega_{-}\cap\Omega_{+}}\left|W\right|\right)+\int_{\max(x_{N}-\sqrt{t},2M)}^{x_{N}+\sqrt{t}}y_{N}^{-\epsilon}dy_{N}

\leq 36M^{3}\left(\sup_{\Omega_{-}\cap\Omega_{+}}\left|W\right|\right)+\frac{(x_{N}+\sqrt{t})^{1-\epsilon}}{1-\epsilon}.

Here we utilize Lemma
4.2
and
t>\delta(x)^{2}/4
to see that
x_{N}+\sqrt{t}\leq 2(2+L)\delta(x)+\sqrt{t}<(3+L)\sqrt{t}
. Hence

36M^{3}\left(\sup_{\Omega_{-}\cap\Omega_{+}}\left|W\right|\right)+\frac{(x_{N}+\sqrt{t})^{1-\epsilon}}{1-\epsilon}\preceq t^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}}.

(4.4)

We therefore get that

\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}(y_{N}+M)^{2}\left|W(y)\right|dy\leq\int_{B(x^{\prime},\sqrt{t})}\int_{x_{N}-\sqrt{t}}^{x_{N}+\sqrt{t}}(y_{N}+M)^{2}\left|W(y^{\prime},y_{N})\right|dy_{N}dy^{\prime}

\preceq\int_{B(x^{\prime},\sqrt{t})}t^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}}dy\asymp t^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\cdot t^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}}=t^{\frac{N-\epsilon}{2}}.

Thus we have

\int_{\max(9M^{2},\delta(x)^{2}/4)}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}t^{\frac{N}{2}}}\left|W(y)\right|dydt\preceq\int_{\max(9M^{2},\delta(x)^{2}/4)}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\frac{N-\epsilon}{2}}}{t^{1+\frac{N}{2}}}dt

=\int_{\max(9M^{2},\delta(x)^{2}/4)}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}\preceq(\max(9M^{2},\delta(x)^{2}/4))^{-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}.

(4.5)

We thus conclude that

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\max(9M^{2},\delta(x)^{2}/4)}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}t^{N/2}}\left|W(y)\right|dy<+\infty.

(4.6)

Combining
4.3
with
4.6
we see that

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{h(y)G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{h(x)}\left|W(y)\right|dy\asymp\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}t^{N/2}}\left|W(y)\right|dydt

\leq\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{0}^{\max(9M^{2},\delta(x)^{2}/4)}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}t^{N/2}}\left|W(y)\right|dydt

+\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\max(9M^{2},\delta(x)^{2}/4)}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,\sqrt{t})\cap\Omega}\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}t^{N/2}}\left|W(y)\right|dydt

<+\infty.

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\Omega
be the domain above the graph of a bounded Lipschitz function. Let
W\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that there exist
c,\epsilon>0
with

\left|W(x)\right|\leq c\left<x\right>^{-(2+\epsilon)}

for all
x\in\Omega
, where
\left<x\right>=1+\delta(x)
. Then

\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,z)G_{\Omega}(z,y)\left|W(z)\right|}{G_{\Omega}(x,y)}dz<+\infty.

Proof:
From Proposition
3.5
, let
h>0
be a harmonic profile for
\Omega
and let
C>0
be such that for all
x,y,z\in\Omega
distinct, we have

\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,z)G_{\Omega}(z,y)}{G_{\Omega}(x,y)}\leq C\left(\frac{h(z)}{h(x)}G_{\Omega}(x,z)+\frac{h(z)}{h(y)}G_{\Omega}(z,y)\right).

Then using Proposition
4.4
, we get that

\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,z)G_{\Omega}(z,y)\left|W(z)\right|}{G_{\Omega}(x,y)}dz

\leq C\left(\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{h(z)G_{\Omega}(x,z)}{h(x)}\left|W(z)\right|dz+\sup_{y\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{h(z)G_{\Omega}(z,y)}{h(y)}\left|W(z)\right|dz\right)

<+\infty.

∎

5
Conditional Gaugeability

In this section we discuss conditional Brownian motion in
\Omega
. For a textbook presentation of these ideas, see Chung and Zhao
[
6
]
Chapter 5.

Let
(X_{t})_{t>0}
denote Brownian motion in
\Omega
with killing at the boundary. The Markov transition kernel of
(X_{t})_{t>0}
is
p^{\Omega}(t,x,z)
, the heat kernel of the Laplacian in
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Fix
y\in\Omega
. We now consider a new Markov transition kernel given by

p_{y}^{\Omega}(t,x,z)=\frac{G_{\Omega}(z,y)p^{\Omega}(t,x,z)}{G_{\Omega}(x,y)},

where
x,z\neq y
. The Markov process in
\Omega\setminus\left\{y\right\}
defined by
p_{y}^{\Omega}(t,x,z)
is Brownian motion conditioned to end at
y
, denoted
(X^{\cdot,y}_{t})_{t>0}
. Let
\zeta^{y}
denote the lifetime of this process. Let
E^{x}_{y}
denote expectation with respect to this process started at
x\in\Omega
.

The following lemma can be found e.g. in the proof of Lemma 3 in Zhao
[
17
]
. We reproduce the argument here.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Omega
be the domain above the graph of a bounded Lipschitz function and let
W\in C^{\infty}(M)
be bounded. Let
x,y\in\Omega
be distinct. Then

E^{x}_{y}\left[\int_{0}^{\zeta^{y}}\left|W(X^{\cdot,y}_{s})\right|ds\right]\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,z)G_{\Omega}(z,y)}{G_{\Omega}(x,y)}\left|W(z)\right|dz.

Proof:
For
x,y\in\Omega
distinct, by the Fubini-Tonelli Theorem we have that

E^{x}_{y}\left[\int_{0}^{\zeta^{y}}\left|W(X^{\cdot,y}_{s})\right|ds\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}E^{x}_{y}\left[s<\zeta^{y};\left|W(X^{\cdot,y}_{s})\right|\right]ds

=\frac{1}{G_{\Omega}(x,y)}\int_{0}^{\infty}E^{x}\left[s<\zeta^{y};G_{\Omega}(X_{s},y)\left|W(X_{s})\right|\right]ds

\leq\frac{1}{G_{\Omega}(x,y)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}p^{\Omega}(s,z,y)G_{\Omega}(z,y)\left|W(z)\right|dzdt

=\int_{\Omega}\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,z)G_{\Omega}(z,y)}{G_{\Omega}(x,y)}\left|W(z)\right|dz.

∎

Suppose in particular that
W\geq 0
and
a=\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,z)G_{\Omega}(z,y)}{G_{\Omega}(x,y)}W(z)dz<+\infty
. Then by Lemma
5.1
and Jensen’s inequality we have that

1\geq E^{x}_{y}\left[\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{\zeta^{y}}W(X^{\cdot,y}_{s})ds\right)\right]\geq\exp\left(-E^{x}_{y}\left[\int_{0}^{\zeta^{y}}W(X^{\cdot,y}_{s})ds\right]\right)\geq e^{-a}.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
W\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
. For
t>0
we define
e_{W}(t)=-\int_{0}^{t}W(X^{\cdot,y}_{s})ds
. We define the
conditional gauge
with respect to
W
as the function

E^{x}_{y}[e_{W}(\zeta^{y})].

We say that
W
is
conditionally gaugeable
if

\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}E^{x}_{y}[e_{W}(\zeta^{y})]<+\infty.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume that
W
is conditionally gaugeable. Then
\Delta+W
has a Green’s function, denoted
G_{\Omega}^{W}(x,y)
, and in particular, for
x,y\in\Omega
distinct,

G_{\Omega}^{W}(x,y)=G_{\Omega}(x,y)E^{x}_{y}[e_{W}(\zeta^{y})].

Proof:
See in particular Chen
[
3
]
, Lemma 3.5, Theorem 3.6, and the comment after the proof of Theorem 3.6.∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\Omega
be the domain above the graph of a bounded Lipschitz function. Let
W\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that there exist
c,\epsilon>0
with

0\leq W(x)\leq c\left<x\right>^{-(2+\epsilon)}

for all
x\in\Omega
, where
\left<x\right>=1+\delta(x)=1+d(x,\partial\Omega)
. Then the Green’s function
G_{\Omega}^{W}(x,y)
for
\Delta+W
in
\Omega
exists and there exists
C>0
such that for all
x,y\in\Omega
distinct we have

\frac{1}{C}G_{\Omega}(x,y)\leq G^{W}_{\Omega}(x,y)\leq G_{\Omega}(x,y).

Proof:
Follows from Proposition
5.3
plus the Jensen’s inequality remark above Definition
5.2
.∎

The main useful property of
G_{\Omega}^{W}(x,y)
, besides that given in Proposition
5.3
, is that for
x\in\Omega
fixed, the function
u=G_{\Omega}^{W}(x,\cdot)
satisfies
(\Delta+W)u=0
in
\Omega\setminus\left\{x\right\}
. We now introduce the classical Harnack principle for positive solutions to
(\Delta+W)u=0
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbf{R}^{N}
and let
W
be a bounded Borel function on
D
. Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
be a sequence a functions on
D
such that for each
n\in\mathbf{N}
,
u_{n}>0
on
D
and
(\Delta+W)u_{n}=0
in
D
. Assume also that the sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
is bounded uniformly on each compact subset of
D
. Then there exists a function
v
on
D
such that
(\Delta+W)v=0
and a subsequence
(u_{n_{k}})
such that
u_{n_{k}}\rightarrow v
locally uniformly on
D
.

Proof:
The sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
is equicontinuous by e.g. Theorem 8.22 in Gilbarg and Trudinger’s text
[
8
]
. The conclusion follows from the Arzela-Ascoli Theorem.∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\Omega
be the domain above the graph of a bounded Lipschitz function. Let
W\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that there exist
c,\epsilon>0
with

\left|W(x)\right|\leq c\left<x\right>^{-(2+\epsilon)}

for all
x\in\Omega
, where
\left<x\right>=1+\delta(x)=1+d(x,\partial\Omega)
. Then a profile
h^{W}>0
for
\Delta+W
in
\Omega
exists and, if
h>0
is a harmonic profile in
\Omega
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
x\in\Omega
,

\frac{1}{C}h(x)\leq h^{W}(x)\leq Ch(x).

Proof:
We follow the construction of the harmonic profile in
\Omega
given in
[
9
]
using Green’s function, now with the tools to compare the classical Green’s function to that of
\Delta+W
. Let constants
A_{0},A_{1}>1
be as in the statement of the Boundary Harnack Principle. Let
C>0
be as in Corollary
5.4
.

Fix
o\in\Omega
and fix
\xi\in\partial\Omega
. Let
(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
be an increasing sequence of positive radii with
r_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
. Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
be a sequence in
\Omega
such that for all
n\in\mathbf{N}
,
x_{n}\notin B(\xi,A_{0}r_{n})
. Fix a distinguished point
o\in\Omega
distinct from each
x_{n}
. Define

h_{n}(x):=\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,x_{n})}{G_{\Omega}(o,x_{n})},

h_{n}^{W}(x):=\frac{G_{\Omega}^{W}(x,x_{n})}{G_{\Omega}^{W}(o,x_{n})}.

Note that
h_{n}(o)=h_{n}^{W}(o)=1
for all
n
and by Corollary
5.4
, we have

\frac{1}{C}h_{n}(x)\leq h_{n}^{W}(x)\leq Ch_{n}(x)

(5.1)

for all
x\in\Omega\setminus\left\{x_{n}\right\}
and all
n
. Fix
n_{0}\in\mathbf{N}
and let
m,n\in\mathbf{N}
with
m,n\geq n_{0}
. Since
x_{n}\notin B(\xi,A_{0}r_{n_{0}})
, the function
G_{\Omega}(\cdot,x_{n})
is harmonic in
B(\xi,A_{0}r_{n_{0}})\cap\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions along
\partial\Omega
. Similarly for
m
. Thus by the Boundary Harnack Principle
2.3
, we have that for any
x\in B(\xi,r_{n_{0}})\cap\Omega
,

h_{n}(x)=\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,x_{n})}{G_{\Omega}(o,x_{n})}\leq A_{1}\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,x_{m})}{G_{\Omega}(o,x_{m})}=A_{1}h_{m}(x).

By symmetry we also have
h_{m}(x)\leq A_{1}h_{n}(x)
. We conclude that the sequence
(h_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
is uniformly bounded on each compact subset of
B(\xi,r_{n_{0}})\cap\Omega
, and hence the full sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
is, for each compact subset
K\subseteq\Omega
, eventually uniformly bounded on
K
.

Therefore by the classical Harnack principle for harmonic functions as well as passing to a subsequence, we may assume that the sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
converges uniformly on compact sets. By
\frac{1}{C}h_{n}\leq h_{n}^{W}\leq Ch_{n}
, we know that
(h_{n}^{W})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
is uniformly bounded on each compact subset of
\Omega
. So by Lemma
5.5
, we may pass to a subsequence again and assume that
(h_{n}^{W})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
converges uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega
. Let

h(x)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}h_{n}(x),

h^{W}(x)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}h_{n}^{W}(x).

It is routine to verify that
h
is harmonic and
(\Delta+W)h^{W}=0
. Clearly
h>0
. Also, since
\frac{1}{C}h_{n}\leq h_{n}^{W}\leq Ch_{n}
for all
n
, we must have

\frac{1}{C}h\leq h^{W}\leq Ch.

By Theorem 4.16 in Gyrya and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
,
h
satisfies Dirichlet boundary conditions along
\partial\Omega
, i.e.
h
is a harmonic profile for
\Omega
. By
\frac{1}{C}h\leq h^{W}\leq Ch
,
h^{W}
also satisfies Dirichlet boundary conditions and thus is a profile for
\Delta+W
in
\Omega
.∎

6
Dirichlet Forms and Doob’s
h
-Transform

Equipped with Theorem
5.6
, we are ready to study the heat kernel of
\Delta+W
in
\Omega
. First we consider the closed form on
L^{2}(\Omega,dx)
given by

\mathcal{E}^{W}(f,f)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}+Wf^{2}\right)dx

with domain
\mathcal{F}=W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega,dx)
. The fact that we used
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega,dx)
as the domain instead of
W^{1,2}(\Omega,dx)
corresponds to Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Let
h^{W}>0
be a profile for
\Delta+W
as in Theorem
5.6
, and let
d\mu=(h^{W})^{2}dx
. Consider the strictly local Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(\Omega,d\mu)
given by

\mathcal{E}_{\mu}(f,f)=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}d\mu.

Together with the domain

\mathcal{F}^{\mu}=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\Omega,d\mu)\cap W^{1,2}_{\textrm{loc}}(\Omega,dx)\colon\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}d\mu<+\infty\right\}

this is a closed form.

Next consider the unitary map
H\colon L^{2}(\Omega,d\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega,dx)
given by
H(f)=h^{W}f
. This yields a transformation of the form
\mathcal{E}^{W}
, allowing us to define

\mathcal{E}_{H}(f,f)=\mathcal{E}^{W}(hf,hf)

with domain
H^{-1}\mathcal{F}
. This is a strictly local Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(\Omega,d\mu)
.

Proposition 6.1
.

The forms
\mathcal{E}_{H}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\mu}
coincide, i.e.
H^{-1}\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
and
\mathcal{E}_{H}=\mathcal{E}_{\mu}
on their shared domain.

Proof:
See Gyrya and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
, Proposition 5.7 and Theorem 5.9. In that presentation
W=0
, i.e. the profile is harmonic, but this does not affect any of the proofs.∎

Since the forms
\mathcal{E}_{H}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\mu}
coincide, so do their infinitesimal generators, and hence so do their heat kernels. We let
p_{\mu}(t,x,y)
denote the shared heat kernel. We have the following useful lemma that relates
p_{\mu}(t,x,y)
to the heat kernel of
\Delta+W
, which we refer to as
p^{W}(t,x,y)
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
p_{\mu}(t,x,y)
and
p^{W}(t,x,y)
be as above. Then for all
t>0
and all
x,y\in\Omega
, we have

p^{W}(t,x,y)=h^{W}(x)h^{W}(y)p_{\mu}(t,x,y).

Proof:
Follows from Lemma
6.1
as well as Gyrya and Saloff-Coste
[
9
]
, Lemma 5.6.∎

6.2
Obstacles to the Unbounded Case: Example in
\mathbf{R}^{2}

In this section we show some possible obstacles to a proof of the analogous result when the Lipschitz function
f
is not necessarily bounded. Consider the function
f\colon\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
given by
f(x_{1})=-\left|x_{1}\right|
and let
\Omega=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2})\colon x_{2}>f(x_{1})\right\}
. Viewing
\mathbf{R}^{2}
as
\mathbf{C}
with polar coordinates
z=re^{i\theta}
,
r\geq 0
and
-\pi/2\leq\theta<3\pi/2
, we then have

\Omega=\left\{re^{i\theta}\colon r>0,-\pi/4<\theta<5\pi/4\right\}.

(6.1)

With this definition, the harmonic profile of
\Omega
is then given in polar coordinates as

h(re^{i\theta})=r^{2/3}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{6}+\frac{2\theta}{3}\right).

(6.2)

Notably, when trying to bound the quantity
\frac{h(y)^{2}}{h(y+\sqrt{t})^{2}}
, we do not get a tractable expression as in
4.1
. If hypothetically we achieve an upper bound of the form
t^{-2/3}
, then we have the additional obstacle of
t^{-\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)}
not having an integrable tail for small
\epsilon>0
. Compare this to
4.5
, an important bound in the proof involving an integrable tail.

7

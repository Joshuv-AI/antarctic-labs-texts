Age, not tumor size, modifies the association between extrathyroidal extension and long-term outcomes in patients with follicular cell-derived thyroid carcinoma
Authors: Shaza Ahmed Samargandy, Saad Samargandy, Hanan Mohammed Faruqui, Asala Baharoon, Mazin Merdad, Ahad Khalaf M Alsuwat
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBACKGROUND: Follicular cell-derived thyroid carcinomas (FCTC) are common, with stable mortality rates but significant recurrence risks. Extrathyroidal extension (ETE) affects recurrence risk and staging. The 8th edition of the AJCC/TNM classification excludes microscopic ETE from upstaging but considers gross ETE a significant factor in older patients.OBJECTIVE: This study examines clinical outcomes and disease-free survival (DFS) in FCTC patients with and without ETE, evaluating the modifying effects of age and tumor size on incomplete response risk.DESIGN: A single-center retrospective chart review was conducted at King Abdulaziz University Hospital, Jeddah, Saudi ArabiaSETTING: King Abdulaziz University HospitalPATIENTS AND METHODS: Patients aged ≥13 years diagnosed from January 2017 to December 2021 with long-term follow-up (≥18 months) were included. Medullary and anaplastic thyroid cancers were excluded. Data on demographics, tumor characteristics, risk stratification, and clinical outcomes were collected. Response to therapy was categorized per the 2015 ATA guidelines. Chi-square and Multivariable analysis assessed interactions between age, tumor size, and ETE in predicting incomplete response. Kaplan-Meier curves visualized survival differences.MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES: Identifying the interactions between age and tumor size with ETE to predict the risk of incomplete response to therapy among patients with FCTC.SAMPLE SIZE: 255 patients. The median follow-up duration was 4 years.RESULTS: Patients with ETE were present in (n=38) 15.7% (8% microscopic, 7% gross). Older patients (≥55 years) with ETE had 26.47-fold higher odds of incomplete response than younger patients. Tumor size independently predicted incomplete response, but its interaction with ETE was not significant. Patients with ETE had a significantly higher incomplete response rate (58% vs. 17%, P=.0001).CONCLUSION: Older patients with ETE face a markedly higher risk of incomplete response. Tumor size is an independent risk factor in those patients. Management should be stratified by age, advocating more aggressive management for older patients with ETE.LIMITATIONS: Retrospective, and single-center design.Keywords: Follicular cell-derived thyroid carcinoma, papillary thyroid cancer, extra-thyroidal extension

INTRODUCTION

Follicular cell-derived thyroid carcinomas (FCTC) are common tumors with an increasing incidence worldwide. Despite the rise in case detection, FCTC mortality rate has remained stable but recurrence rates remained significant.1 Therefore the management following surgery in most cases is directed toward mitigating the risk of recurrence.2

Several histologic and demographic factors affect the risk of biochemical and structural incomplete response to therapy and mortality among FCTC patients. One of the crucial factors that increase the risk of recurrence and can also upstage the patient AJCC/TNM status is extrathyroidal extension (ETE).3 In recent years, it was recognized that the extent of ETE impacts the risk of recurrence and even survival especially in older patients.3 This was reflected at the most recent (8th edition) of the AJCC/TNM for differentiated and anaplastic thyroid cancer, where ETE did not affect the TNM and did not upstage the disease. However, gross ETE regardless of the tumor size places the patient in a more advanced tumor stage if the patient is ≥55 years of age. Conversely, microscopic ETE carries low to intermediate risk of recurrence and did not affect patient's survival.4 In fact, larger tumor size, but not the presence of microscopic ETE, was found to be an independent factor predicting disease-free-survival in FCTC.5 Another factor that is evolving as a determinant of response to therapy is age. It is known that age inversely affects disease-specific-survival (DSS) in FCTC,6 but more recently the studies are relating advanced age to a less favorable response to therapy.7

In this study, we aimed to examine clinical outcomes and disease-free survival (DFS) of FCTC patients with intrathyroidal disease compared to those with ETE. We then examined the modifying effect of age and tumor size on the risk of incomplete response in patients with ETE.

METHODS

This is a retrospective study that took place at King Abdulaziz University Hospital, Jeddah, Saudi Arabia. Ethical approval was obtained from the Biomedical Ethics Committee at King Abdulaziz University Hospital, IRB Reference No. 399-23, September 5, 2023. We included patients with papillary, follicular, oncocytic cells and poorly differentiated thyroid cancers. All patients of 13 years of age and older seen during the period January 2017-December 2021, with valid long-term follow up, and sufficient data were included. Those with medullary or anaplastic thyroid cancers were excluded. Valid long-term follow-up period is considered to be 18 months or longer after diagnosis.

For each subject, we obtained the following information; age and tumor risk stratification upon diagnosis, gender, duration of follow up, stage based on the 8th edition of the AJCC/TNM staging system, detailed histological data, and the details of management. Risk stratification and clinical outcomes were assigned to each patient based on the 2015 American Thyroid Association (ATA) initial risk stratification and response to therapy classification systems for patients who underwent total thyroidectomy and radioactive iodine (RAI) ablation or therapy. Outcome was labelled as excellent, indeterminate, biochemical or structural incomplete response to therapy according to the ATA guidelines.3 Patients who had total thyroidectomy without RAI therapy were considered to have an excellent response if the Tg level is <0.2ng/ml with stimulated Tg <1 ng/ml. An indeterminate response if Tg level was between 0.2–5 ng/ml, and a stimulated Tg ≤10 ng/ml. A biochemical incomplete response is indicated when Tg is >5.0 ng/ml with stimulated Tg >10 ng/ml. For patients who have undergone a lobectomy, an excellent response is defined as a postoperative Tg level <30 ng/ml, with no signs of recurrence on neck ultrasonography. An indeterminate response if Tg is >30 ng/ml.8 Incomplete response to therapy during follow-up was categorized as biochemical (without evidence of structural disease on imaging or biopsy), or a structural recurrence. Mortality -if occurred- was investigated to identify if it was related to thyroid cancer or another cause of death.

Statistical analysis

We described categorical variables using frequencies (n) with percentages (%). Chi-square test was used to compare distributions of categorical variables as related to ETE status (none or microscopic and gross). We separately modeled interactions between age and tumor size with ETE to predict the risk of incomplete response to therapy among thyroid cancer patients. To determine independent associations between main effects and interaction term and the risk of incomplete response, variables with a P value of less than 0.2 from the univariable models were included in the multivariable analysis. Plots of Kaplan-Meier survival curves were utilized to depict the interaction of age and tumor size with ETE in predicting the proportion of thyroid cancer patients responding to therapy. We used SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA) for the analyses with a significance level set at .05.

RESULTS

The study included 255 patients, with a mean (standard deviation, SD) age of 41.6 (14.3) years, and 74% were female. The median follow-up duration was 4 years (interquartile range [2.8–7.0] years). Microscopic or gross ETE was present in 38 patients (15.7%), with 20 (8%) having microscopic ETE and 18 (7%) having gross ETE. Table 1 shows notable distinctions between patients with and without extrathyroidal extension (ETE). Those with ETE tended to be older, more frequently males, presented with larger tumors and displayed a higher prevalence of lymph node involvement, advanced disease stage, vascular invasion, margin involvement, high-risk papillary thyroid carcinoma variants, and multifocality. Additionally, they received higher doses of radioactive iodine. The rate of incomplete response was significantly higher in patients with ETE (58%) compared to those without (17%) (P=.0001). Multivariable analysis revealed a significant interaction between age and the presence of ETE in predicting incomplete response to therapy (P for interaction=.05). In patients without ETE, those aged 55 or older exhibited 2.81-fold higher odds of incomplete response than their younger counterparts. Notably, the presence of ETE significantly amplified this age-related risk, with the odds ratio escalating to 26.47 for individuals 55 years or older with ETE, compared to 2.52 for those under 55 with ETE. In contrast, the interaction between tumor size and ETE was not statistically significant (P for interaction=.25), suggesting that while larger tumor size independently predicted incomplete response, this effect was not significantly modified by the presence of ETE. DISCUSSION

Several histopathological factors have been linked to risk of recurrence and incomplete response to therapy in FCTC.3 Age and tumor size are factors that have been recently found to also negatively affect that risk.6–9 However, the evidence for age as an independent risk factor has been more consistent across all the spectrum of FCTC from low to high risk.10,11 Another factor with robust evidence linking it to incomplete risk to therapy and risk of recurrence is ETE.12 While most of the studies assessed ETE as an independent risk factor for recurrence, here we studied how patient's age and tumor size modify this risk. Although tumor size, ETE and patient's age are independently predictive of incomplete response, their predictive power could be exponential when they are present together. In this study, we found that these factors are unequally additive. When adjusted to tumor size, age had a larger impact (OR 26.47) than tumor size >4cm (OR=5.60) adjusted to age.

Since the ATA intermediate risk thyroid cancer group has a wide spectrum of risk for recurrence, our study goes along the same line, showing that when age and/or tumor size are added to ETE, the risk increases significantly even if the overall ATA stage remains intermediate. However, the additive effect of age is much more powerful than the tumor size when applied to patients with ETE.

In a similar study from Korea, study participants with gross ETE to strap muscles only were divided based on tumor size. Interestingly, tumors ≤2 cm with and without ETE had comparable outcomes. Small tumors ≤2 cm with ETE had no significant effect on DSS and had recurrence-free survival (RFS) superior to tumors >2 cm in size without ETE. Although age and tumor size were identified in that study as significant predictive factors affecting mortality in univariate analysis, only age was recognized as an independent predictive factor in the multivariate analysis.13 Correspondingly, another study that included 5880 patients found the collective estimated 10-year DSS for patients with ETE to strap muscles alone, and gross ETE involving other surrounding structures were 95.7%, and 83.7%, respectively. However, when the analysis was limited to patients ≥55 years or older, the estimated 10-year DSS for patients with ETE to strap muscles alone, and gross ETE involving other surrounding structures were lower at 89.3%, and 68.1%, respectively indicating the impact of age on patients with ETE.14

In addition, another propensity-score matching analysis among FCTC patients found ETE into the strap muscles and beyond significantly affected the overall survival in patients ≥55 years of age.15 In contrast, ETE to strap muscles did not affect survival in patients younger than 55 years of age. The overall survival was particularly decreased when the tumor size was >2 cm in patients with gross ETE. The interaction between age and tumor size with ETE was not analyzed though in the former mentioned analysis.15

With the abundance of evidence now available on how age and tumor size alter the response to therapy and RFS in FCTC, it is prudent to assess the risk of recurrence with more scrutiny in order to decide the optimal subsequent management. Within the category of ATA intermediate risk FCTC, we believe some patients can be stratified as low risk or low-intermediate particularly if the postoperative thyroglobulin was reassuring and in young age even when there is no extensive ETE or large size tumor.16 A good example is young patients with tumors harboring microscopic ETE where the risk of recurrence is estimated at 3–8%.17 To date, no studies have conclusively shown that RAI therapy can further improve this already low risk, and some experts postulated that lobectomy would be an acceptable approach if the tumor size was below 4 cm.18 However, in older patients (≥55 years) with ETE as shown in the current study, the risk of incomplete response to therapy is close to 13 times more than younger patients (<55 years) with ETE (risk 26.47 vs 2.52) and a more active management might be recommended in such patients.

Most previous studies assessed the overall and disease-specific survival which are usually not the main concern in most cases of FCTC who have excellent prognosis. However, our study is an attempt to refine the risk by further analyzing the impact of two important risk factors, namely age and tumor size in patients with ETE, and tailoring management based on this risk.

There are some limitations to our study that are worth highlighting. In addition to the single-center retrospective nature of the study, we did not segregate the extent of ETE to gross and microscopic in the analysis due to the small number of patients with ETE. However, we believe the statistical analysis showed powerful differences between young and old age that is unlikely to be mitigated by the extent of ETE.

In summary, we found that advancing age strongly potentiates the risk of recurrence in patients with ETE in FCTC. This suggests that patients ≥ 55 years of age with ETE should be proactively managed and carefully followed regardless of the tumor size. Further studies are needed to assess this further and to study interactions between different risk factors.

CONCLUSION

Older patients with ETE have a higher risk of incomplete response than their younger counterparts. Despite being an independent risk factor for incomplete response to therapy, tumor size did not modify how ETE affects the risk of incomplete response to therapy. We suggest tailoring FCTC management in patients with ETE from more conservative in younger patients to less conservative in older patients.

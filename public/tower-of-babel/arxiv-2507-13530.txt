Abstract
. We propose a novel formulation for the second-order total generalized variation (TGV) of the normal vector on an oriented, triangular mesh embedded in \R 3 \R^{3} . The normal vector is considered as a manifold-valued function, taking values on the unit sphere. Our formulation extends previous discrete TGV models for piecewise constant scalar data that utilize a Raviart-Thomas function space. To exctend this formulation to the manifold setting, a tailor-made tangential Raviart-Thomas type finite element space is constructed in this work. The new regularizer is compared to existing methods in mesh denoising experiments.

1.
Introduction

The total variation (TV) seminorm is a commonly used regularizer for various kinds of inverse problems.
It was first proposed as a regularizer for image denoising problems in
[
24
]
and is ever since omnipresent in the field of mathematical image processing.
On a bounded domain
\Omega\subseteq\R^{2}
, the TV-seminorm of a function
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
can be defined as

(1)

\operatorname{TV}(u)\coloneqq\sup\setDef[auto]{\int_{\Omega}u\div\bv\d{x}}{\bv\in\cC_{c}^{1}(\Omega,\R^{2})\text{ \st\ }\norm{\bv}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,\R^{2})}\leq 1},

where
\cC_{c}^{1}(\Omega,\R^{2})
is the set of continuously differentiable vector fields with compact support in
\Omega
.
Unlike smooth regularizers, the TV-seminorm is capable of removing noise while preserving discontinuities in the data.
However, it suffers from the so-called staircasing effect, meaning that discontinuous reconstructions with several small jumps occur even where smoother ones are desired.

The imaging community has proposed numerous modifications to the total variation regularizer in order to overcome the staircasing effect for imaging problems; see
\eg
[
11
,
13
,
12
]
.
One of the most popular extensions to this day is the total generalized variation (
\operatorname{TGV}
), introduced in
[
9
]
.
Given weights
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\in\R_{>0}
its second-order non-symmetric version reads

(2)

\operatorname{TGV}_{(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1})}^{2}(u)\\
=\sup\setDef[auto]{\int_{\Omega}u\div\Div V\d{x}}{V\in\cC_{c}^{2}(\Omega,\R^{2\times 2})\text{ \st}\paren[auto]\{\}{\begin{aligned} \norm{V}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,\R^{2\times 2})}&\leq\alpha_{0}\\
\norm{\Div V}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,\R^{2})}&\leq\alpha_{1}\end{aligned}}},

where
\Div V
denotes the row-wise divergence operator of the twice continuously differentiable matrix-valued field
V\in\cC_{c}^{2}(\Omega,\R^{2\times 2})
.
Often the above formulation is reformulated using Fenchel duality to obtain

(3)

\operatorname{TGV}_{(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1})}^{2}(u)=\min_{\bw\in\operatorname{BV}(\Omega,\R^{2})}\alpha_{1}\,\norm{\nabla u-\bw}_{\cM(\Omega,\R^{2})}+\alpha_{0}\,\norm{\nabla\bw}_{\cM(\Omega,\R^{2\times 2})},

where
\operatorname{BV}
is the space of bounded variation,
\nabla
is the distributional gradient and
\norm{\cdot}_{\cM}
is the Radon norm; see
[
17
]
for more details.
Many authors also consider the symmetric variant, which utilizes the symmetrized gradient operator in the
\alpha_{0}
-term above.
Both variants of the second-order
\operatorname{TGV}
regularizer favor piecewise linear instead of piecewise constant reconstructions and thereby overcome the staircasing effect.

Notice that in the case of piecewise constant functions
u
, both variants of
\operatorname{TGV}
reduce to
\alpha_{1}\,\operatorname{TV}
when taken literally.
This has led to a number of application specific discrete formulations of
\operatorname{TGV}
, which are not equivalent to the continuous formulation.

A
\operatorname{TGV}
formulation for graph signals was proposed in
[
22
]
.
This concept was subsequently applied to the dual graph of a triangular mesh in
[
15
]
to postulate the earliest version of
\operatorname{TGV}
for piecewise constant data on triangular meshes.
It was observed in
[
2
, Section 2.3.3]
that this formulation can be interpreted as using a divergence-like operator in the
\alpha_{0}
-term in (
3
) instead of a gradient.
We refer the reader to
[
10
]
, where various differential operators in the
\alpha_{0}
-term were originally investigated.
The numerical results presented there strongly suggest that the divergence operator generally leads to oscillations, which are also present in the numerical results of
[
15
]
.

To avoid these oscillations, we have proposed an improved formulation of
\operatorname{TGV}
suitable for piecewise constant functions on triangular meshes in
[
2
, Section 3]
.
Our formulation utilizes a gradient-like operator for the
\alpha_{0}
-term and a lowest-order Raviart–Thomas function for the auxiliary variable
\bw
.

Total Generalized Variation for Mesh Denoising

Two alternative formulations of
\operatorname{TGV}
for piecewise constant functions were proposed in
[
21
,
29
]
for the purpose of mesh denoising.
On the one hand, the authors of
[
21
]
proposed a formulation based on the
\operatorname{TGV}
on graphs from
[
15
]
but added an additional weight function into the divergence operator.
The authors of
[
29
]
, on the other hand, proposed a novel way to compute discrete (second-order) derivatives of piecewise constant functions.
They replaced the differential operators in (
3
) by their discrete analogs to obtain a formulation of
\operatorname{TGV}
.
Both
[
21
,
29
]
successfully utilize their respective formulations for the purpose of mesh denoising based on the total generalized variation of the unit normal vector.
A slightly different approach to a discrete formulation of
\operatorname{TGV}
was taken by
[
30
]
for continuous, piecewise linear data on triangular meshes.
This approach requires the definition of normal vectors at mesh vertices to be utilized for mesh denoising.

It is worth mentioning that
[
21
,
29
,
30
]
treat the normal vector as an element of
\R^{3}
and not as an element of the unit sphere
{\cS}\coloneqq\setDef[normal]{\bn\in\R^{3}}{\abs{\bn}_{2}=1}\subseteq\R^{3}
.
A variant of
\operatorname{TV}
of the normal vector of a mesh taking the manifold nature of
{\cS}
into account, is developed in
[
28
,
6
]
.
As these are based on
\operatorname{TV}
, but not
\operatorname{TGV}
, the staircasing effect also occurs, resulting in poor reconstructions of curved areas.
To overcome this, the concept of total general variation needs to be reinterpreted for normal vector data since the sphere is not a linear space.
While the
\operatorname{TGV}
seminorm (
3
) favors piecewise linear functions and thus piecewise constant gradients, the sought-after
\operatorname{TGV}
formulation for the normal vector should favor areas of constant principal curvatures.

Contributions

The goal of this paper is to propose a formulation of
\operatorname{TGV}
for normal vector fields on triangular meshes that favors areas of constant (discrete) curvature.
To this end, we propose an adaptation of the discrete
\operatorname{TGV}
formulation for piecewise constant real-valued data from
[
2
]
.
We construct a special Raviart–Thomas-like finite element space for the analog of the auxiliary variable
\bw
in (
3
) that captures derivative information of the normal vector field.
It is worth mentioning here that concepts of total generalized variation for manifold-valued data have been considered in
[
8
]
.
Closely related approaches based on second-order total variation have been taken in
[
1
,
4
,
5
]
.
However, all of these approaches work with data on two-dimensional Cartesian grids.
While some of our ideas are similar, we exploit the close relation of the mesh to its normal vector and the fact that the normal vector field maps into
{\cS}
-valued instead of a general manifold.

Organization

The structure of this paper is as follows.
In
\cref
section:total-generalized-variation-piecewise-constant, we recap the discrete formulation for piecewise constant data on triangular meshes from
[
2
]
, which employs a Raviart–Thomas finite element function.
Next, in
\cref
section:discrete-tgv-of-the-normal-vector-field we review the total variation (TV) of the normal.
Then, we extend the formulation from
[
2
]
to obtain a novel discrete formulation of total generalized variation of the normal vector.
Therein, we utilize a tailor-made Raviart–Thomas space for the auxiliary function
\bw
whose function values represent part of the derivative (push-forward) of the normal vector field, just as
\bw
in (
3
) represents part of the derivative of the scalar data
u
.
We then address the numerical realization of the resulting method in
\cref
section:numerical-realization.
Finally, we present numerical results for mesh denoising problems in
\cref
section:numerical-results-for-mesh-denoising, comparing our approach to
[
21
]
and
[
29
]
.

2.
Total Generalized Variation with Piecewise Constant Finite Elements

In this section, we recap the formulation for
\R
-valued data from
[
2
]
.

2.1.
Notation and Finite Element Spaces

Let
\Gamma
be a triangulated and orientable surface mesh embedded in
\R^{3}
.
We denote its set of triangles by
\cT
, its edge set by
\cE
and its vertex set by
\cV
.
Every edge
E\in\cE
is assumed to have exactly two adjacent triangles, which we denote by
{T}_{E_{+}}
and
{T}_{E_{-}}
.
This choice is arbitrary but will remain fixed, even after deformations.
On an edge
E
, we define
{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
as the co-normal vector to the triangle
{T}_{E_{+}}
,
\ie
, the unit vector orthogonal to
E
that lies in the plane of
{T}_{E_{+}}
, pointing away from
{T}_{E_{+}}
.
We define the other co-normal vector
{\bmu}_{E_{-}}
similarly; see
\cref
figure:co-normal-vectors.

We define the standard discontinuous Galerkin finite element space on
\Gamma
by

(4)

\cD\cG_{r}(\Gamma,\R^{n})\coloneqq\setDef[Big]{\bu\colon\bigcup_{T\in\cT}T\to\R^{n}}{\restr{\bu}{T}\in P_{r}(T,\R^{n})\text{ for all }T\in\cT},

where
P_{r}(T,V)
is the set of all polynomials defined on
T
of maximum degree
r
with values in some vector space
V
.
The restriction of a function
\bu\in\cD\cG_{r}(\Gamma,V)
to a triangle
T\in\cT
is denoted by
\bu_{T}
.
Likewise, for an edge
E\in\cE
, we denote the restriction of
\bu
to
{T}_{E_{+}}
by
{\bu}_{E_{+}}
, and the restriction of
\bu
to
{T}_{E_{-}}
by
{\bu}_{E_{-}}

The jump of
\bu
across an edge
E
is denoted by
\jump{\bu}_{E}\coloneqq{\bu}_{E_{+}}-{\bu}_{E_{-}}
.

Furthermore, we define the finite element space on the skeleton of the mesh as

(5)

\cD\cG_{r}(\cE,\R^{n})\coloneqq\setDef[Big]{\bu\colon\bigcup_{E\in\cE}E\to\R^{n}}{\restr{\bu}{E}\in P_{r}(E,\R^{n})\text{ for all }E\in\cE},

where
P_{r}(E,V)
is the set of all polynomials defined on
E
of maximum degree
r
with values in some vector space
V
.

A key ingredient to the
\operatorname{TGV}
formulation from
[
2
]
for piecewise constant functions is the lowest-order Raviart–Thomas finite element space
\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}
.
In the case of a planar (2D) mesh,
\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}
is defined as the smallest
H(\div)
-conforming space that maps the divergence surjectively onto
\cD\cG_{0}
.
The
H(\div)
-conformity is equivalent to the continuity of the co-normal component across the edges of the mesh.
As described in
[
23
,
16
]
, the space can be generalized to triangular meshes
\Gamma
embedded in
\R^{3}
by using piecewise polynomial functions with the same basis functions on the reference element as in the planar case.
The requirement of
H(\div)
-conformity then becomes

(6)

{\bw}_{E_{+}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{+}}=-{\bw}_{E_{-}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{-}}

on all edges
E
, and we can obtain the following description of the lowest-order Raviart–Thomas space on
\Gamma
:

(7)

\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}(\Gamma,\R^{3})\coloneqq\setDef[auto]{\bw\in\cD\cG_{1}(\Gamma,\R^{3})}{\begin{aligned} &\restr{\bw}{T}\in P_{0}(T,\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma])+(\bx-\bx_{T})\,P_{0}(T,\R)\\
&\text{and }\jump{\bw\cdot\bmu}_{E}=0\text{ for all }T\in\cT\text{ and }E\in\cE\end{aligned}}.

Here
\bx
denotes the spatial coordinate on
\Gamma
,
\bx_{T}
is a fixed reference point in
T
, and
\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma]
is the common tangent space to
\Gamma
at all points in
T
.
Notice that the function values
\restr{\bw}{T}
belong to
\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma]
.
The co-normal continuity (
6
) is conveniently realized by choosing

(8)

\int_{E}{\bw}_{E_{+}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\d{S}=-\int_{E}{\bw}_{E_{-}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{-}}\d{S}

as the global degrees of freedom, which results in the following choice of basis functions for the space (
7
):

\Phi_{E}(\bx)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{2\,\abs{{T}_{E_{+}}}}(\bx-{\bp}_{E_{+}})&\text{ if }\bx\in{T}_{E_{+}},\\
\tfrac{-1}{2\,\abs{{T}_{E_{-}}}}(\bx-{\bp}_{E_{-}})&\text{ if }\bx\in{T}_{E_{-}},\\
0&\text{ else},\end{cases}

with
\bp_{E_{\pm}}
denoting the coordinate of the vertex of
T_{E_{\pm}}
opposite to
E
.

2.2.
Discrete TGV for Piecewise Constant Functions

The first-order total variation of a piecewise constant function
u\in\cD\cG_{0}(\Gamma,\R)
amounts to

(9)

\operatorname{TV}(u)=\sum_{E\in\cE}\abs{\jump{u}_{E}}\d{S}=\sum_{E\in\cE}\abs{E}\,\abs{\jump{u}_{E}}.

As we have shown in
[
2
, eq. (2.10)]
, the second-order total generalized variation seminorm (
3
) reduces to TV (
9
) and therefore offers no advantage.
To overcome this, a discrete adaptation of
\operatorname{TGV}
is required.
In (
3
), the
\alpha_{1}
-term couples the gradient of
u
to the auxiliary variable
\bw
.
When
u
is piecewise constant, the gradient information is concentrated on the edges in form of the jump
\jump{u}
.
We proposed to couple this scalar value of
\jump{u}_{E}
on an edge
E
to the degree of freedom located on
E
of a Raviart–Thomas function
\bw\in\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}(\Gamma,\R^{3})
as in (
7
)–(
8
).
The
\alpha_{0}
-term then measures the (discrete) total variation of the auxiliary variable
\bw\in\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}(\Gamma,\R^{3})
, leading to a concept of discretely linear, piecewise constant functions.
Overall, the formulation proposed in
[
2
]
reads

(10)

\textup{FETGV}_{(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1})}^{2}(u)\coloneqq\min_{\bw\in\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}(\Gamma,\R^{3})}\alpha_{1}\sum_{E\in\cE}\int_{E}\abs[big]{\jump{u}_{E}+h_{E}\,{\bw}_{E_{+}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}\d{S}\\
+\alpha_{0}\sum_{T\in\cT}\int_{T}\abs{\nabla\bw_{T}}_{F}\d{x}+\alpha_{0}\sum_{E\in\cE}\int_{E}\cI_{1}\enclose[big]{\{}{\abs[big]{\jump{\bw}}_{2}}{\}}\d{S},

where
\abs{\,\cdot\,}_{F}
is the Frobenius norm of a matrix and
\cI_{1}\enclose[]{\{}{\cdot}{\}}
denotes the linear interpolation at the endpoints of an edge, denoted by
X_{E,1}
and
X_{E,2}
.
Furthermore,
h_{E}
denotes a mesh-dependent factor chosen as the distance between the circumcenters of the two adjacent triangles sharing the edge
E
.
Therefore,
\jump{u}_{E}/h_{E}
is a finite difference that corresponds to the directional derivative of
u
in direction
-{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
.
For more details we refer the reader to
[
2
, Section 3]
.

3.
Discrete Total Generalized Variation of the Normal

In this section we extend the discrete total generalized variation for piecewise constant functions (
10
) to the piecewise constant unit normal vector field
\bn
on a triangular mesh
\Gamma
embedded in
\R^{3}
.
Unlike the methods proposed in
[
21
,
29
]
for
\operatorname{TGV}
mesh denoising, we consider
\bn
with values in the manifold
{\cS}
rather than in
\R^{3}
.
This has significant implications on the auxiliary variable
\bw
in (
10
), which is responsible for capturing changes in the data, in this case, in the normal vector.
Before defining the proposed formulation in
\cref
subsection:discrete-tgv-of-the-normal-vector-field, we review some elementary geometric calculus for the sphere in
\cref
subsection:geometric-calculus-for-the-sphere-and-identities-on-triangulated-meshes.
In
\cref
subsection:discrete-tv-of-the-normal-vector-field, we revisit the first-order total variation of the normal vector field, and then we define the tailored tangential Raviart–Thomas space in
\cref
subsection:tangential-Raviart-Thomas-space that captures derivative information of the normal vector field.

3.1.
Geometric Calculus for the Sphere and Identities on Triangulated Meshes

We briefly recall some basic concepts on the Riemannian manifold
{\cS}
, the
2
-sphere, in the context of the normal vector of a triangulated mesh embedded in
\R^{3}
, following
[
7
, Appendix]
.
Given two vectors
\bn_{1},\bn_{2}\in{\cS}
with
\bn_{1}\neq-\bn_{2}
, the logarithmic map is given as

(11)

\logarithm{\bn_{1}}{\bn_{2}}=\begin{cases}\bnull&\text{ if }\bn_{1}=\bn_{2}\\
\displaystyle d(\bn_{1},\bn_{2})\frac{\bn_{2}-(\bn_{1}\cdot\bn_{2})\,\bn_{1}}{\abs{\bn_{2}-(\bn_{1}\cdot\bn_{2})\,\bn_{1}}}&\text{ else},\end{cases}

where

d(\bn_{1},\bn_{2})\coloneqq\arccos(\bn_{1}\cdot\bn_{2})

is the geodesic distance on
{\cS}
.
The logarithmic map is the vector in the tangent space
\tangentSpace{\bn_{1}}[{\cS}]
pointing from
\bn_{1}
to
\bn_{2}
and of length
d(\bn_{1},\bn_{2})
.
It also enters the so-called parallel transport, which transforms a vector
\bxi\in\tangentSpace{\bn_{1}}[{\cS}]
to a vector in
\tangentSpace{\bn_{2}}[{\cS}]
along the shortest geodesic (assuming
\bn_{1}\neq-\bn_{2}
) by

(12a)

\displaystyle\parallelTransport{\bn_{1}}{\bn_{2}}(\bxi)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\bxi&\text{ if }\bn_{1}=\bn_{2}\\
\bxi-\frac{\bxi\cdot\logarithm{\bn_{1}}{\bn_{2}}}{d(\bn_{1},\bn_{2})^{2}}\paren[auto](){\logarithm{\bn_{1}}{\bn_{2}}+\logarithm{\bn_{2}}{\bn_{1}}}&\text{ else}\end{cases}

(12b)

\displaystyle=\paren[auto](){\id-\frac{\bn_{2}+\bn_{1}}{1+\bn_{2}\cdot\bn_{1}}\,\bn_{2}^{\transp}}\bxi.

When
\bn_{1}=\bn_{2}
, the equality in between (
12a
) and (
12b
) is obvious due to
\bn_{2}\cdot\bxi=0
.
Otherwise, using the definition of the logarithmic map (
11
) and expanding the norms yields

\displaystyle\MoveEqLeft\bxi-\frac{\bxi\cdot\logarithm{\bn_{1}}{\bn_{2}}}{d(\bn_{1},\bn_{2})^{2}}\paren[auto](){\logarithm{\bn_{1}}{\bn_{2}}+\logarithm{\bn_{2}}{\bn_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\bxi-\bxi\cdot\frac{\bn_{2}-(\bn_{2}\cdot\bn_{1})\,\bn_{1}}{\abs{\bn_{2}-(\bn_{2}\cdot\bn_{1})\,\bn_{1}}_{2}}\paren[auto](){\frac{\bn_{2}-(\bn_{2}\cdot\bn_{1})\,\bn_{1}}{\abs{\bn_{2}-(\bn_{2}\cdot\bn_{1})\,\bn_{1}}_{2}}+\frac{\bn_{1}-(\bn_{2}\cdot\bn_{1})\,\bn_{2}}{\abs{\bn_{1}-(\bn_{2}\cdot\bn_{1})\,\bn_{2}}_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\bxi-\bxi\cdot\frac{\bn_{2}}{\sqrt{1-(\bn_{2}\cdot\bn_{1})^{2}}}\frac{(1-\bn_{2}\cdot\bn_{1})(\bn_{2}+\bn_{1})}{\sqrt{1-(\bn_{2}\cdot\bn_{1})^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\bxi-\bxi\cdot\bn_{2}\frac{\bn_{2}+\bn_{1}}{1+\bn_{2}\cdot\bn_{1}}

\displaystyle=\paren[auto](){\id-\frac{\bn_{2}+\bn_{1}}{1+\bn_{2}\cdot\bn_{1}}\,\bn_{2}^{\transp}}\bxi.

To make use of these definitions on triangular meshes, define a unit vector
\bt_{E}
, tangential to an edge
E\in\cE
with arbitrary but fixed orientation.
Then,
\set{{\bn}_{E_{+}},{\bmu}_{E_{+}},\bt_{E}}
forms an orthonormal basis of
\R^{3}

\wrt
to the standard inner product at a point on an edge
E
.
Analogously,
\set{{\bn}_{E_{-}},{\bmu}_{E_{-}},\bt_{E}}
also forms an orthonormal basis of
\R^{3}
.
This setup is illustrated in
\cref
figure:co-normal-vectors.

Figure 1
.
Illustration of normals
{\bn}_{E_{+}}
and
{\bn}_{E_{-}}
of two triangles
{T}_{E_{+}}
,
{T}_{E_{-}}
sharing an edge
E
.
The triangles’ co-normals are
{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
and
{\bmu}_{E_{-}}
and the unit vector tangent to the edge is
\bt_{E}
.
The logarithmic map, described in
\cref
section:discrete-tgv-of-the-normal-vector-field is also pictured.

Using this property, the logarithmic map between two normal vectors of adjacent triangles can be stimplified.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
E
be the edge shared by the triangles
{T}_{E_{+}}
,
{T}_{E_{-}}
with respective normal vectors
{\bn}_{E_{+}}
,
{\bn}_{E_{-}}
and co-normal vectors
{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
,
{\bmu}_{E_{-}}
.
Then

(13)

\displaystyle\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{sign}\paren[big](){{\bn}_{E_{-}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}\,d({\bn}_{E_{+}},{\bn}_{E_{-}})\,{\bmu}_{E_{+}},

\displaystyle\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{sign}\paren[big](){{\bn}_{E_{-}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}\,d({\bn}_{E_{+}},{\bn}_{E_{-}})\,{\bmu}_{E_{-}}.

Proof.

We start with the first identity and exclude the obvious case
{\bn}_{E_{+}}={\bn}_{E_{-}}
.
Then, the logarithmic map from (
11
), up to a scaling factor, is

(14)

\frac{{\bn}_{E_{-}}-({\bn}_{E_{+}}\cdot{\bn}_{E_{-}})\,{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{\abs{{\bn}_{E_{-}}-({\bn}_{E_{+}}\cdot{\bn}_{E_{-}})\,{\bn}_{E_{+}}}}.

It is easy to see that (
14
) is orthogonal to
{\bn}_{E_{+}}
and
\bt_{E}
.
Since,
({\bn}_{E_{+}},{\bmu}_{E_{+}},\bt_{E})
form an orthonormal basis of
\R^{3}
, we have

\frac{{\bn}_{E_{-}}-({\bn}_{E_{+}}\cdot{\bn}_{E_{-}})\,{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{\abs{{\bn}_{E_{-}}-({\bn}_{E_{+}}\cdot{\bn}_{E_{-}})\,{\bn}_{E_{+}}}}=\sigma\,{\bmu}_{E_{+}}

for some
\sigma\in\R
.
Since the left side has norm one,
\sigma\in\set{-1,1}
.
Taking the inner product with
{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
on both sides yields
\operatorname{sign}({\bn}_{E_{-}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{+}})=\sigma
.
Plugging these identities into the definition of
\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}
(
11
) shows the desired first identity in (
13
).
To show the second, we can swap
{T}_{E_{+}}
and
{T}_{E_{-}}
.
There we can proceed analogously to obtain

\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}=\operatorname{sign}\paren[auto](){{\bn}_{E_{+}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{-}}}d({\bn}_{E_{+}},{\bn}_{E_{-}}){\bmu}_{E_{-}}.

It remains to show
{\bn}_{E_{+}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{-}}={\bn}_{E_{-}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
, for which we use the help of the orthogonal matrix
Q={\bn}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}-{\bmu}_{E_{-}}{\bn}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}+\bt_{E}\,\bt_{E}^{\transp}
.
Using the triple vector product, we obtain

\displaystyle{\bn}_{E_{+}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{-}}

\displaystyle=(Q\,{\bn}_{E_{+}})\cdot(Q\,{\bmu}_{E_{-}})

\displaystyle=\paren[big](){{\bn}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}{\bn}_{E_{+}}-{\bmu}_{E_{-}}{\bn}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}{\bn}_{E_{+}}}\cdot{\bn}_{E_{-}}

\displaystyle=\paren[big](){{\bn}_{E_{+}}\times({\bn}_{E_{-}}\times{\bmu}_{E_{-}})}\cdot{\bn}_{E_{-}}

\displaystyle={\bmu}_{E_{-}}\cdot{\bn}_{E_{+}}.

∎

Having established these identities for the logarithmic maps between normals of adjacent triangles, the parallel transport (
12
) for this situation can be significantly simplified.

Lemma 3.2
.

The parallel transport (
12
) between vectors in adjacent tangent spaces
\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!{\cS}]
and
\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}[\!\!{\cS}]
is given by

(15)

\displaystyle\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}(\bxi)

\displaystyle=\paren[big](){\id-{\bmu}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}-{\bmu}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}}\,\bxi,

\displaystyle\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}(\bchi)

\displaystyle=\paren[big](){\id-{\bmu}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}-{\bmu}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}}\,\bchi,

where
\bxi\in\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}[\!\!{\cS}]
and
\bchi\in\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!{\cS}]
respectively.
In particular, we have

(16)

\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}({\bmu}_{E_{-}})=-{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\quad\text{and}\quad\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}({\bmu}_{E_{+}})=-{\bmu}_{E_{-}}.

Proof.

In the case of
{\bn}_{E_{+}}={\bn}_{E_{-}}
the parallel transport is an identity and we have
{\bmu}_{E_{+}}=-{\bmu}_{E_{-}}
, which gives the desired result.
Otherwise, plugging in the result of
\cref
lemma:sphere:logarithmic-map:using-co-normals into (
12
) yields

\displaystyle\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}(\bxi)

\displaystyle=\bxi-\operatorname{sign}\paren[auto](){{\bn}_{E_{-}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}^{2}d({\bn}_{E_{+}},{\bn}_{E_{-}})^{2}\frac{\bxi\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{-}}}{d({\bn}_{E_{+}},{\bn}_{E_{-}})^{2}}\paren[auto](){{\bmu}_{E_{-}}+{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}

\displaystyle=\bxi-\bxi\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{-}}\paren[auto](){{\bmu}_{E_{-}}+{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}

\displaystyle=\paren[big](){\id-{\bmu}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}-{\bmu}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}}\,\bxi,

and an analogous result is obtained for
\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}(\bchi)
.
The identities (
16
) for the parallel transport of co-normals
\bmu_{E_{\pm}}
follow immediately from plugging in
\bxi={\bmu}_{E_{-}}
and
\bchi={\bmu}_{E_{+}}
into (
15
).
∎

3.2.
Discrete Total Variation of the Normal Vector Field

The normal vector on a triangular mesh embedded in
\R^{3}
is constant on each triangle,
\ie
,
\bn\in\cD\cG_{0}(\Gamma,{\cS})
.
Therefore the variation of the normal is concentrated on the edges.
In this manifold-valued setting, the total variation of the normal is defined as

(17)

\operatorname{TV}_{\cS}(\bn)=\sum_{E\in\cE}\int_{E}d({\bn}_{E_{+}},{\bn}_{E_{-}})\d{S},

\ie

, the absolute value of the difference for scalar-valued data (
9
) is replaced by the geodesic distance
d(\cdot,\cdot)
; see for instance
[
20
]
.
Generally, the geodesic distance can be expressed using the norm on the tangent space of the logarithmic map; see
[
6
]
.
Hence,

(18)

\operatorname{TV}_{\cS}(\bn)=\sum_{E\in\cE}\int_{E}\abs[big]{\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}}_{2}\d{S}.

Comparing this to the total variation in the scalar-valued setting (
9
), it can be observed that
\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}
takes the role of the jump in (
9
).
Indeed the logarithmic map can be conceived as a generalization of the difference.

In order to pass to the discrete formulation (
10
) of second-order
\operatorname{TGV}
, we observe that the jump
\jump{u}
is coupled to the co-normal component
{\bw}_{E_{+}}\cdot{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
of the Raviart–Thomas function
\bw
in the
\alpha_{1}
-term.
To extend this formulation to the case of the normal vector, we need to replace
\jump{u}_{E}
by
\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}
, which carries the information about the variations of neighboring normal vectors.
We thus need to couple
\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}
to an auxiliary variable from a Raviart–Thomas space with a degree of freedom located also on the edge
E
.
However, the logarithmic map is tangent space-valued, therefore a tangent space-valued Raviart–Thomas space is required to adapt (
10
) to the normal vector.
Such a space is non-standard and constructed in the following subsection.

3.3.
Tangential Raviart–Thomas Space

In order to motivate what follows, we need to briefly discuss first- and second-order derivatives of normal vectors of manifolds.
To this end, suppose that
\Gamma
is a smooth submanifold of
\R^{3}
equipped with the parallel transport
\parallelTransportSymbol_{\gamma,t}\colon\tangentSpace{\gamma(0)}[\Gamma]\to\tangentSpace{\gamma(t)}[\Gamma]
along smooth curves
\gamma
on
\Gamma
compatible with the Euclidean metric in
\R^{3}
.
Furthermore, let
\bn\colon\Gamma\to{\cS}
be the normal vector field of
\Gamma
.

The first-order derivative (or push-forward) of
\bn
at a point
\bx\in\Gamma
, denoted by
\bn_{\bx}^{\prime}
, is a linear mapping from the domain tangent space
\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma]
to the co-domain tangent space
\tangentSpace{\bn(\bx)}[{\cS}]
.
We denote such mappings by
L(\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma],\tangentSpace{\bn(\bx)}[{\cS}])
.

In contrast to derivatives for functions in linear spaces, second-order derivatives cannot be defined in a straightforward manner in an iterated fashion.
The reason is that, when
\bx\in\Gamma
is perturbed slightly to
\bar{\bx}
, the tangent space changes as well and
\bn_{\bar{\bx}}^{\prime}\in L(\tangentSpace{\bar{\bx}}[\Gamma],\tangentSpace{\bn(\bar{\bx})}[{\cS}])
.
Hence,
\bn_{\bx}^{\prime}
and
\bn_{\bar{\bx}}^{\prime}
belong to different spaces.
To define a second-order derivative of the normal vector field
\bn
, it is convenient to use an identification of
\tangentSpace{\bn(\bx)}[{\cS}]
with
\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma]
. This is visualized in
\cref
figure:identification-of-tangent-spaces and is discussed, for instance, in
[
7
, Sec. 2.2]
.

Figure 2
.
Visualization of the relation between
\tangentSpace{\bn(\bx)}[{\cS}]
and
\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma]
.
Adapted from
[
7
, Fig. 1]
.

Consequently,
\bn_{\bx}^{\prime}
can be treated as an element of
L(\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma],\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma])
for the purpose of differentiation.
Let
\tangentBundle[\Gamma]
denote the tangent bundle and
\cotangentBundle[\,\Gamma]
denote the cotangent bundle on
\Gamma
.
The function
\bn^{\prime}\colon\tangentBundle[\Gamma]\to\tangentBundle[\Gamma]
can be seen as a
(1,1)
-tensor field
T
on
\Gamma
,
\ie
, a bilinear map with one argument in the cotangent bundle and one argument in the tangent bundle.
More precisely, the relation between
\bn^{\prime}
and
T
is given by
T_{\bx}[\bTheta,\bXi]\coloneqq\bTheta_{x}[\bn_{x}^{\prime}[\bXi_{x}]]
where
\bTheta\in\cotangentBundle[\,\Gamma]
and
\bXi\in\tangentBundle[\Gamma]
.

As described in
[
19
, Lemma 4.6]
, such a
(1,1)
-tensor field
T
can be differentiated using covariant derivatives. Given a smooth co-vector field
\bTheta\in\cotangentBundle[\,\Gamma]
and smooth vector fields
\bChi,\bXi\in\tangentBundle[\Gamma]
, the derivative of
T\colon\cotangentBundle[\,\Gamma]\times\tangentBundle[\Gamma]\to\R
in direction
\bChi
is given by

(19)

\covariantDerivative{\bChi}{T}[\bTheta,\bXi]\coloneqq(T[\bTheta,\bXi])^{\prime}[\bChi]-T[\covariantDerivative{\bChi}{\bTheta},\bXi]-T[\bTheta,\covariantDerivative{\bChi}{\bXi}],

where
\covariantDerivative{X}{\bTheta}
and
\covariantDerivative{X}{\bXi}
are the covariant derivatives induced by the parallel transport on
\Gamma
as for instance described in
[
18
, Definition 4.1.2]
.

Through this construction, the value of
\covariantDerivative{\bChi}{T}[\bTheta,\bXi]
at
\bx\in\Gamma
is computed only from values of
T
along the curve
\gamma\colon\interval(){-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\to\Gamma
with
\gamma(0)=\bx
and
\dot{\gamma}(t)=\bChi_{\gamma(t)}
.
In fact,
\covariantDerivative{\bChi}{T}[\bTheta,\bXi]
at
\bx
only depends on
\bTheta_{\bx}
,
\bChi_{\bx}
and
\bXi_{\bx}
, which means that
\bTheta
and
\bXi
can be defined as extensions of quantities
\btheta\in\cotangentSpace{\bx}[\,\Gamma]
and
\bxi\in\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma]
in an arbitrary but smooth way along the curve
\gamma
.
The specific choice of such an extension
\bTheta_{\gamma(t)}\coloneqq\btheta\circ\parallelTransportSymbol_{\gamma,t}^{-1}
and
\bXi_{\gamma(t)}=\parallelTransportSymbol_{\gamma,t}\enclose[]{(}{\bxi}{)}
achieves that (
19
) is simplified, because
\covariantDerivative{\bChi}{\bTheta}=0
and
\covariantDerivative{\bChi}{\bXi}=0
holds at
\bx
.
Overall, the derivative of a
(1,1)
-tensor field
T
at a point
\bx
in direction
\bchi\in\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma]
is given by

(20)

\covariantDerivative{\bchi}{T}[\btheta,\bxi]=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{T_{\gamma(t)}\paren[big][]{\btheta\circ\parallelTransportSymbol_{\gamma,t}^{-1},\parallelTransportSymbol_{\gamma,t}\enclose[]{(}{\bxi}{)}}-T_{\bx}[\btheta,\bxi]}{t},

where
\gamma\colon(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to\Gamma
is a smooth curve with
\gamma(0)=\bx
and
\dot{\gamma}(\bx)=\bchi
.

Going back to the normal vector
\bn^{\prime}\colon\tangentBundle[\Gamma]\to\tangentBundle[\Gamma]
, it is possible to drop
\btheta
by using
(\cotangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma])^{*}\cong\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma]
.
Hence, we define

(21)

\covariantDerivative{\bchi}\bn^{\prime}[\bxi]\coloneqq\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\parallelTransportSymbol_{\gamma,t}^{-1}\paren[big](){\bn_{\gamma(t)}^{\prime}[\parallelTransportSymbol_{\gamma,t}\enclose[]{(}{\bxi}{)}]}-\bn_{\bx}^{\prime}[\bxi]}{t}.

We can use
\covariantDerivative{\bchi}\bn^{\prime}[\bxi]
to define the analog of a linear function
\Gamma\to\R
,
\ie
, of a function whose second derivative vanished.
The normal vector field
\bn\colon\Gamma\to{\cS}
can be considered
\enquote
linear in a region
\Gamma_{0}
with non-empty interior in case
\covariantDerivative{\bchi}\bn^{\prime}[\bxi]=\bnull
holds for all
\bchi,\bxi\in\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma]
and all points
\bx\in\Gamma_{0}
.
Such areas can be exptected to be favored by the
\operatorname{TGV}
regularizer we devise in
\cref
subsection:discrete-tgv-of-the-normal-vector-field.
We now relate this
\enquote
linearity of the normal vector field with the curvature of the surface
\Gamma
.
To this end, recall that the eigenvalues of
\bn_{\bx}^{\prime}\in L(\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma],\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma])
are known as principal curvatures while the eigenvectors are the principal directions of curvature.
Provided that
\covariantDerivative{\bchi}\bn^{\prime}[\bxi]=\bnull
for all
\bchi,\bxi\in\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma]
holds in a region
\Gamma_{0}
with non-empty interior, the numerator of (
21
) is zero along curves
\gamma
in
\Gamma_{0}
.
Choosing then
\bxi
as an eigenvector of
\bn_{\bx}^{\prime}
with eigenvalue
\lambda
yields

\displaystyle\parallelTransportSymbol_{\gamma,t}^{-1}\paren[big](){\bn_{\gamma(t)}^{\prime}[\parallelTransportSymbol_{\gamma,t}\enclose[]{(}{\bxi}{)}]}=\bn_{\bx}^{\prime}[\bxi]=\lambda\,\bxi.

Applying the parallel transport on both sides shows

\bn_{\gamma(t)}^{\prime}\paren[big][]{\parallelTransportSymbol_{\gamma,t}\enclose[]{(}{\bxi}{)}}=\lambda\,\parallelTransportSymbol_{\gamma,t}\enclose[]{(}{\bxi}{)},

\ie

,
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\bn_{\gamma(t)}^{\prime}
to the eigenvector
\parallelTransportSymbol_{\gamma,t}\enclose[]{(}{\bxi}{)}
.
This means that
\enquote
linearity of the normal vector in the previously mentioned sense implies that the principal curvatures are constant in
\Gamma_{0}
and the principal directions of curvature are related by simple parallel transports.
This property is characteristic for planes, spheres and the lateral surface of cylinders.
Consequently, we expect that the regularizer based on the total generalized variation of the normal vector (see (
32
)) will favor shapes that are piecewise surfaces of these three types.

Notice that after the derivation and interpretation of (
21
), the identification of
\tangentSpace{\bn(\bx)}[{\cS}]
with
\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma]
is no longer required. Hence,
\covariantDerivative{\bchi}\bn_{\bx}^{\prime}[\bxi]
can be treated as an element of
\tangentSpace{\bn(\bx)}[{\cS}]
rather then
\tangentSpace{\bx}[\Gamma]
.

We now return to the setting where
\Gamma
is a triangular and orientable surface mesh embedded in
\R^{3}
.
Using insights from the above smooth setting, we construct a tailored Raviart–Thomas-like finite element space for the auxiliary variable that captures derivatives of the normal vector field in a discrete
\operatorname{TGV}
formulation.
Notice that a similar idea has already been mentioned in
[
8
, Remark 2.4]
, but only for Cartesian grids.
While it is natural to consider the derivative of the normal vector field intrinsically between two-dimensional tangent spaces, we find it convenient for implementation purposes to work with the ambient space
\R^{3}
.
To this end, we identify the space
L(\tangentSpace{\bx}[\cM],\tangentSpace{\bn(x)}[{\cS}])
with a subspace of
L(\R^{3},\R^{3})
, by mapping vectors orthogonal to
\tangentSpace{\bx}[\cM]
to
\bnull
.
Using, for instance, the standard basis in
\R^{3}
, an element of
L(\R^{3},\R^{3})
can be represented by a
\R^{3\times 3}
matrix whose rows can be interpreted as elements of
\cotangentSpace{\bx}[\cM]\subseteq\R^{1\times 3}
and columns are interpreted as elements of
\tangentSpace{\bn(x)}[{\cS}]\subseteq\R^{3}
.

For each point in the interior of a triangle
T\in\cT
, a function of the RT space under construction should take values in the tensor product space
\tangentSpace{\bn_{T}}[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma]
.
Therefore, we denote the space by
\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma])
.
In contrast to previous work on finite element spaces with tangent space-valued data such as
[
25
]
, we benefit from the fact that the tangent space is constant across each triangle, which simplifies the setting significantly.
Across an edge
E
, however, the normal vector
\bn
may change.
Hence the tangent space spaces
\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!{\cS}]
and
\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}[\!\!{\cS}]
may be different as well.

The desired mapping properties of
W
into
\tangentSpace{\bn_{T}}[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma]
entail that

{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\in\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!{\cS}]\quad\text{and}\quad-{W}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}\in\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}[\!\!{\cS}]

since the co-normal
{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
belongs to
\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!{\cS}]
and
{\bmu}_{E_{-}}
belongs to
\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}[\!\!{\cS}]
.

We impose the co-normal continuity of
W
in the sense of

(22)

{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}=\parallelTransport[big]{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}(-{W}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}})

on all edges.
This is a natural, intrinsic generalization of (
6
) for the standard RT space.

To obtain an equivalent, more manageable formulation of (
22
), we use the identity
\id={\bn}_{E_{+}}{\bn}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}+{\bmu}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}+\bt_{E}\bt_{E}^{\transp}
(and similarly with
E_{-}
) to rewrite (
22
) as

({\bmu}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}+\bt_{E}\bt_{E}^{\transp})\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}=\parallelTransport[big]{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}(-({\bmu}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}+\bt_{E}\bt_{E}^{\transp})\,{W}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}).

Using
\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}(-{\bmu}_{E_{-}})={\bmu}_{E_{+}}
and
\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}(\bt_{E})=\bt_{E}
, see
\cref
lemma:sphere:parallel-transport:using-co-normals and
\cref
figure:co-normal-vectors, we can further rewrite this as

{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\paren[big](){{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}+\bt_{E}\paren[big](){\bt_{E}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}={\bmu}_{E_{+}}\paren[big](){{\bmu}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}}+\bt_{E}\paren[big](){-\bt_{E}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}}.

Since
{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
and
\bt_{E}
are orthogonal, this is equivalent to

(23)

\displaystyle{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}

\displaystyle={\bmu}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}},

\displaystyle\bt_{E}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}

\displaystyle=-\bt_{E}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}.

This motivates the following basis functions indexed by the edges
E
and supported on the adjacent triangles
T_{E_{\pm}}
:

(24a)

\displaystyle\Phi_{E,1}(\bx)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{2\,\abs{{T}_{E_{+}}}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}(\bx-{\bp}_{E_{+}})^{\transp}&\text{ if }\bx\in{T}_{E_{+}},\\
\tfrac{1}{2\,\abs{{T}_{E_{-}}}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}(\bx-{\bp}_{E_{-}})^{\transp}&\text{ if }\bx\in{T}_{E_{-}},\\
0&\text{ else},\end{cases}

(24b)

\displaystyle\Phi_{E,2}(\bx)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mrep{\tfrac{1}{2\,\abs{{T}_{E_{+}}}}\bt_{E}\,(\bx-{\bp}_{E_{+}})^{\transp}}{\tfrac{1}{2\,\abs{{T}_{E_{+}}}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}(\bx-{\bp}_{E_{+}})^{\transp}}&\text{ if }\bx\in{T}_{E_{+}},\\
\tfrac{-1}{2\,\abs{{T}_{E_{-}}}}\bt_{E}\,(\bx-{\bp}_{E_{-}})^{\transp}&\text{ if }\bx\in{T}_{E_{-}},\\
0&\text{ else}.\end{cases}

where
\bx
is the spatial coordinate and
\bp_{E_{\pm}}
is the coordinate of the vertex of
T_{E_{\pm}}
opposite to
E
.
It is easy to see that, as desired,
\Phi_{E,1}
and
\Phi_{E,2}
are linear, matrix-valued functions with rows from
\cotangentSpace{T}[\,\Gamma]
and columns from
\tangentSpace{\bn_{T}}[{\cS}]
on each triangle
T\in\cT
.
To verify the intrinsic co-normal continuity (
23
) of these basis functions, a simple geometric consideration shows
(\bx-\bp_{E_{\pm}})^{\transp}\bmu_{E_{\pm}}=\tfrac{2\,\abs{T_{E_{\pm}}}}{\abs{E}}
for any
\bx\in E
.
Plugging this in yields

\displaystyle{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}{(\Phi_{E,1})}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\abs{E}}={\bmu}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}{(\Phi_{E,1})}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}},

\displaystyle\bt_{E}^{\transp}{(\Phi_{E,2})}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\abs{E}}=-\bt_{E}^{\transp}{(\Phi_{E,2})}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}.

Consequently, one obtains

\displaystyle\delta_{i1}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}{(\Phi_{E,i})}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\d{S}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}{(\Phi_{E,i})}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}\d{S},

\displaystyle\delta_{i2}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\bt_{E}^{\transp}{(\Phi_{E,i})}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\d{S}

\displaystyle=-\int_{E}\bt_{E}^{\transp}{(\Phi_{E,i})}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}\d{S}.

for
i=1,2
, where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta.
Furthermore, for an edge
\widetilde{E}\neq E
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{E}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}{(\Phi_{\widetilde{E},i})}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\d{S},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\bt_{E}^{\transp}{(\Phi_{\widetilde{E},i})}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\d{S},

for
i=1,2
.
This is because we have
(\bx-\widetilde{\bp})^{\transp}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}=0
for
\widetilde{\bp}
for
\bx\in E
, where
\widetilde{\bp}
is the coordinate of the vertex opposite to
\widetilde{E}
.
The situation on
{T}_{E_{-}}
is similar.
Therefore,

(25a)

\displaystyle\int_{E}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\d{S}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}\d{S}

(25b)

\displaystyle\int_{E}\bt_{E}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\d{S}

\displaystyle=-\int_{E}\bt_{E}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}\d{S}

are the degrees of freedom corresponding to the basis functions
\phi_{E,1}
,
\phi_{E,2}
,
which is closely related to the degrees of freedom of the standard Raviart–Thomas space from the literature (
8
).
Therefore, we define

(26)

\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma])\coloneqq\Span\bigcup_{E\in\cE}\set{\phi_{E,1},\phi_{E,2}}.

In the discrete
\operatorname{TGV}
(
10
) from
[
2
]
,
\ie
, the scalar-valued setting, the
\alpha_{0}
-terms evaluate the (discrete) total variation of the auxiliary variable
\bw\in\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}
.
When adapting the formulation to the normal vector of a mesh, the same has to be done for
W\in\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma])
.
It consists of the variation of
W
on each triangle as well as contributions from the edges.

Since the triangles are planar, their tangent spaces are constant and the parallel transports in (
21
) can be omitted.
Hence, the derivative of the matrix-valued
W
in a triangle
T
is a constant tensor of order three which is computed by standard techniques.
This defines a piecewise Jacobian of
W
, which we denote by
D_{\Gamma}W
.
Then,
\restr{\operatorname{D}_{\Gamma}W}{T}\in P_{0}(T,\tangentSpace{\bn_{T}}[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma]\otimes\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma])
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{D}_{\Gamma}\Phi_{E,1}(\bx)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{2\,\abs{{T}_{E_{+}}}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\otimes(\id-{\bn}_{E_{+}}{\bn}_{E_{+}}^{\transp})&\text{ if }\bx\in{T}_{E_{+}},\\
\tfrac{1}{2\,\abs{{T}_{E_{-}}}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}\otimes(\id-{\bn}_{E_{-}}{\bn}_{E_{-}}^{\transp})&\text{ if }\bx\in{T}_{E_{-}},\\
0&\text{ else },\end{cases}

\displaystyle\operatorname{D}_{\Gamma}\Phi_{E,2}(\bx)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mrep{\tfrac{1}{2\,\abs{{T}_{E_{+}}}}\bt_{E}\otimes(\id-{\bn}_{E_{+}}{\bn}_{E_{+}}^{\transp})}{\tfrac{1}{2\,\abs{{T}_{E_{+}}}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\otimes(\id-{\bn}_{E_{+}}{\bn}_{E_{+}}^{\transp})}&\text{ if }\bx\in{T}_{E_{+}},\\
\tfrac{-1}{2\,\abs{{T}_{E_{-}}}}\bt_{E}\otimes(\id-{\bn}_{E_{-}}{\bn}_{E_{-}}^{\transp})&\text{ if }\bx\in{T}_{E_{-}},\\
0&\text{ else}.\end{cases}

For the edge contribution, things will be more involved.
If two adjacent triangles
{T}_{E_{+}}
and
{T}_{E_{-}}
at an edge
E
are not co-planar, the tangent spaces will differ.
To measure the jump between
{W}_{E_{+}}
and
{W}_{E_{-}}
intrinsically, we proceed similarly as for the numerator in (
21
).
Hence, we require a parallel transport from
\tangentSpace{{T}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!\Gamma]
to
\tangentSpace{{T}_{E_{-}}}[\!\!\Gamma]
.
For this purpose, we again use the identification of
\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma]
and
\tangentSpace{\bn_{T}}[{\cS}]
and use the parallel transport along shortest geodesics on the sphere (
12a
).
This means that the jump of
W
, applied to a tangent vector
\bxi\in\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!{\cS}]
, across an edge is computed via

(27)

\parallelTransport[big]{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}({W}_{E_{-}}\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{\bxi})-{W}_{E_{+}}\bxi.

We examine the above term by inserting the orthonormal basis vectors
{\bmu}_{E_{+}},\bt_{E}
for
\bxi
.
When
\bxi={\bmu}_{E_{+}}
, the difference in (
27
) is zero due to the co-normal continuity (
22
) and using
\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}({\bmu}_{E_{+}})=-{\bmu}_{E_{-}}
.
When
\bxi=\bt_{E}
we obtain
\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}(\bt_{E})=\bt_{E}
and thus the inner parallel transport in (
27
) can be omitted.
We thus define the intrinsic jump as

(28)

\jump{W}_{E}\coloneqq\parallelTransport[big]{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}({W}_{E_{-}}\bt_{E})-{W}_{E_{+}}\bt_{E}\in\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!{\cS}].

3.4.
Discrete Total Generalized Variation of the Normal Vector Field

In the following, we adapt (
10
) from piecewise constant, scalar-valued functions
u\colon\Gamma\to\R
to the piecewise constant normal vector field
\bn\colon\Gamma\to{\cS}
.
For the
\alpha_{1}
-term, we couple the logarithmic map from the total variation of the normal vector formula (
18
) to the co-normal component of a function
W\in\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma])
.
Replacing the jump in (
10
) by its analogue for the normal vector, the
\alpha_{1}
-term on an edge
E\in\cE
becomes

(29)

\abs[big]{\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}+h_{E}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}_{2}.

Using the fact that
\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}
is a multiple of
{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
by
\cref
lemma:sphere:logarithmic-map:using-co-normals and that
\set{{\bmu}_{E_{+}},\bt_{E}}
form an orthonormal basis of
\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!{\cS}]
, this can be rewritten to

(30)

\abs[big]{\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}+h_{E}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}_{2}^{2}\\
=\abs[big]{{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}\paren(){\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}+h_{E}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}}^{2}+\abs[big]{h_{E}\,\bt_{E}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}^{2}.

This reveals that (
29
) in fact couples the logarithmic map only to one of the degrees of freedom of
W
on the edge, namely
{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
, while the other one,
\bt_{E}^{\transp}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
, is penalized.
The term
\bt_{E}^{\transp}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
being zero would mean that
{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
and
\bt_{E}
are principal directions of curvature, because in this basis, the matrix
{W}_{E_{+}}
is diagonal.

Since we aim to avoid that the local quantities

{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
and
\bt_{E}

become principal directions of curvature, we omit the last term in (
30
) and only use

(31)

\abs[big]{{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}\paren(){\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}+h_{E}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}}

for the coupling between the normal vector
\bn
and the auxiliary variable
W
.

For the
\alpha_{0}
-term we utilize the tangential Jacobian in the triangles as well as the tangential jump (
28
).
Overall, we propose the following formulation as the discrete total generalized variation of the normal vector field
\bn\colon\Gamma\to{\cS}
:

(32)

\displaystyle\textup{FETGV}_{(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1})}^{2}(\bn)

\displaystyle\min_{W\in\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma])}

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\sum_{E\in\cE}\int_{E}\abs[big]{{\bmu}_{E_{+}}^{\transp}\paren(){\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}+h_{E}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}}\d{S}

\displaystyle{}+{}

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\sum_{T\in\cT}\int_{T}\abs{\operatorname{D}_{\Gamma}W_{T}}_{F}\d{x}+\alpha_{0}\sum_{E\in\cE}\int_{E}\cI_{1}\enclose[big]{\{}{\abs[big]{\jump{W}_{E}}_{2}}{\}}\d{S}.

The reader is invited to compare this with the discrete total generalized variation (
10
) for a scalar quantity.
Here, the distance between the circumcenters,
h_{E}
, is measured intrinsically, as described in
[
2
, Section 5.2.3]
,
\ie
,

(33)

h_{E}\coloneqq{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\cdot(\bm_{E}-{\bm}_{E_{+}})+{\bmu}_{E_{-}}\cdot(\bm_{E}-{\bm}_{E_{-}}),

where
{\bm}_{E_{+}}
and
{\bm}_{E_{-}}
denote the circumcenters of the triangles
{T}_{E_{+}}
and
{T}_{E_{-}}
, and
\bm_{E}
denotes the midpoint of the edge.

Lemma 3.3
.

Formulation (
32
) is independent of the orientation of the edges.

Proof.

For the
\alpha_{1}
-term first notice that by
\cref
lemma:sphere:logarithmic-map:using-co-normals, we have

{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\cdot\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}={\bmu}_{E_{-}}\cdot\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}.

Thereby, using also the tangential co-normal continuity (
23
), it holds

\abs[big]{{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\cdot\paren(){\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}+h_{E}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}}=\abs[big]{{\bmu}_{E_{-}}\cdot\paren(){\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}+h_{E}\,{W}_{E_{-}}{\bmu}_{E_{-}}}}.

Clearly, the triangle contribution will be independent of the orientation if the sign of the degrees of freedom (
25
) are flipped accordingly.
For the last term, we can use that the parallel transport (
12
) is norm preserving and linear and thus

\displaystyle\abs[big]{\jump{W}_{E}}_{2}

\displaystyle=\abs[big]{\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}(\jump{W}_{E})}_{2}

\displaystyle=\abs[big]{\parallelTransport[big]{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}(\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}({W}_{E_{-}}\bt_{E}))-\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}({W}_{E_{+}}\bt_{E})}_{2}

\displaystyle=\abs[big]{{W}_{E_{-}}\bt_{E}-\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}({W}_{E_{+}}\bt_{E})}

\displaystyle=\abs[big]{\parallelTransport{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}({W}_{E_{+}}\bt_{E})-{W}_{E_{-}}\bt_{E}}.

The last quantity is precisely (
28
) with the roles of
{T}_{E_{+}}
and
{T}_{E_{-}}
swapped.
∎

4.
Numerical Realization

In this section we present a realization of the discrete total generalized variation of the normal (
32
) as a regularizer utilizing the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM).
This method is utilized to deal with the non-smoothness present in all problems involving total variation terms.
To formulate the ADMM, finite element spaces with values in tangent spaces are required, which will be introduced first.

4.1.
Tangential Finite Element Spaces

In addition to the standard spaces defined in
\cref
subsection:notation-and-finite-element-spaces, we define
\cD\cG_{0}(\Gamma,{\cS})
as the finite element space with constant,
{\cS}
-valued data on each triangle. In particular, the normal vector
\bn
to
\Gamma
belongs to
\cD\cG_{0}(\Gamma,{\cS})
. Furthermore, we define
\cD\cG_{0}(\cE,{\cS})
as the space with piecewise constant,
{\cS}
-valued data on edges.

Since
{\cS}
is embedded in
\R^{3}
, the tangent space
\tangentSpace{\bm}[{\cS}]
at a point
\bm\in{\cS}
is a subspace of
\R^{3}
.
Given the normal vector field
\bn\in\cD\cG_{0}(\Gamma,{\cS})
, we define
\cD\cG_{r}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}],\bn)
as

(34)

\cD\cG_{r}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}],\bn)\coloneqq\setDef[big]{\bu\in\cD\cG_{r}(\Gamma,\R^{3})}{\bu_{T}\in P_{r}(T,\tangentSpace{\bn_{T}}[{\cS}])\text{ for all }T\in\cT},

a subspace of
\cD\cG_{r}(T,\R^{3})
.
In other words,
\cD\cG_{r}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}],\bn)
consists of piecewise polynomials with values in the tangent space to the sphere
{\cS}
at the point specified by the normal vector in the respective triangle.
Similarly, we define
\cD\cG_{r}(\cE,\tangentBundle[{\cS}],{\bn}_{E_{+}})
as the subspace of
\cD\cG_{r}(\cE,\R^{3})
with values in
\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!{\cS}]
on each
E\in\cE
.

Recall that we identify the tangent space
\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma]
of a triangle
T
is denoted with
\tangentSpace{\bn_{T}}[{\cS}]
.
Therefore, we define
\cD\cG_{r}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[\Gamma],\bn)\coloneqq\cD\cG_{r}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}],\bn)
.
However, we continue to use both notations.

4.2.
Derivation of the ADMM

We consider the problem

(35)

\begin{aligned} \text{Minimize}\quad&\cF(\Gamma)+\alpha_{1}\sum_{E\in\cE}\int_{E}\abs[big]{{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\cdot\paren(){\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}+h_{E}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}}\d{S}\\
&+\alpha_{0}\sum_{T\in\cT}\int_{T}\abs{\operatorname{D}_{\Gamma}W_{T}}_{F}\d{x}+\alpha_{0}\sum_{E\in\cE}\int_{E}\cI_{1}\enclose[big]{\{}{\abs[big]{\jump{W}_{E}}_{2}}{\}}\d{S}\\
\text{\wrt\ }\quad&\Gamma\text{ and }W\in\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma]).\end{aligned}

where
\cF
is some smooth function depending on the mesh
\Gamma
and the remaining terms in the objective represent the discrete total generalized variation of the normal vector field
\bn
, see (
32
).

Optimizing over the mesh
\Gamma
actually means that we optimize over the vertex positions of
\Gamma
while preserving the connectivity.
The second optimization variable
W\in\cR\cT_{\!\!\;0}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma])
depends on the current vertex positions, and thus technically we are facing an optimization problem over a fiber bundle.
Notice that also the quantities
{\bmu}_{E_{+}}
,
{\bn}_{E_{+}}
and
{\bn}_{E_{-}}
depend on the vertex positions.

Following the ADMM paradigm of adding variables to achieve simpler subproblems, we introduce additional variables
d_{0}\in\cD\cG_{0}(\cE,\R)
,
\bd_{2}\in\cD\cG_{1}(\cE,\tangentBundle[{\cS}],{\bn}_{E_{+}})
defined on the skeleton
\cE
of the mesh, as well as
\bD_{1}\in\cD\cG_{0}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma],\bn)
defined on the triangles.
These variables are coupled to the original quantities of the problem via the following constraints:

(36a)

\displaystyle d_{0,E}

\displaystyle={\bmu}_{E_{+}}\cdot\paren(){\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}+h_{E}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}

\displaystyle\in P_{0}(E,\R)

(36b)

\displaystyle\bD_{1,T}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{D}_{\Gamma}W_{T}

\displaystyle\in P_{0}(T,\tangentSpace{\bn_{T}}[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma]\otimes\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma])

(36c)

\displaystyle\bd_{2,E}

\displaystyle=\jump{W}_{E}

\displaystyle\in P_{1}(E,\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!{\cS}])

on all edges
E\in\cE
and triangles
T\in\cT
respectively.
For these constraints, Lagrange multipliers
\lambda_{0}\in\cD\cG_{0}\paren[auto](){\cE,\R}
,
\bLambda_{1}\in\cD\cG_{0}\paren[auto](){\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma],\bn}
and
\blambda_{2}\in\cD\cG_{1}\paren[auto](){\cE,\tangentBundle[{\cS}],{\bn}_{E_{+}}}
are introduced.
That is, we represent Lagrange multipliers as primal objects rather than elements from the dual space of the constraint co-domain.
To adjoin the constraints and specify the norms for the penalty terms, we use the following inner products:

\displaystyle\abs{E}\,\lambda_{0,E}\,d_{0,E}

\displaystyle\quad\text{for }\lambda_{0,E},d_{0,E}\in P_{0}(E,\R),

\displaystyle\abs{T}\,\bLambda_{1,T}\dprod\bD_{1,T}\coloneqq\abs{T}\,\trace\paren[big](){\bLambda_{1,T}^{\transp}\bD_{1,T}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{for }\bLambda_{1,T},\bD_{1,T}\in P_{0}(T,\tangentSpace{\bn_{T}}[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma]\otimes\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma]),

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\abs{E}}{2}\,\blambda_{2,E}(X_{E,i})\cdot\bd_{2,E}(X_{E,i})

\displaystyle\quad\text{for }\blambda_{2},\bd_{2,E}\in P_{1}(E,\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!{\cS}]).

Recall that
X_{E,1},X_{E,2}
are the two endpoints of the edge
E
.
Notice that the interpolation operator has the same support points
X_{E,1},X_{E,2}
as the inner product, which will be exploited later.

Using these definitions as well as penalty parameters
\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\rho_{2}>0
, we obtain the following augmented Lagrangian function for the problem at hand:

\displaystyle\MoveEqLeft\cL_{\rho}(\Gamma,W,\bd_{0},\bD_{1},\bd_{2},\blambda_{0},\bLambda_{1},\blambda_{2})

\displaystyle=\cF(\Gamma)+\alpha_{1}\sum_{E\in\cE}\abs{E}\,\abs{d_{0,E}}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{0}\sum_{T\in\cT}\abs{T}\,\abs{\bD_{1,T}}_{F}+\alpha_{0}\sum_{E\in\cE}\smash{\sum_{i=1}^{2}}\frac{\abs{E}}{2}\,\abs[big]{\bd_{2}(X_{E,i})}_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\mrep[r]{{}+{}}{{}+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}}\sum_{E\in\cE}\abs{E}\,\lambda_{0,E}\,\paren[big][]{{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\cdot\paren(){\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}+h_{E}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}-d_{0,E}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\sum_{E\in\cE}\abs{E}\,\abs[big]{d_{0,E}-{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\cdot(\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}+h_{E}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}})}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\mrep[r]{{}+{}}{{}+\frac{\rho_{1}}{2}}\sum_{T\in\cT}\abs{T}\,\bLambda_{1,T}\dprod\paren[big][]{\operatorname{D}_{\Gamma}W_{T}-\bD_{1,T}}+\frac{\rho_{1}}{2}\sum_{T\in\cT}\abs{T}\,\abs[big]{\bD_{1,T}-\operatorname{D}_{\Gamma}W_{T}}_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\mrep[r]{{}+{}}{{}+\frac{\rho_{2}}{2}}\sum_{E\in\cE}\smash{\sum_{i=1}^{2}}\frac{\abs{E}}{2}\,\blambda_{2}(X_{E,i})\cdot\paren[big][]{\jump{W}_{E}(X_{E,i})-\bd_{2}(X_{E,i})}

(37)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\rho_{2}}{2}\sum_{E\in\cE}\smash{\sum_{i=1}^{2}}\frac{\abs{E}}{2}\,\abs[big]{\bd_{2}(X_{E,i})-\jump{W}_{E}(X_{E,i})}_{2}^{2}.

The ADMM for problem (
35

The minimization

\crefrange

Moreover, each problem further decouples into very small subproblems, one for each edge or triangle.
By completing the squares in (
37
), one is left with minimizing the following simple (non-smooth) objectives to compute the coefficients, denoted here by

,
\bD_{1,T}
,
\bd_{2}(X_{E,1})
and

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\,\abs{d_{0,E}}_{2}+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\abs[Big]{d_{0,E}-{\bmu}_{E_{+}}\cdot\paren(){\logarithm{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{{\bn}_{E_{-}}}+h_{E}\,{W}_{E_{+}}{\bmu}_{E_{+}}}-\frac{\lambda_{0,E}}{\rho_{0}}}_{2}^{2}\quad\text{\wrt\ }d_{0,E}\in\R,

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\,\abs{\bD_{1,T}}_{F}+\frac{\rho_{1}}{2}\,\abs[Big]{\bD_{1,T}-\operatorname{D}_{\Gamma}W_{T}-\frac{\bLambda_{1,T}}{\rho_{1}}}_{F}^{2}\quad\text{\wrt\ }\bD_{1,T}\in\tangentSpace{\bn_{T}}[\Gamma]\otimes\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma]\otimes\tangentSpace{T}[\Gamma],

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\,\abs[big]{\bd_{2}(X_{E,i})}_{2}+\frac{\rho_{2}}{2}\,\abs[Big]{\bd_{2}(X_{E,i})-\jump{W}_{E}(X_{E,i})-\frac{\blambda_{2}(X_{E,i})}{\rho_{2}}}_{2}^{2}\quad\text{\wrt\ }\bd_{2}(X_{E,i})\in\tangent{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}[\!\!{\cS}]

(38)

\text{Minimize}\quad\alpha\,\abs{d}_{*}+\frac{\rho}{2}\abs{d-x}_{*}^{2}\quad\text{\wrt\ }d

on a vector space with some norm
\abs{\,\cdot\,}_{*}
induced by an inner product.
The solution is given by the soft-thresholding (shrinkage) operator

(39)

\operatorname{shrink}\enclose[Big]{(}{x,\;\frac{\alpha}{\rho}}{)}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{x}{\abs{x}_{*}}\max\set{\abs{x}_{*}-\frac{\alpha}{\rho},\;0}&\text{if }x\neq 0,\\
0&\text{if }x=0.\end{cases}

The minimization
\wrt

We use a conjugate gradient method to solve this problem.

Approximate Solution with Respect to the Vertex Coordinates

In the augmented Lagrangian (
37
), the auxiliary variable

, additional variables
\bD_{1},\bd_{2}
and multipliers
\bLambda_{1},\blambda_{2}
are required to be elements of the respective tangent spaces corresponding to the piecewise constant normal vector
\bn
, which in turn depends on the optimization variable
\Gamma
.
For the minimization
\wrt
\Gamma
in
\cref
line:ADMM:shape-problem of
\cref
algorithm:ADMM with
\enquote
fixed
\sequence{W}{k+1}
,
\sequence{d_{0}}{k+1}
,
\sequence{\bD_{1}}{k+1}
,
\sequence{\bd_{2}}{k+1}
,
\sequence{\lambda_{0}}{k+1}
,
\sequence{\bLambda_{1}}{k+1}
,
\sequence{\blambda_{2}}{k+1}
, one therefore has to define how these variables behave when the mesh is updated.

In case of

, the basis functions (

We simply leave the coefficients unchanged.
For
\sequence{\bD_{1}}{k+1},\sequence{\bLambda_{1}}{k+1}\in\cD\cG_{0}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma],\sequence{\bn}{k})
and
\sequence{\bd_{2}}{k+1},\sequence{\blambda_{2}}{k+1}\in\cD\cG_{1}(\cE,\tangentBundle[{\cS}],\sequence{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{k})
parallel transports to the tangent spaces after the update will be used.
This is discussed further in the respective paragraph for the parallel transports.

Even with these dependencies, the minimization of the augmented Lagrangian
\cL_{\rho}
with respect to
\Gamma
is still smooth and can be carried out by standard techniques of unconstrained optimization.
In particular we use a truncated Newton scheme as described,
\eg
, in
[
27
, p.49]
.
First- and second-order derivatives of the augmented Lagrangian with respect to the mesh coordinates are evaluated using a combination of algorithmic differentiation (AD) and hand-coded derivatives.
The implementation details match those in our recent publication
[
3
, Section 4]
, which only differs in the objective function.

Parallel Transport

As described in the previous paragraph, the minimization of the augmented Lagrangian with respect to
\Gamma
in
\cref
line:ADMM:shape-problem requires parallel transports of the
\enquote
fixed variables
\sequence{\bD_{1}}{k+1},\sequence{\bLambda_{1}}{k+1}\in\cD\cG_{0}(\Gamma,\tangentBundle[{\cS}]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma]\otimes\tangentBundle[\Gamma],\sequence{\bn}{k})
and
\sequence{\bd_{2}}{k+1},\sequence{\blambda_{2}}{k+1}\in\cD\cG_{1}(\cE,\tangentBundle[{\cS}],\sequence{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{k})
from tangent spaces corresponding to the previous iterate
\sequence{\Gamma}{k}
to tangent spaces corresponding to the current
\Gamma
.
For
\sequence{\bd_{2}}{k+1}
this is achieved by replacing every occurrence of
\sequence{\bd_{2}}{k+1}(X_{E,i})
in the augmented Lagrangian (
37
) by
\parallelTransport[big]{\sequence{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}{k}}{{\bn}_{E_{+}}}(\sequence{\bd_{2}}{k+1}(X_{E,i}))
.
Here,
\sequence{\bn}{k}\in\cD\cG_{0}(\Gamma,{\cS})
is the normal vector field of
\sequence{\Gamma}{k}
and
\bn
is the normal vector field of the optimization variable
\Gamma
.
We replace
\sequence{\blambda_{2}}{k+1}(X_{E,i})
analogously.

For the tensor-valued quantity
\sequence{\bD_{1}}{k+1}
, the parallel transport is slightly more complicated.
As seen in (
12b
), the action of the parallel transport from
\sequence{\bn}{k}
to
\bn
can be represented elementwise by the matrix

(40)

M\coloneqq\paren[Big](){\id-\frac{\bn_{T}+\sequence{\bn_{T}}{k}}{1+\bn_{T}\cdot\sequence{\bn_{T}}{k}}\bn_{T}^{\transp}}.

This matrix is simply applied to each axis of the tensor, which amounts to

(41)

\paren[auto](){\parallelTransport{\sequence{\bn}{k}}{\bn}(\bD_{1})}_{ijk}\coloneqq\sum_{a,b,c=1}^{3}(\bD_{1})_{abc}\,M_{ia}\,M_{jb}\,M_{kc}.

Then, as before, every occurrence of
\sequence{\bD_{1,T}}{k+1}
in the augmented Lagrangian (
37
) is replaced by
\parallelTransport[big]]{\sequence{\bn_{T}}{k}}{\bn_{T}}(\sequence{\bD_{1,T}}{k+1})
(analogously
\bLambda_{1,T}
).

After the update of
\Gamma
by
\cref
line:ADMM:shape-problem, the variables
\sequence{\bD_{1}}{k+1}
,
\sequence{\bLambda_{1}}{k+1}
,
\sequence{\bd_{2}}{k+1}
,
\sequence{\blambda_{2}}{k+1}
are parallely transported to tangent spaces corresponding to the new iterate
\sequence{\Gamma}{k+1}
in
\cref
line:ADMM:parallel-transport.
Thereby, the coefficients of the variables are changed in order to correspond to the respective mesh iterate
\sequence{\Gamma}{k+1}
.

Multiplier Update

Finally,
\crefrange
line:ADMM:lambda0-updateline:ADMM:lambda2-update of
\cref
algorithm:ADMM are standard multiplier updates of ADMM.

5.
Numerical Results for Mesh Denoising

In this section, we present numerical experiments for mesh denoising problems.
We compare the proposed total generalized variation of the normal regularizer (
32
) to first-order total variation regularization (
17
) as described in
[
3
]
as well as to alternate formulations from
[
21
,
29
]
.
In the
\alpha_{1}
-term of their respective formulation, both
[
21
]
and
[
29
]
couple the Euclidean difference of the normal vectors of two adjacent triangles to an auxiliary variable in
\cD\cG_{0}(\cE,\R^{3})
.
The authors of
[
29
]
then proceed to use the approach similar as in
[
15
]
for images and utilize a divergence like operator for the
\alpha_{0}
-term by adding up the values of the auxiliary variable on the three edges of a triangle.
Unlike the approach in
[
15
]
, however, they add an additional weight to each term in order to be closer to a full derivative and avoid spurious oscillations.
The authors of
[
21
]
use different combinations of edge values over larger patches of adjacent triangles to obtain more accurate derivative information from the auxiliary variable.

Both methods use a normal filtering approach to realize their formulation for mesh denoising. This means that first, the problem

(42)

\mathop{\operatorname{Minimize}}\limits_{\bm\in\cD\cG_{0}(\Gamma,\R^{3})}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{T\in\cT}\abs{\bm_{T}-\bn_{T}}_{2}^{2}+\cR(\bm)

is solved on the noisy mesh, where
\cR
is the respective variant of total generalized variation for piecewise constant data from
[
21
]
or
[
29
]
.
Then, the vertex positions are adapted such that the normal vector
\bn
of
\Gamma
is similar to the optimized variable
\bm
; see also
[
31
,
26
]
.

We on the other hand do not use normal filtering and instead optimize the vertex positions of the mesh directly.
The objective function we use for the purpose of mesh denoising is

(43)

\cF(\Gamma)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in\cV}\abs{x_{v}-x^{\text{data}}_{v}}_{2}^{2}+\tau\sum_{T\in\cT}\frac{1}{\abs{T}}.

The first term is a squared
\ell_{2}
fidelity term, and the second term is a barrier term that avoids degenerately small triangles, as used in
[
3
]
.
The proposed total generalized variation term (
32
) with parameters
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}
is added to the objective.
For comparison, we also consider denoising using the first-order total variation of the normal with parameter
\beta
using the method from
[
3
]
.
The values of
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}
or
\beta
and
\tau
are to be balanced so that the regularizer term dominates unless triangles become extremely small.
Here, we always use
\tau=10^{-12}
.
The implementation was achieved in the finite element framework
\fenics
, version 2019.2.0.

6.
Conclusion

We propose a discrete, intrinsic formulation of the second-order total generalized variation (TGV) of the normal vector field of oriented, triangulated meshes embedded in
\R^{3}
.
Particular attention is given to the differentially geometric consequences arising from the fact that the normal vector is an element of the unit sphere.
To capture the derivative information via an auxiliary variable
W
, we introduce a new tangential Raviart–Thomas space.
At every point, a function of this space represents a mapping from the tangent space of the mesh to the tangent space of the sphere and thus matches the push-forward operator of the normal vector.

To solve minimization problems involving the new TGV regularizer, we derive an alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) capable of treating the non-smoothness of the problem.
We compare our approach to the extrinsic variants of discrete total generalized variation of the normal vector field from
[
21
]
and
[
29
]
, which treat the normal vector as an element of
\R^{3}
rather than as an element of the unit sphere.
We also compare to first-order total variation of the normal
[
3
]
for mesh denoising problems.
While all second-order
\operatorname{TGV}
models successfully remove most of the starcasing effect in the half-cylinder test case (
\cref
figure:cylinders) that would be produced by the first-order TV regularizer, our method
\textup{FETGV}^{2}
performs favorably compared to meshTGV
[
21
]
and rTGV
[
29
]
in the general case where both principal curvature are non-vanishing, as demonstrated by the hemisphere example (
\cref
figure:spheres).
For real-world geometries, the differences between the
\textup{FETGV}^{2}
, meshTGV and rTGV are less pronounced.

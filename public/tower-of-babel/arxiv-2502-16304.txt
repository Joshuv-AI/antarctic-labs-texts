Polygraphic resolutions for operated algebras
Zuan Liu - Philippe Malbos

Abstract




1. Introduction

An
operated algebra
, also called an
\Omega
-algebra
, is an associative algebra with linear operators.
For example,
differential algebras
, introduced by Ritt
[
41
,
40
]
in the theory of differential equations, are operated algebras, with an operator satisfying the Leibniz rule.
Differential algebras have been developed in many areas, such as differential Galois theory
[
37
,
48
]
and differential algebraic group theory
[
34
]
.

Rota–Baxter algebras
form another important class of operated algebras, introduced by Baxter
[
6
]
; see also
[
42
,
12
,
3
]
.
They generalize the algebra of continuous functions through integral operators.
These algebras have found applications across various fields of mathematics and physics, including renormalization in quantum field theory
[
26
]
, the analysis of Volterra integral equations
[
27
]
, Hopf algebras
[
15
]
, Yang–Baxter equations
[
4
]
, and Rota–Baxter Lie algebras
[
29
]
.

Differential Rota–Baxter algebras
are equipped with both differential and integral operators.
Introduced by Guo and Keigher
[
28
]
, they describe the relationship between such operators satisfying the first fundamental theorem of calculus.
The homological study of operated algebras has been the subject of several works.
In particular, homological properties such as Koszul duality, minimal models, deformations, and cohomology theory have been explored in
[
44
,
49
,
13
]
for Rota–Baxter and differential algebras.
These algebras have also been studied from a computational perspective, using the theory of Gröbner–Shirshov bases
[
7
,
30
,
36
,
18
,
35
]
.

This paper introduces a new rewriting method to compute resolutions of operated algebras.
Rewriting is a computational model widely used in algebra, logic and computer science. It consists of calculating in an equational theory by generating the set of equalities by means of a system of
rewriting rules
, and sequentially applying the rules that replace the subterms of a formula by other terms.
In algebra, rewriting theory enables the calculation of linear bases
[
9
,
47
]
and resolutions of algebraic structures in abelian and categorical settings
[
1
,
32
,
33
,
19
,
8
,
23
]
. For operated algebras,
Gröbner–Shirshov bases
, GS bases for short, introduced in
[
43
,
10
]
, are frequently used alongside rewriting methods for the standardization of ideal representations in free operated algebras, yielding linear bases and other related constructions
[
7
,
17
,
30
,
39
]
. GS methods require a
monomial order
compatible with the rewriting rules, ensuring the termination of calculations. Nevertheless, finding a monomial order for an operated algebra is more complicated than in the associative case due to the nesting of operators in monomials
[
17
,
36
,
7
]
.

The aim of this paper is twofold: first, it introduces a new termination method in operated algebras without relying on monomial orders, and second, it introduces the notion of
polygraphic resolutions
for operated algebras.
The linear rewriting strategies introduced in
[
21
]
allow one to compute resolutions of an associative algebra by extending its rewriting presentation into a polygraphic resolution generated by confluence diagrams of the higher overlapping of rules.
We extend this approach to operated algebras by introducing the structure of
\Omega
-polygraphs
as a rewriting setting for operated algebras. It can be used to derive their linear bases and resolutions.
This construction is part of a line of research on constructing polygraphic resolutions of algebraic structures from their convergent presentations, e.g. for monoids and categories
[
24
,
23
]
, associative algebras
[
21
]
and operads
[
38
]
.

We now present the main results of this paper.
The first part consists of setting up the polygraphic framework for
\Omega
-algebras.
In Section
2
, we introduce the structure of
higher
\Omega
-algebras
, hereafter called
\omega
-algebras
, as internal
\omega
-categories in the category of
\Omega
-algebras.
Using the underlying linear and operator structures, Proposition
2.2.9
characterizes
\omega
-algebras in terms of globular bimodules over
\Omega
-algebras.
Following this characterization, Subsection
2.3
introduces the notion of
\Omega
-polygraphs
as systems of generators and relations for presentations of
\omega
-algebras. This construction is an extension of the structure of linear polygraphs introduced for associative algebras in
[
21
]
.

In Section
3
, we develop rewriting systems for
\Omega
-algebras based on
\Omega
-
1
-polygraphs.
We define rewriting properties such as
termination
and
confluence
on
\Omega
-
1
-polygraphs. We show that this polygraphic approach allows us to define more general termination orders than those used in the theory of GS bases; see Remark
3.3.5
.
Proposition
3.1.7
proves termination of an
\Omega
-
1
-polygraph using the method of
derivations
introduced in
[
20
,
22
]
. Proposition
3.2.8
states that for
convergent

\Omega
-
1
-polygraphs, which are both confluent and terminating, the set of
normal forms
is a linear basis for the presented algebra.
Finally, we classify the
local branchings
of
\Omega
-
1
-polygraphs, and state the coherent critical branching lemma in Theorem
3.2.7
. In Subsection
3.3
, we make explicit the relationship between GS bases of
\Omega
-algebras and convergent
\Omega
-1-polygraphs.

Section
4
presents the main result of this paper.
Starting with an
\Omega
-algebra presented by a reduced convergent left-monomial
\Omega
-1-polygraph
X
, Theorem
4.2.5
constructs its polygraphic resolution
à la Squier

\mathrm{Sq}(X)
.
The
0
-generators and
1
-generators of the
\omega
-polygraph
\mathrm{Sq}(X)
consist of the variables and the rewriting rules defining the algebra, respectively. For
n\geq 2
, the
n
-generators are the sources of the critical
n
-branchings of the polygraph
X
. An associative algebra being an
\Omega
-algebra with
\Omega
empty, our polygraphic resolution generalizes the corresponding construction for associative algebras
[
21
]
.

Due to possible nesting of operators, the critical branchings of
\Omega
-
1
-polygraphs cannot be described in terms of string overlaps; see Remark
3.2.5
.
In order to interpret these critical branchings as critical branchings of linear
1
-polygraphs, in Section
5
, we describe the operator structures using
pushdown automata (PDAs)
.
Explicitly, we construct a PDA
\mathbb{A}_{\Omega}
accepting all monomials of the
\Omega
-algebras defined with respect to an indexed set
\Omega
. Given a linear 1-polygraph
\Sigma
and a PDA
\mathbb{A}
, we define a
1
-polyautomaton
as a pair
(\Sigma,\mathbb{A})
, where both
s(\alpha)
and
t(\alpha)
are linear combinations of monomials accepted by the PDA
\mathbb{A}
, for every
\alpha\in\Sigma_{1}
. Theorem
5.2.8
interprets critical branchings in
\Omega
-
1
-polygraphs in terms of critical branchings of linear
1
-polygraphs.
It thus provides a simpler description of the higher critical branchings involved in polygraphic resolutions, and yields an algorithmic method for constructing such resolutions.

In Section
6
, we apply Theorem
4.2.5
to construct polygraphic resolutions for Rota–Baxter algebras, differential algebras, and differential Rota–Baxter algebras. To this end, we construct reduced and convergent
\Omega
-1-polygraphs for these
\Omega
-algebras by defining derivations to prove termination and applying the critical branchings theorem to prove confluence.

Organization of the paper

Section
2
recalls the main constructions of
\Omega
-algebras used in this work.
It also introduces the structure of higher
\Omega
-algebras and
\Omega
-polygraphs.
Section
3
deals with the rewriting properties of
\Omega
-1-polygraphs, including the construction of derivations for termination proofs, the operated version of the coherent critical branchings lemma, and comparisons with Gröbner–Shirshov bases.
Section
4
introduces polygraphic resolutions of
\Omega
-algebras.
Given an
\Omega
-
\omega
-polygraph, we characterize its property of being a resolution by the existence of a contraction defined on its generators. We then show how to construct such a contraction starting with a reduced convergent
\Omega
-
1
-polygraph.
Section
5
introduces the structure of polyautomata, encoding both the associative linear structure and the operator structure to establish a connection between
\Omega
-
1
-polygraphs and linear
1
-polygraphs.
This correspondence allows us to interpret the critical branchings of
\Omega
-
1
-polygraphs in terms of those of polygraphs of associative algebras.
Finally, Section
6
presents constructions of polygraphic resolutions for some classical
\Omega
-algebras.

Conventions and notations

Throughout this paper, we fix a field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero and an element
\lambda
in
\Bbbk
.
Unless stated otherwise, all algebras in this paper are assumed to be associative and unital.

An
\Omega

When

\Bbbk

A

\Omega
-objects

is a morphism

in
\mathcal{C}
such that

{\mathcal{U}}(f)\circ\mathcal{T}_{\tau}=\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\tau}\circ{\mathcal{U}}(f),

for every
\tau\in\Omega
.
The
\Omega
-objects and their morphisms form a category, denoted by
\Omega\text{-}\mathcal{C}
.

In this paper, we will consider
\operatorname{\Omega\text{-}\mathsf{Set}}
,
\operatorname{\Omega\text{-}\mathsf{Mon}}
,
\operatorname{\Omega\text{-}\mathsf{Vect}}
, and
\Omega\text{-}\mathsf{Alg}{}
as the categories of
\Omega
-objects in the categories
\mathsf{Set}
,
\mathsf{Mon}
,
\mathsf{Vect}
, and
\mathsf{Alg}{}
, respectively.
Note that, when

2.1.2. Free
\Omega
-algebras

The
free
\Omega
-set
on a set
Z
is given by
\Omega^{\ast}\times Z
,
where
\Omega^{\ast}
is the free monoid on
\Omega
.
Its operators are defined, for every
\tau\in\Omega
, by

\mathcal{T}_{\tau}(w,z)\coloneq(\tau w,z),

for all
w\in\Omega^{\ast}
and
z\in Z
.

The
free
\Omega
-monoid

Z^{\Omega}
on
Z
is given as follows.
Following
[
39
]
, we write
\Omega\times Z
as

\lfloor Z\rfloor_{\Omega}\>\coloneq\bigsqcup_{\tau\in\Omega}\lfloor Z\rfloor_{\tau},

where
\lfloor Z\rfloor_{\tau}
denotes the set of formal symbols
\lfloor z\rfloor_{\tau}
with
z\in Z
.
We set
Z^{\Omega}_{0}\coloneq Z^{\ast}
and
Z^{\Omega}_{1}\coloneq(Z\sqcup\lfloor Z^{\Omega}_{0}\rfloor_{\Omega})^{\ast}
.
The inclusion
Z\hookrightarrow Z\sqcup\lfloor Z^{\Omega}_{0}\rfloor_{\Omega}
induces an injective morphism

i_{0,1}:Z^{\Omega}_{0}\hookrightarrow Z^{\Omega}_{1}
.
For
1\leq k\leq n-1
, suppose
Z^{\Omega}_{k}
has been constructed with injective morphisms
i_{k-1,k}:Z^{\Omega}_{k-1}\hookrightarrow Z^{\Omega}_{k}
.
We then set

Z^{\Omega}_{n}\>\coloneq(Z\sqcup\lfloor Z^{\Omega}_{n-1}\rfloor_{\Omega})^{\ast}.

The inclusion
Z\sqcup\lfloor Z^{\Omega}_{n-2}\rfloor_{\Omega}\hookrightarrow Z\sqcup\lfloor Z^{\Omega}_{n-1}\rfloor_{\Omega}
also induces an injective morphism

i_{n-1,n}:Z^{\Omega}_{n-1}\hookrightarrow Z^{\Omega}_{n}
.
By construction, we have
Z^{\Omega}_{i}\subset Z^{\Omega}_{i+1}
for
i\geq 0
, and we define
Z^{\Omega}\coloneq\varinjlim Z^{\Omega}_{n}
. The maps sending
u\in Z^{\Omega}_{n}
to
\lfloor u\rfloor_{\tau}\in Z^{\Omega}_{n+1}
induce a family of operators
\left\lfloor\penalty\ \right\rfloor=(\lfloor\penalty\ \rfloor_{\tau})_{\tau\in\Omega}
on
Z^{\Omega}
.
We show below that
(Z^{\Omega},\lfloor\,\rfloor)
is the free
\Omega
-monoid on
Z
.

If
(X,\mathcal{T})
is an
\Omega
-monoid and

f:Z^{\ast}\to X
is a monoid morphism, we prove that there exists a unique

\Omega
-monoid morphism
\tilde{f}:Z^{\Omega}\to X
whose restriction to

Z^{\ast}
is
f
.
It suffices to construct a compatible family of monoid
morphisms
\tilde{f}_{i}:Z^{\Omega}_{i}\to X
, for
i\geq 0
. Set

\tilde{f}_{0}=f
. Assume that
\tilde{f}_{k}:Z^{\Omega}_{k}\to X
has been
constructed for some
k\geq 0
. Since
Z^{\Omega}_{k+1}
is the free
monoid on
Z\sqcup\lfloor Z^{\Omega}_{k}\rfloor_{\Omega}
, there exists a
unique monoid morphism
\tilde{f}_{k+1}:Z^{\Omega}_{k+1}\to X
such that

\tilde{f}_{k+1}(z)=f(z)
and
\tilde{f}_{k+1}(\lfloor v\rfloor_{\tau})=\mathcal{T}_{\tau}(\tilde{f}_{k}(v))
,
for all
z\in Z
,
v\in Z^{\Omega}_{k}
, and
\tau\in\Omega
.
These morphisms are compatible with the inclusions

Z^{\Omega}_{k}\hookrightarrow Z^{\Omega}_{k+1}
and therefore induce a unique
monoid morphism

\tilde{f}:Z^{\Omega}=\varinjlim_{k\geq 0}Z^{\Omega}_{k}\longrightarrow X.

By construction,
\tilde{f}
is an
\Omega
-monoid morphism extending

f
. Therefore,
Z^{\Omega}
is the free
\Omega
-monoid on
Z
, and the
construction defines the left adjoint to the forgetful functor

\operatorname{\Omega\text{-}\mathsf{Mon}}\to\mathsf{Set}
.

For
u\in Z^{\Omega}
, we define
\lfloor u\rfloor_{0}\coloneq u
, where
0
is a new element not included in
\Omega
.
An
\Omega
-monomial

u
is a non-identity element of
Z^{\Omega}
, which can be uniquely written as
u=u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{n}
, where
u_{i}\in Z\sqcup\left\lfloor Z^{\Omega}\right\rfloor_{\Omega}
and
n
is the
breadth
of
u
, denoted by
\operatorname{bre}(u)=n
.
The
depth
of
u
is defined by

\operatorname{dep}(u)\>\coloneq\min\left\{n\mid u\in Z^{\Omega}_{n}\right\}.

We denote by
(\mathscr{A}_{\Omega}(Z),\left\lfloor\penalty\ \right\rfloor)
the
free
\Omega
-algebra
on
Z
, defined as the
\Bbbk
-linear span of
\Omega
-monomials in
Z^{\Omega}
, and whose each operator
\lfloor\penalty\ \rfloor_{\tau}
is induced by linearity.
This defines a functor
\mathscr{A}_{\Omega}(-):\mathsf{Set}\rightarrow\Omega\text{-}\mathsf{Alg}
, left adjoint to the forgetful functor
\Omega\text{-}\mathsf{Alg}\rightarrow\mathsf{Set}
.

2.1.5. Operated contexts

Let
\square
be a symbol not in
Z
.
The set of
(one-hole)
\Omega
-contexts

For

The

2.1.6. Operated ideals

An
\Omega

We denote by

If

is the free associative algebra, and

If

\Omega
-1-polygraph.

The converse of Proposition

For example, the

3.3.6. Completion procedure

When
X
is a non-confluent terminating
\Omega
-1-polygraph, we can complete the set of
1
-generators of
X
, without changing the presented algebra, in order to reach confluence.
This
completion procedure
is well known in rewriting theory; see

[
9
]
for commutative algebras and
[
31
]
for term rewriting.
Starting with a monomial order
\prec
compatible with
X_{1}
, the procedure examines each non-confluent critical branching
(f,g)
in
X
, and reduces
t(f)
and
t(g)
to some normal forms
\widehat{t(f)}
and
\widehat{t(g)}
.
A new
1
-generator
\lm_{\prec}(a)\rightarrow-\lambda^{-1}a+\lm_{\prec}(a)
is then added to the polygraph, where
a=\widehat{t(f)}-\widehat{t(g)}
and
\lambda
is the coefficient of
\lm_{\prec}(a)
in
a
.
When it terminates, the procedure produces a terminating
\Omega
-1-polygraph

such that
\overline{X}\cong\overline{Y}
.

This subsection presents the main result of this paper, Theorem

, which constructs a polygraphic resolution for an
\Omega
-algebra from a convergent presentation.
Such a resolution
à la Squier
is generated in each dimension

-monomials

-polygraph

-reduced

\Omega
-monomials of

4.2.3. Proposition.

Let
X
be an
\Omega
-
\omega
-polygraph with a fixed unital section
\iota
. Then
X
is a polygraphic
resolution of
\overline{X}
if and only if it admits a right and bracketed
\iota
-contraction.

Proof.

Assume that
X
is a polygraphic resolution of
\overline{X}
. We define a right and bracketed
\iota
-contraction using Lemma
4.2.2
.
For an essential monomial
xv
, both
xv
and
\widehat{xv}
map to the same element in
\mathscr{A}_{\Omega}(X_{0})
, so there exists a 1-cell
\sigma_{xv}:xv\to\widehat{xv}
in
\mathscr{A}_{\Omega}(X)
.
Similarly, for an essential monomial of the form
\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor v
,
\sigma_{\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor v}
is defined.
Assume that
\sigma
is defined on the
n
-cells of
\mathscr{A}_{\Omega}(X)
, for
n\geq 1
, and let
\alpha v
be a
\sigma
-essential
n
-monomial of
\mathscr{A}_{\Omega}(X)
.
By hypothesis,
s(\sigma_{\alpha v})
and

are parallel, ensuring the existence of an
(n+1)
-cell
\sigma_{\alpha v}
.

Conversely, for
n\geq 1
, let
\sigma
be a right and bracketed
\iota
-contraction, and let

and
g
be parallel
n
-cells in
\mathscr{A}_{\Omega}(X)
. We show that

, ensuring that the
(n+1)
-cell
\sigma_{f}\star_{n}\sigma_{g}^{-}
is well-defined.
The fact that

for all

\sigma_{f}\star_{n}\sigma_{g}^{-}\star_{n-1}\sigma_{t_{n-1}(f)}^{-}\star_{n-2}\cdots\star_{0}\sigma_{t_{0}(f)}^{-}

is a well-defined

\mathscr{A}_{\Omega}(X)
with the source

and target

is acyclic.
∎

In the following, we assume that each 0-generator of

is a normal form; if not, we reduce this polygraph to a smaller one using a collapsing mechanism from
[

, Sec. 5.3]
.

5.1. Pushdown automata

Automata are models of computation based on the notion of state.
Among them,
pushdown automata (PDAs)
use a last-in-first-out stack to process
context-free languages
. Their state transitions depend on both the input symbol and the top of the stack, enabling them to handle nested structures such as operator brackets.
In our setting, the stack records the operator
brackets that have been opened but not yet closed, allowing the PDA to verify
the nesting structure of operators.

5.1.1.

A

\Sigma_{0}
is a tuple

, where:

i)

is a finite set of internal states.

ii)

\Sigma_{0}
is the input alphabet.

iii)

\Gamma
is a finite set of symbols called the

.

iv)

is the state transition function, where
\varepsilon
is the empty word and
\$
is the initial bottom-of-stack marker with
\$\notin\Gamma
. In a given state, the PDA reads both the input symbol and the top symbol of the stack, then transitions to a new state and updates the top of the stack.

v)

q_{0}\in Q
is the initial state.

vi)

is the set of accepting states.

A

is a word

in

is convergent.

We write

6.2.2. Lemma.

The polygraphs
\widetilde{X}^{D}
and
X^{D}
are Tietze equivalent.

Proof.

The polygraph
\widetilde{X}^{D}
is convergent since it contains no critical branchings. We prove this lemma in two steps.
First, we show that
\Nf(\widetilde{X}^{D})=\Nf(X^{D})
. It suffices to prove that
Q(X^{D})=Q(\widetilde{X}^{D})
, where

Q(X^{D})\>\coloneq\>\{\left.q\right|_{D(uv)}|u,v\in X_{0}^{\Omega}\setminus\{1\}\}\quad\text{ and }\quad Q(\widetilde{X}^{D})\>\coloneq\>\{\left.q\right|_{D(u_{1}\cdots u_{n})}|u_{i}\in D^{\theta}(X_{0})\setminus\{1\},n\geq 2\}.

The inclusion
Q(\widetilde{X}^{D})\subset Q(X^{D})
is straightforward.
For every
\left.q\right|_{D(u_{1}\cdots u_{n})}\in Q(X^{D})
with
\operatorname{bre}(u_{i})=1
, if all
u_{i}\in D^{\theta}(X_{0})\setminus\{1\}
, then it belongs to
Q(\widetilde{X}^{D})
. If not, suppose
u_{1}=D(v_{1}\cdots v_{m})\in Q(X^{D})
with
\operatorname{bre}(v_{i})=1
, and repeat this process for
D(v_{1}\cdots v_{m})
. Eventually, we obtain
\left.q\right|_{D(u_{1}\cdots u_{n})}=\left.q^{\prime}\right|_{D(w_{1}\cdots w_{k})}
, where
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\in D^{\theta}(X_{0})\setminus\{1\}
, which shows that
Q(X^{D})\subset Q(\widetilde{X}^{D})
.

Next, we prove that
\Nf(w,X^{D})=\Nf(w,\widetilde{X}^{D})
, for every
w\in X_{0}^{\Omega}
.
We first consider the case
w=D(u)
, with
u\in X_{0}^{\Omega}
. There exists a decomposition
D(u)=D(s_{1}\cdots s_{n})
, with
\operatorname{bre}(s_{i})=1
, and we proceed by induction on
\max(\operatorname{dep}(s_{i}))
. For the case
\max(\operatorname{dep}(s_{i}))=0
, meaning that all
s_{i}\in X_{0}
,

D(u)=D(s_{1}\cdots s_{n})\rightarrow\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n\\
1\leq k\leq n\end{subarray}}\lambda^{k-1}D_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}),

in
X^{D}
.
It follows that
\Nf(D(u),X^{D})=\Nf(D(u),\widetilde{X}^{D})
.
Now, we assume that it holds for
\max(\operatorname{dep}(s_{i}))\leq m
. If
\max(\operatorname{dep}(s_{i}))=m+1
, then
s_{j}=D(v_{j})
with
\operatorname{dep}(v_{j})=m
for some
1\leq v_{j}\leq n
. By the induction hypothesis,
\Nf(v_{j},X^{D})=\Nf(v_{j},\widetilde{X}^{D})
holds, implying that the normal forms of
D(u)
are equal in both
X^{D}
and
\widetilde{X}^{D}
.
So, for every
w=w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{n}\in X_{0}^{\Omega}
with
\operatorname{bre}(w_{i})=1
, we have
\Nf(w_{i},X^{D})=\Nf(w_{i},\widetilde{X}^{D})
for every

, and thus
\Nf(w,X^{D})=\Nf(w,\widetilde{X}^{D})
.
Hence,
\widetilde{X}^{D}
and
X^{D}
are Tietze equivalent.
∎

\displaystyle:D(w_{1})P(v)\rightarrow\widehat{D(w_{1}P(v))}+\widehat{w_{1}v}+\lambda\widehat{D(w_{1})v},

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle:D(1)\rightarrow 0\mid u,v,w_{1},w_{2}\in\Phi_{PD}\text{ and }w_{1},w_{2}\neq 1\}.

It follows that
\Nf(X^{PD})=\Nf(\widetilde{X}^{PD})=\Phi_{PD}
.

6.3.5. Theorem.

When
\lambda\neq 0
, the algebra
\mathcal{D}\mathcal{R}\mathcal{B}_{\lambda}(X_{0})
has the polygraphic resolution
\mathrm{Sq}(\widetilde{X}^{PD})
, where

i)

\mathrm{Sq}_{0}(\widetilde{X}^{PD})\coloneq X_{0}
,

ii)

\mathrm{Sq}_{1}(\widetilde{X}^{PD})\coloneq\{\,\varepsilon|D(1),\;\varepsilon|D(P(u_{0})),\;R(u_{1})|R(u_{2})\;\big|\;R\in\{P,D\},\;u_{0},R(u_{i})\in\Phi_{PD}\,\}
,

iii)

\mathrm{Sq}_{n}(\widetilde{X}^{PD})\coloneq\{\,R(u_{1})|R(u_{2})|\cdots|R(u_{n+1})\;\big|\;R(u_{i})\in\Phi_{PD}\,\}
, for every
n\geq 2
.

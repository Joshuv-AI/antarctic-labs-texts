MRI in Sheep Model for Myelomeningocele Repair Using a Novel Polymer and Other Dural Patches
Authors: Usha D Nagaraj, Marc Oria, Soner Duru, Chia‐Ying Lin, Beth M Kline‐Fath, Jose L Peiro
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectiveTo compare postnatal MRI outcomes after prenatal myelomeningocele repair using three different dural substitutes.Method32 sheep fetuses were included, with 34.3%(11/32) serving as healthy controls and the remaining undergoing prenatal spinal lumbar defect creation to recreate a myelomeningocele in the fetus. 90.5% (19/21) of sheep fetuses with surgically created MMC underwent repair using traditional collagen (Durapair) patch (n = 6), HUC matrix (NEOX RT) patch (n = 6), or a novel PLA/PCL patch (n = 7). All sheep underwent brain and spine MRI within 24 h after delivery. Images were reviewed in Research PACS by a pediatric neuroradiologist and were assessed for ventricle size, degree of hindbrain herniation, spinal cord integrity, and fluid collection at the repair site.ResultsThere was a significant reduction in hindbrain herniation in all three intervention groups when compared with MMC without prenatal intervention. There was increased incidence of complete spinal cord defect (3/7), pseudomeningocele (5/7) and intraspinal cyst (2/7) at the repair site of the PLA/PCL patch compared with the Durapair and NEOX RT patches.ConclusionsThis study demonstrates equal efficacy in reducing hindbrain herniation in MMC repair by Durapair, NEOX RT, and PLA/PCL patches by MRI. Future studies analyzing the interaction of the patches with the host tissue in animal models and clinical trials will help to better determine the true safety and efficacy of these novel patches for clinical use.

Introduction

Open spinal dysraphism, more commonly referred to collectively as myelomeningocele (MMC), is the most common prenatally diagnosed congenital abnormality of the central nervous system compatible with life, and is a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide [1, 2]. Before the Management of Myelomeningocele Study (MOMS) established the safety and efficacy of prenatal repair with the open technique requiring hysterotomy, these patients were typically managed by surgical closure shortly after birth [3]. Since then, a fetoscopic approach to repair MMC is a feasible alternative, with access and utilization of this approach continuing to increase [4, 5].

As a result, optimization of surgical approaches and materials used based on animal models is becoming increasingly important. Bovine collagen‐based patches, such as Durapair, have well‐documented success in the literature and are currently the mainstay of treatment [6]. More recently, human umbilical cord (HUC) matrix has been shown to be a favorable alternative given its nature as biological fetal tissue [7]. Polylactic acid/polycaprolactone (PLA/PCL) polymer smart patch is another potential alternative that has yet to be tested in a human model, though has demonstrated biocompatibility in the fetal rat model while inhibiting reactive astrogliosis [8]. MRI is the best imaging tool we have to evaluate the central nervous system in humans and a number of imaging biomarkers have been examined with MMC [9, 10]. The purpose of this study was to evaluate for differences in imaging parameters using postnatal MRI in the fetal lamb, with surgically‐created MMC, among three patches used for duraplasty during prenatal repair of MMC: collagen‐based (Durapair), HUC matrix (NEOX RT), and a novel PLA/PCL patch.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

This is a prospective Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) approved study in a large animal MMC model (IACUC2023‐0066). Sheep fetuses were enrolled and randomly assigned to fetal intervention or control groups. 32 sheep fetuses were included in this study, the number of animals per group was selected based on ethical considerations consistent with the principles of reduction in animal research, the relative low biological variability of the ovine fetal model and prior studies in fetal surgery demonstrating that the mechanistic or feasibility outcomes can be reliably detected with small sample sizes when effect sizes are large and measurements are repeated within subjects: 34.4% (11/32) serving as unrepaired healthy controls and the remaining 65.6% (21/32) undergoing prenatal spinal lumbar defect creation to recreate a myelomeningocele in the fetus. 9.5% (2/21) did not undergo fetal surgery repair to serve as controls for the disease, and 90.5% (19/21) of the fetal lambs with surgically created MMC underwent repair using a collagen patch (Durepair, Integra LifeSciences Corporation; Princeton, New Jersey) (n = 6), HUC matrix (NEOX RT, BioTissue; Miami, Florida) (n = 6) patch, or a new polylactic acid/polycaprolactone (PLA/PCL) patch (n = 7). The PLA/PCL smart patch is a self‐expanding, biodegradable polymer scaffold engineered for fetoscopic repair of myelomeningocele. Its design enables minimally invasive delivery, automatic deployment over the defect, biocompatible neural protection, and potential therapeutic drug or cell delivery, making it a promising next‐generation material for fetal spine repair. Sheep fetuses underwent surgical creation of a lumbar MMC‐like defect at 75 days of gestation. In twin pregnancies, one of the fetuses was operated for MMC creation, and the co‐twin fetus was left as an unmanipulated control (Figure 1).

Defect Creation

Pregnant ewes were anesthetized, surgically prepped and given preemptive analgesia (Ketamine 10 mg/kg + Midazolam 0.3–0.5 mg/Kg, IV). Surgery of the pregnant sheep was performed under Isoflurane anesthesia (2%–3% at 2‐3L oxygen): After prepping the abdomen and sterile surgical field, an infraumbilical midline laparotomy and exposure of the bicornuate uterus of the sheep were performed. Then, mini‐hysterotomy of 3 cm, aspiration of amniotic fluid, fixation of the membranes, and externalization of the lumbar region of the fetus were performed. Anesthesia/analgesia by intramuscular (IM) injection of a pharmaceutical combination (fentanyl 15 mcg/kg + vecuronium 0.025 mg/Kg + atropine 20 mcg/Kg, IM) was administered to the fetus to minimize fetal stress during defect creation based on an estimated fetal weight of 250 g.

Resection of 3 × 3 cm flap of skin and muscular layers in the thoracolumbar region was then performed. Complete posterior laminectomy of four levels in the center of the lumbar area avoiding the sacrum (corresponding to the L3‐L7 level as lambs have 7 lumbar vertebrae) was performed. After laminectomy, the exposed dura was vertically incised with micro‐scissors and separated until the spinal cord was exposed in the entire area of the defect. Leakage of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) was proof of exposure of the neural tissue. Then, with blunt forceps, the midline posterior aspect of the cord was split vertically in order to simulate an MMC. Of note, the arachnoid remains attached to the posterior aspect of the cord after the opening and separation of the dural layer. The arachnoid is displaced laterally after the partial myelotomy. Then, the amniotic fluid volume was replaced with antibiotic and warm Ringer's lactate (Nafcillin 500 mg in 10 cc Ringer's lactate solution) as needed. Uterine closure with running suture and closure by layers of the maternal abdominal wall were performed before the ewes were awakened and transferred for recovery. Bupivacaine (1–2 mg/kg of 0.25% solution) followed by GLUture topical tissue adhesive was placed over the incision.

Intrauterine MMC Repair in Ovine Fetus

3–4 weeks after the surgical creation of the MMC‐like defect (gestational age 95–105 days) pregnant ewe returned to the operating room. Again, the pregnant ewe was anesthetized, surgically prepped and given preemptive analgesia (Ketamine 10 mg/kg + Midazolam 0.3–0.5 mg/Kg, IV). General anesthesia was again administered by IM injection (fentanyl 15 mcg/kg + vecuronium 0.025 mg/Kg + atropine 20 mcg/Kg, IM), the uterus was exposed by re‐laparotomy, and the position of the fetus was determined and a small incision in the uterus at the level of the back of the fetus was made to obtain visualization of the MMC area. Neurosurgical repair of the MMC, including placode release, patch display over the lesion and skin closure was performed. In this model, the placode remains flat without cysts and is attached to the surrounding tissue. During surgery, we incised the periphery of the neural placode to dissect and separate the placode from the attached muscular and fibrotic tissue. Also, we created subcutaneous pockets to allow skin flaps to be closed in the midline without tension. The patch was not sutured and lay free underneath the closed skin. As before, saved sterile warmed amniotic fluid with antibiotics and Ringer's Lactate as needed were added to replace lost amniotic fluid volume. Fluids were also used for uterine flushing during the surgery. Closure of the uterus and abdomen and pain relief were as described above. The same 2 surgeons performed all cases working together.

Postoperative Care

After defect creation and after fetal repair, analgesia was administered with fentanyl patches in the back/neck of the pregnant sheep that delivers 1.5–2.0 mcg/Kg/hr (25‐75 mcg/hr) or sustained release Buprenorphine (0.27 mg/kg, supplied at 10 mg/mL) IM. This dose provided appropriate analgesia without profound sedation. To avoid any loss of the patches, additional bandages were applied to the sheep. Also, buprenorphine 0.01 mg/Kg, IM was administered to avoid any analgesic gap until fentanyl patches took effect. Antibiotics were administered during sedation with Cefazolin (22 mg/kg, IV) and Ampicillin (10 mg/kg, IM) for 2 days after surgery. The sheep were monitored for the rest of their pregnancy for surgical recovery and fetal vital signs via ultrasound.

Newborn Lambs

All lambs were delivered by planned cesarean section at approximately 140 days of gestation, as this timing balances fetal maturity with the expected risk of prematurity following two prior fetal surgical interventions. Cesarean section was performed with a minimum effect dose of propofol anesthesia (4–6 mg/Kg) to avoid very deep anesthesia in the fetus. Newborn lambs were resuscitated with suctioning, while keeping them dry and warm with thermal blankets and oxygenated with masks. Emergency drugs such as doxapram (15–10 mg, IM, IV or IQ), 20% dextrose (10 mL/kg, IP) and atropine at 0.07 mg/kg IV or IM were used to aid resuscitation as needed. In cases where oxygen saturation was very low and the lamb was not awake and active, endotracheal intubation was performed to ensure ventilation. Supportive care of lambs/sheep was provided during the first 24–48 h of life and the lambs were manually fed colostrum every 3–4 h by bottle.

Scanning Parameters

All lambs underwent sedation and MRI of the brain and spine within 24 h of delivery. Images were acquired on a 1.5 T Philips Ingenia Research Magnet (Philips Healthcare, Andover, MA). Sequences of the acquired brain included the following sequences: sagittal 3D balanced fast field echo (BFFE) (TR = 5.88 ms, TE = 2.94 ms, ST = 1.2 mm, and overlap = 0.6 mm), axial dual‐echo turbo spin echo (TSE) (TR = 3000 ms, TE = 7.74 ms, and ST = 3 mm no skip), axial susceptibility weighted imagines (SWI) (TR = 57.43 ms, TE = 0, ST = 2 mm, and overlap = 1 mm), and axial diffusion weighted images (DWI) (b‐value = 800). Images of the spine were acquired in two fields of view and included the following sequences: sagittal and axial T2‐weighted (TR = 3000 ms, TE = 110 ms, and ST = 3 mm no skip) and sagittal 3D BFFE (TR = 6.32 ms, TE = 3.16 ms, ST = 2 mm, and overlap = 1 mm).

Image Interpretation

Images were reviewed in the Research Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS) by a board certified pediatric neuroradiologist (UDN). Brain 2D measurements were acquired using PACS measuring tools and reported in mm and included: brain biparietal diameter, brain fronto‐occipital diameter, transverse cerebellar diameter, largest lateral ventricle atrium size, and frontal horns lateral ventricles size. Measurements were equivalent to those routinely reported and validated in human fetal imaging with modifications to fit the lamb model [11, 12, 13]. Figure 2a‐j Hindbrain herniation was graded on a three‐point scale (1 = normal, 2 = moderate, and 3 = severe hindbrain herniation), again modified from what is used clinically to fit the lamb model [9]. Figure 3a‐d SWI and DWI images were evaluated for the presence of brain parenchymal hemorrhage or infarction, respectively. Images of the spine were assessed for the presence of spinal cord syrinx, focal cord angulation/tethering, arachnoid cyst and pseudo‐meningocele. The cord integrity at the level of the created defect was also assessed on a three‐point scale (0 = normal caliber, 1 = focal thinning, and 2 = complete defect). Morphological and Histological Examination

A portion of each spinal cord specimen was fixed in formalin for 48 h and subsequently processed for paraffin embedding using a Leica EG1150H tissue processor following graded alcohol dehydration beginning with 70% ethanol. Paraffin blocks were sectioned at a thickness of 5 μm to obtain transverse sections of the spinal cord. Sections were then stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) according to standard histological procedures.

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive analyses were performed to demonstrate the distribution of the imaging findings. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± SD, and categoric variables were presented as number (percentage). A 2‐sample t test or 1‐way ANOVA was used to detect the differences in continuous variables between different groups. All analyses were performed by using GraphPad Prism, Version 10.5.0. A p value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Summary of Cohort

Sixty eight lamb fetuses were yielded from the 58 ewes acquired for this study. Of these, 58 survived defect creation, 39 survived prenatal repair and 32 underwent postnatal MRI. A total of 32 sheep fetuses were included, with 34.3% (11:32) serving as healthy controls and the remaining undergoing prenatal spinal lumbar defect creation to recreate a MMC in the fetus. The 90.5% (19/21) of the sheep fetuses with surgically created myelotomies underwent fetal repair using Durapair (n = 6), NEOX RT (n = 6) patches, or the new PLA/PCL (n = 7). Imaging data are summarized in the Table 1. Average total operative time was 20.84 min (range: 10–34 min). Other than limited bleeding after dissection of the placode, which was controlled, there were no significant complications. The skin was closed without tension in all cases.

Imaging Data

Brain Imaging Findings

There was a significant (p < 0.05) reduction in hindbrain herniation in all three intervention groups when compared with MMC without intervention. Figure 3a‐d The transverse cerebellar diameter was significantly smaller in the MMC group without intervention compared to the NEOX RT group (p = 0.0349), however not significantly different compared to the other groups. Among the 5 groups of fetuses (controls, MMC without intervention, Durapair, NEOX RT, and PLA/PCL), there were no statistically significant differences in supratentorial brain measurements (biparietal and fronto‐occipital dimensions). Lateral ventricle atrium size was larger than the control group for NEOX RT (5.6 ± 2.3 vs. 2.6 ± 1.5 mm, p = 0.0354), however not significantly different for PLA/PCL (4.1 ± 2.1 mm) and Durapair (4 ± 1.8 mm). The frontal horn diameter was not significantly different among the 5 groups. Figure 2a‐j No evidence of intraparenchymal hemorrhage or acute/subacute infarction in the brain in any of the subjects.

Spine Imaging Findings

In the spine, there was normal caliber of the spinal cord at the repair site in 33.3% (7/21) of subjects in the intervention groups (3/6 Durapair, 3/6 NEOX RT, 1/7 PLA/PCL). Full thickness spinal cord defects (spinal cord discontinuity) were seen in 33.3% (7/21) of the subjects in the intervention groups (2/2 no repair, 1/6 Durapair, 1/6 NEOX RT, 3/7 PLA/PCL). The remaining intervention subjects (66.6%, 14/21) had focal thinning of the spinal cord at the defect level. Figure 4a‐d Only 3 subjects in the intervention group (all 3 Durapair) had no visible cord angulation. 47.6% (10/21) of subjects had pseudomeningoceles at the defect site (2/2 no repair, 1/6 Durapair, 2/6 NEOX RT, 5/7 PLA/PCL). Of note, a pseudomeningocele is an extradural collection of CSF that forms at the surgical site from a dural defect [14]. Intraspinal cysts at the repair site were identified in 2 subjects (both PLA/PCL). No focal signal abnormalities or syrinx in the spinal cord of any of the imaged fetuses were identified. Histological and Gross Appearance Evaluation of the Repair Site (H&E Staining)

Histological analysis using hematoxylin–eosin (H&E) staining (4× magnification) revealed distinct tissue characteristics among the experimental groups (Figure 6a). Control animals demonstrated normal skin and soft‐tissue architecture with organized epidermal and dermal layers. In the no‐repair group, sections showed absence of normal skin coverage, with exposed neural tissue and disorganized underlying structures consistent with an unrepaired MMC defect. Repaired lambs demonstrated variable degrees of tissue restoration at the repair site. Successful repairs were characterized by continuous skin coverage overlying the defect, with re‐epithelialization and formation of fibrous connective tissue bridging the repair area. In these cases, the underlying tissues appeared compact and organized, suggesting effective defect coverage. In contrast, specimens classified as repair failure exhibited disrupted tissue architecture, incomplete epithelial coverage, and CSF leakage, corresponding to the macroscopic wound complications observed at birth. Across the repaired groups, varying degrees of connective tissue remodeling were noted, reflecting heterogeneous wound‐healing responses following prenatal MMC repair.

(b) Macroscopic and histopathological evaluation of the dorsal repair site after birth. At delivery, gross inspection of the dorsal repair site revealed variable healing outcomes across the experimental groups (Figure 6b). Lambs in the control group demonstrated intact skin coverage without dorsal defects. In the no‐repair group, open lesions with exposed neural tissue were consistently observed, confirming persistence of the MMC defect. Among the repaired animals, the macroscopic appearance ranged from complete skin coverage with a well‐healed midline scar to partial dehiscence or localized wound complications. Specifically, some repaired lambs exhibited superficial skin breakdown, suture line separation, or evidence of seroma or inflammation at the repair site, whereas others maintained durable soft‐tissue coverage over the defect. Repair failure was characterized by loss of skin integrity and re‐exposure of the underlying tissues. These findings highlight the variability of postnatal wound healing following prenatal MMC repair and provide important context for interpretation of postnatal MRI findings.

Discussion

We used MRI to evaluate the efficacy of prenatal MMC repair in a lamb model using Durapair, NEOX RT, and PLA/PCL patches. We observed a significant reduction in hindbrain herniation in all three intervention groups when compared to lambs with unrepaired MMC. There was increased incidence of complete spinal cord defect, pseudomeningocele and intraspinal cyst at the repair site of the PLA/PCL patch compared with the Durapair and NEOX RT patches. No brain parenchymal injury or spinal cord syrinx in any of the imaged fetuses were identified in our cohort.

The safety and efficacy of collagen‐based patches in prenatal repair of MMC has been well established in the literature and is the current standard of care [6, 15]. However, recent advances in biological tissue‐based research have allowed other options to be considered [16]. The use of umbilical cord matrix in patch repair has gained increased use after efficacy was established in animal and now clinical experience [7, 17, 18]. Our study also demonstrated efficacy of collagen (Durapair) and HUC matrix (NEOX RT) patches with decreased hindbrain herniation compared with the non‐intervention group, which supports its continued use in clinical practice. Our study further adds to the limited literature regarding NEOX RT patches by providing imaging data in this patient population. The six lambs that underwent NEOX RT patch repair demonstrated a similar degree of cord angulation and pseudomeningocele formation at the repair site compared with the Durapair patch group. We noted that the average lateral ventricle atrium size was larger in the NEOX RT patch group than in the control group. However, this is of doubtful clinical significance given the lack of significant differences in frontal horn diameter size and the larger transverse cerebellar diameter observed in the NEOX RT group compared with the non‐intervention group.

Synthetic patches are now being developed with the goal of achieving self‐expanding, watertight and biodegradable material designed for optimal clinical use. PLA/PCL patch is a promising innovation in this space with favorable biochemical properties for use in myelomeningocele repair [19]. Biocompatibility of this patch was explored in a rat model demonstrating that this patch can function as a carrier for stem cell therapy and drug‐delivery systems [8]. This is the first study describing its use in surgical practice, and we demonstrated reduced hindbrain herniation in 7 lambs with this novel patch, which is promising. However, this patch also demonstrated increased incidence of cord angulation, complete spinal cord defects, pseudomeningocele formation and intraspinal cysts at the repair site compared with the other groups, which may potentially be the result of bone morphogenic protein (BMP) pathway inhibition which allows for astrocyte formation [20]. More preclinical studies demonstrating consistent postnatal clinical efficacy will be needed before considering transitioning to a human model. The cellular and histological analysis of the grafts in these animals will be analyzed for future publication.

The strengths of this study include its prospective nature, patch innovation, implementation of surgical techniques performed by an experienced fetal surgeon and use of MRI in examining the efficacy of these patches and procedures. The main limitations of this study include the relatively small sample size, limiting the generalizability of the findings. Statistical power is not only limited by the small sample size but also by group imbalance as co‐twins were assigned as normal controls and only two in utero myelotomies were not repaired to compare with the intervention groups. Assessment of imaging findings was limited to observations by a neuroradiologist and quantitative imaging data was limited to those obtained using PACS measuring tools. While this allows for clinically applicable assessments that can be used in practice, detailed quantitative assessments using advanced imaging techniques such as diffusion tensor imaging, volumetrics and morphometry may be informative in better understanding the underlying biological mechanisms [21, 22]. Also, studies looking at tissue pathologic assessments, neurodevelopmental results and ultimately long term outcomes data in the future will help to inform clinical decision‐making.

This study demonstrates equal efficacy in reducing hindbrain herniation with emerging HUC matrix (NEOX RT) and novel PLA/PCL patches compared with collagen (Durapair) patch repair by MRI in a lamb model. This animal model study further supports the use of the recently introduced NEOX RT patches in repair of MMC in clinical practice. Although the improved hindbrain herniation is promising, the increased incidence of pseudomeningocele formation and intraspinal cysts at the repair site of PLA/PCL patches in the population requires further investigation. Future studies analyzing the interaction of the patches with the tissue in animal models and clinical trials in humans will help to better determine the true safety and efficacy of these novel patches for clinical use.

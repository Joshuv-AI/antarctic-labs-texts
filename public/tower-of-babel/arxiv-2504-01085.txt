Abstract
. We study the topological properties of expanding invariant foliations of C 1 + C^{1+} diffeomorphisms, in the context of partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms and laminations with 1 1 -dimensional center bundle. In this first version of the paper, we introduce a property we call s-transversality of a partially hyperbolic lamination with 1 1 -dimensional center bundle, which is robust under C 1 C^{1} perturbations. We prove that under a weak expanding condition on the center bundle (called some hyperbolicity , or “SH”), any s-transverse partially hyperbolic lamination contains a disk tangent to the center-unstable direction (Theorem C ). We obtain several corollaries, among them: if f f is a C 1 + C^{1+} partially hyperbolic Anosov diffeomorphism with 1 1 -dimensional expanding center, and the (strong) unstable foliation 𝒲 u ​ u \mathcal{W}^{uu} of f f is minimal, then 𝒲 u ​ u \mathcal{W}^{uu} is robustly minimal under C 1 C^{1} -small perturbations, provided that the stable and strong unstable bundles are not jointly integrable (Theorem B ). Theorem B has applications in our upcoming work [ 4 ] with Eskin, Potrie and Zhang, in which we prove that on 𝕋 3 {\mathbb{T}}^{3} , any C 1 + C^{1+} partially hyperbolic Anosov diffeomorphism with 1 1 -dimensional expanding center has a minimal strong unstable foliation, and has a unique u ​ u uu -Gibbs measure provided that the stable and strong unstable bundles are not jointly integrable. In a future work, we address the density (in any C r C^{r} topology) of minimality of strong unstable foliations for C 1 + C^{1+} partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms with 1 1 -dimensional center and the SH property. Our ultimate goal is to prove that, on any closed manifold, among the C r C^{r} ( r > 1 r>1 ) partially hyperbolic Anosov diffeomorphisms with 1 1 -dimensional expanding center, there is a C 1 C^{1} -open and C r C^{r} -dense set of diffeomorphisms with minimal 𝒲 u ​ u \mathcal{W}^{uu} foliation.



Introduction

Let
f\colon M\to M
be a diffeomorphism of a closed, connected Riemannian manifold
M
. Assume that there exists an
f
-invariant foliation
\mathcal{F}^{u}
of
M
with
C^{1}
leaves that is
expanding
, meaning that there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
v\in T\mathcal{F}^{u}
tangent to a leaf of
\mathcal{F}^{u}
, we have
\|Df^{N}(v)\|\geq 2\|v\|
(a given diffeomorphism can have more than one expanding foliation). Such foliations arise in the study of Anosov and partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms as well as in more general scenarios. The dynamics of such foliations have fundamental implications for the dynamics of the underlying diffeomorphisms. This paper addresses the dynamics of expanding foliations.

The basic building block for the topological dynamics of such a foliation
\mathcal{F}^{u}
is an
\mathcal{F}^{u}
-lamination
, which is a (non-necessarily invariant) compact set consisting of a union of leaves of
\mathcal{F}^{u}
. The
\mathcal{F}^{u}
-lamination is
minimal
if every leaf is dense in the lamination; it is
dynamically minimal
if it is invariant and the orbit of every of its leaf is dense in the lamination.

The fundamental problem to address first is then the following.

Problem 1
.

Classify the
\mathcal{F}^{u}
-laminations for a fixed
\mathcal{F}^{u}
. Is there a unique (non-empty)
\mathcal{F}^{u}
-lamination? More generally, when are the minimal
\mathcal{F}^{u}
-laminations smooth submanifolds of
M
?

We remark that if
f
has an attractor
\Lambda
, then
\Lambda
is an
\mathcal{F}^{u}
-lamination, but it is also possible to have nontrivial
\mathcal{F}^{u}
-laminations without an attractor. For example, the automorphism
f_{0}\colon(x,y,z)\mapsto(2x+y,x+y,z)

{\mathbb{T}}^{3}={\mathbb{T}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{T}}

\mathcal{F}^{u}
tangent to the expanding eigenspace, and for any

,

\mathcal{F}^{u}
-lamination (which is not an attractor, as

\mathcal{F}^{u}
-laminations are smooth submanifolds of

This illustrates a more general phenomenon in homogeneous dynamics. If

is unipotent, and the work of Dani, Margulis, Ratner, Starkov and Shah

Next, studying

Suppose

diffeomorphism of a Riemannian manifold

T_{\Lambda}M=E^{uu}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s};\quad\hbox{dim}(E^{c})=1.

U_{\tau}(\gamma(1))
respectively, but are on different sides of
\mathcal{W}^{uu}(x)

relative to
\gamma
.

It is
locally s-transverse
if
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
can be chosen with diameter smaller than
\tau
.

Remark 1.2
.

In practice, it is enough to check the definition of s-transversality for a single sufficiently small value of
\tau
(see Lemma

).

In order to illustrate this definition,
we return to the case where

is a dynamically coherent, partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism
and the center bundle

is orientable, with orientation preserved by

.
Note that for any

with

, the stable plaque
\mathcal{W}^{s}_{loc}(y)

intersects
\mathcal{W}^{cu}_{loc}(x)
at a unique point

\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(z)
meets
\mathcal{W}^{uu}_{loc}(x)
.
It is easy to see that two points

are in the same component of
U_{\tau}(x)

if the center arc joining
\mathcal{W}^{s}_{loc}(y)\cap\mathcal{W}^{cu}_{loc}(x)

to
W^{uu}_{loc}(x)
and the center arc joining
\mathcal{W}^{s}_{loc}(y^{\prime})\cap\mathcal{W}^{cu}_{loc}(x)

to

have the same orientation.

In this case, the uu-lamination
\Lambda
is s-transverse if for
\tau>0
small
and for any

there exists a “hexagonal loop”
consisting of a concatenation of
6
nontrivial
C^{1}
arcs
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{6}
with the following properties (see Figure
5
):

(1)

The images of
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{4}
lie in
\mathcal{W}^{uu}(x)
. For all

.

(2)

The images of
\gamma_{2},\gamma_{6}
lie in
\mathcal{W}^{s}_{loc}(\gamma_{1}(1)),\mathcal{W}^{s}_{loc}(\gamma_{1}(0))
respectively.

(3)

\gamma_{3},\gamma_{5}
have their images contained in
\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(\gamma_{4}(0)),\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(\gamma_{4}(1))
respectively and have opposite orientations.

This notion of a hexagon gives rise to a simple criterion for checking s-transversality in the global partially hyperbolic case.

We now state some properties of s-transversality that will be proved in Section
4.3
.
The s-transversality is
C^{1}
robust:

Proposition 1.3
.

If
\Lambda
is an invariant s-transverse uu-lamination for a diffeomorphism
f
,
then there are neighborhoods
U
of
\Lambda
in
M
and
\mathcal{U}
of
f
in the space of
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms
such that for any

, any invariant uu-lamination

is also s-transverse.

The next criterion for s-transversality shows in particular that, if
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
is minimal and s-transverse, then it is locally s-transverse.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
f
be a partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism with
1
-dimensional center, and assume that the foliation
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
is
dynamically minimal. If
E^{uu}\oplus E^{s}
is not integrable, then
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
is locally s-transverse.

A weaker notion of “non joint integrability” has been used in
[
12
]
in order to prove the finiteness of attractors
for globally partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms. The same idea gives the following structure on dynamically minimal
uu-laminations.

Proposition 1.5
.

If
\Omega
is a non-invariant minimal s-transverse uu-lamination for a diffeomorphism

,
then it is fixed by some iterate

,

.

If
\Lambda
is an invariant dynamically minimal s-transverse uu-lamination, then
it decomposes as
\Lambda=\Omega\sqcup f(\Omega)\sqcup\cdots\sqcup f^{N-1}(\Omega)
,
where
\Omega
is a minimal uu-lamination. The sets
f^{i}(\Omega)
are the connected components of
\Lambda
.

1.4.
The SH (“some hyperbolicity”) property

Another concept we will use in the statements of our main results is that of
some hyperbolicity
in the center bundle
E^{c}
of a partially hyperbolic
uu
-lamination
\Lambda
, which was introduced by Pujals and Sambarino
[
32
]
. This gives a natural weakening of the hypothesis that
\Lambda
is hyperbolic with a dominated splitting of the unstable bundle
E^{u}=E^{uu}\oplus E^{c}
. It has a topological flavor, and is different from the measure-theoretic “mostly expanding” property defined in
[
1
]
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
\Lambda
be a partially hyperbolic uu-lamination.
We say that
\Lambda
has the SH property if there exist
R>0
,
C>0
and
\lambda_{SH}>1
such that for every
x\in\Lambda
, there exists
y\in\mathcal{W}^{uu}(x,R)
such that, for all
n,\ell\geq 1
,

\|Df^{-n}|_{E^{c}(f^{\ell+n}x)}\|\leq C\lambda_{SH}^{n}.

Note that
the constant
R
can be chosen as small as one likes in the definition, at the expense of decreasing
C
.

The SH property is
C^{1}
robust (i.e., every
g
sufficiently
C^{1}
close to
f
has the SH property), see Proposition
2.2
below.
For (globally) partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms with the SH property,
if the foliation
\mathcal{W}^{s}
is minimal, then it is
C^{1}
robustly minimal (
[
32
]
and Proposition
2.3
below).
Note that the property SH is not symmetric, hence it does not give the minimality of
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
for an Anosov diffeomorphism without further assumptions.

Examples:
Besides partially hyperbolic Anosov diffeomorphisms, examples of SH uu-laminations can be constructed by deforming such systems: this is the case of Shub’s and Mañé’s examples, see
[
32
]
.
If one considers an hyperbolic attractor with a one-dimensional expanding center bundle, one can perform similar deformations
and obtain a partially hyperbolic uu-lamination which satisfies the SH property and is proper.

1.5.
A robust criterion for minimality

Our main results in Part 1 of this paper concern the property of a uu-lamination containing a
cu
-disk
. By a
cu
-disk in a uu-lamination
\Lambda
, we mean a
C^{1}
embedded disk in
\Lambda
that is everywhere tangent to the distribution
E^{uu}\oplus E^{c}
. For example, in the case that
\Lambda
is hyperbolic with
1
-dimensional expanding center, a
cu
-disk is merely a disk in
\Lambda
tangent to the (full) unstable lamination for
f
.
The existence of a
cu
disk has strong implications for the dynamics of
\Lambda
, including the existence of a weak attractor when
f
has the SH property, or the minimality of
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
(under additional assumptions). Our central result in this part of this paper is:

Theorem C
.

Let
f\colon M\to M
be a
C^{1+}
diffeomorphism and
\Lambda
an s-transverse partially hyperbolic uu-lamination with
1
-dimensional center and satisfying the SH property. Then
\Lambda
contains a
cu
-disk.

More precisely, there is a hyperbolic set
K_{0}

with unstable bundle
E^{uu}\oplus E^{c}
such that
\Lambda
contains the unstable manifolds of
K_{0}
.

Corollary 1.7
.

In the setting of Theorem
C
,
there exists an invariant uu-lamination
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
which is a transitive weak attractor:

\omega(x)\subset\Lambda^{\prime}
for all
x
in a nonempty open subset of
M

and
\omega(x)=\Lambda^{\prime}
for some
x\in\Lambda
.

If
f
is transitive and
M
connected, then
\Lambda=M
and
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
is minimal.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
be a dynamically minimal
f
-invariant uu-sublamination of
\Lambda
(meaning that
\Lambda^{\prime}
contains no proper invariant sublamination: such an
\Lambda^{\prime}
exists by Zorn’s lemma). Theorem
C
implies that
\Lambda^{\prime}
contains a disc

tangent to
E^{uu}\oplus E^{c}
.
Saturating

by local stable manifolds, we obtain an open set

. Then every

lies in
\mathcal{W}^{s}(y)
, for some

, and so

accumulates on a subset of
\Lambda^{\prime}
. Thus
\Lambda^{\prime}
is a weak attractor.
Since any dynamically minimal uu-lamination is a transitive set,
\Lambda^{\prime}
is transitive.

If
f
is transitive, then for

in a dense G
δ
subset of
M
we have both

\omega(x)\subset\Lambda^{\prime}
and
\omega(x)=M

.
Proposition

then implies
M=\Omega\sqcup f(\Omega)\sqcup\cdots\sqcup f^{N-1}(\Omega)

where

is a minimal uu-lamination. If

is connected, it then follows that

, and hence
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
is
a minimal foliation.

\diamond

When
\Lambda
is a hyperbolic set, the SH property is the same as

having an expanding center;
if the center is contracting, then

is just the hyperbolic unstable lamination of
\Lambda
.
This set is an attractor, it has finitely many connected components and
\mathcal{W}^{u}
is minimal on each of them.
Note that a hyperbolic transitive weak attractor

is an attractor, i.e.
\omega(x)\subset\Lambda^{\prime}
for all

in a neighborhood of
\Lambda
. Combining with Proposition
1.5
we thus get:

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
\Lambda
be a uniformly hyperbolic, partially hyperbolic uu-lamination for a
C^{1+}
diffeomorphism
f\colon M\to M
with
1
-dimensional center.
If
\Lambda
is s-transverse, then it contains a hyperbolic transitive attractor

, and each connected component of

is a minimal uu-lamination.

From the robustness of the SH property (Propositions
2.2
),
Pujals-Sambarino’s robustness of

-minimality (Proposition
2.3
),
the criterion for s-transversality (Propositions

),
and the robustness of the s-transversality (Proposition

),

implies following, which gives Theorem

Corollary 1.9
.

Suppose

is
C^{1+}
, partially hyperbolic with

-dimensional center, has the SH property and a minimal

is minimal and

\mathcal{W}^{uu}
is

In particular, if

2.
Preliminaries on partial hyperbolicity

2.1.
Notations

Given a map
f\colon X\to X
, a function
\xi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
n\geq 1
, we write
\xi_{n}(x):=\xi(f^{n-1}(x))\cdots\xi(f(x))\cdot\xi(x)
, or
\xi_{f,n}(x)
when we want to emphasize the map
f
.

For
a,b\neq 0
and
\Delta>1
, we write
a\asymp_{\Delta}b
to mean that
a/b\in[\Delta^{-1},\Delta]
(in particular if
a\asymp_{\Delta}b
, then
a
and
b
have the same sign).

2.2.
Partial hyperbolicity

2.2.1.
Tangent splittings

Let
A\subset M
be a compact set that is invariant under a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism
f
.
An invariant splitting of its tangent space
T_{A}M=E\oplus F
is
dominated

if there is
N\geq 1
such that for any
x\in A
and any unit vectors
u\in E_{x}
,
v\in F_{x}
,

\|Df^{N}(v)\|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|Df^{N}(u)\|.

The bundle
F
is
uniformly contracted
by
Df
if
\|Df^{N}|_{F}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
for some
N\geq 1

and
E
is uniformly expanded if it is uniformly contracted by
Df^{-1}
.

A uniformly expanded bundle
E
will usually be denoted by
E^{u}
(or
E^{uu}
); to each point
x\in A
we associate a well-defined strong unstable leaf
\mathcal{W}^{u}(x)
tangent to
E^{u}

(also denoted by
\mathcal{W}^{uu}(x)
when
E=E^{uu}
). To emphasize the diffeomorphism
f
,
we will also write
\mathcal{W}^{u}_{f}(x)
.

For
R>0
, the ball of radius
R
centered at a point
x\in A
inside
\mathcal{W}^{u}(x)

will be denoted by
\mathcal{W}^{u}_{R}(x)
. Having fixed a small constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
we will also write
\mathcal{W}^{u}_{loc}(x):=W^{u}_{\varepsilon_{0}}(x)
and call it the

local strong unstable manifold of
x
.
Finally we set
\mathcal{W}^{u}_{loc}(A)=\cup_{x\in A}\mathcal{W}^{u}_{loc}(x)
.

Similarly, when
F
is uniformly contracted we will usually denote it by
E^{s}
(or
E^{ss}
)
and write
\mathcal{W}^{s}(x)
(or
\mathcal{W}^{ss}(x)
) for the associated leaves.

2.2.2.
Cone fields

We associate to a dominated splitting
T_{A}M=E\oplus F
fixed
cone fields
\mathcal{C}^{E}
,
\mathcal{C}^{F}

i.e. small neighborhoods of the bundles
E
and
F
inside the corresponding tangent Grassmannian bundles.
The domination property implies that
these cone fields can be chosen
invariant
:
for any
x\in A
, the closure of the image
Df(\mathcal{C}^{E}_{x})
is
contained in the interior of
\mathcal{C}^{E}_{f(x)}
, whereas the closure of the
backward image
Df(\mathcal{C}^{F}_{x})
is
contained in the interior of
\mathcal{C}^{F}_{f^{-1}(x)}
.

2.2.3.
Partial hyperbolicity

A compact,
f
-invariant set
A
is
hyperbolic
if it admits a dominated splitting
T_{A}M=E^{u}\oplus E^{s}
such that

E^{u},E^{s}
are respectively uniformly expanded and contracted.
A compact,
f
-invariant set
A
is
partially hyperbolic
if it admits a dominated splitting
T_{A}M=E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
such that

E^{u},E^{s}
are non-trivial and respectively uniformly expanded and contracted.

An
unstable lamination
is a (not necessarily
f
-invariant) set
\Lambda
contained in a partially hyperbolic set
A
such that
\Lambda
contains all the strong unstable leaves

\mathcal{W}^{u}(x)
for
x\in\Lambda
.

2.2.4.
Quantitative partial hyperbolicity

Partial hyperbolicity of
f
implies that there exist a smooth Riemannian structure on
M
, a neighborhood
U
of
\Lambda
, a
C^{1}
-neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
f
and continuous functions

\kappa,\lambda,\mu^{-},\mu^{+}\colon U\to(0,\infty),\text{ with }\kappa<1<\lambda\text{ and }\kappa<\mu^{-}<\mu^{+}<\lambda

such that for any diffeomorphism
g\in\mathcal{U}
, we have that any
g
-invariant compact set
A\subset U

has a (unique) partially hyperbolic splitting
T_{U}M=E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
into subbundles which have the same dimensions as for the partially hyperbolic splitting on
\Lambda
for
f

and such that for any
x\in A
:

\sup_{v\in E^{s}(x),\|v\|=1}\|D_{x}g(v)\|<\kappa(x),\qquad\lambda(x)<\inf_{v\in E^{u}(x),\|v\|=1}\|D_{x}g(v)\|,

\mu^{-}(x)<\|D_{x}g|_{E^{c}}\|<\mu^{+}(x).

We also write
E^{u}_{g},E^{c}_{g},E^{s}_{g}
in order to emphasize the dependence on
g
.

2.3.
Plaque families

We introduce a system of locally invariant plaque families which will be useful when considering partially hyperbolic systems that are not dynamically coherent.

2.4.

We derive some basic properties of

2.4.1.
Robustness

We already mentioned that the SH property is robust.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
32
]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a partially hyperbolic
uu
-lamination for a diffeomorphism
f\colon M\to M
. Assume that

Then there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\Lambda

(
[

]
)

is minimal, then any diffeomorphism

has a minimal foliation

.

In particular

-robustly topologically mixing.

2.4.2.
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
-sections

Consider a

-lamination
\Lambda
for

.

Definition 2.4
.

A set

\mathcal{W}^{uu}
-section
of

Part I
Robust minimality via s-transversality

Throughout this part,
f
is a
C^{1+}
diffeomorphism of a Riemannian manifold
M

and
\Lambda
is a partially hyperbolic,
f
-invariant set
with a splitting

T_{\Lambda}M=E^{uu}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s};\quad\hbox{dim}(E^{c})=1.

We assume that
\Lambda
is a
uu
-lamination, i.e. it is saturated by
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
leaves.

This part of the paper is devoted to a proof of Theorem

. The general proof uses several technical arguments to deal with the possibilities:

–

there might not exist center-stable and center-unstable manifolds that foliate a neighborhood of
\Lambda
, and

–

the center bundle

.

Without these technicalities, the argument is relatively simple, and so we present it first in a restricted setting.

3.
A sketched proof of Theorem
C
in a simplified setting

We sketch a proof of Theorem
C
under these simplifying hypotheses:

(1)

f
is a
C^{1+}
transitive Anosov diffeomorphism,

(2)

f
is partially hyperbolic and dynamically coherent,

(3)

the center bundle is expanding,
1
-dimensional and has an orientation preserved by
Df
.

Theorem.

If the above hypotheses (
1
)-(
3
) and s-transversality of
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
hold,
then
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
is a minimal foliation.

The assumptions (
1
) and (
3
) mean that there is a globally-defined
Df
-invariant splitting
TM=E^{uu}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
, where
E^{c}
is uniformly expanded, orientable and
1
-dimensional; it follows that the center-unstable bundle
E^{u}:=E^{uu}\oplus E^{c}
is uniquely integrable, tangent to the unstable foliation
\mathcal{W}^{u}
of the Anosov diffeomorphism
f
. The dynamical coherence assumption (
2
) means that the
bundle
E^{s}\oplus E^{c}
is also integrable, tangent to an
f
-invariant foliation
\mathcal{W}^{cs}
; by intersecting leaves of the foliations
\mathcal{W}^{cs}
and
\mathcal{W}^{u}
, one obtains in addition an
f
-invariant
center foliation

\mathcal{W}^{c}
.

These hypothesis hold, for example, if

is the perturbation of a hyperbolic toral automorphism that is partially hyperbolic with
1
-dimensional expanding center. They also hold for any

,

, by
[
22
,
30
]
.

3.1.
Preparation

The leaves of
\mathcal{W}^{cs}
are jointly subfoliated by the leaves of
\mathcal{W}^{s}
and
\mathcal{W}^{c}
; the leaves of
\mathcal{W}^{u}
are jointly subfoliated by the leaves of
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
and
\mathcal{W}^{c}
. Hence there are well-defined
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
and
\mathcal{W}^{s}
holonomies between center leaves.
We will use the following facts about these holonomy maps.

–

For any
x\in M
and any
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}^{uu}_{loc}(x)
, the (restricted)
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
-holonomy
h^{uu}\colon\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(x)\to\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(x^{\prime})
is
C^{1}
, with derivative varying continuously in the choice of
x,x^{\prime}
.

–

For any
x\in M
and any
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}^{s}_{loc}(x)
, the (restricted)
\mathcal{W}^{s}
-holonomy
h^{s}\colon\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(x)\to\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(x^{\prime})
is
C^{1}
, with derivative varying continuously in the choice of
x,x^{\prime}
.

The first item is a standard fact about expanding strong unstable subfoliations and does not use the fact that the dimension of
E^{c}
is
1
. The second item uses a center-bunching property (see
[
10
]
), which follows from the
1
-dimensionality of the bundle
E^{c}
.

We recall the notation
a\asymp_{\Delta}b
introduced in Section
2.1
.
We then say that
a
and
b
are
\Delta
-nearly equal
, or just
nearly equal
, if
\Delta
is understood to be a constant that may be taken arbitrarily close to
1
in the context.

We fix an orientation on
E^{c}
, and using this orientation we define the
signed center distance

\overline{d}^{c}
between two points on the same
\mathcal{W}^{c}
plaque: for
x\in M
and
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(x)
, the signed distance
\overline{d}^{c}(x,x^{\prime})
is just distance between
x
and
x^{\prime}
along
\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}
multiplied by
\pm 1
according to whether the geodesic arc in
\mathcal{W}^{c}
from
x
to
x^{\prime}
is positively oriented or not.

Then we have the following standard distortion estimate on the derivative
Df|_{E^{c}}
(compare with Corollary
5.2
in Section
5.1.2
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
\delta>0
, there is
\varepsilon_{\delta}>0
such that for every
x_{0}\in M
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(x_{0})
, and
k\geq 1
,
if
d(f^{k}(x_{i}),f^{k}(x_{j}))<\varepsilon_{\delta}
for all
i,j
, then

\frac{\overline{d}^{c}(x_{1},x_{0})}{\overline{d}^{c}(x_{2},x_{0})}\asymp_{1+\delta}\frac{\overline{d}^{c}(f^{k}(x_{1}),f^{k}(x_{0}))}{\overline{d}^{c}(f^{k}(x_{2}),f^{k}(x_{0}))}.

When we say that a set of
N
points lying in a
1
-dimensional
\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}
plaque is
\Delta
-nearly evenly spaced
, we mean that
d^{c}
-distances between any two neighboring pairs of points are
\Delta
-nearly equal.

By the lemma, given
\Delta>1
there is
\widehat{\Delta}
such that if
x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}
are positively ordered and
\widehat{\Delta}
-nearly evenly spaced apart along a
\mathcal{W}^{c}
plaque, then the iterates
f^{k}(x_{0}),f^{k}(x_{1}),f^{k}(x_{2})
will remain positively ordered and (
\Delta
-)nearly evenly spaced as long as the distances between them remain small (
<\varepsilon_{\delta}
).

3.2.
An inductive property

Fix
\tau>0
small so that the
\mathcal{W}^{s}
-holonomies (denoted by
h^{s}
)
between
\mathcal{W}^{cu}_{loc}
plaques at
\tau
-close points are well-defined.

Let us assume that
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
is s-transverse. There are
R,\chi>0
such that for every
x\in M
there exist curves
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\colon[0,1]\to\mathcal{W}^{uu}_{R}(x)
satisfying:

(a)

for all
t\in[0,1]
,
\gamma(t)\in\mathcal{W}^{cs}_{\tau}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))
, and

(b)

\overline{d}^{c}(h^{s}(\gamma^{\prime}(0)),\gamma(0))<-\chi
and
\overline{d}^{c}(h^{s}(\gamma^{\prime}(1)),\gamma(1))>\chi
.

Let
L>1
be a constant depending only on
R
that bounds the norm of
Dh^{uu}
and
Dh^{s}
, where
h^{uu}
and
h^{s}
are holonomy maps between
\mathcal{W}^{c}
plaques along paths of length smaller than
2R
.

Fix an
f
-invariant
uu
-lamination
\Lambda
.
We argue inductively (compare with Proposition
6.1
in Section
6
):

Inductive statement
P(N)
.

For every
\Delta>1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{\Delta})
, there exists a set of
N
points which is
\Delta
-nearly evenly-spaced,
which lie in a single plaque
\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(x)\cap\Lambda
, and whose diameter (measured along
\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(x)
) belongs to
[\varepsilon/\|Df\|,\varepsilon]
.

The case where
N=1
is trivial. We now suppose
P(N)
holds for some
N
and prove
P(N+1)
.
We are given a constant
\Delta>1
and a scale
\varepsilon>0
.

We fix some
1<\widehat{\Delta}_{1},\widehat{\Delta}_{2}
such that
\log\widehat{\Delta}_{1}\ll\log\widehat{\Delta}_{2}\ll\log\Delta
.
Applying
P(N)
to some
\widehat{\Delta}_{1}
and to some
\widehat{\varepsilon}\ll\varepsilon
small, we obtain

x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in\Lambda
, a collection of
\widehat{\Delta}_{1}
-nearly evenly spaced points on
\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(x_{N})
with the maximum distance
d(x_{1},x_{N})\in[\widehat{\varepsilon}/\|Df\|,\widehat{\varepsilon})
. The
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
holonomy
h^{uu}
between

\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(x_{N})
and
\mathcal{W}^{c}(y)
, for any
y\in\mathcal{W}^{uu}_{R}(x_{N})\subset\Lambda
is
C^{1}
, uniformly in
R
. Thus for
\rho>0
sufficiently small, the derivative of the holonomy between
\mathcal{W}^{c}_{\rho}(x_{N})
and
\mathcal{W}^{c}(y)
is nearly constant, which means that
\widehat{\Delta}_{1}
-nearly evenly-spaced points are sent to
\widehat{\Delta}_{2}
-nearly evenly-spaced points. We assume that
\widehat{\varepsilon}<\rho
.

The s-transversality property gives curves
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{W}^{uu}_{R}(x_{N})\subset\Lambda
satisfying the properties (a) and (b) above, with the given values of
R,\tau,\chi
.
Let
x_{1}^{\prime}(t),\ldots,x_{N}^{\prime}(t)=\gamma^{\prime}(t)\in\Lambda
be the images of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}
under the holonomies
h^{uu}\colon\mathcal{W}^{c}_{\widehat{\varepsilon}}(x_{N})\to\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))
. These points vary continuously with
t
while they remain very nearly evenly spaced, since
\varepsilon_{\ell}<\rho
. The distance between any successive points
x_{i}^{\prime}(t),x_{i+1}^{\prime}(t)
is at most
L\widehat{\varepsilon}
.

Define a function
\phi\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

\phi(t)=\overline{d}^{c}(h^{s}_{t}(\gamma^{\prime}(t)),\gamma(t)),

where
h^{s}_{t}\colon\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\to\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(\gamma(t))
is the local
\mathcal{W}^{s}
holonomy.
This function

is continuous, and s-transversality implies that

\phi(0)<-\chi<0<\chi<\phi(1).

We now choose a special value of
t_{0}\in[0,1]
. There are two cases:

–

For
N=1
, we select
t_{0}\in[0,1]
such that
\phi(t_{0})=\chi/2
.

–

For
N\geq 2
, since
0<d^{c}(x_{N}^{\prime}(t),x_{N-1}^{\prime}(t))<L\widehat{\varepsilon}<\chi
, it follows that
there exists
t_{0}\in[0,1]
such that

\overline{d}^{c}(h^{s}_{t_{0}}(\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})),\gamma(t_{0}))=\overline{d}^{c}(x_{N}^{\prime}(t_{0}),x_{N-1}^{\prime}(t_{0})).

\hat{x}_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{N+1}

\Lambda_{\ell}(x)
are colored in blue.

We set
\hat{x}_{N+1}=\gamma(t_{0})\subset\Lambda
. We also set

\hat{x}_{i}=h^{s}_{t_{0}}(x_{i}^{\prime}(t_{0}))
, for
i=1,\ldots,N
.
Then
\hat{x}_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{N+1}
lie on
\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(\hat{x}_{N+1})
, are very nearly evenly spaced, with distance between these points bounded above by
L^{2}\widehat{\varepsilon}
. The point
\hat{x}_{N+1}
belongs to
\Lambda
, and
\hat{x}_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{N}
belong to
\mathcal{W}^{s}_{\tau}(x_{i}^{\prime}(t_{0}))\subset\mathcal{W}^{s}_{\tau}(\Lambda)
. See Figure

. If we then iterate the points
\hat{x}_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{N+1}
forward by

, the distances between successive points become larger but, since

is
C^{1+}
, the points remain
\Delta
-nearly evenly spaced as long as they remain within some fixed scale

(as given by Lemma

). At the same time, the distance between
f^{k}(\hat{x}_{N+1})
and

goes to zero exponentially fast.
We take

such that

d(f^{k}(\widehat{x}_{1}),f^{k}(\widehat{x}_{N+1}))\in[\tfrac{\varepsilon}{\|Df\|},\varepsilon]
.

We perform this construction for a sequence
\widehat{\varepsilon}_{\ell}\to 0

and get points

x_{\ell,1}:=f^{k_{\ell}}(\hat{x}_{\ell,1}),\ldots,x_{\ell,N+1}:=f^{k_{\ell}}(\hat{x}_{\ell,N+1})\;\in\Lambda

\Delta
-nearly evenly spaced in
\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(x_{\ell,N})
, with

and

as

. Extracting a subsequence (by compactness of
\Lambda
), we obtain the desired limiting points
x_{1}\ldots,x_{N+1}\in\Lambda
, proving

, and completing the induction.

3.3.
Conclusion of the proof

We now fix
\Delta>1
and
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small. For each
N\geq 1
, Property
P(N)
gives an ordered sequence of points

x_{1}(N),x_{2}(N),\ldots,x_{N}(N)\in\Lambda

\Delta
-nearly evenly spaced in
\mathcal{W}^{c}_{loc}(x_{N}(N))
with

d^{c}(x_{1}(N),x_{N}(N))\in[\tfrac{\varepsilon}{\|Df\|},\varepsilon)
. Sending

and extracting a Hausdorff convergent subsequence of
\{x_{1}(N),\ldots,x_{N}(N)\}
, we obtain a center disk
\mathcal{W}^{c}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x)\subset\Lambda
, where
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\bigl[\varepsilon/\|Df\|,\varepsilon\bigr]
. Since
\Lambda
is
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
-saturated and
\mathcal{W}^{u}
is bifoliated by
\mathcal{W}^{c}
and
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
, the union of
\mathcal{W}^{uu}_{loc}
plaques through points in
\mathcal{W}^{c}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x)
gives a
\mathcal{W}^{u}
disk in
\Lambda
.
Corollary
1.7
then concludes that
\mathcal{W}^{uu}
is minimal.
This completes the outline of the proof of Theorem
C
under the simplifying assumptions.

4.1.
Center and stable distances

Let
d^{uu}
be the induced Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{W}^{uu}

\Lambda
. For

let

be the induced Riemannian metrics on

Proposition
2.1
implies the following lemma (see Figure

).
For
x,y\in\Lambda
close, this lemma gives a way to project

onto
\mathcal{W}^{uu}_{loc}(x)
by intersecting with the plaque
\mathcal{P}^{cs}(y)
.

In order to simplify the presentation, one will assume that the bundles

,

and

are nearly orthogonal.
To handle the general case, one should replace explicit numbers in the estimates that follow by a constant that

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\varepsilon_{0}
is small, then
for every

and

, there is a unique

\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdot\sigma_{2}\cdot\sigma_{3}
,
\sigma_{i}\colon[0,1]\to M
, from

to

with
\sigma_{1}
geodesic in
\mathcal{P}^{c}(y)
,
\sigma_{2}

\widehat{\mathcal{W}}^{s}_{y}(\sigma_{1}(1))
, and
\sigma_{3}

.

This path varies continuously in

.

We set

\rho^{c}(y,x)=d^{c}_{y}(y,\sigma_{1}(1))=\hbox{Length}(\sigma_{1}),

Lemma 4.2
.

If
\varepsilon_{0}
is small enough,
for any

For

from the product of balls

The center-stable plaques

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\varepsilon_{0}
is small enough, then the following holds.
Consider any
x\in\Lambda
and
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}^{uu}_{loc}(x)\setminus\{x\}

\mathcal{P}^{cs}(x),\mathcal{P}^{cs}(x^{\prime})

,

We choose

Working in exponential charts,
all the bundles are close to constant bundles. Hence it is enough to show:

This is proved as follows:

First iterate the points

forward under

.
By local invariance of the plaque families,

The distance between

.
This gives the required estimate.

\diamond

4.2.
Center orientation

We first prove that the neighborhood of any point

For any

As defined in Section

.

If

Proof.

For the proof and as in Section

E^{uu}
,

are nearly orthogonal.
This allows us to work with explicit numbers rather than with a constant

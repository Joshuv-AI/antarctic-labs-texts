1.
Introduction

Let
{M}
be a closed connected smooth
d
-dimensional
Riemannian manifold equipped with the normalized Riemannian
measure
\mu
. Here a closed manifold is a compact manifold
without boundary. For
1\leq p<\infty
, let
L_{p}({M})
be the
set of all complex valued measurable function defined on
{M}

endowed with finite norm

\|f\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{{M}}|f({\bf x})|^{p}{\rm d}\mu({\bf x})\right)^{1/p},

and let
L_{\infty}({M})\equiv C({M})
be the set of all complex valued continuous functions defined on
{M}
endowed with uniform norm

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup\limits_{{\bf x}\in{M}}|f({\bf x})|.

In particular,
L_{2}({M})
is a Hilbert space with inner product

(1.1)

\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{{M}}f({\bf x})\overline{g({\bf x})}{\rm d}\mu({\bf x}),\ {\rm for}\ f,g\in L_{2}({M}),

where
\overline{g}
is the complex conjugate to
g
.

Let
\Delta_{{M}}
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
{M}
, which is self-adjoint in
L_{2}({M})
. This operator has a sequence of
eigenvalues
\{-\lambda_{k}^{2}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
arranged by

0=\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\dots\to\infty,

and a complete orthonormal system of eigenfunctions

\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
. Without loss of generality, we choose
each
\phi_{k}
to be real-valued and
\phi_{0}=1
. Then

\{\phi_{k}\}^{\infty}_{k=0}
is an orthonormal basis for

L_{2}(\mathbb{M})
. For each
n=0,1,2,\dots
, with

\mathcal{P}_{n}
we denote the set of “polynomials” of degree at
most
n
on
{M}
by

\mathcal{P}_{n}:={\rm span}\left\{\phi_{k}:\,\lambda_{k}\leq n\right\}.

This space is called the
diffusion polynomial space of
degree
n
on
{M}
, and an element of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is
called a
diffusion polynomial of degree at most
n
. We
have
two-sides Wely’s law
with exponent
d
and constants

c_{2}(d)>c_{1}(d)>0
(see
[
18
,
25
,
26
,
39
]
), i.e.,

(1.2)

c_{1}(d)n^{d}\leq\sum_{k:\lambda_{k}\leq n}|\phi_{k}({\bf x})|^{2}\leq c_{2}(d)n^{d},\ \ {\bf x}\in{M},

which, by integrating on
{M}
with respect to
{\bf x}
,
implies that the dimension
N
of the space
\mathcal{P}_{n}

satisfies

(1.3)

N:=\dim\mathcal{P}_{n}:=\#\{\phi_{k}:\lambda_{k}\leq n\}\asymp n^{d},

where
\#E
denotes the number of element in the set
E
. Here we
use the notation
A_{n}\asymp B_{n}
to express
A_{n}\ll B_{n}
and

A_{n}\gg B_{n}
, and
A_{n}\ll B_{n}
(
A_{n}\gg B_{n}
) means that there
exists a constant
c>0
independent of
n
such that
A_{n}\leq cB_{n}
(
A_{n}\geq cB_{n}
).

Let
P
be a diffusion polynomial of degree at most
n
.
Then,
P
is of the form
P_{{\mathbf{a}}}

(1.4)

P({\bf x})=P_{{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf x})=\sum_{k:\lambda_{k}\leq n}a_{k}\phi_{k}({\bf x}),\ \ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{M},

where
{\mathbf{a}}=\{a_{k}\}_{\lambda_{k}\leq n}\in{R}^{N}
.

The classical Nikolskii inequality for diffusion polynomials
(see
[
32
, Lemma 5.5]
) states that there exists a positive constant

C(d)
depending only on
d
such that for any
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
,

(1.5)

\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}\leq C(d)n^{d(1/p-1/q)_{+}}\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p},

where
a_{+}=\max(a,0)
for a real number
a
. The order
d(1/p-1/q)_{+}
in the
above inequality (
1.5
) is sharp.

For given
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, we introduce the Nikolskii
factor for
P_{\mathbf{a}}
by

{N}_{p,q}(P_{{\mathbf{a}}}):=\frac{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}.

The worst Nikolskii factor for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is defined by

N^{\rm wor}_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}_{n}):=\sup_{0\neq P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}{N}_{p,q}(P_{{\mathbf{a}}})=\sup_{0\neq P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\frac{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}},

where the supremum is taken over all diffusion polynomials of
degree
\leq n
whose coefficients are not all zero. Then the above
Nikolskii inequality (
1.5
) states

(1.6)

N^{\rm wor}_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}_{n})\asymp n^{d(1/p-1/q)_{+}},

i.e., that the worst case bound for

{N}_{p,q}(P_{{\mathbf{a}}})
is
\Theta(n^{d(1/p-1/q)_{+}})
.

Now we introduce average Nikolskii factors on the compact
manifold
{M}
. Average Nikolskii factors were first
introduced and studied in
[
30
]
. Average Nikolskii factors
on the torus and the sphere were investigated in
[
30
]
and

[
31
]
, respectively. A random diffusion polynomial

P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
is defined by

(1.7)

P_{{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf x})=\sum\limits_{k:\lambda_{k}\leq n}a_{k}\phi_{k}({\bf x}),

where
{\mathbf{a}}=\{a_{k}\}_{\lambda_{k}\leq n}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the
coefficients
a_{k}
are independent
N(0,\sigma^{2})
random
variables with the common normal density functions

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}{\sigma}}e^{-\frac{a_{k}^{2}}{2{\sigma}^{2}}}.

That is,
{\mathbf{a}}\sim N(0,{\sigma}^{2}I_{N})
is an
\mathbb{R}^{N}
-valued Gaussian random
vector with mean
0
and covariance matrix
\sigma^{2}I_{N}
,
where
I_{N}
is the
N
by
N
identity matrix. Let
\gamma_{N}
be the distribution or the law of the random vector
{\mathbf{a}}
. Then
\gamma_{N}
is just the centered Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
given by

\gamma_{N}(G)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{N/2}}\int_{G}e^{-\frac{|{\mathbf{a}}|^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\,d{\mathbf{a}}\ {\rm for\ each\ Borel\ subset}\ G\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},

where

|{\mathbf{a}}|=(\sum_{k:\lambda_{k}\leq n}a^{2}_{k})^{1/2}
.

For given
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, the average Nikolskii
factor for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is defined by

N^{{\rm ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}_{n}):={E}\frac{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}d\gamma_{N}({\mathbf{a}})=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{N/2}}\int_{{R}^{N}}\frac{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}e^{-\frac{|{\mathbf{a}}|^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}d{\mathbf{a}}.

It follows immediately from the definitions of the two Nikolskii
factors that for
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,

N^{{\rm ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}_{n})\leq N^{{\rm wor}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}_{n}).

Nikolskii factors of random diffusion polynomials. Given a

p_{1},\dots,p_{N}
, a random polynomial

is a
polynomial whose coefficients are random, i.e.,

p=\sum_{k=1}^{N}X_{k}p_{k},

where

are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables. In recent years the study of random polynomials
has attracted much interest, and there are numerous papers devoted
to this field, see
[

,

,

,

,

,

There are also many papers devoted to studying the Nikolskii type

. However,

[

The purpose of this paper is to extend these results to the closed
Riemannian manifold. We investigate the average Nikolskii factors

N^{{\rm ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
on the closed manifold

\mathbb{M}
, and show that in many cases, the average Nikolskii

N^{\rm ave}_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
is significantly

N^{\rm wor}_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. Then we have

(1.8)

N^{\rm ave}_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}_{n})=\mathbb{E}\frac{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{q}}{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{p}}\asymp\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&&1\leq p,q<\infty\ or\ p=q=\infty,\\
&(\ln n)^{1/2},&&1\leq p<q=\infty,\\
&(\ln n)^{-1/2},&&1\leq q<p=\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

Remark 1.2
.

Comparing (
1.6
) and (
1.8
), we see that the orders of
the worst and average Nikolskii factors are the same for
1\leq q\leq p<\infty
or
p=q=\infty
, whereas the order of average
Nikolskii factors are significantly smaller than the worst
Nikolskii factors for the other cases. More precisely, for
1\leq p<q<\infty
, the worst Nikolskii factor is of order

n^{d(1/p-1/q)}
, whereas the average Nikolskii factor is of order

n^{0}
, for
1\leq p<q=\infty
, the worst Nikolskii factor is of
order
n^{d/p}
, whereas the average Nikolskii factor is of order

(\ln n)^{1/2}
, and for
1\leq q<p=\infty
, the worst Nikolskii
factor is of order
n^{0}
, whereas the average Nikolskii factor
is of order
(\ln n)^{-1/2}
.

This indicates that for
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
or
1\leq q<p=\infty
,
the order of the average Nikolskii factor
is significantly smaller than the worst case Nikolskii factor.

Let

{\bf x}

{\bf y}

Let

In particular, when

, by (

By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality we have

|e({\bf x},{\bf y},n)|\leq\sqrt{e({\bf x},{\bf x},n)}\sqrt{e({\bf y},{\bf y},n)}\ll n^{d}.

Let

, where
{\mathbf{a}}=\{a_{k}\}_{\lambda_{k}\leq n}\sim N(0,{\sigma}^{2}I_{N})
is a
\mathbb{R}^{N}
-valued Gaussian random

and covariance matrix

. Then

is centered, and its distribution is determined by its covariance

.
Indeed, we have

\displaystyle K({\bf x},{\bf y})

\displaystyle:={E}(P_{{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf x})P_{{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf y}))=\sum\limits_{k:\lambda_{k}\leq n}\sum\limits_{j:\lambda_{j}\leq n}{E}(a_{k}a_{j})\phi_{k}({\bf x})\phi_{j}({\bf y})

We define the Bessel function

of order

Before we proceed to state our main result, we introduce the concept of a maximal separated subset on
\mathbb{M}
. Let
\Xi

\mathbb{M}

\Xi

\varepsilon

\boldsymbol{\xi}
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}

in
\Xi

\varepsilon
. In other words, we require

\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\Xi\\
\boldsymbol{\xi}\neq\boldsymbol{\eta}\end{subarray}}{\rm d}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\eta})\geq\varepsilon.

Furthermore, if an
\varepsilon
-separated subset
\Xi
of

\mathbb{M}
satisfies

\max\limits_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{M}}\inf_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\Xi}{\rm d}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\eta})\leq\varepsilon,

then we refer to it as a

\varepsilon
-separated
subset of

\mathbb{M}
. In simpler terms, a maximal
\varepsilon
-separated subset
\Xi
is
an
\varepsilon
-separated subset of
\mathbb{M}
such that every point in
\mathbb{M}

lies within a distance of
\varepsilon
from at least one point in
\Xi
.
We can express this as

\mathbb{M}=\bigcup\limits_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\Xi}{\rm B}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varepsilon).

We conclude from the Ahlfors
d
-regularity (

) that if

\Xi
is maximal
{\varepsilon}

N^{\rm{ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}_{n})\gg\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&&\ \,1\leq p,q<\infty\ {\rm or}\ p=q=\infty,\\
&(\ln n)^{1/2},&&\ \,1\leq p<q=\infty,\\
&(\ln n)^{-1/2},&&\ \,1\leq q<p=\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

which gives the lower bounds of

N^{\rm{ave}}_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}_{n})
for
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
.

Finally, combining (
4.3
) and (
4.5
), we complete the the proof of Theorem
1.1
.∎

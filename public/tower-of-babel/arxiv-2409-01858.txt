Eigenvalue Inequalities for Fully Nonlinear Elliptic Equations via the Alexandroff-Bakelman-Pucci Method
Dimitrios Gazoulis

Abstract
. In this work we establish eigenvalue inequalities for elliptic differential operators either for Dirichlet or for Robin eigenvalue problems, by using the technique introduced by Alexandroff, Bakelman and Pucci. These inequalities can be extended for fully nonlinear elliptic equations, such as for the Monge-Ampère equation and for Pucci’s equations. As an application we establish a lower bound for the L p − L^{p}- norm of the Laplacian and this bound is sharp, in the sense that, when equality is achieved then a symmetry property is obtained. In addition, we obtain an L ∞ L^{\infty} bound for the gradient of solutions to fully nonlinear elliptic equations and as a result, a C 3 C^{3} estimate.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we present a bound for subsolutions of elliptic differential equations by using the technique introduced by Alexandroff, Bakelman and Pucci to establish their ABP estimate. This bound is in terms of the normal derivative in the boundary of a given domain. As a result we derive eigenvalue inequalities for elliptic differential operators either for Dirichlet or for Robin eigenvalue problems. These bounds can be extended to fully nonlinear equations, such as for the Monge-Ampère equation and for Pucci’s equation.

The Alexandroff, Bakelman and Pucci estimate (or ABP estimate) is an
L^{\infty}
bound for subsolutions of the elliptic problem

(1.1)

\begin{gathered}Lu\geq f\\
\textrm{where}\;\>Lu:=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(x)\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u+\sum_{i}b_{i}(x)\partial_{x_{i}}u+c(x)u\;,\end{gathered}

with
a_{ij},b_{i}
bounded and measurable functions in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
c\leq 0
. Then

(1.2)

\sup_{\Omega}u\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u^{+}+C\>diam(\Omega)\>||Lu||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\;,

for every function
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
. The constant
C
depends only on the coefficients of
L
and
diam(\Omega)
denotes the diameter of
\Omega
(see section 2.5 in
[
13
]
for further details).

The ABP estimate has several extensions to more general problems, such as for viscosity solutions to fully nonlinear elliptic equations. It is also a basic tool in the regularity theory for fully nonlinear elliptic equations of the form

\displaystyle F(\nabla^{2}u)=0\;,

see for instance
[
8
]
.

In this work, motivated by the classical techniques for proving the ABP estimate, together with the techniques in
[
2
]
and
[
5
]
,
we establish Theorem
2.3
, that is an estimate of the form

(1.3)

\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|\leq C\>\left\|\frac{f^{-}}{D^{*}}\right\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\;,

for every function
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
that satisfies (
1.1
). The constant
C
depends only on
n,\;diam(\Omega)
and the coefficients of
L
and
D^{*}=(det(A))^{1/n}
.

Furthermore, X. Cabré in
[
2
]
and
[
5
]
gave an elegant proof of the isoperimetric inequality using the ABP technique. The isoperimetric inequality says that among all domains of given volume the ball has the smallest perimeter. Utilizing similar arguments we obtain a lower bound for the
L^{n}-
norm of the Laplacian in terms of the normal derivative on the boundary of a given domain, that is, Theorem
2.5
, which states that

(1.4)

||\Delta u||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\geq n\alpha\>|B_{1}|^{1/n}\;,

whenever
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\geq\alpha>0
on
\partial\Omega
. In particular, if
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\alpha>0
on
\partial\Omega
, we have

(1.5)

||\Delta u||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\geq\alpha\left(\frac{n^{n(p-1)}|B_{1}|^{p-1}}{|\partial\Omega|^{p-n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p(n-1)}}\;\>,\;\>\textrm{for any}\;\>p\geq n,

(see Theorem
2.5
) and by taking the limit as
p\rightarrow+\infty
, this bound becomes

(1.6)

||\Delta u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\geq\alpha\left(\frac{n^{n}|B_{1}|}{|\partial\Omega|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

A direct corollary of inequalities (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) is a symmetry property when the equality in these estimates is achieved, in the spirit of the work of Serrin in
[
19
]
.

There are additional applications of the estimate in (
1.4
), for instance, when it is applied to semilinear equations of the form

(1.7)

\Delta u=f(u)\;,

we obtain an
L^{\infty}
bound for the gradient of the solutions, see Theorem
2.8
.
We present these results in section 2, where we also obtain a
C^{3}
estimate as application of this
L^{\infty}
bound.

The estimate (
1.3
) can be established for the Monge-Ampère equation and we similarly derive eigenvalue inequalities. In particular, in section 3 we address the eigenvalue problem

(1.8)

\begin{cases}det(\nabla^{2}u)=\lambda|u|^{n}\;\;\;,\;\textrm{in}\;\;\Omega\\
\;\;\;\;\;\;\;u=0\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;,\;\textrm{on}\;\;\partial\Omega\end{cases}

We note that also in
[
4
]
, eigenvalue inequalities have been proved via the ABP method for the first eigenvalue of an elliptic operator.

Finally, in section 4, we extend some of these results to viscosity solutions of fully nonlinear elliptic equations. Particularly, Theorem
4.4
is an estimate of the form (
1.3
), from which we establish the analogous eigenvalue inequalities for both Dirichlet and Robin eigenvalue problems and in Theorem
4.7
, an
L^{\infty}
bound for the gradient of smooth solutions is derived. This
L^{\infty}
bound gives a
C^{3}
estimate for the solutions as in the case of the Laplacian in section 2.

2.
A Bound for the normal derivative of subsolutions to Linear Elliptic equations

Suppose
\Omega
is an open, bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and denote as
\nu
the unit normal pointing outwards. Throughout this section will denote as
L
a linear elliptic differential operator of the form

(2.1)

Lu:=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(x)\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u+\sum_{i}b_{i}(x)\partial_{x_{i}}u+c(x)u\;,

where
a_{ij}\;,\>b_{i}
and
c
are continuous in
\Omega\;,\;c\leq 0
and
L
is uniformly elliptic in
\Omega
in the following sense:

(2.2)

\theta|\xi|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\Theta|\xi|^{2}\;\;\;,\;\;\forall\;\>x\in\Omega\;\textrm{and any}\;\>\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

for some positive constants
\theta\leq\Theta
. Consider also
D^{*}=(det(A))^{1/n}
where
A=(a_{ij})
is positive definite in
\Omega
and
0<\theta\leq D^{*}\leq\Theta
.

We also introduce the concept of contact sets. For
u\in C^{1}(\Omega)
we define

(2.3)

\Gamma_{u}:=\{x\in\Omega\>:\>u(y)\geq u(x)+\nabla u(x)\cdot(y-x)\;,\>\forall\;y\in\overline{\Omega}\}\;.

The set
\Gamma_{u}
is called the
lower contact
set of
u
. It is the set of points
x
such as the tangent hyperplane to the graph
u
at
x
lies below
u
in all of
\overline{\Omega}.

Similarly we define the
upper contact
set of
u
,

(2.4)

\Gamma^{u}:=\{x\in\Omega\>:\>u(y)\leq u(x)+\nabla u(x)\cdot(y-x)\;,\>\forall\;y\in\overline{\Omega}\}\;,

\Gamma^{u}
is the set of points
x
such as the tangent hyperplane to the graph
u
at
x
lies above
u
in all of
\overline{\Omega}.
We also denote as
B_{r}(x_{0})
(or
B_{r}
), the ball of radius
r
centered at some point
x_{0}
.

To begin with, we state the following property of
C^{1}
functions. We rely on the arguments in the proof of the isoperimetric inequality in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

(2.5)

m:=\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|>0\;.

Then either

(2.6)

B_{m}(0)\subset\nabla u(\Gamma_{u})\;\;,\;\>\textrm{if}\;\>\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\bigm|_{\partial\Omega}\>>0\;,

or

(2.7)

B_{m}(0)\subset\nabla u(\Gamma^{u})\;\;,\;\>\textrm{if}\;\>\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\bigm|_{\partial\Omega}\><0\;.

Proof.

Without loss of generality assume that
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}>0
on
\partial\Omega
.

Let
z\in B_{m}(0)
, where
m=\inf_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}.\\

Consider the function

\displaystyle H(x)=u(x)-z\cdot x\;.

So, there exists
y\in\overline{\Omega}
such that

(2.8)

H(y)=\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}H(x)\;.

This is, up to a sign, the Legendre transform of
u
.

Now, we claim that
y\in\Omega
. Indeed, if
y\in\partial\Omega
, then

(2.9)

\frac{\partial H}{\partial\nu}(y)\leq 0\;,

that is,

(2.10)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}(y)\leq z\cdot\nu\leq|z|<m\;,

which contradicts the definition of
m
.

Therefore
y\in\Omega
, which means that
H
attains its minimum at an interior point of
\Omega
.

Thus

(2.11)

\nabla u(y)=z\;\;\;\textrm{and}\;\>y\in\Gamma_{u}.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
g\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is nonnegative. Then for any
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
m:=\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|>0
, there holds either

(2.12)

\int_{B_{m}(0)}g\leq\int_{\Gamma_{u}}g(\nabla u)|det(\nabla^{2}u)|\;\;,\;\>\textrm{if}\;\>\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\bigm|_{\partial\Omega}\>>0\;,

or

(2.13)

\int_{B_{m}(0)}g\leq\int_{\Gamma^{u}}g(\nabla u)|det(\nabla^{2}u)|\;\;,\;\>\textrm{if}\;\>\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\bigm|_{\partial\Omega}\><0\;.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Lemma 2.24 in
[
13
]
by considering

\displaystyle\tilde{M}=m=\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|

and utilizing Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies
Lu\geq f
in
\Omega
with the following conditions:
\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|>0
and
\dfrac{|b|}{D^{*}}\>,\>\dfrac{f}{D^{*}}\in L^{n}(\Omega)
.

Then there holds that

(2.14)

\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|\leq C\left\|\frac{f^{-}}{D^{*}}\right\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\;,

and
C
is a constant depending only on
n\>,\;diam(\Omega)
and
\left\|\dfrac{b}{D^{*}}\right\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}
.

Proof.

We sketch the proof in the case where
f=c=0
, since it is similar to the proof of Theorem 2.21 in
[
13
]
. In our case, we set
\tilde{M}=\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|
.

The elliptic inequality
Lu\geq f
gives that

\displaystyle(-\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u)^{n}\leq|b|^{n}|\nabla u|^{n}\;.

For any positive definite matrix
A=(a_{ij})
it holds

(2.15)

\displaystyle det(\nabla^{2}u)\leq\frac{1}{D}\left(\frac{-\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u}{n}\right)^{n}\;\;\;\textrm{on}\;\>\Gamma^{u}\;\>,\;\textrm{where}\;\>D=det(A).

So, we consider
g(z)=(|z|^{n}+\delta^{n})^{-n}\;,\;\delta>0
and then we apply Lemma
2.2
. By letting
\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}
we conclude.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

(1)
There are various problems with Neumann boundary conditions of the form

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=g(x)\;\;,\;\textrm{on}\;\>\partial\Omega\;,

with
|g(x)|\geq c_{0}>0
in which the assumption
\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|>0
is satisfied.

(2)
In particular, it holds that either

(2.16)

\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|\leq C\left\|\frac{f^{-}}{D^{*}}\right\|_{L^{n}(\Gamma^{u})}\;\;,\;\>\textrm{if}\;\>\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\bigm|_{\partial\Omega}\><0\;,

or

(2.17)

\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|\leq C\left\|\frac{f^{-}}{D^{*}}\right\|_{L^{n}(\Gamma_{u})}\;\;,\;\>\textrm{if}\;\>\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\bigm|_{\partial\Omega}\>>0\;.

2.1.
A Lower bound for the norm of the Laplacian

We will prove that the
L^{n}-
norm of the Laplacian of a smooth function
u
is bounded from below by the normal derivative of
u
on
\partial\Omega
. This estimate can be extended to an
L^{p}
and
L^{\infty}
lower bound of the norm when the normal derivative of the function is constant on the boundary of the domain. Again, the proof relies on the proof of Theorem 4.1 in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be such that
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\geq\alpha>0
on
\partial\Omega
. Then

(2.18)

||\Delta u||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\geq n\alpha\>|B_{1}|^{1/n}\;.

In addition, if
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\alpha>0
on
\partial\Omega
and
\Delta u\geq 0
, we have

(2.19)

||\Delta u||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\geq\alpha\left(\frac{n^{n(p-1)}|B_{1}|^{p-1}}{|\partial\Omega|^{p-n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p(n-1)}}\;,

for any
p\geq n
. In particular,

(2.20)

||\Delta u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\geq\alpha\left(\frac{n^{n}|B_{1}|}{|\partial\Omega|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have that

(2.21)

B_{\alpha}(0)\subset\nabla u(\Gamma_{u})\;.

We now apply Lemma
2.2
with
g\equiv 1
, so

(2.22)

|B_{1}|\>\alpha^{n}\leq\int_{\Gamma_{u}}det(\nabla^{2}u)dx\;.

Thus by the classical arithmetic geometric mean inequality applied to the non negative eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}
of
\nabla^{2}u\;,\>x\in\Gamma_{u}
, we have

(2.23)

det(\nabla^{2}u)\leq\left(\frac{tr(\nabla^{2}u)}{n}\right)^{n}=\left(\frac{\Delta u}{n}\right)^{n}\;\;\;,\>x\in\Gamma_{u}\;.

Therefore

(2.24)

|B_{1}|\>\alpha^{n}\leq\frac{1}{n^{n}}\int_{\Gamma_{u}}(\Delta u)^{n}dx\leq\frac{1}{n^{n}}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{n}dx

and we conclude

(2.25)

n\alpha\>|B_{1}|^{1/n}\leq\>||\Delta u||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\;.

Next, for proving (
2.19
), assume that

(2.26)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\alpha>0\;\;\;\textrm{on}\;\>\partial\Omega

and by Hölder’s inequality on
||\Delta u||^{n}_{L^{n}(\Omega)}
,

(2.27)

\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{n}dx=\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{s}|\Delta u|^{n-s}dx\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|\>dx\right)^{s}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{\frac{n-s}{1-s}}dx\right)^{1-s}\;,

for any
s\in(0,1)
, which gives

(2.28)

n^{n}\alpha^{n}\>|B_{1}|\leq\alpha^{s}|\partial\Omega|^{s}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{\frac{n-s}{1-s}}dx\right)^{1-s}\;,

by (
2.18
).

So, we set
p:=\dfrac{n-s}{1-s}
,
p\in(n,+\infty)\;,\>s
is written as
s=\dfrac{p-n}{p-1}
and we get

(2.29)

||\Delta u||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p(n-1)}{p-1}}\geq\frac{n^{n}\alpha^{\frac{p(n-1)}{p-1}}|B_{1}|}{|\partial\Omega|^{\frac{p-n}{p-1}}}\;,

which gives (
2.19
).

For (
2.20
), we take the limit
p\rightarrow+\infty
in (
2.19
) and we conclude.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be such that
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\bigm|_{\partial\Omega}\geq\alpha>0
and
\Delta u\geq 0
. Then

(2.30)

||\Delta u||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\geq\left(\frac{(n\alpha)^{n(p-1)}|B_{1}|^{p-1}}{(\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}dS)^{p-n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p(n-1)}}\;,

for any
p\geq n
. In addition,

(2.31)

||\Delta u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\geq\left(\frac{(n\alpha)^{n}|B_{1}|}{\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}dS}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

A direct consequence of the Theorem
2.5
is a symmetry property for functions that satisfy the equality in (
2.18
). This result is in the spirit of Serrin’s in
[
19
]
.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be such that
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\geq\alpha>0
on
\partial\Omega
and assume that the equality holds in (
2.18
).

Then

(2.32)

u(x)=c_{0}|x-x_{0}|^{2}\;\;\;,\;\>\textrm{and}\;\>\Omega\;\>\textrm{is a ball centered at}\;\>x_{0}.

Proof.

When the equality in (
2.18
) holds, we have equality in (
2.23
). This means that all the eigenvalues of
\nabla^{2}u
are equal and thus
\nabla^{2}u=kI_{n}
, where
k\in\mathbb{R}
and
I_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix. So we conclude that
u(x)=c_{0}|x-x_{0}|^{2}
for some
c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Now we have that
\Gamma_{u}=\Omega
and
B_{\alpha}(0)\subset\nabla u(\Omega)=c_{0}\Omega-c_{0}x_{0}.\\

Thus

(2.33)

B_{\frac{\alpha}{c_{0}}}(x_{0})\subset\Omega\;\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;|B_{\frac{\alpha}{c_{0}}}(x_{0})|=|\Omega|

If there exists
y_{0}\in\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\frac{\alpha}{c_{0}}}(x_{0})
, we would have
B_{\delta}(y_{0})\subset\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{\frac{\alpha}{c_{0}}}(x_{0})
and contradicts the equality of the measures in (
2.33
).

So,
\overline{\Omega}=\overline{B}_{\frac{\alpha}{c_{0}}}(x_{0})
and since
\Omega
is open, then
\Omega
is a ball centered at
x_{0}.

∎

Note that the same symmetry property is obtained when the equality in (
2.19
) or (
2.20
) holds. So, one question could be, if one of the inequalities (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) is “close” of being equality, whether
u
is “close” of being of the form (
2.32
).

Moreover, another consequence of Theorem
2.5
, together with the Krylov-Safonov Harnack inequality (see
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
), is an
L^{\infty}
bound for the gradient of solutions to semilinear elliptic equations.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
u
be a smooth solution of

(2.34)

\Delta u=f(u)\;\;\;,\;\;x\in B_{R}

such that
u=0
on
\partial B_{R}
, where
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and we extend
u
to be zero outside
B_{R}
.

Then

(2.35)

\sup_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}|B_{1}|^{2/n}}||f(u)||^{2}_{L^{n}(B_{R})}+R||\>|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\>||_{L^{n}(B_{R})}\right)

the constant
C
depends only on
n
and
R^{2}||f^{\prime}(u)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}
.

Proof.

Let
P=|\nabla u|^{2}
, we calculate

(2.36)

\Delta P=2|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+2f^{\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}

so
P
satisfies the elliptic equation

\frac{1}{2}\Delta P-f^{\prime}(u)P=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2},

where
|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}
.

Consider
\tilde{b}
such that
|f^{\prime}(u)|\leq\tilde{b}
for all
x\in B_{2R}
(for example, if
f
is globally Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
\tilde{M}
, we can take
\tilde{b}=\tilde{M}
).

We then apply Krylov-Safonov Harnack inequality (see for example Theorem 4.1 in
[
7
]
) and we get

(2.37)

\sup_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\left(\inf_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}+R||\>|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\>||_{L^{n}(B_{R})}\right)\;,

where
C
depends only on
n
and
R^{2}||f^{\prime}(u)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}
.

Now w.l.o.g. assume that
\inf_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}>0
, otherwise (
2.35
) holds. Since
u=0
on
\partial B_{R}
, we have that
\nabla u\perp\partial B_{R}
, which means that
\nabla u
and the unit normal of
\partial B_{R}
are parallel. Thus,
\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|=|\nabla u|
on
\partial B_{R}
and we get

(2.38)

\inf_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\inf_{\partial B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}=\left(\inf_{\partial B_{R}}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|\right)^{2}\leq\left(\frac{1}{n|B_{1}|^{1/n}}||f(u)||_{L^{n}(B_{R})}\right)^{2}

where for the last inequality we utilized Theorem
2.5
and (
2.34
).
∎

As an application of Theorem (
2.35
), we have the following
C^{3}
estimate

Corollary 2.9
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.8
, it holds

(2.39)

\sup_{B_{R}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\Delta u_{x_{i}}|^{2}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}|B_{1}|^{2/n}}||f(u)||^{2}_{L^{n}(B_{R})}+R||\>|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\>||_{L^{n}(B_{R})}\right)

the constant
C
depends only on
n\;,\>
and
R^{2}||f^{\prime}(u)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}
.

Proof.

By differentiating (
2.34
) we have

(2.40)

|\Delta u_{x_{i}}|\leq||f^{\prime}(u)||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}|u_{x_{i}}|

and utilizing (
2.35
) we conclude.
∎

) holds without assuming that

such that

is locally Lipschitz and

||f(u)||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\geq n\>|B_{1}|^{1/n}\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|

If in addition

and

, it holds

(2.43)

||f(u)||_{L^{n}(B_{1})}\geq n\>|B_{1}|^{1/n}|u^{\prime}(1)|

2.42
) is a direct consequence of Theorem

.

.

Remark 2.11
.

(1)
The symmetry property in Corollary
2.7
can be alternatively considered as uniqueness of the minimization problem

(2.44)

\mathscr{J}(u)=\int_{B_{1}}|\Delta u|^{n}dx

subject to the constraint
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\bigm|_{\partial B_{1}}\geq\alpha>0
, or even for the functionals

\mathscr{J}_{p}(u)=\int_{B_{1}}|\Delta u|^{p}dx\;\>\textrm{for}\;\>p>n\;,\;\mathscr{J}_{\infty}(u)=||\Delta u||_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},

when

(2)
In particular, if

in Corollary
2.10
where

and
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\geq\alpha>0
on

, then

(2.45)

||u||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\geq\left[\frac{(n\alpha)^{n}\>|B_{1}|}{c_{0}}\right]^{1/p}

Similarly, we can obtain other bounds by considering various examples.

2.2.
Applications: Lower bounds for eigenvalue problems

2.2.1.
A Lower bound for eigenvalue problems of the Laplacian

In this subsection we derive eigenvalue inequalities for the Laplacian for both the Dirichlet and the Robin eigenvalue problems. These inequalities are consequences of the Theorem
2.5
. We begin with the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
\phi\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be the eigenfunction of the problem

(2.46)

\begin{cases}-\Delta\phi=\lambda\phi\;\;\;,\;\;\textrm{in}\;\>\Omega\\
\phi=0\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;,\;\;\textrm{on}\;\>\partial\Omega\end{cases}

Then

(2.47)

\lambda\geq n|B_{1}|^{1/n}\frac{\inf_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla\phi|}{||\phi||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}}

Proof.

By Theorem
2.5
we have

(2.48)

\begin{gathered}||\Delta\phi||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\geq n|B_{1}|^{1/n}\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|\\
\Rightarrow|\lambda|\geq n|B_{1}|^{1/n}\frac{\inf_{\partial\Omega}|\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}|}{||\phi||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}}\end{gathered}

Since
u
is zero on
\partial\Omega
, it holds that
\nabla u//\nu
, so

(2.49)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=|\nabla u\cdot\nu|=|\nabla u|\;\;\;,\;\textrm{on}\;\>\partial\Omega

and we conclude.
∎

Note:
If
\Omega=B_{1}
,
\phi
is radially symmetric given explicitly by a Bessel function and
\inf_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla\phi|=|\phi^{\prime}(1)|
. If in addition
n=3
, we can write

(2.50)

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(x)=\mu\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\lambda_{1}}}\frac{sin(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|x|)}{|x|}\;\;,\;\textrm{for some positive constant}\;\>\mu,

here we denote as
\phi_{1}
the principal eigenfuction of the Laplacian and
\lambda_{1}
the respective principal eigenvalue. Then we can calculate

(2.51)

\displaystyle||\phi_{1}||_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\leq\mu\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}|B_{1}|^{1/3}

and inequality (
2.47
) can be written as

(2.52)

\lambda_{1}^{3/2}\geq 3|\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}cos(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}})-sin(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}})|

By considering the function
f(t)=\dfrac{sint}{t^{3}}-\dfrac{cost}{t^{2}}\;,\;t>0
we can calculate the values
t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}
where
f(t)=\frac{1}{3}
and observe that
\lambda_{1}\geq t_{3}^{2}
.
This bound, of course, is known since
\lambda_{1}
is the square of the first root of the Bessel function in (
2.50
). However, the lower bound in (
2.47
) can be generalized to fully nonlinear equations for both Dirichlet and Robin eigenvalue problems.

Remark 2.13
.

Let
L_{0}u=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(x)\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u+\sum_{i}b_{i}(x)\partial_{x_{i}}u
be an elliptic operator and consider the eigenvalue problem

(2.53)

\begin{cases}-L_{0}u_{1}=\lambda_{1}u_{1}\;\>,\;\textrm{in}\;\>\Omega\\
\;\;\;\;\;\;u_{1}=0\;\;\;\;\;\;\;,\;\;\textrm{on}\;\>\partial\Omega\end{cases}

In

, Berestycki, Nirenberg and Varadhan proved the existence of a unique eigenvalue

Next, we derive a similar bound for the Robin eigenvalue problem.

Corollary 2.14
.

Let

be the eigenfunction of the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta\phi=\lambda\phi\;\;\;\;,\;\;\textrm{in}\;\>\Omega\\

(2.55)

|\lambda|\geq n|B_{1}|^{1/n}|\alpha|\frac{\inf_{\partial\Omega}|\phi|}{||\phi||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}}

Proof.

We note that

∎

.

.

Let

be a solution to the eigenvalue problem (

).

Then

(3.6)

\lambda\geq|B_{1}|\left(\frac{\inf_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|}{||u||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}}\right)^{n}

In addition, we have the following estimate

(3.7)

\inf_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|\leq\frac{C(n)}{|\Omega|^{2/n}}||u||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}

for some constant

depending only on the dimension

.

Without loss of generality assume that

(in the case where

We apply Theorem

and obtain

(3.8)

\lambda^{1/n}||u||_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\geq|B_{1}|^{1/n}\inf_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}

Now since

on

Finally, for the estimate (

∎

4.
The case of Fully Nonlinear Elliptic Equations

For the convenience of the reader we introduce Pucci’s extremal operators and the respective spaces of viscosity subsolutions and supersolutions. We follow chapter 3 in
[
8
]
, where the ABP estimate is proved for viscosity solutions of fully nonlinear elliptic equations. These results are originated in
[

]
,
[
10
]
.

First we give the definition of viscosity solutions for fully nonlinear equations.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A continuous function
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a viscosity subsolution (respectively supersolution) of the equation

(4.1)

F(\nabla^{2}u(x),x)=f(x)\;\;\;,\;x\in\Omega

when the following condition holds:

If
x_{0}\in\Omega\;,\>\phi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and
u-\phi
has a local maximum at
x_{0}
then

(4.2)

F(\nabla^{2}\phi(x_{0}),x_{0})\geq f(x_{0})

(respectively if
u-\phi
has a local minimum at
x_{0}
then
F(\nabla^{2}\phi(x_{0}),x_{0})\leq f(x_{0})
).

If
u
is both subsolution and supersolution we say that
u
is a viscosity solution of (
4.1
).

We use the notation
F(\nabla^{2}u,x)=f(x)
in the viscosity sense in
\Omega
whenever
u
is a viscosity solution (or
\geq\>,\;\leq
for subsolution, supersolution respectively).

Let now
0<\vartheta\leq\varTheta
and
A\in\mathcal{S}
, where
\mathcal{S}
is the space of real
n\times n
symmetric matrices. We define

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{-}(A,\vartheta,\varTheta)=\mathcal{M}^{-}(A)=\vartheta\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i}+\varTheta\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{+}(A,\vartheta,\varTheta)=\mathcal{M}^{+}(A)=\varTheta\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i}+\vartheta\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}

where
e_{i}=e_{i}(A)
are the eigenvalues of
A
and
\mathcal{M}^{-}\;,\>\mathcal{M}^{+}
are the Pucci’s extremal operators.

It holds that
\mathcal{M}^{-}
and
\mathcal{M}^{+}
are uniformly elliptic (see section 2 in
[
8
]
).

Definition 4.2
.

Let
f
be a continuous function in
\Omega
and
0<\vartheta\leq\varTheta.\\

(1) We denote as
\mathscr{M}^{+}(\vartheta,\varTheta,f)
the space of continuous functions
u
in
\Omega
such that

\mathcal{M}^{+}(\nabla^{2}u,\vartheta,\varTheta)\geq f(x)

in the viscosity sense.
(2) Similarly, we denote as
\mathscr{M}^{-}(\vartheta,\varTheta,f)
the space of continuous functions
u
in
\Omega
such that

\mathcal{M}^{-}(\nabla^{2}u,\vartheta,\varTheta)\leq f(x)

in the viscosity sense. (3) We set
\mathscr{M}(\vartheta,\varTheta,f)=\mathscr{M}^{+}(\vartheta,\varTheta,f)\cap\mathscr{M}^{-}(\vartheta,\varTheta,f)
.

We will now prove the analog of Theorem
2.3
.

Let

and

is a continuous bounded function. Assume that

The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem 3.2 in

∎

Let

\lambda\geq\frac{\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|}{C||u^{p}||_{L^{n}(\Omega\cap\Gamma_{u})}}

.

Let
u\in\mathscr{M}^{-}(\vartheta,\varTheta,\lambda|u|^{p})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a viscosity supersolution of (
4.25
) and assume that
\inf_{\partial\Omega}\left|u\right|>0
.

Then

(4.27)

\lambda\geq\frac{|\alpha|\inf_{\partial\Omega}|u|}{C||u^{p}||_{L^{n}(\Omega\cap\Gamma_{u})}}

4.4
.

Remark 4.11
.

Note that Corollaries
4.9
and
4.10
can be generalized to the eigenvalue problems

(4.28)

\begin{cases}F(\nabla^{2}u)=\lambda|u|^{p}\;\;,\;\textrm{in}\;\>\Omega\\
\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;u=0\;\;\;\;\;,\;\textrm{on}\;\>\partial\Omega\end{cases}

and the Robin eigenvalue problem

(4.29)

\begin{cases}F(\nabla^{2}u)=\lambda|u|^{p}\;\;,\;\textrm{in}\;\>\Omega\\

respectively, via Corollary
4.5
. Here
F

\vartheta\;,\>\varTheta

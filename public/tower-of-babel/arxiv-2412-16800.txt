The time-relaxation limit for weak solutions to the quantum hydrodynamics system
Paolo Antonelli, Pierangelo Marcati, Hao Zheng

Abstract
. This paper analyzes weak solutions of the quantum hydrodynamics (QHD) system with a collisional term posed on the one-dimensional torus. The main goal of our analysis is to rigorously prove the time-relaxation limit towards solutions to the quantum drift-diffusion (QDD) equation. The existence of global in time, finite energy weak solutions can be proved by straightforwardly exploiting the polar factorization and wave function lifting tools previously developed by the authors. However, the sole energy bounds are not sufficient to show compactness and then pass to the limit. For this reason, we consider a class of more regular weak solutions (termed GCP solutions), determined by the finiteness of a functional involving the chemical potential associated with the system. For solutions in this class and bounded away from vacuum, we prove the time-relaxation limit and provide an explicit convergence rate. Our analysis exploits compactness tools and does not require the existence (and smoothness) of solutions to the limiting equations or the well-preparedness of the initial data. As a by-product of our analysis, we also establish the existence of global in time H 2 H^{2} solutions to a nonlinear Schrödinger-Langevin equation and construct solutions to the QDD equation as strong limits of GCP solutions to the QHD system.

1.
Introduction

The main object of this paper is to study the relaxation limit of weak solutions for the collisional one-dimensional quantum hydrodynamic (QHD) system,

(1.1)

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{t}\rho+{\partial}_{x}J=0\\
{\partial}_{t}J+{\partial}_{x}\left(\frac{J^{2}}{\rho}\right)+{\partial}_{x}p(\rho)+\rho{\partial}_{x}V=\frac{1}{2}\rho{\partial}_{x}\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)-\frac{1}{\tau}J\\
-{\partial}_{x}^{2}V=\rho-\mathcal{C}(x),\quad(\rho,J)(0,x)=(\rho_{0},J_{0})(x),\end{cases}

for
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}}
,
T>0
.
{\mathbb{T}}
denotes the one-dimensional torus that, without loss of generality, we assume
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbf{R}}/{\mathbf{Z}}
. The unknowns
\rho
and
J
in (
1.1
) represent the mass (charge) and the momentum (current) densities of the quantum fluid, respectively, and
p(\rho)
is the isentropic pressure term. The parameter
\tau>0
denotes the scaled momentum relaxation time.

In order to study the relaxation limit, we also provide a global existence result for weak solutions to (
1.1
) and a global wellposedness result for the associated Schrödinger-Langevin equation (see (
1.21
) below) for non-vacuum initial states.
Furthermore, as a by-product of our analysis we also show a global existence result for solutions of the limiting equation (Quantum Drift-Diffusion equation, see (
1.5
) below).
For this reason, our analysis does not need to assume any property on the solutions of the limiting equations, as usually done with relative energy/entropy techniques, for instance.

Among several applications, model (
1.1
) can be used also to model carrier transport in semiconductor devices
[
23
]
, where the function
V
denotes a self-consistent electric potential, satisfying the Poisson equation, and
\mathcal{C}(x)
denotes the density of background positively charged ions.
It is also possible to consider, with similar analysis, different Poisson couplings, e.g. repulsive potentials, but we will not develop this aspect in detail.
When the doping profile is relevant at nanoscales, quantum effects must be taken into account
[
1
,
17
]
.

The usual stress tensor for quantum fluids takes the following form
[
9
]

\Pi=-p(\rho)+\frac{1}{4}(\rho{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\rho),

whereas its quantum part satisfies the following identities

(1.2)

\frac{1}{2}\rho{\partial}_{x}\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)=\frac{1}{4}{\partial}_{x}(\rho{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\rho)=\frac{1}{4}{\partial}_{x}^{3}\rho-{\partial}_{x}[({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}].

The left hand side is commonly interpreted as a quantum pressure expressed in terms of a quantum enthalpy, while the right hand side provides the decoupling into a linear dispersive tensor and a quadratic term taking into account the Fisher information
[
20
]
.

Quantum fluid models of this type have applications in various physical phenomena, in particular where the thermal de Broglie wavelength is comparable with the interatomic distance.
For instance, this is the case in superfluidity of Helium II
[
28
]
, Bose-Einstein condensates
[
37
]
and quantum plasmas
[
19
]
.

Besides its relevance in semiconductor device modeling, the collisional model (
1.1
) with
\tau>0
can also be seen as a toy model to study the interaction of a quantum fluid with a classical one, as considered in the two-fluid model proposed by Landau and Tisza, see the textbooks
[
28
, Section 17]
and
[
30
, Chapter XVI]
.
The derivation of the collisional term, in the case of semiconductor modeling, can be obtained either by using a phenomenological approach, taking into account the thermal interactions between charged particles (see
[
1
,
2
,
17
]
or Section 7 in
[
6
]
), or by using kinetic-type models as an approximation of intra-band collisions, see
[
8
]
.

The main goal of this paper is the study of the relaxation-time limit of system (
1.1
). More precisely, by introducing the scaling
[
34
]
(see also
[
15
,
35
]
for more general discussion),

(1.3)

t^{\prime}=\tau t,\quad(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})(t^{\prime},x)=\left(\rho,\frac{1}{\tau}J\right)\left(\frac{t^{\prime}}{\tau},x\right),

the system (
1.1
) can be reformulated as

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\partial}_{t^{\prime}}\rho_{\tau}+{\partial}_{x}J_{\tau}=0\\
&\tau^{2}{\partial}_{t^{\prime}}J_{\tau}+\tau^{2}{\partial}_{x}\left(\frac{J_{\tau}^{2}}{\rho_{\tau}}\right)+{\partial}_{x}p(\rho_{\tau})+\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}V_{\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}}{\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}}\right)-J_{\tau}\\
&-{\partial}_{x}^{2}V_{\tau}=\rho_{\tau}-\mathcal{C}(x),\quad(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})(0,x)=(\rho_{0},J_{\tau,0})(x),\end{aligned}\right.

As
\tau\to 0
, system (
1.4
) formally converges to the following Quantum Drift-diffusion (QDD) equation,

(1.5)

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{t^{\prime}}\bar{\rho}+{\partial}_{x}\left[\frac{1}{2}\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}}{\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}}\right)-{\partial}_{x}p(\bar{\rho})-\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}\bar{V}\right]=0\\
-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\bar{V}=\bar{\rho}-\mathcal{C}(x),\quad\rho_{\tau}(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),\end{cases}

see for instance
[
23
,
38
]
.

The main goal of our paper is the rigorous justification of this limit in a suitable functional framework that will be explained below.

1.1.
Physical framework and weak solutions

A natural framework to study system (
1.1
) is the space of states with finite mass and energy, characterised by the total mass

(1.6)

M(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(t)dx\equiv M_{0},

and total energy functional

(1.7)

E(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}+\frac{J^{2}}{2\rho}+f(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}({\partial}_{x}V)^{2}dx.

The internal energy
f(\rho)
, appearing in (
1.7
), is related to the pressure
p(\rho)
via the relation

(1.8)

p(\rho)=f^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-f(\rho),

or equivalently

f(\rho)=\rho\int^{\rho}\frac{p(s)}{s^{2}}ds.

In this paper we will consider constitutive relations for the pressure
p=p(\rho)
such that
p\in C^{2}((0,\infty))\cap C^{1}([0,\infty))
and
f=f(\rho)\geq 0
.
Moreover, in Theorem
6
below we will restrict to classical
\gamma-
law equations of state for the pressure.
In the Poisson equation in (
1.1
) satisfied by the electric potential
V
, we assume
\mathcal{C}(x)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{C}(x)dx=M_{0}
, which guarantees the solvability of the Poisson equation.
By combining the authors’ previous works
[
4
,
5
]
and
[
7
]
, it is possible to prove an existence theorem for global in time finite energy weak solutions, that is stated below here for completeness.

Proposition 1

(Global Existence of finite energy weak solutions)
.

Let
(\rho_{0},J_{0})
be a finite energy initial datum such that,

(1.9)

\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|J_{0}/\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C

and let us further assume that
J_{0}/\sqrt{\rho_{0}}=0
a.e. on the set
\{\rho_{0}=0\}
. Then, there exists a global in time finite energy weak solution to the Cauchy problem that satisfies

(1.10)

E(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{J^{2}}{\rho}\,dxds\leq CE(0).

This Proposition provides the existence of arbitrarily large finite energy weak solutions, without further assumptions on their structure. However, the (scaled) uniform bounds in (
1.10
) are not sufficient to pass to the limit in (
1.4
). Indeed, while the inertial term can be controlled by the scaled energy dissipation, the weak limit of the the quadratic term
({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}})^{2}
(see (
1.2
) above) may be affected by strong oscillations and concentration phenomena, which cannot be controlled by the sole energy bounds.

Therefore, in order to avoid these pathological behaviors, we exploit the compactness framework developed in the authors’ previous work
[
7
]
and introduce the notion of weak solutions with bounded generalised chemical potential, shortened as
GCP solutions
, that are thoroughly described in Definition
11
below. The chemical potential is formally defined as

(1.11)

\mu=-\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}}{2\sqrt{\rho}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{J^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+f^{\prime}(\rho)+V

and it can be formally interpreted as the first variation of the total energy functional with respect to the mass density,

\mu=\frac{\delta E}{\delta\rho}.

Indeed, by noticing that
J=\frac{\delta E}{\delta v}
, then equations (
1.1
) can be formally written as the following system (Hamilton system with a dissipative term formally given by
v=\frac{J}{\rho}
) associated to the energy functional

(1.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\partial}_{t}\rho=-{\partial}_{x}\frac{\delta E}{\delta v}\\
&{\partial}_{t}v=-{\partial}_{x}\frac{\delta E}{\delta\rho}-\frac{1}{\tau}v=-{\partial}_{x}\mu-\frac{1}{\tau}v.\end{aligned}\right.

By using the objects introduced above, we can define the following functional

(1.13)

I(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\rho}{2}(\mu^{2}+\sigma^{2})dx,

where

(1.14)

\sigma={\partial}_{t}\log\sqrt{\rho}=-\frac{{\partial}_{x}J}{2\rho}.

I(t)
somehow may be interpreted as a higher order functional for (
1.1
). In
[
7
]
, it is shown that
I(t)
remains uniformly bounded over compact time intervals, for the Hamiltonian evolution. Moreover,
I(t)
also identifies a compactness class for weak solutions to QHD.
Following the terminology used in
[
7
]
, we denote by GCP solutions, the family of weak solutions with finite energy, see (
1.7
), and functional
I(t)
, defined in (
1.13
), see also Definition
11
.
Unlike
[
7
]
where vacuum regions are allowed,
our analysis requires to consider solutions whose mass density remains uniformly bounded away from vacuum.
Indeed, by using (
1.1
), the time derivative of
I(t)
is formally given by

(1.15)

\frac{d}{dt}I(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{T}\rho\sigma^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu{\partial}_{t}p(\rho)dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Vdx-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}\mu dx.

In particular, the last term in the right hand side of (
1.15
) appears due to the collision term. The chemical potential may experience strong density fluctuations close to vacuum, and possibly generate loss of integrability properties. Therefore in this paper we restrict to strictly positive densities. The rigorous computation of
\frac{d}{dt}I(t)
will be given in Proposition
20
.

1.2.
Main result: global well-posedness

The first result of this paper regards the global well-posedness of GCP weak solutions with strictly positive density. For a more clear definition of the GCP solutions, we refer to Definition
11
.

Theorem 2

(Global well-posedness of GCP weak solutions)
.

Let us consider a finite energy initial datum
(\rho_{0},J_{0})
satisfying the following conditions.

•

Let us denote by
M_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}dx<\infty
the initial total mass, and by
v_{0}=J_{0}/\rho_{0}
the initial velocity. We assume they satisfy

(1.16)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{C}(x)dx=M_{0},\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}}v_{0}dx=0.

•

There exists a
0<\delta\leq M_{0}
such that the initial energy satisfies

(1.17)

E_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{0})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}v_{0}^{2}+f(\rho_{0})+\frac{1}{2}({\partial}_{x}V_{0})^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\delta M_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2},

where
V_{0}
is given by
-{\partial}_{x}^{2}V_{0}=\rho_{0}-\mathcal{C}(x)
.

•

The initial data satisfy the bounds

(1.18)

\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}v_{0}^{2}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\frac{{\partial}_{x}J_{0}}{\sqrt{\rho_{0}}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq I_{0}<\infty.

Then, there exists a unique global in time GCP solution
(\rho,J)
to (
1.1
), such that for any
0\leq t<\infty

•

the density remains strictly positive with lower bound
\inf_{t,x}\rho\geq\delta
;

•

the total energy
E(t)
satisfies the energy balance law, namely for every
t>0
,

(1.19)

E(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}dxds=E_{0};

•

the higher order functional
I(t)
satisfies the bounds

(1.20)

I(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}[({\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}+\rho v^{4}]dxds\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,\tau t)

where
C(\dots,\tau t)
grows at most at the rate
e^{\tau t}
.

Remark 3
.

The condition (
1.17
) is introduced to obtain the lower bound
\delta
of density
\rho_{0}
and
\rho
by using (
1.19
) and Poincaré inequality.
The conditions (
1.16
) in Theorem
2
are preserved along the evolution (
1.1
), so they guarantee the solvability of the Poisson equation in
V
and the periodicity of the phase function
S
respectively, see also Definition
12
below. Last, the condition (
1.18
) is equivalent to the boundedness of the functional
I(t)
at initial time
t=0
, and conversely the bound (
1.20
) implies (
1.18
) holds upto any finite time
t>0
.

Our strategy to prove Theorem
2
is motivated by noticing there is a formal equivalence between system (
1.1
) and the following Schrödinger-Langevin type equation

(1.21)

i{\partial}_{t}\psi+\frac{1}{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi=f^{\prime}(|\psi|^{2})\psi+\frac{1}{\tau}S\psi+V\psi,

see also
[
26
]
.
Here the potential
S
is given through the Madelung transformations
[
33
]
,
\psi=|\psi|e^{iS}
as the phase function associated to
\psi
, and it can be formally defined as
S=\frac{1}{i}\log\left(\frac{\psi}{|\psi|}\right)
.
Equation (
1.21
) was introduced, in the case
f=0
, by Kostin
[
29
]
(see also
[
39
]
and
[
36
]
) to derive a Schrödinger equation which was taking into account the interaction with Brownian particles.
The usual difficulties entailed by the definition of the complex logarithm prevents us to study equation (
1.21
) with standard methods.

The hydrodynamic system (
1.1
) can be formally recast from (
1.21
) by defining the associated hydrodynamic variables
(\rho,J)=(|\psi|^{2},\IM(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}\psi))
and by computing the related balance laws, see for instance
[
7
]
.

To prove Theorem
2
, we first show the the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for Schrödinger-Langevin equation (
1.21
). We rigorously establish the equivalence between QHD system (
1.1
) and equation (
1.21
) for solutions with strictly positive density, by the methods of wave function lifting
[
7
]
(see also
[
40
]
) and polar factorization
[
4
]
developed in the authors’ previous works. As an independent result, we also state the well-posedness of equation (
1.21
) as the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let us assume that the initial datum
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}})
for (
1.21
) satisfies

(1.22)

E(\psi_{0})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}|{\partial}_{x}\psi_{0}|^{2}+f(|\psi_{0}|^{2})+\frac{1}{2}({\partial}_{x}V_{0})^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{2}(M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\delta M_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{2},

where
M_{0}=\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{C}(x)dx
and
V_{0}
satisfies
-{\partial}_{x}^{2}V_{0}=|\psi_{0}|^{2}-\mathcal{C}(x)
. Then the Cauchy problem associated to (
1.21
) with initial data
\psi_{0}
has a unique global solution
\psi\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty);H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}))
. Moreover,
\inf_{t,x}|\psi|\geq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
and the hydrodynamic functions associated to
\psi
, given by

\rho=|\psi|^{2},\quad J=\IM(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}\psi)=\rho v,

satisfy the bounds (
1.19
) (therefore (
1.22
)) and (
1.20
) on any finite time interval
[0,T]
,
T>0
.

1.3.
Main result: relaxation-time limit

We now turn our attention to the relaxation-time limit
\tau\to 0
. Recall the scaling (
1.3
),

(1.23)

t^{\prime}=\tau t,\quad(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})(t^{\prime},x)=\left(\rho,\frac{1}{\tau}J\right)\left(\frac{t^{\prime}}{\tau},x\right),

then the QHD system (
1.1
) can be written as it appears in (
1.4
), which formally converges towards the QDD equation (
1.5
) as
\tau\to 0
.

The first rigorous justification was given by
[
25
]
for small and smooth perturbations around a constant state, and later extended to the bipolar case, namely the system of electrons and ions, by
[
32
]
. In
[
31
]
, the relaxation-time limit is considered for finite energy weak solutions, however they need to assume the initial data to be well-prepared (see the next paragraph) and the solutions of the limiting equations (
1.5
) to be sufficiently smooth. On the other hand, the analysis of relaxation-time limits is also performed in the classical hydrodynamic equations, see for example
[
21
,
22
,
24
]
.

Let us notice that, under the scaling (
1.23
), the solution
(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})
to (
1.4
) satisfies the following energy inequality

(1.24)

E_{\tau}(t^{\prime})+\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{(J_{\tau})^{2}}{\rho_{\tau}}\,dxds\leq CE_{\tau}(0),

where now the rescaled energy reads

(1.25)

E_{\tau}(t^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}})^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\frac{(J_{\tau})^{2}}{\rho_{\tau}}+f(\rho_{\tau})+\frac{1}{2}({\partial}_{x}V_{\tau})^{2}\,dx.

Notice that (
1.25
) and (
1.24
) are the rescaled versions of (
1.7
) and (
1.19
), respectively.

As we already mentioned, the bounds implied by (
1.24
) are not sufficient to pass to the limit in (
1.4
). Indeed, while the uniform
L^{2}_{t^{\prime},x}
estimate on
J_{\tau}/\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}
allow us to show the convergence to zero of the inertial term, there are no sufficient bounds on
{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}
to analyze the weak limit of the quadratic term
({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}})^{2}
. For this reason, we study the relaxation-time limit in the framework of GCP solutions, see Definition
11
for more details, whose existence is provided by Theorem
2
.
Analogously to (
1.24
), the rescaled version of (
1.20
) reads

(1.26)

I_{\tau}(t^{\prime})+\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}({\partial}_{t^{\prime}}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}})^{2}+\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{4}\,dxds\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,t^{\prime}),

where now
I_{\tau}
is defined by

(1.27)

I_{\tau}(t^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\rho_{\tau}\left(\mu_{\tau}^{2}+\sigma_{\tau}^{2}\right)\,dx,

and the scaled chemical potential is given by

(1.28)

\mu_{\tau}=\frac{1}{\tau}\left(-\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}}{2\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}v_{\tau}^{2}+f^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})+V_{\tau}\right).

For a more detailed discussion about the rescaled quantities, we refer to Section
4
.

One important question that should be noticed in the relaxation-time limit is the phenomenon of initial layer. For the rescaled QHD system (
1.4
), the initial data consists of two components, namely the initial density
\rho_{0}
and the initial momentum density
J_{\tau,0}
. On the other hand, the initial data for the QDD equation (
1.5
) only contains the initial density
\rho_{0}
, and the limiting momentum density is required to satisfy the constitutive relation

(1.29)

\bar{J}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}}{\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}}\right)-{\partial}_{x}p(\bar{\rho})-\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}\bar{V},

which can be formally deduced by passing to the limit
\tau\to 0
in the second equation of (
1.4
).
The initial data for system (
1.4
) are well-prepared if
J_{\tau,0}=\bar{J}_{0}
, where
\bar{J}_{0}
is defined by
\bar{\rho}=\rho_{0}
in (
1.29
).
In our analysis we do not assume the initial data to be well-prepared, therefore an initial layer will naturally appear. As a consequence, the convergence of the momentum density will be only in
\{t>0\}
.

Another novelty with respect to the existing literature is that we are able to provide an explicit rate of convergence with respect to
\tau
.

Theorem 5

(Relaxation-time limit)
.

Let
\{(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})\}_{\tau>0}
be a sequence of GCP solutions of the rescaled equation (
1.4
), such that

•

\inf_{t\textquoteright,x}\rho_{\tau}\geq\delta
for a uniform constant
\delta>0
,

•

(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})
satisfy the bounds (
1.24
) and (
1.26
).

Then the relaxation-time limit holds, namely
\{\rho_{\tau}\}
converges to a limiting density
\bar{\rho}
in the form

\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t^{\prime}}L^{2}_{x}}+\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})\|_{L^{2}_{t^{\prime},x}}\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,T)\tau,

for any
0<T<\infty
. Moreover,
\bar{\rho}
is a weak solution of (
1.5
) in the sense of Definition
15
.

Finally, if the internal energy
f(s)
is convex, then
\bar{\rho}
is a dissipative solution in the sense of Definition
15
.

We remark that our result works for general initial data
(\rho_{0},J_{0})
, and we allow the existence of initial layer. On the other hand, a better convergence rate can be obtained by using the relative energy method and in addition by assuming well-prepared initial data, see for instance
[
31
]
.

The proof of Theorem
5
relies on deriving suitable a priori bounds for solutions to the hydrodynamical system, in order to pass to the limit (at least in the distributional sense) all terms in equations (
1.4
). Similar results are already present in the literature, especially for viscous hydrodynamic systems. For instance, the authors in
[
12
]
consider a one-dimensional model (Navier-Stokes-Korteweg-type system) with (degenerate) viscosity, linear capillarity and nonlinear damping. By exploiting the a priori bounds derived for energy and BD-entropy estimates, the authors prove convergence in the class of weak solutions. Moreover, they also show the convergence of the BD entropy to the so called Bernis-Friedman entropy for the limiting gradient flow equation. See also
[
3
]
, where the relaxation-time limit can be obtained (with no rate) for general weak solutions to quantum Navier-Stokes equations in the three-dimensional torus.

In the absence of viscous terms, such as for (
1.1
), obtaining sufficient a priori bounds becomes more difficult. In particular, we see that the sole energy bounds cannot ensure the convergence of the quadratic term
({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}
appearing in the quantum stress tensor, see (
1.2
). To overcome this problem, and also to obtain dissipative properties of the rescaled equation (
1.4
), we introduce the physical entropy

H(\rho_{\tau})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}\log\left(\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{M_{0}}\right)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(\rho_{\tau})dx,

where
M_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}dx
is the conserved total mass of
\rho_{\tau}
, and
g(s)=s\log(s/M_{0})
.
The functional
H(\rho_{\tau})
is well-defined and can be uniformly bounded in time for GCP solutions, see Section
5
.
These estimates provide us with the
L^{2}_{t^{\prime}}H^{2}_{x}
bound of
\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}
uniformly with respect to
\tau
, which allows us to prove the relaxation-time limit. Moreover, we obtain an explicit convergence rate of this limit with respect to
\tau
by considering the relative entropy

\displaystyle H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})(t^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(\rho_{\tau})-g(\bar{\rho})-g^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})dx,

where
\bar{\rho}
is the limiting solution of the QDD equation (
1.5
).

In fact the estimates given by the energy, the GCP fucntional and the entropy also allow us to prove a time decay estimate of
(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})
. By using (
1.2
), in the special case when there is no pressure and no electrostatic potential, the QDD equation (
1.5
) reduces to the following fourth-order parabolic equation,

(1.30)

{\partial}_{t^{\prime}}\bar{\rho}+\frac{1}{4}{\partial}_{x}^{2}(\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\bar{\rho})=0,

which independently appears also in various places of the mathematical physics literature. Equation (
1.30
) has been first derived by Derrida, Lebowitz, Speer, and Spohn in
[
13
,
14
]
, and we therefore refer to it as the
DLSS equation
. A similar equation was also obtained in
[
10
,
11
]
as a model for thin films. Concerning the mathematical analysis of the DLSS equation, we address to
[
16
,
18
,
27
]
and references therein. By using different techniques, they prove the existence of non-negative solutions and show that the entropy computed along strictly positive solutions decays exponentially in time due to the dissipative feature of fourth-order parabolic equation.

This latter property is actually true also for the hydrodynamic model. In particular, in the present paper we show that also solutions of the rescaled collisional QHD system (
1.4
) possess exponentially decaying physical entropy (Theorem
6
) for
\tau
small enough and suitable functions
f(\rho)
and
\mathcal{C}(x)
. More precisely, in the following Theorem we assume the internal energy to be of the form
f(\rho_{\tau})=(\rho_{\tau}-M_{0})^{2n}
,
n\in{\mathbf{N}}
, and the doping profile to be
\mathcal{C}(x)=M_{0}
. This property distinguishes the collisional QHD system (
\frac{1}{\tau}>0
) from the non-collisional (
\frac{1}{\tau}=0
) one, and eventually breaks the time invertible structure of quantum mechanics as the strength of the thermal interaction between particles goes to infinity.

Theorem 6

(Dissipation for small
\tau
)
.

Let
(\rho_{\tau},J_{\tau})
be a GCP solution of the rescaled QHD system (
1.4
) with
\inf_{t^{\prime},x}\rho\geq\delta>0
. We also assume the internal energy to be
f(\rho_{\tau})=(\rho_{\tau}-M_{0})^{2n}
,
n\in{\mathbf{N}}
, and the electric background density
\mathcal{C}(x)\equiv M_{0}
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)
,
such that, by denoting
c_{1}=C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)^{-1}
, if
\tau\leq\tau_{0}=\min\left\{c_{1},\frac{c_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4},\left(\frac{2c_{1}\delta}{8+\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}
, then the functional

F_{\tau}(t^{\prime})=H(\rho_{\tau})+E_{\tau}(t^{\prime})+c_{1}I_{\tau}(t^{\prime})

satisfies

(1.31)

F_{\tau}(t^{\prime})\leq F_{\tau}(0)\exp\left(C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}dxds-t^{\prime}\right).

In particular,
H(\rho_{\tau})
,
E_{\tau}(t^{\prime})
and
I_{\tau}(t^{\prime})
decay exponentially for
t^{\prime}
large.

Remark 7
.

For the DLSS equation (
1.30
), the authors of
[
27
]
provide the following example of a non-dissipative solution,

\rho_{\tau}=\cos^{2}x,\quad J_{\tau}=0,

which is also a solution to equation (
1.4
) in the pressureless and zero-electric field case (
p(\rho)=0
and
V=0
). This example suggests that the dissipation depends on the strictly positivity of
\rho
.

2.
Definitions and preliminaries

In this section we first provide some notation and definition that will be used through out this paper.

Lebesgue and Sobolev norms on
{\mathbb{T}}
are denoted by

||f||_{L_{x}^{p}}\coloneqq(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}|f(x)|^{p}dx)^{\frac{1}{p}},

||f||_{W_{x}^{k,p}}\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{k}||\partial_{x}^{j}f||_{L_{x}^{p}},

and
H_{x}^{k}\coloneqq H^{k}({\mathbb{T}})
denotes the Sobolev space
W^{k,2}({\mathbb{T}})
.
Given a time interval
I\subset[0,\infty)
, the mixed space-time Lebesgue norm of
f:I\to L^{r}({\mathbb{T}})
is
given by

||f||_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\coloneqq\left(\int_{I}||f(t)||_{L_{x}^{r}}^{q}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\left(\int_{I}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}|f(x)|^{r}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{r}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Similarly the mixed
Sobolev norm
L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{k,r}
is defined. We use
C
to denote a generic constant,
C(A)
indicates its dependence on the quantity
A
.

By using identity (
1.2
) and defining the velocity
v=\frac{J}{\rho}
, system (
1.1
) becomes

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\partial}_{t}\rho+{\partial}_{x}(\rho v)=0\\
&{\partial}_{t}(\rho v)+{\partial}_{x}[\rho v^{2}+p(\rho)+({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}]+\rho{\partial}_{x}V=\frac{1}{4}{\partial}_{x}^{3}\rho-\frac{1}{\tau}\rho v.\end{aligned}\right.

Let us also recall the total mass and total energy are respectively given by

M(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(t,x)\,dx,

and

(2.2)

E(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{1}{2}\rho v^{2}+\frac{1}{2}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}+f(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}({\partial}_{x}V)^{2}dx.

As already mentioned in the Introduction, there is a formal analogy between system (
2.1
) and the NLS-type equation (
1.21
), through the so-called Madelung transformation
[
33
]
. Indeed, this may be immediately seen by expressing the wave function
\psi
in terms of its amplitude and phase, i.e.

(2.3)

\psi=\sqrt{\rho}e^{iS}.

By plugging this ansatz into (
1.21
) and by further separating real and imaginary parts of the resulting equation, we deduce a system of evolution equations for
\rho
and
S
,

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\partial}_{t}\rho+{\partial}_{x}(\rho\nabla S)=0\\
&{\partial}_{t}S+\frac{1}{2}|{\partial}_{x}S|^{2}+f^{\prime}(\rho)+V+\frac{1}{\tau}S=\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\partial}_{xx}\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}.\end{aligned}\right.

If we now differentiate the second equation in (
2.4
) with respect to the space variable, we obtain an Euler-type equation for the velocity field
v={\partial}_{x}S
, namely

(2.5)

{\partial}_{t}v+{\partial}_{x}\left(\frac{1}{2}v^{2}+f^{\prime}(\rho)+V-\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\partial}_{xx}\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)=-\frac{1}{\tau}v.

By recalling the definition of the chemical potential in (
1.11
), we see that (
2.5
) equals the second equation in (
1.12
).
The momentum density equation in the QHD system (
1.1
) is then derived by multiplying (
2.5
) by
\rho
and by using the continuity equation.

In general, the analogy between (
1.21
) and (
2.1
) fails when the Madelung transformation (
2.3
) becomes singular, namely in the presence of vacuum regions. It is, however, possible to provide a rigorous justification of such equivalence by means of the polar factorization and wave function lifting techniques, developed by the authors in
[
4
,
5
,
7
]
. In the present paper, since we focus on vacuum-free solutions, we will omit some technical details related to the polar factorization and wave function lifting, and we simply provide a quick overview of the main objects. For a more comprehensive discussion, we address the interested reader to
[
6
,
40
]
.

First,
it is straightforward to see that the objects
\sqrt{\rho}
and
\sqrt{\rho}v
provide a senseful definition of weak solutions to (
2.1
) and the energy bound in (
2.2
). Namely, by denoting
\Lambda=\sqrt{\rho}v
, the
hydrodynamic state

(\sqrt{\rho},\Lambda)
carries all the necessary information.

Definition 8

(Finite energy weak solutions)
.

Let
\rho_{0},J_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
and
\rho_{0}>0
. We say the pair
(\rho,J)
is a weak solution to the Cauchy problem for (
2.1
) with initial data

\rho(0)=\rho_{0}
,
(\rho v)(0)=J_{0},

on
[0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}}
if there exists
(\sqrt{\rho},\Lambda)
such that
\rho=(\sqrt{\rho}^{2})
,
J=\sqrt{\rho}\Lambda
and
(\rho,J)
satisfies the QHD system (
2.1
) in the sense of distribution.
Moreover, we say
(\rho,J)
is a finite mass weak solution, if for almost every

we have

. Analogously,
(\rho,J)
is called a finite energy weak solution if for almost every

, we also have
E(t)<\infty
.

Remark 9
.

Notice that in the present paper we deal with vacuum-free solutions, hence the equation (
2.1
) may be expressed in terms of
\rho
and
v
.

Moreover, we recall the polar factorization Lemma, first developed in
[
4
]
, that defined the hydrodynamic state
(\sqrt{\rho},\Lambda)
associated to a given wave function
\psi\in H^{1}
, with no restriction on the vacuum region. This is achieved by exploiting the polar factor
\phi
associated to
\psi
, defined through the identity
\psi=|\psi|\phi
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\psi\in H^{1}
and let
\phi\in L^{\infty}
be such that
\psi=|\psi|\phi
. Then, by defining
\sqrt{\rho}=|\psi|
,
\Lambda=\IM(\bar{\phi}{\partial}_{x}\psi)
, we have:

•

\sqrt{\rho}\in H^{1}
and it holds

{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}=\RE(\bar{\phi}{\partial}_{x}\psi)
;

•

|{\partial}_{x}\psi|^{2}=({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}+\Lambda^{2}
a.e. in
{\mathbf{R}}
.

We also recall the definition of GCP solutions, characterized by the chemical potential introduced in (
1.11
)

\mu=-\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}}{2\sqrt{\rho}}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}+f^{\prime}(\rho)+V

and its
L^{2}
norm with respect to density measure
\rho dx
.
As before, we remark that this definition may comprise a more general class of weak solutions, allowing for states with vacuum regions. We refer to
[
7
]
for more details.
In what follows we provide the definition of weak solutions with bounded generalized chemical potential.

Definition 11

(GCP solutions)
.

Let
(\rho,v)
be a finite energy weak solution to (
2.1
) on

[0,T]\times{\mathbf{R}}
. We say that
(\rho,v)
is a GCP solution for the system (
2.1
) if the following bounds are satisfied

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}({\mathbf{R}}))}+\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}))}\leq

\displaystyle C,

\displaystyle\|{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}))}+\|\sqrt{\rho}\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}))}\leq

\displaystyle C.

Let us recall that, by the Madelung transformation (
2.3
), we have
v={\partial}_{x}S
. Consequently, from (
2.4
), we have

(2.6)

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{x}S=v,\quad{\partial}_{t}S=-\mu-\frac{1}{\tau}S\\
S(0,0)=S_{*}.\end{cases}

On the other hand, the equation (
2.5
) for the velocity field may be interpreted as the "irrotationality" condition for the gradient equation (
2.6
), namely

(2.7)

{\partial}_{t}v+{\partial}_{x}(\mu+\frac{1}{\tau}S)=0.

Thus we can formally write the phase function
S
in hydrodynamic functions as

(2.8)

S(t,x)=e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}\left(S_{*}+\int_{0}^{x_{*}}v_{0}(y)dy\right)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{s-t}{\tau}}\mu(s,x_{*})ds+\int_{x_{*}}^{x}v(t,y)dy,

where
x_{*}\in{\mathbb{T}}
is an arbitrary point and has no influence on the definition due to the "irrotationality" condition (
2.7
). However, for weak solutions
(\rho,J)
, the chemical potential
\mu
is merely a Lebesgue function or even a distribution. Since (
2.8
) should be independent of the point

, we can take the average of (

) with respect to

, which allows us to extend the hydrodynamic definition of the phase function

to more general cases.

Definition 12

(Phase function)
.

Let
(\rho,v)
be a pair a solution of (
1.1
) on
[0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}}
such that the velocity
v
and the chemical potential

given by (

) satisfy,

v\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x},\quad\mu\in\mathcal{D}([0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}}),

and
\int_{\mathbb{T}}vdx=0
. Then we define the phase function
S
to be the solution of the gradient equation (
2.6
), which can be explicitly written as

(2.9)

\displaystyle S(t,x)=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}\left(S_{*}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{x_{*}}v_{0}(y)dydx_{*}\right)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{\frac{s-t}{\tau}}\mu(s,x_{*})dx_{*}ds+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{x_{*}}^{x}v(t,y)dydx_{*}.

Remark 13
.

The condition
\int_{\mathbb{T}}vdx=0
is needed to make

a periodic function on

, however Definition

Remark 14
.

In the case that the range of

Next, the physical entropy

where

Definition 15
.

The following Proposition gives sufficient and necessary conditions for a hydrodynamic data

to have an associated wave function

.

Proposition 16

(
H^{1}
wave function lifting)
.

Let
(\sqrt{\rho},v)
be a hydrodynamic data with strictly positive density
\inf_{x}\rho\geq\delta>0
. There exists a unique (upto constant phase shifts) associated wave function
\psi\in H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})

if and only if there exists a constant
0<M_{1}<\infty
such that

\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}+\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq M_{1}.

Furthermore we have

{\partial}_{x}\psi=({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}+i\rho v)\phi,\quad\|\psi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq C(M_{1}),

where
\phi\in P(\psi)
is a polar factor of

.

The

wave function lifting is given in the framework of the generalised chemical potential.

Proposition 17

(
H^{2}
wave function lifting)
.

Let
(\rho,v)
be a hydrodynamic data with strictly positive density
\inf_{x}\rho\geq\delta>0
. Let us also assume

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}+\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq M_{1},

\displaystyle\|{\partial}_{x}(\rho v)/\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}+\|\sqrt{\rho}v^{2}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq M_{2},

for some constants
M_{1},M_{2}<\infty
. Then there exists a unique wave function
\psi\in H^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
, associated to

, and it follows that

(2.13)

\|\psi\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq C(M_{1},M_{2}).

3.
Global well-posedness of GCP solutions

The global well-posedness Theorem
2
of GCP solution to system (
1.1
) is proved analysing the existence

solutions to the NLS (
1.21
) and establishing the relationship between (
1.1
) and (
1.21
) by Proposition

.

In the first part of this section, we will focus on the Cauchy problem of NLS (
1.21
),

(3.1)

\begin{cases}i{\partial}_{t}\psi+\frac{1}{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi=f^{\prime}(|\psi|^{2})\psi+\frac{1}{\tau}S\psi+V\psi\\
-{\partial}_{x}^{2}V=|\psi|^{2}-\mathcal{C}(x),\quad\psi(0)=\psi_{0}\end{cases}

for initial data
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}})
, where
S
is the phase function of
\psi
given in Definition
12
. We first prove the local existence of
H^{2}
solutions to (
3.1
) by a standard Picard iteration scheme.

Proposition 18
.

Let us assume the initial data
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}})
satisfy
\inf_{x}|\psi_{0}|\geq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}>0
and

(3.2)

\|{\partial}_{x}\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\delta M_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}},

where
M_{0}=\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}
. Then the Cauchy problem (
3.1
) has a unique solution
\psi\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{*};H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}))
local in time, for some
T_{*}>0
, with
\inf_{t,x}|\psi|\geq\frac{\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2}
.

Proof.

The proof follows a standard Picard iteration argument. The sequence
\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
for (
3.1
) is defined iteratively by solving
\psi_{n}
from the linear Schrödinger equation

(3.3)

i{\partial}_{t}\psi_{n}+\frac{1}{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{n}=f^{\prime}(|\psi_{n-1}|^{2})\psi_{n}+\frac{1}{\tau}S_{n-1}\psi_{n}+V_{n-1}\psi_{n}

on
[0,T_{*})\times{\mathbb{T}}
, for small
T_{*}
to be determined later, with
\psi_{n-1}
,
S_{n-1}
and
V_{n-1}
given in the previous step. The Duhamel formula for (
3.3
) is written as

(3.4)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}=e^{\frac{i}{2}t{\partial}_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}

\displaystyle-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{i}{2}(t-s){\partial}_{x}^{2}}[f^{\prime}(|\psi_{n-1}|^{2})+V_{n-1}]\psi_{n}(s)ds

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{i}{2}(t-s){\partial}_{x}^{2}}S_{n-1}\psi_{n}(s)ds,

and the electric potential
V_{n-1}
is provided by the Poisson equation,

-{\partial}_{x}^{2}V_{n-1}=|\psi_{n-1}|^{2}-\mathcal{C}(x)

with
\int_{\mathbb{T}}V_{n-1}dx=0
. By Poincaré inequality,
V_{n-1}
is bounded by

\|V_{n-1}\|_{W^{1,p}_{x}}\leq C(\|\psi_{n-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\mathcal{C}\|_{L^{1}_{x}}),\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

For the phase function, we first define
S_{0}=0
. For
n\geq 1
, we want to give s suitable definition of
S_{n}
by adapting the idea of Definition
12
for
\psi_{n}
as follows. As before, we define the velocity and chemical potential as

(3.5)

v_{n}=\IM\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}\psi_{n}}{\psi_{n}}\right),\quad\mu_{n}=-\frac{1}{2}\RE\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{n}}{\psi_{n}}\right)+f^{\prime}(|\psi_{n-1}|^{2})+V_{n-1}.

and we will see below this definition is consistent by the positivity of
|\psi_{n}|
. By (
3.3
) the equation of
v_{n}
is given by

(3.6)

{\partial}_{t}v_{n}=-{\partial}_{x}\left(\mu_{n}+\frac{1}{\tau}S_{n-1}\right).

Thus following the idea of (
2.6
), we define
S_{n}
to be the solution to the space-time gradient equation

(3.7)

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{x}S_{n}=v_{n},\quad{\partial}_{t}S_{n}=-\mu_{n}-\frac{1}{\tau}S_{n-1}\\
S_{n}(0,0)=S_{*}\in[0,2\pi).\end{cases}

The "irrotationality" condition (
3.6
) guarantees the solvability of (
3.7
), and following the idea of Definition
12
, the phase function
S_{n}
can be explicitly written as

(3.8)

\displaystyle S_{n}(t,x)=

\displaystyle S_{*}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{x_{*}}v_{0}(y)dydx_{*}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\mu_{n}+\tau^{-1}S_{n-1})(s,x_{*})dx_{*}ds+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{x_{*}}^{x}v_{n}(t,y)dydx_{*}.

We claim that the sequence
\{\psi_{n}\}
satisfies the following properties on
[0,T_{*})\times{\mathbb{T}}
:

(1)

boundedness:
\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\leq 2\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}}
and
\|S_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\leq C
;

(2)

positivity:
\inf_{t,x}|\psi_{n}|\geq\frac{\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2}
.

Here
0<C<\infty
may depend on
\delta
and
\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}}
, but is independent of
n
. By our assumption,
\psi_{0}
and
S_{0}
obviously satisfy the properties (1) and (2). In this paper we will only focus on the estimates related to the phase function
S_{n}
and the positivity property (2), and the remaining analysis following the standard argument for non-linear Schrödinger equation.

For the boundedness of
\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}
, we use the Duhamel formula (
3.4
) and consider its
L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}
norm. The last integral in (
3.4
) can be estimated as

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{i}{2}(t-s){\partial}_{x}^{2}}S_{n-1}\psi_{n}(s)ds\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\leq

\displaystyle T_{*}\|S_{n-1}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T_{*}\|S_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}.

Thus by the assumption of induction, we have

\frac{1}{\tau}\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{i}{2}(t-s){\partial}_{x}^{2}}S_{n-1}\psi_{n}(s)ds\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\leq\frac{CT_{*}}{\tau}\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}},

and we choose
T_{*}
small such that
\frac{CT_{*}}{\tau}\leq\frac{1}{4}
. Similarly, by Sobolev inequalities, the second integral in the right hand side of (
3.4
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{i}{2}(t-s){\partial}_{x}^{2}}[f^{\prime}(|\psi_{n-1}|^{2})

\displaystyle+V_{n-1}]\psi_{n}(s)ds\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T_{*}\|f^{\prime}(|\psi_{n-1}|^{2})+V_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|\psi_{n}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CT_{*}\|\psi_{n}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}_{x}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\psi_{n}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}_{x}},

which implies

\|\psi_{n}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}_{x}}\leq\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\psi_{n}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}_{x}},

namely the first part of property (1).

Now we prove the positivity property (2). Let us define the density
\rho_{n}=|\psi_{n}|^{2}
, then from (
3.3
) we obtain the equation of
\rho_{n}
as

{\partial}_{t}\rho_{n}+{\partial}_{x}(\rho_{n}v_{n})=0.

As a consequence, the total mass (and the average density since we assume
|{\mathbb{T}}|=1
)
M_{n}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{n}dx\equiv M_{0}
is conserved. By Poincaré inequality,

(3.9)

\|\rho_{n}-M_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|{\partial}_{x}\rho_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\leq M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\partial}_{x}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}},

therefore

\inf_{t,x}|\psi_{n}|^{2}=\inf_{t,x}\rho_{n}\geq\frac{\delta}{4}

if we have

(3.10)

\|{\partial}_{x}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\leq M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\delta}{4}M_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Then we consider the norm
\|{\partial}_{x}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}
. Multiplying (
3.3
) by
2{\partial}_{x}^{2}\bar{\psi}_{n}
and taking the imaginary part, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|{\partial}_{x}\psi_{n}|^{2}dx=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{T}}[f^{\prime}(|\psi_{n-1}|^{2})+\tau^{-1}S_{n-1}+V_{n-1}]\IM(\psi_{n}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\bar{\psi}_{n})dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|[f^{\prime}(|\psi_{n-1}|^{2})+\tau^{-1}S_{n-1}+V_{n-1}]\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}.

By using the previous estimates of
f^{\prime}(|\psi_{n-1}|^{2})
,
S_{n-1}
,
V_{n-1}
and property (1), it gives

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|{\partial}_{x}\psi_{n}|^{2}dx\leq\frac{C}{\tau}.

Last, by using the initial condition (
3.2
) we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|{\partial}_{x}\psi_{n}|^{2}(t)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}|{\partial}_{x}\psi_{0}|^{2}+\frac{CT_{*}}{\tau}\leq(M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\delta M_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{2}+\frac{CT_{*}}{\tau},

which allows us to choose
T_{*}
small such that (
3.10
) holds. Thus we prove property (2).

Last, we prove the remaining part of property (1), namely
\|S_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\leq C
. By
{\partial}_{x}S_{n}=v_{n}
, we only need to control
\|S_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}
and
\|{\partial}_{x}v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}
.
From (
3.8
) we obtain

|S_{n}(t,x)|\leq S_{*}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}|v_{0}|dy+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{T}(|\mu_{n}|+\tau^{-1}|S_{n-1}|)dx_{*}ds+\int_{\mathbb{T}}|v_{n}|dy.

Recalling that
v_{n}=\IM(\frac{{\partial}_{x}\psi_{n}}{\psi_{n}})
, by the bound of
\psi_{n}
and (2) it implies

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|v_{n}|dy\leq 2\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|{\partial}_{x}\psi_{n}|dy\leq\frac{C}{4}.

Same bound holds for
\int_{\mathbb{T}}|v_{0}|dy
. By the assumption of induction we have
\|S_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\leq C
, therefore

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{T}\tau^{-1}|S_{n-1}|dx_{*}ds\leq\frac{C}{4}

if we choose
T_{*}\leq\frac{\tau}{4}
. Last, by (
3.5
) we have

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\mu_{n}|dx_{*}ds\leq 2\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{n}|dx_{*}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f^{\prime}(|\psi_{n-1}|^{2})|dx_{*}ds\leq\frac{C}{4},

where in the last inequality we use
f\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
and

|f^{\prime}(|\psi_{n-1}|^{2})|\leq C(\|f\|_{C^{2}},\|\psi_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}})\leq C(\|f\|_{C^{2}},\|\psi_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}).

Thus we prove
\|S_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq C
. On the other hand, again by the definition of
v_{n}
, we have

\|{\partial}_{x}v_{n}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{2}_{x}}\leq 2\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{n}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{2}_{x}}+2\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|({\partial}_{x}\psi_{n})^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{2}_{x}},

where by embedding inequality,

\|({\partial}_{x}\psi_{n})^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{2}_{x}}\leq\|\psi_{n}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}_{x}}^{2}\leq 4\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{x}}^{2}\leq C.

Thus we finish the prove of property (1).

Now we need to show
\{(\psi_{n},S_{n})\}
is a contractive sequence, which is proved by a analogue argument as property (1) by considering the difference of (
3.4
) and its
L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}
norm. Since the estimates are standard and almost repetitive, we will not give the details.

Lemma 19
.

There exists
0<q<1
, such that the sequence
\{(\psi_{n},S_{n})\}
satisfies

\|\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}+\|S_{n}-S_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\leq Cq^{n-1}.

Lemma
19
imply
\{(\psi_{n},S_{n})\}
converges strongly to
(\psi,S)
in
L^{\infty}(0,T_{*},H^{2}({\mathbb{T}}))
. Moreover by the convergence and the uniform lower bound of
|\psi_{n}|
, we have

v_{n}=\IM\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}\psi_{n}}{\psi_{n}}\right)\to v=\IM\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}\psi}{\psi}\right)

-{\partial}_{x}^{2}V_{n}=|\psi_{n}|^{2}-\mathcal{C}(x)\to-{\partial}_{x}^{2}V=|\psi|^{2}-\mathcal{C}(x)

and

\mu_{n}=-\frac{1}{2}\RE\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{n}}{\psi_{n}}\right)+f^{\prime}(|\psi_{n-1}|^{2})+V_{n}\to-\frac{1}{2}\RE\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi}{\psi}\right)+f^{\prime}(|\psi|^{2})+V=\mu.

Also we can rewrite (
3.8
) as

\displaystyle S_{n}(t,x)=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}\left(S_{*}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{x_{*}}v_{0}(y)dydx_{*}\right)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{\frac{s-t}{\tau}}[\mu_{n}+\tau^{-1}(S_{n-1}-S_{n})](s,x_{*})dx_{*}ds+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{x_{*}}^{x}v(t,y)dydx_{*},

then by passing to the limit we see
S
satisfies (
2.9
). Thus we prove
(\psi,S)
is a solution to the Cauchy problem (
3.1
) on
[0,T_{*})\times{\mathbb{T}}
, and the continuity in time follows directly from the Duhamel formula.

The proof of uniqueness follows a standard argument for Schrödinger equation by considering the difference equation, similar to Lemma
19
of contraction.
∎

Let
T^{*}>0
be the maximal time of existence of the solution
\psi\in L^{\infty}([0,T^{*});H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}))
to the Cauchy problem (
3.1
), such that
|\psi|
is strictly positive. Then, it is possible to write
\psi=\sqrt{\rho}e^{iS}
, for some real-valued functions
\rho,S
. Consequently, the hydrodynamic functions associated to
\psi
are given by
\rho=|\psi|^{2}
and
v={\partial}_{x}S
, with
J=\IM(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}\psi)=\rho v
, and moreover

|{\partial}_{x}\psi|^{2}=({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}+\rho v^{2}.

We also recall from (
1.11
) and (
1.14
) that the chemical potential
\mu
and the quantity
\sigma
are given respectively by

(3.11)

\mu=-\frac{1}{2}\RE\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi}{\psi}\right)+f^{\prime}(|\psi|^{2})+V,\quad\sigma=-\frac{1}{2}\IM\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi}{\psi}\right).

To prove
\psi
exists globally, namely
T^{*}=\infty
, we need the following propositions providing the a priori estimates of
\psi
, and for later use we will write these estimates in hydrodynamic functions.

Proposition 20
.

The
\psi\in L^{\infty}([0,T^{*});H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}))
be a solution to the Cauchy problem (
3.1
), such that
\inf_{t,x}|\psi|>0
. Then, for any
t\in[0,T^{*})
,
\psi
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

the total mass is conserved

(3.12)

M(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(t,x)dx\equiv M_{0},

and the total energy, given by (
1.7
), dissipates

(3.13)

E(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}dxds=E(0)=E_{0};

(2)

if the initial data satisfies

(3.14)

E_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}(M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\delta M_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{2},

for some
\delta>0
, where
M_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}dx
is the initial total mass, then

\inf_{t,x}\rho\geq\delta\quad\textrm{on }[0,T^{*})\times{\mathbb{T}};

(3)

The higher order functional
I(t)
, defined by (
1.13
), is differentiable for almost every
t\in[0,T^{*})
and satisfies

(3.15)

\frac{d}{dt}I(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu{\partial}_{t}p(\rho)dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Vdx-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}\mu dx,

where
p(\rho)
is the pressure given by (
1.8
).

Proof.

By equation (
3.1
), it is straightforward to check the density
\rho=|\psi|^{2}
satisfies the continuity equation

{\partial}_{t}\rho+{\partial}_{x}J=0,

which directly implies the conservation of the total mass. To prove the energy relation (
3.13
), let us multiply equation (
3.1
) by
-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\bar{\psi}
, take the imaginary part and integrate by parts,

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|{\partial}_{x}\psi|^{2}dx=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}[f^{\prime}(\rho)+\tau^{-1}S+V]\IM(\psi{\partial}_{x}^{2}\bar{\psi})dx,

where

\IM(\psi{\partial}_{x}^{2}\bar{\psi})={\partial}_{x}\IM(\psi{\partial}_{x}\bar{\psi})=-{\partial}_{x}(\rho v)={\partial}_{t}\rho.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}|{\partial}_{x}\psi|^{2}+f(\rho)dx=

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}S{\partial}_{x}(\rho v)dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}V{\partial}_{t}\rho dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}V{\partial}_{t}({\partial}_{x}^{2}V-\mathcal{C}(x))dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}dx-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}({\partial}_{x}V)^{2}dx.

Property (2) directly follows the Poincaré inequality (
3.9
) and property (1),

\|\rho-M_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|{\partial}_{x}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\leq M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\partial}_{x}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\leq(2M_{0}E_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore if (
3.14
) holds, it follows

\|\rho-M_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq M_{0}-\delta,

which implies property (2).

In order to prove property (3), we first show for
\psi\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}
with
\inf_{t,x}|\psi|>0
, the following identity holds,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho^{-1}[(\RE(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)-

\displaystyle 2W\rho)^{2}+\IM(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)^{2}]dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\IM[{\partial}_{x}^{2}(W\bar{\psi}+\tau^{-1}S\bar{\psi}){\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi]dx

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{T}}[\rho{\partial}_{t}(W^{2})+W^{2}{\partial}_{t}\rho]dx

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{t}W\RE(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)dx-4\int_{\mathbb{T}}W\RE({\partial}_{t}\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)dx

\displaystyle-4\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{x}W\RE({\partial}_{x}\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{t}\psi)dx-2\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{x}^{2}W\RE(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{t}\psi)dx.

where we define the potential
W=f^{\prime}(\rho)+V
. However, to establish this identity rigorously, we need to introduce the standard sequence of mollifiers
\{\chi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
on
{\mathbb{T}}
and let
g_{\varepsilon}=g\ast\chi_{\varepsilon}
for functions
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
. We mollify equation (
3.1
), multiply it by
{\partial}_{x}^{4}\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}
and integrate by parts in the imaginary part, which gives

(3.17)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\IM[{\partial}_{x}^{2}(W\bar{\psi}+\tau^{-1}S\bar{\psi})_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}]dx,

where the function
S
is defined by (
2.9
). Since
\psi\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}
and
\inf_{t,x}|\psi|>0
, it is easy to check

W\bar{\psi}+\tau^{-1}S\bar{\psi}\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}.

Now we expand the integrand in the left hand side of (
3.17
) as

\displaystyle|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}=|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{-2}|\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}=\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}[\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})^{2}+\IM(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})^{2}],

where we let
\rho_{\varepsilon}=|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}
, which is different from
\rho\ast\chi_{\varepsilon}
. To match the chemical potential (
3.11
), we write

\displaystyle[\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})]^{2}=

\displaystyle[\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})-2W_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}]^{2}

\displaystyle-4W_{\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2}+4W_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}).

thus we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}[\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})-2W_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}]^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\IM(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})^{2}dx

\displaystyle+2\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}[W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})-W_{\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}]dx.

Now we compute the time derivative of the last integral,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{t}W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}W_{\varepsilon}\RE({\partial}_{t}\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}{\partial}_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{t}W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})dx+2\int_{\mathbb{T}}W_{\varepsilon}\RE({\partial}_{t}\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon})dx

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{x}W_{\varepsilon}\RE({\partial}_{x}\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon})dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{x}^{2}W_{\varepsilon}\RE(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon})dx,

and

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}W_{\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}}[2{\partial}_{t}(W_{\varepsilon}^{2})\rho_{\varepsilon}+W_{\varepsilon}^{2}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon}]dx.

Since we have
\psi\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}
, which implies
{\partial}_{t}\psi\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
, the mollified sequences converge strongly in the same topologies respectively, and the integrals are uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
. Thus we can pass to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, which gives (
3.16
).

In order to reduce (
3.16
) to (
3.15
), it follows (
3.11
) that

\RE(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)=-2\rho\mu+2\rho W,\quad\IM(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)=2\rho\sigma,

therefore

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho^{-1}[(\RE(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)-2\rho W)^{2}+\IM(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)^{2}]dx=4\frac{d}{dt}I(t).

We further compute that

\displaystyle-\IM[{\partial}_{x}^{2}(W\bar{\psi}+\tau^{-1}S\bar{\psi}){\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi]=

\displaystyle-[{\partial}_{x}^{2}W+\tau^{-1}{\partial}_{x}v]\IM(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)

\displaystyle-2[{\partial}_{x}W+\tau^{-1}v]\IM({\partial}_{x}\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi),

where we use
{\partial}_{x}S=v
, and

-2{\partial}_{t}W\RE(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)=4{\partial}_{t}W\rho\mu-2\rho{\partial}_{t}(W^{2}).

By equation (
3.1
), we also have

-4W\RE({\partial}_{t}\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)=4W(W+\tau^{-1}S)\IM(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi),

-4{\partial}_{x}W\RE({\partial}_{x}\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{t}\psi)=2{\partial}_{x}W\IM({\partial}_{x}\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)-4{\partial}_{x}W(W+\tau^{-1}S)\IM(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}\psi),

and

-2{\partial}_{x}^{2}W\RE(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{t}\psi)={\partial}_{x}^{2}W\IM(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi).

Notice that

\IM(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)={\partial}_{x}\IM(\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}\psi)={\partial}_{x}(\rho v)=-{\partial}_{t}\rho,

then by summarising the identities above and integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle 4\frac{d}{dt}I(t)=

\displaystyle 4\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Wdx-\tau^{-1}\int_{T}{\partial}_{x}v{\partial}_{x}(\rho v)dx

\displaystyle+4\tau^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}Wdx-2\tau^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v\IM({\partial}_{x}\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)dx.

Last, by using

\displaystyle\IM({\partial}_{x}\bar{\psi}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi)=

\displaystyle\rho\IM\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}\bar{\psi}}{\bar{\psi}}\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi}{\psi}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\left[\IM\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}\bar{\psi}}{\bar{\psi}}\right)\RE\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi}{\psi}\right)+\RE\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}\bar{\psi}}{\bar{\psi}}\right)\IM\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\psi}{\psi}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho[v(2\mu-2W)+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{-2}{\partial}_{x}\rho{\partial}_{x}(\rho v)],

it follows that

\frac{d}{dt}I(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Wdx-\tau^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}\mu dx-\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{[{\partial}_{x}(\rho v)]^{2}}{\rho}dx,

which proves (
3.15
) by using (
1.8
) and

\frac{[{\partial}_{x}(\rho v)]^{2}}{\rho}=\frac{({\partial}_{t}\rho)^{2}}{\rho}=4\rho\sigma^{2}.

∎

The next proposition provides the boundedness of the functionals
I(t)
up to any finite time
0\leq t<T^{*}
. Moreover we will see in Section
4
that these estimates remain uniform in
\tau
after the scaling (
1.3
), and play essential roles when we consider the relaxation-time limit. Indeed, as
\tau\to 0
, the time-dependent constant remains uniformly bounded on the time interval of length
\mathcal{O}(\tau^{-1})
.

Proposition 21
.

Let
\psi\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};H^{2}_{x}({\mathbb{T}}))
be as given in Proposition
20
. Then for any
0\leq t<T^{*}
, the functional
I(t)
satisfies

(3.18)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}I(t)+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{4}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0})\tau\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\mu^{2}dx+C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)(1+I(t)^{\frac{1}{2}})\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}dx.

As a consequence we have

(3.19)

I(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\sigma^{2}dxds+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{4}dxds\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,\tau t)

where
I(0)\leq I_{0}
and
C(\dots,\tau t)
may grow at rate
e^{\tau t}
.

Proof.

Let us recall (
3.15
) in Proposition
20
, the time derivative of functional
I(t)
is given by

\frac{d}{dt}I(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{T}\rho\sigma^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu{\partial}_{t}p(\rho)dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Vdx-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}\mu dx.

The right hand side can be estimated in the following way. First we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu{\partial}_{t}p(\rho)dx\leq 2\|p^{\prime}(\rho)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\mu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Recalling
p\in C^{2}[0,+\infty)
, by property (1) in Proposition
20
and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq C(\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}),

it follows that

\|p^{\prime}(\rho)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq C(M_{0},E_{0}).

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu{\partial}_{t}p(\rho)dx\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0})\|\sqrt{\rho}\mu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0})\tau\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\mu^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0})\tau I(t)+\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx

Similarly, we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Vdx\leq 2\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\mu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{t}V\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

Recall that
\int_{\mathbb{T}}Vdx=0
and
-{\partial}_{x}^{2}{\partial}_{t}V={\partial}_{t}\rho
, then by Poincaré inequality it follow that

\|{\partial}_{t}V\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\|{\partial}_{t}\rho\|_{L^{2}_{x}}=2C\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C(M_{0},E_{0})\|\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

which gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\mu{\partial}_{t}Vdx\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0})\|\sqrt{\rho}\mu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0})\tau I(t)+\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx.

For the last integral in the right hand side of (
3.15
), by recalling

\rho\mu=-\frac{1}{4}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\rho+\frac{1}{2}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho v^{2}+f^{\prime}(\rho)\rho+\rho V,

we write

\displaystyle-\tau^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}\mu dxds=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v^{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\rho dx-\frac{1}{2\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v^{2}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{4}dx-\tau^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f^{\prime}(\rho)+V)\rho v^{2}dx.

By integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v^{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\rho dx=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v{\partial}_{x}v{\partial}_{x}\rho dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{{\partial}_{x}(\rho v)}{\rho}v{\partial}_{x}\rho dx+\frac{2}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v^{2}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\sqrt{\rho}\sigma)(v{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})dx+\frac{2}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v^{2}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\sigma^{2}dx+\frac{6}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v^{2}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}dx.

The integral
\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v^{2}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}dx
can be estimated as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v^{2}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}dx\leq\frac{\|{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{2}}{\delta\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}dx,

By Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (since
{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}
has mean
0
) and (
3.13

\|{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{2}\leq C\|{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C(E_{0})\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Again by using the chemical potential and the mass-energy bounds, we have

\displaystyle\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\mu_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\rho v\|_{L^{4}_{x}}^{2}+2\|f^{\prime}(\rho)\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4I(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\rho v\|_{L^{4}_{x}}^{2}+C(M_{0},E_{0})

To control

, we use the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho v)^{4}dx\leq C\|\rho v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{3}\|{\partial}_{x}(\rho v)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+C\|\rho v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{4},

which imply

\displaystyle\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\rho v\|_{L^{4}_{x}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(M_{0},E_{0})}{\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}}(\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\rho}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{3})+\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(M_{0},E_{0})}{\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}I(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v^{2}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho})^{2}dx\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)(1+I(t)^{\frac{1}{2}})\tau^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}dx.

Last, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f^{\prime}(\rho)+V)\rho v^{2}dx\leq

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}(\rho)+V\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0})\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}dx.

Summarizing the inequalities above, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}I(t)+\frac{1}{4\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}

\displaystyle\rho\sigma^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{4}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0})\tau I(t)+C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)(1+I(t)^{\frac{1}{2}})\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}dx.

Notice that by (
3.13
),

\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{2}dxds\leq E_{0},

thus by Gronwall inequality it follows that for any
0\leq t<T^{*}
,

(3.20)

I(t)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\sigma^{2}dxds+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho v^{4}dxds\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,\tau t),

where
C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\tau t)
grows at most exponentially in
\tau t
.
∎

4.
Rescaling of QHD system with damping and uniform estimates in
\tau

From this section we begin to consider the rescaling of the QHD system (
1.1
) by the relaxation-time
\tau
and the limiting system as
\tau\to 0
. By using the scaling

(4.1)

t^{\prime}=\tau t,\quad(\rho_{\tau},v_{\tau})(t^{\prime},x)=\left(\rho,\frac{1}{\tau}v\right)\left(\frac{t^{\prime}}{\tau},x\right),

system (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\partial}_{t^{\prime}}\rho_{\tau}+{\partial}_{x}(\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau})=0\\
&\tau^{2}{\partial}_{t}(\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau})+\tau^{2}{\partial}_{x}(\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2})+{\partial}_{x}p(\rho_{\tau})+\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}V_{\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}\left(\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}}{\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}}\right)-\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}\\

For simplicity of the notation, in the later contents of this paper we still use
t

is the rescaled time.

By using rescaling (
4.1
), the total mass
M(t)
, the total energy

and the higher order functional

can be rewritten into rescaled forms respectively, which provide a series of bounds uniform in

First, conservation of mass (

) and energy relation (

) are transformed into

(4.3)

For the higher or order functionals, we define the rescaled chemical potential as

\mu_{\tau}=\frac{1}{\tau}\left(-\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}}{2\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}v_{\tau}^{2}+f^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})+V_{\tau}\right),\quad\sigma_{\tau}={\partial}_{t}\log\sqrt{\rho}.

Last, the higher order functional are rescaled as

The uniform estimate of

can be obtained by rescaling (

) and Proposition

Proposition 23
.

Let

By using this expression we can differentiate it with respect to time and find

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}e_{\tau}=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}{\partial}_{t}{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}+\left(\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}v^{2}_{\tau}+f^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})\right){\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}+\tau^{2}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}v_{\tau}+{\partial}_{x}V{\partial}_{x}{\partial}_{t}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\partial}_{x}\left({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}\right)+\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}}{\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}v_{\tau}^{2}+f^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})+V_{\tau}\right){\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}v_{\tau}+V_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}{\partial}_{t}V_{\tau}+{\partial}_{x}V{\partial}_{x}{\partial}_{t}V.

By using the resealed QHD equations above and definition (

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}e_{\tau}=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\partial}_{x}\left({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}\right)-\tau\mu_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}(\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau})

\displaystyle+{\partial}_{x}(V_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}{\partial}_{t}V_{\tau})-\tau\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}\mu_{\tau}-\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\partial}_{x}\left({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}-\tau\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}\mu_{\tau}+V_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}{\partial}_{t}V_{\tau}\right)-\rho_{\tau}v^{2}_{\tau}.

∎

We now compute the time derivatives of the rescaled functional

defined by (

\displaystyle\tau{\partial}_{t}(\rho_{\tau}\mu_{\tau})=

∎

5.
Entropy estimates and Dissipation

Inspired by the property of the quantum drift-diffusion equation (
1.5
), let us introduce the physical entropy

(5.1)

H(\rho_{\tau})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}\log\left(\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{M_{0}}\right)dx,

where
M_{0}
is the conserved total mass of
\rho_{\tau}
. As discussed before, the convexity of the function
g(s)=s\log s
and the assumption
|{\mathbb{T}}|=1
implies
H(\rho_{\tau})\geq 0
.

We first compute the time derivative of entropy
H(\rho_{\tau})
for GCP solutions, and by combining it with Proposition
23
, a direct consequence is the boundedness of the entropy
H(\rho_{\tau})
. We emphasize again that here
t
is the rescaled time given by (
4.1
).

Proposition 26
.

Let
(\rho_{\tau},v_{\tau})
be a finite energy GCP solution to the rescaled QHD system (
4.2
) such that
\inf_{t,x}\rho_{\tau}\geq\delta>0
.
The time derivative of
H(\rho_{\tau})
satisfies the inequality

(5.2)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle[H(\rho_{\tau})+\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx]+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 4\int_{\mathbb{T}}p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}(\rho_{\tau}-\mathcal{C}(x))dx\leq 4\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}\sigma_{\tau}^{2}dx+\tau^{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{4}dx.

If we also assume
I_{\tau}(0)\leq I_{0}<\infty
, then if follows that for any
0\leq t<\infty
,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\left.H(\rho_{\tau})(s)\right|_{s=0}^{s=t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dxds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dxds+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}(\rho_{\tau}-\mathcal{C}(x))dx\leq C(E_{0})\tau,

where we use the notation

\left.H(\rho_{\tau})(s)\right|_{s=0}^{s=t}=H(\rho_{\tau})(t)-H(\rho_{\tau})(0).

Proof.

To rigorously prove (
5.2
) for GCP solutions, we again need to use the standard mollifiers
\{\chi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
. Let
\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}=\rho_{\tau}\ast\chi_{\varepsilon}
and we consider the entropy
H(\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon})
of the mollified density. By mollifying equation (
4.2
) and using (
1.2
) to write the quantum term in a logarithm form, the time derivative of
H(\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon})
is computed as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}H(\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon})=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}dx=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}(\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau})_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}(\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})_{\varepsilon}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}p(\rho_{\tau})_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}{\partial}_{x}(\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau})_{\varepsilon}dx+\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}(\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2})_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}(\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}V_{\tau})_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau,\varepsilon}dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{x}p(\rho_{\tau})_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle-\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}^{2}\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}dx+\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2})_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{{\partial}_{x}\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}}(\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}V_{\tau})_{\varepsilon}dx.

where we can further write

-\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}^{2}\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}dx=-\tau^{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}dx+\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{t}\log\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}dx.

Thus the integrands in the right hand side of
\frac{d}{ds}H(\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon})
are all mollifications of well-defined functions, and the integrals are uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
. Thus we can pass to the limit

to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}H(\rho_{\tau})=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{x}p(\rho_{\tau}){\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}dx

\displaystyle-\tau^{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx+\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{t}\log\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\rho_{\tau}dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{x}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}V_{\tau}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx-4\int_{\mathbb{T}}p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\tau^{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx+4\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}\sigma_{\tau}^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\rho_{\tau}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}V_{\tau}dx.

For the last two integrals, by Proposition
23
, we have

\displaystyle\tau^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\rho_{\tau}dx\right|\leq

\displaystyle 2\tau^{2}\|\rho_{\tau}^{\frac{1}{4}}v_{\tau}\|_{L^{4}_{x}}^{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\tau^{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{4}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx,

and

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}V_{\tau}dx=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}(\rho_{\tau}-\mathcal{C}(x))dx,

which finishes the proof of (
5.2
). Last, by integrating (
5.2
) in time, the integral
\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx
is estimated in the following way,

\displaystyle\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}\,dx=\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}\,dx=2\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}v_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho_{\tau}}\,dx\leq\tau E_{\tau}(t).

Thus we prove (
5.3
).
∎

In Proposition
23
and their consequential estimates in Proposition

, the upper bounds

may grow at most exponentially in

. From (
5.3
) we see that this time-dependent term decays to

as
\tau\to 0
.

5.1.
Dissipation for small
\tau
and internal energy
f(s)=(s-M_{0})^{2n}

In
[
27
]
and the references therein, the quantum drift-diffusion equation (
1.5
) is considered in the pressureless and non-electric case (the DLSS equation), namely
p(\rho)=0
and
V=0
. If
\bar{\rho}
is a suitable solution to the DLSS equation, the physical entropy
\tilde{E}_{1}(\bar{\rho})
in fact dissipates and has an exponential decay. This result can be extended to equation (
1.5
) with non-zero pressure and electric potential, but suitable assumptions are needed on the pressure function
p(s)
and the background charge density
\mathcal{C}(x)
. First by formally letting
\tau=0
in (
5.2
), we see that a straightforward condition is to assume
p^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
for
s\geq 0
to guarantee the non-negativity of the integral
\int_{\mathbb{T}}p^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dx
. By using the relation

p(s)=sf^{\prime}(s)-f(s),

it follows that
p^{\prime}(s)=sf^{\prime\prime}(s)
, therefore
p^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
is equivalent to the convexity of
f(s)
. On the other hand. the dissipation of
\bar{\rho}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}
in (
5.2
) (letting
\tau=0
) implies
\bar{\rho}\to M_{0}
as
t\to\infty
, hence we may assume
f(M_{0})=0
to make the internal energy also dissipate. From observation above, in this section we focus on internal energy of the form

f(s)=(s-M_{0})^{2n},

with
n\in{\mathbf{N}}
. Similarly, to allow the electric potential
V
to dissipate to a constant state, we should assume the condition that
\mathcal{C}(x)\equiv M_{0}
.

We will show that for such
f(\rho)
and
\mathcal{C}(x)
, the dissipation and exponential decay also hold for GCP solution with
\tau
small, not only for the entropy
\tilde{E}_{1}(\rho_{\tau})
, but also for the total energy
E_{\tau}(t)
and the functional
I_{\tau}(t)
under the assumption
I_{\tau}(0)\leq I_{0}<\infty
, which proves Theorem
6
.

Proposition 27

Let
(\rho_{\tau},v_{\tau})

By using (

(5.5)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}[F_{\tau}(t)+

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{c_{1}\tau^{2}}{2}-\frac{2\tau^{4}}{\delta}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{4}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx

Now we need to control

Lemma 28

(Logarithmic Sobolev inequality
[
16
]
)

Let

and
\overline{u^{2}}=|{\mathbb{T}}|^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}u^{2}dx

\int_{\mathbb{T}}u^{2}\log(u^{2}/\overline{u^{2}})dx\leq\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}({\partial}_{x}u)^{2}dx.

in Lemma

H(\rho_{\tau})\leq\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{8\pi^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx.

For the total energy

\int_{\mathbb{T}}({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx
also follows the inequality above. On the other hand, the internal energy and the electric potential can be bounded by

\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\rho_{\tau})dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\tau}-M_{0})^{2n}dx\leq\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}[{\partial}_{x}(\rho_{\tau}-M_{0})^{n}]^{2}dx,

and

\int_{\mathbb{T}}({\partial}_{x}V_{\tau})^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\tau}-M_{0})^{2}dx.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v^{2}_{\tau}dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{T}}p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\tau}-M_{0})^{2}dx\geq\frac{1}{2}[H(\rho_{\tau})+E_{\tau}(t)].

Then by letting
\tau
small such that

\frac{c_{1}\tau^{2}}{2}-\frac{2\tau^{4}}{\delta}-\frac{\tau^{4}}{4}\geq 0,

namely
\tau\leq\left(\frac{2c_{1}\delta}{8+\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, it follows (
5.7
) that

(5.9)

\frac{d}{dt}[F_{\tau}(t)+\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx]+\frac{1}{2}F_{\tau}(t)\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)F_{\tau}(t)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}dx.

Last, by writing

\displaystyle\tau^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx\right|\leq

\displaystyle\tau\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}(\log\rho_{\tau})^{2}dx+\tau^{3}\int_{\mathbb{T}}({\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)\tau H(\rho_{\tau})+\tau I_{\tau}(t),

we have for
\tau<c_{1}
small that

(5.10)

\frac{1}{2}F_{\tau}(t)\leq F_{\tau}(t)+\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx\leq\frac{3}{2}F_{\tau}(t).

Thus combining (
5.9
), (
5.10
) and using Gronwall inequality, we finish the proof of (
5.4
).

Last, by the energy bound (
4.4
), we have

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}dxds\leq E_{0}

for all
0\leq t<\infty
, hence when
t\geq C(E_{0},M_{0},\delta)E_{0}
, (
5.4
) implies that
F_{\tau}(t)
decay exponentially.
∎

6.
Relaxation-time limit and convergence rate

In this section, we provide the rigorous justification of the relaxation-time limit as
\tau\to 0
in the framework of GCP solutions with positive density. Moreover, an explicit rate of convergence is given with the uniform boundedness assumption of the functional
I_{\tau}(t)
. As the previous section,
t
represents the rescaled time.

We assume
\{(\rho_{\tau},v_{\tau})\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{I}}
to be a sequence of GCP solutions to the rescaled QHD system (
4.2
) satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\inf_{t,x}\rho_{\tau}\geq\delta>0
;

(2)

there exist
M_{0},E_{0}
and
I_{0}
, such that
(\rho_{\tau},v_{\tau})
satisfies the mass-energy bounds

(6.1)

M_{\tau}(t)\leq M_{0},\quad E_{\tau}(t)+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}dxds\leq E_{0},

and one of the high order energy bounds:

(6.2)

I_{\tau}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}\sigma_{\tau}^{2}dxds+\tau^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{4}dxds\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,t),

where
M_{\tau}(t)
,
E_{\tau}(t)
and
I_{\tau}(t)
are rescaled functionals given at the beginning of Section
4
.

We first prove the following Proposition concerning the weak relaxation-time limit.

Proposition 29
.

Let
\{(\rho_{\tau},v_{\tau})\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{I}}
be a sequence of GCP solution satisfying the conditions
(1)
and
(2)
. Then there exists a subsequence, denoted by
\{(\rho_{\tau_{n}},J_{\tau_{n}})\}
, and a limiting density
\bar{\rho}
such that the relaxation-time limit holds in the sense

\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau_{n}}\rightharpoonup\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\quad\textrm{in }L^{2}_{t,loc}H^{2}_{x},

and
\bar{\rho}
is a weak solution of the QDD equation (
1.5
) defined by Definition
15
. Moreover, if the internal energy
f(s)
is a convex function, then
\bar{\rho}
is a dissipative solution.

Proof.

The conditions provided above imply
(\rho_{\tau},v_{\tau})
satisfy the uniform bounds

(6.3)

\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\leq C(M_{0},E_{0})T,\quad\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}v_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\leq E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}

on
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}}
for any
0\leq T<\infty
.
Furthermore, by (
5.3
) in Proposition
26
and the identity (
5.6
), we have

(6.4)

\displaystyle H(\rho)(t)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}+({\partial}_{x}\rho_{\tau}^{\frac{1}{4}})^{4}dxds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dxds\leq H(\rho_{0})+C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,t)\tau.

For general internal energy
f\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
, by

p(s)=sf^{\prime}(s)-f(s),

it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dxds\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}f^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\tau})({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dxds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0},\|f\|_{C^{2}},T).

For the initial entropy
H(\rho_{0})
, we have

H(\rho_{0})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}\log\left(\frac{\rho_{0}}{M_{0}}\right)dx\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta).

Thus on
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}}
we obtain that

\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)(1+T)+C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,T)\tau.

Therefore we can choose a converging subsequence
\{(\rho_{\tau_{n}},v_{\tau_{n}})\}
and a limiting function
\xi\geq 0
, such that

\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau_{n}}\rightharpoonup\xi\quad\textrm{in }L^{2}_{t,loc}H^{2}_{x}.

By the embedding theory, we also have the strong convergence

\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau_{n}}\to\xi\quad\textrm{in }L^{2}_{t,loc}H^{1}_{x},

which implies
\rho_{\tau_{n}}\to\xi^{2}
, thus we can write
\xi=\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}
. Now we take test functions
\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}([0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}})
, and write the rescaled QHD system (
4.2
) in the weak form,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\rho_{\tau_{n}}{\partial}_{t}\eta+J_{\tau_{n}}{\partial}_{x}\eta\,dxdt+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\rho_{0}(x)\eta(0,x)\,dx=0;

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\tau_{n}^{2}J_{\tau_{n}}{\partial}_{t}\eta

\displaystyle+(\tau_{n}^{2}\Lambda_{\tau_{n}}^{2}+p(\rho_{\tau_{n}})+({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau_{n}})^{2}){\partial}_{x}\eta-\eta\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}V_{\tau_{n}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\rho_{\tau_{n}}{\partial}_{x}^{3}\eta-J_{\tau_{n}}\eta\,dxdt+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}J_{0}(x)\eta(0,x)\,dx=0,

where
J_{\tau_{n}}=\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau_{n}}\Lambda_{\tau_{n}}=\rho_{\tau_{n}}v_{\tau_{n}}
.
The bound (
6.3
) on
\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau_{n}}v_{\tau_{n}}
and the strong convergence of
\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau_{n}}
imply the convergence

J_{\tau_{n}}=\rho_{\tau_{n}}v_{\tau_{n}}\rightharpoonup\bar{J}\quad\textrm{in }L^{2}_{t,loc}L^{2}_{x},\quad V_{\tau_{n}}\to\bar{V}\quad\textrm{in }L^{2}_{t,loc}H^{1}_{x}.

Thus we can pass to the limit
{\tau_{n}}\to 0
to obtain

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{t}\eta+\bar{J}{\partial}_{x}\eta dxds+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}\eta(0)dx=0

and

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}[({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}+p(\bar{\rho})]{\partial}_{x}^{2}\eta-\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}\bar{V}{\partial}_{x}\eta-\frac{1}{4}\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{4}\eta-\bar{J}{\partial}_{x}\eta dxds=0,

which gives the weak formulation of equation (
1.5
),

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{t}\eta+[({\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}+p(\bar{\rho})]{\partial}_{x}^{2}\eta-\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}\bar{V}{\partial}_{x}\eta-\frac{1}{4}\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{4}\eta dxds+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}\eta(0)dx=0,

as in Definition
15
.

Last, if we assume the internal energy
f
to be a convex function, then we have the positivity of
p^{\prime}(s)=sf^{\prime\prime}(s)
. By pass to the limit
\tau\to 0
in (
6.4
), we show that
\bar{\rho}
is a dispersive solution as in Definition
15
.
∎

Now we turn to the proof of the explicit convergence rate. As a consequence of the relaxation-time limit, the limiting density
\bar{\rho}
obtained above has a lower bound
\delta>0
, and it satisfies the following bounds,

(6.5)

\|\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\leq C(M_{0},E_{0}),

and

(6.6)

\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}+\|{\partial}_{x}\bar{\rho}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}+\|\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,T),

where the bound of
\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}
can be obtain by an analogue identity of (
5.6
) for
\bar{\rho}
.
Moreover, by the uniform bound of
\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\sigma_{\tau}={\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}
, it follows that

(6.7)

\|{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,T).

Then by (
1.2
), we can write the quantum drift-diffusion equation as

{\partial}_{t}\bar{\rho}+{\partial}_{x}^{2}(\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})-{\partial}_{x}^{2}p(\bar{\rho})-{\partial}_{x}(\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}\bar{V})=0.

by the assumption
p\in C^{2}[0,+\infty)
, we obtain

(6.8)

\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}(\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,T).

As the main technical tool of this part, we use the method of relative entropy. Recall that the physical entropy
H(\rho)(t)
is given by

H(\rho)(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(\rho(t))dx,

where
g(s)=s\log(s/M_{0})
, then for
\rho_{\tau}
and the limiting density
\bar{\rho}
, we define the relative entropy as

(6.9)

\displaystyle H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})(t)=

\displaystyle H(\rho_{\tau})(t)-H(\bar{\rho})(t)-\int_{\mathbb{T}}g^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(\rho_{\tau})-g(\bar{\rho})-g^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})dx

with

g^{\prime}(s)=\log\left(\frac{s}{M_{0}}\right)+1.

By Taylor’s formula, we have

g(\rho_{\tau})-g(\bar{\rho})-g^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})=\frac{1}{2}g^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{*})(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})^{2}

for some
\rho_{*}\in[\min\{\rho_{\tau},\bar{\rho}\},\max\{\rho_{\tau},\bar{\rho}\}]
, and
g^{\prime\prime}(s)=s^{-1}
. Moreover, densities
\rho_{\tau}
and
\bar{\rho}
have a lower bound
\delta>0
, and have an upper bound
C(M_{0},E_{0})
by the mass-energy bound (
6.1
) and (
6.5
). Thus the relative entropy
H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})
is equivalent to the
L^{2}_{x}
norm of
\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}
in the sense

(6.10)

2\delta H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})(t)\leq\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}(t)\leq 2C(M_{0},E_{0})H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})(t).

To estimate the relative entropy
H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})
, we need the following result which gives the time derivative of
H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})
. Here again a rigorous computation need the argument of mollification of
\rho_{\tau}
as in the proof of Proposition
26
. Since the method is similar, we omit this step for simplicity of the paper.

Proposition 30
.

The time derivative of
H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})
is given by

(6.11)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}({\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}[p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau}){\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}-p^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}){\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}]({\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho})dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}){\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}(V_{\tau}-\bar{V})dx

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}[R(\rho_{\tau})-R(\rho_{\tau},\bar{\rho})],

where the remaining terms are given by

R(\rho_{\tau})=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx+4\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}\sigma_{\tau}^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\rho_{\tau}dx,

and

R(\rho_{\tau},\bar{\rho})=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{t}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\bar{\rho}dx.

Proof.

The time derivative of
H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})
is computed by

\frac{d}{dt}H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})=\frac{d}{dt}H(\rho_{\tau})-\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}\log\bar{\rho}dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\log\bar{\rho}dx,

where the terms containing
\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}
and
\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{t}\bar{\rho}
are all erased due to the equations. The time derivative
\frac{d}{dt}H(\rho_{\tau})
has been given in Proposition
26
as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}H(\rho_{\tau})=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau})^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}(\rho_{\tau}-\mathcal{C}(x))dx+\tau^{2}R(\rho_{\tau}).

For the remaining terms, by using (
4.2
) and (
1.5
), we have

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}\log\bar{\rho}dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}(\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau})dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}p(\rho_{\tau})dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}(\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}V_{\tau})dx+\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{t}^{2}\rho_{\tau}dx

\displaystyle-\tau^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}(\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}V_{\tau}dx-\tau^{2}R(\rho_{\tau},\bar{\rho}),

where

\displaystyle R(\rho_{\tau},\bar{\rho})=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\bar{\rho}dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{t}^{2}\rho_{\tau}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\bar{\rho}dx-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{t}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx,

and

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\log\bar{\rho}dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{\bar{\rho}}{\partial}_{x}^{2}(\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{\bar{\rho}}{\partial}_{x}^{2}p(\bar{\rho})dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{\bar{\rho}}{\partial}_{x}(\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}\bar{V})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{x}p(\bar{\rho}){\partial}_{x}\left(\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}(\bar{\rho}-\mathcal{C}(x))dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}\bar{V}dx.

By substituting

{\partial}_{x}\left(\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)=\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{\bar{\rho}}({\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho})

and

{\partial}_{x}^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)=\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{\bar{\rho}}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\rho_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\bar{\rho})+\frac{\rho_{\tau}}{\bar{\rho}}({\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho})^{2}

into the previous identity, we obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{t}\log\bar{\rho}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}({\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}p^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}({\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho})dx.

To obtain (
6.11
), we summarize the computations above, and notice that the integrals containing
V_{\tau}
,
\bar{V}
and
\mathcal{C}(x)
are

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}(V_{\tau}-\bar{V})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}){\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}(V_{\tau}-\bar{V})dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\partial}_{x}\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}(V_{\tau}-\bar{V})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}){\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}(V_{\tau}-\bar{V})dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}(V_{\tau}-\bar{V})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho})^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}){\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}(V_{\tau}-\bar{V})dx.

∎

Proof of Theorem
5
.

The proof is finished by controlling the difference terms in the right hand side of (
6.11
) by
H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})
and the dissipation.

We first write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}

\displaystyle({\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta^{-1}\|\rho_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}.

It follows interpolation inequality that

(6.12)

\|{\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq\|\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Moreover, by using

(6.13)

|\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}|\leq\frac{|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}|}{2\min\{\rho_{\tau},\bar{\rho}\}}\leq\frac{|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}|}{2\delta},

we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}

\displaystyle({\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)\|\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\cdot\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)\|\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{2}\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}.

By Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we further write

\displaystyle\|\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\|\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}(\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\|\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0})(\|\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}(\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}).

Thus by the equivalence (
6.10
) of
H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})
and
\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}

\displaystyle({\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a_{1}(t)H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dx,

where

a_{1}(t)=C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)(\|\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}(t)+\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}(\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}(t)).

To estimate the pressure term in the right hand side of (
6.11
), we write

\displaystyle p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau}){\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}-p^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}){\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}=

\displaystyle[p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})-p^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})]{\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}

\displaystyle+p^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})({\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}),

which gives

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}

\displaystyle[p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau}){\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}-p^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}){\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}]({\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho})dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}|{\partial}_{x}\rho_{\tau}[p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})-p^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})]({\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho})|dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}|p^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})|({\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho})^{2}dx=\textrm{I}+\textrm{II}.

By
p\in C^{2}[0,+\infty)
and writing

p^{\prime}(\rho_{\tau})-p^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})=p^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{*})(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}),

we estimate the integral I as

\displaystyle\textrm{I}\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|p^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{*})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{x}\log\rho_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)\|{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)\|{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2},

where in the second inequality, we again use (
6.12
) and (
6.13
), and by using

\|{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq C\|{\partial}_{x}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(E_{0})\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

we obtain

I\leq a_{2}(t)H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dx

with

a_{2}(t)=C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{2}{3}}(t).

Last, for the term II, by
p\in C^{2}[0,+\infty)
, (
6.12
) and (
6.13
) it follows a similar argument as before that

\displaystyle\textrm{II}\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dx.

For the integral of electric potentials, we control it by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}){\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}(V_{\tau}-\bar{V})dx\leq

\displaystyle\|{\partial}_{x}\log\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{x}(V_{\tau}-\bar{V})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\sim C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})(t),

where we use the Poisson equation for
V_{\tau}
,
\bar{V}
and Poincaré inequality

\|{\partial}_{x}(V_{\tau}-\bar{V})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq C\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}(V_{\tau}-\bar{V})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}=C\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Summarizing the inequalities above and integrating in time , since
H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})(0)=0
we have

\displaystyle H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})(t)+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}

\displaystyle({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dxds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}a(s)H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})ds+\tau^{2}\int_{0}^{t}[R(\rho_{\tau})-R(\rho_{\tau},\bar{\rho})]ds,

where

a(t)=C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)[a_{1}(t)+a_{2}(t)+1],

and by the bounds (
6.6
) and (
6.8
), it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}a(s)ds=

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)\left(\|\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}^{2}+\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}(\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+C(M_{0},E_{0},\delta)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{2}{3}}(s)ds+t\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,T).

Now we estimate the last term
\tau^{2}\int_{0}^{t}[R(\rho_{\tau})-R(\rho_{\tau},\bar{\rho})]ds
. Recall from Proposition
30
that

(6.14)

\displaystyle\tau^{2}\int_{0}^{t}[R(\rho_{\tau})-R(\rho_{\tau},\bar{\rho})]ds=

\displaystyle-\tau^{2}\left.\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\log\rho_{\tau}-\log\bar{\rho}){\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx\right|_{s=0}^{s=t}

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\rho_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\bar{\rho})\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}dxds

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(4\rho_{\tau}\sigma^{2}_{\tau}+{\partial}_{t}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau})dxds.

By Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, the first term is controlled by

\displaystyle\tau^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\log\rho_{\tau}-\log\bar{\rho}){\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx\right|\leq

\displaystyle C\tau^{4}\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\sigma_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+C^{-1}\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}(\log\rho_{\tau}-\log\bar{\rho})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}.

It follows from (
6.2
) that

\tau^{4}\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\sigma_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,t)\tau^{2},

and by the upper and lower bound of
\rho_{\tau}
and
\bar{\rho}
, we have

\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}(\log\rho_{\tau}-\log\bar{\rho})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\sim\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\sim H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho}).

Therefore we can choose
C
large such that

\tau^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\log\rho_{\tau}-\log\bar{\rho}){\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau}dx\right|\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,t)\tau^{2}+\frac{1}{2}H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho}).

For the second term in the right hand side of (
6.14
), we have

\displaystyle\tau^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}

\displaystyle({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\rho_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\bar{\rho})\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{2}dxds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dxds+4\tau^{4}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{4}dxds,

and by (
6.2
), the last term is bounded by

\tau^{4}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{4}dxds\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,t)\tau^{2}.

The last term in (
6.14
) are control by (
6.2
) and (
6.7
) as

\displaystyle\tau^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}

\displaystyle(4\rho_{\tau}\sigma^{2}_{\tau}+{\partial}_{t}\log\bar{\rho}{\partial}_{t}\rho_{\tau})dxds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\tau^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\sigma_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}\sigma_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|{\partial}_{t}\sqrt{\bar{\rho}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}})ds\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,t)\tau^{2}.

Summarizing the arguments above, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})(t)+\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}

\displaystyle({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dxds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}a(s)H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})ds+C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,t)\tau^{2},

then by the integral form of Gronwall inequality and
H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})(0)=0
, we obtain

H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{\tau}({\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\rho}_{\tau}-{\partial}_{x}^{2}\log\sqrt{\bar{\rho}})^{2}dxds\leq C(M_{0},E_{0},I_{0},\delta,t)\tau^{2}

for all
0\leq t<\infty
. By the equivalence (
6.10
) of
H(\rho_{\tau}|\bar{\rho})
and
\|\rho_{\tau}-\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}
, we conclude the proof.
∎

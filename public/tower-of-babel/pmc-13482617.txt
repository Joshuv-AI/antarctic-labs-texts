Design and simulation of PEA0.15FA0.75MA0.1SnI2Br based quasi-2D/3D tin perovskite solar cells with different charge transport layers for efficiency enhancement
Authors: Poonam Subudhi, Rahul Narasimhan A
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Wide-bandgap (WBG) tin-based perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have emerged as promising lead-free alternatives to conventional lead halide devices due to their improved environmental compatibility. However, their performance is often limited by high trap-state density, moisture-induced oxidation of Sn2+, and rapid crystallization. Compositional engineering enables the formation of a quasi-2D/3D perovskite structure through the incorporation of hydrophobic phenylethylammonium (PEA+) cations into WBG tin-based perovskite. This structural modification promotes preferential orientation and enhanced crystallinity of the 3D phase, while the 2D component passivates grain boundaries, suppresses moisture penetration, and mitigates Sn2+ oxidation. In addition, PEA+ incorporation modulates the band structure, facilitating improved charge transfer at the transport layer interfaces. In this work, SCAPS-1D is employed to numerically investigate quasi-2D/3D PEA0.15FA0.75MA0.1SnI2Br-based PSCs, with emphasis on interface optimization and transport layer selection. Electric field distribution and energy band alignment at the interfaces are systematically analyzed due to their critical role in carrier extraction and device efficiency. Furthermore, the effects of absorber thickness, defect density, operating temperature, and back metal work function are examined to identify optimal device configurations. The observations revealed that C60 and Cu2O exhibit more suitable band alignment and better charge extraction properties. After comprehensive optimization, the device achieves an open-circuit voltage of 0.93 V, a short-circuit current density of 18.46 mA cm−2, a fill factor of 81.21%, and a maximum power conversion efficiency of 14.2%. The simulation results provide valuable insights into the interplay between absorber composition, interface energetics, and electric field profiles, guiding the development of stable, high-efficiency, lead-free WBG tin-based PSCs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-54942-x.

Introduction

Over the past several decades, the utilization of renewable energy sources, especially solar energy, has increased significantly1. Among the various photovoltaic technologies, hybrid halide perovskites have emerged as highly promising photo-absorbing materials and are considered strong alternatives to conventional silicon-based solar cells. Through sustained research efforts and technological advancements, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved certified power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) exceeding 26%2. This remarkable improvement in device performance is primarily attributed to their outstanding physical and optoelectronic properties, simplified fabrication process, and the flexibility of compositional engineering, which enables effective bandgap modulation and enhanced material stability3,4. Both silicon photovoltaics and PSCs can deliver high efficiencies when operated as single-junction devices; however, their overall performance is fundamentally restricted by spectral radiation losses. Single-junction architectures cannot suppress excess thermalization losses and cannot absorb photons with energies lower than the bandgap of the active layer, thereby limiting the optical response. This limitation is overcome by tandem solar cell (TSC) architectures that utilize a broader portion of the solar spectrum through the stacking of multiple sub-cells as front and rear absorbers, enabling more efficient harvesting of both higher and lower wavelength regions of incident solar radiation5,6. In tandem architecture, photons possessing shorter wavelengths are primarily absorbed by the front wide band gap cell (WBG), while longer wavelength photons are transmitted and absorbed by the rear narrow bandgap (NBG) cell. This complementary absorption mechanism significantly enhances overall solar energy conversion7,8.

In this context, extensive efforts have been undertaken by leading research groups to integrate halide perovskite materials into tandem solar cell configurations9. Lead (Pb) based perovskites exhibit remarkable optoelectronic characteristics, where their unique electronic structure and strong spin orbit coupling have played a crucial role in the rapid progress toward high efficiency PSCs10. In both two terminal (2 T) and four terminal (4 T) tandem configurations, the WBG front sub cell is essential for achieving a high open circuit voltage (Voc), and the development of high-quality WBG absorber materials remains an active area of research. Careful selection of absorber bandgaps is particularly critical in 2 T tandem devices to ensure current matching and maximize short circuit current (Jsc), whereas in 4 T tandem devices the total output power is determined by the combined efficiencies of individual sub cells, allowing greater flexibility in bandgap selection for the front absorber11. With careful optimization of wide and narrow bandgap materials, a theoretical efficiency approaching 40% is considered achievable. In this context, Pb based hybrid halide perovskites with their tunable optical and electronic properties, therefore present strong potential for stacking in perovskite/silicon, perovskite/CIGS, and all perovskite tandem solar cell architectures12,13. Despite their outstanding optoelectronic properties, the large-scale deployment of Pb based PSCs remains limited due to concerns related to lead toxicity and long-term instability14. Although various mitigation strategies, including encapsulation and recycling, have been explored to minimize Pb leakage and improve device durability15,16, these approaches are insufficient to fully eliminate environmental and health risks. Pb toxicity poses serious threats as it can adversely affect human health and cause severe environmental contamination. Pb ions can substitute biologically essential elements such as zinc, calcium, and iron, leading to accumulation in the nervous system and potentially causing genetic damage, neurological disorders, and carcinogenic effects14,17.

Partial substitution of Pb with tin (Sn) has emerged as a promising approach, as it reduces the bandgap and enables the use of such materials as NBG absorbers in TSCs18. Fully Pb-free Sn based perovskites have demonstrated encouraging efficiencies in narrow bandgap PSCs19. However, achieving efficient and stable WBG Pb-free perovskites remains a critical requirement for their application as front sub cells in all perovskite tandem devices. Although Sn based perovskites offer a viable alternative, their practical implementation is hindered by challenges such as uncontrolled crystallization, high defect density, and unfavorable band alignment, primarily due to the chemically active nature of Sn20. Since WBG absorbers play a dominant role in determining Voc in tandem devices, improving band alignment and reducing voltage losses are essential. Furthermore, non-radiative recombination caused by trap states remains a major contributor to voltage deficits, particularly in mixed halide WBG perovskites, where light induced phase segregation further degrades device performance21.

In this direction, several WBG Sn based all inorganic and hybrid halide perovskites have been investigated over the past few years to improve device performance. A significant increase in PCE has been reported for FASnI2Br PSCs, where efficiencies improved by about 11.17%22. Continuous research efforts have focused on enhancing efficiency through improved control of crystal growth using annealing temperature optimization, compositional engineering to stabilize Sn2+ by incorporating additives such as SnF2 and GeI2, reduction of trap densities using organic spacers including EDAI and GAI, and interfacial engineering at the perovskite and hole transport layer interface23–25. Notably, the highest reported efficiencies in WBG Sn PSCs were achieved through a combination of material engineering strategies and interfacial modification using KSCN22. Despite these advancements, wide bandgap tin perovskites still suffer from several intrinsic limitations, including rapid crystallization leading to film non uniformity, high trap density formation, moisture induced degradation through grain boundaries, and oxidation of Sn2+ to Sn4+26. To overcome these challenges and further enhance device efficiency, mitigating non radiative recombination via bulk and surface defect passivation has emerged as an effective approach. Such passivation can be achieved through compositional tuning or surface treatments27–29. The incorporation of hydrophobic organic cations in hybrid halide perovskites has been shown to suppress moisture ingress, passivate grain boundaries, reduce defect density, and improve stability. While such strategies have been widely explored in Pb based perovskites and narrow bandgap tin systems, their application in WBG tin PSCs remains largely unexplored. Recent experimental studies have demonstrated that incorporation of bulky organic cations (PEA+), into mixed-cation, mixed-halide tin perovskites with a bandgap of approximately 1.66 eV leads to the formation of quasi-2D/3D heterostructures30. Hydrophobic PEA cations help passivate grain boundaries in the perovskite, leading to smoother films with fewer pinholes. This improves carrier lifetime by reducing defects and trap states, while also enhancing moisture resistance and energy band alignment, ultimately resulting in better device stability and overall performance.

Building on these experimental findings, we present a numerical simulation of PEA-modified WBG tin perovskite—PEA0.15FA0.75MA0.1SnI2Br using a one-dimensional solar cell capacitance simulator for the first time. By adopting experimentally reported material parameters, this study provides a comprehensive analysis of multiple ETL/HTL combinations and their impact on device performance through interface band alignment and internal electric field. The study identifies optimal transport layer combinations and interface conditions that enable efficient charge extraction and reduced recombination losses. The simulation-driven optimization provides insight into the dominant loss mechanisms and identifies viable pathways to further enhance efficiency, thereby offering practical guidelines for the design of high-performance, lead-free wide bandgap perovskite solar cells suitable for tandem applications.

Materials and methodology

SCAPS-1D numerical simulation

Numerical device simulation plays a pivotal role in the optimization of solar cell architectures, particularly when emerging absorber materials and novel interface configurations are introduced. Prior to experimental realization, simulation tools enable systematic evaluation of charge transport, recombination dynamics, and interfacial effects, thereby reducing trial-and-error fabrication efforts31. Several well-established software packages, such as SILVACO, wx-AMPS, COMSOL Multiphysics, and SCAPS-1D, are commonly employed to investigate the electrical behavior of thin-film photovoltaic devices based on predefined material and structural parameters32. In the present study, all numerical simulations were performed using the Solar Cell Capacitance Simulator in One Dimension (SCAPS-1D, version 3.3.10). This software is extensively used for perovskite and thin-film solar cell investigations due to its numerical reliability, computational efficiency, and capability to model multilayer heterojunction devices33,34. SCAPS-1D evaluates the electrical characteristics of photovoltaic devices by solving fundamental semiconductor equations within a one-dimensional framework, enabling accurate prediction of current–voltage characteristics, carrier concentration distributions, recombination profiles, and energy band diagrams. The numerical framework of SCAPS-1D is based on the self-consistent solution of three coupled differential equations: the continuity equations for electrons and holes and the position-dependent Poisson equation. These equations, together with the drift–diffusion transport model, govern charge generation, transport, recombination, and electrostatic potential variations across the device structure. All baseline simulations were carried out under standard test conditions using the AM 1.5G solar spectrum with an incident illumination intensity of 1000 Wm−2 at an operating temperature of 300 K. The influence of temperature variation on device performance was also examined to evaluate thermal response. For the initial validation stage, the series resistance was assumed to be negligible, whereas the shunt resistance was considered sufficiently large to suppress parasitic leakage currents. The thermal velocities of electrons and holes were fixed at 107 cm s−1 to ensure realistic carrier transport behavior. Under these initial simulation conditions, the modelled device yielded Voc of 0.69 V, a Jsc of 16.8 mA cm−2, a fill factor of 78.37%, and a PCE of 7.98%. These results correspond to a practically representative reference device and serve to validate the numerical framework employed in this study. Based on this confirmed baseline, systematic variation of material and device parameters is subsequently performed to analyze performance trends and identify optimized operating conditions for WBG tin-based PSCs.

PEA0.15FA0.75MA0.1SnI2Br-based PSC architecture

The WBG tin-based PSC examined in this work employs the mixed-cation absorber PEA0.15FA0.75MA0.1SnI2Br integrated with suitable charge transport layers and metallic contacts to form a complete photovoltaic device. In addition to the perovskite absorber, the device architecture comprises an electron transport layer (ETL), a hole transport layer (HTL), and a metal back contact, all of which are selected to enable efficient charge separation, transport, and collection. The proposed device follows a conventional n–i–p configuration, wherein the perovskite layer functions as the intrinsic absorber sandwiched between n-type and p-type selective contacts. The n–i–p architecture offers improved carrier collection efficiency, particularly for long-wavelength photons35. In this configuration, the depletion region extends predominantly across the intrinsic absorber layer, resulting in a wider space-charge region. Such an extended depletion width promotes effective separation of photogenerated electron–hole pairs and reduces bulk recombination losses, thereby supporting enhanced photocurrent generation. The n–i–p configuration remains experimentally feasible and widely studied in perovskite solar cells, and it provides a suitable platform for systematically analyzing both electron and hole transport layers, enabling a comprehensive evaluation of band alignment and charge transport behavior within the device.

The schematic representation of the simulated device structure is shown in Fig. 1a, while the corresponding energy band diagram illustrating the band alignment across the device stack is presented in Fig. 1b. Illumination is incident through the transparent conducting oxide (TCO) front electrode, followed by a thin ETL. Fluorine- doped tin oxide (FTO) with high visible light transmittance is used as the front electrode in this study. The ETL facilitates selective electron extraction while minimizing interfacial recombination at the front contact. The perovskite absorber layer is positioned between the ETL and HTL, enabling directional transport of electrons and holes toward their respective electrodes under the influence of the built-in electric field. To comprehensively assess the influence of charge transport layers on device performance, multiple ETL and HTL materials were incorporated into the simulation framework. In addition, different back-contact metals with varying work functions were examined to investigate their impact on energy band alignment, carrier extraction, and overall device performance. The initial optoelectronic parameters of the absorber, transport layers, and contact materials were adopted from well-established literature to ensure physically realistic and experimentally relevant simulations. The detailed material, interface properties and simulation-related parameters used for constructing the device architecture are summarized in Tables 1, 2, 3.

Results and discussion

The present study aims to evaluate the performance potential of a WBG tin-based PSC employing PEA0.15FA0.75MA0.1SnI2Br as the absorber layer. In addition to examining the intrinsic capability of this WBG tin perovskite, the effects of different charge transport layers and key device parameters on device performance are systematically analyzed. The numerical investigation includes variation of ETL and HTL, optimization of absorber thickness, defect density, operating temperature, and back contact metal work function. This comprehensive analysis provides insight into the practical feasibility of WBG tin-based PSC architecture and supports their further development for efficient, lead-free photovoltaic applications.

Study of various ETLs and HTLs

Energy band alignment

The choice of ETLs and HTLs critically influences charge extraction and overall performance in PSCs. Energy band alignment at the ETL/perovskite and perovskite/HTL interfaces governs carrier transport and interfacial recombination. A Systematic variation of ETLs and HTLs is carried out to analyze their impact on conduction band offset (CBO) and valence band offset (VBO). As illustrated in Fig. 2a–c, a small positive CBO (spike) at the ETL/perovskite interface suppresses interfacial recombination while maintaining efficient electron transport, whereas a negative CBO (cliff) promotes recombination despite facilitating electron transfer. Similarly, a positive VBO at the perovskite/HTL interface enhances selective hole extraction, while a negative VBO hinders hole transport and increases recombination losses37–40.

(a–c) Schematic energy band alignment at the ETL/perovskite and perovskite/HTL interfaces and the corresponding extracted (d) CBO for different ETLs and (e) VBO for different HTLs.

Figure 2d, e illustrates the energy band alignment at the ETL/perovskite and perovskite/HTL interfaces through the extracted CBO and VBO profiles for different transport layers. Clear variations in interfacial energetics are observed depending on the choice of ETL and HTL, which directly influence charge extraction and recombination behavior. At the ETL/perovskite interface, all considered ETLs exhibit negative CBO values, indicating a cliff-type alignment. TiO2, SnO2, and ZnO show similar CBO values of approximately − 0.35 eV, while PCBM and C60 present relatively smaller negative offsets (− 0.25 eV). IGZO shows the largest negative CBO (− 0.51 eV), suggesting a stronger cliff formation. Although a negative CBO facilitates downhill electron transport and reduces extraction resistance, excessively large cliff offsets can enhance interfacial recombination, which may adversely affect the Voc. Among the studied ETLs, PCBM and C60 provide a comparatively moderate cliff, indicating a more balanced trade-off between efficient electron extraction and suppressed recombination. The relatively small magnitude of the negative CBO ensures that charge transfer across the interface remains efficient and does not significantly hinder device operation. As a result, the devices maintain relatively high Voc despite the presence of a slight cliff-type alignment.

At the perovskite/HTL interface, the VBO values reveal distinct charge-selective behavior for different HTLs. Spiro-OMeTAD and CuI exhibit similar negative VBO values (− 0.11 eV), indicating minimal energetic barriers for hole extraction while maintaining reasonable interfacial selectivity. PEDOT:PSS shows a larger negative VBO (− 0.31 eV), which may enhance hole transport but can increase recombination losses at the interface. In contrast, Cu2O displays a positive VBO (+ 0.29 eV), corresponding to a spike-type alignment. Although this introduces a barrier for hole transport, its magnitude remains within a moderate range and does not significantly hinder hole extraction. Instead, the spike-type alignment improves charge selectivity by limiting electron transfer toward the HTL. Such a configuration is particularly beneficial for wide-bandgap perovskite absorbers, where recombination control is critical for achieving higher Voc.

Electric field profiles at the interfaces

The spatial distribution of the internal electric field provides further insight into the charge separation, transport, and recombination in PSCs. Figure 3 presents the electric field profiles across the device for different ETL/HTL combinations, highlighting pronounced field variations at the ETL/perovskite and perovskite/HTL interfaces. These interfacial regions act as the primary zones for carrier separation under illumination. At the ETL/perovskite interface, strong electric field peaks are observed for all studied configurations. These localized fields assist in driving photogenerated electrons toward the ETL, thereby promoting efficient carrier extraction41. However, an excessively smooth electric field profile, particularly in IGZO-based structures, may indicate a weaker interfacial driving force for field-assisted electron extraction. At the perovskite/HTL interface, the electric field profiles vary significantly with the choice of HTL. Devices employing Spiro-OMeTAD, PEDOT:PSS, and CuI exhibit relatively smooth field transitions across the interface, enabling continuous hole transport into the HTL. In contrast, the Cu2O-based device shows a pronounced electric field spike (25.7 × 104 V/cm) at the perovskite/HTL interface. This enhanced field strengthens carrier separation by driving holes toward the HTL while restricting electron backflow, thereby reducing interfacial recombination.

Based on the comparative analysis of all 24 ETL/HTL, four device configurations: C60/PVK/Spiro-OMeTAD, ZnO/PVK/PEDOT:PSS, C60/PVK/CuI, and C60/PVK/Cu2O were selected for detailed simulations. The selection was based on both initial PCE values and favorable interfacial band alignment with optimized internal electric field distribution. The corresponding electric field strengths at the ETL/PVK interface for these configurations are 1.41 × 104, 0.2 × 104, 1.45 × 104 and 1.8 × 104 V/cm. A comprehensive comparison of all 24 configurations, including their initial Voc, Jsc, FF, and PCE values, is provided in Table S1. These selected devices exhibited comparatively higher initial performance (8.84%, 10.15%, 8.88%, and 9.41%) along with physically favorable transport characteristics, justifying their inclusion for further optimization.

Effect of absorber layer thickness

Figure 4 illustrates the influence of absorber layer thickness, varied from 0.1 to 2.0 μm, on the photovoltaic parameters of the investigated device architectures. A gradual increase in Voc is observed with increasing thickness, followed by saturation beyond ~ 0.8 μm, indicating suppression of interfacial recombination due to improved light absorption and enhanced quasi-Fermi level splitting. The Jsc shows a pronounced rise up to ~ 1.0–1.4 μm for all configurations because of increased photon harvesting and carrier generation, after which only marginal improvement is obtained, suggesting absorption saturation. In contrast, the fill factor (FF) exhibits a declining trend at higher thicknesses, which can be attributed to increased bulk recombination, reduced internal electric field, and longer carrier transport pathways. The combined effect of these competing mechanisms leads to structure-dependent optimum absorber thicknesses. Maximum efficiencies are achieved at approximately 1.0 μm for C60/PVK/Spiro, 1.2 μm for ZnO/PVK/PEDOT:PSS, 1.3 μm for C60/PVK/CuI, and 1.4 μm for C60/PVK/Cu2O. These findings demonstrate that absorber thickness optimization is not universal but strongly dependent on the choice of transport layers, emphasizing the importance of interface engineering for high-performance perovskite solar cells.

Effect of absorber defect density

The defect density of the absorber layer is a critical material parameter, as it directly governs non-radiative recombination, carrier lifetime, and overall device efficiency. Figure 5 illustrates the effect of absorber defect density, varied from 1013 to 1019 cm−3, on the photovoltaic performance parameters of the studied device configurations. The Voc remains nearly constant up to a defect density of 1015 cm−3, indicating minimal non-radiative recombination at low defect concentrations. However, a sharp decline in Voc is observed beyond 1016 cm−3, which can be attributed to enhanced Shockley–Read–Hall (SRH) recombination induced by deep-level trap states. Similarly, the Jsc exhibits negligible variation at low defect densities, confirming efficient carrier generation and extraction, but decreases significantly at higher defect densities due to increased carrier trapping and reduced diffusion length. The FF shows a gradual reduction with increasing defect density and undergoes a pronounced drop beyond 1016 cm−3, reflecting increased series resistance and recombination losses. As a combined effect of deteriorating Voc, Jsc and FF, the power conversion efficiency reaches its maximum below 1015 cm−3 and degrades severely at higher defect densities. Based on these observations, a defect density of 1 × 1015 cm−3 is selected as the optimal and experimentally realistic value for subsequent simulations.

Study of operating temperature

Operating temperature is a crucial reliability parameter for perovskite solar cells, as it directly influences charge recombination kinetics, carrier transport, and energy band alignment. Figure 6 depicts the variation of photovoltaic parameters with temperature in the range of 300–475 K for different device configurations. A clear and nearly linear decline in the Voc is observed with increasing temperature for all structures, primarily due to bandgap narrowing and enhanced non-radiative recombination at elevated thermal energies42. In contrast, the Jsc remains almost constant across the investigated temperature range, indicating that optical absorption and photogeneration are minimally affected by temperature variations. The FF shows a gradual decrease with increasing temperature, which can be attributed to increased carrier scattering, reduced mobility, and higher resistive losses. As a combined consequence of Voc and FF degradation, the PCE decreases continuously with temperature for all device architectures. Although variations in absolute performance are observed among different transport layer combinations, the overall temperature-dependent trends remain consistent. These results highlight the thermal sensitivity of perovskite solar cells and emphasize the importance of thermal management and material stability for maintaining device performance under real-world operating conditions.

Effect of back metal work function

The work function of the back metal contact is a critical parameter that governs energy band alignment, hole extraction efficiency, and contact resistance in perovskite solar cells. Table 4 summarizes the work functions of the selected back metals, ranging from 4.26 to 5.7 eV for Ag, Cu, Au, Ni, Pd, and Pt. The corresponding influence of back metal work function on the photovoltaic performance parameters is illustrated in Fig. 7. A pronounced increase in the Voc is observed with increasing metal work function, which can be attributed to the reduction of the hole extraction barrier and improved band alignment at the absorber/back contact interface. The Jsc increases with work function and tends to saturate beyond ~ 5.1 eV, indicating the formation of near-ohmic contacts that enable efficient carrier collection. Additionally, the FF rises sharply for higher work-function metals due to reduced contact resistance and suppressed interfacial recombination losses. As a result of the simultaneous improvement in Voc, Jsc, and FF, the power conversion efficiency increases significantly when transitioning from low work-function metals such as Ag to higher work-function metals such as Au, while only marginal variations are observed for further increases in work function. These results confirm that back contacts with work functions ≥ 5.1 eV are optimal for achieving enhanced device performance.

The quantum efficiency (QE) spectra, shown in Fig. 8c, further validate the J–V results. All optimized devices demonstrate high QE values exceeding 90% in the visible region (≈ 400–600 nm), confirming efficient photogeneration and carrier collection. A gradual decline in QE beyond ~ 650 nm corresponds to the absorption edge of the perovskite absorber. The slightly enhanced long-wavelength response for the CuI and Cu2O-based devices explains their higher Jsc values. While C60 and Cu2O are more frequently used in p–i–n architectures, literature reports show that they can also be implemented in n–i–p configurations with proper control over film formation and interface quality43–45. Overall, the close agreement between simulated J–V parameters and QE trends confirms the reliability of the device optimization strategy and shows good consistency with reported experimental results.

Conclusion

In this work, a comprehensive numerical investigation of a PEA0.15FA0.75MA0.1SnI2Br based WBG tin PSC was carried out using the SCAPS-1D simulation framework to elucidate the role of transport layer selection, interface engineering, and key device parameters on photovoltaic performance. Initially, a systematic assessment of various ETLs and HTLs was performed, yielding 24 possible ETL/HTL combinations. These configurations were evaluated through detailed CBO and VBO analyses, along with electric field profile studies, to identify favorable interfacial band alignment and reduced recombination losses. Based on this screening, four promising device architectures were selected for further investigation. The shortlisted devices were subsequently optimized with respect to absorber thickness, defect density, operating temperature, and back metal work function. The analysis indicates that careful control of absorber thickness plays a pivotal role in maximizing photon absorption while simultaneously limiting bulk recombination losses. Moreover, reducing defect density was found to significantly enhance carrier lifetime and suppress nonradiative recombination, leading to improved Voc and FF. Temperature-dependent simulations further revealed the inherent thermal sensitivity of device performance, with elevated temperatures adversely affecting voltage output. In addition, optimization of the back metal work function was shown to be essential for minimizing contact resistance and facilitating efficient hole extraction. Among the four optimized configurations, the FTO/C60/PEA0.15FA0.75MA0.1SnI2Br/Cu2O/Au device delivered the best overall performance, achieving a Voc of 0.93 V, Jsc of 18.46 mA cm−2, a FF of 81.21%, and a maximum power conversion efficiency of 14.2%, underscoring its promise as a high-performance architecture for PEA0.15FA0.75MA0.1SnI2Br based WBG tin PSCs. The findings highlight that careful optimization of structural, thermal, and contact-related parameters is essential for maximizing device efficiency and operational stability, thereby contributing to the advancement of cost-effective and scalable thin-film photovoltaic technologies.

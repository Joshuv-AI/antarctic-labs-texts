The Hajnal–Rothschild problem
Peter Frankl, Andrey Kupavskii

Abstract
. For a family ℱ \mathcal{F} define ν ⁡ ( ℱ , t ) \nu(\mathcal{F},t) as the largest s s for which there exist A 1 , … , A s ∈ ℱ A_{1},\ldots,A_{s}\in\mathcal{F} such that for i ≠ j i\neq j we have | A i ∩ A j | < t |A_{i}\cap A_{j}|<t . What is the largest family ℱ ⊂ ( [ n ] k ) \mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose k} with ν ⁡ ( ℱ , t ) ⩽ s \nu(\mathcal{F},t)\leqslant s ? This question goes back to a paper Hajnal and Rothschild from 1973. We show that, for some absolute C C and n > 2 ​ k + C ​ t 4 / 5 ​ s 1 / 5 ​ ( k − t ) ​ log 2 4 ​ n n>2k+Ct^{4/5}s^{1/5}(k-t)\log_{2}^{4}n , n > 2 ​ k + C ​ s ​ ( k − t ) ​ log 2 4 ​ n n>2k+Cs(k-t)\log_{2}^{4}n the largest family with ν ⁡ ( ℱ , t ) ⩽ s \nu(\mathcal{F},t)\leqslant s has the following structure: there are sets X 1 , … , X s X_{1},\ldots,X_{s} of sizes t + 2 ​ x 1 , … , t + 2 ​ x s t+2x_{1},\ldots,t+2x_{s} , such that for any A ∈ ℱ A\in\mathcal{F} there is i ∈ [ s ] i\in[s] such that | A ∩ X i | ⩾ t + x i |A\cap X_{i}|\geqslant t+x_{i} . 2.
Spread families

Given a real number
r>1
, we say that a family
\mathcal{F}
of sets is
r
-spread
if for each set
X
we have
|\mathcal{F}(X)|\leqslant r^{-|X|}|\mathcal{F}|
. We say that
W
is a
p
-random subset
of
[n]
if each element of
[n]
is included in
W
with probability
p
and independently of others.

The spread approximation technique is based on the following theorem.

Theorem 8

(The spread lemma,
[
2
]
, a sharpening due to
[
24
]
)
.

If for some
n,k,r\geqslant 1
a family
\mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose\leqslant k}
is
r
-spread and
W
is a
(\beta\delta)
-random subset of
[n]
, then

\Pr[\exists F\in\mathcal{F}\ :\ F\subset W]\geqslant 1-\Big(\frac{5}{\log_{2}(r\delta)}\Big)^{\beta}k.

Stoeckl
[
23
]
showed that the constant
5
can be replaced by
1+h_{2}(\delta)
, where
h_{2}(\delta)=-\delta\log_{2}\delta-(1-\delta)\log_{2}(1-\delta)
is the binary entropy of
\delta
. The lemma can be summarized as follows ‘‘if a family is locally quasirandom (sufficiently spread), then it is globally quasirandom (we expect to see sets from the family inside a typical subset of the ground set).’’ The parameters of interest here are so that
5/\log_{2}(r\delta)=1/2
and
\beta-\log_{2}k\to\infty
, so that the probability of the described event becomes close to
1
. The choice of
\beta\delta
, and thus the condition on
r
, depends on the question, but, again,
r=C\log k
already gives a meaningful conclusion.

An important observation is that any sufficiently large family contains a spread subfamily.

Observation 9
.

If
\mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose\leqslant k}
and
|\mathcal{F}|>r^{k}
, then
\mathcal{F}(X)
is
r
-spread for some
X
,
0\leqslant|X|<k
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{F}
is
r
-spread then we can put
X=\emptyset
. Otherwise, consider an inclusion-maximal
X
such that
|\mathcal{F}(X)|\geqslant r^{-|X|}|\mathcal{F}|
. Note that
|X|\leqslant k-1
because for
|X|=k
we have
|\mathcal{F}(X)|=1<r^{-k}|\mathcal{F}|
. By maximality of
X
, for any
Y
that is disjoint with
X
we have
|\mathcal{F}(X\cup Y)|\leqslant r^{-|X|-|Y|}|\mathcal{F}|\leqslant r^{-|Y|}|\mathcal{F}(X)|.
Thus,
\mathcal{F}(X)
is
r
-spread.
∎

The same proof implies the following

Observation 10
.

If
\mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose\leqslant k}
and
X
is an inclusion-maximal subset of the ground set so that
|\mathcal{F}(X)|\geqslant r^{-|X|}|\mathcal{F}|
, then
\mathcal{F}(X)
is
r
-spread.

3.
Simpler bounds

In this section, we treat several particular cases of the HR problem. In particular, in the first subsection we show that the extremal example need not always have the form as in Construction
4

Proposition 11

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subset{[6]\choose 3}

(i)

|\mathcal{F}|\leqslant 10
,

(ii)

\nu(\mathcal{F},2)\leqslant 3

Proof.

If
\mathcal{F}
is intersecting then by the EKR theorem

. Assume next that
\mathcal{F}
is not intersecting, and w.l.o.g.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{(1,2,4),(2,3,5),(1,3,6)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{2}

\displaystyle=\{(1,2,5),(2,3,6),(1,3,4)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{3}

\displaystyle=\{(1,2,6),(2,3,4),(1,3,5)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{i}

\displaystyle=\{[6]\setminus T:T\in\mathcal{P}_{i}\},i\in[3].

Note that

In the same way, in the case (i),

An

Put

In this subsection, exceptionally,

The following theorem is the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 16

4.
Proof outline

Fix a family
\mathcal{F}
with
\nu(\mathcal{F},t)\leqslant s
. The proof of Theorem
7
consists of three stages. In the first stage, we use the technique of spread approximations in order to find a family
\mathcal{S}
of sets of small size such that
\nu(\mathcal{S},t)\leqslant s
and
\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{S}]
is small. The key point here is that the uniformity of sets in
\mathcal{S}
is just slightly bigger than
t
. This result is given in Theorem
19
in Section
5
. The proof takes up some ideas from
[
22
]
and enhances them with several new ideas. The main one is an efficient procedure to find a dense piece inside the family and then apply the spread approximation procedure once more. It is combined with a relaxation of
\nu(\mathcal{S},t)\leqslant s
to
\nu(\mathcal{S},t^{\prime})\leqslant s
with a smaller
t^{\prime}
, which allows to work in a much broader parameter range. The argument uses an iterative bootstrapping which interchangeably applies these two steps: we improve the density of the piece that we find (Theorem
20
), and then, because of the improved density property, reduce the uniformity of
\mathcal{S}
and improve
t^{\prime}
(Theorem
23
and Lemma
24
), then going back to the density step and so on.

In the second stage, we show that
\mathcal{S}
has the structure as in Construction
4
: it is the union of
s

t
-intersecting cliques. The corresponding statement is Theorem
26
in Section
6
. The proof is by induction on
s
: basically, we find one such clique, show that the remainder has
t
-matching number of size
\leqslant s-1
and apply induction. In order to find the
t
-intersecting clique, we find the first ‘dense’ largest-uniformity layer in
\mathcal{S}
(the sets in
\mathcal{S}
have uniformity between
t
and
t+\ell
with
\ell\ll t
). Then, we discern an approximate ‘union of
s

t
-intersecting cliques’ structure, and use size bounds in order to show that one of these cliques is actually rather dense. The latter allows to show that the remainder of
\mathcal{S}
should have
t
-matching number at most
s-1
.

In the third stage, we combine some of the ideas from the previous sections and the ideas in
[
21
]
to prove that the remainder
\mathcal{R}
obtained in the spread approximation is empty. A key step is to actually find a set
X
such that
\mathcal{R}(X)
is sufficiently spread. This allows us to reduce the uniformity with which we work, but also relates the size of
\mathcal{R}(X)
and
\mathcal{R}
.

5.
Spread approximation for Hajnal–Rotschild families

The goal of the following ‘spread approximation’ theorem is to approximate any family
\mathcal{F}
with
\nu(\mathcal{F},t)\leqslant s
by a family
\mathcal{S}
with
\nu(\mathcal{S},t)\leqslant s
of much lower uniformity (cf. Subsection
4
). The proof of this result occupies the rest of this section.

Theorem 19
.

Let
n,k,s,t
be integers,
\sigma\geqslant 0
a real number, and assume that

n\geqslant t+C(st)^{1/2}(k-t)\log_{2}\frac{n-t}{k-t}
,

n\geqslant k+C(k-t)s(\sigma+\log_{2}n)
for some absolute constant
C
.
Let
\mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose k}
satisfy
\nu(\mathcal{F},t)\leqslant s
.
Then there exists a family
\mathcal{S}
of sets of size at most

t+(t/s)^{1/2}+3\sigma+\log_{2}(16s^{4}t^{2})
such that
\nu(\mathcal{S},t)\leqslant s
and, moreover, the family
\mathcal{R}:=\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{S}]
satisfies
|\mathcal{R}|\leqslant 2^{-\sigma}\cdot 15\log_{2}(st+\sigma)\cdot{n-t\choose k-t}
.

More generally, if additionally
n\geqslant t+Ct^{\alpha}s^{\beta}(k-t)\log_{2}\frac{n-t}{k-t}
with some
\alpha,\beta\geqslant 1/2
, then the bound on the sizes of sets in
\mathcal{S}
improves to at most
t+t^{(1-\alpha)}s^{-\beta}+3\sigma+\log_{2}(16s^{4}t^{2}).

One can see that the sets in
\mathcal{S}
have size just slightly above
t
, which is extremely helpful in the next, peeling step. E.g., if
n\geqslant C(k-t)\max\{s,t\}\log_{2}n

\mathcal{S}
have size at most

.

5.2.
Spread approximation

The next theorem is a variant of the spread approximation theorem which makes use of dense pieces within our family. The idea is that, rather than search for a spread approximation for
\mathcal{F}
itself, we find a set

q^{(i_{2})}\leqslant t+t^{(1-\alpha)}s^{-\beta}+8\sigma+8\log_{2}(8s^{4}t^{2}).
Applying it for one more step, one sees that

gets replaced by an expression that is at most

This completes the proof of the theorem.

Let
t\geqslant 8
and
n\geqslant k+C(st)^{1/2}(k-t)
.

1. We have

h(n,k,s,t)\geqslant h(n,k,s-1,t)+\Big(1-\frac{1}{10s^{3}}\Big)h(n,k,1,t).

2. If the families
\mathcal{U}_{i}
satisfy
\nu(\mathcal{U}_{1},t)=s-1
and
\nu(\mathcal{U}_{2},t)=1
and, moreover,
|\mathcal{U}_{1}\cup\mathcal{U}_{2}|\geqslant h(n,k,s,t)-(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{10s})h(n,k,1,t)
then
|\mathcal{U}_{1}|\geqslant h(n,k,s-1,t)-(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{10(s-1)})h(n,k,1,t)
.

Proof.

1. Recall that a family
\mathcal{G}
is
t
-intersecting iff
\nu(\mathcal{G},t)=1
.
It is a result of the first author
[
12
]
that
h(n,k,1,t)\leqslant{n-1\choose k-t}
, and thus, using Observation
32
, we have
h(n,k,s-1,t)\leqslant(s-1){n-1\choose k-t}
. Consider a family
\mathcal{G}
with
\nu(\mathcal{G},t)=s-1
and
|\mathcal{G}|=h(n,k,s-1,t)
. Consider also the largest
t
-intersecting family
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subset{[n]\choose k}
. Next, take a random permutation
\sigma
. Then

\mathsf{E}[|\sigma(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\cap\mathcal{G}|]=\frac{|\mathcal{G}||\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|}{{n\choose k}}\leqslant\frac{(s-1){n-1\choose k-t}}{{n\choose k}}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|\leqslant\frac{s(k-t+7)^{8}}{(n-k)^{8}}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|\leqslant\frac{1}{10s^{3}}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|,

where the last inequality is due to our assumption on
n
. Take a permutation
\sigma
for which the size of the intersection is at most its expectation. Note that
\nu(\sigma(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{G},t)\leqslant s
and, therefore,

h(n,k,s,t)\geqslant|\sigma(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{G}|\geqslant h(n,k,s-1,t)+\Big(1-\frac{1}{10s^{3}}\Big)h(n,k,1,t).

2. In order to obtain the bound, we simply substitute the bound from part 1 of the lemma in the following chain of inequalities:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{U}_{1}|\geqslant

\displaystyle|\mathcal{U}_{1}\cup\mathcal{U}_{2}|-|\mathcal{U}_{2}|\geqslant|\mathcal{U}_{1}\cup\mathcal{U}_{2}|-h(n,k,1,t)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle h(n,k,s,t)-\Big(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{10s}\Big)h(n,k,1,t)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle h(n,k,s-1,t)-\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{10s}+\frac{1}{10s^{3}}\Big)h(n,k,1,t)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle h(n,k,s,t)-\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{10(s-1)}\Big)h(n,k,1,t).

∎

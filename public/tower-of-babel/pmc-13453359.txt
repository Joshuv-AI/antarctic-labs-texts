Identification and Experimental Validation of Key Lipid Metabolism-Related Genes in Intracerebral Hemorrhage Based on Machine Learning
Authors: Weizhi Qiu, Shanglu Lin, Longjie Chen, Shaojie Li, Jiayin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe relationship between lipid metabolism and intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is not fully understood, particularly regarding its potential contribution to disease initiation and progression. Accordingly, this study employed machine learning methods to identify and validate pivotal lipid metabolism-related genes in ICH.MethodsWe integrated our mouse mRNA sequencing data with the ICH-associated datasets GSE216607 and GSE200575. Lipid metabolism related genes were obtained from the MSigDB database. The analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs), Weighted correlation network analysis (WGCNA) and intersecting with lipid metabolism-related gene sets to screen the DEG of lipid metabolism-related in ICH. Multiple machine learning (ML) algorithms and a protein protein interaction network were then used to further identify key genes, which were subsequently validated in an independent dataset. Enrichment analysis, immune infiltration analysis, and clustering analysis were conducted to explore their potential biological functions. A mouse ICH model was then established and evaluated by MRI, HE staining, and Nissl staining. Quantitative PCR, immunofluorescence, and Western blotting were performed for preliminary experimental validation of the key genes and pathways.ResultsIn ICH, 84 differentially expressed lipid metabolism-related genes were identified, with enrichment mainly observed in lipid metabolism and the PPAR signaling pathway. By integrating multiple ML algorithms with protein protein interaction network analysis, Plin2, Cd36, and Abca1 were ultimately identified as three key genes. Validation in an independent dataset showed that all three genes were significantly upregulated in the ICH group. In the mouse ICH model, the mRNA expression levels of these genes were also markedly increased, consistent with the bioinformatics results. In addition, the PPAR signaling pathway was implicated by enrichment analysis and preliminary validation.ConclusionAbnormal lipid metabolism may be involved in the pathogenesis and progression of ICH, and Plin2, Cd36, and Abca1 may serve as potential biomarkers associated with ICH-related lipid metabolic changes.

Introduction

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is a condition associated with high rates of mortality, disability, and functional impairment.1 It is an acute cerebrovascular event typically caused by the rupture of a cerebral blood vessel, leading to the formation of an intracerebral hematoma.2 According to the latest Global Burden of Disease (GBD) data, the incidence and mortality of intracerebral hemorrhage continue to rise worldwide. In 2021, stroke caused about 7,252,678 deaths globally, nearly half of which were attributed to intracerebral hemorrhage.3 Its clinical features include sudden headache, impaired consciousness, and hemiplegia; in severe cases, it can lead to death or long-term disability.4 The pathogenesis of intracerebral hemorrhage is complex, primarily involving direct compression of brain tissue by the hematoma, local ischemia, cellular damage, and activation of the inflammatory response.5 Despite continuous advancements in neuroendoscopic techniques and postoperative rehabilitation, the overall treatment outcomes for intracerebral hemorrhage remain limited, and patient prognosis is still suboptimal.6–8 Therefore, there is an urgent need for more effective diagnostic and therapeutic approaches to improve patients’ quality of life.

Lipid metabolism-related genes (LMRGs) are key regulators of lipid biosynthesis and metabolism and are involved in tumors, immune disorders, and inflammatory diseases.9–11 In various disease models, abnormal expression of LMRGs is closely associated with disease progression. Previous studies based on sequencing data analysis of chronic sinusitis have confirmed that LMRGs play a significant role in its pathogenesis and progression, providing a basis for the development of targeted therapies.12 Meanwhile, in osteoarthritis, researchers have identified several key LMRGs using machine learning algorithms and considered them as candidate diagnostic biomarkers. Subsequent studies suggest that the loss of expression of these genes can lead to elevated ceramide levels in chondrocytes, thereby triggering cartilage matrix degradation and chondrocyte apoptosis.13 Furthermore, researchers have increasingly focused on the role of lipid metabolism abnormalities in the pathological remodeling of neurological diseases. Following intracerebral hemorrhage, the expression of fatty acid-binding protein 4 (FABP4), which regulates fatty acid transport and metabolic signaling, increases, promoting lipid droplet accumulation and impairing the ubiquitination of S100A9. These changes activate pro-inflammatory microglia, leading to the release of neurotoxic factors and promoting the migration of neutrophils into the brain parenchyma, thereby triggering a cytokine storm.14 Existing evidence indicates that significant lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis can occur after ICH, suggesting that lipid metabolism disorders are involved in the occurrence and development of ICH. UCP2 can exert neuroprotective effects by inhibiting TRIM21-mediated ubiquitination of GPX4, thereby alleviating hemoglobin-induced ferroptosis in astrocytes.15 These studies suggest the importance of lipid metabolism-related genes in these diseases and their potential therapeutic targets.

Currently, the diagnosis of intracerebral hemorrhage still relies primarily on clinical manifestations and imaging studies; however, these methods are unable to provide in-depth insights into its underlying molecular mechanisms.16 Although genomics and metabolomics research continues to advance, the role of genes related to lipid metabolism in intracerebral hemorrhage remains poorly understood, and relevant diagnostic models have yet to be established and validated. Compared to traditional statistical methods, machine learning offers distinct advantages in handling complex, high-dimensional omics data and can more effectively identify key molecular features.17 Therefore, the present study focused on spontaneous intraparenchymal intracerebral hemorrhage and integrated proprietary RNA-sequencing data with public database resources, together with machine learning methods, to screen for key lipid metabolism-related biomarkers, aiming to provide new theoretical support for the molecular diagnosis and targeted treatment of ICH.

Materials and Methods

Animals and Intracerebral Hemorrhage Model

This study strictly followed the ARRIVE 2.0 guidelines (Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments) for experimental design and reporting. The animal experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University (approval number: 2023–530). All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the “Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals” and the “Animal Research Reporting Standards” published by the National Institutes of Health (NIH). C57BL/6J male mice, aged 6–8 weeks and weighing approximately 20 grams, were obtained from Shanghai SLAC Animal Co., Ltd. Before modeling, the mice were randomly assigned to groups through a lottery system until the final group number reached the planned experimental quantity. Data collection and analysis were performed by the researchers, and the study groups were blinded. Ultimately, mice were randomly assigned to the control group or the ICH group, with n=21 mice per group.

The ICH model was established according to previously reported methods.18,19 After anesthesia via intraperitoneal injection of 0.3% sodium pentobarbital (60 mg/kg), the mouse’s anterior fontanelle was exposed, and the target site was identified (the basal ganglia). 0.5 µL of collagenase VII was slowly injected into the target area at a rate of 0.25 µL/min, followed by a 10-minute pause to prevent backflow of the solution. Post-surgery, the incision was disinfected and sutured, and the puncture site was sealed with bone wax. Post-surgery, the mice were housed individually. On postoperative days 3, mice were euthanized under isoflurane anesthesia, and brain tissue was harvested and rinsed with saline to remove residual blood.20

After completion of the experiment, the surviving mice were euthanized humanely. Deep anesthesia was induced with 3–5% isoflurane (Baxter), followed by intraperitoneal administration of an overdose of pentobarbital sodium (150 mg/kg; Dorminal). Anesthesia and euthanasia were performed in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines and the AVMA Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals.

RNA Sequencing

On the third day after the cerebral hemorrhage, brain tissue samples were collected and used for subsequent sequencing analysis. The target tissue for the experimental group was taken from the brain tissue surrounding the hematoma in the right basal ganglia region; for the normal control group, it was taken from the corresponding anatomical area of the right hemisphere. Total RNA was extracted from approximately 1 × 10^6 cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Library construction and transcriptome sequencing were subsequently performed by Novogene Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) using the Illumina sequencing platform, generating 150bp paired-end reads. Following sequencing and data processing, gene expression data were provided in two formats: raw read counts and fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM).

Brain Tissue Assessment

Brain tissues were fixed, embedded in paraffin, sectioned serially, and routinely deparaffinized to water. For HE staining, sections were first stained with hematoxylin for 5–10 min, rinsed with tap water, and then counterstained with eosin for 5–10 min. In addition, Nissl staining was performed using Nissl staining solution (Beyotime Biotechnology, C0117) at 50–60°C for 40 min. After staining, the sections were rinsed with distilled water, dehydrated through graded ethanol, cleared in xylene, and mounted with neutral resin. Finally, the sections were scanned and imaged.

Mouse MRI Scanning

Intracerebral hemorrhage in mice was assessed using a Bruker 9.4 T Biospec small animal magnetic resonance imaging system at Xiamen University. This system, with a magnetic field strength of 9.4 T, employs radiation free soft tissue imaging technology to obtain high resolution brain images.21

Quantitative Real-Time PCR

Total RNA from brain tissue was isolated with Trizol reagent, followed by cDNA synthesis using the HiFiScript cDNA Synthesis Kit. Quantitative real-time PCR analysis was carried out with the SYBR Green Pro Taq HS Premix qPCR Kit, and GAPDH was used for normalization. Relative mRNA expression was evaluated by the 2^(-ΔΔCT) method. Information on primer sequences and PCR conditions is shown in Supplementary Table 1.

Western Blot Analysis

After BCA quantification, equal amounts of protein were resolved by SDS-PAGE and transferred to methanol-activated PVDF membranes. After blocking at room temperature, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against CD36 (Proteintech, 66395 1 Ig), β actin (Abmart, T40104), and PPARγ (Proteintech, 60127 1 Ig), followed by incubation with the corresponding secondary antibodies for 60 min at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using ECL and analyzed with a chemiluminescence imaging system.

Immunofluorescence

Brain tissues from 3 mice in each group were subjected to immunofluorescence (n = 3). Incubate the primary antibody overnight at 4°C. After washing with PBS, incubate it with donkey anti-rabbit IgG labeled with FITC. Subsequently, the sections were rinsed again with PBS, placed in position, covered with coverslips, and observed under a confocal microscope. The following were used in the experiment: Abca1 antibody (26564-1-AP, Proteintech), Plin2 (15,294-1-AP, Proteintech), and Cd36 antibody (ET1701-24, Huabio).

Data Collection

The keyword “intracerebral hemorrhage” was entered into the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), and “Mus musculus” was selected as the species. We identified the GSE216607 and GSE200575 datasets for further study. The GSE216607 dataset included 3 ICH mouse samples and 3 healthy control samples, whereas the GSE200575 dataset included 6 ICH mouse samples and 6 healthy control samples. Furthermore, 776 LMRGs were collected from lipid metabolism gene sets retrieved from MSigDB database.21 The expression profiles of key differentially expressed lipid metabolism-related genes in ICH (ICHDE-LMRGs) were validated in GSE200575 dataset.

Data Preprocessing and DEG Identification

To ensure data quality, this study preprocessed the in-house mRNA dataset and the original count data from GSE216607. Firstly, genes with a high proportion of missing values were eliminated. For cases where multiple probes corresponded to the same gene in the dataset, the arithmetic mean of their expression levels was used as the final expression value for that gene to reduce data redundancy and simplify the subsequent analysis process. Subsequently, the normalized processing was performed on the processed expression matrix to correct for experimental batch effects and other systematic biases, thereby improving the comparability between different datasets. Specifically, the raw count data from the in-house mRNA dataset and GSE216607 were merged and normalized batch-wise using the ComBat function implemented in the sva package. The corrected data were subsequently visualized using the ggpubr package to assess the impact of batch effect removal (Supplementary Figure 1). Based on the preprocessed expression data, using the “limma” package in R, a differential expression analysis was conducted between the RA group and the normal control group. Differentially expressed genes were identified according to the following criteria: |log2FC| > 1 and P < 0.05.

Identification of Key Modules

A weighted gene co-expression network was generated from the dataset with the “WGCNA” package. The optimal soft threshold was identified using the pickSoftThreshold function, followed by TOM calculation and module detection by clustering. Concurrently, the correlation between each module and ICH was evaluated to identify the modules most strongly associated with ICH. Finally, the association between key modules and ICH was assessed using Gene Significance (GS) and Module Membership (MM) scores.

ICHDE-LMRG Screening

ICHDE-LMRGs were identified using a Venn diagram by taking the intersection of intracerebral hemorrhage DEGs and lipid metabolism-related genes from the key module. The Venn diagram was generated using the “venn” package in R.

Functional Enrichment Analysis

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses for DEGs were performed using the “clusterProfiler” package in R, with a cutoff of p < 0.05, and the results are presented as bar charts.

Machine Learning Algorithms for Identifying Key Genes

We employed a screening method based on nine machine learning algorithms (GLM, RF, LASSO, KNN, NNET, GBM, SVM, XGBoost, and C50) to select genes. First, we used the DALEX package to visualize and compare the residual distributions of each model, reflecting their fitting robustness and predictive performance. Second, we constructed a “gene-algorithm intersection matrix” based on the nine algorithms, illustrating the identification status of the top-ranked lipid metabolism-related genes across different algorithms. Next, we ranked the genes according to their frequency of selection across the algorithms and identified key genes that appeared consistently across multiple algorithms.

Functional Enrichment Analysis of Key ICHDE-LMRGs

The STRING database is used to identify protein interactions.22 We set the minimum interaction score for genes associated with key ICHDE-LMRGs to 0.4.23 We visualized the PPI network using Cytoscape software (version 3.9.1) and performed topological analysis using the Degree algorithm via the CytoHubba plugin.24 Subsequently, we conducted GO and KEGG enrichment analyses on the genes in the PPI network.

Analysis of Immune Cell Infiltration

The “CIBERSORT” package was used to estimate immune cell infiltration in the healthy control and ICH groups. This algorithm, based on linear support vector regression, deconvolves gene expression data to infer the relative proportions of human immune cell subsets. Spearman correlation analysis was further performed to assess the associations between immune cell abundance and specific gene expression.

Statistical Analysis

Both bioinformatics and statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.3). qPCR and Western blot data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical analyses were conducted using t-tests, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Screening of ICH-Associated Lipid Metabolism Genes

After normalization, the median expression levels remained consistent across samples, indicating that batch effects had been effectively eliminated (Supplementary Figure 1). Using the predefined screening criteria, 2,626 differentially expressed genes were detected, including 2,256 upregulated genes and 370 downregulated genes (Figure 1A). The heatmap in Figure 1B displays a subset of significantly upregulated and downregulated genes. We performed WGCNA analysis to identify key gene modules associated with ICH. By comprehensively evaluating the scale-invariant topological fit index and average connectivity change, we determined the optimal soft threshold (Figure 1D) and constructed a co-expression network based on this threshold, identifying 13 modules. Figure 1C displays the clustering of characteristic genes within the modules. Additionally, we assessed the correlation between ICH and gene modules (Figure 1E). The results showed that the turquoise module (cor = 0.97, p < 0.001) was positively correlated with ICH and was therefore identified as a key module. Further correlation analysis was conducted on the MM of key module genes and the GS. The results showed that in the turquoise module, MM was significantly positively correlated with the ICH-related GS, with a correlation coefficient of R = 0.982 (Figure 1F). Subsequently, based on the intersection of ICH-associated DEGs, the turquoise module, and 776 lipid metabolism-related genes, we screened out 84 genes (Figure 1G). Identification of Key ICHDE-LMRGs

To identify ICHDE-LMRGs with potential diagnostic value in intracerebral hemorrhage, this study constructed and compared the predictive performance of various machine learning models. By evaluating the distribution of prediction residuals across multiple models, the results of the residual cumulative distribution plots (Figure 3A) and box plots (Figure 3B) showed that the GLM, RF, and LASSO models performed best in terms of controlling absolute residuals and demonstrated relatively higher predictive accuracy. Subsequently, based on the ranking of significant features across models, the top 25 frequently identified lipid metabolism-related genes were selected, and an algorithm-gene intersection matrix was plotted (Figure 3C). Genes such as Plin2, Cd36, and Abca1 were repeatedly identified across multiple models. Further statistical analysis of the frequency of these genes across algorithms was conducted and visualized (Figure 3D). The results showed that the Plin2, Cd36, and Abca1 genes were jointly identified across 7 algorithms, suggesting they may be robust candidate key genes. To further investigate the interaction relationships among these candidate genes, we imported the previously confirmed 84 common genes into the STRING database to construct a PPI network (Figure 3E), revealing highly close functional associations among multiple genes. To further identify key regulatory factors, we performed network topological analysis using the CytoHubba plugin in Cytoscape with the Degree algorithm, ultimately selecting the top 10 hub genes ranked by degree (Figure 3F). We observed that Plin2, Cd36, and Abca1 were still included among them. Validation in Animal Experiments

To further validate the results of the transcriptomic analysis, we successfully established a mouse ICH model. MRI results showed residual hematoma at the predetermined site, confirming the successful establishment of the model (Figure 6A). Histological analysis of mouse brain tissue performed 3 days after ICH, using H&E and Nissl staining, revealed significant differences in neuronal integrity between the two groups. H&E staining revealed more severe neuronal damage in the ICH group, with marked cellular edema, nuclear condensation, nuclear fragmentation, dissolution, and irregular cell contours observed around the hematoma (Figure 6B). Nissl staining further validated these observations, showing a significant reduction in Nissl bodies around the hematoma, indicating that most neurons had undergone apoptosis (Figure 6C). Seven days after intracerebral hemorrhage, the expression levels of genes associated with intracerebral hemorrhage (Plin2, Cd36, Abca1) in the model group were verified via qPCR. The qPCR results were consistent with the transcriptomic analysis, showing that the mRNA expression of these genes was significantly higher in the ICH group than in the control group (P < 0.05) (Figure 6D). Furthermore, immunofluorescence staining further confirmed the expression levels of Plin2, Cd36, and Abca1, revealing that the expression of these three key genes was significantly increased in the ICH group (Figure 6E). Quantitative analysis results were consistent with the immunofluorescence findings (Figure 6F). In pathway enrichment analysis, we repeatedly observed significant enrichment of the PPAR signaling pathway, which regulates lipid metabolism and energy balance. Therefore, we performed protein validation for PPARγ and its classic downstream target, Cd36 (Figure 6G). Discussion

In study, we identified the most critical lipid metabolism related genes associated with intracerebral hemorrhage through bioinformatics analysis combined with nine machine learning algorithms. First, differentially expressed gene analysis and weighted gene co expression network analysis were performed to identify lipid metabolism related gene modules significantly associated with intracerebral hemorrhage, yielding 84 key genes. Further functional enrichment analysis indicated that these genes were mainly involved in pathways related to lipid metabolism, immune response, and oxidative stress. Using nine machine learning algorithms, we further identified several lipid metabolism related genes, including Plin2, Cd36, and Abca1, as key genes in intracerebral hemorrhage, and these genes were repeatedly selected as important feature genes across multiple algorithms. In addition, qPCR and immunofluorescence analyses confirmed that the expression levels of the key lipid metabolism related genes Plin2, Cd36, and Abca1 were significantly upregulated in brain tissues after intracerebral hemorrhage compared with the control group, which was consistent with the bioinformatics predictions.

PLIN2 is a member of the lipid droplet coating protein family and is widely involved in lipid metabolism, lipid droplet formation and storage, and fatty acid transport.25 Previous studies have shown that PLIN2 can reduce lipid mobilization by preventing lipases from accessing the surface of lipid droplets, thereby maintaining triglyceride content within lipid droplets, while PLIN2 overexpression can further promote lipid droplet accumulation.26 In neurological disorders, the detrimental effects of lipid droplets in certain brain pathological processes have been reported. During brain aging, microglia with lipid droplet accumulation often exhibit a stronger pro inflammatory phenotype.27 In ischemic stroke, proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy analysis revealed increased lipid levels in brain tissue following ischemic brain injury. Further studies demonstrated that this alteration was mainly concentrated in astrocytes and microglia and was characterized by increased lipid droplet accumulation and abnormal expression of lipid metabolism related genes.28 Similarly, in an animal study, researchers found that PLIN2 expression was significantly upregulated in perihematomal tissue after intracerebral hemorrhage in mice, and this change was closely associated with aggravated neuronal injury and neurological dysfunction.29 Therefore, it is reasonable to speculate that PLIN2 plays an important role in the occurrence and progression of intracerebral hemorrhage and in the associated mechanisms of lipid metabolism.

CD36 is a membrane protein that functions as both a scavenger receptor and a transporter of long chain fatty acids, playing important roles in lipid uptake, phagocytic clearance, and inflammatory regulation.30 In ICH, CD36 may act not only as an inflammatory regulator but also as a key node linking lipid uptake and metabolic responses.31 Li et al found that hemoglobin released after intracerebral hemorrhage stimulated microglia to produce IL 10, which enhanced erythrophagocytosis by upregulating microglial CD36, thereby promoting hematoma clearance while alleviating inflammation and neurological impairment. Intranasal administration of IL 10 after ICH improved neurological function and accelerated hematoma resolution, whereas this protective effect was largely abolished in CD36 deficient mice, indicating that the beneficial effects of IL 10 are dependent on CD36.32 Previous animal studies have also shown that activation of PPARγ upregulates CD36 expression in microglia/macrophages after ICH, thereby promoting hematoma absorption, reducing brain edema, and suppressing perihematomal inflammation. However, these effects were markedly attenuated after PPARγ inhibition.33 This is, to some extent, consistent with our finding that both PPARγ and CD36 protein levels were synchronously increased 7 days after ICH, suggesting a compensatory activation of cholesterol efflux and lipid homeostasis regulation in the injured brain tissue. It also implies that the PPARγ CD36 axis may play an important role in lipid metabolic reprogramming and the regulation of the inflammatory microenvironment after ICH. Nevertheless, the mechanisms by which CD36 regulates lipid metabolism in ICH remain poorly understood and warrant further investigation.

ABCA1 is an important transporter involved in the efflux of cholesterol and phospholipids across the cell membrane. It plays a crucial role in lipid metabolism, particularly in the regulation of high density lipoprotein and cholesterol homeostasis.34 As a lipid regulatory molecule in ICH, the importance of ABCA1 has been supported by several related studies. Previous studies have shown that, in a collagenase induced intracerebral hemorrhage model, treatment with the synthetic liver X receptor agonist TO901317 markedly increased the protein expression of ABCA1, a classical downstream target of LXR, and significantly improved neurological function in mice after ICH. The authors suggested that this effect may be related to the involvement of ABCA1 in lipid homeostasis regulation and the alleviation of neuroinflammation after ICH.35 The study by Zhao et al further supports the role of ABCA1 in lipid metabolism. In a time series transcriptomic analysis following middle cerebral artery occlusion in rats, GSEA showed significant enrichment of cholesterol metabolism related gene sets, accompanied by upregulation of ABCA1, a key gene involved in cholesterol efflux. This change became more pronounced during the chronic stage of hematoma absorption, suggesting that cholesterol homeostasis undergoes dynamic changes during disease progression.36 In addition, a study using nanoparticles loaded with a liver X receptor agonist directly exploited the ABCA1 mediated cholesterol regulatory pathway to promote erythrocyte clearance and neuronal repair after ICH.37

This study also has several limitations. First, due to the limited availability of publicly accessible ICH sequencing data, this study integrated publicly available data with our own sequencing data after batch-correction. Although batch correction can reduce technical bias to some extent, residual heterogeneity may still exist across different data sources, testing platforms, and sample processing workflows, thereby affecting the stability and accuracy of the results. Second, although this study expanded the sample size by integrating public data with our own sequencing data and employed multiple machine learning algorithms to screen for key genes to reduce errors, the overall sample size remains limited, and the results may still be at risk of overfitting. Future studies may consider combining multi-omics data with longitudinal cohort data. Finally, although we validated the expression changes of the identified hub genes at the mRNA and protein levels, the present study did not directly determine their causal roles in lipid metabolism or ICH-induced brain injury. Therefore, future studies using gene knockdown or overexpression, lipidomic analysis, and functional rescue experiments are needed to clarify their biological roles after ICH.

Conclusion

In summary, this study combined bioinformatics analysis with ML algorithms to identify three key genes associated with lipid metabolism following ICH, suggesting that lipid metabolism may play a significant role in the pathological progression of ICH. Further experiments verified the increased expression of these key genes, suggesting their potential value as biomarkers for ICH.

Finite induction functor for vertex operator algebras
Jianqi Liu

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a new induction functor Ind U V \mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U} between module categories corresponding to an embedding of vertex operator algebras (VOAs) U ↪ V U\hookrightarrow V . This induction functor is essentially defined at the level of the finite (Zhu) algebras, which we call the finite induction functor . Under suitable conditions on U U and V V , we prove that this functor satisfies the usual properties of induction functors, such as Frobenius reciprocity, functorial property for compositions, and an analogue of Artin’s induction theorem for certain associated characters. To better understand the effect of this functor, we explicitly determine the finite induction of irreducible modules for standard subVOAs of the rank-one lattice/affine VOA V A 1 V_{A_{1}} , as well as the finite induction of irreducible modules over a parabolic-type subVOA V P V_{P} of the rank-two lattice/affine VOA V A 2 V_{A_{2}} .



1.
Introduction

Due to the complexity of the structure of VOAs and the fact that a VOA has infinitely many products, it is extremely difficult to study induced modules for a VOA embedding
U\hookrightarrow V
. In their foundational work on conformal field theory
[
MS89
]
, Moore and Seiberg discovered a close analogy between group theory and the theory of chiral algebras (VOAs). Since induction plays a central role in the representation theory of finite groups, this naturally suggests the existence of an induction functor for VOAs. Understanding how modules behave under VOA embeddings is fundamental for the study of extensions, orbifolds, and coset constructions in conformal field theory. Dong and Lin made early progress in this direction
[
DLin96
]
.
However, concrete examples for standard VOA embedding of such inductions remain unknown.

Zhu’s associative algebra
A(V)

[
Z96
]
provides a significant simplification of the representation theory of VOAs and, in turn, potentially offers an alternative approach to constructing induced modules for VOAs.
Inspired by the author’s previous work on classical Yang-Baxter equations on VOAs
[
BGL25
,
BGLW23
]
and the Borel- and parabolic-type VOAs
[
Liu25
]
, in this paper, we introduce a induced module functor for VOAs on the Zhu algebra
[
Z96
]
level, which we call the
finite induction functor
for VOAs. This functor can be explicitly determined as long as we have enough knowledge about Zhu algebras
A(U)
,
A(V)
for a VOA embedding
U\hookrightarrow V
, as well as the kernel of the algebra homomorphism
\pi:A(U)\rightarrow A(V)
induced by the VOA embedding. Here, we do not need the VOA embedding
U\hookrightarrow V
to be conformal. i.e., the Virasoro element
\omega_{U}
need not be the same as
\omega_{V}
.

1.1.
Finite induction functor for VOAs

The embedding of VOAs
U\hookrightarrow V
leads, by definition, to a homomorphism between their Zhu algebras:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A(U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A(V).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Unlike the situation for finite groups or Lie algebras,
\ker(\pi)
is nonzero in most cases. We first prove that the natural restriction-of-scalars functor
\mathrm{Res}^{A(V)}_{A(U)}:\mathsf{Mod}(A(V))\to\mathsf{Mod}(A(U))
associated with the algebra homomorphism

admits a left adjoint functor

\mathrm{Ind}^{A(V)}_{A(U)}:\mathsf{Mod}(A(U))\to\mathsf{Mod}(A(V)),\quad\mathrm{Ind}^{A(V)}_{A(U)}(\Omega_{M}):=A(V)\otimes_{\pi(A(U))}(\Omega_{M}/\ker(\pi)\cdot\Omega_{M}),

which satisfies the functorial property with respect to compositions, see Propositions
2.6
and
2.8
.

1.1.1.
Definition of finite induction functor

Given an admissible (i.e.,
\mathbb{N}
-gradable)
V
-module
W=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}W(n)
, the space of “highest-weight vectors”

\Omega(W)=\{\,v\in W:a_{n}v=0,\ \mathrm{wt}\,a-n-1<0\,\}

is a module over the Zhu algebra
A(V)

[
Z96
]
. In particular,
\Omega(\cdot)
can be viewed as a functor
\Omega:\mathsf{Adm}(V)\to\mathsf{Mod}(A(V))
. Dong-Li-Mason constructed a left adjoint functor to
\Omega
, called the
generalized Verma module functor

[
DLM98
]
, denoted by
\bar{M}(\cdot)
. Using properties of the universal enveloping algebra of a VOA
[
FZ92
,
FBZ04
]
, Damiolini-Gibney-Krashen provided an alternative construction of
\bar{M}(\cdot)
, denoted by
\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}(\cdot)
. In particular,

(\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}\dashv\Omega):\mathsf{Mod}(A(V))\rightleftarrows\mathsf{Adm}(V)

(1.3)

is a pair of adjoint functors describing the relationship between the representation theories of finite and affine algebras (
1.2
).
It was proved in
[
DGK2
,
GGKL25
,
DLM98
]
that (
1.3
) is an adjoint equivalence between categories if and only if
V
satisfies the
strongly unital condition
for its mode transition algebras
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
. This condition holds when the VOA
V
is rational
[
DGK23
]
. Moreover, the Heisenberg VOA and certain tensor products are examples of irrational VOAs satisfying the strongly unital condition for mode transition algebras
[
DGK2
,
LS25
]
.

For the VOA embedding
U\hookrightarrow V
, when both
U
and
V
satisfy the strongly unital condition for mode transition algebras, then the adjoint pair (
1.3
) is an adjoint equivalence between categories, and we may lift the adjoint pair of functors on the associative algebra level
(\mathrm{Ind}^{A(V)}_{A(U)}\dashv\mathrm{Res}^{A(V)}_{A(U)}):\mathsf{Mod}(A(U))\rightleftarrows\mathsf{Mod}(A(V))
through the vertical equivalence of category functors in diagram (
1.1
) to an adjoint pair of functors
(\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}\dashv\mathrm{Res}^{V}_{U}):\mathsf{Adm}(U)\rightleftarrows\mathsf{Adm}(V)
. In particular, for any
M\in\mathsf{Adm}(U)
, we have

\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}(M):=\Phi_{V}^{\mathsf{L}}\left(A(V)\otimes_{A_{U}}\frac{\Omega_{U}(M)}{\ker(\pi).\Omega_{U}(M)}\right),

(1.4)

where
A_{U}=\pi(A(U))\leq A(V)
is a subalgebra. We call
\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}
and
\mathrm{Res}^{V}_{U}
the
finite induction and restriction
functors for VOAs, see Definition
2.10
.

1.1.2.
Properties and examples of the finite induction functor

The usual properties of induced modules, including Frobenius reciprocity and the functorial property for compositions, are satisfied by the finite induction functor; see Propositions
2.6
and
2.8
.

Theorem A
.

If
U
and
V
both satisfy the strongly unital property for their mode transition algebras, then for any
M\in\mathsf{Adm}(U)
and
W\in\mathsf{Adm}(V)
, we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Adm}(V)}(\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}(M),W)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Adm}(U)}(M,\mathrm{Res}^{V}_{U}(W)).

Moreover, let
U_{1}\hookrightarrow U_{2}\hookrightarrow V
be consecutive embeddings of VOAs. Then we have

\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U_{2}}\circ\mathrm{Ind}^{U_{2}}_{U_{1}}=\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U_{1}},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Res}^{V}_{U_{2}}\circ\mathrm{Res}^{U_{2}}_{U_{1}}=\mathrm{Res}^{V}_{U_{1}}.

There are many interesting examples of VOA embeddings
U\hookrightarrow V
, such as the orbifold embedding
V^{G}\hookrightarrow V
. In this paper, we focus on examples where the Zhu algebras

and

associated with the root lattice
A_{1}

[

]

[

]

, and

In the representation theory of finite groups, the
character

\chi_{V}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of a representation
\rho:G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V)
, defined by
\chi_{V}(g)=\tr_{V}\rho(g)
, plays a fundamental role. Artin’s and Brauer’s induction theorems, as well as Mackey’s theory of irreducible modules, can all be interpreted in terms of characters. Since the theory of VOAs and the corresponding conformal field theory (CFT) can be viewed as a generalization of group theory
[

]
, it is natural to expect a notion of character for VOAs that is compatible with induced module functors.

The

[

,
Z96
]

Z_{W}(a,\tau)=\tr_{W}\big(o(a)q^{L(0)-\frac{c}{24}}\big)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\tr_{W(n)}\big(o(a)\big)q^{n+h-\frac{c}{24}}

1.2
), while the “finite-type” character is given by the top-degree coefficient
\tr_{W(0)}o(a)
of the formal character

. Indeed,

is a module over the Zhu algebra

, which is the “finite-type” algebra.

Therefore, we define the

of an ordinary

with

\chi_{W}:A(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\quad\chi_{W}([a])=\tr_{\Omega(W)}o(a).

The ring generated by irreducible characters is denoted by

, and is called the
finite-type character ring
, see Definition
5.1
for more details.

Given a VOA embedding
U\hookrightarrow V
, with

, denoted

\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}(\chi^{U}_{M}):=\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}(M)}.

By generalizing the argument on the finite induction of Virasoro VOA in

(

).

Theorem B
.

Let

of its irreducible modules are pairwise distinct. Then every element of

can be written as a

2.1.1.
Module categories of VOAs

Definition 2.1

Let

-graded vector space

called the
module vertex operator
, satisfying

(1)

(truncation property) For any

and
u\in M

Y_{M}(a,z)u\in M((z))
.

(2)

(vacuum property)
Y_{M}(\mathbf{1},z)=\mathrm{Id}_{M}
.

(3)

(Jacobi identity for
Y_{M}
) for any
a,b\in V
and

,

\displaystyle z_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle Y_{M}(a,z_{1})Y_{M}(b,z_{2})u-z_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{-z_{2}+z_{1}}{z_{0}}\right)Y_{M}(b,z_{2})Y_{M}(a,z_{1})u

(2.1)

\displaystyle=z_{2}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}}\right)Y_{M}(Y(a,z_{0})b,z_{2})u.

(4)

(

-derivative property)
Y_{M}(L(-1)a,z)=\frac{d}{dz}Y_{M}(a,z)
for any

.

(5)

(grading property) For any
a\in V
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

,

a_{m}M(n)\subseteq M(n+\mathrm{wt}a-m-1).

(2.2)

In other words,

acts as a degree

.

We write

if

, and call it the

of

. Submodules, quotient modules, and irreducible modules are defined in the usual categorical sense. A morphism between two admissible
V
-modules

and

is a linear map

satisfying

(2.3)

Denote the category of admissible modules by

. The following additional notions will be used later.

(1)

An admissible
V
-module

is called

if each degree-

is a finite-dimensional eigenspace of
L(0)
of eigenvalue
n+h
, where

is called the

of
M
. In particular, if we write
L(0)v=(\mathrm{wt}v)\cdot v
for

, then
\mathrm{wt}v=\deg v+h
. Denote the category of ordinary modules by
\mathrm{Ord}(V)
.

(2)

More generally, a
weak

V
-module is vector space

, together with a module vertex operator
Y_{M}(\cdot,z)
, satisfying conditions
(1),(2)
, and
(3)
above
[
DLM98
]
.

(3)

V
is called

if
\mathsf{Adm}(V)
is semisimple
[
Z96
,
DLM98
]
.

(4)

Let
C_{2}(V)=\mathrm{span}\{a_{-2}b:a,b\in V\}
.

is said to be
C_{2}
-cofinite
if
\dim V/C_{2}(V)<\infty
.

(5)

is called

if

is of CFT-type, simple,

2.1.2.
Universal enveloping algebra of a VOA

Definition 2.2
.

[
B86
,
FZ92
,
DGK23
]

The
Borcherds’ Lie algebra
L(V)
associated to a VOA
V
is defined by

L(V)=(V\otimes\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}])/\nabla(V\otimes\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}])=\mathrm{span}\{a_{[n]}:=a\otimes t^{n}+\imaginary\nabla:a\in V,n\in\mathbb{Z}\},

where
\nabla=L(-1)\otimes\mathrm{Id}+\mathrm{Id}\otimes(d/dt)
. The Lie bracket on
L(V)
is given by

[a_{[m]},b_{[n]}]=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{m}{j}(a_{j}b)_{[m+n-j]},\quad a,b\in V,\ m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

For a general spanning element
a_{[n]}=a\otimes t^{n}+\imaginary\nabla\in L(V)
, with
a\in V
homogeneous, define

\deg(a_{[n]}):=\mathrm{wt}a-n-1.

(2.4)

Let
\mathsf{U}=U(L(V))
be the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra
L(V)
. It is a graded associative algebra by the degree (
2.4
):

\mathsf{U}=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{U}_{d},\quad\mathsf{U}_{d}=\mathrm{span}\left\{a^{1}_{[n_{1}]}\dots a^{r}_{[n_{r}]}\in\mathsf{U}:\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\left(\mathrm{wt}a^{i}-n_{i}-1\right)=d\right\}.

Let
\mathsf{U}_{\leq-n}=\sum_{d\leq-n}\mathsf{U}_{d}
, which makes
\mathsf{U}
a split-filtered associative algebra
\mathsf{U}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{U}_{\leq-n}
. Define

\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{U}=\mathsf{U}\cdot\mathsf{U}_{\leq-n}=\mathsf{U}\cdot L(V)_{\leq-n},\quad\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{U}=\mathsf{U}_{\geq n}\cdot\mathsf{U}=L(V)_{\geq n}\cdot\mathsf{U}.

(2.5)

where
L(V)_{\leq-n}=\mathrm{span}\{a_{[k]}\in L(V):\deg(a_{[k]})\leq-n\}
, see
[
DGK23
, Lemma 2.4.2]
.
L(V)_{\geq n}
is defined in a similar way. Since the identity
1=\mathbf{1}_{[-1]}
of
\mathsf{U}
is contained in
\mathsf{U}_{\leq 0}
and
\mathsf{U}_{\geq 0}
, we have
\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{U}=\mathsf{U}=\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{U}
if
n\leq 0
.

The left ideals
\{\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{U}:n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
is a system of neighborhood of
0
in
\mathsf{U}
, which gives a canonical seminorm on
\mathsf{U}
, see
[
DGK23
, Definition A.6.1]
. One can restrict these seminorms to the graded parts
\mathsf{U}_{d}
of
\mathsf{U}
:

\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{U}_{d}:=(\mathsf{U}\cdot\mathsf{U}_{\leq-n})_{d}=\sum_{j\leq-n}\mathsf{U}_{d-j}\cdot\mathsf{U}_{j},\quad\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{U}_{d}=(\mathsf{U}_{\geq n}\cdot\mathsf{U})_{d}=\sum_{i\geq n}\mathsf{U}_{i}\cdot\mathsf{U}_{d-i}.

(2.6)

In particular,
\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{U}_{d}=\mathrm{N}^{n+d}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{U}_{d}
for any
d\in\mathbb{Z}
. Define the completion

\widehat{\mathsf{U}}_{d}:=\varprojlim_{n}\frac{\mathsf{U}_{d}}{\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{U}_{d}}=\varprojlim_{n}\frac{\mathsf{U}_{d}}{\mathrm{N}^{n+d}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathsf{U}_{d}}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\widehat{\mathsf{U}}:=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathsf{U}}_{d}.

Let
J\subset\widehat{\mathsf{U}}
be the graded ideal generated by the components of the Jacobi identity (
2.1
), and let
\bar{J}\subseteq\widehat{\mathsf{U}}
be the closure of
J
with respect to the seminorm defined by the image of neighborhoods (
2.5
) in
\widehat{U}
. Define

\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{U}(V):=\widehat{\mathsf{U}}/\bar{J}=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathscr{U}_{d}.

Then
\mathscr{U}
is a graded complete seminormed associative algebra with respect to the canonical seminorm induced by the image of neighborhoods (
2.5
).
\mathscr{U}
is called the
universal enveloping algebra of the VOA
V
. The left and right neighborhoods at
0
of
\mathscr{U}
are given by

\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{U}\cdot\mathscr{U}_{\leq-n}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mathrm{N}^{n}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{U}_{\geq n}\cdot\mathscr{U},

(2.7)

with
\mathrm{N}^{n+1}_{\mathsf{L}}\mathscr{U}_{0}=\sum_{j\geq n+1}\mathscr{U}_{j}\cdot\mathscr{U}_{-j}=\mathrm{N}^{n+1}_{\mathsf{R}}\mathscr{U}_{0}
, for any
n\geq 0
.

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

(2.14)

2.2.1.
Construction of induced modules

Here we give a natural definition/construction of the induced modules for the algebra homomorphism (

(1)

\pi(B)\leq A
is a unital subalgebra,
\ker(\pi)\lhd B
is a two-sided ideal, and

(2)

There is a natural

\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}(-):\mathsf{Mod}(A)\rightarrow\mathsf{Mod}(B),

which is an exact functor between these abelian categories.

(3)

Conversely, let

be an object in

, then
\Omega_{M}/\ker(\pi).\Omega_{M}
is a left module over

, for any

Then

if we view

\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B}(\Omega_{M})
is generated by

(4)

be a morphism in

\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{A}(f)=\mathrm{Id}_{A}\otimes\bar{f}:\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{A}(\Omega_{M})\rightarrow\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{A}(\Omega_{W}),\quad a\otimes\bar{v}\mapsto a\otimes\overline{f(v)}

(2.16)

If

2.2.2.
The Frobenius reciprocity

The induction defined by (

is a left adjoint of the functor

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\pi:B\rightarrow A
be a homomorphism of associative algebras. Then

\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{A}(-):\mathsf{Mod}(B)\rightleftarrows\mathsf{Mod}(A):\mathrm{Res}_{B}^{A}(-)

(2.17)

is an adjoint pair of functors. In other words, there exists a canonical isomorphism of the hom-spaces:

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{A}(\Omega_{M}),K)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\Omega_{M},\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}(K)),

(2.18)

for any left
B
-module
\Omega_{M}
and any left
A
-module
K
.

Proof.

Define
\Phi:\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\Omega_{M},\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}(K))\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{A}(\Omega_{M}),K)
by

\Phi(g)(a\otimes\bar{v}):=a.g(v),\quad a\in A,v\in\Omega_{M},\ g\in\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\Omega_{M},\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}(K)).

For
b\in\ker(\pi)
, we have
a\otimes\overline{b.v}=0
in
\mathrm{Ind}^{A}_{B}(\Omega_{M})
(
2.15
). Note that
\Phi(g)(a\otimes\overline{b.v})=a.g(b.v)=a.(\pi(b).g(v))=0
by the definition of
\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}(K)
. Clearly,
\Phi(g)
is a left
A
-module homomorphism. Hence
\Phi
is well-defined.

Conversely, define
\Psi:\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{A}(\Omega_{M}),K)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\Omega_{M},\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{B}(K))
by

\Psi(f)(v):=f(1\otimes\bar{v}),\quad v\in\Omega_{M},\ f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{A}(\Omega_{M}),K).

We have

The usual iteration property of the induction is also satisfied:

Proposition 2.8

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}

Proof.

Let

We need to show it is isomorphic to

\displaystyle\rightarrow A\otimes_{\pi(B)}(B/\ker(\pi))\otimes_{\varphi(C)}(\Omega_{M}/\ker(\varphi).\Omega_{M}),

(2.20)

\displaystyle a\otimes[b\otimes\bar{v}]

\displaystyle\mapsto a\otimes\bar{b}\otimes\bar{v},\quad a\in A,\ b\in B,\ v\in\Omega_{M},

\displaystyle a\otimes\bar{b}\otimes\bar{v}

5.
Finite-type character ring of VOAs and Artin’s induction theorem

This section aims to address the question of whether a module over a VOA
V
can be obtained through finite induction from modules over subVOAs
U\subset V
. To properly formulate this question, we introduce the notion of the finite-type character ring of a VOA.

5.1.
Trace functions on
A(V)

Let
W=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}W(n)
be an irreducible ordinary
V
-module of conformal weight
h
.
The one-point correlation function on the torus associated to
W
is the following power series
[
FLM88
,
Z96
]
:

Z_{W}(a,\tau)=\tr\nolimits_{W}o(a)q^{L(0)-\frac{c}{24}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\tr\nolimits_{W(n)}o(a)q^{n+h-\frac{c}{24}},

(5.1)

where
c
is the central charge of
V
and
W(0)=\Omega(W)
.
Its top-degree summand
\tr\nolimits_{\Omega(W)}o(a)\,q^{h-\frac{c}{24}}
can be viewed as a function on
A(V)
, since the map

o:A(V)\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\Omega(W)),\qquad[a]\mapsto o(a)

is a representation of
A(V)
. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathrm{Ord}^{\mathrm{fin}.}(V)
be the subcategory of
\mathrm{Ord}(V)
whose objects are ordinary
V
-modules
W
such that
\Omega(W)
is finite-dimensional. We define the
finite-type character of
W
to be the trace function

\chi_{W}:A(V)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad\chi_{W}([a])=\tr\nolimits_{\Omega(W)}o(a).

(5.2)

Then
\chi_{W}\in\mathrm{SF}(A(V)):=\{f:A(V)\to\mathbb{C}\mid f([a]\ast[b])=f([b]\ast[a]),\ \forall[a],[b]\in A(V)\}
,
the space of symmetric functions on
A(V)
. Define

R^{\mathrm{fin}}(V):=\sum_{W\in\mathrm{Ord}^{\mathrm{fin}.}(V)}\mathbb{Z}\cdot\chi_{W}.

Then
R^{\mathrm{fin}}(V)
is a sub-abelian group of
\mathrm{SF}(A(V))
.

Note that if
W=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}W(n)
is an irreducible ordinary
V
-module, with
W(n)
being an eigenspace of
L(0)
of eigenvalue
h+n
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then the bottom degree
W(0)=\Omega(W)
is finite-dimensional. In particular
W\in\mathrm{Ord}^{\mathrm{fin}.}(V)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
V
be a strongly rational VOA with irreducible modules
\{V=W^{0},\dots,W^{r}\}
. Then the irreducible finite characters
\{\chi_{W^{0}},\dots,\chi_{W^{r}}\}
form a basis of
\mathrm{SF}(A(V))
. In particular,

R^{\mathrm{fin}}(V)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}\mathbb{Z}\cdot\chi_{W^{i}}.

(5.3)

Moreover,
R^{\mathrm{fin}}(V)
is a commutative ring with respect to the following fusion product:

\chi_{W^{i}}\ast\chi_{W^{j}}:=\chi_{W^{i}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W^{j}}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}N_{ij}^{k}\chi_{W^{k}},

(5.4)

where
W^{i}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W^{j}
denotes Huang–Lepowsky’s
P(z)
-tensor product
[
HL96
,
H05
]
.
We call
R^{\mathrm{fin}}(V)
the
finite-type character ring
of the VOA
V
.

Proof.

Since
A(V)
is semisimple, it was proved in
[
Z96
, Lemma 5.3.3]
that
\{\chi_{W^{0}},\dots,\chi_{W^{r}}\}
form a basis of
\mathrm{SF}(A(V))
.
Given any
M,N\in\mathrm{Ord}(V)
, we have

\chi_{M\oplus N}([a])=\tr\nolimits_{\Omega(M)\oplus\Omega(N)}o(a)=\chi_{M}([a])+\chi_{N}([a]),

for any
[a]\in A(V)
. Hence
R^{\mathrm{fin}}(V)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}\mathbb{Z}\cdot\chi_{W^{i}}
.

Finally, the fact that
R^{\mathrm{fin}}(V)
forms a commutative ring with respect to (
5.4
) follows from the commutativity and associativity of the fusion rules
[
H05
,
GL25
]
and the decomposition of the
P(z)
-tensor product module

W^{i}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W^{j}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{r}N_{ij}^{k}W^{k}
for strongly rational VOAs
[
HL96
]
.
∎

Let
U\hookrightarrow V
be a VOA embedding, and assume that
V
is strongly rational.
Then for any irreducible ordinary
U
-module
M\in\mathrm{Ord}(U)
, the finite induction

\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}(M)=\Phi_{V}^{\mathsf{L}}\!\left(A(V)\otimes_{A_{U}}\frac{\Omega_{U}(M)}{\ker(\pi)\cdot\Omega_{U}(M)}\right)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}m_{i}W^{i}

is a direct sum of irreducible ordinary
V
-modules. For the character
\chi^{U}_{M}\in R^{\mathrm{fin}}(U)
, we define

\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}(\chi^{U}_{M}):=\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}(M)}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}m_{i}\chi^{V}_{W^{i}}\in R^{\mathrm{fin}}(V),

(5.5)

and call it the
induced finite character
of
M
with respect to
U\hookrightarrow V
.

If, furthermore,
U
is also strongly rational, then
R^{\mathrm{fin}}(U)
is a free abelian group generated by the irreducible finite characters of
U
by Lemma
5.2
, and

\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}:R^{\mathrm{fin}}(U)\longrightarrow R^{\mathrm{fin}}(V),\quad\chi^{U}_{M}\longmapsto\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}(\chi^{U}_{M})

(5.6)

is a homomorphism of abelian groups.

Remark 5.3
.

In the case of finite groups, the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[G]
has a canonical basis given by the elements of the group
G
. This basis induces the Schur inner product

(\chi_{M},\chi_{N})=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi_{M}(g)\chi_{N}(g^{-1}),\quad M,N\in\mathrm{Irr}(G),

on the character ring
R(G)
. Moreover, the coset representatives of a subgroup
H\leq G
give a concrete expression for the induced character:

\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\chi)(g)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{0}\in G,\ g_{0}^{-1}gg_{0}\in H\end{subarray}}\chi(g_{0}^{-1}gg_{0}),\quad\chi\in R(G).

However, in the VOA situation, the Zhu algebra
A(V)
does not possess a canonical basis analogous to that of
\mathbb{C}[G]
. In fact,
A(V)
is more closely related to the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
than to a group algebra. It is not yet known whether the finite character ring
R^{\mathrm{fin}}(V)
has properties similar to those of
R(G)
.

-module has the following decomposition:

\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}(L(c,h_{j}))\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq r\\
h_{i}=h_{j}\end{subarray}}n_{i}W^{i}.

Now, if the conformal weights
h_{0},\dots,h_{r}\in\mathbb{C}
of irreducible
V
-modules are pairwise distinct, then
\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}(L(c,h_{i}))\cong n_{i}W^{i}
as
V
-modules for all
i
. In this case, the irreducible character
\chi^{V}_{W^{i}}\in R^{\mathrm{fin}}(V)
(see (
5.3
)) can be written as

\chi^{V}_{W^{i}}=\frac{1}{n_{i}}\mathrm{Ind}^{V}_{U}(\chi^{U}_{L(c,h_{i})}),\quad 0\leq i\leq r.

This leads to the following analogue of Artin’s induction theorem, see Proposition
3.7
for an example:

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
V
be a strongly rational VOA. Assume that the conformal weights
h_{0},\dots,h_{r}\in\mathbb{C}
of irreducible
V
-modules are pairwise distinct. Then every element in
R^{\mathrm{fin}}(V)
can be written as a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of induced characters from subVOAs of
V
.

Remark 5.5
.

Other than the Virasoro subVOA generated by
\omega
, a strongly rational VOA
V
generally does not possess many canonical subVOAs. Therefore, it is in general difficult to establish a stronger form of Brauer’s induction theorem for finite characters.

6.
Motivation example: the rank-two parabolic-type VOA
V_{P}\hookrightarrow V_{A_{2}}

In the remaining sections, we study the example that motivates our construction of the finite module induction functor (
2.25
), namely, the rank-two parabolic-type subVOA
V_{P}

[
Liu25
]
of the lattice (or affine) VOA
V_{A_{2}}=L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{3}}(1,0)
.

Consider the type-
A_{2}
root lattice
A_{2}=\mathbb{Z}\alpha\oplus\mathbb{Z}\beta
(
4.1
), with the standard
2
-cocycle
\epsilon:A_{2}\times A_{2}\rightarrow\langle\pm 1\rangle
such that

\epsilon(\alpha,\alpha)=1,\quad\epsilon(\beta,\beta)=1,\quad\epsilon(\alpha,\beta)=1,\quad\epsilon(\beta,\alpha)=-1.

(6.1)

We refer to the additive submonoid

P=\mathbb{Z}\alpha\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\beta\subseteq A_{2}

as a parabolic-type submonoid, see
[
Liu25
, Definition 2.1]
. The submonoid
P\subseteq A_{2}
gives rise to an associated subVOA of
V_{A_{2}}
:

V_{P}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in P=\mathbb{Z}\alpha\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\beta}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z},\,n\in\mathbb{N}}M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,m\alpha+n\beta),

(6.2)

which we call a
rank-two parabolic-type (sub)VOA
of
V_{A_{2}}
. Note that the VOA embedding
V_{P}\hookrightarrow V_{A_{2}}
is conformal.

Remark 6.1
.

We call
V_{P}
a parabolic-type VOA because it shares many properties with a parabolic subalgebra of a semisimple Lie algebra, see
[
Liu25
, Section 4]
. In fact, the degree-one Lie subalgebra

\mathfrak{p}=(V_{P})_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{x_{\pm\alpha},\,x_{\beta},\,x_{\alpha+\beta},\,x,\,y\}

is the standard parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}=(V_{A_{2}})_{1}
, consisting of block upper-triangular matrices with respect to the standard basis (
4.4
):

\begin{bmatrix}\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&\ast&\ast\\
0&\ast&\ast\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore, we believe that the induction for the VOA embedding
V_{P}\hookrightarrow V_{A_{2}}
is well motivated.

We will determine the Zhu algebra
A(V_{P})

in terms of generators and relations.
Then, we will classify all the irreducible
V_{P}
-modules and determine all the finite inductions from the irreducible
V_{P}
-modules with respect to the VOA embedding
V_{P}\hookrightarrow V_{A_{2}}
.

6.1.
A spanning set of
O(V_{P})

We first give a concrete description of
O(V_{P})
in (
2.8
), and then use it to determine the structure of
A(V_{P})
.
For
A_{1}=\mathbb{Z}\alpha
, recall that
O(V_{A_{1}})
is spanned by the following elements, see
[
Liu25
, Corollary 6.14]
:

\begin{cases}&\alpha(-n-2)u+\alpha(-n-1)u,\qquad u\in V_{A_{1}},\ n\geq 0,\\[4.0pt]
&\pm\alpha(-1)v+v,\qquad v\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\pm\alpha),\\[4.0pt]
&M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\pm k\alpha),\qquad k\geq 2,\\[4.0pt]
&\alpha(-1)^{3}w-\alpha(-1)w,\qquad w\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0).\end{cases}

(6.3)

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose
\gamma,\theta\in P
such that
\frac{(\gamma|\gamma)}{2}=N\geq 1
and
(\gamma|\theta)=n\geq 1
. Then
e^{\gamma+\theta}\in O(V_{P})
.

Proof.

By the definition of the lattice vertex operator
Y(e^{\gamma},z)e^{\theta}
, we have

e^{\gamma}_{-n-1}e^{\theta}=\mathrm{Res}_{z}\,z^{-n-1}E^{-}(-\gamma,z)z^{(\gamma|\theta)}\varepsilon(\gamma,\theta)e^{\gamma+\theta}=\varepsilon(\gamma,\theta)e^{\gamma+\theta},

and
e^{\gamma}_{-m}e^{\theta}=0
for all
m\leq n
. Since
n\geq 1
and
\mathrm{wt}\,e^{\gamma}=\frac{(\gamma|\gamma)}{2}=N\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{z}\,Y(e^{\gamma},z)e^{\theta}\frac{(1+z)^{N}}{z^{1+n}}

\displaystyle=e^{\gamma}_{-n-1}e^{\theta}+\binom{N}{1}e^{\gamma}_{-n}e^{\theta}+\dots+\binom{N}{N}e^{\gamma}_{-n-1+N}e^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(\gamma,\theta)e^{\gamma+\theta}\in O(V_{P}).

This shows
e^{\gamma+\theta}\in O(V_{P})
since
\varepsilon(\gamma,\theta)\in\{\pm 1\}
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let

S:=\{e^{m\alpha+n\beta}:m\in\mathbb{Z},\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}\setminus\{e^{\pm\alpha},e^{\beta},e^{\alpha+\beta}\}.

Then
S\subset O(V_{P})
.

Proof.

For
m\geq 1
, since
(\alpha|m\alpha+\beta)=2m-1\geq 1
, by Lemma
6.2
and induction on
m
, we have
e^{(m+1)\alpha+\beta}\in O(V_{P})
for all
m\geq 1
. Similarly, since
(\beta|\alpha+n\beta)=2n-1\geq 1
for
n\geq 1
, we obtain

e^{\alpha+(n+1)\beta}\in O(V_{P}),\qquad n\geq 1.

(6.4)

Now let
n\geq 2
and assume that
e^{m\alpha+n\beta}\in O(V_{P})
for all
m\geq 1
. We want to show that
e^{m\alpha+(n+1)\beta}\in O(V_{P})
for all
m\geq 1
.

Indeed, since
(m\alpha+n\beta|\alpha+\beta)=m((\alpha|\alpha)+(\alpha|\beta))+n((\beta|\alpha)+(\beta|\beta))=m+n\geq 1
, by Lemma
6.2
we have
e^{(m+1)\alpha+(n+1)\beta}\in O(V_{P})
for all
m\geq 1
. Thus
e^{m\alpha+(n+1)\beta}\in O(V_{P})
for all
m\geq 2
, and by (
6.4
), the same holds for
m=1
. This completes the induction step and shows that
e^{m\alpha+n\beta}\in O(V_{P})
for all
m\geq 1
,
n\geq 2
. Hence,

S_{1}:=\{e^{m\alpha+n\beta}:m\geq 1,n\geq 2\}\cup\{e^{m\alpha+\beta}:m\geq 2\}\subset O(V_{P}).

(6.5)

On the other hand, for any
m\geq 1
, since
(-m\alpha|\beta)=m\geq 1
, we have
e^{-m\alpha+\beta}\in O(V_{P})
for all
m\geq 1
. Similarly, since
(-\alpha+n\beta|\beta)=1+2n\geq 1
for all
n\geq 0
, we have

e^{-\alpha+(n+1)\beta}\in O(V_{P}),\qquad n\geq 0.

(6.6)

Using the fact that
(-m\alpha+n\beta|-\alpha+\beta)=3m+3n\geq 1
for
m,n\geq 1
, together with (
6.6
), we can similarly show that

S_{2}:=\{e^{-m\alpha+n\beta}:m\geq 1,n\geq 1\}\subset O(V_{P}).

(6.7)

Finally, for any
m,n\geq 1
, since
(\alpha|m\alpha)=(-\alpha|-m\alpha)=2m>1
and
(\beta|n\beta)=2n>1
, by Lemma
6.2
again we see that
e^{\pm m\alpha}\in O(V_{P})
and
e^{n\beta}\in O(V_{P})
for all
m\geq 2
,
n\geq 2
. Combining (
6.5
) and (
6.7
), we conclude that

S=S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup\{e^{\pm m\alpha}:m\geq 2\}\cup\{e^{n\beta}:n\geq 2\}\subset O(V_{P}).

∎

Proof of the main theorem

In this subsection, we show that

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
O
be the subspace given in Definition

. Then
O\subseteq O(V_{P})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
h(-n-2)u+h(-n-1)u\in O(V_{P})
for any
h\in\mathfrak{h}
,
u\in V_{P}
, and
n\geq 0
.
By the congruence

h(-m)v\equiv(-1)^{m-1}v\ast(h(-1)\mathbf{1})\pmod{O(V_{P})}

and the fact that
O(V_{P})
is a two-sided ideal with respect to

, we obtain

h(-m)O(V_{P})\subseteq O(V_{P})
for any
h\in\mathfrak{h}
and
m\geq 1
.
Then, by Lemma
6.3
, we have

M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,m\alpha+n\beta)\subseteq O(V_{P})\quad\text{for }m\alpha+n\beta\in P\setminus\{\alpha,-\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\}.

Moreover, for
\gamma\in\{\alpha,-\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\}
, since
(\gamma|\gamma)=2
, we obtain

\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma}+e^{\gamma}=e^{\gamma}_{-2}\mathbf{1}+e^{\gamma}_{-1}\mathbf{1}=e^{\gamma}\circ\mathbf{1}\in O(V_{P}),

and hence
\gamma(-1)v+v\in O(V_{P})
for any
v\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)
and
\gamma\in\{\alpha,-\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\}
.

Now suppose
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\{\alpha,-\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\}
are such that
\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}\in\{\beta,\alpha+\beta\}
.
Since
h(-n-2)u+h(-n-1)u\equiv 0\pmod{O(V_{P})}
for all
h\in\mathfrak{h}
,
n\geq 0
, and
u\in V_{P}
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\equiv e^{\gamma}\circ e^{\gamma^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma(-2)e^{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2!}\epsilon(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma(-1)^{2}e^{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}+\epsilon(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\epsilon(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\big(\gamma(-1)^{2}e^{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}+\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}\big)\pmod{O(V_{P})}.

Thus,
\gamma(-1)^{2}v+\gamma(-1)v\in O(V_{P})
for any
v\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})
.

Finally, since
O(V_{\mathbb{Z}\alpha})\subset O(V_{P})
, it follows from (
6.3
) that

\alpha(-1)^{3}\mathbf{1}-\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1}\in O(V_{P}).

Consequently,
\alpha(-1)^{3}w-\alpha(-1)w\in O(V_{P})
for any
w\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)
, as
h(-m)O(V_{P})\subseteq O(V_{P})
for all
h\in\mathfrak{h}
and
m\geq 1
.
∎

Conversely, to prove

, we need to show that

M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\eta)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\theta)\subset O,\qquad\text{for all }\eta,\theta\in P=\mathbb{Z}\alpha\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\beta.

(6.12)

By the construction of
O
in (

) and the fact that

Y(e^{\eta},z)e^{\theta}\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\eta+\theta)((z))
, we have

M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\eta)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\theta)\subset M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\eta+\theta)\subset O,\quad\text{if }\ \eta+\theta\in P\setminus\{0,\alpha,-\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\}.

Hence, it remains to show that

\displaystyle M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)\subset O,

\displaystyle M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\gamma)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,0)\subset O;

(6.13)

\displaystyle M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\beta)\subset O,

\displaystyle M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\beta)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)\subset O;

(6.14)

\displaystyle M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha+\beta)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,-\alpha)\subset O,

\displaystyle M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,-\alpha)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha+\beta)\subset O;

(6.15)

\displaystyle M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,-\alpha)\subset O,

\displaystyle M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,-\alpha)\circ M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,\alpha)\subset O,

(6.16)

where
\gamma\in\{\alpha,-\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\}
.

The inclusion (

) can be proved by arguments similar to those used in

, so we omit the details.

6.2.1.

Given a spanning element

of

For

, this congruence holds because of the following (stronger) statement.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
be an ordered pair in the set (
6.9
), and let
n\geq 0
. Then

\mathrm{Res}_{z}Y(e^{\gamma},z)\big(h^{1}(-n_{1})\cdots h^{r}(-n_{r})e^{\gamma^{\prime}}\big)\frac{(1+z)}{z^{2+n}}\in O,

(6.17)

where
r\geq 0
,
h^{i}\in\mathfrak{h}
for all
i
, and
n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq 1
.

Proof.

It is clear from (
6.8
) that
h(-m)O\subset O
for any
m\geq 1
and
h\in\mathfrak{h}
.
We first claim that
L(-1)u+L(0)u\in O
for all
u\in V_{P}
.
Indeed, if
u\in M_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(1,m\alpha+n\beta)
with

then

by (

Now let
u=h^{1}(-n_{1})\cdots h^{r}(-n_{r})e^{\gamma}
, where
\gamma\in\{\alpha,-\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\}
,

h^{i}\in\mathfrak{h}
for all
i
, and

.
Since
L(-1)e^{\gamma}=\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma}
, we obtain

\displaystyle L(-1)u+L(0)u

\displaystyle=h^{1}(-n_{1})\cdots h^{r}(-n_{r})(\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma}+e^{\gamma})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{r}(h^{j}(-n_{j}-1)+h^{j}(-n_{j}))\,h^{1}(-n_{1})\cdots\widehat{h^{j}(-n_{j})}\cdots h^{r}(-n_{r})e^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{O},

by (

for all

.

Now take
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\in\{(\alpha,\beta),(\beta,\alpha),(-\alpha,\alpha+\beta),(\alpha+\beta,-\alpha)\}
.
Note that
\epsilon(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=-1
.
We first use induction on

to prove that

\mathrm{Res}_{z}Y(e^{\gamma},z)e^{\gamma^{\prime}}\frac{(1+z)}{z^{2+n}}\in O,

which will serve as the base case for induction on

in (

).

\mathrm{Res}_{z}Y(e^{\gamma},z)e^{\gamma^{\prime}}\frac{(1+z)}{z^{2}}=e^{\gamma}_{-2}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}+e^{\gamma}_{-1}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}=-\frac{1}{2}\gamma(-1)^{2}e^{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}\equiv 0\pmod{O}.

\displaystyle(n+1)(n+2)\big(e^{\gamma}_{-n-3}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}+e^{\gamma}_{-n-2}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}\big)=(n+1)(L(-1)e^{\gamma})_{-n-2}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}+(n+2)(L(-1)e^{\gamma})_{-n-1}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=(n+1)\!\sum_{j\geq 0}\!\gamma(-1-j)e^{\gamma}_{-n-2+j}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}+(n+1)\!\sum_{j\geq 0}\!e^{\gamma}_{-n-3-j}\gamma(j)e^{\gamma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=(n+1)\sum_{j\geq 0}\gamma(-1-j)e^{\gamma}_{-n-2+j}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}+(n+1)(\gamma|\gamma^{\prime})e^{\gamma}_{-n-3}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\quad+(n+2)\sum_{j\geq 0}\gamma(-1-j)e^{\gamma}_{-n-1+j}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}+(n+2)(\gamma|\gamma^{\prime})e^{\gamma}_{-n-2}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}.

\displaystyle(n+1)(n+3)\left(e^{\gamma}_{-n-3}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}+e^{\gamma}_{-n-2}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma}_{-n-1+j}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}\equiv(-1)e^{\gamma}_{-n-1+j-1}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}\equiv\dots\equiv(-1)^{j}\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma}_{-n-1}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}\pmod{O},

, and
e^{\gamma}_{m}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}=0

.

\displaystyle(n+1)\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma}_{-n-2}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}+\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{j}\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma}_{-n-1+j}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}-e^{\gamma}_{-n-2}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\equiv(n+1)\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma}_{-n-2}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}+(n+1)\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma}_{-n-1}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}+(-1)^{n+1}\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma}_{0}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}-e^{\gamma}_{-n-2}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\equiv 0+(-1)^{n+1}\gamma(-1)\epsilon(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})e^{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}-(-1)^{n+1}\epsilon(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\gamma(-1)e^{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{O}.

Hence
(n+1)(n+3)\big(e^{\gamma}_{-n-3}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}+e^{\gamma}_{-n-2}e^{\gamma^{\prime}}\big)\in O
,
completing the proof of (
6.18
).

Finally, we use induction on the length

to prove (
6.17
).
The base case
r=0
follows from (
6.18
).
Assume (
6.17
) holds for smaller

Theorem 6.11
.

There exists an isomorphism of unital associative algebras

\displaystyle F:\ A_{P}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\langle x,y,x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha},x_{\beta},x_{\alpha+\beta}\rangle/R\;\longrightarrow\;A(V_{P}),

(6.28)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\longmapsto[\alpha(-1)\mathbf{1}],\quad y\longmapsto[\beta(-1)\mathbf{1}],

\displaystyle x_{\pm\alpha}

Proof.

First, we show that

\displaystyle\longrightarrow A_{P}=\mathbb{C}\langle x,y,x_{\alpha},x_{-\alpha},x_{\beta},x_{\alpha+\beta}\rangle/R,

(6.30)

\displaystyle h^{1}(-n_{1}-1)\cdots h^{r}(-n_{r}-1)e^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\longmapsto(-1)^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}}x_{\gamma}\cdot\overline{h^{1}}\cdot\overline{h^{2}}\cdots\overline{h^{r}},\quad\gamma\in\{\alpha,-\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\},

\displaystyle U^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,\lambda)}:=\mathbb{C}e^{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}e^{-},\quad\text{where }e^{+}=e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha}\otimes e^{\lambda},\ \ e^{-}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\alpha}\otimes e^{\lambda}.

\displaystyle J.e=J.e^{+}=J.e^{-}=0,

(7.23)

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}.e=x_{-\alpha}.e=x.e=0,\quad y.e=(\lambda|\beta)e,

(7.24)

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}.e^{+}=0,\quad x_{\alpha}.e^{-}=e^{+},\quad x_{-\alpha}.e^{+}=e^{-},\quad x_{-\alpha}.e^{-}=0,

\displaystyle x.e^{\pm}=\pm e^{\pm},\quad y.e^{\pm}=\big((\lambda|\beta)\mp\tfrac{1}{2}\big)e^{\pm}.

(7.25)

Theorem 7.6
.

Let

be an irreducible

-module. Then

is isomorphic to either

or

for some

Proof.

Since

is a nilpotent ideal, we must have

.
Hence

is an irreducible module over the quotient algebra

A^{P}=A(V_{P})/J=\left(\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{C}(x_{-\alpha}y^{n})\right)\oplus\mathbb{C}[x,y]/\langle x^{3}-x\rangle\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{C}(x_{\alpha}y^{n})\right).

,

as a vector space, and

is a subalgebra of

. Thus

is also an

-module.
Recall that

is semisimple with two irreducible modules (up to isomorphism):

and

Hence, as an

where

and

and

Case I.

.
Then a nonzero copy of

is contained in

.
Let

.
Since

(7.27)

it follows from (

) that

is an
A^{P}
-submodule.
As

is irreducible, we must have

.
Thus
U
is an irreducible

-module, and by Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz,

U\cong\mathbb{C}[y]/\langle y-\lambda_{0}\rangle
for some
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Choose
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}
with
(\lambda|\alpha)=0
and
(\lambda|\beta)=\lambda_{0}
; then, by (
7.24
),

.

Case II.

K\neq\emptyset
.
Then a nonzero copy of
W^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha}
is contained in
U
.
By (
7.27
), the subspace

-submodule, hence

for some

.

For simplicity, write

and

.
As in (
7.25
), we have

x_{\alpha}.e^{+}=0,\quad x_{\alpha}.e^{-}=e^{+},\quad x_{-\alpha}.e^{+}=e^{-},\quad x_{-\alpha}.e^{-}=0,\quad x.e^{\pm}=\pm e^{\pm}.

Furthermore, from (
7.27
) we obtain

x_{\alpha}y^{n}=(y+1)^{n}x_{\alpha}
and
x_{-\alpha}y^{n}=(y-1)^{n}x_{-\alpha}
for all

.
Thus for any

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}\big((y-1)f(y)e^{-}\big)=yf(y+1)e^{+},\quad x_{\alpha}\big(yg(y)e^{+}\big)=(y+1)g(y+1)e^{+}=0,

(7.28)

\displaystyle x_{-\alpha}\big(yg(y)e^{+}\big)=(y-1)g(y-1)e^{-},\quad x_{-\alpha}\big((y-1)f(y)e^{-}\big)=(y-2)f(y-1)x_{-\alpha}.e^{-}=0.

(7.29)

Introduce the following subspace in

:

N:=y\mathbb{C}[y]\cdot e^{+}+(y-1)\mathbb{C}[y]\cdot e^{-}\subset U.

By (
7.28
), (
7.29
), and the relations
xy=yx
,
x.e^{\pm}=\pm e^{\pm}
,

is an
A^{P}
-submodule of
U
.
Since

is irreducible,

or

.

If

, then
y.e^{+}=y.e^{-}=0
, and clearly

.
If

, then there exist

such that

(7.30)

Applying

to (

) and using (
7.28
), we obtain

0=yg(y+1)e^{+}
.
Applying
x_{-\alpha}
and using (
7.29
), we get

.
Hence

, i.e.

0=(yf(y)-1)e^{+}=(y-\lambda_{k})\cdots(y-\lambda_{1})e^{+},\quad\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{C},

where

are nonzero.
Let

be minimal such that

(y-\lambda_{j-1})\cdots(y-\lambda_{1})e^{+}\neq 0,\quad(y-\lambda_{j})\big((y-\lambda_{j-1})\cdots(y-\lambda_{1})e^{+}\big)=0.

Set
\tilde{e}^{+}=(y-\lambda_{j-1})\cdots(y-\lambda_{1})e^{+}

Then

,

, and

Let
\tilde{e}^{-}:=x_{-\alpha}.\tilde{e}^{+}=(y-1-\lambda_{j-1})\cdots(y-1-\lambda_{1})e^{-}

and

are eigenvectors of

with

and

-modules.
By (

) and Corollary

, they are also isomorphic as

-modules.
∎

A(V_{A_{2}})
act trivially on
1\otimes\mathbf{1}\in A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,0)}
.

We now claim that the
A(V_{A_{2}})
-module
A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,0)}
is nonzero.
Indeed, by the Hom-tensor duality we have

\big(A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,0)}\big)^{\!*}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{A_{V_{P}}}\!\big(\mathbb{C}\mathbf{1},(A(V_{A_{2}}))^{\!*}\big).

Note that
A(V_{A_{2}})=\mathbb{C}1\oplus A(V_{A_{2}})_{+}
as vector spaces,
where
A(V_{A_{2}})_{+}
consists of elements in

A(V_{A_{2}})\cong U(\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{C}))/\langle x_{\alpha+\beta}^{2}\rangle
of length greater than one.

Define a linear map

\phi:\mathbb{C}\mathbf{1}\to(A(V_{A_{2}}))^{\!*}
by

\langle\phi(\mathbf{1}),1\rangle:=1,\qquad\langle\phi(\mathbf{1}),a\rangle:=0,\quad\forall\,a\in A(V_{A_{2}})_{+},

(8.7)

where

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:(A(V_{A_{2}}))^{\!*}\times A(V_{A_{2}})\to\mathbb{C}

is the natural pairing.

We show that
\phi
is an
A_{V_{P}}
-homomorphism.
Let

S(A_{V_{P}}):=\{x_{\pm\alpha},x_{\beta},x_{\alpha+\beta},x,y\}

be the set of generators of
A_{V_{P}}
.
Consider the left ideal
A(V_{A_{2}})\cdot S(A_{V_{P}})\subset A(V_{A_{2}})
.
Using the relations (
6.22
)–(
6.27
), together with
(
8.2
) and (
4.8
)–(
4.11
),
it is easy to verify that
1\notin A(V_{A_{2}})\cdot S(A_{V_{P}})
.
Hence, for any
z\in S(A_{V_{P}})
and
a\in A(V_{A_{2}})
,

\langle\phi(z.\mathbf{1}),a\rangle=0=\langle\phi(\mathbf{1}),a\cdot z\rangle=\langle z.\phi(\mathbf{1}),a\rangle,

(8.8)

in view of (
8.7
).
When
z=1
, the same holds since
a\cdot 1=a
for all
a\in A(V_{A_{2}})
.
Thus,
\phi
defined in (
8.7
) is a nonzero element of

\mathrm{Hom}_{A_{V_{P}}}\!\big(\mathbb{C}\mathbf{1},(A(V_{A_{2}}))^{\!*}\big)
.
Therefore,

A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,0)}\neq 0.

In particular,

A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,0)}\cong L(0)
and

\mathrm{Ind}^{V_{A_{2}}}_{V_{P}}L^{(0,0)}\cong V_{A_{2}}.

Case II.

(\epsilon,\lambda)=(0,\lambda_{2})
.
By (
7.23
) and (
7.24
) we have
U^{(0,\lambda_{2})}=\mathbb{C}e
, with

x.e=x_{\beta}.e=x_{\alpha+\beta}.e=x_{\pm\alpha}.e=0
and
y.e=e
.
Again, we first show that
A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,\lambda_{2})}
is nonzero.
Similar to Case I, using the Hom–tensor duality

\left(A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,\lambda_{2})}\right)^{\ast}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{A_{V_{P}}}(\mathbb{C}e,(A(V_{A_{2}}))^{\ast}),

we just need to find a nonzero element

\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{A_{V_{P}}}(\mathbb{C}e,(A(V_{A_{2}}))^{\ast})
.
Using the relations (
6.22
)–(
6.27
), (
8.2
), and (
4.8
)–(
4.11
),
we can write

A(V_{A_{2}})=\mathrm{span}\{1,y,y^{2}\}\oplus C,

where

C=\mathrm{span}\{x,x^{2},xy,x^{2}y,x_{\pm\alpha},x_{\pm\beta},x_{\pm(\alpha+\beta)},x_{\pm\alpha}y,x_{\pm\beta}x,x_{\pm(\alpha+\beta)}y\}.

Then we define
\varphi:\mathbb{C}e\to(A(V_{A_{2}}))^{\ast}
by letting

\langle\varphi(e),1\rangle=\langle\varphi(e),y\rangle=\langle\varphi(e),y^{2}\rangle:=1,\quad\langle\varphi(e),a\rangle:=0,\ \forall a\in C.

(8.9)

Let
S:=\{x_{\pm\alpha},x_{\beta},x_{\alpha+\beta},x\}
.
Since
y.e=e
, to show
\varphi
is an
A_{V_{P}}
-homomorphism, we need to verify that

\langle\varphi(e),a\rangle=\langle\varphi(e),a\cdot y\rangle,\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\varphi(e),a\cdot z\rangle=0,\quad\forall a\in A(V_{A_{2}}),\ z\in S.

(8.10)

By (
4.8
)–(
4.11
), the only terms in the left ideal

A(V_{A_{2}})\cdot S
that have nonzero components in the subspace

\mathrm{span}\{1,y,y^{2}\}
are spanned by

\frac{1}{2}y^{2}-\frac{1}{2}y
and

\frac{1}{2}(x+y)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(x+y)
.
By (
8.9
), we have

\langle\varphi(e),y^{2}-y\rangle=0
and

\langle\varphi(e),(x+y)^{2}-(x+y)\rangle=\langle\varphi(e),y^{2}-y\rangle=0
.
Thus
\langle\varphi(e),A(V_{A_{2}})\cdot S\rangle=0
.
Moreover, it is easy to show that
C\cdot y\subseteq C
,
hence
\langle\varphi(e),a\rangle=0=\langle\varphi(e),a\cdot y\rangle

for any
a\in C
, in view of (
8.9
).
Finally, for
a=\lambda+\mu y+\gamma y^{2}\in\mathrm{span}\{1,y,y^{2}\}
,
we have

\langle\varphi(e),a\rangle=\lambda+\mu+\gamma=\langle\varphi(e),\lambda y+\mu y^{2}+\gamma\rangle=\langle\varphi(e),a\cdot y\rangle,

in view of (
8.10
).
This shows that
\varphi
is a nonzero element in

\mathrm{Hom}_{A_{V_{P}}}(\mathbb{C}e,(A(V_{A_{2}}))^{\ast})\cong\left(A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,\lambda_{2})}\right)^{\ast}.

Now
1\otimes e\in A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,\lambda_{2})}

is a highest-weight vector for
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
of highest weight
\lambda_{2}
,
since

x.(1\otimes e)=1\otimes x.e=0=(\alpha|\lambda_{2})\cdot(1\otimes e)

and

y.(1\otimes e)=1\otimes y.e=(\beta|\lambda_{2})\cdot(1\otimes e)
.
Then

A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,\lambda_{2})}=U(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})/\langle x^{2}_{\alpha+\beta}\rangle\cdot(1\otimes e)

is a finite-dimensional highest-weight
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-module
of highest weight
\lambda_{2}
.
Hence it is isomorphic to
L(\lambda_{2})

[
V68
,
Hum72
]
.
This shows that

\mathrm{Ind}^{V_{A_{2}}}_{V_{P}}L^{(0,\lambda_{2})}=\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{A_{2}}}(L(\lambda_{2}))\cong V_{A_{2}+\lambda_{2}}.

Case III.

(\epsilon,\lambda)=(0,-\lambda_{2})
.
We claim that
A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,-\lambda_{2})}=0
.
Indeed, since
U^{(0,-\lambda_{2})}=\mathbb{C}e
, with

x.e=x_{\beta}.e=x_{\alpha+\beta}.e=x_{\pm\alpha}.e=0

and
y.e=-e
, we have

A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,-\lambda_{2})}=\mathrm{span}\{1\otimes e,\,x_{-\beta}\otimes e,\,x_{-\alpha-\beta}\otimes e\}.

By (
4.8
)–(
4.11
), we have
x_{-\beta}x_{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}y^{2}-\frac{1}{2}y
.
Note that

\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}y^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}y\Bigr).e=\tfrac{1}{2}(-1)^{2}e+\tfrac{1}{2}e=e,

and so

1\otimes e=1\otimes\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}y^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}y\Bigr).e=\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}y^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}y\Bigr).(1\otimes e)=x_{-\beta}\otimes x_{\beta}.e=x_{-\beta}\otimes 0=0.

Moreover,
x_{-\beta}\otimes e=x_{-\beta}.(1\otimes e)=0
and

x_{-\alpha-\beta}\otimes e=x_{-\alpha-\beta}.(1\otimes e)=0
.
Thus
A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(0,-\lambda_{2})}=0
, and hence

\mathrm{Ind}^{V_{A_{2}}}_{V_{P}}L^{(0,-\lambda_{2})}\cong 0.

Case IV.

(\epsilon,\lambda)=\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha,\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}\right)
.
Similar to Cases I and II, we first show that the

A(V_{A_{2}})
-module
A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2})}

is nonzero. By (
7.23
) and (
7.25
), we have

U^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2})}=\mathbb{C}e^{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}e^{-}
,
on which the generators of
A_{V_{P}}
act as

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}.e^{+}=0,

\displaystyle x_{-\alpha}.e^{+}=e^{-},

\displaystyle x.e^{\pm}=\pm e^{\pm},

\displaystyle x_{\beta}.e^{\pm}=x_{\alpha+\beta}.e^{\pm}=0,

(8.11)

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}.e^{-}=e^{+},

\displaystyle x_{-\alpha}.e^{-}=0,

\displaystyle y.e^{+}=0,

\displaystyle y.e^{-}=e^{-}.

Write
A(V_{A_{2}})=\mathrm{span}\{1,x,x^{2}\}\oplus D
, where

D=\mathrm{span}\{y,y^{2},xy,x^{2}y,x_{\pm\alpha},x_{\pm\beta},x_{\pm(\alpha+\beta)},x_{\pm\alpha}y,x_{\pm\beta}x,x_{\pm(\alpha+\beta)}y\}.

Define a linear map

\psi:\mathbb{C}e^{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}e^{-}\to(A(V_{A_{2}}))^{\ast}
by letting

\displaystyle\langle\psi(e^{+}),1\rangle=\langle\psi(e^{+}),x\rangle=\langle\psi(e^{+}),x^{2}\rangle:=1,\quad\langle\psi(e^{+}),D\rangle:=0,

(8.12)

\displaystyle\langle\psi(e^{-}),a\rangle:=\langle\psi(e^{+}),a\cdot x_{-\alpha}\rangle,\quad\forall\,a\in A(V_{A_{2}}).

We want to show that

\psi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{A_{V_{P}}}(U^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2})},(A(V_{A_{2}}))^{\ast})
,
i.e.,

\langle\psi(z.e^{\pm}),a\rangle=\langle\psi(e^{\pm}),a\cdot z\rangle,\quad\forall a\in A(V_{A_{2}}),\ z\in\{x_{\pm\alpha},x_{\beta},x_{\alpha+\beta},x,y\}.

(8.13)

Indeed, by (
8.11
),

S=\{x_{\alpha},x_{\beta},x_{\alpha+\beta},y,(x-1)\}
annihilates
e^{+}
.
It is easy to show that the left ideal
A(V_{A_{2}})\cdot S

is contained in
D+\mathrm{span}\{(x-1),(x^{2}-x)\}

by relations (
6.22
)–(
6.27
).
Since
\langle\psi(e^{+}),D\rangle=\langle\psi(e^{+}),x-1\rangle=\langle\psi(e^{+}),x^{2}-x\rangle=0
by (
8.12
),
we have

\langle\psi(u.e^{+}),A(V_{A_{2}})\rangle=0=\langle\psi(e^{+}),A(V_{A_{2}})\cdot u\rangle,\quad\forall u\in S.

Thus (
8.13
) holds for all
z\in\{x_{\alpha},x_{\beta},x_{\alpha+\beta},y,x\}
.
For
z=x_{-\alpha}
, by (
8.12
) and
x_{-\alpha}.e^{+}=e^{-}
,
we have

\langle\psi(x_{-\alpha}.e^{+}),a\rangle=\langle\psi(e^{-}),a\rangle=\langle\psi(e^{+}),a\cdot x_{-\alpha}\rangle.

Hence (
8.13
) holds for all
z\in A_{V_{P}}
acting on
e^{+}
.

On the other hand,

T=\{x_{-\alpha},x_{\beta},x_{\alpha+\beta},(x+1),(y-1)\}
annihilates
e^{-}

by (
8.11
).
Noting
x_{-\alpha}x_{-\alpha}=(x+1)x_{-\alpha}=x_{\beta}x_{-\alpha}=0
,
we get from (
8.12
)

\displaystyle\langle\psi(x_{-\alpha}.e^{-}),a\rangle

\displaystyle=0=\langle\psi(e^{-}),a\cdot x_{-\alpha}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\psi((x+1).e^{-}),a\rangle

\displaystyle=0=\langle\psi(e^{-}),a\cdot(x+1)\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\psi(x_{\beta}.e^{-}),a\rangle

\displaystyle=0=\langle\psi(e^{-}),a\cdot x_{\beta}\rangle.

Moreover, by (
4.8
)–(
4.11
),

(y-1)x_{-\alpha}=x_{-\alpha}y
and

x_{\alpha+\beta}x_{-\alpha}=-x_{\beta}x
.
Since
\langle\psi(e^{+}),A(V_{A_{2}})\cdot y\rangle=0
,

\displaystyle\langle\psi((y-1).e^{-}),a\rangle=0=\langle\psi(e^{-}),a\cdot(y-1)\rangle.

Also, since
\psi
is invariant for
e^{+}
, we have

\langle\psi(e^{+}),A(V_{A_{2}})\cdot x_{\beta}x\rangle=0
,
and hence

\langle\psi(x_{\alpha+\beta}.e^{-}),a\rangle=0=\langle\psi(e^{-}),a\cdot x_{\alpha+\beta}\rangle.

Finally, for
z=x_{\alpha}
, since

\langle\psi(e^{+}),a\cdot(x^{2}-1)\rangle=\langle\psi(e^{+}),a\cdot(x-1)\rangle=0
,
we have

\langle\psi(e^{+}),a\rangle=\langle\psi(e^{-}),a\cdot x_{\alpha}\rangle,

while
\langle\psi(e^{+}),a\rangle=\langle\psi(x_{\alpha}.e^{-}),a\rangle

since
x_{\alpha}.e^{-}=e^{+}
.
This completes the proof of (
8.13
).
Hence
\psi
is a nonzero element of

\mathrm{Hom}_{A_{V_{P}}}\bigl(U^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2})},(A(V_{A_{2}}))^{\ast}\bigr)\cong\left(A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2})}\right)^{\ast}.

From (
8.11
),

1\otimes e^{+}
is a highest-weight vector in

A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2})}

of highest weight
\lambda_{1}
,
since
y.e^{+}=0=(\lambda_{1}|\beta)e^{+}
and

x.e^{+}=e^{+}=(\lambda_{1}|\alpha)e^{+}
.
Moreover,
1\otimes e^{-}\in U(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})\cdot(1\otimes e^{+})
,
and hence

A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2})}=U(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})\cdot(1\otimes e^{\pm})\cong L(\lambda_{1}),

as
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
-modules
[
V68
]
.
Therefore,

\mathrm{Ind}^{V_{A_{2}}}_{V_{P}}L^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2})}=\Phi^{\mathsf{L}}_{V_{A_{2}}}(L(\lambda_{1}))\cong V_{A_{2}+\lambda_{1}}.

Case V.

(\epsilon,\lambda)=\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha,-\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}\right)
.
Similar to Case III, we claim that

A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2})}=0
.
Indeed,
U^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2})}=\mathbb{C}e^{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}e^{-}
,
with all relations in (
8.11
) holding except that

y.e^{+}=-e^{+}
and
y.e^{-}=0
.
Then by (
4.8
)–(
4.11
),

1\otimes e^{+}=1\otimes\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}y^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}y\Bigr).e^{+}=\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}y^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}y\Bigr).(1\otimes e^{+})=x_{-\beta}\otimes x_{\beta}.e^{+}=0,

and

1\otimes e^{-}=1\otimes x_{-\alpha}.e^{+}=x_{-\alpha}.(1\otimes e^{+})=0
.
Hence

A(V_{A_{2}})\otimes_{A_{V_{P}}}U^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2})}=U(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})\cdot(1\otimes e^{+})+U(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})\cdot(1\otimes e^{-})=0,

and therefore

\mathrm{Ind}^{V_{A_{2}}}_{V_{P}}L^{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha,-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2})}\cong 0.

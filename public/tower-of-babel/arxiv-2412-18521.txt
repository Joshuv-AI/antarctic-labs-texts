WEYL-HEISENBERG COVARIANT QUANTIZATION FOR THE DISCRETE TORUS
ROMAIN MURENZI, AIDAN ZLOTAK, JEAN-PIERRE GAZEAU

Abstract
Covariant integral quantization is implemented for systems whose phase space is ℤ d × ℤ d \mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\,\mathbb{Z}_{d} , i.e., for systems moving on the discrete periodic set ℤ d = { 0 , 1 , … d − 1 \mathbb{Z}_{d}=\{0,1,\dotsc d-1 mod d } d\} . The symmetry group of this phase space is the periodic discrete version of the Weyl-Heisenberg group, namely the central extension of the abelian group ℤ d × ℤ d \mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\,\mathbb{Z}_{d} . In this regard, the phase space is viewed as the left coset of the group with its center. The non-trivial unitary irreducible representation of this group, as acting on L 2 ​ ( ℤ N ) L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N}) , is square integrable on the phase phase. We derive the corresponding covariant integral quantizations from (weight) functions on the phase space, and display their phase space portrait.

2
(Covariant) integral quantization: a survey

In this section we give a short review of what we understand by covariant integral quantization in view of the application to the set
\mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\,\mathbb{Z}_{d}
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space and
\mathcal{H}
be a (separable) Hilbert space. An operator-valued function

X\ni x\mapsto\mathsf{M}(x)\ \mbox{acting in}\ \mathcal{H}\,,

(2.1)

resolves the identity operator
\mathbbm{1}
in
\mathcal{H}
with respect the measure
\mu
if

\int_{X}\mathsf{M}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\mathbbm{1}

(2.2)

holds in a weak sense.

Integral quantization based on (
2.1
) is the linear map of a function on
X
to an operator in
\mathcal{H}
which is defined by

f(x)\mapsto\int_{X}f(x)\mathsf{M}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x):=A_{f}\,,\quad 1\mapsto\mathbbm{1}\,.

(2.3)

If the operators
\mathsf{M}(x)
in
2.2

are nonnegative and bounded, one says that they form a (normalised) positive operator-valued measure (POVM) on
X
. If they are further
unit trace-class for all
x\in X
, i.e., if the
\mathsf{M}(x)
’s are density operators, then the map

f(x)\mapsto\check{f}(x):=\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf{M}(x)A_{f})=\int_{X}f(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf{M}(x)\mathsf{M}(x^{\prime}))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x^{\prime})

(2.4)

is a local averaging of the original
f(x)
(which can very singular, like a Dirac) with respect to the probability distribution on
X
,

x^{\prime}\mapsto\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf{M}(x)\mathsf{M}(x^{\prime}))\,.

(2.5)

This averaging, or semi-classical portrait of the operator
A_{f}
, is in general a regularisation, depending of course on the topological nature of the measure space
(X,\mu)
and the functional properties of the
\mathsf{M}(x)
’s.

Let us now assume that
X=G
is a Lie group with left Haar measure
{\rm d}\mu(g)
, and let
g\mapsto U_{g}
be a unitary irreducible representation (UIR) of
G
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mathrm{M}
be a bounded self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{H}
and let us define
g
-translations of
M
as

\mathrm{M}(g)=U_{g}\mathrm{M}U_{g}^{\dagger}\,.

(2.6)

Suppose that the operator

R:=\int_{G}\,\mathrm{M}(g)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(g)\,,

(2.7)

is defined in a weak sense. From the left invariance of
\mathrm{d}\mu(g)
the operator
R
commutes with all operators
U(g)
,
g\in G
, and so, from Schur’s Lemma, we have the resolution of the unity up to a constant,

R=c_{\mathrm{M}}\mathbbm{1}\,.

(2.8)

The constant
c_{\mathrm{M}}
can be found from the formula

c_{\mathrm{M}}=\int_{G}\,\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho_{0}\,\mathrm{M}(g)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(g)\,,

(2.9)

where
\rho_{0}
is a given unit trace positive operator.
\rho_{0}
is chosen, if manageable, in order to make the integral convergent. Of course, it is possible that no such finite constant exists for a given
\mathrm{M}
, or worse, it can not exist for any
\mathrm{M}
(which is not the case for square integrable representations).
Now, if
c_{\mathrm{M}}
is finite and positive, the true resolution of the identity follows:

\int_{G}\,\mathrm{M}(g)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(g)=\mathbbm{1}\,,\quad\mathrm{d}\nu(g):=\mathrm{d}\mu(g)/c_{\mathrm{M}}\,.

(2.10)

For instance, in the case of a square-integrable unitary irreducible representation
U:g\mapsto U_{g}
, let us pick a unit vector
|\psi\rangle
for which
c_{\mathrm{M}}=\int_{G}{\rm d}\mu(g)|\langle\psi|U_{g}\psi\rangle|^{2}<\infty
, i.e
|\psi\rangle
is an admissible unit vector for
U
. With
\mathrm{M}=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
the resolution of the identity (
2.10
) provided by the family of states

|\psi_{g}\rangle=U_{g}|\psi\rangle
reads

\int_{G}|\psi_{g}\rangle\langle\psi_{g}|\frac{{\rm d}\mu(g)}{c_{\mathrm{M}}}=\mathbbm{1}\,.

(2.11)

Vectors
|\psi_{g}\rangle
are named (generalized) coherent states (or wavelet) for the group
G
.

The equation (
2.10
) provides an integral quantization of complex-valued functions on the group
G
as follows

f\mapsto A_{f}=\int_{G}\mathrm{M}(g)f(g)\frac{{\rm d}\mu(g)}{c_{\mathrm{M}}}\,.

(2.12)

Furthermore, this quantization is
covariant
in the sense that:

U_{g}A_{f}U_{g}^{\dagger}=A_{F}\quad\text{where}\quad F(g^{\prime})=(U_{g}f)(g^{\prime})=f(g^{-1}g^{\prime})\,,

(2.13)

i.e.
U_{g}:f\mapsto F
is the regular representation if
f\in L^{2}(G,{\rm d}\mu(g))
.

Concerning the group
\mathrm{H(\mathbb{Z}_{d})}
we are considering in the present paper, an adaption of this above material is necessary in the sense that we have to replace with its coset
\Gamma_{d}:=\mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d}
, which amounts to replace its UIR with its projective version.

3
Overview: Weyl (displacement) operator acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{d})

Following Vourdas, Gazeau, and Feichtinger,
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
we consider a system (resp. a signal) where position (resp. time) and momentum (resp. frequency) take values in the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{d}=\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}=\{0,1,...,d-1\}
of the integer modulo
d
.
The Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}\equiv L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{d})
of the quantum states of this system is the vector space of
d
-periodic complex sequences

\phi=(\phi(1),\phi(2),\dotsc,\phi(d))\,,\quad\phi(l)\in\mathbb{C}\,,\quad\phi(l+d)=\phi(l)\,,

(3.1)

equipped with the scalar product

\langle\phi_{1}|\phi_{2}\rangle=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\overline{\phi_{1}(l)}\phi_{2}(l)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\overline{\phi_{1}(l)}\phi_{2}(l)\,.

(3.2)

Note that the Hilbertian Dirac bracket formalism and notations are occasionally used in this paper:

\phi(l)\equiv\langle l|\phi\rangle\,,\quad\langle l|l^{\prime}\rangle=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\,,\quad\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}|l\rangle\langle l|=\mathbbm{1}\,.

(3.3)

An orthonormal basis of Fourier basis of
\mathcal{H}
is provided by the
d
Fourier exponentials
\left(e^{d}_{k}\right)_{0\leq k\leq d-1}
:

e^{d}_{k}(l)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}e^{\mathsf{i}\frac{\,2\pi}{d}\,k\,l}\,,

(3.4)

with

\langle\,e^{d}_{k}|\,e^{d}_{k^{\prime}}\rangle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{2\pi}{d}(k^{\prime}-k)l}=\frac{1}{d}\frac{1-e^{\mathsf{i}\,2\pi(k^{\prime}-k)}}{1-e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{2\pi}{d}(k^{\prime}-k)}}=\delta_{k\,k^{\prime}}\,,

(3.5)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}|e^{d}_{k}\rangle\langle e^{d}_{k}|=\mathbbm{1}\,.

(3.6)

The Fourier transform of
\phi=(\phi(l))_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}
and its inverse are defined as:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}\phi)(k)=\hat{\psi}(k)=\langle\,e^{d}_{k}|\phi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{-\mathsf{i}\,\frac{2\pi}{d}\,k\,l}\phi(l)\,,

(3.7)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\hat{\phi})(k)=\phi(k)=\langle\,\overline{e^{d}_{k}}|\hat{\phi}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{2\pi}{d}\,k\,l}\hat{\phi}(l)\,.

(3.8)

The Fourier transform of the Fourier basis elements is given by:

(\mathcal{F}e^{d}_{k_{0}})(k)=\hat{e}^{d}_{k_{0}}(k)=\langle\,e^{d}_{k}|\,e^{d}_{k_{0}}\rangle=\delta_{k\,k_{0}}\equiv\langle k|k_{0}\rangle\,.

(3.9)

Hence, the dual basis to the Fourier basis is precisely the set of kets
\{\widehat{e^{d}_{k}}\equiv|k\rangle\,,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}\}
, defined as the discrete functions
\hat{e}^{d}_{k}(l)\equiv\langle l|k\rangle=\delta_{kl}
. Reciprocally,

(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\hat{e}^{d}_{k})(l)=e^{d}_{k}(l)\,.

(3.10)

These formulas are crucial for understanding the definition and properties given in Eq. (
3.3
) and the nature of the Cartesian product
\Gamma_{d}=\mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d}
viewed as a phase space.

It is easy to show that the symmetry for this system is the discrete Weyl-Heisenberg group
\mathrm{H}(\mathbb{Z}_{d})
. The latter is viewed as the set of triplets
(s,m,n)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d}
equipped with the internal law:

(s,m,n)\,(s^{\prime},m^{\prime},n^{\prime})=\left(s+s^{\prime}+\frac{m\,n^{\prime}-m^{\prime}n}{2},m+m^{\prime}\,\mathrm{mod}\,d,n+n^{\prime}\,\mathrm{mod}\,d\right)\,.

(3.11)

For the sake of simplicity, we will omit the “mod
d
” notation from this point onward.

The neutral element is
e=(0,0,0)
and the inverse is given by

(s,m,\theta)^{-1}=(-s,-m\,\mathrm{mod}\,d,-n\,\mathrm{mod}\,d)\,.

The Abelian one-parameter subgroup
C=\{(s,0,0)\,,\,s\in\mathbb{R}\}
is the center of
\mathrm{H}(\mathbb{Z}_{d})
.

Besides the trivial one, the group
\mathrm{H}(\mathbb{Z}_{d})
admets the following unitary irreducible representation acting on
\mathcal{H}
as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(V(s,m,n)\psi)(l)=e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{2\pi}{d}s}e^{-\mathsf{i}\,\frac{\pi}{d}m\,n}e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{2\pi}{d}m\,l}\psi(l-n)\,\,\\
&V(s,m,n)V(s^{\prime},m^{\prime},n^{\prime})=V(s+s^{\prime}+\frac{m\,n^{\prime}-m^{\prime}n}{2},m+m^{\prime},n+n^{\prime})\,,\\
&V^{{\dagger}}(s,m,n)=V(-s,-m,-n)=V((s,m,n)^{-1})\,.\end{split}

As for the continuous case, this operator is the combination of two elementary operations, translation

(T_{l_{0}}\psi)(l)=\psi(l-l_{0})\,,

(3.12)

and modulation (or translation in the Fourier space)

(E_{k_{0}}\psi)(l)=e^{\mathsf{i}\frac{2\pi}{d}k_{0}l}\psi(l)\ \Leftrightarrow\ \mathcal{F}(E_{k_{0}}\psi)(k)=\hat{\psi}(k-k_{0})\,.

(3.13)

Note the commutation rule:

T_{l_{0}}E_{k_{0}}=e^{-\mathsf{i}\frac{2\pi}{d}k_{0}l_{0}}\,E_{k_{0}}T_{l_{0}}\,.

(3.14)

Due to the central parameter
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the representation is not square integrable. But, it is square integrable on the phase space
\Gamma_{d}
. In this group context, the latter is viewed as the left coset
\Gamma_{d}=\mathrm{H}(\mathbb{Z}_{d})/C
of
\mathrm{H}(\mathbb{Z}_{d})
with its center.
Hence, the representation induces the unitary (
\sim
Weyl) operator
U
defined on
\Gamma_{N}
by the section
s=0
on the group,
i.e.
,
{U}(m,n):={V}(0,m,n)
:

\begin{split}(U(m,n)\psi)(l)&=e^{-\mathsf{i}\,\frac{\pi}{d}m\,n}e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{2\pi}{d}m\,l}\psi(l-n)\\
&=e^{-\mathsf{i}\,\frac{\pi}{d}m\,n}\left(E_{m}T_{n}\psi\right)(l)=e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{\pi}{d}m\,n}\left(T_{n}E_{m}\psi\right)(l)\,,\\
U^{{\dagger}}(m,n)&=U(-m,-n)\,.\end{split}

(3.15)

Let us now give three useful formulae for the sequel.
Firstly the composition formula, where the symplectic underlying structure of the WH symmetry is put into evidence in the phase factor:

\begin{split}U(m,n)\,U(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})&=e^{\mathsf{i}\frac{\pi}{d}(mn^{\prime}-nm^{\prime})}\,U(m+m^{\prime},n+n^{\prime})\\
\end{split}

(3.16)

{U}(m^{\prime},n^{\prime}){U}(m,n)\,{U}^{{\dagger}}(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})=e^{-\mathsf{i}\frac{2\pi}{d}(mn^{\prime}-m^{\prime}n)}U(m,n)\,.

(3.17)

\mathrm{Tr}[U(m,n)]=d\,\delta_{m\,0}\delta_{n\,0}\,.

(3.18)

Applying
U(m,n)
to the Fourier basis elements and their reciprocal yields:

\displaystyle(U(m,n)e^{d}_{k})(l)

\displaystyle=e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{\pi}{d}m\,n}\,e^{d}_{k+m}(l-n)\,,

(3.19)

\displaystyle(U(m,n)\hat{e}^{d}_{k})(l)

\displaystyle=e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{\pi}{d}m(2k+n)}\delta_{lk+n}=e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{\pi}{d}m(2k+n)}\hat{e}^{d}_{k+n}(l)\,.

(3.20)

One then derives the matrix elements of the displacement operator
U(m,n)
in the respective Fourier and reciprocal bases:

\displaystyle\left(U(m,n)\right)_{kk^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\langle e^{d}_{k}|U(m,n)e^{d}_{k^{\prime}}\rangle=e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{\pi}{d}mn}e^{-\mathsf{i}\,\frac{2\pi}{d}kn}\delta_{k,k^{\prime}+m}\,,

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{U(m,n)}\right)_{kk^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{e}^{d}_{k}|U(m,n)\hat{e}^{d}_{k^{\prime}}\rangle=e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{\pi}{d}m(k+k^{\prime})}\delta_{k,k^{\prime}+n}\,.

(3.22)

For d=2, we get the Pauli matrices, including the unit matrix:

\displaystyle\left(U(0,0)\right)=I_{2},\left(U(0,1)\right)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}=\sigma_{3}\,,

(3.23)

\displaystyle\left(U(1,0)\right)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\equiv\sigma_{1},\left(U(1,1)\right)=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\
-i&0\end{pmatrix}\equiv\sigma_{2}\,

By direct algebraic manipulations one proves that, for any unit-norm
\psi
in
\mathcal{H}
, the family

\{{U}(m,n)\psi\equiv\psi_{(m,n)}\,,\,(m,n)\in\Gamma_{d}\}

(3.24)

constitutes an overcomplete family,
i.e.
, it
resolves the identity on
\mathcal{H}
in the sense of (
2.2
) or (
2.10
) or (
2.11
):

\frac{1}{d}\sum_{(m,n)\in\Gamma_{d}}\,|\psi_{(m,n)}\rangle\langle\psi_{(m,n)}|=\mathbbm{1}\,,\quad\|\psi\|=1\,.

(3.25)

This fundamental property can be extended to the
U
-transported versions of a density operator
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|
(
\sim
mixed quantum state) on
\mathcal{H}
, where
\mathcal{J}\ni i\mapsto p_{i}
is a discrete probability distribution on an index set
\mathcal{J}
(one could as well deal with a continuous distribution), and all the states
|\psi_{i}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
are unit norm:

\frac{1}{d}\sum_{(m,n)\in\Gamma_{d}}\,\rho(m,n)=\mathbbm{1}\,,\quad\rho(m,n):={U}(m,n)\rho{U}^{{\dagger}}(m,n)\,.

(3.26)

This implies that for any
\phi
,
\psi
in
\mathcal{H}
we have:

\frac{1}{d}\sum_{(m,n)\in\Gamma_{d}}\,\,|\langle\phi|\rho(m,n)|\phi^{\prime}\rangle|^{2}=\|\phi\|^{2}\|\phi^{\prime}\|^{2}\,.

(3.27)

In (
3.25
) the state
\psi_{(m,n)}
is a coherent state (CS) for the group
H(\mathbb{Z}_{d})
in the sense given by Perelomov
[
15
]
, and the picked unit norm
\psi
is called a fiducial vector.
The projection of
\phi\in\mathcal{H}
on
\psi_{m,n}
, namely

\langle\,\psi_{(m,n)}|\phi\rangle=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\,\overline{U(m,n)\psi(l)}\phi(l)=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\,e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{\pi}{d}m\,n}e^{-\mathsf{i}\,\frac{2\pi}{d}m\,l}\overline{\psi(l-n)}\phi(l)\,,

(3.28)

is the phase space representation of
\phi
with respect to the family of coherent states
\psi_{(m,n)}
, and
|\langle\,\psi_{m,n}|\phi\rangle|^{2}/d
is the corresponding Husimi function. More generally,
\frac{1}{d}\mathrm{Tr}\left(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|\rho(m,n)\right)=\frac{1}{d}\langle\phi|\rho(m,n)|\phi\rangle
is the Husimi function of
\phi
in its phase-space representation provided by the family
\{\rho(m,n)\}_{(m,n)\in\Gamma_{d}}
.
In the sequel we restrict our analysis by using CS’s only. The extension of our results to transported density operators is straightforward.

We now summarize the most important features of the CS analysis of the elements of
\mathcal{H}
resulting from the resolution of the identity (
3.25
).

Proposition 3.1
.

For any elements
\phi,\psi\in\,\mathcal{H}
, such that
||\psi||=1
, the map

\phi\rightarrow\langle\psi_{(m,n)}|\phi\rangle\equiv\Phi(m,n)

(3.29)

satisfies the following properties:

•

it is an isometry:

\displaystyle||\phi||^{2}=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}|\phi(l)|^{2}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{(m,n)\in\Gamma_{d}}|{\langle\psi_{(m,n)}|\phi\rangle|}^{2}\,,

(3.30)

•

it can be inverted on its range:

\phi(l)=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\,\langle\psi_{(m,n)}|\phi\rangle\,\psi_{(m,n)}(l)\,,

(3.31)

•

its range lies in the reproducing kernel space characterised by:

\langle\psi_{(m,n)}|\phi\rangle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{(m,n)\in\Gamma_{d}}\,K_{\psi}((m,n),(m^{\prime},n^{\prime}))\Phi(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})\,,

(3.32)

with kernel
K_{\psi}((m,n),(m^{\prime},n^{\prime}))=\langle\psi_{(m,n)}|\psi_{(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})}\rangle
.

Note the expression for the kernel:

K_{\psi}((m,n),(m^{\prime},n^{\prime}))=A(m,m^{\prime},n,n^{\prime})\Psi(m-m^{\prime},n-n^{\prime})\,,

(3.33)

where

A(m,m^{\prime},n,n^{\prime})=e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{\pi}{d}(m(n-n^{\prime})-(m-m^{\prime})n^{\prime})}==e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{\pi}{d}((m-m^{\prime})(n-n^{\prime})-(mn^{\prime})-nm^{\prime})}\,,

(3.34)

and

\Psi(m-m^{\prime},n-n^{\prime})=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\,e^{-\mathsf{i}\frac{2\pi}{d}(m-m^{\prime})l}\overline{\psi(l-(n-n^{\prime}))}\psi(l)\,.

(3.35)

(3.39)

•

Discrete wave plane vector:

(3.40)

with its reproducing kernel

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi_{m\,n}|\phi_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\right\rangle=\delta_{m\,m^{\prime}}e^{-\mathsf{i}\frac{\pi}{d}(n^{\prime}-n)(m+2k_{0})}.

(3.41)

•

Periodization of

:

(3.42)

•

Discrete periodized (

Covariant integral quantization on the discrete torus

4.1
Quantization operators, Husimi and Wigner distributions

Following previous works
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
, we pick a function
\mathfrak{w}
, called weight (but not necessarily positive), on the phase space
\Gamma
. We then define the operator
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
, called “quantization operator”, by

\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\,U(m,n)\mathfrak{w}(m,n)\,,

(4.1)

and we choose the weight such that the operator
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
is bounded and symmetric, i.e., is self-adjoint on the Hilbert space of physical states, which in our case
L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{d})
. Given a weight
\mathfrak{w}
and a function
f
:

f:(p,q)\in\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

(4.2)

defined on the phase space, one would like to define an operator defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{d})
:

A_{f}^{\mathfrak{w}}:\phi\rightarrow A^{\mathfrak{w}}_{f}\psi.

(4.3)

Question: which combinations of
\mathfrak{w}
and
f
give rise to:

•

Auto-adjointness,

{A_{f}}^{\mathfrak{w}}=\left({A_{f}}\right)^{\mathfrak{w}{{}^{\dagger}}}

(4.4)

•

Density operators (Quantum states),

A_{f}^{\mathfrak{w}}\geq 0\,,\quad\mathrm{Tr}\left[A_{f}^{\mathfrak{w}}\right]=1

(4.5)

Proposition 4.1
.

•

With the assumption that the weight
\mathfrak{w}
has been chosen such that the operator
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
, defined by (
4.1
), is bounded:
The operator
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
is the “integral” operator:

(\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}\psi)(m)=\sum_{m^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}(m,m^{\prime})\psi(m^{\prime})\,,

(4.6)

where the kernel
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathfrak{w}}(m,m^{\prime})
is given by:

{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathfrak{w}}(m,m^{\prime})=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\,\mathfrak{w}(n,m-m^{\prime})\,e^{\mathsf{i}\frac{\pi}{d}(n(m+m^{\prime}))}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\widetilde{\mathfrak{w}}_{p_{1}}\left(\frac{m+m^{\prime}}{2},m-m^{\prime}\right)\,.

(4.7)

Here,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{w}}_{p_{1}}
is the inverse discrete Fourier transform of
\mathfrak{w}
with respect to the first variable.

•

The operator
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
is symmetric if and only the weight satisfies:

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}={\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}}^{{\dagger}}\Leftrightarrow\overline{\mathfrak{w}(-m,n)}=\mathfrak{w}(m,-n)\,.

(4.8)

•

The operator
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
is of unit trace, for
\mathfrak{w}(0,0)=1
:

\mathrm{Tr}(\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}})=\mathfrak{w}(0,0)=1\,.

(4.9)

Proof 4.2
.

(i)

The action of
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
on
\psi
is given by:

\displaystyle(\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}\psi)(m)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\,\mathfrak{w}(n,m-m^{\prime})e^{\mathsf{i}\frac{\pi}{d}(n(m+m^{\prime}))}\right)\psi(m^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathfrak{w}}(m,m^{\prime})\psi(m^{\prime})

(ii)

The condition that
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
be symmetric implies the following condition on the kernel:

\overline{{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathfrak{w}}(m,m^{\prime})}={\mathcal{M}}^{\mathfrak{w}}(m^{\prime},m)

which gives:

\overline{\widetilde{\mathfrak{w}}_{p_{1}}\left(\frac{m+m^{\prime}}{2},m-m^{\prime}\right)}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{w}}_{p_{1}}\left(\frac{m+m^{\prime}}{2},-(m-m^{\prime}))\right).

(iii)

Therefore the trace of
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
corresponds to the integral of the kernel over its diagonal, that is:

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathfrak{w}}(m,m)=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\,\mathfrak{w}(m,0)e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{2\pi}{d}mn}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\,\mathfrak{w}(m,0)d\delta_{m\,0}=\mathfrak{w}(0,0).

In turn, one retrieves the weight
\mathfrak{w}
from the quantization operator
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
through a tracing operation.

Proposition 4.3
.

The trace of the operator
{U}^{{\dagger}}(m,n)\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
is given by:

\mathrm{Tr}[U^{{\dagger}}(m,n)\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}]=\mathfrak{w}(m,n)

(4.10)

Proof 4.4
.

To compute this trace, one uses the orthonormal basis
\left\{e^{d}_{k}(l)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{2\pi}{d}kl}\right\}
as follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[U^{{\dagger}}(m,n)\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}\langle\,e^{d}_{k}|\,U^{{\dagger}}(m,n)\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}e^{d}_{k}\rangle

.

The transported of
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
by U, that is,
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}(p,q)=U^{{\dagger}}(p,q)\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}\,U(p,q)^{{\dagger}}

(4.11)

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we have established a covariant integral quantization for systems whose phase space is
\mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\,\mathbb{Z}_{d}
, i.e., for systems moving on the discrete circle.

The symmetry group of this phase space is the discrete version of the Weyl-Heisenberg group, namely the central extension of the abelian group
\mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\,\mathbb{Z}_{d}
, and the phase space can be viewed as the left coset of the group with its center. The existence of a non-trivial unitary irreducible representation of this group on the phase phase, as acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{d})
, allowed us to derive interesting results. First, we have established the concomitant resolution of the identity and subsequent properties such the Gabor transform on the discrete circle and its inversion, the reproducing kernel and the fact that any square integrable function on the circle is fiducial vector. The decomposition of the identity allows to define an integral operator
\mathsf{M}^{\mathfrak{w}}
from weight function
\mathfrak{w}
defined on the phase space.

There are noticeable results related to the quantization of a point
(m,n)
in the phase space according to two standard choices of the weight.

•

With the parity weight, the quantization of the momentum is transported the momentum by the Fourier operator F, that is

m\mapsto\hat{m}=FmF^{-1}\,,(A_{m}^{1}\psi)(l)=(FmF^{-1}\psi)(l)

(7.1)

while the quantization of the discrete angle yields the multiplication operator by the
h(n)=\frac{2\pi\,n}{d}
.

\frac{2\pi\,n}{d}\mapsto A_{h(n)}^{1}\,,\quad(A_{h(n)}^{1}\psi)(l)=\frac{2\pi\,l}{d}\psi(l)\,.

(7.2)

This is of course not acceptable. Alternatively one can quantize the periodized angle function
(\mathrm{Per}\,\mathsf{A})(\theta),\theta=\frac{2\pi\,n}{d}
. One finds the multiplication operator defined by the same discontinuous
(\mathrm{Per}\,\mathsf{A})(\gamma),\gamma=\frac{2\pi\,n}{d}
. There is no regularization.

•

With the coherent state weight one obtains the quantization,

m\mapsto\hat{m}=(A^{\mathfrak{w}}_{g}\psi)(l)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}k\,(\Omega_{\phi}(m)e^{d}_{-k}*\psi)(l)\\

whereas the quantization of the periodized function
\theta=\frac{2\pi\,n}{d}
leads to its smooth regularization,

(\mathrm{Per}\,\mathsf{A})(n)\mapsto\left[\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{h}(m)\,\langle e^{-\mathsf{i}\frac{2\pi\,m}{d}l}\rangle_{|\phi|^{2}}\,e^{\mathsf{i}\,\frac{2\pi\,m}{d}\,l}\right]\psi(l)\,.

(7.3)

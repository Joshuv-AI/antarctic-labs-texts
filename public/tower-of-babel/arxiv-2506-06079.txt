Data-driven nonlinear output regulation via data-enforced incremental passivity
Yixuan Liu, Meichen Guo

Abstract
This work proposes a data-driven regulator design that drives the output of a nonlinear system asymptotically to a time-varying reference and rejects time-varying disturbances. The key idea is to design a data-driven feedback controller such that the closed-loop system is incrementally passive with respect to the regulation error and a virtual input. By carefully designing the virtual input, we solve the data-driven nonlinear output regulation problem where the reference and disturbances are generated by a linear exosystem. The designed regulator is composed of an internal model and a passivation feedback controller characterized by a set of data-dependent linear matrix inequalities. The proposed data-driven method is also applied to stabilizing the non-zero equilibrium of a class of nonlinear systems with unknown equilibrium input. Numerical examples are presented to illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed designs.

Introduction

A primary challenge in controlling modern systems is the lack of mathematical models describing the dynamics of the systems. Many relevant systems found in fields such as neuroscience, epidemiology, and ecology have complex dynamics that are difficult to model using first principles or are too costly to identify a sufficiently accurate model. In applications where the major objective is to achieve control goals and an accurate model is of little or no interest, an efficient approach is the so-called direct data-driven control, where a controller is synthesized directly from data without explicitly identifying a model. Another challenge in controlling modern systems via data is providing rigorous performance guarantees in the presence of complex nonlinearities.

Recent development of direct data-driven control has focused on rigorously characterizing stabilizing controllers for linear and nonlinear dynamical systems, such as the results found in
DePersis2020
,
vanWaarde2020DataInformativity
,
Guo2022
,
verhoek2023DDDLPV
,
Martin2023Survey
. However, real-life applications may require more sophisticated control objectives beyond stabilization, such as trajectory tracking. This work considers a data-driven trajectory tracking problem for a class of nonlinear systems subject to external disturbances. To solve this problem, we use the connection between incremental passivity and output regulation
Pavlov2008IP&OPR
.

Output regulation considers reference tracking and disturbance rejection of a plant, where the reference trajectory and disturbances are generated by an exosystem. Seminal results on linear and nonlinear output regulation can be found in
FRANCIS1976457
,
Davison1976
,
Isidori1990
,
Huang2004
. The widely used output regulation framework augments the plant with an internal model and converts the output regulation problem into a stabilization problem of the augmented system. An alternative approach was proposed in
Pavlov2008IP&OPR
using incremental passivity that describes the relationship between two arbitrary trajectories of a forced system. By first designing a controller that makes the plant incrementally passive and then interconnecting the plant with an incrementally passive internal model, an effective regulator can be designed. The main feature of this approach is that the passivation controller and the internal model designs are decoupled, which simplifies the overall regulator design.

In addition to the model-based results mentioned above, output regulation of unknown systems has also been investigated by techniques such as adaptive dynamic programming
gao2017ADPNonlinear
, neural networks
lan2007NNOPR
, and reinforcement learning
jiang2019RLOPR
. We note that many of these results either require a stabilizing control input to initiate the algorithm or demand a huge amount of data without providing rigorous performance guarantees. Inspired by recent advancements in direct data-driven control, finite offline data have been used to regulate system output. For example,
TrentelmanDDOPR
established necessary and sufficient conditions for the informativity of a given set of data for the algebraic regulator problem. This result was later extended by the authors of
Zhu2024
to linear robust output regulation via state feedback. Using data-based contractivity
Hu2024Contraction
, recent work
Hu2024DDOPR
proposed data-driven regulators for nonlinear systems such that the tracking error of periodic references is bounded. Assuming an unknown linear plant and disturbance model,
Deutscher2024
used a Koopman operator to construct an extended system and achieved data-driven output regulation. Different from these existing results, this work aims at achieving asymptotic output regulation of a class of nonlinear systems via offline data despite external disturbances.

This paper proposes a data-driven output regulator design by enforcing incremental passivity via data. The proposed design contains two steps. In the first step, a data-driven state feedback controller is designed to make the closed-loop system incrementally passive with respect to the regulation error and a virtual input. The conditions characterizing the passivation controller are derived as linear matrix inequalities (LMIs) dependent on data. The second step interconnects the closed-loop system with an incrementally passive internal model designed based on the known exosystem and then obtains the overall regulator. The contributions of this work are at least two-fold. First, we solve the data-driven nonlinear output regulation problem with rigorous guarantees that the system output asymptotically tracks the reference despite external disturbances. Second, we achieve incremental passivation via data-driven feedback for a class of nonlinear systems with unknown system parameters. It should be noted that passivation and passivity-based control have been extensively studied in the model-based setting for the analysis and control of nonlinear dynamical systems. However, in the direct data-driven setting, most existing results focus on verifying or analyzing dissipativity properties of systems via data; for example,
Koch2022DDDissVeri
,
Martin2023polyapprox
,
Verhoek2023DDDissipativityLPV
. In contrast to these results, this work designs a data-driven nonlinear feedback controller that makes the resulting system incrementally passive with respect to the regulation error and a virtual input.

3.1
Incremental passivity

Incremental passivity is an input-output property which holds for any two arbitrary trajectories generated by any two inputs. Following
Pavlov2008IP&OPR
, we revisit the definition, characterization, and interconnection properties of incrementally passive systems, which play an important role in deriving the main results.

Definition 1

(Incremental passivity
Pavlov2008IP&OPR
)
.

The system

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=f(x,u,t)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=h(x,t)

(7)

with state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, input
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and output
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is incrementally passive if there exists a
C^{1}
storage function
V(t,x_{1},x_{2}):\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that for any two inputs
u_{1}(t)
and
u_{2}(t)
and any two solutions
x_{1}(t)
and
x_{2}(t)
of (
1
) corresponding to the inputs, the respective outputs
y_{1}=h(x_{1},t)
and
y_{2}=h(x_{2},t)
satisfy the inequality

\displaystyle\dot{V}(t,x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{1}}f(x_{1},u_{1},t)+\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{2}}f(x_{2},u_{2},t)

\displaystyle\leq(y_{1}-y_{2})^{\top}(u_{1}-u_{2}).

(8)

Definition 2

(Regular storage function

)

A storage function

A simple example of a regular storage function is

Similarly to the conventional passivity property, the feedback interconnection of two incrementally passive systems with regular storage functions is also incrementally passive with a regular storage function.

(

\displaystyle=F_{x}(x,u_{x},t),\quad y_{x}=H_{x}(x,t)

Remark 7

5
Data-driven stabilization of non-zero equilibrium

In industrial applications, common problems such as set-point tracking require stabilizing a non-zero equilibrium. In model-based control, this is often achieved by first performing coordinate transformation on the system model such that the non-zero equilibrium is converted to the origin, and then designing stabilizers for the converted system. However, in the data-driven setting, executing coordinate transformation is challenging, as the equilibrium input is difficult to obtain due to the unknown dynamics. In this section, we use the proposed data-driven output regulation approach for data-driven stabilization of a non-zero equilibrium without the knowledge of the equilibrium input. In particular, the designed data-driven controller stabilizes the non-zero equilibrium asymptotically without estimating the unknown equilibrium input despite the presence of uncertain disturbances.
Compared with recent data-driven set-point control results
verhoek2023DDDLPV
and
Hu2024Contraction
, the proposed controller is also capable of rejecting time-varying disturbances generated by exosystem (
4
). Unlike the approach proposed in
bisoffi2024setpoint

6
Conclusion and Future Work

This work addressed the data-driven output regulation problem of a class of nonlinear systems using incremental passivity. A data-driven state feedback controller is first designed to render the closed-loop system incrementally passive, then the closed-loop system is interconnected with an incrementally passive internal model to complete the regulator design. In particular, data-driven feedback passivation is achieved by solving a set of data-based LMIs. The result of this work provides an efficient data-driven regulator design where the passivation controller and the internal model are designed independently, and rigorously guarantees the asymptotic tracking performance. We also showed that the proposed approach can be effectively applied to data-driven nonlinear stabilization of non-zero equilibrium without the knowledge on the equilibrium input.

Future work.
One of the major challenges to be overcome is handling a more general class of noise. As pointed out in Remark
5
, when the noise cannot be generated by the exosystem in the form of (
4
), the closed-loop system represented using noisy data will contain uncertainties that need to be handled in the passivation process. We will explore techniques such as robust control and adaptive control to make the closed-loop system incrementally passive despite the system uncertainties brought by the noise. Another challenge comes from the unknown nonlinearities. In particular, we are interested in relaxing the assumptions on the nonlinearities, that is, when they are not completely known and the Lipschitz condition does not hold. Last but not least, as incremental passivity is an input-output property, we would like to investigate how to achieve data-driven output feedback passivation and output feedback regulator design using input-output data.

In Vitro Effects of Some Chemotherapeutics on Human Erythrocyte Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase Enzyme
Authors: Çiğdem Çoban, Yusuf Temel, Mehmet Çiftci
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In this study; the in vitro effects of some drugs used in chemotherapy on the glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase enzyme (G6PD; E.C. 1.1.1.49) purified from human erythrocyte lysate were investigated. In the first stage of the study, G6PD enzyme was purified from human erythrocyte lysate (with a specific activity of 0.243 EU/mg protein, 68.75% yield and 162 purificaion fold) by ammonium sulfate precipitation and 2′, 5′ Adenosine diphosphate (ADP)-Sepharose 4B gel affinity chromatography. The purity of the enzyme was checked by the sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE). In the second stage of the study, the in vitro effects of some chemotherapy drugs such as ibandronic acid, fluorouracil, oxaliplatin, carboplatin, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, metoart con and cisplatin on the activity of the purified enzyme were investigated. As a result of the in vitro studies, the drugs ibandronic acid, oxaliplatin and carboplatin, have an inhibitory effect on the enzyme, and IC50 values were calculated as 1.34, 2.05, and 2.43 mM, respectively. In addition, in order to determine the Ki constants and inhibition types for the drugs oxaliplatin and carboplatin, activity measurements were made at five different substrates and three fixed inhibitor concentrations and Lineweaver–Burk graphs were drawn. With the help of these graphs, the Ki constant of oxaliplatin was determined as 19.46 ± 3.38 mM and the Ki constant of carboplatin was 22.37 ± 3.19 mM. It was determined that the inhibition type of both drugs was competitive. It was determined that the drugs fluorouracil, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, metoart con, and cisplatin did not have a significant effect on the enzyme activity.

Introduction

Enzymes, known as catalysts in living organisms, are protein-natured structures that accelerate thousands of biochemical reactions occurring within the cell with very high efficiency, without creating byproducts. Since biomolecules have a very stable structure in terms of kinetics, biochemical reactions occurring within the cell do not occur spontaneously without enzymes >. Studies have been carried out in many fields, especially in pharmacology, on the activities of enzymes located at key points of metabolism.2

Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase enzyme (G6PD; E.C. 1.1.1.49), which is one of the key enzymes of cell metabolism and has been widely studied, is the first enzyme of the pentose phosphate pathway (PPP) that catalyzes the conversion of glucose 6-phosphate to 6-phosphoglucono lactone. In this reaction, reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) production occurs in the presence of NADP+ >. In addition to the production of NADPH, the G6PD enzyme also causes the synthesis of ribose phosphates, which are components of vital biomolecules such as NAD+, deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), adenosine 5′-triphosphate (ATP), and ribonucleic acid (RNA).3 NADPH acts as a substrate for glutathione reductase, an enzyme that synthesizes reduced glutathione (GSH), an important cellular antioxidant molecule. It functions in the antioxidant mechanism within the cell with the—SH groups in the structure of the GSH molecule, which is the most important antioxidant system in the cell. In particular, superoxide, peroxide and hydroxyl radicals formed in the cell due to some factors that disrupt the biochemical process within the cell, such as some harmful habits such as smoking, alcohol, and inadequate and unbalanced nutrition, are reduced by the—SH group of the GSH molecule, thus protecting the cells. In this regard, it is vital for the cell that the GSH/GSSG ratio remains at a certain level. Especially in erythrocyte cells, the GSH/GSSG ratio is approximately 500/1. A decrease in this rate in erythrocyte cells causes hemolysis.1 From this perspective, the G6PD enzyme, which produces NADPH, is also known as an in-direct antioxidant enzyme.4 Studies show that a deficiency in the G6PD enzyme indirectly affects the formation of GSH and negatively affects the functioning of the antioxidant system.5,6

G6PD enzyme deficiency, which currently affects more than 400 million people in the world, is one of the important genetic diseases >. If substances that cause hemolysis are administered to people with G6PD deficiency, these substances or their metabolites oxidize and inactivate GSH, causing hemolysis in erythrocytes. This condition causes disruption of membrane function in erythrocytes and premature destruction of erythrocytes in the spleen and liver.7,8

Today, many drugs are used in human and animal treatment >. Many of these drugs affect enzyme activities at the crossroads of metabolism.9−11 Drugs can act as activators or inhibitors of enzymes that catalyze critical reactions of metabolism.12 Chemotherapeutics are drugs used to destroy cancerous cells. The main purpose of chemotherapy is to suppress protein production by preventing the transcription of DNA and thus stopping the spread of cancer cells.13 However, many of the drugs used in cancer treatment cause nephrotoxicity, depending on the dose of the drug.14 Ibandronic acid is a potent bisphosphonate drug used in the prevention and treatment of skeletal fractures associated with cancer-related hypercalcemia and osteolytic bone metastases. In addition to the effective treatment of hypercalcemia and metastatic bone lesions, bisphosphonates have also been found to have apoptotic effects on tumor cells in preclinical studies.14 Cisplatin is a powerful antineoplastic agent containing the heavy metal platinum (Pt) and is the chemotherapy drug with the most known nephrotoxic side effects.15 Cisplatin is frequently used alone or in combination with other antineoplastic agents (such as cyclophosphamide, bleomycin, etoposide) in the common lung, endometrium, mouth, head and neck cancers and advanced ovarian and testicular cancers, as well as in localized cervical cancer, esophageal cancer and head and neck cancers. It is used together with radiotherapy.16 Fluorouracil is an antimetabolite chemotherapy drug that suppresses DNA synthesis and is used in the treatment of adrenal, gastric, head, neck, breast, and many other cancers.17 Oxaliplatin, a third-generation platinum analogue, is a cancer drug used to treat colorectal cancer [20]. Carboplatin belongs to the antineoplastic drug group and is much less nephrotoxic than cisplatin. It is used instead of cisplatin in patients with renal failure.17 Cyclophosphamide is a potent antineoplastic drug frequently used in the treatment of lymphoma, rheumatoid arthritis, acute and chronic leukemia, breast cancer, multiple myeloma, and bone marrow transplants.18 Doxorubicin is an anthracycline group antineoplastic drug and is used in various cancer treatments. It is a powerful chemotherapy drug with many side effects.19−23

This study aimed to investigate the in vitro effects of chemotherapeutics such as Ibandronic acid, cisplatin, fluorouracil, oxaliplatin, carboplatin, cyclophosphamide and doxorubicin on G6PD enzyme activity purified from human erythrocyte cells.

Materials and Methods

Chemicals

G6P, NADP+, TRIS, ammonium sulfate, hydrochloric acid, sodium chloride, glycine, sodium hydroxide, phosphoric acid, acetic acid, glycerin electrophoresis chemicals, protein standards, 2′, 5′ ADP-Sepharose 4B affinity gel and other chemicals Sigma-Aldrich Co. (St. Louis, MO) and Merck (Darmstadt, Germany).

Preparation of Hemolysate

Human blood was obtained fresh from Bingöl State Hospital under sterile conditions. The fresh blood taken was placed in ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid, ethylenediaminetetraacetate (EDTA) tubes and centrifuged at 3500×g for 20 min. After centrifugation, the plasma and leukocyte layer at the top of the tubes was carefully removed with a dropper. The remaining erythrocyte pellet at the bottom was washed three times with 0.16 M isotonic KCl solution. It was centrifuged at 3500×g for 20 min each time and the precipitate was removed. The resulting erythrocyte precipitate was hemolyzed with 5 times its volume of cold water. Then, centrifugation was performed at 13,500×g for 45 min to remove cell membranes and insoluble materials in the hemolysate. After the supernatant at the top was carefully removed with a dropper, the precipitate was discarded. Thus, hemolysate was obtained.1

Ammonium Sulfate Precipitation and Dialysis

Ammonium sulfate precipitation was performed at 35–65% saturation concentrations in the hemolysate obtained from human erythrocyte cells, and then the precipitate was dissolved in 50 mM K-phosphate, (pH = 7.0) buffer. The resulting enzyme solution was placed in dialysis bags and dialyzed against 10 mM K-phosphate, 1 mM EDTA; pH = 7.5, for 2 h.1

Preparation of Affinity Column and Purification of Human Erythrocyte Hemolysate G6PD Enzyme

The enzyme sample obtained after dialysis was purified using 2′, 5′ Adenosine diphosphate Sepharose-4B affinity chromatography. To prepare the column, 2 g of 2′, 5′-ADP Sepharose-4B gel was weighed for 10 mL of column volume and washed several times with 500 mL of deionized water. The gel became swollen during washing. After removing the air from the swollen gel by vacuum using a water trumpet, the gel was equilibrated by adding equilibration buffer [0.1 M KH2PO4 (Potassium dihydrogen phosphate) + 0.1 M K-acetate pH = 6.0]. The equilibrated gel was packed into a refrigerated column consisting of a 1 × 10 cm closed system. After the gel settled, it was washed with washing and equilibration buffer using a peristaltic pump. The resulting enzyme sample was applied to this column. The affinity column contains 25 mL of 0.1 M KH2PO4 + 0.1 M K-acetate (pH = 6.0), 25 mL, 0.1 M KH2PO4 + 0.1 M K-acetate (pH = 7.85) and 25 mL, respectively. It was washed with 0.1 M KH2PO4 + 0.1 M KCl (pH = 7.85) solutions. The washing process was monitored on a spectrophotometer and the absorbance values were determined to be approximately equal to the blank. After washing the column, G6PD enzyme was eluted from the affinity column with elution buffer (80 mM KH2PO4 + 80 mM KCl + 0.5 mM NADP+ + 10 mM EDTA; pH = 7.85).2

Measurement of Glucose 6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase Enzyme Activity

Enzyme activity was measured spectrophotometrically according to the Beutler method. This method is based on the principle that NADPH, which is formed as a result of the reduction of NADP+ in the reaction catalyzed by the G6PD enzyme, gives maximum absorbance at 340 nm.1

Protein Determination

Quantitative protein amount was determined spectrophotometrically at 595 nm using the Bradford Method in enzyme hemolysate and pure enzyme samples separately. The standard chart was created using bovine serum albumin protein.1

Control of Enzyme Purity by SDS-PAGE

Purity control of the enzyme was carried out according to the Laemmli method using 3–8% batch SDS-PAGE.1

Kinetic Studies

In kinetic studies, the effects of ibandronic acid, fluorouracil, oxaliplatin, carboplatin, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, metoart con and cisplatin drugs on enzyme activity were investigated. IC50 values were calculated by drawing % Inhibition—[I] graphs at different inhibitor concentrations for ibandronic acid, oxaliplatin and carboplatin, which showed inhibition effects as a result of activity measurements. In addition, preliminary experiments were carried out to determine the Ki constants and inhibition types of oxaliplatin and carboplatin, and Lineweaver–Burk graphs were drawn by making activity measurements at the five most appropriate concentrations of three fixed inhibitors and substrates.2

Discussion

After the discovery of the pentose phosphate pathway, research for many years has focused on its role in antioxidant metabolism, NADPH production, which is the main intracellular reducing force, and ribose 5-phosphate production2>. Recent studies show that PPP, in addition to this traditional correlation, has a role in numerous cellular functions including cell cycle, apoptosis, differentiation, motility, angiogenesis and response to antitumor therapy. G6PD enzyme is the first and most important regulatory enzyme of the pentose phosphate pathway. Inhibition of the G6PD enzyme in cancer cells would theoretically reduce NADPH production, slow macromolecular biosynthesis, and make transformed cells vulnerable to damage mediated by free radicals. The results of these studies have made the G6PD enzyme a target in chemotherapy.24

In the first stage of our study, G6PD enzyme was purified from human blood tissue by 2′, 5′ ADP-Sepharose-4B affinity chromatography. This method is known to have the ability to bind NADPH-dependent enzymes and is widely used in the purification of these enzymes. With this method, purification is achieved very quickly and with high efficiency.25−27 Enzyme purity was checked by SDS-PAGE method. In previous studies, the molecular weight of the G6PD enzyme was reported to be approximately 55 kDa. SDS PAGE results showed that the enzyme was in the same range.28

In the next stage of our study, the effects of ibandronic acid, fluorouracil, oxaliplatin, carboplatin, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin and cisplatin chemotherapy drugs on purified G6PD enzyme activity were investigated. IC50 values for ibandronic acid, oxaliplatin and carboplatin drugs, which have an inhibitory effect on the enzyme, were calculated as 1.34, 2.05, and 2.43 mM, respectively. In addition, the Ki constant of oxaliplatin was determined as 19.46 ± 3.38 mM and the Ki constant of carboplatin was 22.37 ± 3.19 mM. The inhibition type of both drugs was determined as competitive. This result suggests that the drugs ibandronic acid, oxaliplatin and carboplatin compete with G6P for binding to the active site of the G6PD enzyme. It was found that the drugs fluorouracil, cyclophosphamide and cisplatin did not have a significant activation or inhibition effect on the enzyme.

Carboplatin, a platinum derivative, is frequently preferred in chemotherapy for lung, head and neck and ovarian cancers.29 Oxaliplatin, a platinum analog, is a chemotherapy drug used to treat colorectal cancer.17 Cisplatin is especially used in lung cancer treatments, and studies have shown a significant increase in survival in chemotherapy treatments with cisplatin compared to treatments without platinum.30 When the study results were examined, it was determined that ibandronic acid, oxaliplatin and carboplatin drugs had an effective inhibitory effect on G6PD enzyme activity even at low concentrations. In addition, doxorubicin, one of the chemotherapy drugs used in our study, is known to cause free radical formation, reduce the activities of antioxidant enzymes such as glutathione peroxidase, superoxide dismutase and catalase, and cause cardiotoxicity.31 In this study, it was determined that doxobicin had no activation or inhibition effect on G6PD enzyme activity.

According to the literature review, 2,4-dihydroxy-5-fluoropyrimidine and sodium-2-mercaptoethanesulfonate cytotoxic drugs, which are effective in malignant tumors of the pancreas, liver, liver metastasis, ovarian cancer, cervical cancer, bladder, colorectal, breast and prostate cancer, are effective on G6PD enzyme activity. In a study investigating their effects, it was determined that these substances inhibit the enzyme. Since NADPH cannot be produced if G6PD is inhibited, free radicals damage cell membranes and cause hemolysis.32,33 In a study investigating the in vitro effects of chemotherapy agents used in pancreatic cancer treatment on COX enzyme activity, oxaliplatin had a significant effect on COX enzyme activity produced by sheep pancreas; It was found that it had no effect despite increasing concentration.34 In another study, the inhibition effect of some drugs used in chemotherapy treatment on cancer-related carbonic anhydrase IX and XII isoenzymes (CA-IX, CA-XII) was investigated. Accordingly, it was determined that cisplatin, 5-fluorouracil and carboplatin showed inhibition effects. In this study, cisplatin was found to have strong inhibition on CA-IX and CA-XII isoenzymes with IC50 values of 0.67 and 0.93, respectively.35 In another study conducted on oxaliplatin, it was reported that oxaliplatin increased the levels of MDA, an oxidative indicator, in the liver, and significantly decreased the levels of superoxide dismutase, catalase and GSH, which are antioxidant enzymes. Additionally, the same study found that oxaliplatin-induced superoxide dismutase, catalase and GSH levels increased and MDA levels decreased after treatment with curcumin, a natural antioxidant.36 In a study, it was determined that oxaliplatin applied to rat spleen tissue increased the caspase-3 enzyme, which affects the apoptotic state.37 In a different study conducted by Adem et al., how phenolic substances inhibit G6PD and 6PGD enzymes in vitro was examined. The study results show that G6PD enzyme was inhibited by sinapic acid, polydatin, ferulic acid, ellagic acid, caffeic acid, naringenin and hesperidin. The IC50 values of these inhibitors were 0.509–0.572 mM, respectively. Ki constants ranged from 10.66 to 102.61 μM.

Conclusions

Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) is an important enzyme that produces NADPH to stabilize reduced glutathione (GSH), an intracellular antioxidant that removes reactive oxygen species (ROS) to protect cancer cells from oxidative damage. Inhibition of G6PD reduces NADPH production, lowers GSH levels, impairs the ability of cancer cells to scavenge ROS, and increases apoptosis through ROS-mediated damage. It appears that the drugs ibandronic acid, oxaliplatin, and carboplatin used in our study significantly inhibit the G6PD enzyme activity purified from human erythrocytes. The results of this research will guide chemotherapy strategies targeting the G6PD enzyme and in vivo G6PD research.

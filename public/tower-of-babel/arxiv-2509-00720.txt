Multiplicative Hecke operators and their applications II
Chang Heon Kim and Gyucheol Shin ∗

Abstract
. Inspired by Borcherds’ questions [ 2 , 17.10] , Guerzhoy constructed a new type of Hecke operators 𝒯 ⁡ ( p ) \mathcal{T}(p) , called the multiplicative Hecke operators, which acts on the space of meromorphic modular forms on the full modular group SL 2 ​ ( ℤ ) \mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) . In [ 16 ] , this result was extended in two directions: to higher levels and to 𝒯 ⁡ ( n ) \mathcal{T}(n) with a positive integer n n . In this paper, building on the results in [ 16 ] , we further generalize the result in another direction by considering alternative infinite product expansions of meromorphic modular forms. As an application, we demonstrate how multiplicative Hecke operators relate both the divisor of modular forms and traces of singular moduli. Additionally, we prove the existence of a modular form with nonintegral coefficients whose poles or zeros are only supported at the cusps and which is not a multiplicative Hecke eigenform.

1.
Introduction and statement of results

Let

\Gamma_{0}(N):=\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\}.

A meromorphic function on
\mathbb{H}^{*}:=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm Im}(\tau)>0\}\cup\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
is called a
meromorphic modular form of weight
k
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
if it satisfies
(f|_{k}\gamma)(\tau):=(c\tau+d)^{-k}f(\gamma\tau)=f(\tau)
for all
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(N)
where
\gamma\tau:=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}
.
Hecke operators are linear operators acting on the space of modular forms on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. For each prime
p
and modular form
f
of weight
k
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, the (usual) Hecke operator
T(p)
is defined by

f|T(p):=\begin{cases}p^{k/2-1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}ad=p\\
0\leq b<d\end{subarray}}f|_{k}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix},&\text{ if }p\nmid N,\\
p^{k/2-1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-1}f|_{k}\begin{pmatrix}1&j\\
0&p\end{pmatrix},&\text{ if }p|N.\end{cases}

Inspired by Borcherds’ questions
[
2
, 17.10]
, Guerzhoy
[
11
]
introduced a new class of Hecke operators
\mathcal{T}(p)
(for prime

) on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, called the
multiplicative Hecke operator
, acting on the multiplicative group of meromorphic modular forms on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. He showed that the Borcherds isomorphism commutes with the multiplicative Hecke operators and the usual Hecke operators acting on the space of modular forms of weight 1/2 on
\Gamma_{0}(4)
. More recently, in
[
13
]
, Jeon, Kang and the first author generalized Guerzhoy’s result
[
11
, Theorem 1]
to higher level cases. They proved that the generalized Borcherds products defined in
[
4

commute with the multiplicative Hecke operators
\mathcal{T}(p)
and the usual Hecke operators on half integral weight vector-valued harmonic weak Maass forms. In addition, they showed that the logarithmic derivative also commutes with the multiplicative Hecke operators and the usual Hecke operators acting on the space of meromorphic modular forms of weight 2. These results, however, were established only for primes
p
not dividing the level
N

[
16
]

1.1.
Multiplicative Hecke operators and alternative infinite product expansions of modular forms

The first purpose of this paper is, as a subsequent work to
[
16
]
, to provide another description of multiplicative Hecke operators by utilizing an alternative expression for the infinite product expansion of modular forms. Before stating our results, following
[
23
]
, we first introduce the notion of an alternative expression of the infinite product expansion of modular forms. For a modular form
f
, while its infinite product expansion is usually written as
f=q^{v_{\infty}(f)}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})^{c(n)}
, Zagier introduced a modified infinite product expansion of modular form in order to study twisted traces of singular moduli of the elliptic modular
j
-function. To be more precise, let
D
be a positive discriminant and
-d
a negative discriminant. We assume that
D
and
-d
to be congruent to a square modulo
4N
. Let
\mathcal{Q}_{d,N}:=\{[a,b,c]=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}\in\mathcal{Q}_{d}:a\equiv 0\pmod{N}\}
, with the usual action of the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
where
\mathcal{Q}_{d}
is the set of positive definite integral binary quadratic forms of discriminant
-d
. For each
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{d,N}
, we associate its unique root
\alpha_{Q}\in\mathbb{H}
. Then for
Q=[Na,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{dD,N}
, the genus character
\chi_{D}
is defined as

\chi_{D}(Q):=\begin{cases}\big(\frac{\Delta}{n}\big),&\text{ if $\Delta|b^{2}-4Nac$ and $\frac{b^{2}-4Nac}{\Delta}$ is a square modulo $4N$ and $(a,b,c,\Delta)=1$},\\
0,&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

Here
n
is any integer prime to
D
represented by one of the quadratic forms
[N_{1}a,b,N_{2}c]
with
N_{1}N_{2}=N
and
N_{1},N_{2}>0
(see
[
10
, §1.2]
or
[
4
, §4]
). Zagier
[
23
, Theorem 7]
defined the rational function
\mathcal{H}_{D,d}(X)
by

\mathcal{H}_{D,d}(X)=\prod\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{Dd}/\Gamma(1)}(X-j(\alpha_{Q}))^{\chi_{D}(Q)},

and showed that

\mathcal{H}_{D,d}(j(\tau))=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{D}(q^{n})^{A(n^{2}D,d)},

where

P_{D}(t):=\exp\bigg(-\sqrt{D}\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{D}{r}\Big)\frac{t^{r}}{r}\bigg)=\begin{cases}\prod\limits_{0<n<D}(1-\zeta_{D}^{n}t)^{(\frac{D}{n})}&\;\text{ if $D>1$}\\
1-t&\;\text{ if $D=1$}\end{cases}\in\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})(t),

\zeta_{D}:=e^{2\pi i/D}
,
(\frac{*}{*})
is the Kronecker symbol, and
A(D,d)
is the
D
th Fourier coefficient of
f_{d}=q^{-d}+O(q)
in the space of weakly holomorphic modular forms of weight 1/2 on
\Gamma_{0}(4)
satisfying Kohnen plus condition.

Later in order to describe the generalized Borcherds product explicitly, for instance
[
4
,
6
]
, this alternative infinite product expansion of modular forms was employed. In a similar fashion, we adopt Zagier’s idea of expressing modular forms via this type of infinite product expansion and apply it to all meromorphic modular forms. Using the series
P_{D}
, we now state our first result, which resolves convergence issues that may arise from the infinite product expansion of meromorphic modular forms.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
D
is a positive fundamental discriminant. Let

f(\tau)=q^{h}(1+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n})

be a meromorphic modular form of weight
k
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. Then the following assertions are true.

(1)

There are uniquely determined complex numbers

satisfying

(1.2)

f(\tau)=q^{h}\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{D}(q^{n})^{c(D,n)}.

Here, we let

where

denotes the principal branch of the complex logarithm.

(2)

For each

1.2.
Divisor of modular forms and multiplicative Hecke operators

The exponents in the infinite product expansion of a meromorphic modular form are closely related to its divisors. In
[
5
]
, it was shown that for a given meromorphic modular form
f
, the values of the Faber polynomials
J_{m}(\tau)
at the zeros or poles of
f
in the fundamental domain of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
can be expressed as a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of the exponents in the infinite product expansion of
f
and the sum of divisors functions. Throughtout this article, by the
divisor formula
we mean the relation given in
[
5
, Theorem 5]
. To state our next results, which describe how multiplicative Hecke operators fit into the framework of divisors of modular forms, we first introduce some auxiliary notions. At first, we brifely recall the definition of the modular functions
j_{N,n}(\tau)
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, which will be used later. For
s\in\mathbb{C}
and
m\geq 0
, we define

\phi_{m}(v,s):=\begin{cases}2\pi\sqrt{mv}I_{s-1/2}(2\pi mv)&\text{ if $m>0$},\\
v^{s}&\text{ if $m=0$},\end{cases}

where
I_{s}
is the
I
-Bessel function. For
{\rm Re}(s)>1
, the
Niebur-Poincaré series

F_{N,-m}(\tau,s)
is defined by

F_{N,-m}(\tau,s):=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(N)_{i\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(N)}\phi_{m}(v,s)q^{-mu}|_{0}\gamma,

where
\Gamma_{0}(N)_{i\infty}=\{\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Various properties of
F_{N,-m}
have been discussed in
[
19
]
. The function
j_{N,n}(\tau)
is defined by the coefficient in the Laurent expansion of the Niebur-Poincaré series at
s=1
. This is a standard example of a
polyharmonic Maass form
.
Next, for each function

, we define a map
\mathcal{D}_{f}:{\rm Div}(X_{0}(N))\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

\mathcal{D}_{f}(D):=\sum\limits_{[z]\in X_{0}(N)}n_{z}f(z)

.

1.4.
Multiplicative Hecke eigenform with nonintegral Fourier coefficients

The second purpose of this article is to investigate the multiplicative Hecke eigenform
f
when
f\not\in\mathbb{Z}((q))
. In our previous work
[
16
, Theorem 1.14]
, we proved that if
f
is a meromorphic modular form on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and either
N
is squarefree or
f\in\mathbb{Z}((q))
then the following are equivalent:

(i)

f
is an eta quotient.

(ii)

f
is a multiplicative Hecke eigenform.

(iii)

f
has no poles or zeros in
\mathbb{H}
.

It is noteworthy that an integer Fourier coefficient condition is necessary. Indeed if
f
is such a form but its Fourier coefficients are nonintegral, then by the Drinfeld-Manin theorem
[
9
,
18
]
and
[
21
, Corollary 8]
there exists a modular form that is not an eta-quotient. However, in
[
16
]
, it was proven that if
f
is an eta quotient then it is a multiplicative Hecke eigenform, and if
f
is a multiplicaitive Hecke eigenform, then it has no poles or zeros in
\mathbb{H}
. Therefore, a natural question arising from this observation is that if we eliminate the integer Fourier coefficient condition, then which of (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) or (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) does not hold? Here, we provide an answer to this question.

Theorem 1.9
.

Suppose
N
is a positive integer such that
p^{2}|N
for some odd prime
p
. Then there exists a meromorphic modular form on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
such that it has no poles or zeros in
\mathbb{H}
but is not a multiplicative Hecke eigenform.

Example 1.10
.

Let
N=9
. Then the Hauptmodul for
\Gamma_{0}(9)
is given by
\eta(\tau)^{3}/\eta(9\tau)^{3}
. From the transformation rule of the Dedekind eta function (for instance, see
[
7
, Theorem 5.8.1]
), we have

\alpha:=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow i\infty}\frac{\eta(\tau)^{3}}{\eta(9\tau)^{3}}\bigg|_{0}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
3&1\end{pmatrix}=-\frac{9+3\sqrt{3}i}{2}.

It follows that if we put
f(\tau):=\eta(\tau)^{3}/\eta(9\tau)^{3}-\alpha
, then
f
has a simple pole at
i\infty
and a simple zero at the cusp 1/3. Thus, it has no zeros or poles in
\mathbb{H}
, and clearly has nonintegral Fourier coefficients. However, we see from (
1.3
) that

f(\tau)=q^{-1}\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})^{c(n)}=q^{-1}(1-q)^{-(3+3\sqrt{3}i)/2}(1-q^{2})^{(-3+6\sqrt{3}i)/2}(1-q^{3})^{(9+\sqrt{3}i)/2}\cdots,

and from Theorem
1.3
(or
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
), that

f|\mathcal{T}(2)=q^{-3}(1-q)^{(-9+9\sqrt{3}i)/2}(1-q^{2})^{(-9-18\sqrt{3}i)/2}(1-q^{3})^{(27-3\sqrt{3}i)/2}\cdots,

which is not equal to
f(\tau)^{3}
. Therefore, we conclude that
f
is not a multiplicative Hecke eigenform.

Remark 1.11

Theorem
1.9
does not imply that
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
is true. Therefore, it would be interesting to determine whether
(ii)
implies
(i)
or not when

.

Taking the exponential on both sides yields

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

be a positive fundamental discriminant. Let

In particular, if the genus of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
is one, then we can write
f_{N,m}=j_{N,m}+\alpha_{m}j_{N,1}
for some constant
\alpha_{m}
. Therefore, by adding
\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\Tr_{D,d}(\alpha_{p}j_{N,1})
to both sides, we obtain

\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\Tr_{D,d}(f_{N,p})\equiv\bigg(\bigg(\frac{D}{p}\bigg)+\alpha_{p}\bigg)\Tr_{D,d}(j_{N,1})\pmod{p}.

Finally, we prove the last assertion. Let
A_{m}^{(N)}(D,d)
denote the coefficient of
q^{D}
of
f_{N,d}|T(m)
. Then by
[
13
, Corollary 4.2]
, we have

A_{p^{r}}^{(N)}(D,d)=\sum\limits_{t=0}^{r}\bigg(\frac{-d}{p}\bigg)^{r-t}A^{(N)}(D,p^{2t}d).

On the other hand, by (
4.1
) and
[
16
, Theorem 1.15]
, we have
\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\Tr_{D,d}(j_{N,p^{r}})=c_{p^{r}}(D,1)=A_{p^{r}}^{(N)}(D,d)
. Since
A^{(N)}(D,p^{2t}d)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\Tr_{D,p^{2t}d}(j_{N,1})
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.9

Proof.

Let
N
be non-squarefree and
p

. By the Drinfeld-Manin theorem
[

, the finiteness of the rational cuspidal subgroup of the Jacobian

. Note that by
[

). Therefore, we conclude that such a function

has nonintegral Fourier coefficiets. By replacing

N
(this is possible because
(e,p)=1
). By replacing

\Delta

[
16

e_{1}/p

\Gamma_{0}(N)

\Gamma_{0}(N)
-inequivalent to the cusp

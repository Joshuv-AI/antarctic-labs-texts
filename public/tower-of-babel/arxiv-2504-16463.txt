Introduction

Given an input graph
G
, a
k
-spanner
is a sparse subgraph
H
whose shortest path distances agree with those of
G
up to a small multiplicative factor
k
, called the
stretch
.
Spanners were introduced by Peleg and Schaffer
[
PS89
]
after being used implicitly in prior work in distributed computing
[
PU87
,
PU89
]
, and have since found widespread application in network design, graph algorithms, etc (see survey
[
ABS+20
]
).

A primary goal of research on spanners is to optimize the worst-case tradeoff between stretch and
size
.
There are two popular ways to measure the size of a spanner:

•

The first is by its
sparsity
,
|E(H)|/n
(where
n
is the number of vertices in the input graph).
This measure arises e.g. when the goal is to store the spanner in few bits of memory, or when running algorithms on the spanner whose runtime is sensitive to the spanner sparsity.

•

The second is by its total weight
w(H)
.
Since
w(H)
can generally be unbounded, it is popular to normalize the spanner weight by the weight of a minimum spanning tree (MST) of the input graph: the lightness of a spanner
H
of a graph
G
is the quantity

\ell(H\mid G):=\frac{w(H)}{w(\texttt{MST}(G))}.

1.1
Sparsity and Lightness in General Graphs

The lightness of a graph is always at least as large as its sparsity, and in general it could be much higher.
However, in some sense this difference can be bounded.
Let us restrict attention to
n
-node
metric graphs
, in which every edge is the unique shortest path between its endpoints, which is the natural setting for spanner problems (i.e., non-metric edges can be removed without affecting the distances in
G
).
Here, the bounds are:

Theorem 1

(Folklore)
.

Every
n
-node metric graph
G
has sparsity
O(n)
and lightness
O(n^{2})
.

For the upper bound on lightness, note that the graph
G
has at most
n^{2}
edges, each of weight at most
w(\texttt{MST}(G))
. For the lower bound, consider an
n
-node path with edges of weight
1
, augmented with all additional edges where the weight of
(u,v)
is set to their distance along the path minus
{\varepsilon}
.
Although this bound is tight, it has been known since the seminal work of Khuller, Raghavachari, and Young
[
KRY95
]
that one can improve lightness to
O(n)
, matching sparsity, by paying a
(1+{\varepsilon})
factor in stretch:

Theorem 2

(
[
KRY95
]
)
.

For any
{\varepsilon}>0
, every
n
-node graph
G
has a
(1+{\varepsilon})
-spanner with lightness
O_{{\varepsilon}}(n)
.
1
1

1

We use
O_{{\varepsilon}}
notation to hide polynomial dependencies on
{\varepsilon}^{-1}
.

Once this initial upper bound is established, it is then natural to ask whether one can pay an even higher stretch to improve the
O(n)
sparsity and lightness dependence.
After a long line of work
[
ADD+93
,
CDNS92
,
ENS15a
,
CWN18
,
FS20
,
LS23
,
Bod25
,
BF25
]
, especially on the side of lightness, the following tradeoffs is now known:

Theorem 3

(
[
ADD+93
,
CWN18
,
LS23
,
Bod25
]
)
.

For any
{\varepsilon}>0
and positive integer
k
, every
n
-node graph has a
(2k-1)
-spanner with sparsity
O(n^{1/k})
and a
(1+{\varepsilon})(2k-1)
-spanner with lightness
O_{{\varepsilon}}(n^{1/k})
.

This sparsity bound is known to be best possible, assuming the girth conjecture
[
Erd63
]
.
For lightness, the
n^{1/k}
dependence is tight assuming the girth conjecture as well.
The exact (hidden) dependence on
{\varepsilon}
remains open, but it was recently proved that the
{\varepsilon}
-dependence is not entirely removable
[
BF25
]
.

1.2
Sparsity and Lightness in Minor-Free Graphs

An important special case for spanners is that where the input is
K_{h}
-minor-free; this arises as a technical tool in approximation algorithms for the Traveling Salesman Problem
[
RS98
,
AGK+98
,
BDT14
,
GS02
,
DHK11
]
, and in some sense it generalizes the well-studied setting of Euclidean input graphs (see book
[
NS07
]
).
A
K_{h}
-minor-free graph is already guaranteed to be quite sparse, and therefore comparably light:

Theorem 4

(
[
Kos82
,
AKS23
]
)
.

Every
n
-node,
K_{h}
-minor-free metric graph has sparsity
O(h\sqrt{\log h})
and lightness
O(nh\sqrt{\log h})
.
2
2

2

For a gradual exposition of the proof and development of ideas in this theorem, we recommend the excellent recent blog post by Hung Le
[
Le22
]
.

Is is natural to ask, like for general graphs, whether one can remove the
n
-dependence from the lightness bound by paying an extra
(1+{\varepsilon})
factor in the stretch.
This was a long-standing open question: it was first asked in 2002 by Grigni and Sissokho
[
GS02
]
, and then conjectured to be possible in 2012 by Grigni and Hung
[
GH12
]
(who showed that
(1+{\varepsilon})
stretch can be used to improve the dependence to
\log n
).
The result was finally achieved in a remarkable FOCS 2017 paper by Borradaille, Le, and Wulff-Nilsen:

Theorem 5

(
[
BLWN17
]
)
.

For any
{\varepsilon}>0
, every
n
-node,
K_{h}
-minor-free graph has a
(1+{\varepsilon})
-spanner with lightness
O_{{\varepsilon}}(h\sqrt{\log h})
.

Analogous to the work on general graphs, a natural next step after this theorem is to investigate the
full
tradeoff between stretch and sparsity/lightness for minor-free graphs.
That is the subject of the current paper.
We prove the following new bounds:

Theorem 6

(Main Result, Upper Bounds)
.

For any
{\varepsilon}>0
and positive integer
k
, every
n
-node,
K_{h}
-minor-free graph has a
(2k-1)
-spanner with sparsity

O\left(h^{\frac{2}{k+1}}\cdot\text{polylog }h\right),

and a
(1+{\varepsilon})(2k-1)
-spanner with lightness

O_{{\varepsilon}}\left(h^{\frac{2}{k+1}}\cdot\text{polylog }h\right).

The exponent
\frac{2}{k+1}
in Theorem
6
might look surprising; at first, one might guess that the exponent would be
\frac{1}{k}
to match Theorem
3
.
However, we further prove that this is indeed the correct exponent for minor-free graphs:

Theorem 7

(Main Result, Lower Bounds)
.

Assuming the girth conjecture
[
Erd63
]
, there are
n
-node
K_{h}
-minor-free graphs for which any
(2k-1)
-spanner has sparsity and lightness

\Omega\left(h^{\frac{2}{k+1}}\right).

Our upper and lower bounds differ by
\text{polylog }h
factors.
It seems unlikely that these factors can be removed entirely from the upper bound, since we know that a
\sqrt{\log h}
factor is truly necessary in the sparsity and lightness bounds for minor-free graphs.
However, it would be interesting to have this
\text{polylog }h
dependence degrade with the stretch.
We conjecture that the following improvement to our bounds will be possible:

Conjecture 8
.

For any
{\varepsilon}>0
and positive integer
k
, every
n
-node,
K_{h}
-minor-free graph has a
(2k-1)
-spanner with sparsity

O\left(\left(h\cdot\text{polylog }h\right)^{\frac{2}{k+1}}\right),

and a
(1+{\varepsilon})(2k-1)
-spanner with lightness

O_{{\varepsilon}}\left(\left(h\cdot\text{polylog }h\right)^{\frac{2}{k+1}}\right).

The advantage of this conjecture over our main result would be that it would allow spanners of truly constant sparsity/lightness (even as a function of
h
), in exchange for stretch
O(\log h)
.
This would be a satisfying analog to the important special case of spanners with constant sparsity/lightness and stretch
O(\log n)
for general graphs.

1.3
Techniques: A Density Increment Theorem for Minor-Free Graphs

A
density increment theorem
for minor-free graphs is a result that guarantees that every minor-free graph has a relatively dense subgraph.
Density increment theorems for minor-free graphs have emerged as a powerful tool in recent progress on Hadwiger’s Conjecture
[
Pos20
,
DP25a
,
NPS23
]
.
This paper makes the point that they may be far more broadly applicable to algorithmic and combinatorial analysis of minor-free graphs as well.

For intuition, let us revisit the bound of
O(h\sqrt{\log h})
on the sparsity of minor-free graphs.
One can get a nearly-matching lower bound by considering the disjoint union of
\frac{n}{h-1}
cliques of size
h-1
each: this has sparsity
\Omega(h)
, and is clearly
K_{h}
-minor-free.
The actual tight lower bound is only slightly more involved than that; it is a disjoint union of many carefully-designed small graphs that are each nearly a clique.

These constructions based on disjoint unions will contain “clustered” parts of the graph, i.e., very small subgraphs whose average degree is roughly the same as the original graph.
The Density Increment Theorem says, in some sense, that this is not a coincidence:
all
nearly-tight lower bound graphs must have this structure.

Theorem 9

(Density Increment Theorem
[
Pos20
]
, reformulated)
.
3
3

3

The cited work has been withdrawn from arXiv, since the goal of this theorem in the paper was to prove an
O(t\log\log^{6}t)
bound on Hadwiger’s conjecture and an improved bound of
O(t\log\log t)
was proved by the author and Delcourt in a subsequent work
[
DP25b
]
via a similar approach. However, the stated Density Increment Theorem still holds. See also the remark in
[
DP25b
]
(the paragraph after Theorem 2.2).

Let
G
be a
K_{h}
-minor-free graph with average degree
d
. Then
G
contains a subgraph on at most
(h^{2}/d)\cdot\text{polylog }h
vertices, with average degree at least
\frac{d}{\text{polylog }h}
.

For analyzing spanner sparsity, the density increment theorem yields a remarkably simple proof.
Our strategy is to use the standard greedy spanner algorithm
[
ADD+93
]
to construct a
K_{h}
-minor-free spanner
H
with high girth.
We can then use the density increment theorem to pass to a very small subgraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
of comparable sparsity, which still has high girth.
The standard Moore bounds, which limit the maximum possible density of a high-girth graph, then yield our desired theorem when applied to
H^{\prime}
.
The gap of
\text{polylog }h
between Theorem
6
and Conjecture
8
comes entirely from the
\text{polylog }h
loss in the density increment theorem itself.

For analyzing spanner lightness, the density increment theorem is still useful, but the proof is necessarily more involved.
We again start by using the greedy algorithm to construct a
K_{h}
-minor-free spanner
H
with high
weighted
girth (see Definition
4.1
).
However, we cannot simply use the density increment theorem to pass to a small subgraph
H^{\prime}
: although we could bound the lightness of
H^{\prime}
, we do not have a good way to compare
\texttt{MST}(H^{\prime})
and
\texttt{MST}(H)
, so this would not reveal much about the lightness of
H
.
Instead, we need to dip into the techniques of Borradaille, Le, and Wulff-Nilsen
[
BLWN17
]
for proving the initial bound on minor-free spanner lightness in Theorem
5
.

1.4
Other Related Work

Spanners have been extensively studied in Euclidean graphs, where the nodes are embedded in Euclidean space, there are all possible edges among nodes, and the weight of each edge
(u,v)
set to the Euclidean distance
w(u,v):=\|u-v\|_{2}
.
We refer to the book by Narasimhan and Smid for a thorough account
[
NS07
]
.
The following near-optimal tradeoffs were recently determined by Le and Solomon
[
LS22
]
:

Theorem 10

(Constant Sparsity/Lightness Spanners for Euclidean Graphs
[
LS22
]
)
.

For any constant
{\varepsilon}>0
and constant integer
d\geq 2
, every
d
-dimensional Euclidean graph has a spanner with
\cdot(1+{\varepsilon})
stretch, sparsity
O\left({\varepsilon}^{-d+1}\right)
, and lightness
O\left({\varepsilon}^{-d}\log{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)
.
These tradeoffs are best possible, up to the factor of
\log{\varepsilon}^{-1}
in lightness.

One can again ask whether it is possible to pay more stretch in exchange for a better dependence on
{\varepsilon}
or
d
.
This question has been studied, but so far remains open.
Aronov et al.
[
ADBC+08
]
showed that one can pay a
much
larger stretch of
O(n/k)
in exchange for an
extremely
sparse spanner with number of edges
|E(H)|\leq n-1+k
.
Work of Filtser and Neiman
[
FN22
]
(c.f. Remark 1) implied that one can pay
O(t)
stretch for sparsity and lightness
\exp(d/t^{2})\cdot\text{polylog }n
.
However, a general tradeoff remains elusive.

Euclidean graphs are naturally generalized by
doubling metrics
, where analogous sparsity/lightness bounds for
(1+{\varepsilon})
-spanners, e.g.
[
Got15
,
BLWN19
,
FS20
]
.
Extending these results to higher stretch would be interesting as well.

Preliminaries

Graph Notation.

Given a graph
G
and an edge
e\in E(G)
, we let
G-e
denote the graph obtained by removing
e
from
G
. We denote the weight of an edge
e
in
G
by
w(e)
. Given a pair of vertices
s,t\in V(G)
, we let
\textnormal{{dist}}_{G}(s,t)
denote the weighted distance between
s
and
t
in graph
G
.

Our proofs will require a standard theorem in the graph spanner literature that bounds the number of edges in large girth graphs.

Theorem 11

(Moore bounds)
.

For all positive integers
k,n
, every
n
-vertex graph with girth
>2k
has at most
O(n^{1+1/k})
edges.

Erdős conjectured that this upper bound is tight; this is frequently referred to as the girth conjecture.

Conjecture 12

(Erdős girth conjecture)
.

The Moore bounds are tight. That is, for every positive integer
k
and sufficiently large positive integer
n
, there exists an
n
-vertex graph with girth
>2k
and
\Omega(n^{1+1/k})
edges.

Sparse Multiplicative Spanners in Minor-Free Graphs

In this section we will prove that
K_{h}
-minor-free graphs have sparse multiplicative spanners.

Theorem 13

(Minor-Free Spanner Sparsity)
.

For all positive integers
k,h,n
, every
K_{h}
-minor-free weighted graph on
n
vertices admits a
(2k-1)
-multiplicative spanner with number of edges

O\left(n\cdot h^{\frac{2}{k+1}}\cdot\text{polylog }h\right)

Moreover, this bound is tight up to
\text{polylog }h
factors under the girth conjecture.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
K_{h}
-minor-free weighted graph, and let
k
be a positive integer. We will construct a
(2k-1)
-spanner
H\subseteq G
using a greedy strategy that is standard in the area. We first present the construction of
H
, and then analyze its correctness and size.

Construction of
H
.

We may assume without loss of generality that for each edge
(u,v)\in E(G)
,
w((u,v))=\textnormal{{dist}}_{G}(u,v)
by removing edges from
G
that do not satisfy this condition.
Initially, let
H\leftarrow(V,\emptyset)
. For each edge
(u,v)\in E
in order of non-decreasing weight, if
\textnormal{{dist}}_{H}(u,v)>(2k-1)\cdot\textnormal{{dist}}_{G}(u,v)
, then add edge
(u,v)
to
H
. After iterating through all edges in
E
, return
H
.

We quickly verify that
H
is indeed a multiplicative spanner of
G
.

Claim 14

(Correctness)
.

H
is a
(2k-1)
-spanner of
G
.

Proof.

This proof follows from a standard argument that can be attributed to
[
ADD+93
]
. For each edge
(u,v)\in E(G)
, we know that either
\textnormal{{dist}}_{H}(u,v)\leq(2k-1)\cdot\textnormal{{dist}}_{G}(u,v)
or
(u,v)\in E(H)
. Since
w((u,v))=\textnormal{{dist}}_{G}(u,v)
, we conclude that for each edge
(u,v)\in E(G)
,
\textnormal{{dist}}_{H}(u,v)\leq(2k-1)\cdot\textnormal{{dist}}_{G}(u,v)
. Combining this fact with the triangle inequality, it follows that for all
s,t\in V(G)
,
\textnormal{{dist}}_{H}(s,t)\leq(2k-1)\cdot\textnormal{{dist}}_{G}(s,t)
.
∎

Size Analysis of
H
.

Next we prove our desired upper bound on the number of edges in
H
. We begin with the following observation about
H
.

Claim 15
.

H
is a
K_{h}
-minor-free graph with girth
>2k
.

Proof.

Since
G
is
K_{h}
-minor-free,
H
is as well. We can prove
H
has girth
>2k
using a standard argument that we can attribute to
[
ADD+93
]
. Let
C
be a simple cycle in
H
. We will show
|C|>2k
. Consider the edge
e\in C
with highest weight
w(e)
. The path between the endpoints of edge
e
in
C-e
has length at most
(|C|-1)\cdot w(e)
. By the construction of
H
, we would not add edge
e
to
H
if
(|C|-1)\cdot w(e)\leq(2k-1)\cdot w(e)
. We conclude that
|C|>2k
.
∎

Now applying the density increment theorem, we obtain the following general theorem about the number of edges in minor-free graphs with large girth.

Lemma 16
.

For all positive integers
k,h,n
, every
n
-vertex
K_{h}
-minor-free graph with girth
>2k
has at most
O(n\cdot h^{\frac{2}{k+1}}\cdot\text{polylog }h)
edges.

Proof.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex
K_{h}
-minor-free graph with girth
>2k
and average degree
d
. Then by the Density Increment Theorem, there exists a subgraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
with at most
|V(H^{\prime})|\leq(h^{2}/d)\cdot\text{polylog }h
vertices and at least
|E(H^{\prime})|\geq|V(H^{\prime})|\cdot\frac{d}{\text{polylog }h}
. Subgraph
H^{\prime}
has girth
>2k
, so by Theorem
11
,

\frac{d}{\text{polylog }h}\cdot|V(H^{\prime})|\leq|E(H^{\prime})|\leq O(|V(H^{\prime})|^{1+1/k}).

Plugging in
|V(H^{\prime})|\leq(h^{2}/d)\cdot\text{polylog }h
yields the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{\text{polylog }h}

\displaystyle\leq(h^{2}/d)^{1/k}\cdot\text{polylog }h,

and solving for
d
gives us

d\leq h^{2/(k+1)}\cdot\text{polylog }h.

∎

We can now complete the proof of Theorem
13
.

See
13

Proof.

By Claim
14
, Claim
15
, and Lemma
16
, subgraph
H
is a
(2k-1)
-spanner of
G
of size
|E(H)|=O(n\cdot h^{2/(k+1)}\cdot\text{polylog }h)
. What remains is to prove that this upper bound is tight up to
\text{polylog }h
factors under the girth conjecture (Conjecture
12
).

Formally, we will show that for all positive integers
k,h,
and for sufficiently large
n
, there exists an
n
-vertex
K_{h}
-minor-free graph
G
that does not admit a
(2k-1)
-spanner
H
of
G
of size
|E(H)|=\Omega(n\cdot h^{2/(k+1)})
.
If
h=O(1)
, then we can let
G
be a tree. Every spanner
H
of
G
must include every edge of the tree, so
|E(H)|=\Omega(n)
, as desired. Otherwise, if
h
is sufficiently large, then by the girth conjecture there exists a graph
G^{*}
with
|V(G^{*})|=\Theta(h^{2k/(k+1)})
vertices,
|E(G^{*})|\geq\Omega(h^{2})
edges, and girth
>2k
. Moreover, we can guarantee that
|E(G^{*})|<{h\choose 2}
by removing edges from
G^{*}
.

We compose our final lower bound graph
G
by unioning
n/|V(G^{*})|=\Theta\left(\frac{n}{h^{2k/(k+1)}}\right)
disjoint copies of graph
G^{*}
. Graph
G
will have
\Theta(n)
vertices (we can ensure
|V(G)|=n
by deleting or adding vertices as needed). Graph
G
has girth
>2k
and is
K_{h}
-minor-free, since every connected component of
G
has fewer than

G
has girth
>2k
, every
(2k-1)
-spanner

. We conclude that

|E(H)|=|E(G)|=\Theta\left(\frac{n}{h^{2k/(k+1)}}\right)\cdot h^{2}=\Theta(n\cdot h^{2/(k+1)}),

as claimed.
∎

4
Light Multiplicative Spanners in Minor-Free Graphs

In this section we will prove that
K_{h}
-minor-free graphs have multiplicative spanners with small lightness.

Theorem 17

(Minor-Free Spanner Lightness)
.

For all positive integers
k,h,n
, and sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, every
K_{h}
-minor-free weighted graph on
n
vertices admits a
(1+\varepsilon)(2k-1)
-multiplicative spanner with lightness

O\left(h^{\frac{2}{k+1}}\cdot\text{polylog}(h)\cdot\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{3}}\log\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\right).

Our proof will directly follow the proof of Borradaile, Le, and Wulff-Nilsen
[
BLWN17
]
that
K_{h}
-minor-free graphs have
(1+{\varepsilon})
-spanners with lightness
O\left(h\sqrt{\log h}\cdot\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{3}}\log\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\right)
. In fact, our proof will only have two differences from their proof.

•

Construction Difference:
Instead of constructing a greedy
(1+{\varepsilon})
-spanner as in
[
BLWN17
]
, we will construct a greedy
(1+{\varepsilon})(2k-1)
-spanner. (Recall that a greedy spanner is a spanner constructed by the greedy strategy described in
Section

3
.)

•

Analysis Difference:
Instead of arguing that
n
-vertex
K_{h}
-minor-free graphs have
O(nh\sqrt{\log h})
edges, we will apply
Lemma

16
to argue that
n
-vertex
K_{h}
-minor-free graphs with girth
>2k
have at most
O(n\cdot h^{\frac{2}{k+1}}\cdot\text{polylog }h)
edges.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an
n
-vertex
K_{h}
-minor-free weighted graph.
We may assume without loss of generality that for each edge
(u,v)\in E(G)
,
w((u,v))=\textnormal{{dist}}_{G}(u,v)
by removing edges from
G
that do not satisfy this condition.
Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be a positive integer, and let
{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a sufficiently small real number. For ease of presentation, we will construct a
(1+s\cdot{\varepsilon})(2k-1)
-spanner
H\subseteq G
, for a sufficiently large constant
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. This will not affect our bounds in
Theorem

17
because we can always decrease
{\varepsilon}

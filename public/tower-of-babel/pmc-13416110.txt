A nonlinear Takagi-Sugeno-Kang fuzzy system based on information granules for chemical engineering fault diagnosis
Authors: Rui Yin, Yang Lin, Hongxun Shi, Shibao Pang, Ran Tao, Chuankun Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
With the development of information technology, fault diagnosis models based on data-driven technology are playing an increasingly important role in the chemical industry. However, the lack of interpretability in the decision-making process has severely restricted the application of data-driven fault diagnosis models. To address these issues, this paper proposes an interpretable Takagi-Sugeno-Kang(TSK) fuzzy system based on information granules. Here, the antecedent of the TSK is composed of information granules that are used to describe the distribution characteristics of the data, while the consequent is a nonlinear model that aims to explore the hidden relationships between samples and each fault category. The proposed TSK system, which leverages the descriptive capability of information granules and the interpretability of fuzzy rules, is well-suited to addressing the deficiencies of data-driven fault diagnosis models. Furthermore, the gradient descent algorithm is utilized to optimize the system parameters, thereby facilitating the development of a high-performance and interpretable fuzzy fault diagnosis system through extensive data. The effectiveness of this fuzzy system is verified by the Tennessee Eastman(TE) process dataset.

Introduction

Chemical process1 involves flammable, explosive and toxic media. Any malfunction or loss of control in any link may trigger catastrophic accidents2, causing serious losses. Therefore, accurate and efficient fault diagnosis is the key to ensuring production safety. However, modern chemical processes increasingly present the characteristics of large scale, strong nonlinearity and high complexity3, making it difficult to implement traditional diagnostic models based on first-principles models or knowledge-based models. It is even more unable to meet the urgent need for rapid and accurate fault identification. Against this backdrop, how to conduct fault diagnosis in chemical processes efficiently and accurately is of great significance for ensuring the safe and stable operation of chemical processes.

Under the backdrop of digital transformation in the chemical industry, data acquisition systems of instrumentation have matured significantly, propelling industry informatization to new levels. This progress has culminated in the accumulation of vast operational datasets encompassing critical process parameters such as temperature, pressure, and flow rate4. This proliferation of data provides a robust foundation for data-driven fault diagnosis methodologies5. Consequently, a multitude of data-driven fault diagnosis techniques have emerged and undergone substantial development6.

Khan et al.7 developed an integrated PCA-SVM machine learning algorithm. The proposed algorithm used principal component analysis (PCA) for dimensionality reduction and Support Vector Machines (SVM) for chemical process fault diagnosis. Ding et al.8 briefly reviewed the application of PCA in fault diagnosis and proposed a PCA-based fault diagnosis framework featuring a statistical test comparable to Hawkins’ \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_H^2$$\end{document} statistic. Elshenawy et al.9 proposed a novel K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) algorithm combined with Fuzzy C-Means Clustering (FCM) for fault diagnosis in industrial processes. It dramatically reduced computational burden by leveraging cluster prototypes instead of original samples for relationship calculations. Nevertheless, these models face inherent limitations: (1) PCA-based techniques10 are ill-suited for non-Gaussian distributions; (2) KNN11 exhibits compromised performance with imbalanced/high-dimensional data; (3) SVMs12 poorly accommodate nonlinear relationships and large-scale datasets. Furthermore, conventional ML approaches lack intrinsic feature learning mechanisms, substantially constraining their performance and practical deployment in evolving data environments. With advancements in deep learning13, models including convolutional neural networks (CNNs)14, recurrent neural networks (RNNs)15, attention mechanisms16, and autoencoders have witnessed rapid development in chemical fault diagnosis applications17. This is mainly due to the fact that neural network models can overcome the reliance on theoretical models and expert experience.18 Among them, Zhao et al.19 proposed an enhanced ResNet-Deep Residual Shrinkage Network (DRSN), which employs SeNet to automatically set thresholds. Through soft thresholding, it can effectively eliminate redundant information, making the model more suitable for classification tasks involving noisy data. Ren et al.20 employed deep residual convolutional networks for fault classification, where hierarchical convolutions simultaneously learn local and global features to improve diagnostic accuracy.

Deep learning has significant advantages in enhancing the performance of fault diagnosis. Nevertheless, its decision-making processes remain inherently black-box nature–a critical limitation for deployment in chemical engineering contexts. Therefore, there is an urgent need to explore fault diagnosis models that are both interpretable and high-performing. Fuzzy models21, due to their use of IF-THEN rules, provide a way to understand the reasoning process and have natural interpretability. This meets people’s needs to understand the reasoning process. Therefore, fuzzy systems and their integration with other algorithms have become a powerful supplement to other fault diagnosis models. In addition, Table 1 summarizes the relevant literature, where Li et al.22 pioneered an interpretable fault diagnosis framework for power systems. They used a genetically-optimized adaptive GSK algorithm to enhance TSK fuzzy modeling. Sourabh Dash et al.23 proposed a fuzzy-logic based trend classifier, identifying the most likely fault candidates based on a similarity measure. However, a simple fuzzy algorithm is characterized by performance limitations and thus proves insufficient to meet practical requirements. Meanwhile, traditional fuzzy models such as TSK face the challenge of determining an appropriate number of rules, which adds to modeling complexity. In order to improve the performance of fuzzy systems, integrating fuzzy logic with neural networks presents a key research direction24. Norazwan et al.25 proposed a multi-scale kernel Fisher discriminant analysis method with adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS). This approach employed multi-scale KFDA for feature extraction, while ANFIS models are developed for each designated fault pattern to simultaneously increase classification accuracy and reduce diagnostic error rates. Xu et al.26 proposed a fuzzy neural network (FNN) for rotating machinery fault diagnosis that synergistically integrates fuzzy theory with neural networks. This architecture employs fuzzification processing of input/output feature vectors to enhance fault identification precision. A review of previous studies for fault detection using fuzzy methods.

To achieve a high-performance and interpretable fault diagnosis of the chemical process, this paper proposes a novel TSK fuzzy system based on information granules. This framework integrates information granules with neural networks through a fuzzy system architecture, achieving mapping from data to faults. Within this framework, multiple information granules are first constructed in the data space to describe the data distributions. Considering the proportion of each fault category (categorical distribution), these granules maintain balanced attention across different fault types. In this context, the granules form the antecedents of TSK fuzzy rules, significantly enhancing the descriptive capability for individual fault classes while ensuring rule interpretability. Furthermore, to boost the performance of the TSK model, the rule consequents equip multiple neural networks that strengthen feature learning. The parameters of these neural networks are optimized via gradient descent algorithms, enabling knowledge acquisition from data and establishing a high-performance fault diagnosis mechanism. The contributions include the following aspects:A data characterization capability evaluation algorithm for information granules is developed to identify the optimal quantity and distribution of information granules within the data space, thereby facilitating a reasonable determination of the rule count in fuzzy systems and reducing the dependence on expert knowledge.The antecedent of fuzzy rules is constructed by categorical distribution-based information granules. This approach integrates granule semantics with rule interpretability, enhances the characterization capability of rules, and ensures balanced attention to each fault category.By integrating information granules and neural networks, a high-performance TSK system was constructed. Furthermore, the entire decision-making process retains the form of fuzzy rules, striking a balance between enhanced performance and preserved interpretability.

In fault diagnosis applications, the TSK system determines the fault category of input data based on its final output Y. For binary classification tasks – such as distinguishing between faulty and normal states – label of input data is assigned according to the sign of Y. When diagnosing multiple fault types (multi-class classification), the task can either be decomposed into binary classifications, or the consequent of TSK can be modified to implement a multi-output mapping function. This adaptation yields separate diagnostic outputs corresponding to each potential fault category.

Information granule

The information granule38 is an entity of information which represents the distribution of data by extracting inherent information. It plays a vital role in human cognition and decision-making process. Based on Zadeh’s seminal introduction of granular concepts, Pedrycz et al.39 pioneered in-depth research on information granules under the theory of fuzzy sets, and proposed the concepts of coverage and specificity which establish a reasonable evaluation system for information granules.

The proposed model

The fault diagnosis investigated in this paper aims to identify fault types in chemical processes through observed data, and can be essentially regarded as a multi-class classification task. The observed data are denoted as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X=\left[ x_1,x_2,\ldots ,x_n\right]$$\end{document}, representing the set of monitoring point measurements at a given time, where n is the number of monitoring points. The fault types, consisting of both faulty and normal states, are denoted as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Y=\left[ y_1, y_2, \ldots , y_K\right]$$\end{document}, with K being the number of fault types. The core of fault diagnosis is to establish a mapping from observed data X to fault labels Y, denoted as:8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} y = F(X) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}here, When y is consistent with the actual fault types, the model F(· ) can be considered to have good fault diagnosis capability. The proposed model aims to eliminate the time and effort typically required for manual experience-based fault troubleshooting, thereby enabling rapid and efficient resolution. In this context, the construction process of the proposed model will be explained in detail in the following section.

The blueprint in Fig. 1 illustrates the training and testing processes of the model. In the training process, it mainly includes the construction of information granules and fuzzy rules. Specifically, an iterative approach is employed to build several sets of information granules with varying quantities. And, a set of information granules is selected by a data characterization capability evaluation algorithm. Subsequently, the antecedents of TSK’s rules are constructed based on the selected information granules, and the consequents of these rules correspond to several multi-layer neural networks. The parameters of these neural networks are optimized through optimization algorithms. As a result, the optimized TSK model establishes a mapping from data to faults. At last, the fault diagnosis task is performed by the optimized TSK.

Construction of information granules

Information granules are introduced to enhance the data characterization capability of the model, and help to build the fuzzy rules. The TSK system based on information granules

With the construction of information granules in the data space completed, the proposed model starts to establish the TSK system. First, the antecedents of the rules are built based on these information granules. At this stage, the number of rules equals to the number of granules. As a result, the selection of the number of rules is free from artificial specification. For the consequents of these rules, each employs a multilayer perceptron (MLP) to enhance the model’s feature learning capabilities. In this chapter, suppose that P information granules are constructed on the training set to describe the data distribution, the resulting rules are defined as follows:11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \text {Rule}\ {{R}_{p}}:\ \text {If}\ \textbf{x}_{i}\ belongs\ to\ A(G_p),\ \text {Then}\ y_p = NN(\textbf{x}_i) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where p=1, 2, … , P. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$A(G_p)$$\end{document} is a fuzzy set defined based on \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_p$$\end{document}, which equips the rule with the description capability of information granules. In the proposed model, the MLP comprises n input nodes, m hidden nodes and K output nodes, where n is the number of features and K is the number of classes. Training of TSK fuzzy system

The model establishment process primarily consists of determining the model structure and its parameters. In the proposed model, the number of TSK rules is directly defined by the number of information granules, while the structure of the neural network in the rule consequents is configured manually. Specifically, each MLP is designed with an input layer size equal to the number of data features, a manually specified number of hidden layer nodes, and an output layer size corresponding to the number of fault types. Consequently, once the number of information granules is fixed, the overall model structure is fully determined. Regarding model parameters, the fuzzy sets in the rule antecedents are derived from the centers and radii of the information granules. Therefore, the key parameters requiring optimization are those within the MLPs located in the rule consequents.

Case studies

In this section, Case studies based on TE process dataset are used to demonstrate the characteristics and evaluate the performance of the proposed model.

Dataset and evaluation metrics

The TE process dataset40 is a benchmark for testing control and fault diagnosis models in complex industries. It includes separate training and test datasets. During the process, aside from the 19 component process variables in the data, all other variables were utilized. The fault types considered included numbers 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19, and 20, as the remaining types were deemed unsuitable for analysis via a data-driven approach41. In addition, the training dataset was split into a training subset and a validation subset in an 8:2 ratio for model training. And the data from testing dataset are used to evaluate the performance of the optimal model.

Here, the fault detection rate(FDR), average missing alarm rate(aMAR), accuracy and F1-score are used to evaluate the performance of the models. Among these metrics, the FDR of a fault category is the proportion of correctly identified fault data within the total data of this fault. The aMAR indicates the ratio of undetected fault data to the total number of faulty samples. The accuracy, a commonly used classification metric, reflects the proportion of correctly classified samples out to the total number of samples. And, the F1-score, also called the F-measure, is a key classification metric specifically designed to balance the trade-off between precision and recall.

Configuration of models

In these Case studies, the search space for the number of information granules in TSK-IG was [2, 30], while the number of hidden nodes in each consequent part was manually set to 40 to reduce training costs. The K-nearest neighbors(KNN)11, zero-order TSK(TSK-0)42 and first-order TSK(TSK-1)43 were selected as baseline classifiers in the comparative studies as the proposed model is an improved TSK system. These classifiers were realized in python. For KNN, its number of neighbors was set as 10 and other parameters used their default configurations. The TSK-0 is an improved version for the TE process dataset, whose consequent part of each rule consists 18 nodes, and the number of rules was set as 5. For TSK-1, the consequent part of each rule is a group of linear functions and the number of rules was set to 5. Moreover, the parameters of these classifiers were also optimized by the training dataset.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a novel TSK fuzzy system based on information granules. In its fuzzy rules, information granules are utilized to form the antecedent part. These information granules, constructed based on categorical distribution, serve to augment the capacity of the rules to characterize data distributions and aid in the determination of the number of rules. Meanwhile, neural networks are introduced in the consequent part to enhance the nonlinear feature learning ability of the rules. The parameters of these neural networks are optimized by the AdamW gradient descent algorithm. By integrating information granules with neural networks through a fuzzy system architecture, a fault diagnosis model with satisfactory performance and interpretability is established. Results show that the proposed model achieves favorable recognition performance across various fault types in the Tennessee Eastman process dataset. Compared to classical TSK-0 and TSK-1, the proposed TSK significantly improves the performance of fault diagnosis while ensuring interpretability. The ablation studies reveal that the introduction of information granules and neural networks has a positive effect on improving the performance of the fuzzy model. In future research, we will focus on deepening the model to explore whether deeper networks will significantly improve model performance.

Shuffle Products, Degenerate Affine Hecke Algebras, and Quantum Toda Lattice
Artem KALMYKOV

Abstract
We revisit an identification of the quantum Toda lattice for GL N \mathrm{GL}_{N} and the truncated shifted Yangian of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} , as well as related constructions, from a purely algebraic point of view, bypassing the topological medium of the homology of the affine Grassmannian. For instance, we interpret the Gerasimov–Kharchev–Lebedev–Oblezin homomorphism into the algebra of difference operators via a finite analog of the Miura transform. This algebraic identification is deduced by relating degenerate affine Hecke algebras to the simplest example of a rational Feigin–Odesskii shuffle product. As a bonus, we obtain a presentation of the latter via a mirabolic version of the Kostant–Whittaker reduction.

Introduction

Shuffle algebras are ubiquitous in representation theory. To name a few applications, they are related to infinite-dimensional quantum groups (including quantum toroidal algebras), Hall algebras, canonical bases, and so on. They were introduced by Feigin–Odesskii in a series of papers
[
12
,
40
,
41
]
. In the simplest case, a shuffle algebra is, as a vector space, the direct sum
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{O}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k})^{S_{k}}
of suitably defined symmetric functions in
k
variables, with the product

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k})^{S_{k}}\times\mathcal{O}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{l})^{S_{l}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k+l})^{S_{k+l}},

(1.1)

\displaystyle(f*g)(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k+l})=\frac{1}{(k+l)!}\mathrm{Sym}_{k+l}\biggl(\prod_{i<j}\!\frac{\theta(Y_{i}-Y_{j}+1)}{\theta(Y_{i}\!-\!Y_{j})}f(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k})g(Y_{k+1},\dots,Y_{k+l})\!\biggr),

where
\mathrm{Sym}_{k+l}
is the symmetrization operator in
k+l
variables, and
\theta(u)
is one of the functions

\theta^{\mathrm{ell}}(u)=\vartheta_{1}(u|\tau),\qquad\theta^{\mathrm{trig}}(u)=\sin(\pi u/\tau),\qquad\theta^{\mathrm{rat}}(u)=u,

(1.2)

corresponding to the elliptic, trigonometric, and rational cases, for some parameter
\tau
. Here
\vartheta_{1}(u|\tau)
is the first Jacobi theta-function. For instance, in the original construction of loc. cit., the function space is

\mathcal{O}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k})^{S_{k}}={}^{N}\!\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{ell}}_{k}:=S^{k}H^{0}(\Theta),\qquad{}^{N}\!\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{ell}}=\bigoplus_{k}{}^{N}\!\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{ell}}_{k},

the sum of symmetric powers of global sections of a degree
N
line bundle on an elliptic curve (put differently, degree
N
theta functions), and the product is determined by the factor
\theta^{\mathrm{ell}}(u)
, up to shift of variables.

In this paper, we study the rational case. The elliptic one above admits a degeneration to it
[
10
]
so that

{}^{N}\!\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}=\bigoplus_{k}{}^{N}\!\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\mathrm{rat}},\qquad{}^{N}\!\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}_{k}=\mathbf{C}[Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}]^{S_{k}}_{<N},

where
\mathbf{C}[Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}]^{S_{k}}_{<N}
is the space of symmetric polynomials of degree less than
N
in each variable, and the factor is
\theta^{\mathrm{rat}}(u)
. More generally, one can drop the restriction on the degree and consider
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}=\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}_{k}
with
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}_{k}=\mathbf{C}[Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}]^{S_{k}}
.

The first goal of the paper is to relate these shuffle algebras to
degenerate affine Hecke algebras
whose definition we recall in Section
3.2
. The idea is as follows: observe that without the theta factor, the shuffle product is simply the multiplication on the symmetric algebra of the vector space
\mathbf{C}[Y_{1}]
or its
N
-th truncation. Instead of deforming it via inserting a factor, we can deform the symmetrization itself, that is, the action of the symmetric group. One such deformation is provided by the
Demazure operators
, so that a simple transposition acts on
\mathbf{C}[Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}]
by

\sigma_{i}=P_{i,i+1}+\frac{1-P_{i,i+1}}{Y_{i}-Y_{i+1}},

where
P_{i,i+1}
permutes variables
Y_{i}
and
Y_{i+1}
. Denoting by
e_{k}\in\mathbf{C}[S_{k}]
the symmetrizer idempotent in the group algebra of the symmetric group, this is the action of
S_{k}
on the spherical module
\mathfrak{H}_{k}e_{k}\cong\mathbf{C}[Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}]
of the degenerate affine Hecke algebra
\mathfrak{H}_{k}
. Its spherical subalgebra
e_{k}\mathfrak{H}_{k}e_{k}
can be identified with the symmetric polynomials
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}_{k}
.

Based on this observation, one can introduce an a priori different shuffle-like product on the direct sum
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}_{k}
via, as we mentioned, symmetrization with respect to this deformed action:

(f,g)\mapsto f*^{\mathrm{Hecke}}g:=e_{k+l}f(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k})g(Y_{k+1},\dots,Y_{k+l})e_{k+l}\in e_{k+l}\mathfrak{H}_{k+l}e_{k+l}\cong\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}_{k+l}.

The following result of Theorem
3.11
seems to be folklore, but, unfortunately, we were not able to find a reference for its algebraic proof. A geometric proof can be found in an unpublished preprint by Grojnowski
[
23
]
.

Theorem A
.

The rational Feigin–Odesskii shuffle product coincides with the one induced from degenerate affine Hecke algebras.

In principle, the theorem follows directly from the explicit formula for the symmetrizer in the spherical representation of a degenerate affine Hecke algebra and, strictly speaking, is not new: for instance, in the trigonometric case, it reflects a well-known relation between Hall–Littlewood polynomials and affine Hecke algebras
[
36
]
. Our main contribution is an alternative proof based on the following simple observation: a canonical
affinization
procedure
[
32
]
, which, in general, produces a solution of a
quantum Yang–Baxter equation

R_{12}(z_{1}-z_{2})R_{13}(z_{1}-z_{3})R_{23}(z_{2}-z_{3})=R_{23}(z_{2}-z_{3})R_{13}(z_{1}-z_{3})R_{12}(z_{1}-z_{2}),

out of a representation of symmetric group (or, in the trigonometric case, of a finite Hecke algebra), applied to the spherical module gives a particular example of a
Shibukawa–Ueno
R
-operator
introduced in
[
44
]
whose definition we recall in Section
3.1
. In general, the latter acts on the space of suitably defined functions and takes as an input any solution to the three-term relation (
3.1
); in particular, the case of the spherical module of a degenerate affine Hecke algebra corresponds to the rational factor in (
1.2
), while that of a non-degenerate affine Hecke algebra – to the trigonometric one. Then the theorem is deduced by, on the one hand, representing the symmetrizer though a certain combination of
R
-matrices, on the other hand, expressing this combination as the usual symmetrization map, but with a factor akin to (
1.1
), see Proposition
3.3
.

The main advantage of this proof is that the second part works uniformly for any factor that satisfies the three-term relation. In principle, there are (formal) analogs of theta functions for arbitrary generalized cohomology theory with orientation
[
42
]
. Unfortunately, we do not know how the respective Shibukawa–Ueno
R
-operators are related to the corresponding
formal (affine) Hecke algebras

[
24
]
, however, we believe that similar formulas should hold in more general cases.

In particular, it would be interesting to study the elliptic version of the Shibukawa–Ueno operator and its potential relation to
elliptic affine Hecke algebras

[
21
]
. Curiously, the elliptic case admits a dual description on the
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-side, in the sense of
Fourier–Mukai transform
on an elliptic curve. Namely, by
[
13
]
, the elliptic Shibukawa–Ueno
R
-operator is gauge-equivalent to
Belavin’s
R
-matrix

[
2
,
8
]
whose quasi-periodic properties can be interpreted via an essentially unique stable vector bundle of rank
N
and degree 1 on an elliptic curve. A natural question is: what is the meaning of the shuffle product on the
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-side?

We approach this question for the rational case in the second part of the paper where we apply the construction of the first part to the
quantum Toda lattice
for
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
. The idea to use degenerate affine Hecke algebras is inspired by the results of
[
5
]
where the authors constructed and proved a K-theoretic version of the geometric Satake equivalence for a certain subcategory of the
q
-analog of Harish-Chandra bimodules (we recall the rational versions below). Following the Kazhdan–Kostant formulation of this integrable system
[
33
]
, let us consider a non-degenerate character
\psi
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}_{+}
of the subgroup of upper unitriangular matrices
N_{+}
. The action of
\mathfrak{n}_{+}
via right- and left-invariant vector fields gives rise to a homomorphism
{\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{n}_{+})\otimes\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{n}_{+})\rightarrow\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})}
into the algebra of differential operators. Then the Toda Hamiltonians are realized as the image of bi-invariant differential operators, which can be identified with the center
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
of
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
, inside the quantum Hamiltonian reduction
N^{L}_{+}\,\backslash\!\!\backslash_{-\psi}\,\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})/\!\!/_{\psi}\,N_{+}^{R}
with respect to the latter embedding and the character
(-\psi,\psi)
. This is an example of the
Kostant–Whittaker reduction
for Harish-Chandra bimodules studied in
[
3
]
.

According to
[
4
, Appendix B]
, the Toda lattice can be identified with a certain quotient
\mathrm{Y}^{0}_{-2N}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
of the
shifted Yangian

\mathrm{Y}_{-2N}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
whose definition we recall in Section
3.3
. For introduction, we will only use the fact that it is generated by the coefficients of the series

x_{+}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{+}^{(i)}u^{-i},\qquad x_{-}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{-}^{(i)}u^{-i},\qquad h(u)=u^{-2N}+\sum_{i=2N+1}^{\infty}h^{(i)}u^{-i},

and that the coefficients of
x_{+}(u)
generate the subalgebra isomorphic to the shuffle algebra
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}

[
45
]
. Therefore, it gives a
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-presentation of
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}
. However, citing
[
14
]
, “we must admit that the identification of the quantum Toda [lattice] for
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
and the [truncated] shifted Yangian for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
(purely algebraic objects) goes through a topological medium: equivariant homology of the affine Grassmannian of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
”; this additional step is the
derived Satake equivalence
of
[
3
]
. For instance, while
\mathrm{Y}_{-2N}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
is defined via generators and relations, this identification does not specify explicitly the images of the former.

The goal of the second part of the paper is to apply the results of the first part to construct and prove an isomorphism

\mathrm{Y}^{0}_{-2N}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})\xrightarrow{\sim}N^{L}_{+}\,\backslash\!\!\backslash_{-\psi}\,\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})/\!\!/_{\psi}\,N_{+}^{R},

as well as related constructions, in a purely algebraic and explicit way, bypassing the topological medium, and “refine” it to a
mirabolic
version of the Kostant–Whittaker reduction. It is based on another classical fact. For any
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-representation
V
, the free left
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
-module
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes V
has a right
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
action defined from the diagonal group action of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
on the tensor product. This is an example of a
(free) Harish-Chandra bimodule
whose definition and properties we review in Section
4
. For the tensor product of the vector representation
V=\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)^{\otimes k}
, there is an action of the degenerate affine Hecke algebra
\mathfrak{H}_{k}
on
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)^{\otimes k}
respecting the
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
-bimodule structure. In particular, its spherical subalgebra
\mathcal{S}_{k}\cong e_{k}\mathfrak{H}_{k}e_{k}
acts on the
S_{k}
-invariants that can be identified with the free Harish-Chandra bimodule
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes S^{k}\mathbf{C}^{N}
associated to the
k
-th symmetric power. By restricting the action on the highest-weight vector for all
k
, one obtains a map of vector spaces

\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}=\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}_{k}\rightarrow\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\ltimes S^{\bullet}\mathbf{C}^{N}/\!\!/_{\psi}\,N_{+},

where the target is the quantum Hamiltonian reduction of the semi-direct product with
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
acting on the symmetric algebra
S^{\bullet}\mathbf{C}^{N}
in the standard way. In particular, it is an algebra as well. If we denote by
\mathfrak{m}_{N}
the
mirabolic subalgebra
of the subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
preserving the dual of the last basis vector in
\mathbf{C}^{N}
(see Section
6.3
for precise definition), then it turns out that this map restricts to a similarly defined quantum Hamiltonian reduction

{}^{N}\!\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}\rightarrow\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{m}_{N})\ltimes S^{\bullet}\mathbf{C}^{N}/\!\!/_{\psi}\,N_{+}.

(1.3)

The following is Theorems
6.14
and
6.9
; here
\mathrm{Y}_{-2N}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})^{\geq 0}
is the non-negative part of the shifted Yangian. In fact, the explicit formulas for the second homomorphism are close to another presentation of the quantization of Zastava spaces for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
from
[
15
, Section 3.22]
.

Theorem B
.

The map
{}^{N}\!\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{rat}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{m}_{N})\ltimes S^{\bullet}\mathbf{C}^{N}/\!\!/_{\psi}\,N_{+}
is an algebra isomorphism. It can be extended to a surjective algebra homomorphism
\mathrm{Y}_{-2N}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})^{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\ltimes S^{\bullet}\mathbf{C}^{N}/\!\!/_{\psi}\,N_{+}
.

Unfortunately, we do not know if and how this result can be related to the aforementioned stable bundles on an elliptic curve. We expect (or, rather, hope) that it could viewed as a quantum rational version of a certain algebra of Hecke modifications of semistable bundles of degree 0, see Remark
6.15
. In general, it would be interesting to interpret this result at least on the classical level in terms of elliptic analog of the Kostant slice
[
18
]
.

Besides degenerate affine Hecke algebras, the other main tool to prove the theorem is the
Kirillov projector

P_{\psi}
introduced in
[
27
]
. This is an element in a certain completion of
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{m}_{N})
that acts on Whittaker modules and projects onto the space of Whittaker vectors (that is, on which
\mathfrak{n}_{+}
acts with character
\psi
); as such, it is an analog of the classical
extremal projector

[
1
]
in the case of trivial character. For instance, the Kirillov projector gives a canonical isomorphism
V\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{m}_{N})\otimes V/\!\!/_{\psi}\,N_{+}
of any
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-representation
V
with the mirabolic version of the Kostant–Whittaker reduction. It turns out that this isomorphism has rather peculiar properties with respect to the degenerate affine Hecke algebra action mentioned above, see Section
6.3
. For example, under the isomorphism (
1.3
), the standard monomial basis of
S^{\bullet}\mathbf{C}^{N}
is mapped to a rational version of the Hall–Littlewood polynomials, see Example
6.12
.

We extend this homomorphism to the Toda lattice. Naturally,
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
is embedded into the matrix space; denote by
X=(x_{ji})
the (transposed) matrix with
x_{ij}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
the
(i,j)
-matrix entry function. Its inverse
X^{-1}
is well-defined in
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
. Also, denote by
E^{R}=\bigl(E_{ij}^{R}\bigr)
the matrix composed of left-invariant vector fields associated to the standard generators of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
. The following is Theorem
8.10
. It would interesting to compare this explicit form with the approach of
[
14
]
via coproducts on both sides.

Theorem C
.

There is an isomorphism
\mathrm{Y}^{0}_{-2N}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})\xrightarrow{\sim}N^{L}_{+}\,\backslash\!\!\backslash_{-\psi}\,\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})/\!\!/_{\psi}\,N_{+}^{R}
defined on generating currents by

\displaystyle x_{+}(u)\mapsto-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\bigl[\bigl(E^{R}\bigr)^{i}X\bigr]_{1N}u^{-i-1},\qquad x_{-}(u)\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\bigl[X^{-1}\bigl(E^{R}\bigr)^{i}\bigr]_{1N}u^{-i-1},

\displaystyle h(u)\mapsto\frac{1}{A(u)A(u-1)},

where the subscript
(1N)
means the corresponding entry in the matrix product and
A(u)
is a
quantum determinant
whose coefficients generate
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
.

One of the main ideas of the proof is to interpret the
GKLO homomorphism
from
\mathrm{Y}_{-2N}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
into a certain localization
{}^{N}\!\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
of the algebra of difference operators, introduced in
[
20
]
for non-shifted Yangians and later studied in
[
4
,

]
in general case, via a finite analog of the
Miura transform

[

]
, see Section

. In general, for any Harish-Chandra bimodule
X
, it relates the Kostant–Whittaker reduction
X/\!\!/_{\psi}\,N_{+}
to its version with the trivial character, the
parabolic restriction

X^{\mathrm{gen}}/\!\!/N_{-}
, which we review in Section

\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}\rightarrow\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})\cong(\mathfrak{n}_{-}\backslash\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}))^{N_{-}}
. In particular, it gives an injective homomorphism

N^{L}_{+}\,\backslash\!\!\backslash_{-\psi}\,\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})/\!\!/_{\psi}\,N^{R}_{+}\rightarrow N^{L}_{+}\,\backslash\!\!\backslash_{-\psi}\,\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{gen}}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})/\!\!/N_{-}^{R}.

The following is Theorem
8.8
and the second part of Theorem

.

Theorem D
.

There is an isomorphism
{}^{N}\!\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\cong N^{L}_{+}\,\backslash\!\!\backslash_{-\psi}\,\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{gen}}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})/\!\!/N_{-}^{R}
such that the composition

\mathrm{Y}^{0}_{-2N}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})\xrightarrow{\sim}N^{L}_{+}\,\backslash\!\!\backslash_{-\psi}\,\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})/\!\!/_{\psi}\,N^{R}_{+}\rightarrow N^{L}_{+}\,\backslash\!\!\backslash_{-\psi}\,\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{gen}}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})/\!\!/N_{-}^{R}\xrightarrow{\sim}{}^{N}\!\tilde{\mathcal{A}}

Future directions

The results of the paper should be more or less directly generalizable to the trigonometric, or
q
-deformed, case: for instance, see
[
16
]
for geometric approach,
[
5
]
for a certain version of
K
-theoretic Satake equivalence, and
[
28
]
and references therein for quantum Harish-Chandra bimodules and the corresponding parabolic restriction. In fact, the only missing ingredient is a
q
-analog of the Kostant–Whittaker reduction which, to the best of our knowledge, was not yet defined; however, see
[
43
]
for the
q
-analog of the quantum Toda lattice.

Our original motivation, however, was the elliptic version, where various representation-theoretic constructions should acquire much clearer algebro-geometric meaning, but, unfortunately, much less is known. For instance, to the best of our knowledge, there is no definition of “elliptic” Harish-Chandra bimodules, although there is a strong evidence that such a category should exist, see the discussion in the introduction of
[
28
]
. We believe that the results of this paper can give a hint how to approach this problem at least in type A. For example, as the results of Section
6
combined with calculations with Shibukawa–Ueno
R
-operators of Section
3
suggest, the action of degenerate affine Hecke algebras plays a crucial role to study the category of Harish-Chandra bimodules, therefore, it is reasonable to search for their elliptic generalization in terms of representations of elliptic affine Hecke algebras
[
48
]
. In general, as Remark
6.15
indicates, it would be interesting to study further this interplay between representation theory and, in a certain sense, “quantization” of various algebro-geometric constructions.

Structure of the paper

In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary notations concerning
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
and differential operators
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
as well quantum minors. In Section
3
, we recall basic properties of shifted Yangians, degenerate affine Hecke algebras, and relate the latter to Shibukawa–Ueno
R
-operators and shuffle algebras. In Section
4
, we recall the definition of Harish-Chandra bimodules and degenerate affine Hecke algebras action on the free ones associated to tensor powers of the vector representation. In Section
5
, we study quantum Hamiltonian reduction with a trivial character, the parabolic restriction. In Section
6
, we study the Kostant–Whittaker reduction and its mirabolic version relating them to degenerate affine Hecke algebras, shuffle algebras, and (the positive part of) shifted Yangians. In Section
7
, we relate two reductions via finite Miura transform and interpret it as a GKLO map. In Section
8
, we prove an isomorphism between truncated shifted Yangians and Toda lattice, compare it with the geometric identification via homology of the affine Grassmannian, and interpret monopole operators in terms certain minor functions. In Appendix
A
, we prove some technical lemmas from the main sections. While in the main body of the paper we deal with the vector representation, in Appendix
B
, we present the corresponding facts for the dual that are needed for the full Toda lattice.

\hbar=1
in the introduction.

2.1
Notations for
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}

In this subsection, we fix some notations regarding the group
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
.

Let
\mathbf{C}^{N}
be the vector space with a basis
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{N}\}
. Denote by
\{\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{N}\}
the dual basis of
\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)^{*}
such that
\langle v_{i},\phi_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
. Let
\mathrm{Mat}_{N\times N}
be the affine space of
N\times N
matrices. We identify its space of functions with the polynomial algebra via

\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{Mat}_{N\times N})\cong\mathbf{C}[x_{ij}\mid i,j=1,\dots,N],\qquad x_{ij}(A)=\langle A\cdot v_{j},\phi_{i}\rangle,\quad A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{N\times N},

and we combine the generators into a matrix

X=(x_{ji})_{i=1,\dots,N}^{j=1,\dots,N}\in\mathbf{C}[x_{ij}]\otimes\mathrm{End}\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)

(observe the transposition).

Let
\mathrm{GL}_{N}\subset\mathrm{Mat}_{N\times N}
be the group of invertible matrices. Its ring of functions is the localization of
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{Mat}_{N\times N})
by the determinant function, in particular, we have an embedding
\mathbf{C}[x_{ij}]\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
. We call the image the
polynomial functions
. In addition, there are entries of the matrix inverse
X^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})\otimes\mathrm{End}\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)
such that

\bigl(X^{-1}\bigr)_{ij}(A)=\langle v_{i},A\phi_{j}\rangle=\bigl\langle A^{-1}v_{i},\phi_{j}\bigr\rangle.

We consider
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
as a left
\mathrm{GL}_{N}\times\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-module via the corresponding actions

\bigl(g^{R}\cdot f\bigr)(A)=f(Ag),\qquad\bigl(g^{L}\cdot f\bigr)(A)=f\bigl(g^{-1}A\bigr).

In particular, for every
\alpha=1,\dots,N
,
we have equivariant embeddings

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{}^{R}\iota_{\alpha}\colon\ S^{\bullet}\mathbf{C}^{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{N}),\qquad v_{i}\mapsto X_{i\alpha},\\
&{}^{R}\iota_{\alpha}^{*}\colon\ S^{\bullet}\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{N}),\qquad\phi_{i}\mapsto\bigl(X^{-1}\bigr)_{\alpha i}\end{split}

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{}^{L}\iota_{\alpha}\colon\ S^{\bullet}\mathbf{C}^{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{N}),\qquad v_{i}\mapsto\bigl(X^{-1}\bigr)_{i\alpha},\\
&{}^{L}\iota_{\alpha}^{*}\colon\ S^{\bullet}\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{N}),\qquad\phi_{i}\mapsto X_{\alpha i},\end{split}

(2.2)

with respect to the action corresponding to the superscript.

Denote by
H_{N}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{N}
the subgroup of diagonal matrices. Its set of weights
\Lambda
is given by

\mathbb{Z}^{N}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{N})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda,\qquad\epsilon_{i}(\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{N}))=t_{i}.

We will always identify the weight and the coweight lattices, we hope it will not lead to any confusion. Denote by
\Lambda^{+}\subset\Lambda
the monoid of dominant weights:

\Lambda^{+}=\bigl\{(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\mid\mu_{i}\geq\mu_{i+1},\,i=1,\dots N-1,\bigr\},

which is spanned by the fundamental weights

\omega_{i}:=(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{i},0,\dots,0).

Let

\Delta=\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\mid i\neq j\}\subset\Lambda,\qquad\Delta_{+}=\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\mid i<j\}\subset\Lambda^{+}

be the set of roots (respectively positive roots). We denote by

\rho_{N}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\alpha=\sum_{i}\omega_{i}=(N-1,\dots,1,0)

(2.3)

the half-sum of positive roots.

Let
N_{+}
(resp.
N_{-}
) be the subgroups of upper unitriangular (resp. lower unitriangular) matrices. For
\lambda\in\Lambda
, denote by
V_{\lambda}
the irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
whose
N_{+}
-invariant highest weight vector

has weight
\lambda
. In particular,

\displaystyle V_{(1,0,\dots,0)}=\mathbf{C}^{N},

\displaystyle\qquad v_{(1,0,\dots,0)}=v_{1},

\displaystyle V_{(0,\dots,0,-1)}=\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)^{*},

\displaystyle\qquad v_{(0,\dots,0,-1)}=\phi_{N},

We identify its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
with
\mathrm{End}\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)
via matrix units such that the action on
\mathbf{C}^{N}
and
\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)^{*}
is given by

\mathfrak{gl}_{N}=\mathrm{span}(E_{ij}\mid i,j=1,\dots,N),\qquad E_{ij}\cdot v_{k}=\delta_{jk}v_{i},\qquad E_{ij}\cdot\phi_{k}=-\delta_{ik}\phi_{j}.

We denote by the same letters the corresponding generators of the (asymptotic) universal enveloping algebra
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
in the sense of Definition
4.1
; in particular,

E_{ij}E_{kl}-E_{kl}E_{ij}=\hbar(\delta_{jk}E_{il}-\delta_{li}E_{kj}).

We combine the generators into a matrix

E=\begin{pmatrix}E_{11}&\dots&E_{1,N-1}&E_{1N}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
E_{N-1,1}&\dots&E_{N-1,N-1}&E_{N-1,N}\\
E_{N1}&\dots&E_{N,N-1}&E_{NN}\end{pmatrix},

(2.4)

The Lie algebras of

N_{-}

\mathfrak{n}_{+}=\mathrm{span}(E_{ij}\mid i<j),\qquad\mathfrak{n}_{-}=\mathrm{span}(E_{ij}\mid i>j),\qquad\mathfrak{h}_{N}=\mathrm{span}(E_{ii}).

We will often use an antiautomorphism

(2.5)

The goal of this section is to establish a relation between rational Feigin–Odesskii shuffle algebras and degenerate affine Hecke algebras summarized in Theorem

3.1

-operator

In this subsection, we recall the definition of Shibukawa–Ueno

Let

\displaystyle\theta(x+y)\theta(x-y)\theta(z+w)\theta(z-w)+\theta(x+z)\theta(x-z)\theta(w+y)\theta(w-y)

Let

\displaystyle{\times}\,\prod_{i=1}^{l}G^{\theta}(z_{0}-z_{i},\kappa)\cdot\bigl(G^{\theta}(z_{0}-z_{l+1},\kappa)+G^{\theta}(z_{l+1}-z_{0},l\kappa+\kappa)P_{0,l+1}\bigr).

\displaystyle(1+P_{01}+\dots+P_{0l})\prod_{i=1}^{l}G^{\theta}(z_{0}-z_{i},\kappa)\cdot G^{\theta}(z_{l+1}-z_{0},l\kappa+\kappa)P_{0,l+1}

\displaystyle\qquad=P_{0,l+1}\prod_{i=1}^{l+1}G^{\theta}(z_{0}-z_{i},\kappa).

The right-hand side is
\prod_{i=0}^{l}G^{\theta}(z_{l+1}-z_{i},\kappa)P_{0,l+1}
. Observe that we have
P_{0i}P_{0,l+1}=P_{0,l+1}
for any

as operators on
\mathcal{M}\raisebox{-0.5pt}{${}_{k+1}^{S_{l+1}}$}
.
Therefore, the left-hand side is

\sum_{i=0}^{l}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0,\dots,l,\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}G^{\theta}(z_{i}-z_{j},\kappa)\cdot G^{\theta}(z_{l+1}-z_{i},l\kappa+\kappa)P_{0,l+1}.

Hence, the equality would follow from

\prod_{i=0}^{l}G^{\theta}(z_{l+1}-z_{i},\kappa)=\sum_{i=0}^{l}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0,\dots,l,\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}G^{\theta}(z_{i}-z_{j},\kappa)\cdot G^{\theta}(z_{l+1}-z_{i},l\kappa+\kappa).

This is the content of Lemma
A.1
for
u=z_{l+1}
.
∎

Consider the

-antisymmetrizer

Denote by

the standard antisymmetrization map.

Corollary 3.4
.

We have

\bigl(\check{A}^{\theta}_{k}f\bigr)(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})=\prod_{i<j}G^{\theta}(z_{i}-z_{j},-\kappa)\cdot\mathrm{Sym}^{-}_{k}f(z_{1},\dots,z_{k}).

Proof.

Let us consider
\mathrm{Sym}_{k}
and
\mathrm{Sym}_{k}^{-}
as elements of
\mathbf{C}[S_{k}]
embedded into semi-direct product
\mathbf{C}[S_{k}]\ltimes\mathcal{M}_{k}

for the quantization parameter

in

Then

4

The

A

\mathrm{Rep}(G)
. In other words, it is a
G

such that the action map

G

\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(G)

For

x\xi:=\xi x-\hbar\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}(x),\qquad x\in X,\ \xi\in\mathfrak{g}.

Remark 4.3

5
Parabolic restriction and difference operators

In this section, we recall the notion of the parabolic restriction for Harish-Chandra bimodules essentially following
[
28
]
, as well as the classical construction of Mickelsson algebras
[
49
]
. In particular, we identify the one associated to the symmetric algebra
S^{\bullet}\mathbf{C}^{N}
with a negative half of the algebra of difference operators, see Theorem
5.11
. It will be used in Section
7
to define a quantum Toda lattice counterpart of the GKLO homomorphism of Theorem
3.16
, one of the key steps for the main result of the paper Theorem
8.10
.

5.1
Harish-Chandra bimodules for torus

Let
H_{N}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{N}
be the subgroup of diagonal matrices as in Section
2.1
. In this subsection, we explicitly describe the category
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H_{N})
and its generic version
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}
that we will use for the parabolic restriction.

Since
H_{N}
is commutative, the algebra
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})
can be identified with the functions
\mathcal{O}\bigl(\mathfrak{h}_{N}^{*}\!\times\!\mathbb{A}^{1}\bigr)
, where
\mathbb{A}^{1}
corresponds to
\hbar
. We will mostly use
\rho_{N}
-shifted coordinates (
2.3
) on
\mathfrak{h}_{N}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}

w_{i}:=E_{ii}+N\hbar-i\hbar\in\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})\subset\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}.

Somewhat abusing notations, we will denote a typical element of
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})
by

f(w):=f(w_{1},\dots,w_{N},\hbar)\in\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}.

We hope it will not lead to any confusion.

Since irreducible representations of
H_{N}
are classified by characters in
\Lambda
, we have

\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H_{N})\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})}[\lambda],

(5.1)

where
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})}[\lambda]
is the category of
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})
-modules to which we assign degree
\lambda
. Under this identification, if
X\in\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})}[\lambda]
, then the left action is that of
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})
and the right one is

x\cdot f(w):=f(w-\hbar\lambda)\cdot x,\qquad x\in X,\ f(w)\in\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N}).

(5.2)

The tensor structure is given component-wise by

\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})}[\lambda]\times\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})}[\mu]\rightarrow\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})}[\lambda+\mu],\qquad(X,Y)\mapsto X\otimes_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})}Y,

(5.3)

where the right action on
X
is used.

We will also need to extend the scalars. Consider the localized algebra

\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}:=\mathbf{C}[\hbar][w_{1},\dots,w_{N}]\biggl[\frac{1}{w_{i}-w_{j}+k\hbar}\>\bigg|\>i,j=1,\dots,N,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\biggr],

(5.4)

and define the category of
generic
Harish-Chandra bimodules
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}
as

\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}}[\lambda],

with similar definitions of the right action (
5.2
) and tensor structure (
5.3
).

5.2
Parabolic restriction

In this subsection, we recall the notion of parabolic restriction as in
[
28
]
and explicitly compute in some concrete examples.

For any weight element
x\in\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
and
f(w)\in\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})
, observe that

f(w)x=xf(w+\hbar\cdot\mathrm{wt}(x)).

In particular, the tensor product
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}:=\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}\otimes_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
has a well-defined algebra structure. Denote by
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}
the corresponding generic version of Harish-Chandra bimodules for
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
and by

X^{\mathrm{gen}}:=\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}\otimes_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})}X\in\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}

for any
X\in\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
.

Definition 5.1
.

The
parabolic restriction functor
is a functor
\mathrm{res}_{-}\colon\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})\rightarrow\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}
defined by

X\mapsto X^{\mathrm{gen}}/\!\!/{N_{-}}:=(\mathfrak{n}_{-}\backslash X^{\mathrm{gen}})^{N_{-}}=\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}\otimes_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})}(\mathfrak{n}_{-}\backslash X)^{N_{-}},\qquad X\in\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N}),

where the quotient is taken by the
left
action of
\mathfrak{n}_{-}
and the invariants are with respect to the diagonal action of
N_{-}
.

In fact, one can explicitly compute the parabolic restriction as follows. By
[
1
]
, there exists an element
P
of
\mathfrak{h}_{N}
-weight zero in a certain completion of
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}\bigl[\hbar^{-1}\bigr]
whose action is well-defined on any right
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}
-module
F
with a locally-finite action of
\mathfrak{n}_{-}
satisfying
F\mathfrak{n}_{-}\subset\hbar F
; this element is called the
extremal projector
. It satisfies the following properties:

\displaystyle P\in 1+\mathfrak{n}_{-}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}\cap\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}\mathfrak{n}_{+},

\displaystyle E_{ij}P=PE_{ji}=0,\qquad 1\leq i<j\leq N,

\displaystyle P^{2}=P.

(5.5)

Notation 5.2
.

For any Harish-Chandra bimodule
X\in\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
, we denote by

X^{\mathrm{gen}}\mapsto\mathfrak{n}_{-}\backslash X^{\mathrm{gen}},\qquad x\mapsto Px

the image of
x\in X^{\mathrm{gen}}
under the projection map. This is a well-defined notation since for any
{\xi\in\mathfrak{n}_{-}}
, we have
P\xi x=0
.

The extremal projector defines an isomorphism

P\colon\mathfrak{n}_{-}\backslash X^{\mathrm{gen}}/\mathfrak{n}_{+}\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathfrak{n}_{-}\backslash X^{\mathrm{gen}})^{N_{-}},\qquad[x]\mapsto PxP.

(5.6)

In particular, for any
V\in\mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
, there is a canonical isomorphism

\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}\otimes V\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}\otimes V/\!\!/N_{-},\qquad f(w)\otimes v\mapsto f(w)PvP,

(5.7)

see
[
49
]
.

Remark 5.3
.

Usually, the cited results use the positive version of the parabolic restriction
X\mapsto(X^{\mathrm{gen}}/\mathfrak{n}_{+})^{N_{+}}
. For Miura transform of Section
7
, the negative one will be more convenient as the former is a map of
left

\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}
-modules; however, both versions are equivalent thanks to the property
\varpi_{N}(P)=P
. For instance, assume
\varpi_{N}
can be extended to an isomorphism of a Harish-Chandra bimodule
X
. Then there is an anti-isomorphism

\varpi_{N}\colon\ (\mathfrak{n}_{-}\backslash X^{\mathrm{gen}})^{N_{-}}\xrightarrow{\sim}(X^{\mathrm{gen}}/\mathfrak{n}_{+})^{N_{+}},\qquad PxP\mapsto\varpi_{N}(PxP)=P\varpi_{N}(x)P.

Example 5.4
.

If
V=\mathbf{C}
is the trivial representation, then
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathrm{gen}}/\!\!/N_{-}\cong\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}
. In particular, let us determine the image of the center
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
. Recall that by (
2.8
), its generators are given by the quantum minor
A(u)
. Using explicit formula (
2.7
) and the properties of the extremal projector, one can conclude that

PA(u)=A(u)P=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(u-w_{i}),

(5.8)

where, as usual, the first part is the image of
A(u)
in the quotient
\mathfrak{n}_{-}\backslash\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
and the second part is its image in the quotient
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})/\mathfrak{n}_{+}
by the right action of
\mathfrak{n}_{+}
, see
[
38
, Theorem 7.1.1]
. In particular, this defines an isomorphism
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}\cong\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})^{W}
, where
W=S_{N}
acts by permutations of coordinates
\{w_{i}\}
. In terms of coordinates
\{E_{ii}\}
, it corresponds to the
\rho_{N}
-shifted action

\sigma\bullet h:=\sigma(h+\rho)-\rho,\qquad h\in\mathfrak{h}_{N},\ \sigma\in W.

This is the standard Harish-Chandra isomorphism
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}\cong\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h}_{N})^{W}
, see
[
25
, Theorem 1.10]
.

5.3

In this subsection, we describe explicitly the parabolic restriction of
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes\mathbf{C}^{N}
. We will use a slightly renormalized invariant vectors. Define

\bar{v}_{i}=\sum\limits_{N\geq i_{1}>\dots>i_{s}>i}P(w_{i}-w_{j_{1}})\cdots(w_{i}-w_{j_{r}})v_{i_{1}}E_{i_{2}i_{1}}\cdots E_{ii_{s}}\cdot\in\mathfrak{n}_{-}\backslash\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes\mathbf{C}^{N},

(5.9)

where
\{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\}

\{i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\}

[

4.6
, and Remark

and satisfy

\bar{v}_{i}=(w_{i}-w_{i+1})\cdots(w_{i}-w_{N})\cdot Pv_{i}P

(5.10)

(more precisely, in the notations of loc. cit., they correspond to

Using the parabolic restriction, one can deduce the following explicit description of the Harish-Chandra bimodule
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes\mathbf{C}^{N}
as bimodule over the center
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
. Before the statement, we need the following lemma which is essentially a part of

30

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-representations
V
and
W
, the map

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})}(\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes V,\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes W)

\displaystyle\qquad\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}}(\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes V/\!\!/N_{-},\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes W/\!\!/N_{-})

is injective.

Proof.

Due to (
4.1
), we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})}(\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes V,\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes W)\cong(\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes W\otimes V^{*})^{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}.

For brevity, let us assume that
V=\mathbf{C}
. Let
s_{\hbar}\in(\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes W)^{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}
. Consider the induced homomorphism

s\in(\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes W)^{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}/(\hbar)\cong(\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{*})\otimes W)^{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}.

In other words,
s
is a
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-equivariant section of the trivial vector bundle
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{*}\times W\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{*}
. Since
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes W
is a free
\mathbf{C}[\hbar]
-module, we can assume that
s\neq 0
by diving by powers of
\hbar
if necessary.

Recall that parabolic restriction is isomorphic to the functor
X\mapsto\mathfrak{n}_{-}\backslash X^{\mathrm{gen}}/\mathfrak{n}_{+}
as in (
5.6
). For the category
\mathrm{QCoh}_{[\mathfrak{g}^{*}/G]}
as a subcategory of Harish-Chandra bimodules on which
\hbar
acts by zero as in Remark
4.3
, this functor is simply the restriction along the embedding
(\mathfrak{h}_{N}^{*})^{\mathrm{reg}}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Since its

-orbit is dense, the restriction

is non-zero as well. Since the parabolic restriction functor commutes with

-action, we obtain that
\mathrm{res}(s_{\hbar})
is non-zero too.
∎

The next lemma concerns the canonical homomorphism
\Omega
of (
4.2
) under the parabolic restriction.

Lemma 5.6
.

For any
i
, we have
P\Omega(v_{i})P=w_{i}Pv_{i}P
.

Proof.

We have

P\Omega(v_{i})P=\sum_{b=1}^{N}PE_{ib}v_{b}P.

Using the properties of the extremal projector (
5.5
), we obtain

\sum_{b>i}PE_{ib}v_{b}P=\sum_{b>i}Pv_{b}E_{ib}P+\hbar(N-i)Pv_{i}P=\hbar(N-i)Pv_{i}P,

as well as

\sum_{b<i}PE_{ib}v_{b}P=0,\qquad PE_{ii}v_{i}P=E_{ii}Pv_{i}P.

Therefore, we have

P\Omega(v_{i})P=(E_{ii}+\hbar(N-i))Pv_{i}P=w_{i}Pv_{i}P,

as required.
∎

The equality in the following proposition is understood as in Remark
5.8
.

Proposition 5.7
.

For any
v\in\mathbf{C}^{N}\subset\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes\mathbf{C}^{N}
, we have

A(u)^{-1}vA(u)=\frac{u-\Omega+\hbar}{u-\Omega}v

as elements of
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigl[\hskip-0.56905pt\bigl[u^{-1}\bigr]\hskip-0.56905pt\bigr]
.

Proof.

Both sides are actions of certain elements of
\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})}\bigl(\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)\bigl[\hskip-0.56905pt\bigl[u^{-1}\bigr]\hskip-0.56905pt\bigr]
. By Lemma
5.5
, it is enough to show the equality under their parabolic restriction functor. It follows from Lemma
5.6
that

\frac{u-\Omega+\hbar}{u-\Omega}Pv_{i}P=\frac{u-w_{i}+\hbar}{u-w_{i}}Pv_{i}P.

At the same time, recall that by Example
5.4
, we have
PA(u)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(u-w_{j})
, therefore,

Pv_{i}A(u)P=P\prod_{j=1}^{N}v_{i}(u-w_{j})P=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(u-w_{i}+\delta_{ij}\hbar)\cdot Pv_{i}P.

Hence

PA(u)^{-1}v_{i}A(u)P=\frac{u-w_{i}+\hbar}{u-w_{i}}Pv_{i}P,

and the statement follows.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

Strictly speaking, we understand the equality of Proposition
5.7
in the sense of power series expansion in

. The right-hand side can be presented as

\frac{u-\Omega+\hbar}{u-\Omega}=1+\frac{\hbar}{u-\Omega}=1+\hbar\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\Omega^{i}u^{-i-1}\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})}\bigl(\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)\bigl[\hskip-0.56905pt\bigl[u^{-1}\bigr]\hskip-0.56905pt\bigr],

while the left-hand side can be equivalently presented as conjugation by
\mathrm{qdet}(T(-u+N\hbar-\hbar))
in the notations of Section
2.2
which also defines an element of
\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})}\!\bigl(\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)\!\bigl[\hskip-0.56905pt\bigl[u^{-1}\bigr]\hskip-0.56905pt\bigr]
. Another way is via the coefficients (
2.8
): for any
v\in\mathbf{C}^{N}
, we have

A_{i}v-A_{i-1}\Omega v=vA_{i}-(\Omega-\hbar)vA_{i-1}.

Remark 5.9
.

In fact, using Lemma
5.6
and (

: observe that for every

, we have

PA(\Omega)v_{i}P=\prod_{j=1}^{N}P(\Omega-w_{j})v_{i}P=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(w_{i}-w_{j})Pv_{i}P=0.

Then we apply Lemma
5.5

5.4
Mickelsson algebras and difference operators

By
[

, the natural lax monoidal structure on

Lemma A.1

The following result about symmetric polynomials is used in Proposition

Lemma A.2

1.

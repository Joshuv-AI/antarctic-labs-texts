On the Anti-Ramsey Number Under Edge Deletion
Ali Ghalavand, Qing Jie, Zemin Jin, Xueliang Li, Linshu Pan

Abstract
According to a study by Erdős et al. in 1975, the anti-Ramsey number of a graph G G , denoted as A ​ R ​ ( n , G ) AR(n,G) , is defined as the maximum number of colors that can be used in an edge-coloring of the complete graph K n K_{n} without creating a rainbow copy of G G . In this paper, we investigate the anti-Ramsey number under edge deletion and demonstrate that both decreasing and unchanging are possible outcomes. For three non-negative integers k k , t t , and n n , let G = k ​ P 4 ∪ t ​ P 2 G=kP_{4}\cup tP_{2} . Let E ′ E^{\prime} be a subset of the edge set E ⁡ ( G ) E(G) such that every endpoint of these edges has a degree of two in G G . We prove that if one of the conditions (i) t ≥ k + 1 ≥ 2 t\geq k+1\geq 2 and n ≥ 8 ​ k + 2 ​ t − 4 n\geq 8k+2t-4 ; (ii) k , t ≥ 1 k,t\geq 1 and n = 4 ​ k + 2 ​ t n=4k+2t ; (iii) k = 1 k=1 , t ≥ 1 t\geq 1 , and n ≥ 2 ​ t + 4 n\geq 2t+4 , occurs then the behavior of the anti-Ramsey number remains consistent when the edges in E ′ E^{\prime} are removed from G G , i.e., A ​ R ​ ( n , G ) = A ​ R ​ ( n , G − E ′ ) AR(n,G)=AR(n,G-E^{\prime}) . However, this is not the case when k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , t = 0 t=0 , and n = 4 ​ k n=4k . As a result, we calculate A ​ R ​ ( k ​ P 4 ∪ t ​ P 2 ) AR(kP_{4}\cup tP_{2}) for the cases: (i) t ≥ k + 1 ≥ 2 t\geq k+1\geq 2 and n ≥ 8 ​ k + 2 ​ t − 4 n\geq 8k+2t-4 ; (ii) k , t ≥ 1 k,t\geq 1 and n = 4 ​ k + 2 ​ t n=4k+2t ; (iii) k = 1 k=1 , t ≥ 0 t\geq 0 , and n ≥ 2 ​ t + 4 n\geq 2t+4 ; (iv) k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , t = 0 t=0 , and n = 4 ​ k n=4k .

Introduction

This study examines the impact of edge deletion on anti-Ramsey numbers for linear forests made up of paths of lengths two and four (i.e.,
P_{2}
and
P_{4}
). We will start by reviewing important definitions and providing some historical context.
Let
G
be a finite, simple graph. An
edge-coloring
of
G
is a function

, where
\mathcal{C}
is a set of colors. A subgraph

is considered

if all its edges have distinct colors according to

H
or an edge set

, we denote the set of colors on its edges by
c(H)
or
c(E^{\prime})
, respectively.
For a vertex subset
V_{1}\subseteq V(G)
, the
induced subgraph

G[V_{1}]
has the vertex set
V_{1}
and includes every edge of
G
whose endpoints are in
V_{1}
. For another subset
V_{2}
of
V(G)
, the notation
[V_{1},V_{2}]_{G}
represents the set of edges in
G
that have one endpoint in
V_{1}
and the other in
V_{2}
. The
disjoint union
of two graphs

and

is denoted
G\cup G^{\prime}
.
For a positive integer
k
,
kG
denotes the disjoint union of
k
copies of
G
. For a subset

of
E(G)
, the notation
G-E
denotes the graph obtained from

by removing the edges in
E^{\prime}
.
For a positive integer
z
, we define
[z]=\{1,2,\ldots,z\}
and
[0]=\emptyset
. We denote the complete graph, cycle, and path on
l
vertices by
K_{l}
,
C_{l}
, and

, respectively.

For a positive integer
n
and a graph

, the
anti-Ramsey number

AR(n,H)
is defined as the maximum number of colors in an edge-coloring of the complete graph
K_{n}
that does not contain a rainbow copy of
H
. This concept was introduced by Erdős et al. in 1975
[
3
]
, where they established its connection to Turán numbers and conjectured values for the anti-Ramsey numbers of paths and cycles.
Subsequent research has extensively explored anti-Ramsey numbers for graphs consisting of multiple small, disjoint components. A foundational result is the identification of the anti-Ramsey number for matchings (disjoint edges) in complete graphs
[
2
,
5
,
8
,
10
,
19
]
. More recently, the focus has shifted to more complex structures, particularly the vertex-disjoint union of small connected graphs and matchings. Our work contributes to this ongoing inquiry by studying linear forests composed of short odd paths. To clarify the findings related to our study so far, we outline them below.
Simonovits and Sós
[
18
]
proved that if
t\geq 2
and

is sufficiently large, then the following holds:

AR(n,P_{t})={\left\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2}\right\rfloor\choose 2}+\left(\left\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2}\right\rfloor-1\right)\left(n-\left\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)+1+\epsilon,

where
\epsilon=1
if

is even and
\epsilon=0
otherwise.
Montellano-Ballesteros and Neumann-Lara
[
16

established that for any

,

AR(n,C_{t})={t-1\choose 2}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{t-1}\right\rfloor+\left\lceil\frac{n}{t-1}\right\rceil+{n\mod(t-1)\choose 2}.

Schiermeyer
[
17

(t-2)(n-\frac{t-1}{2})+1&\text{ \rm when }n\geq\frac{5t-7}{2}.\end{cases}

(1)

The remaining case

(2)

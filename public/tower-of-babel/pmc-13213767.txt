Prevalence, Genetic Risks, and Cardiovascular Outcomes of Metabolic and Alcohol-Associated Liver Disease in a Longitudinal Multiethnic US Cohort
Authors: Jie Yao, Xiuqing Guo, Jingyi Tan, Callie Zaborenko, Jerome I Rotter, Naga P Chalasani
Published: 2026

Metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is the most common liver disease worldwide.1 Metabolic and alcohol-associated liver disease (Met-ALD) is a recently described entity characterized by SLD in individuals with both metabolic risk factors and moderate alcohol consumption.1 However, its epidemiology, genetic architecture, and relationship with cardiovascular (CV) risk are not well defined in diverse populations. Here, we investigated the prevalence of Met-ALD compared to MASLD and their relevant contributions to the CV risk in the Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA), a large, longitudinal, multiethnic US cohort. To understand the genetic background of Met-ALD, we evaluated the association between multiple MASLD and ALD-related polygenic risk scores (PRSs) and Met-ALD in comparison to MASLD.

The MESA study includes a diverse, population-based sample of 6814 asymptomatic men and women aged 45–84. Thirty-eight percent of the recruited participants are self-described as White, 28 percent African American, 22 percent Hispanic, and 12 percent Chinese, predominantly of Chinese descent. This study included 5182 individuals (mean age 62.2 ± 10.2 years; 53% women) from the 3 populations (44% White, 29.5% African American, 26.4% Hispanic) from the MESA study2 (Supplementary Table 1). We excluded Chinese participants due to the very small number of Met-ALD. MESA was approved by local institutional boards at all participating sites, and all participants signed a written informed consent.

Hepatic steatosis was defined by computed tomography imaging (liver attenuation <51 Hounsfield units or liver-to-spleen ratio <1), per previously published criteria.3 MASLD and Met-ALD were defined using the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases criteria1 (Supplementary Methods). Both should have hepatic steatosis with at least 1 metabolic risk factor and the absence of other known liver diseases or corticosteroid/amiodarone use. In addition, MASLD should have no alcohol use or history of alcohol use (up to 1 drink/d for women, up to 2 drinks/d for men), while Met-ALD should have a history of alcohol use (2–3 drinks/d for women, 3–4 drinks/d for men). The prevalence of MASLD and Met-ALD was estimated in each of the 3 self-reported race/ethnic groups (White, African American, and Hispanic) in MESA. The association of MASLD/Met-ALD with the prevalence of subclinical coronary atherosclerosis, defined as coronary artery calcium (CAC) >0, was tested using a logistic regression model while adjusting for age, sex, self-reported ethnicity, sites, and the first 3 genetic principal components. Cox proportional hazards models were used to test for the contribution of MASLD and/or Met-ALD to incident CV disease (CVD), defined as the first occurrence of hard coronary artery disease (myocardial infarction, resuscitated cardiac arrest, or death) or stroke (nonfatal or fatal), based on MESA’s adjudication procedures. Participants with prevalent CVD at baseline were excluded.

Five PRSs for SLD/cirrhosis were calculated based on previous publications: Chen et al (16 single nucleotide polymorphisms [SNPs], PubMed identifier [PMID] 37709864), Emdin et al (12 SNPs, PMID 33310085), Whitfield et al (3 SNPs, PMID 34656649), Schwantes-An et al (20 SNPs, PMID 38727677), and Vujkovic et al (77 SNPs, PMID 35654975)4, 5, 6, 7, 8 (Supplementary Table 2). Associations between each of the 5 PRSs with MASLD and Met-ALD were assessed by logistic regression, adjusting for age, sex, self-reported ethnicity, sites, and the first 3 genetic principal components.

Baseline characteristics of the study cohort are described in Supplementary Table 1. Hepatic steatosis was present in 15.8% of participants. The prevalence of MASLD was 15.1%, while Met-ALD was rare (1.3%). MASLD prevalence was highest among Hispanic participants (24.8%), compared to non-Hispanic White (11.8%) and non-Hispanic Black (11.2%) individuals. Met-ALD prevalence was 1.6% in Hispanic, 1.6% in non-Hispanic White, and 0.5% in non-Hispanic Black participants. Obesity (body mass index ≥30) was present in 36.4% of the cohort, and 17.3% developed incident CVD over a median follow-up of 14.3 years.

Genetically, all 5 PRSs were significantly associated with MASLD (eg, PRS-Chen: β = 0.46, P = 7.44 E−31; PRS-Whitfield: β = 0.39, P = 1.91 E−23). For Met-ALD, PRS-Chen (β = 0.49, P = 3.3 E−05) and PRS-Schwantes-An (β = 0.42, P = 5.28 E−04) showed the strongest associations (Table). These findings suggest shared genetic risk between MASLD and Met-ALD.

Both MASLD and Met-ALD were significantly associated with prevalent subclinical coronary atherosclerosis (CAC) (Table). MASLD was significantly associated with incident CVD (hazard ratio: 1.29; 95% CI: 1.08–1.55; P = .0048). Met-ALD showed a trend toward higher incident CVD (hazard ratio: 1.56; 95% CI: 0.95–2.57; P = .077), though this did not reach statistical significance (Table).

In this large, multiethnic US cohort, Met-ALD was uncommon and exhibited a genetic architecture similar to MASLD, indicating overlapping pathogenesis. Both conditions were associated with prevalent subclinical coronary atherosclerosis (CAC), and MASLD was linked to incident CVD. The rarity of Met-ALD and its genetic and clinical overlap with MASLD question whether Met-ALD represents a distinct clinical entity. Limitations of our study include lack of liver-related outcomes and objectively testing for alcohol use with tests such as phosphatidyl ethanolamine. Strengths of our study include a large sample size, which is multiethnic and comprehensive evaluation of genetic risk factors and prevalent and incident CVD. However, we advocate caution in interpreting our results due to very few individuals with Met-ALD we identified in this cohort. Nonetheless, our article adds substantially to the growing literature to better understand the characteristics of Met-ALD.

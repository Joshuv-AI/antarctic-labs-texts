Laryngeal mask airway combined with visual bronchial blocker versus double-lumen tube for lung isolation in video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery: a protocol for a multicentre randomised controlled trial
Authors: Yaodan Zhang, Tingting Li, Juan Wei, Rui Bao, Xin Lv, Jia-feng Wang, Jingxiang Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroduction Postoperative sore throat and hoarseness are common complications following lung isolation with double-lumen tubes (DLTs) in video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS). Laryngeal mask airway (LMA) combined with a visual bronchial blocker (VBB) may reduce airway trauma while maintaining effective lung isolation.Methods and analysisThis is a prospective, randomised, controlled, single-blind, multicentre clinical trial conducted at three major thoracic surgery centres in Shanghai, China. A total of 270 patients aged ≥18 years scheduled for elective VATS anatomical lung resection will be randomly allocated 1:1 to either the VBB group (n=135) using LMA combined with VBB or the DLT group (n=135) using conventional DLT (see Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials diagram). The primary outcome is the incidence of sore throat and hoarseness at 24 hours postoperatively. Secondary outcomes include sore throat and hoarseness at 1 and 48 hours, intraoperative device performance, lung collapse quality, intubation time, haemodynamic changes, emergence quality, device-related complications and hospital length of stay.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Chest Hospital (KS24042). Introduction

Video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) has revolutionised thoracic surgery by offering reduced surgical trauma, improved visualisation and faster recovery compared with traditional open approaches.1 Effective lung isolation is essential for optimal surgical conditions in VATS procedures, with one-lung ventilation being the standard technique.2

Double-lumen tubes (DLTs) represent the most widely used method for lung isolation.3 However, their large external diameter and rigid construction are frequently associated with airway trauma and postoperative complications, including pharyngolaryngeal pain, hoarseness and oedema.4 5 Such complications can delay recovery and extend hospital stays.6

The combination of laryngeal mask airway (LMA) with bronchial blockers has emerged as a less traumatic alternative.7 LMAs avoid direct laryngoscopy and tracheal intubation, potentially reducing postoperative sore throat (POST) and hoarseness.6 7 Nonetheless, conventional bronchial blockers present challenges, such as difficult positioning, poor malleability and dependence on fibreoptic bronchoscopy (FOB).8 9

Recent evidence indicates that bronchial blockers may reduce airway injury and POST compared with DLTs.10 However, most studies have focused on traditional non-visual devices, which remain limited by their dependency on FOB.11 12 In parallel, technological advances have led to visual lung-isolation devices, such as video double-lumen endobronchial tubes, which incorporate real-time imaging to improve positioning and reduce the need for FOB.13

Building on these developments, a novel visual bronchial blocker (VBB; LTBLOCKER, Hangzhou Lantai, China) has been introduced, which integrates real-time visualisation with enhanced manoeuvrability. This device combines a disposable bronchoscope with an integrated blocking balloon, offering several potential advantages: independent, precise positioning without FOB, adjustable angulation for easier passage through vocal cords, real-time visualisation and continuous intraoperative monitoring.

Despite these benefits, high-quality evidence comparing LMA combined with the VBB versus DLTs for lung isolation in VATS is lacking. This multicentre randomised controlled trial therefore aims to determine whether this combination reduces POST and hoarseness compared with DLTs while maintaining effective lung isolation. The results could influence clinical practice in thoracic anaesthesia and improve patient-centred outcomes.

The clinical significance of this research extends beyond technical performance. POST and hoarseness may lead to prolonged discomfort, delayed oral intake, increased analgesic requirements and voice changes.7 For patients undergoing VATS—who anticipate quicker recovery—minimising such complications is essential. Furthermore, within the growing framework of enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) protocols in thoracic surgery, identifying lung isolation techniques that reduce morbidity while providing adequate surgical exposure is crucial for improving outcomes (figure 1).

Randomisation and blinding

Participants will be randomised 1:1 to either the VBB group or DLT group using computer-generated randomisation sequences stratified by centre. Block randomisation with varying block sizes (4, 6 and 8) will be employed to ensure balanced allocation within each centre. This study employs a single-blind design. Due to the distinct nature of the interventions, anaesthesiologists and surgical teams cannot be blinded to group allocation. However, patients and independent outcome assessors will remain blinded to treatment allocation.

Interventions

All airway management procedures will be performed by attending anaesthesiologists with at least 5 years of clinical experience in thoracic anaesthesia. Prior to the trial, each participating anaesthesiologist must complete a structured training programme conducted on airway manikins and successfully perform at least 10 supervised procedures using the LMA combined with the VBB technique.

Throat pain score: 0=no pain with swallowing, 1=pain only with swallowing, 2=continuous pain worsened by swallowing and 3=pain affecting eating or requiring analgesics.

Sample size calculation

Sample size calculation was performed using PASS V.18.0 software, based on the primary endpoint of 24-hour sore throat and hoarseness incidence. Based on prior reports of 35–45% with DLT,6 19 we assumed a control (DLT) event rate of 30% and hypothesised a reduction to 15% with VBB (absolute difference 15%). With α=0.05 (two-sided) and power (1−β)=0.80, the required sample size is 121 patients per group. Allowing for a 10% dropout rate, the target enrolment is 135 per group (n=270 in total). Enrolment will be stratified by centre with permuted blocks, and each of the three centres will recruit 90 patients (45 per group).

Statistical analysis

All analyses will be performed using SPSS V.24.0 software following intention-to-treat principles. Continuous variables will be presented as means±SD or medians with IQRs, depending on distribution normality assessed by the Shapiro-Wilk test. Categorical variables will be presented as frequencies and percentages.

Primary outcome analysis

The incidence of POST and hoarseness at 24 hours will be compared between groups using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. Risk ratios and 95% CIs will be calculated. A post hoc exploratory multivariable logistic regression analysis will be performed only if the number of events is sufficient with prespecified covariates, including centre, age, sex and duration of surgery.

Secondary outcome analyses

Independent t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for continuous variables—χ² or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical variables—ordinal logistic regression for ordered categorical outcomes (pain/hoarseness scores).

All statistical tests will be two-sided, with statistical significance set at p<0.05.

Discussion

This multicentre randomised controlled trial will provide crucial evidence regarding the comparative effectiveness and safety of LMA combined with VBB versus DLT for lung isolation in VATS. The study addresses a significant knowledge gap in thoracic anaesthesia practice and has the potential to transform clinical practice by providing evidence for less invasive airway management techniques.

Expected outcomes and clinical implications

Based on current evidence and theoretical considerations, we hypothesise that the VBB approach will demonstrate:

Reduced POST and hoarseness incidence due to avoidance of direct laryngoscopy and tracheal intubation.

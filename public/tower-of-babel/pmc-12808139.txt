Mitigating catastrophic forgetting in lifelong learning: a hybrid architecture integrating neural ordinary differential equations with memory-augmented transformers
Authors: Song Zhou, Qiang Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCatastrophic forgetting remains a critical challenge in lifelong learning where neural networks severely degrade on previous tasks when learning new ones. This paper proposes the first systematic integration of neural ordinary differential equations (Neural ODEs) with memory-augmented transformers to address this limitation. Neural ODEs enable smooth representation learning through continuous-time dynamics, while memory-augmented transformers provide explicit knowledge consolidation through attention-based retrieval. We derive rigorous PAC-learning theoretical bounds characterizing the relationships between model capacity, task sequence length, and forgetting severity, complemented by an adaptive memory management strategy. Comprehensive experiments on Split CIFAR-100, Permuted MNIST, and CORe50 demonstrate substantial improvements: 24% forgetting reduction and 10.3% accuracy gain over state-of-the-art methods. This work provides principled foundations for scalable lifelong learning systems capable of continuous knowledge accumulation without destructive interference.Keywords: Lifelong learning, Catastrophic forgetting, Neural ordinary differential equations, Memory-augmented transformers, Continual learning, Theoretical boundsSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Lifelong learning, also known as continual learning or incremental learning, has emerged as a fundamental paradigm shift in artificial intelligence research, aiming to enable intelligent systems to continuously acquire new knowledge while preserving previously learned information1. The evolution of lifelong learning can be traced back to early connectionist models, where researchers first recognized the necessity for adaptive systems capable of learning from non-stationary data streams without requiring complete retraining2. However, the advancement of this field has been persistently hindered by the catastrophic forgetting phenomenon, wherein neural networks exhibit severe degradation in performance on previously learned tasks when adapting to new ones3. This challenge represents one of the most critical obstacles preventing artificial intelligence systems from achieving human-like cognitive flexibility and knowledge accumulation capabilities.

Recent developments in neural ordinary differential equations (Neural ODEs) have opened new avenues for modeling continuous-time dynamics in deep learning architectures, offering potential solutions to mitigate information loss through their inherent continuous representation capabilities4. Concurrently, transformer architectures have demonstrated remarkable success in capturing long-range dependencies and complex patterns across various domains, leading researchers to explore memory-augmented variants specifically designed for sequential task learning scenarios5. Autonomous online learning systems have further demonstrated the importance of adaptive mechanisms for continuous knowledge acquisition, where tutorial-generating methods enable self-directed learning without human intervention53. These parallel developments in continuous-time modeling, memory augmentation, and autonomous learning motivate our integrated approach to lifelong learning.

Despite these promising directions, current approaches suffer from significant limitations including the lack of rigorous theoretical frameworks for characterizing forgetting bounds and the absence of principled optimization strategies that can effectively balance plasticity-stability trade-offs6.

The necessity of this research stems from the growing demand for artificial intelligence systems capable of operating in dynamic, real-world environments where knowledge must be continuously updated without catastrophic interference. Existing methodologies predominantly rely on heuristic techniques such as regularization-based approaches or memory replay mechanisms, which lack theoretical guarantees regarding their asymptotic behavior and scalability7. This work addresses these fundamental gaps by proposing a unified theoretical framework that establishes rigorous bounds on catastrophic forgetting while simultaneously developing optimal learning strategies. The primary innovation of this study lies in the synergistic integration of neural differential equations with memory-augmented transformer architectures, creating a novel computational framework that leverages continuous-time dynamics for knowledge retention while exploiting attention mechanisms for selective information preservation8. Through comprehensive theoretical analysis and algorithmic design, this research aims to advance the foundational understanding of lifelong learning systems and provide practical solutions for next-generation adaptive artificial intelligence.

Experimental setup and benchmark datasets

The experimental validation is conducted on three widely-adopted lifelong learning benchmark datasets that represent diverse challenges in continual learning scenarios46. Split CIFAR-100 partitions the original CIFAR-100 dataset into sequential classification tasks, where the 100 classes are divided into disjoint subsets, typically configured as 10 or 20 tasks with 10 or 5 classes per task respectively, enabling controlled evaluation of class-incremental learning capabilities. Permuted MNIST constructs task sequences by applying distinct random pixel permutations to the original MNIST digits, creating a series of domain-shift tasks where each permutation defines a unique task while preserving the underlying digit recognition objective, thereby testing the model’s ability to learn diverse input transformations without interference. CORe50 represents a more realistic continual learning benchmark featuring continuous video streams of 50 objects captured under varying conditions, organized into temporal batches that simulate online learning from non-stationary visual data with natural distribution shifts.

Discussion

The proposed hybrid architecture combining neural differential equations with memory-augmented transformers demonstrates substantial theoretical and practical advantages in addressing catastrophic forgetting through multiple synergistic mechanisms. At the theoretical level, the continuous-time formulation inherent in neural ODEs provides a fundamentally different parameterization of knowledge representation compared to discrete layer architectures, where learning manifests as smooth trajectory evolution in continuous state space rather than discrete weight updates. This continuity enables more controlled knowledge integration, as new task learning corresponds to trajectory refinement rather than abrupt parameter perturbations that characterize traditional networks. Practically, the experimental validation across diverse benchmarks confirms that this architectural design achieves superior forgetting mitigation while maintaining computational tractability, bridging the gap between theoretical elegance and engineering feasibility.

The mechanism through which neural differential equations alleviate catastrophic forgetting operates at multiple levels of abstraction. Fundamentally, the ODE formulation decouples representation capacity from architectural depth, allowing the model to adaptively allocate computational resources based on task complexity without structural modifications. Regarding scalability to extremely long task sequences, preliminary experiments with extended benchmarks (50–100 tasks on Split CIFAR-100 and Permuted MNIST) reveal that the architecture maintains stable performance with sublinear forgetting growth, although memory requirements increase linearly as predicted by Eq. (13). For task sequences exceeding 100 tasks, hierarchical memory organization and progressive knowledge distillation strategies become necessary to prevent capacity saturation. The adaptive memory management strategy demonstrates effectiveness across standard benchmarks but exhibits context-dependent limitations; when tasks are highly similar with significant knowledge overlap, importance-based selection effectively identifies shared representations and maximizes storage efficiency, whereas extreme task diversity with minimal transferable structure challenges the dynamic allocation algorithm, potentially leading to biased forgetting patterns favoring recent tasks. Future optimization directions include task-relationship-aware allocation using graph-based clustering, meta-learned adaptive strategies that automatically adjust retention policies based on task characteristics, and hierarchical memory architectures with multi-resolution storage to better handle diverse long-term learning scenarios.

The continuous dynamics impose implicit smoothness constraints on learned functions through Lipschitz-bounded vector fields, preventing the sharp decision boundary shifts that typically accompany sequential learning in discrete networks. Furthermore, the adjoint sensitivity method for gradient computation naturally regularizes learning by integrating gradient information over temporal trajectories, effectively averaging out noisy gradient directions that might otherwise cause destructive interference with previous knowledge.

The theoretical contribution of deriving rigorous PAC-learning bounds for catastrophic forgetting advances beyond existing heuristic approaches lacking formal guarantees. By establishing relationships between model capacity, task sequence length, and forgetting severity through VC-dimension analysis, this work provides principled architecture design guidelines. The derived lower bounds on memory requirements (Eq. 13) clarify that linear memory growth with task number is fundamentally unavoidable under reasonable separability assumptions. These theoretical results validate the necessity of explicit memory mechanisms and quantify minimum resources required for bounded forgetting, enabling informed performance-resource trade-offs.

The adaptive memory management strategy demonstrates effectiveness across standard benchmarks but exhibits context-dependent limitations that warrant careful consideration. In scenarios with highly similar tasks where significant knowledge overlap exists, the importance-based sample selection effectively identifies shared representations, maximizing storage efficiency. However, when tasks exhibit extreme diversity with minimal transferable structure, the dynamic allocation algorithm may struggle to balance retention across disparate knowledge domains, potentially leading to biased forgetting patterns favoring recent or frequently accessed tasks. The temporal decay mechanism, while preventing unbounded memory growth, introduces a systematic bias against older tasks that may be undesirable in applications requiring uniform long-term retention.

Experimental analysis reveals noteworthy phenomena beyond aggregate metrics. The observed sublinear forgetting growth suggests the model develops robust shared representations generalizing across tasks rather than accumulating isolated task-specific knowledge. Asymmetric transfer patterns in the knowledge matrix indicate continuous-time dynamics naturally impose temporal ordering bias, where earlier tasks serve as foundational priors shaping subsequent learning. This emergent hierarchical organization aligns with cognitive theories of human knowledge acquisition, suggesting connections to developmental learning frameworks.

The fundamental distinction between this work and existing continual learning approaches lies in the integration philosophy rather than individual components. While prior methods typically address forgetting through isolated mechanisms such as parameter regularization or memory replay, the proposed architecture achieves holistic mitigation by aligning multiple design choices around the principle of smooth, continuous knowledge evolution. Neural ODEs provide the continuous substrate, memory modules offer explicit consolidation, and the optimization strategy ensures balanced gradient flow, collectively creating an ecosystem where forgetting is systematically suppressed at architectural, representational, and algorithmic levels simultaneously.

Regarding generalization and extensibility, the framework exhibits promising adaptability to diverse application domains beyond image classification. The continuous-time formulation naturally accommodates irregular temporal data such as time-series forecasting or event sequence prediction, where the ODE dynamics can model inherent temporal dependencies. The memory-augmented transformer component provides flexibility for different modalities including language, audio, and multimodal inputs through appropriate embedding adaptations. However, scalability to extremely long task sequences exceeding several hundred tasks remains unexplored, where the theoretical memory bounds suggest potential capacity bottlenecks requiring alternative architectural innovations.

Current limitations include elevated computational overhead compared to baseline methods, primarily attributable to ODE solver iterations and memory attention operations. While the 1.85× training time increase delivers substantial forgetting reduction, deployment in resource-constrained environments or real-time applications may necessitate efficiency optimizations. The theoretical analysis relies on assumptions including Lipschitz continuity and bounded gradient norms that, while reasonable, may not hold universally across all network configurations and task distributions. The derived bounds, though validated empirically, exhibit some looseness in highly non-stationary scenarios, suggesting room for tightening through refined analysis techniques.

Future research directions encompass theoretical refinement and practical enhancement across multiple dimensions. Lightweight architectural variants employing neural controlled differential equations or reduced-order ODE approximations could reduce computational overhead by 40–50% while preserving forgetting mitigation capabilities. Theoretical extensions deriving tighter data-dependent bounds through refined complexity measures beyond VC-dimension would enhance predictive accuracy. Integration with meta-learning frameworks for automated hyperparameter adaptation represents a promising direction for cross-domain generalization. Extending the framework to diverse modalities including natural language processing, audio processing, and multimodal learning scenarios would validate broader applicability. Investigating hierarchical memory architectures with multi-resolution storage could address the linear memory growth limitation for ultra-long task sequences (> 1000 tasks). Incorporating robustness to distribution shift, safety under uncertainty, and interpretability of learned representations through explainable AI techniques constitute essential directions for real-world deployment. Developing open-source benchmarking tools and standardized evaluation protocols would facilitate reproducibility and accelerate community progress. Ultimately, achieving human-like lifelong learning requires interdisciplinary collaboration spanning machine learning theory, cognitive neuroscience, and systems engineering to address fundamental challenges in continuous knowledge accumulation.

Conclusion

This research addresses catastrophic forgetting in lifelong learning through architectural innovation, theoretical analysis, and algorithmic optimization. The principal contributions include: (1) a novel hybrid architecture integrating neural ODEs with memory-augmented transformers, (2) rigorous PAC-learning theoretical bounds characterizing forgetting behavior, and (3) adaptive optimization strategies balancing knowledge retention and acquisition.

Empirical validation demonstrates that the proposed model achieves 24% forgetting reduction and 10.3% accuracy gain over state-of-the-art methods across diverse benchmarks (Split CIFAR-100, Permuted MNIST, CORe50). These results establish that continuous-time dynamics combined with explicit memory mechanisms provide a viable pathway toward sustained knowledge accumulation without destructive interference. The theoretical framework offers fundamental insights into trade-offs between model capacity, memory requirements, and forgetting guarantees, enabling principled architecture design from first principles.

However, several limitations warrant acknowledgment. Computational overhead remains elevated with 1.85× training time compared to standard fine-tuning, potentially limiting real-time applications. Memory requirements grow linearly with task number (Eq. 13), becoming unsustainable for extremely long sequences (> 500 tasks). Theoretical assumptions including Lipschitz continuity and bounded gradients may not universally hold across all network configurations. The derived bounds exhibit some looseness in highly non-stationary scenarios, suggesting room for tightening through data-dependent analysis. Experimental validation focuses primarily on vision tasks; generalization to other modalities (NLP, audio, multimodal) requires further investigation.

Practical application potential spans numerous domains where adaptive intelligence operating on non-stationary data streams constitutes critical functionality. In robotics, the proposed architecture enables autonomous agents to continuously acquire new skills through interaction with dynamic environments while preserving previously learned manipulation capabilities and navigation strategies. Recommendation systems can leverage this framework to adapt to evolving user preferences and emerging content categories without forgetting historical behavioral patterns, maintaining personalization quality across extended temporal horizons. Natural language processing applications including conversational agents and machine translation systems benefit from the ability to incrementally incorporate new linguistic phenomena, domain terminologies, and stylistic variations without degrading performance on established competencies. The memory-efficient design renders the approach particularly suitable for edge deployment scenarios where computational resources and storage capacity face stringent constraints.

Despite these advances, several limitations warrant acknowledgment and suggest directions for refinement. The theoretical bounds, while empirically validated, exhibit some looseness particularly in highly non-stationary scenarios, indicating opportunities for tightening through more sophisticated complexity measures or data-dependent analysis techniques. Computational overhead remains elevated compared to simpler baseline methods, with training time approximately 85% greater than standard fine-tuning, potentially limiting applicability in time-critical or resource-constrained contexts. The assumption of Lipschitz continuity and bounded gradients underlying theoretical guarantees may not universally hold across all network configurations and optimization landscapes, suggesting that boundary conditions require further investigation. Memory management strategies, while effective on standard benchmarks, have not been exhaustively evaluated under extreme task diversity or adversarial task orderings that might expose failure modes.

Future research trajectories encompass both theoretical deepening and practical broadening of the proposed framework. Investigating neural differential equation scalability to extremely long task sequences numbering in the hundreds or thousands represents an important direction for validating applicability to truly lifelong learning scenarios approaching human-scale knowledge accumulation timescales. Developing more efficient memory mechanisms through hierarchical organization, lossy compression techniques, or neurobiologically-inspired consolidation processes could substantially reduce storage requirements while maintaining retention quality. Extending the theoretical analysis to derive instance-dependent bounds that account for task structure and data distribution properties would enhance predictive accuracy and design guidance. Integration with meta-learning and neural architecture search methodologies promises automated adaptation to diverse application domains without manual hyperparameter tuning. Ultimately, the path toward real-world lifelong learning systems necessitates addressing robustness to distributional shift, safety under uncertainty, and interpretability of learned representations, challenges that require interdisciplinary collaboration spanning machine learning theory, cognitive science, and systems engineering. This work establishes foundational principles and demonstrates empirical viability, contributing essential building blocks toward the long-term vision of artificial intelligence systems that learn continuously throughout their operational lifetimes.

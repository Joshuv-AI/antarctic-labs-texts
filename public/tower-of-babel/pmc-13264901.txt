Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation for moderate to severe chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome (TASC-P) in China: study protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Xinyao Wei, Jiaming Cui, Zhiwei Zang, Ran Pang, Leilei Qi, Yu Wang, Hai-Bo Wang, Yangfeng Wu, Zongshi Qin, Jiani Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionChronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CP/CPPS) affects around 35%–50% of men during their lifetime. The efficacy of current oral medication for CP/CPPS remains limited. Recent studies demonstrated that vagus nerve stimulation may improve chronic pelvic and abdominal pain. Accordingly, transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS) might represent a promising, non-invasive therapeutic approach for the clinical management of CP/CPPS.Methods and analysisThe trial of Transcutaneous Auricular vagus nerve Stimulation for moderate to severe Chronic Prostatitis/CPPS is a prospective, randomised, sham-controlled trial with a 1:1 allocation ratio. Participants will be assigned randomly to either the taVNS group or the sham-taVNS group. The intervention period will consist of a 4-week treatment (a total of 40 sessions), followed by an 8-week follow-up period. The primary outcome is the change from baseline in the National Institutes of Health Chronic Prostatitis Symptom Score Index total score at week 4. Secondary outcomes include the International Prostate Symptom Score Scale, European Quality of Life 5-Dimensions-5-Levels questionnaire, Self-Rating Anxiety Scale and Self-Rating Depression Scale. Safety assessments will be conducted throughout the entire study period.Ethics and disseminationThis study protocol and informed consent documents were reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Guang’anmen Hospital, China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences (approval number: 2023-250 KY). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants and/or their legal guardians prior to trial participation. The findings will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal and presentations at scientific conferences. The research data will be made available on reasonable request.Trial registration numberNCT06287970.

Introduction

Chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CP/CPPS) is a common benign urogenital disorder affecting around 35%–50% of men during their lifetime1 2 and is recognised by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) as one of the most serious chronic conditions impacting quality of life (QOL),3 comparable to myocardial infarction, unstable angina or active Crohn’s disease.4 Characterised by pelvic pain and diverse urinary symptoms, CP/CPPS is often accompanied by systemic and psychological comorbidities.5 Given its non-bacterial nature,5 6 unclear pathogenesis and lack of curative treatment,7 current management primarily aims to relieve symptoms, improve QOL and reduce recurrent rather than to achieve complete recovery.7

Conventional treatment for CP/CPPS mainly includes oral medications including alpha-blockers, antibiotics, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and phytomedicine agents like pollen extracts. However, these drugs generally provide limited and short-term relief, are associated with drug-related side effects and often result in poor long-term adherence.7 8 A network meta-analysis involving 25 commonly used oral drugs further demonstrated only modest overall efficacy for CP/CPPS.9

Given the limitations of pharmacotherapy, non-pharmacological interventions have attracted increasing interest.1014 Among these, transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS) has emerged as a promising approach. The auricular branch of the vagus nerve (ABVN), being the only superficial branch of the vagus nerve, provides an anatomically accessible site for neuromodulation.15 Notably, its distribution overlaps with specific auricular acupoint regions, supporting their selection as effective stimulation targets in taVNS.

Previous studies have shown that vagus nerve stimulation effectively alleviates chronic pain in pelvic organs1618 and could attenuate inflammation by activating cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathways and other neuroimmune circuits.1922 A crossover randomised controlled trial found that taVNS demonstrated a reduction trend in pelvic pain in female patients,18 whereas the evidence on CP/CPPS remains limited. Considering the chronic pain and immune dysregulation characteristic of CP/CPPS,2325 taVNS may appear to be a mechanistically sound and promising therapeutic approach.

Accordingly, we aim to evaluate the efficacy and safety of taVNS for CP/CPPS through a randomised, sham-controlled trial and to generate new clinical evidence for its potential therapeutic value.

Methods

Patient and public involvement

No public involvement is planned for the design, recruitment, conduct or reporting phases of this study. Public dissemination of results will occur through academic papers and conference presentations.

Study design

This randomised controlled trial will be conducted at two centres in China: Guang’anmen Hospital, China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences and Yantai Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine. Randomisation

The randomisation protocol was prepared by Peking University Clinical Research Institute. The random codes will be sealed in opaque envelopes, which will be arranged sequentially according to the serial numbers on the outside. A staff member who does not participate in the treatment and evaluation will keep these envelopes and be responsible for the assignment.

Blinding

Participants, outcome evaluators and statisticians will be blinded; however, the acupuncturists cannot be blinded for that taVNS is a manipulated intervention. To ensure participant blinding, the ear clips of both taVNS and sham-taVNS groups will be identical in appearance, although the stimulation sites will differ. Both groups will receive electrical stimulation, making it difficult for participants to distinguish their group assignment. A blinding assessment will be conducted at the end of the treatment period.

Participants

The inclusion criteria contain: (1) According to the diagnostic criteria of CP/CPPS in European Association of Urology5 or Chinese Urological Association,26 patients present with recurrent and persistent prostate pain accompanied by abnormal urination or psychiatric symptoms without infection or other obvious pathological conditions, lasting more than 3 months within the last 6 months; (2) patients aged between 18 and 50 years; (3) patients with the NIH Chronic Prostatitis Symptom Score Index (NIH-CPSI) ≥15 (indicating moderate to severe CP/CPPS) and (4) patients volunteered to participate. Patients will be excluded if they: (1) have other diseases associated with urinary symptoms including benign prostatic hyperplasia, testicular, epididymal or spermatic cord diseases, overactive bladder, neurogenic bladder, interstitial cystitis, glandular cystitis, sexually transmitted diseases, bladder or prostate tumours, tuberculosis of the urogenital system, anorectal diseases, lumbar diseases, central or peripheral neuropathy or other urological or neurological conditions that may affect urinary symptoms; (2) have severe cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, renal or haematologic diseases or poor overall nutritional status; (3) have severe mental or emotional disorders that could result in an inability to cooperate and (4) have received treatment for CP/CPPS within 4 weeks prior to screening.

Recruitment procedure

Patients with CP/CPPS will be recruited through online platforms (including the hospital’s WeChat platform, official website and news outlets) and posters displayed in community and hospital settings. All potential participants will undergo a 1-week screening period. Graduate students will be responsible for participants’ reception and initial screening. They will complete the screening forms recording basic demographic data, diagnostic information, medical history information and assist patients in completing essential laboratory tests (such as routine blood tests and urine cultures). Two urologists will be responsible for the diagnosis and differential diagnosis. Eligible patients or their authorised representatives will provide written informed consent and will then be randomly assigned to either the taVNS or sham-taVNS group. Treatment will begin immediately after the randomisation.

Intervention

Acupuncturists who are licensed physicians with formal training in traditional Chinese medicine and certification in acupuncture with 2 or more years of clinical experience will be responsible for administering the taVNS or sham-taVNS intervention, given the need for accurate and standardised localisation of auricular stimulation sites. Researchers involved in the clinical process, such as acupuncturists and outcome evaluators, will be systematically trained before the recruitment. Hwato ear clip electrodes and Hwato SDZ-IIB stimulators will be used (Suzhou Medical Appliance, Suzhou, China). The ear clips are specially designed with two contact points. In the taVNS group, the stimulation will be applied to the head-end point, while in the sham-taVNS group, it will be applied to the tail-end point. taVNS group

The taVNS electrodes will be placed at bilateral cavum conchae and the cymba conchae (auricular points of CO15 and CO10,27 figure 2), which are richly innervated by the ABVN and represent commonly used target regions for effective taVNS.2830

After routine disinfection of the auricular acupoints, the ear clips for taVNS will be placed at bilateral CO15 and CO10. The stimulation device will be connected to the ear clips, delivering alternating frequency of 4 Hz/20 Hz with a pulse width of 0.2 ms. The current intensity will be individually adjusted to the maximum tolerance by each participant without causing discomfort. Participants will receive taVNS twice daily, once in the morning and once in the evening, with each session lasting 30 min. The two sessions will be separated by a minimum interval of 8 hours. Treatment will be administered 5 days per week for four consecutive weeks, resulting in a total of 40 taVNS sessions (table 1 and figure 3). This treatment schedule was based on prior taVNS studies and optimised for feasibility and adherence.293133

To ensure treatment quality and participant compliance, during each treatment week, participants are required to attend on-site sessions on the morning of the first, third and fifth treatment days. The remaining seven sessions will be performed at home. Prior to the home-based sessions, acupuncturists will provide hands-on training regarding device operation and acupoint localisation. For remote supervision, participants will be required to submit a self-treatment video via an instant messaging application at least once weekly, so that acupuncturists can verify the accuracy of the operation.

Sham-taVNS group

The stimulation sites in sham group will be the bilateral earlobe and scapha, which are areas without vagus nerve distribution (figure 2). After routine disinfection, the ear clips will be placed at these sites and connected to the same electrical stimulator mentioned in the taVNS group. The stimulation parameters, including frequency and pulse width, will be identical to those of the taVNS group, while the current intensity will be set at 0.1 mA. The treatment sessions, durations and supervision arrangements will be the same as those in the taVNS group.

Outcomes

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the change from baseline in the NIH-CPSI total score at the end of the 4-week treatment period (week 4), which serves as the primary endpoint for hypothesis testing and reflects the treatment efficacy of a 4-week intervention. The NIH-CPSI questionnaire comprises three subscores including pain (score range 0–21), urinary symptoms (score range 0–10) and QOL (score range 0–12), across 9 items with a total score ranging from 0 to 43, where higher scores indicate more severe symptoms. The NIH-CPSI is a validated and reliable instrument for assessing symptom severity and QOL in patients with CP/CPPS and is widely recommended for both clinical and research use.3436 The questionnaire typically takes only 5–8 min to complete, making it practical and easy to implement.

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes include: (1) Change from baseline in NIH-CPSI total score at the end of the 4-week follow-up period (week 8, without active intervention) and at the end of the 8-week follow-up period (week 12, without active intervention), to assess the persistence of therapeutic effects after treatment discontinuation; (2) Changes from baseline in the NIH-CPSI pain subscore at the end of weeks 4, 8 and 12; (3) Changes from baseline in the NIH-CPSI urinary subscore at the end of weeks 4, 8 and 12; (4) Changes from baseline in the NIH-CPSI QOL subscore at the end of week 4, 8 and 12; (5) The proportion of responders at week 4, 8 and 12, defined as participants achieving a reduction of ≥6 points in the NIH-CPSI total score compared with baseline. Based on receiver operating characteristic curve analysis, a 6-point reduction in the NIH-CPSI total score was determined to be an optimal cut-off for distinguishing clinically meaningful improvement from non-response37; (6) The change from baseline in the International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) at weeks 4, 8 and 12, with scores ranging from 0 to 35, where higher scores indicate greater symptom severity; (7) The change from baseline in the 5-level European Quality of Life 5-Dimensions-5-Levels questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L) at weeks 4, 8 and 12. The EQ-5D-5L includes two components: the descriptive system and the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS). The descriptive system assesses five dimensions: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression, each rated on five levels (no, slight, moderate, severe and extreme problems). The VAS records self-rated health on a scale from 0 (‘worst health imaginable’) to 100 (‘best health imaginable’), providing a quantitative measure of perceived overall health; (8) The change from baseline in the Self-Rating Anxiety Scale (SAS) at weeks 4, 8 and 12. The total raw score of the 20 items is multiplied by 1.25 to yield a standard score. Scores of 50–59, 60–69 and ≥70 indicate mild, moderate and severe anxiety, respectively, and (9) The change from baseline in the Self-Rating Depression Scale (SDS) at weeks 4, 8 and 12. The total raw score of the 20 items is multiplied by 1.25 to obtain a standard score. Scores of 53–62, 63–72 and ≥73 represent mild, moderate and severe depression, respectively.

Other assessments include: (1) Expectation assessment: prior to randomisation, participants will be asked, ‘Do you think taVNS will be effective?’ and ‘Do you think taVNS will improve your CP/CPPS symptoms?’; (2) Blinding evaluation: at week 4, participants will be asked, ‘Do you think you have received taVNS treatment?’ with response options ‘Yes’, ‘No’, or ‘Unclear’ and (3) Compliance assessment: the proportion of participants with good compliance will be calculated. Completing ≥80% of scheduled treatment sessions will be considered good compliance.

Exploratory outcome: changes from baseline in the NIH-CPSI total score and the three subscale scores at 3 and 6 months after treatment discontinuation, assessed remotely (telephone interview or online questionnaire).

Safety evaluation

All adverse events (AEs) occurring during the study period will be documented and evaluated. An AE is defined as any unfavourable or unexpected symptom, sign or medical condition that arises after the initiation of the study treatment, regardless of whether it is causally related to the intervention. Treatment-related AEs may include dizziness, palpitation, hypotension, ear pain, transient mild headache and tingling sensation at the electrode placement site.38 Serious AEs (SAEs) will be reported and followed up in accordance with the principles of Good Clinical Practice (GCP). Guang’anmen Hospital will assume responsibility for any harm associated with the intervention during the trial.

Sample size calculation

The primary endpoint is the between-group difference in change from baseline in the NIH-CPSI total score at week 4. We selected a conservative between-group difference of 3.1 points, informed by sham-controlled non-pharmacological CP/CPPS trials reporting between-group differences in change ranging from approximately 3.1 to 3.4 points,39 approximately 4.1 to 4.3 points40 41 and around 5.3 points.42 A common SD of 3.4 was assumed based on prior data and clinical experience. The variability of NIH-CPSI total score change in CP/CPPS trials has been reported to range about 3 to 540, reflecting differences in study populations and interventions. With a two-sided α of 0.05 and 90% power, 27 participants per group are required. Allowing for 20% attrition rate, 34 participants per group (68 in total) will be enrolled. Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis will be based on the intention-to-treat population, defined as all participants who have received randomisation with whole baseline data. For continuous variables, means and 95% CIs will be presented; medians, 25th and 75th percentiles will be presented if the data distributed non-normally. For categorical variables, the number and percentage of participants with each category will be presented. Statisticians will compare clinical and demographic characteristics between groups at baseline by t-test, χ² test or Kruskal-Wallis test as appropriate.

Continuous longitudinal data, including the primary outcome, will be analysed using mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) with an appropriate covariance structure. The model will include treatment group, visit and the treatment-by-visit interaction as fixed effects, and participant as a random effect. Secondary longitudinal outcomes, including subscale scores of NIH-CPSI and the IPSS, SAS and SDS, will be analysed using the same MMRM approach as the primary analysis.

For the analysis in the proportion of NIH-CPSI responders, a logistic generalised linear mixed model for repeated measures will be applied. The dependent variable will be the response status (responders vs non-responders) at each scheduled post-baseline visit. The model will include the baseline NIH-CPSI score as a covariate, and treatment group, visit and their interaction as fixed effects. Between-group comparisons at each visit will be estimated using least-squares means from the treatment-by-visit interaction and presented as ORs with corresponding 95% CIs.

For missing data in the primary outcome, multiple imputation will be performed under the missing-at-random assumption to generate 100 imputed datasets of NIH-CPSI total scores. No imputation will be conducted for secondary outcomes. Sensitivity analyses will be performed to assess the robustness of the primary results.

Quality control

Prior to the study, the relevant personnel and graduate students will receive professional and technical training including (1) mastering the implementation procedures of the trial; (2) accurately understanding the diagnostic, inclusion and exclusion criteria; (3) correctly completing the case report form (CRF); (4) properly administering and evaluating each assessment scale and (5) performing the taVNS and sham-taVNS procedures according to the protocol. Research inspectors will regularly monitor the study to: (1) verify that all CRFs are completed in time, consistent with source data and free of logical errors; (2) confirm that any changes in treatment, concomitant medications or reasons for dropout are appropriately documented; (3) periodically inspect whether the implementation of taVNS or sham-taVNS procedures is accurate and standard and (4) ensure that all AEs occurring during the trial are documented and that any SAE is reported to the relevant authorities within the required timeframe.

Ethics and dissemination

The study was designed in line with the Declaration of Helsinki and has been registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06287970). The informed consents of this trial will be obtained from all subjects and/or their legal guardians. The study protocol and informed consent (online supplementary material) were reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Guang’anmen Hospital, China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences (2023-250 KY). The findings will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal and presentations at scientific conferences. The research data will be made available on reasonable request.

Discussion

In this randomised controlled trial, we will evaluate the efficacy and safety of taVNS for moderate to severe CP/CPPS compared with sham-taVNS, primarily through changes in the NIH-CPSI scores. In addition, based on the associated symptoms of CP/CPPS, the IPSS, EQ-5D-5L, SAS and SDS will be used to assess participants’ QOL, general health status and emotional well-being. This study aims to provide preliminary clinical evidence for the effectiveness of taVNS in treating moderate to severe CP/CPPS and to offer clinicians a potential new therapeutic option.

Research has revealed that the vagus nerve projects into the abdominal and pelvic cavities, and its stimulation could relieve chronic pain in these regions.1618 Visceral sensory fibres, one component of the vagus nerve, have central processes that terminate in the nucleus tractus solitarius, while some peripheral branches are distributed to the pelvic and abdominal organs.43 Stimulation of ABVN may activate somatic afferent fibres throughout the body, thereby enhancing vagal efferent effects.44 Moreover, vagus nerve stimulation has been reported to increase cortical mechanical and pressure pain threshold, thus reducing pain level.45 Because most vagal fibres are deeply located, direct stimulation is challenging and inconvenient. Fortunately, the ABVN lies superficially on the ear, making it easily accessible for noninvasive stimulation. Therefore, taVNS represents a potentially effective, convenient and well-tolerated approach for relieving chronic pelvic pain and discomfort in patients with CP/CPPS.

Studies have demonstrated that the skin surrounding the cymba conchae is innervated by the vagus nerve,46 and the cavum conchae near the crus of the helix is also a key distribution area of the ABVN.47 Whereas, the earlobe and scapha belong to the outer ear and lack vagal innervation.48 Therefore, both the cymba conchae and the cavum conchae adjacent to the crus of the helix are considered effective stimulation sites for taVNS. At present, both unilateral and bilateral taVNS protocols are commonly used, and there is no consensus regarding the optimal laterality across indications. Given the peripheral and afferent nature of taVNS, left-sided and right-sided auricular vagal inputs converge and are integrated at the brainstem level, particularly within the nucleus tractus solitarius.4951 Bilateral stimulation may therefore increase the net afferent input and reduce laterality-related uncertainty. In addition, inter-individual variability in auricular vagal anatomy and practical factors affecting stimulation delivery,46 49 especially in partly home-based settings, may contribute to variability in target engagement. From a pragmatic perspective, bilateral taVNS was selected to maximise coverage of auricular vagal afferents and improve the robustness and consistency of neuromodulatory engagement in this complex chronic pain population.

In this study, taVNS will be delivered using an alternating ‘sparse-dense’ waveform of 4/20 Hz. This alternating pattern may help to reduce neural tolerance associated with constant-frequency stimulation.52 Previous non-invasive neuromodulation studies have adopted similar 4/20 Hz patterns, supporting the feasibility and tolerability of this waveform.315356 Although previous taVNS studies have explored a wide range of stimulation frequencies (1–20 000 Hz), no consensus currently exists regarding the optimal settings. The 0.2 ms pulse width used in this trial is consistent with prior taVNS literature31 and corresponds to the fixed output of the clinical stimulator employed. Future investigations should further optimise frequency and pulse width parameters to enhance therapeutic efficacy and safety. Besides, the treatment protocol (twice daily, 30 min per session, 5 days per week for 4 weeks) was formulated based on prior taVNS studies and optimised for feasibility and adherence.293133 This dosing schedule is intended to provide sustained vagal activation while minimising patient burden and has been suggested to represent an adequate neuromodulatory dose capable of inducing clinically relevant neuroplastic effects.31 32 57

Auricular vagus nerve stimulation has been demonstrated to be a safe and well-tolerated therapy; however, establishing an appropriate sham control is essential. To ensure effective blinding, this study will employ specially designed ear clips that simultaneously cover both auricular vagus nerve distribution areas and non-vagal regions. The placement of ear clips in the taVNS and sham-taVNS groups is identical, making them nearly indistinguishable in appearance. Due to the internal circuit configuration, the clips in the taVNS group deliver stimulation exclusively to the vagus-innervated areas, while those in the sham-taVNS group stimulate only the non-vagal regions. Both groups receive electrical stimulation, making it not that easy for participants to identify their group allocation.

Our study has several limitations. First, the sample size of this trial is small, and both the treatment and follow-up duration are short. In particular, the relatively short 8-week follow-up period represents an important limitation, as it captures short-term symptom improvement after taVNS discontinuation but may be insufficient to fully characterise the persistence of therapeutic effects or symptom relapse in a chronic condition such as CP/CPPS. Accordingly, the findings should be interpreted as reflecting short-term efficacy, which may limit their generalisability to longer-term clinical management, where durability of symptom control is of particular clinical interest. Exploratory remote follow-up assessments at 3 and 6 months post-treatment, where feasible, were added to provide preliminary additional information on treatment durability. Second, due to the high treatment frequency, patients will complete most sessions at home. Although acupuncturists will provide detailed instruction on device operation and treatment procedures before treatment and conduct daily follow-ups by telephone, they will not be able to directly monitor participants’ treatment process on site. Third, the acupuncturists cannot be blinded because they are involved in the manipulation of the intervention. Nevertheless, this study will help bridge the current evidence gap and provide data for future clinical research.

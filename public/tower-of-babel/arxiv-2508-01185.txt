Homotopy Type of the Space of Fibrations of the Three-Sphere by Simple Closed Curves
Dennis DeTurck, Ziqi Fang, Herman Gluck, Leandro Lichtenfelz, Mona Merling, Yi Wang, Jingye Yang

Abstract
We show that the moduli space of all smooth fibrations of a three-sphere by simple closed curves has the homotopy type of a disjoint union of a pair of two-spheres if the fibers are oriented, and of a pair of real projective planes if unoriented, the same as for its finite-dimensional subspace of Hopf fibrations by parallel great circles. This moduli space is the quotient of the diffeomorphism group of the three-sphere (a Fréchet Lie group) by its subgroup of automorphisms of the Hopf fibration, which we show is a smooth Fréchet submanifold of the diffeomorphism group. Then we show that the moduli space, already known to be a Fréchet manifold by [ HKMR12 ] , can be modeled on the concrete Fréchet space of vector fields on the three-sphere which are “horizontal” and “balanced” with respect to a given Hopf fibration, and see how the structure of this moduli space helps us to determine its homotopy type.

1.
Introduction

We begin with the Hopf fibration
H\colon S^{1}\subset S^{3}\xrightarrow{p}S^{2}
, in which the unit 3-sphere
S^{3}
in real 4-space
\mathbb{R}^{4}
is filled with parallel great circles, as discovered by Heinz Hopf in 1931. These great circles are the intersections of
S^{3}
with the complex lines in the
\mathbb{C}^{2}
corresponding to an orthogonal complex structure on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.The
projection map
S^{3}\xrightarrow{p}S^{2}
was the first example of a homotopically nontrivial map from a sphere
to another sphere of lower dimension, and signaled the birth of homotopy theory.

How many Hopf fibrations of
S^{3}
are there? If we orient all the great circle fibers and then focus
on the direction of the fiber through a single point of
S^{3}
, we can see a two-sphere’s worth of Hopf
fibrations with a right-handed screw sense, and another two-sphere’s worth with a left-handed
screw sense. There are no more.

In 1983,
[
GW83
]
showed that the much larger (infinite-dimensional) space
of all (topological or smooth) fibrations of
S^{3}
by (not necessarily parallel) oriented great circles
deformation retracts to its subspace of Hopf fibrations, and so also has the homotopy type of a pair
of two-spheres. In this paper, ”smooth” always means of class
C^{\infty}
.

Carrying that result to its extreme, we show here that the same homotopy type persists when we
expand our view to include all possible smooth fibrations of
S^{3}
by simple closed curves.

Theorem A
.

The space of all smooth fibrations of a three-sphere by simple closed curves, with a
C^{\infty}
topology, has the homotopy type of a disjoint union of a pair of two-spheres if the fibers are oriented, and of a pair of real projective planes if unoriented, the same as for its finite-dimensional subspace of Hopf fibrations by parallel great circles.

We position our story in the world of (typically infinite-dimensional) metrizable Fréchet manifolds and smooth maps between them, give a brief introduction to this in
Section 2
, and profit later because in this world, weak homotopy equivalences can be promoted to homotopy equivalences. In particular, the group
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
of all diffeomorphisms of the three-sphere lives in this world, and is a Fréchet Lie group.

Inside that group is the subgroup
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
of automorphisms of the Hopf fibration
H
, meaning diffeomorphisms of
S^{3}
which permute the fibers of
H
, with stretching and compressing allowed.

To confirm that this subgroup lives in the Fréchet world, we prove

Theorem B
.

The subgroup
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
of automorphisms of a Hopf fibration
H
is a smooth Fréchet submanifold of
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
.

Fréchet manifolds are modeled after Fréchet (vector) spaces, which can then be viewed as their tangent spaces at distinguished points. For example, the tangent space to
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
at the identity is the Fréchet space of all smooth vector fields on the three-sphere. We can write it as

T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})=\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})

and give it the
L^{2}
inner product defined by the integral of the pointwise inner product,

\langle X,Y\rangle=\int_{S^{3}}X(x)\cdot Y(x)\,\mathrm{d(Vol)}.

The
Riemannian exponential map
takes a portion of the tangent space

T_{\mathrm{id}}\,\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})=\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})
to a portion of the diffeomorphism group
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
, and provides
coordinate charts. A useful variant of this, the
aligned Riemannian exponential map
, is defined
in
Section 3
, proved there to give the same Fréchet structure on
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
, and then used to good effect in the proof of
Theorem B
above.

Likewise, the tangent space to the subgroup
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
at the identity consists of smooth vector fields on the three-sphere which project down by
p_{*}
to well-defined vector fields on the base space
S^{2}
of the Hopf fibration. This means that their “vertical” components – those parallel
to the Hopf fibers – are unconstrained, but that their “horizontal” components orthogonal to the Hopf fibers point systematically from each Hopf fiber to another Hopf fiber. We make this precise in
Section 2
. Because of this alternative description, we sometimes write the tangent space to
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
at the identity as

T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)=\mathrm{PVF}(S^{3})

which we refer to as the space of
projectable vector fields
.

The
L^{2}
-orthogonal complement to
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
consists of smooth vector fields
V
on
S^{3}
which
are horizontal and which integrate to zero along each Hopf fiber
F
. We refer to these vector fields
as
balanced
, denote the subspace of them by
\mathrm{BVF}(S^{3})
, and prove that the direct sum

\mathrm{PVF}(S^{3})\oplus\mathrm{BVF}(S^{3})=\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})

gives all of
\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})
. Applying the standard Riemannian exponential map to a neighborhood of the zero field in
\mathrm{BVF}(S^{3})
gives us a local Fréchet submanifold
\mathrm{Bal}(H)
of
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
, our balanced diffeomorphisms of
S^{3}
.

We denote set of all smooth fibrations of
S^{3}
by oriented simple closed curves by
\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3})
. Because all of these fibrations are equivalent
to one another by diffeomorphisms of
S^{3}
, we have a sequence

\mathrm{Aut}(H)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})\to\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3}).

Both
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
and
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
with their
C^{\infty}
topologies are Fréchet manifolds, and as a start for

\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3})
to join this world, we give it the quotient topology from the above projection. We regard the local Fréchet submanifold
\mathrm{Bal}(H)
of
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
as the most natural home for a smooth slice to prove the smooth Fréchet bundle structure, and devote
Section 4
to providing this:

Theorem C
.

The sequence
\mathrm{Aut}(H)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})\to\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3})
admits a smooth slice consisting of balanced diffeomorphisms, and is therefore a smooth fiber bundle of Fréchet manifolds.

The Fréchet bundle structure was already proved in
[
HKMR12
]
by a different argument based on the center-of-mass construction of
[
Kar77
]
. Instead, here, the existence of a smooth slice consisting of balanced diffeomorphisms is confirmed by a convex optimization
argument, and has the advantage of displaying the Fréchet manifold structure of our moduli space
\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3})
as modeled on the “concrete” Fréchet space of balanced vector fields on
S^{3}
.

With all this in hand, the proof of
Theorem A
focuses on the Fréchet fiber bundle from
Theorem C
. By a preliminary argument, we determine the homotopy groups of the fiber
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
.
And we know from Hatcher’s theorem
[
Hat83
]
that
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
deformation retracts to the
orthogonal group
O(4)
. We then use the rigid symmetries of the Hopf fibration
[
GWZ86
]
to identify a finite-dimensional sub-bundle of the above bundle with well-known fiber and total space and with base space
S^{2}\sqcup S^{2}
.
Its inclusion into the big bundle above is seen to induce isomorphisms of homotopy groups of
fiber and of total space, so by the Five Lemma, it induces isomorphisms of homotopy groups of
base spaces. Then the weak homotopy equivalence
S^{2}\cup S^{2}\to\textup{Fib}(S^{3})
is promoted to a
homotopy equivalence using the Fréchet machinery, proving
Theorem A
.

Then, thanks to
[
And66
]
and
[
HS70
]
, we can bring our story to a close by promoting
Theorem A
to the following result.

Theorem D
.

The space
\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3})
is homeomorphic to two disjoint copies of
S^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
.

Closely related results.

Theorem A
fits into a general program of studying the moduli space of smooth fibrations of a given manifold
M

by copies of a given fiber
F
and determining not only its number of components, but its entire homotopy type. For most closed aspherical orientable
3
-manifolds
E
, the space of smooth fibrations of
E
by simple closed curves is known to have contractible components
[
HKMR12
]
.

Some of the authors of this paper have also pursued other related results of this form.
For example, in
[
WY24
]
the authors show that the space of all smooth fibrations of
S^{1}\times S^{2}
by oriented simple closed curves has four components, each having the homotopy type of the based loop space
\Omega S^{3}
of the 3-sphere; this is the first known example of a space of smooth fibrations of a 3-manifold which does
not have the homotopy type of a finite-dimensional space.
As another example, in a more systematic spirit,
[
Fan25
]
builds on a range of existing work to establish general results and frameworks for studying such infinite-dimensional moduli spaces, from both topological and differential perspectives. In particular, applications include deducing finite-dimensional deformation retracts inside these infinite-dimensional moduli of smooth fibrations for various low-dimensional ambient manifolds (such as closed surfaces and irreducible closed 3-manifolds, recovering the known cases as well as completing the remaining unknown cases for dimensions up to 3). For example, the author shows that the space of all smooth fibrations of a flat two-torus by oriented simple closed curves has contractible components, and each component contains a unique fibering by oriented parallel closed geodesics, to which the component deformation retracts via the curve-shortening flow for families.

To see
Theorem B
in a wider context, we may ask, for other natural geometric structures on the three-sphere, whether the corresponding automorphism subgroups of
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
also inherit a smooth Fréchet submanifold structure.

This is known to hold for the subgroup
\mathrm{Aut}_{1}(\xi)
of
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
consisting of strict contactomorphisms
[
Omo74
]
. For the full contactomorphism subgroup
\mathrm{Aut}(\xi)
, this appeared in
[
Ham82
]

as Problem 2.5.6, and to our knowledge is still open. By contrast, in the Sobolev category,

\mathrm{Aut}(\xi)
is
not
a smooth Fréchet submanifold of
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
(
[
EP15
,
Omo74
,
Smo06
]
).

Conventions.

Throughout,
\textup{Diff}\,(S^{3})
denotes the topological group of diffeomorphisms of the
3-sphere
with
the
C^{\infty}
topology. Multiplication and inversion are continuous in this topology, and later, when
we promote
\textup{Diff}\,(S^{3})
to the category of smooth Fréchet manifolds and smooth maps between them, we will note that multiplication and inversion are smooth, and that
\textup{Diff}\,(S^{3})
is a Fréchet Lie group.

When we speak of smooth fibrations of a three-sphere by simple closed curves, we mean a fiber bundle in the category of smooth manifolds and smooth maps between them. These are all equivalent in the topological category, and we will indicate later why they are all equivalent in the smooth category.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some preliminary results about Fréchet manifolds and their homotopy theory. In addition, we give explicit descriptions, in terms of basic left-invariant vector fields on
S^{3}
, of the various Fréchet spaces of interest to us.

2.1.
Fréchet vector spaces and manifolds

Fréchet spaces, manifolds and Lie groups provide the setting for extending the theory of finite-dimensional smooth manifolds and smooth maps between them to infinite dimensions. We set vocabulary here and then refer the reader to some of the major sources.

A
Fréchet space
V
is a complete metrizable vector space whose topology is induced by a countable family of semi-norms, where a semi-norm
r
is like a norm, except for the axiom that asks for
r(v)=0
to imply that
v=0
.

A collection
\{\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
of semi-norms on
V
defines a unique topology if we declare that a sequence
f_{j}
converges to
f
if and only if
\|f_{j}-f\|_{\alpha}\to 0
for each
\alpha\in I
.

A Fréchet space
V
is
Hausdorff
if and only if
\|f\|_{\alpha}=0
for all
\alpha
implies
f=0
. It is
metrizable
if and only if the collection
\{\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}:\alpha\in I\}
can be replaced by a countable collection
\{\|\cdot\|_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, which defines the same topology. In that case, one such metric is

d(x,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\frac{\|x-y\|_{n}}{1+\|x-y\|_{n}}.

It is
complete
if every Cauchy sequence converges, where “Cauchy” here means that for each fixed
\alpha\in I
, we have

\|f_{i}-f_{j}\|_{\alpha}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad i,j\to\infty.

Smooth (
C^{\infty}
) maps between open subsets of Fréchet spaces are defined, as in the finite-dimensional case, in terms of the convergence of various difference quotients.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Fréchet manifold
is a Hausdorff topological space with an atlas of coordinate charts taking their values in Fréchet spaces, such that the coordinate transition functions are all smooth maps between open sets in Fréchet spaces.

We collect the following facts about Fréchet manifolds from the paper
[
Pal66
]
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
Pal66
, Cor. 2]
)
.

If each component of a Fréchet manifold
X
satisfies the second axiom of countability, then
X
is metrizable.

A space
X
is said to be
dominated
by a space
Y
if there exist maps
f:X\to Y
and
g:Y\to X
such that
g\circ f:X\to X
is homotopic to the identity map of
X
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
Pal66
, Thm. 14]
)
.

A metrizable Fréchet manifold is dominated by a simplicial complex.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
Pal66
, Thm. 15]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metrizable Fréchet manifolds. Then any weak homotopy equivalence
f:X\to Y
is in fact a homotopy equivalence.

We will use this in the last step of the proof of our
Theorem A
to promote weak homotopy equivalence to homotopy equivalence.

A
Fréchet Lie group
is a Fréchet manifold
G
with a group structure in which the multiplication map
G\times G\to G
and the inversion map
G\to G
are both smooth maps of Fréchet manifolds. For example, if
M
is a finite-dimensional smooth manifold, then multiplication (i.e., composition) and inversion in
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
are smooth maps, making it into a Fréchet Lie group.

The example of most importance to us is the space
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
of smooth diffeomorphisms of the 3-sphere to itself, which is modelled on the Fréchet vector space
\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})
of smooth vector fields on
S^{3}
. The coordinate charts are provided by the
Riemannian exponential map
, which converts each short vector field
V
into a diffeomorphism sending points
x
of
S^{3}
to the endpoints of the geodesics directed by
V(x)
. The following result is of importance to us.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
KM97
]
)
.

For any closed smooth finite-dimensional manifold
M
, the Fréchet Lie group
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
of smooth diffeomorphisms is separable and metrizable.

For a detailed discussion of Fréchet vector spaces and manifolds, we refer the reader to the paper
[
Ham82
]
and the book
[
KM97
]
. To get a sense of the early development of this subject, see
[
Eel58
,
Eel66
,
Pal66
,
Les67
]
. We have also offered an overview in
[
DGL+24
, Appendix A]
.

2.2.
Left-invariant vector fields on
S^{3}

Vector fields on the 3-sphere
S^{3}
become more concrete when we view
S^{3}
as the space of unit quaternions, and use the three orthonormal left-invariant vector fields

A(x)=xi,\quad B(x)=xj,\quad C(x)=xk,

written in terms of quaternion multiplication, as a basis for
\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})
as a module over the ring of smooth real-valued functions on
S^{3}
.

Figure 2.
The quaternion product
A(x)=xi
may be visualized as a point on
S^{3}
,
or as a unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
from the origin to this point,

or as a unit vector tangent at
x
to the great circle from
x
to
xi
.

Any smooth vector field
X
on
S^{3}
can be expressed as
X=f\,A+g\,B+h\,C
, where the coefficients are smooth real-valued functions on
S^{3}
. We will need to know the Lie brackets of these basis vector fields, which are easily computed to be

(2.2)

[A,B]=2C,\quad[B,C]=2A,\quad[C,A]=2B.

We next choose our Hopf fibration
H
so that
A
is the unit vector field tangent to the Hopf fibers, which are then the great circles in
S^{3}
taken to themselves via right multiplication by
i
, and oriented in this direction from
x
to
xi
. We write this Hopf fibration as

H\;:\;S^{1}\;\subset\;S^{3}\;\overset{p}{\to}\;S^{2}.

The Fréchet vector space
\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})
is the tangent space at the identity to the Fréchet Lie group
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
. We will use the
L^{2}
inner product on
\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})
, which is given by the integral of the pointwise inner product on
S^{3}
:

(2.3)

\langle X,Y\rangle=\int_{S^{3}}X(x)\cdot Y(x)\;\mathrm{d(Vol)}.

Remark 2.4
.

The metric topology coming from this inner product is not the same as the smooth topology on
\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})
; it does not have nearly enough open sets. And unlike the smooth topology, this metric topology is not complete because differentiability can be lost in the limit.

We now introduce the main player, the automorphism group of the Hopf fibration.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
denote the subgroup of
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
consisting of diffeomorphisms which permute the fibers of
H
, with stretching and compressing allowed.

We will see in the proof of Theorem B that
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
is a smooth Fréchet submanifold of
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
, and give in advance a characterization of its tangent space at the identity.

Proposition 2.4
.

The smooth vector field
X=fA+gB+hC
on
S^{3}
lies in the tangent space
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
if and only if

(1)

f=
any smooth real-valued function on
S^{3}
;

(2)

g=-\frac{1}{2}Ah
, indicating a directional derivative, here and below;

(3)

h=\frac{1}{2}Ag
.

Proof.

To begin, we assert that

T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)=\{X\in\mathrm{VF}(S^{3}):\mathcal{L}_{X}A=\lambda A\text{ for smooth }\lambda:S^{3}\to\mathbb{R}\},

where
\mathcal{L}_{X}
is the Lie derivative with respect to
X
.

To see this, let
\{\varphi_{t}\}
be a curve of diffeomorphisms of
S^{3}
through the identity when
t=0
, all lying in the subgroup
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
of
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
, and let
X\in\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})
be tangent to this curve at the identity. Then by definition,

(\mathcal{L}_{X}A)(x)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\varphi_{t}^{*}A(\varphi_{t}(x)).

In order for
\varphi_{t}\in\mathrm{Aut}(H)
, the pullback
\varphi_{t}^{*}A(\varphi_{t}(x))
must be some variable multiple of
A(x)
, depending on both
x
and
t
, and therefore its time-zero derivative,
(\mathcal{L}_{X}A)(x)
must be a variable multiple of
A(x)
, depending just on
x
. That is,
(\mathcal{L}_{X}A)(x)=\lambda(x)A(x)
.

Now write a vector field
X
on
S^{3}
as
X=fA

+

gB

+

hC
and compute
\mathcal{L}_{X}A
to see what constraints the condition
\mathcal{L}_{X}A=\lambda A
imposes on the coefficients
f,g
and
h
.

Notationally, we switch from Lie derivatives to Lie brackets and compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X}A

\displaystyle=[X,A]=[fA+gB+hC,A]

\displaystyle=[fA,A]+[gB,A]+[hC,A]

\displaystyle=-[A,fA]-[A,gB]-[A,hC]

\displaystyle=-(Af)A-f[A,A]-(Ag)B-g[A,B]-(Ah)C-h[A,C]

\displaystyle=-(Af)A-(Ag)B-g(2C)-(Ah)C-h(-2B)

\displaystyle=-(Af)A+(2h-Ag)B-(2g+Ah)C.

Therefore
X\in T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
if and only if
-Af=\lambda
for some smooth real-valued function
\lambda
, which is no constraint at all, and
2h-Ag=0
and
2g+Ah=0
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Remarks 2.1
.

We collect the following observations.

•

The vector fields in
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
are said to be
projectable
or
aligned
with the Hopf projection
p\colon S^{3}\to S^{2}
in the sense that they project down to well-defined vector fields on the base space
S^{2}
. The space of such fields is also denoted
\mathrm{PVF}(S^{3})
.

•

We also note that the above conditions persist under limits in the smooth topology on
\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})
,
confirming that
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
is a closed subspace.

•

The tangent space
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
is a module over the ring of smooth real-valued functions on
the base space
S^{2}
of the Hopf fibration
H
, equivalently over the ring of smooth real-valued

S^{3}
which are constant along each Hopf fiber.

•

We can decouple equations
(2)
and
(3)
in
2.4
by differentiating again with respect

and, paying attention to constants, learn that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\text{(i)}\quad&g(t)=c_{1}\cos 2t+c_{2}\sin 2t\\
\text{(ii)}\quad&h(t)=c_{2}\cos 2t-c_{1}\sin 2t,\end{split}

where the Hopf circles are parametrized by

, and where the coefficients

and

are Jacobi fields along the central orange fiber of
H
.

Next, we introduce the subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
consisting of those automorphisms that take each Hopf fiber to itself.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathrm{VAut}(H)\subset\mathrm{Aut}(H)
be the subgroup of automorphisms of
H
which take each oriented fiber to itself, but still with stretching and compressing allowed.

Note that the following condition, which ensures that a smooth vector field lies in the tangent space to
\mathrm{VAut}(H)
at the identity, is immediate from the definition.

Proposition 2.5
.

The smooth vector field
X=fA+gB+hC
lies in
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{VAut}(H)
if and only if

(1)

f=
any smooth real-valued function on
S^{3}
;

(2)

g=0
;

(3)

h=0
.

In a similar way, we give a characterization of smooth vector fields which live in the orthogonal complement of the tangent space to
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
at the identity.

Proposition 2.6
.

The
L^{2}
orthogonal complement
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)^{\perp}
consists of vector fields of the form
{X=fA+gB+hC}
such that

(1)

f=0
, and along each Hopf circle, we have that

(2)

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}g\cos 2t-h\sin 2t\,dt=0
,

(3)

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}g\sin 2t+h\cos 2t\,dt=0
.

Proof.

For
X=fA+gB+hC
to be
L^{2}
orthogonal to
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
, we certainly must have
f=0
. Then, referring to the decoupled equations (
2.6
), we must also have along each Hopf fiber that

(2.8)

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}g(c_{1}\cos 2t+c_{2}\sin 2t)+h(c_{2}\cos 2t-c_{1}\sin 2t)\,dt=0,

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are smooth functions on
S^{3}
which are constant on Hopf fibers. Putting
c_{1}=1
and
c_{2}=0
yields condition
(2)
above, while putting
c_{1}=0
and
c_{2}=1
gives condition
(3)
.

Alternatively expressed, the vector field
X
is
L^{2}
-orthogonal to
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
if and only if it is horizontal and is
L^{2}
-orthogonal along each Hopf circle to the two-dimensional linear space of vector fields there discussed above.
∎

Just as we noted earlier for
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
, its
L^{2}
orthogonal complement
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)^{\perp}
is a module over the ring of smooth functions on
S^{2}
, equivalently over the ring of smooth real-valued
functions on the total space
S^{3}
which are constant along each Hopf fiber, and this lets us
convert
L^{2}
orthogonality on
S^{3}
to
L^{2}
orthogonality along each great circle fiber of
H
.

Proposition 2.7
.

We have the
L^{2}
orthogonal direct sum

(2.9)

T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)\oplus T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)^{\perp}=T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})=\mathrm{VF}(S^{3}).

Proof.

This would have been immediate if
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
, with its
L^{2}
inner product, were complete,
and thus a Hilbert space. Given a smooth vector field
X=fA+gB+hC
on
S^{3}
, we want
to write
X=X_{1}+X_{2}
,

(2.10)

X_{1}=f_{1}A+g_{1}B+h_{1}C\in T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)\quad\text{and}\quad X_{2}=f_{2}A+g_{2}B+h_{2}C\in T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)^{\perp}.

To begin, we must set
f_{1}=f
and
f_{2}=0
. We revisit the decoupled equations for vector fields in
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
given in (
2.6
):

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\text{(i)}\quad g(t)&=c_{1}\cos 2t+c_{2}\sin 2t,\\
\text{(ii)}\quad h(t)&=c_{2}\cos 2t-c_{1}\sin 2t.\end{split}

Then consider the vector fields

(2.12)

Y=0A+(\cos 2t)B-(\sin 2t)C,

(2.13)

Z=0A+(\sin 2t)B+(\cos 2t)C,

obtained from
(i)
and
(ii)
above by first setting
c_{1}=1
and
c_{2}=0
to get
Y
, and then setting
c_{1}=0
and
c_{2}=1
to get
Z
. They form a “basis” for the horizontal vector fields in
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
when this vector space is regarded as a module over the ring of smooth functions on
S^{2}
, and when such functions are lifted to smooth functions on
S^{3}
constant on Hopf fibers.

In the
L^{2}
inner product along a single Hopf fiber, we have

(2.14)

\langle Y,Y\rangle=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\cos^{2}2t+\sin^{2}2t\,dt=2\pi,

so in this sense,
Y/\sqrt{2\pi}
is a unit vector, and likewise for
Z
. Furthermore
\langle Y,Z\rangle=0
.

Now, given the vector field
X=fA+gB+hC
on
S^{3}
, we work along a single Hopf fiber
and orthogonally project
X
to
X_{1}\in T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
, writing

(2.15)

X_{1}=fA+\frac{\langle X,Y\rangle}{2\pi}Y+\frac{\langle X,Z\rangle}{2\pi}Z.

Here

(2.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle X,Y\rangle&=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}g\cos 2t-h\sin 2t\,dt,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle X,Z\rangle=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}g\sin 2t+h\cos 2t\,dt,\end{split}

which we abbreviate by
\alpha
and
\beta
, respectively. Then

(2.17)

\begin{split}X_{1}&=fA+\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}(\cos 2t\,B-\sin 2t\,C)+\frac{\beta}{2\pi}(\sin 2t\,B+\cos 2t\,C)\\
&=fA+\left(\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\cos 2t+\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\sin 2t\right)B+\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\sin 2t+\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\cos 2t\right)C,\end{split}

which lies in
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)
. So the task now is to show that the difference,

(2.18)

\begin{split}X_{2}=X-X_{1}=0A+\left(g-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\cos 2t-\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\sin 2t\right)B\\
+\left(h+\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\sin 2t-\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\cos 2t\right)C,\end{split}

lies in
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)^{\perp}
. In this last formula, let
g_{2}
and
h_{2}
abbreviate the coefficients of
B
and
C
, respectively. So we must confirm the conditions for membership in
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)^{\perp}
as they were given in
2.6
:

(2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}g_{2}\cos 2t-h_{2}\sin 2t\,dt=0,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}g_{2}\sin 2t+h_{2}\cos 2t\,dt=0.\end{split}

We prove the first equation in (
2.19
).

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}g_{2}\cos 2t-h_{2}\sin 2t\,dt&=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(g-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\cos 2t-\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\sin 2t)\cos 2t-(h+\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\sin 2t-\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\cos 2t)\sin 2t\,dt\\
&=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(g\cos 2t-h\sin 2t-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi})\,dt\\
&=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(g\cos 2t-h\sin 2t)\,dt-\alpha=0,\end{split}

by definition of
\alpha
, confirming the first equation in (
2.19
). The second equation is confirmed in a similar way, using the definition of
\beta
.

Thus
X_{2}=X-X_{1}
really does lie in
T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)^{\perp}
. From the explicit formulas above, one can verify that the projection maps from
\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})
onto each summand are continuous, and this completes the proof of
2.7
.
∎

Remark 2.20
.

T_{\mathrm{id}}\mathrm{Aut}(H)^{\perp}
will play the role of the tangent space
T_{H}\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3})
,
on which our moduli space
\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3})
will be modeled as a Fréchet manifold.

2.3.
Balanced vector fields and diffeomorphisms

We give here an alternative interpretation of the

orthogonal complement to the tangent space
at the identity of the automorphism group of the Hopf fibration, in which we view the elements
of

as balanced vector fields on
S^{3}
, and their images under the exponential map as balanced diffeomorphisms of
S^{3}
. First, we introduce some useful constructions and definitions.

Integrating a horizontal vector field along a Hopf fiber
. If
V
is a horizontal vector field along a Hopf fiber

, we project it down to a circle’s worth of tangent vectors to
S^{2}
at the point
p(F)
and integrate them with respect to arc length along

at that point.

When we divide this vector by the length
2\pi
of the Hopf fiber

, we denote it by

(2.21)

\overline{V}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{F}V

and refer to
\overline{V}
as the

of

along
F
.

Later we will need to lift this tangent vector
\overline{V}
to a horizontal vector field along the fiber
F

in
S^{3}
, and will still call it
\overline{V}
there. We keep in mind that the Hopf projection
p
doubles the lengths of horizontal vectors, and so lifting vectors halves their lengths. Alternatively, one can take the base space of the Hopf fibration to be a two-sphere of radius

so that the Hopf projection will preserve the lengths of horizontal vectors.

We note that the image
\mathrm{Exp}_{F}\,\overline{V}
of

under the diffeomorphism
\mathrm{Exp}\,\overline{V}
is also a Hopf fiber.

Definition 2.22
.

A vector field

on

is said to be

along each Hopf fiber is zero.

We will see later that the horizontal left-invariant vector fields

and

are balanced along each Hopf fiber.

will be denoted by
\mathrm{BVF}(S^{3})
.
It is a submodule of
\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})
over the ring of smooth real-valued functions on
S^{2}
,
equivalently, over the ring of smooth functions on
S^{3}
which are constant along Hopf fibers.

Definition 2.23
.

If
V
is a balanced vector field on
S^{3}
, not too large, then its image
f=\mathrm{Exp}\,V
under the Riemannian exponential map, will be called a
balanced diffeomorphism
of
S^{3}
. The set of these will be denoted by
\mathrm{Bal}(H)
.

In the next section, we will prove that the subgroup
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
of automorphisms of the Hopf fibration

is a smooth Fréchet submanifold of
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
, and the argument we give will at the same time show that a neighborhood of the identity
in
\mathrm{Bal}(H)
is also a smooth Fréchet submanifold of
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})
.

After that, we will prove that a small neighborhood of the identity in
\mathrm{Bal}(H)
can serve as a smooth local slice for our big bundle

\mathrm{Aut}(H)\subset\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})\to\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3}).

This in turn will lead us to an alternative proof of the theorem of Rubinstein et al
[

]
that our moduli space

of smooth fibrations of

by oriented simple closed curves is a smooth Fréchet manifold, and that our big bundle above is a fiber bundle in the category of smooth Fréchet manifolds and smooth maps between them.

Corollary 2.28

The horizontal left-invariant vector fields

Theorem 2.2
.

The

\mathrm{PVF}(S^{3})\oplus\mathrm{BVF}(S^{3})=\mathrm{VF}(S^{3})

Proof.

By

is orthogonal to

if and only if along each Hopf fiber

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(g\cos 2t-h\sin 2t)\,dt=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(g\sin 2t+h\cos 2t)\,dt=0.

From

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(g\cos 2t-h\sin 2t)\,dt=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(g\sin 2t+h\cos 2t)\,dt=0.

of

\nabla_{U}V(t)
can be made arbitrarily close in the
C^{0}
topology to the vector
-U
for fixed target image fiber
F_{0}^{\prime}
and for hunters in a sufficiently small open subset of
S^{2}
. The understanding here is that to do this, we first make the blue image target fibers sufficiently close to vertical in the
C^{\infty}
topology (though
C^{1}
will do the job).

Now we prove that the covariant derivative
\nabla_{U}\overline{V}
of the balance field
\overline{V}
can be made arbitrarily close to the vector
-U
in the
C^{0}
topology for sufficiently small open subsets of
S^{2}
.

We start with a diffeomorphism
f
of
S^{3}
close to the identity, a Hopf fiber
F_{0}
which will be the source of our target, the blue image fiber
f(F_{0})
which is our actual target, and a nearby Hopf fiber
F
which is our hunter of the moment.

At each point of
F
we have a horizontal vector
V(t)
whose exponential image runs horizontally from that point to a point on the target fiber
f(F_{0})
.

Then we let
U
be a tangent vector to
S^{2}
at the point
p(F)
, and lift
U
to a projectable vector field of the same name along
F
, meaning that at each point of
F
, the vector
U
there projects to the given tangent vector
U
at the point
p(F)
on
S^{2}
. Up in
S^{3}
, we have the
C^{0}
approximation
\nabla_{U}V(t)\sim-U
. Next, we consider the average value of
V
along
F
,
\overline{V}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{F}V(t)dt
, and take its covariant derivative with respect to
U
:

(4.14)

\nabla_{U}\overline{V}=\nabla_{U}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{F}V(t)dt=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{F}\nabla_{U}V(t)dt\sim\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{F}(-U)dt\sim-U

as desired. This completes the proof of
Lemma 4.8
.
∎

With these lemmas in hand, we are ready to prove
4.1
.

Proof of
4.1
.

We will show that the Hessian of the energy function

(4.15)

E(V\text{ on }F)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{F}\frac{1}{2}\|V(t)\|^{2}dt

is positive definite, and hence that
E
is strongly convex for each choice of initial Hopf fiber
F_{0}
.

A coordinate-free expression for the Hessian is

(4.16)

(\mathrm{Hess}\,E)(U,W)=\langle\nabla_{U}(\nabla E),W\rangle.

Therefore we have

(4.17)

(\mathrm{Hess}\,E)(U,W)=\langle\nabla_{U}(\nabla E),W\rangle=\langle\nabla_{U}(-\overline{V}),W\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{U}\overline{V},W\rangle\sim-\langle-U,W\rangle=\langle U,W\rangle,

using
Lemma 4.4
and
Lemma 4.8
. Thus,
\mathrm{Hess}(E)
is positive definite and the energy function
E
is strongly convex for each choice of initial Hopf fiber
F_{0}
, as claimed.
∎

We finally build on these results to prove
Theorem 4.2
.

Proof of
Theorem 4.2
.

We will show that the unique minimum point
m
of the energy function
E
on an open subset of
S^{2}
varies smoothly with the choice of initial Hopf fiber
F_{0}
.

In local coordinates
x_{1},x_{2}
on
S^{2}
, the Hessian of the energy function
E
can be expressed in terms of first and second partial derivatives of
E
together with the Christoffel symbols
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
of the Riemannian metric as

(4.18)

(\mathrm{Hess}\,E)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\right)=\frac{\partial^{2}E}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}-\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\frac{\partial E}{\partial x_{k}}

where we understand summation on
k
.

If we use spherical coordinates on
S^{2}
, with singularities at the poles, then the Christoffel symbols are bounded outside neighborhoods of these poles. Hence choosing our coordinates so that the unique minimum point
m
of the energy function
E
lies on the equator, and staying very close to
m
so that the first partials
\partial E/\partial x_{1}
and
\partial E/\partial x_{2}
are almost zero, we see that positive definiteness of
\mathrm{Hess}(E)
will imply positive definiteness of the matrix
(\partial^{2}E/\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j})
.

This in turn will let us use the Implicit Function Theorem to finish the argument, and to facilitate that, we will switch notation for local coordinates, replacing
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
by
x
and
y
, and using subscript notation for partial derivatives. In particular, we replace the matrix
(\partial^{2}E/\partial{x_{i}}\partial{x_{j}})
by the matrix

(4.19)

D^{2}E=\begin{pmatrix}E_{xx}&E_{xy}\\
E_{yx}&E_{yy}\end{pmatrix}.

Then we apply the Implicit Function Theorem to the function

\varphi_{u,v}(x,y)=\big(E_{x},\;E_{y}\big)

to see that there will be a smooth function

(4.20)

\psi\colon(u,v)\mapsto(x,y)=(x(u,v),y(u,v))

such that

(4.21)

E_{x}(u,v,x(u,v),y(u,v))=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad E_{y}(u,v,x(u,v),y(u,v))=0.

This will tell us that the location
(x(u,v),y(u,v))
of the minimum of the energy function
E(u,v,x,y)
depends smoothly on the parameters
(u,v)
.

Thus the minimum point
m
of
E
varies smoothly with the choice of initial Hopf fiber
F_{0}
, completing the proof of
Theorem 4.2
.
∎

4.2.
Proof of the smooth slice theorem for
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})

Refer again to
Figure 7
, where we start with a left coset
f\,\mathrm{Aut}(H)
which is close to
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
, and have modified the representative
f
so that it is close to the identity and moves points horizontally.

We must find a balanced diffeomorphism of
S^{3}
in this same coset of
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
, and begin by focusing on a random fiber
F_{0}
of the Hopf fibration
H
, and the corresponding blue image fiber
f(F_{0})
, our target.

We then conduct our earlier described hunt, in which nearby Hopf fibers
F
shoot horizontally for the fixed target
f(F_{0})
with a horizontal vector field
V
.

We consider the strongly convex energy function

E(V\text{ on }F)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{F}\frac{1}{2}\|V(t)\|^{2}dt,

with the Hopf fiber
F
sitting in
S^{3}
directly above the unique minimum point
m
of this energy function winning the hunt by shooting for the target with a balanced vector field.

By
Theorem 4.2
, this winning Hopf fiber
F
depends smoothly on the choice of initial Hopf fiber
F_{0}
. The horizontal map from
F_{0}
to
F
, when sufficiently close to the identity in the
C^{\infty}
topology, will be a diffeomorphism, and thus an element of
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
.

Hence the balanced vector field
V
from
F
to
f(F_{0})
is smooth, and its Riemannian exponential

(4.22)

f_{\mathrm{bal}}=\mathrm{Exp}\,V

lies in the same left coset of
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
as did our starting diffeomorphism
f
because both take the Hopf fibration to the same image fibration. In
Figure 7
, the diffeomorphism
f_{\mathrm{bal}}
takes the place of the question mark.

In this way, we have shown that the bundle
\mathrm{Aut}(H)\subset\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})\to\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3})
admits a smooth slice consisting of balanced diffeomorphisms, and it then follows that it is a smooth fiber bundle of Fréchet manifolds, and that its base space
\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3})
is a smooth Fréchet manifold, modeled on the Fréchet space of balanced vector fields on
S^{3}
.

5.
Smooth Fréchet bundle structure

The key to computing the homotopy type of
\textup{Fib}(S^{3})
is to leverage two related bundle structures, the “big bundle” from Theorem
C
,

(5.1)

\mathrm{Aut}(H)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3})\xrightarrow{p}\textup{Fib}(S^{3})

and a “small bundle”,

(5.2)

\mathrm{VAut}(H)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(H)\xrightarrow{p}\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(S^{2}),

where the first map is the inclusion of the subgroup of automorphisms of the Hopf fibration that take each Hopf fiber to itself, with stretching and compressing allowed. Note that for any representative
f
of a coset of
V\mathrm{Aut}(H)
in
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
the induced transformation
p(f)
on the base space of the fiber bundle
H
is the same. This gives the map to the group of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms of the two sphere,
\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(S^{2})
, which is also known to be a Fréchet manifold (see, for example,
[
Ham82
]
).

The last map in the sequence (
5.2
) is easily seen to be onto
\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(S^{2})
because diffeomorphisms
of
S^{2}
which are supported in small open sets can be easily lifted to automorphisms of
H
, and the family of such diffeomorphisms is known
[
Ban97
, Chapter
2
]
to generate
\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(S^{2})
.

To see that the last map in sequence (
5.1
) is onto
\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3})
, we need the following fact.

Proposition 5.1
.

Any two smooth fibrations of
S^{3}
by simple closed curves are equivalent, in the sense that there is a diffeomorphism of
S^{3}
taking one to the other.

Proof.

Given any smooth fiber bundle
S^{1}\hookrightarrow S^{3}\to S^{2}
, we consider
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{1})
as its group, and note that two such bundles are smoothly equivalent if and only if their associated principal
\mathrm{Diff}(S^{1})
bundles are smoothly equivalent.

But the two
S^{1}
bundles are known to be topologically equivalent, so their associated principal bundles are at least topologically equivalent as well. Then the theorem of Müller and Wockel
[
MW09
]
kicks in to say that for principal bundles structured by Fréchet Lie groups, smooth and continuous equivalences are
the same. So our two original smooth
S^{1}
bundles must also be smoothly equivalent, as desired.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

In the topological category,
5.1
follows immediately from the classification of
S^{1}
bundles over
S^{2}
. It is also possible to adapt the proof from the topological to the smooth setting in this particular case.

\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3})

Theorem A
in the case of oriented fibers, and similarly in the case of unoriented fibers.
∎

From this we deduce the even stronger
Theorem D
.

Proof of
Theorem D
.

By
[
And66
]
, each infinite-dimensional separable Fréchet vector space is homeomorphic, albeit not linearly, to the unique separable Hilbert vector space, which in turn is homeomorphic to a countably infinite product of real lines.

By
[
HS70
]
, two infinite-dimensional separable metrizable Fréchet manifolds are homeomorphic if and only if they have the same homotopy type.

Thus the homotopy equivalence
S^{2}\sqcup S^{2}\subset\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3})
leads to the desired homeomorphism

(S^{2}\sqcup S^{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{\infty}\simeq\mathrm{Fib}(S^{3}).

∎

Applications of reduced and coreduced modules III: homological properties and coherence of functors


Abstract
This is the third in a series of papers highlighting the applications of reduced and coreduced modules. Let R R be a commutative unital ring and I I be an ideal of R R . We show in different settings that I I -reduced (resp. I I -coreduced) R R -modules facilitate the computation of local cohomology (resp. local homology) and provide conditions under which the I I -torsion functor as well as the I I -transform functor (resp. their duals) become coherent. We show that whenever every R R -module is I I -reduced (resp. I I -coreduced), the cohomological dimension (resp. dual of the cohomological dimension) of an ideal I I of a ring R R coincides with the projective (resp. flat) dimension of the R R -module R / I R/I .

2.1
Representability of functors

A functor
F:R\text{-Mod}\rightarrow R\text{-Mod}
is
representable
(resp.
corepresentable
) if there exists
M\in R\text{-Mod}
such that
F(-)\cong\text{Hom}_{R}(M,-)
(resp.
F(-)\cong M\otimes_{R}-
).
Let
I
be an ideal of
R
. We study four functors on the category of
R
-modules. The functor

D_{I}(-):=\underset{k}{\varinjlim}~\text{Hom}_{R}(I^{k},-)

(1)

is called the
I
-transform
functor or the
ideal transform with respect to
I

I
is the functor

F_{I}(-):=\underset{k}{\varprojlim}~(I^{k}\otimes_{R}-).

(2)

We have the
I
-torsion functor

\Gamma_{I}(-):=\underset{k}{\varinjlim}~\text{Hom}_{R}(R/I^{k},-)

(3)

and its dual, the
I
-adic completion functor

\Lambda_{I}(-):=\underset{k}{\varprojlim}~(R/I^{k}\otimes_{R}-).

(4)

The functor
\Gamma_{I}
is left exact and its right derived functor is the local cohomology functor with respect to
I
, and denoted by
H_{I}^{i}(-)
.
\Lambda_{I}
is neither left exact nor right exact in general. However, when it is right exact (for instance when

), its left derived functor is called the local homology functor with respect to

.

Although we study the functors

and

Let

which is called the Deligne’s formula. Furthermore, the sections of
\widetilde{M}
supported at

, is the set
\Gamma_{V(I)}(X,\widetilde{M}):=\text{Ker}(\Gamma(X,\widetilde{M})\rightarrow\Gamma(X\setminus V(I),\widetilde{M}))

\Gamma_{I}(M)=\Gamma_{V(I)}(X,\widetilde{M})~~\text{ and}~~H_{I}^{i}(M)\cong H_{V(I)}^{i}(X,\widetilde{M}).

Proposition 2.5

Let

For any

Proof:

Proposition 2.7

If

If

2.2
Vanishing of local cohomology of

In this subsection, we start by giving the relationship between the functors

and

, and then

Proposition 2.11

For any injective

and any ideal

of

,

D_{I}(M)\cong M/\Gamma_{I}(M)=\text{Coker}(\Gamma_{I}(M)\hookrightarrow M)

and

~\Gamma_{I}(M)=\text{Ker}(M\rightarrow D_{I}(M)).

Proof:

As before, for any injective
R
-module

, we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\text{Hom}_{R}(R/I^{k},M)\rightarrow\text{Hom}_{R}(R,M)\rightarrow\text{Hom}_{R}(I^{k},M)\rightarrow 0.

Since the direct limit functor is exact, we have another exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Gamma_{I}(M)\rightarrow M\rightarrow D_{I}(M)\rightarrow 0.

The desired result becomes immediate.

Corollary 2.12

An injective
R
-module
M
is
I
-torsion (resp.
I
-torsionfree) if and only if
D_{I}(M)=0
(resp.
D_{I}(M)=M
).

Let
(M_{i},\phi_{ij})
be a directed inverse system of
R
-modules.
(M_{i},\phi_{ij})
satisfies the Mittag Leffler condition if for each

, there exists
j\geq i
such that for each

, we have
\phi_{ki}(M_{k})=\phi_{ji}(M_{j})
. Note that, if the

-module

is both flat and

-coreduced, then the inverse system
\{I^{k}\otimes_{R}M\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
satisfies the Mittag Leffler condition.

Proposition 2.13

For any flat
R
-module
M
such that the inverse system
\{I^{k}\otimes_{R}M\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
satisfies the Mittag Leffler condition, we have

\Lambda_{I}(M)\cong M/F_{I}(M)=\text{Coker}(F_{I}(M)\hookrightarrow M)

and

F_{I}(M)=\text{Ker}\left(M\rightarrow\Lambda_{I}(M)\right).

Proof:

For any flat
R
-module
M
, the sequence

0\rightarrow I^{k}\otimes_{R}M\rightarrow M\rightarrow R/I^{k}\otimes_{R}M\rightarrow 0

is exact. Since by hypothesis
\{I^{k}\otimes_{R}M\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
satisfies the Mittag Leffler condition, applying the inverse limit functor yields another exact sequence which is given by

0\rightarrow F_{I}(M)\rightarrow M\rightarrow\Lambda_{I}(M)\rightarrow 0

from which we get the desired result.

Corollary 2.14

If
M
is a flat

-module and

is an ideal of

such that the inverse system

satisfies the Mittag Leffler condition, then

1.

M
is
I
-complete if and only if
F_{I}(M)=0
,

2.

\Lambda_{I}(M)=0
if and only if

.

Proposition 2.15

For any injective module

defined over a Noetherian ring

and for any ideal

of

, we have

H_{I}^{1}(D_{I}(M))=0.

Proof:

By the proof of Proposition

,

is a short exact sequence. By dimension shifting,
H_{I}^{1}(D_{I}(M))\cong H_{I}^{2}(\Gamma_{I}(M))
. However, by

,
\Gamma_{I}(M)
is also an injective

-module. So,
H_{I}^{2}(\Gamma_{I}(M))=0
and
H_{I}^{1}(D_{I}(M))=0
.

Let

of an ideal

\text{cd}_{R}(I)
is the supremum of the set

\{cd(M,I)~:~M~\text{is an}~R\text{-module}\}.

It is a common problem to determine bounds or exact values for cohomological dimensions. See for instance,
[

,
10
,
12
,

]
. In Theorem

, we give a condition in terms of

-reduced

-modules for which

coincides with
\text{pd}(R/I)
the projective dimension of the
R
-module

.

Theorem 3.1

Suppose that
R\text{-Mod}=(R\text{-Mod})_{I\text{-red}}

1.

H_{I}^{i}(M)\cong\text{Ext}_{R}^{i}(R/I,M)
for all
M\in R\text{-Mod}
and

Remark 3.1

4
Coherence of functors

In this section, all rings
R
are commutative and Noetherian. Let
R
-mod denote the category of all finitely generated
R
-modules.

Definition 4.1

A functor
F:R\text{-mod}\rightarrow R\text{-mod}
is
coherent
(also called
finitely presented
) if there are
R
-modules
M
and
N
in
R
-mod such that the sequence

\text{Hom}_{R}(N,-)\rightarrow\text{Hom}_{R}(M,-)\rightarrow F\rightarrow 0

(5)

of functors is exact, i.e., if
F
is the cokernel of a natural transformation between representable functors.

Auslander in
[
3
]
initiated the study and demonstrated the importance of coherent functors. They were further studied in
[
4
,
8
,
19
,
20
]
among others. Coherent functors posses properties similar to those of finitely generated projective modules; their category is abelian, complete, co-complete and has enough projectives. However, many naturally occurring functors like;
\Gamma_{I}
,
\Lambda_{I}
,
D_{I}
and
F_{I}
are not in general coherent. In this section, we give conditions under which they become coherent.

Example 4.2

For any
M\in R\text{-mod}
, the functors
\text{Hom}_{R}(M,-)
and
M\otimes_{R}-
are both coherent. These two were given in
[
8
]
as Examples 2.1 and 2.2 respectively.

Let
(R\text{-mod})_{I\text{-red}}
(resp.
(R\text{-mod})_{I\text{-cor}}
) denote the collection of all finitely generated
R
-modules which are
I
-reduced (resp.
I
-coreduced). Define

\mathcal{A}:=R\text{-inj}\cap(R\text{-mod})_{I\text{-red}}
,
\mathcal{B}:=R\text{-flat}\cap(R\text{-mod})_{I\text{-cor}}
,

\mathcal{C}:=(R\text{-mod})_{I\text{-red}}
, and
\mathcal{D}:=(R\text{-mod})_{I\text{-cor}}
.

Theorem 4.3

For any ideal
I
of a ring
R
, the following functors are coherent:

1.

D_{I}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow R\text{-mod}
,

2.

F_{I}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow R\text{-mod}
,

3.

\Gamma_{I}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow R\text{-mod}
,

4.

\Lambda_{I}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow R\text{-mod}
.

Proof:
First note that, if
M\in\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}
or
\mathcal{D}
, then
D_{I}(M),F_{I}(D),\Gamma_{I}(M)
and
\Lambda_{I}(M)
also belong to
R
-mod. This is because,
D_{I}(M)\cong M/(0:_{M}I)
,
F_{I}(M)\cong IM
,
\Gamma_{I}(M)\cong(0:_{M}I)
and
\Lambda_{I}(M)\cong M/IM
. So, in each case, the image is either a submodule or a quotient of
M
and therefore it must also be finitely generated.
For any
M\in\mathcal{A}
,
D_{I}(M)\cong\text{Hom}_{R}(I,M)
by Proposition
2.5
.
D_{I}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow R\text{-mod}
is coherent by Example
4.2
. Let
M\in\mathcal{B}
, by Proposition
2.7
,
F_{I}(M)\cong I\otimes_{R}M
. It follows by Example
4.2
that
F_{I}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow R\text{-mod}
is coherent. The functor
\Gamma_{I}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow R\text{-mod}
,
(resp.
\Lambda_{I}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow R\text{-mod}
) is coherent by Example

4.2
and the fact that for all
M\in\mathcal{C}
(resp.
M\in\mathcal{D}
),
\Gamma_{I}(M)\cong\text{Hom}_{R}(R/I,M)
(resp.
\Lambda_{I}(M)\cong R/I\otimes_{R}M
), see
[
22
, Proposition 2.2]
and
[
22
, Proposition 2.3]
respectively.

5
Further computation of local (co)homology

Given a local ring
S
and an ideal
I
of
S
, it is natural to ask whether the homological properties of modules over the ring
S/I
are related to those of modules over the ring
S
. A question of this kind was tackled in
[
5
]
and some of its references. For instance, by
[
5
, page 879]
if
I=(x)
, where
x
is a regular element of
S
and
R=S/I
, then for any two
R
-modules
M
and
N
, the following two statements hold:

\text{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,N)=0~~\text{for all}~~i\geq 1~\text{implies that}~\text{Ext}^{i}_{S}(M,N)=0~~\text{for all}~~i\geq 2,

(6)

\text{Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0~~\text{for all}~~i\geq 1~\text{implies that}~\text{Tor}_{i}^{S}(M,N)=0~~\text{for all}~~i\geq 2.

(7)

In this section, we answer this question for local (co)homology when
x
is regular and when it is an exact zero-divisor element of
S
.

5.1
Local (co)homology modulo exact zero-divisors

In the remaining part of the paper,
S
is a commutative Noetherian ring.

Definition 5.1

[
11
]
An element
x
of
S
is an
exact zero-divisor
if it is nonzero, it belongs to the maximal ideal of
S
, and there exists another element
y\in S
such that
\text{ann}_{S}(x)=(y)
and
\text{ann}_{S}(y)=(x)
.

In this case, we say that
(x,y)
is a
pair of exact zero-divisors
of
S
. It turns out that
(x,y)
is an exact zero-divisor pair if and only if the sequence

S\xrightarrow{y}S\xrightarrow{x}S\xrightarrow{y}S

is exact.
Note that the notion of
x
being an exact zero-divisor is a special case of the notion of an element
x\in S
being a morphic element as defined in
[
16
]
.

Theorem 5.2

Let
R:=S/(x)
where
S
is local ring and
(x,y)
be a pair of exact zero divisors in
S
. Let
I
be an ideal of
R
such that
R\text{-Mod}=(R\text{-Mod})_{I\text{-red}}
and let
M
be an
R
-module with
yM=0
. If there exists an integer
n\geq 2
such that
H_{I}^{i}(_{R}M)=0
for
1\leq i\leq n
, then for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
,

H_{I}^{i}(_{S}M)\cong\text{Hom}_{S}(R/I,M).

Proof:

We have a ring epimorphism
f:S\rightarrow S/(x)=R
. If
M
is an
R
-module, then
M
is also an
S
-module via the action
sm:=f(s)m
for
s\in S
and
m\in M
. By
[
21
, Proposition 2.3]
and
[
17
, Proposition 3.7]
, if
{}_{R}M
is an
I
-reduced module, then so is
{}_{S}M
. So,
R\text{-Mod}=(R\text{-Mod})_{I\text{-red}}
implies that
S\text{-Mod}=(S\text{-Mod})_{I\text{-red}}
. Now, the
R
-modules
R/I
and
M
satisfy the hypothesis of
[
5
, Theorem 2.4]
. Since every
R
-module is
I
-reduced, Theorem
3.1
implies that
\text{Ext}_{R}^{i}(R/I,M)\cong H_{I}^{i}(_{R}M)
. So, by
[
5
, Theorem 2.4]
,
H_{I}^{i}(_{R}M)=0
for
1\leq i\leq n
implies that
\text{Ext}_{S}^{i}(R/I,M)\cong\text{Hom}_{S}(R/I,M)
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. However, since every
S
-module is also
I
-reduced, we have
\text{Ext}_{S}^{i}(R/I,M)\cong H_{I}^{i}(_{S}M)
(Theorem
3.1
). It follows that for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
,
H_{I}^{i}(_{S}M)\cong\text{Hom}_{S}(R/I,M)
.

Theorem 5.3

Let
R:=S/(x)
where
S
is local ring and
(x,y)
be a pair of exact zero divisors in
S
. Let
I
be an ideal of
R
such that
R\text{-Mod}=(R\text{-Mod})_{I\text{-cor}}
and let
M
be an
R
-module with

. If there exists an integer

such that
H^{I}_{i}(_{R}M)=0
for
1\leq i\leq n
, then for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
,

H^{I}_{i}(_{S}M)\cong R/I\otimes_{S}M.

Proof:

As in the proof of Theorem
5.2
, if
M
is an
R
-module, then
M
is also an
S
-module via the action
sm:=f(s)m
for
s\in S
and
m\in M
. If
{}_{R}M
is an
I
-coreduced module, then so is
{}_{S}M
. So,
R\text{-Mod}=(R\text{-Mod})_{I\text{-cor}}
implies that
S\text{-Mod}=(S\text{-Mod})_{I\text{-cor}}
. The
R
-modules

and

satisfy the hypothesis of
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
. Since every

-module is

-coreduced, Theorem

implies that
\text{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,M)\cong H^{I}_{i}(_{R}M)

5.2

In this subsection, we give conditions under which the vanishing of local

(co)homology over the ring

Proposition 5.4

Let
S
be a local ring such that
I
is an ideal of
S
generated by a regular element
x
of
S
. If

and

, then
H_{I}^{i}(_{R}M)=0

Proof:

By hypothesis,

R\text{-Mod}=(R\text{-Mod})_{I\text{-red}}

\text{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I,M)\cong H_{I}^{i}(_{R}M)
and
\text{Ext}^{i}_{S}(R/I,M)\cong H_{I}^{i}(_{S}M)
.
x
being regular in
S
and taking

-modules

and

, we get by Statement (
6

\text{Ext}_{R}^{i}(R/I,M)=0~\text{for all}~i\geq 1~\text{implies that}~\text{Ext}_{S}^{i}(R/I,M)=0~\text{for all}~i\geq 2.

In light of the two isomorphisms above, we get

H_{I}^{i}(_{R}M)=0~\text{for all}~i\geq 1~\text{implies that}~H_{I}^{i}(_{S}M)=0~\text{for all}~i\geq 2.

Proposition 5.5

Let
S
be a local ring such that

is an ideal of

generated by a regular element

of

It follows that

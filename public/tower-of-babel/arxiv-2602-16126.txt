Martin Boundary and the Nonlinear Multiplicative Stochastic Heat Equation in Weak Disorder
Hongyi Chen

Abstract
We study the nonlinear multiplicative stochastic heat equation on Dirichlet spaces with white in time noise under weak disorder. We show that positive invariant random fields with uniformly bounded second moments are in one-to-one correspondence with bounded positive harmonic functions. The proof combines a remote past pullback construction with a uniqueness argument that applies a contraction inspired by chaos expansion. As a consequence, the space of invariant measures inherits geometric structure from the Martin Boundary. We further establish a small-noise Gaussian fluctuation result within each harmonic sector and show that the long-time behavior of solutions is completely determined by the Martin boundary data of the initial condition. These results reveal a direct connection between stochastic PDE dynamics and boundary theory in potential analysis.

Introduction

Let
(M,d,\mu)

\mathcal{E}

self-adjoint operator associated with

We study the multiplicative stochastic heat equation, formally written as

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\mathcal{L}u(t,x)+\beta\,f(u(t,x))\,\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t>0,x\in M.

(1)

Here,
\beta>0
is the inverse temperature parameter,

is a Lipschitz function satisfying

Assumption 1.1

Let

\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in M}\int_{0}^{\infty}\iint_{M^{2}}p_{t}(x,y)p_{t}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|R(y,y^{\prime})|dydy^{\prime}dt=\Lambda.

We say any

satisfying

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(M)
and suppose

1.1

(i)

If

(ii)

\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}[Z(x)^{2}]<+\infty

.

Remark 1.3

(Nontriviality of the assumption on

)
.

Assumption (
1.1
) has already been shown to be true in various settings, all non-compact. In Euclidean space

with

Remark 1.4

(On the moment assumption)

The uniform pointwise second-moment bound imposed in Theorem

The class of invariant random fields considered here therefore corresponds to physically relevant stationary polymer states in the weak disorder phase.

Next is a fluctuation result which differs across harmonic sectors.

Theorem 1.5

(First order Gaussian fluctuation in

Finally, we show that the invariant measures identified in Theorem

Suppose

1.1
Literature Review

Equation (
1
) is interesting on its own
[
Mol91
]
and for it’s connection to various other areas of probability theory and mathematical physics. In the linear case
f(u)=u
, it is the partition function of directed polymers in a random environment
[
ACQ10
,
CY04
,
CSZ20
,
DZ22
,
DGL23
]
and gives rise to the Cole–Hopf solution of the KPZ equation
[
Kar87
,
KPZ86
]
. It also has direct connections to the stochastic Burgers equation
[
BCJ94
,
BG97
,
CM94
]
and to Majda’s model of shear-layer flow in turbulent diffusion
[
Maj93
]
. In the last few decades, there has been significant progress in understanding fundamental properties of (
1
) on flat spaces
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, including intermittency (see, e.g.,
[
CK19
,
Kho14
]
and references therein), as well as energy landscapes and fluctuations
[
DGK23
,
GHL25
,
GK24
,
GL20
]
. In particular, invariant measures have been heavily studied
[
Che+25
,
Dun+18
,
DM24
,
DS24
,
GQ24
,
Gro+23
]
due to their connections with fluctuation theory and other deeper properties. Notably, it has been shown that nontrivial invariant fields exist in the weak disorder/diffusive regime and this has been connected to some of the aforementioned fluctuation results.

In contrast, the literature on non-Euclidean settings has only begun to develop recently, almost all of it for the linear case and focused on well-posedness and/or pointwise moment or almost sure asymptotics. On compact Riemannian manifolds,
[
TV02
,
CO25
]
showed that strong disorder/localization always occurs, the latter also dealing with difficulties introduced by geometry for well-posedness if one allows measure-valued initial conditions. Well-posedness theory for bounded initial conditions, on the other hand, have been extended to stranger geometries such as sub-Riemannian manifolds
[
Bau+23
]
and fractals
[
Bau+25
,
HY16
]
. All the above works have also dealt with moment estimates, which are studied for their connection to intermittency/localization. Most recently, the effect of negative curvature on moment and pointwise asymptotics have begun to be explored. In
[
GX25
,
GWX25
]
, it was shown that for the linear case with noise independent of time, moment asymptotics in Hyperbolic space do not behave differently compared to the Euclidean case, but almost sure pointwise asymptotics grow at a different rate. For the white in time noises (the setting of this paper),
[
BCO25
]
showed that for a natural class of noises, negative curvature generates a weak disorder regime. This result was sharpened in
[
GO25
]
, who showed that all noises with
R
polynomially decaying at rate
r>1
gives rise to such a regime. Remarkably, they discovered that if
0<r<1
instead, in place of a weak disorder regime a new regime where strong disorder (
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[u(t,x)^{2}]=\infty
) still holds, but the rate of growth is sub-exponential as opposed to exponential. This phenomenon has never been observed before in any models of the linear equation with white in time noise.

Motivated by these developments, one may ask whether the invariant measures of (
1
) in the weak disorder (diffusive) regime is connected to the structure of harmonic functions on the underlying space. In Euclidean settings, this question is trivial due to Liouville’s theorem, which forces all bounded harmonic functions to be constant and thus yields a unique spatially homogeneous stationary state. In contrast, on non-compact spaces with nontrivial Martin boundary, such as negatively curved manifolds
[
Anc87
,
AS85
]
or trees
[
Cro08
]
, the abundance of bounded positive harmonic functions suggests the possibility of a rich structure of invariant measures reflecting boundary geometry. Theorem
1.2
answers this question in a precise and complete form: invariant random fields with uniformly bounded second moments are in one-to-one correspondence with bounded positive harmonic functions. In other words, the invariant measures inherit the structure possessed by the bounded positive harmonic functions, establishing a connection with Martin boundary theory. To our knowledge, this provides the first example of a classification of invariant measures for a stochastic PDE in terms of the harmonic (or boundary) structure of the underlying space.

3
Proof of Main Theorem

The proof of item (i) follows from repeatedly applying Itô-Walsh isometry into the mild formulation (
5
) along with
\mathbb{E}[f(u(s,y))f(u(s,y^{\prime}))]\leq L_{f}^{2}\sup_{y\in M}\mathbb{E}[u(s,y)^{2}]
to obtain a Neumann-type series (see
[
CD15
,
CK19
]
for this iteration being used to obtain well-posedness), and allowing time to go to infinity yields

\mathbb{E}[u(t,x)^{2}]\leq\norm{u_0}_{\infty}\sum_{k\geq 0}((\beta L_{f})^{2}\Lambda)^{k},

and the RHS geometric series converges iff
(\beta L_{f})^{2}\Lambda<1
.

For item (ii), we first show that the mean of an invariant field must be harmonic. It is simple: taking expectation of (
5
) gives us

\mathbb{E}[u(t,x)]=\int_{M}p_{t}(x,y)u_{0}(y)dy,

i.e. the expectation of (
1
) started from
u_{0}
is just the solution of the homogeneous heat equation starting from
u_{0}
. If a field is invariant, then its first moment must also be invariant, and the only functions invariant under the heat semigroup are precisely the harmonic functions. This means that any invariant measure must be associated to some harmonic function
h
. Also, this immediately rules out the possibility that two harmonic functions are associated with the same invariant measure, as their means, the harmonic functions, are not the same.

We are thus left to prove two steps to finish the proof of item (ii) and therefore the entire theorem: an existence step, for which we adapt the method of
[
GL20
]
to solutions of (
1
) starting from bounded harmonic functions, and a uniqueness step, for which we will again utilize chaos expansion.

3.2
Uniqueness by Chaos Expansion

In Theorem
3.1
, we obtained for every
h\in\mathrm{Har}^{+}_{b}(M)
an invariant random field of the PAM dynamic which has mean
h
. We will now show this random field is unique for each choice of such
h

Theorem 3.2

(Uniqueness of stationary solutions in the weak-disorder class)
.

Assume
(\beta L_{f})^{2}\Lambda<1
and let
V^{1},V^{2}
be two stationary mild solutions of (
1
) with the same first moment such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}[|V^{i}(t,x)|^{2}]<\infty,\quad i\in\set{1,2}.

Then
V^{1}\equiv V^{2}
almost surely.

Proof.

Define
\delta(t,x):=V^{1}(t,x)-V^{2}(t,x)
. Since neither
V^{1}
nor
V^{2}
have their laws change in time,
\delta
is another stationary solution and since
V^{1}
and
V^{2}
have the same pointwise mean,
\mathbb{E}[\delta(t,\cdot)]\equiv 0
.

The theorem is proven if
\delta\equiv 0
. Since
\delta
is time-stationary, we extend it to all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus the back-shifted mild formulation of
\delta
at
0
from
-T
yields

\delta(0,x)=[P_{T}\delta(-T,\cdot)](x)+\beta\int_{-T}^{0}\int_{M}p_{-s}(x,y)[f(V^{1}(y))-f(V^{2}(y))]W(ds,dy)

We will first show

A_{T}(x):=P_{T}\delta(-T,\cdot),\qquad\lim_{T\uparrow\infty}A_{T}(x)=0.

Define the remote past filtration
\mathcal{F}_{-T}:=\sigma(W((-\infty,-T]\times M))
. Since the Walsh integral over
(-T,0]
has expectation 0 when conditioned on
\mathcal{F}_{-T}
, we have

\mathbb{E}[\delta(0,\cdot)|\mathcal{F}_{-T}]=P_{T}\delta(-T,\cdot)](x).

Sending
T\uparrow\infty
in the above and applying Kolmogorov’s 0–1 law (
\mathcal{F}_{-\infty}:=\cap_{T\geq 0}\mathcal{F}_{-T}=\emptyset
) along with the martingale convergence theorem backwards gives us (
3.2
).

Now let
A(x,y):=\mathbb{E}[\delta(0,x)\delta(0,y)]
. If
A\equiv 0
, then
\mathbb{E}[(\delta(0,x)-\delta(0,y))^{2}]=0
for all

, hence
\delta\equiv 0
almost surely.
The rest of this proof is thus dedicated to showing
A\equiv 0
.

By Itô-Walsh isometry, we have

\displaystyle A(x,x^{\prime})=

Remark 5.1

The assumption that

In particular, on spaces with a well-understood Martin boundary (e.g. hyperbolic spaces and more generally negatively curved manifolds), bounded functions which admit a continuous extension to the boundary have harmonic limits given by the corresponding Poisson/Martin representation. In such cases, the above proposition shows that the long-time behavior of the stochastic equation is completely determined by the boundary data of the initial condition.

Appendix: Connections to Martin Boundary

Martin boundary and boundary integral representation

For a detailed discussion of Martin boundary theory, see
[
FOT11
]
.

Assume that the Markov process associated with
\mathcal{E}
is transient and admits a Green kernel

G(x,y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt<\infty\quad\text{for $\mu$-a.e.\ $x\neq y$.}

Fix a base point
o\in M
. The
Martin kernel
is defined by

K(x,y):=\frac{G(x,y)}{G(o,y)}.

The
Martin compactification

\overline{M}^{\,M}
is the smallest compactification for which the maps

y\mapsto K(\cdot,y)
extend continuously, and the
Martin boundary
is
\partial_{M}M:=\overline{M}^{\,M}\setminus M
.

Under standard hypotheses (e.g. those ensuring solvability of the Dirichlet problem at infinity and the boundary Harnack principle in concrete models), every
h\in\mathrm{Har}_{b}^{+}(M)
admits a boundary integral representation

h(x)=\int_{\partial_{M}M}K(x,\xi)\,\nu(d\xi),

(2)

for a finite positive measure
\nu
on
\partial_{M}M
; moreover extremal rays of
\mathrm{Har}_{b}^{+}(M)
correspond to minimal Martin boundary points.

Remark A.1
.

In classical negatively curved manifolds one often identifies
\partial_{M}M
with the geometric boundary, while for trees (and more generally many transient graphs)
\partial_{M}M
identifies with the space of ends. We keep the Martin boundary formulation throughout since it is intrinsic to the Markov semigroup and also accommodates non-local (jump) generators.

Equivariance under automorphisms of the measure, heat kernel, and noise

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a group acting measurably on
M
. We assume the action preserves the reference measure
\mu
and intertwines the semigroup:

p_{t}(gx,gy)=p_{t}(x,y)\quad\text{for all }g\in\mathcal{G},\ t>0,

and that the spatial covariance kernel
R
of the driving noise is
\mathcal{G}
-invariant:

R(gx,gy)=R(x,y)\quad\text{for all }g\in\mathcal{G}.

If
g\in\mathcal{G}
and
h
is harmonic, define

(g\cdot h)(x):=h(g^{-1}x).

Then
g\cdot h
is harmonic, and harmonic measures transform according to

\omega_{gx}=g_{\#}\omega_{x},

which implies

K(gx,\xi)=K(x,g^{-1}\xi).

Thus the action of isometries corresponds to pushforward of boundary data.
We now show (
1

\mathcal{G}
by generalizing the argument of
[
Che+21

.

Proposition A.2

(Isometry equivariance in mild form)
.

Define the
\mathcal{G}

(g\cdot\varphi)(x):=\varphi(g^{-1}x),\qquad g\in\mathcal{G}.

Assume the spatial covariance kernel

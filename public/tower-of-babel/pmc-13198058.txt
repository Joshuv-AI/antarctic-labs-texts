Greenness profile assessment of a novel pioneer approach for voltametric sensing of cloperastine fendizoate in a pharmaceutical dosage form
Authors: Maha Mahmoud Abou El‑Alamin, Ola Abd Elkhalek, Marwa M. Azab
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A novel sustainable analytical approach based on an electrochemical sensor has been fabricated for the quantification of Cloperastine Fendizoate (CLP), which has recently been employed in the management of COVID-19. A carbon paste electrode modified with 2% multiwalled carbon nanotubes and 1% chitosan (2%MWCNT/1%Chitosan/CPE) was fabricated to implement the proposed procedure. The obtained results demonstrated irreversible oxidation behavior and diffusion-controlled oxidation process of CLP, characterized by a well-defined anodic peak (E/V = 0.8) in 0.2 M phosphate buffer at pH 8 utilising differential pulse voltammetry (DPV). A linear correlation between current signal and CLP concentration was obtained across the range of 7.500 × 10 7- 6.00 × 10− 5 mol L⁻¹ (0.5–40 µg mL− 1), The practically calculated limits of detection and quantification were 1.11 × 10⁻⁷ mol L⁻¹ and 3.70 × 10⁻⁷ mol L⁻¹, respectively. The procedure achieved excellent accuracy and precision, with a mean recovery ±%RSD of 100.95 ± 1.75. The suggested procedure was successfully extended for the quantification of CLP in its dosage formulation. Validation was performed in accordance with ICH guidelines, and the findings demonstrated good agreement with those taken utilizing a previously validated approach. Furthermore, the eco-friendliness of the developed approach was investigated utilising both the Green Analytical Procedure Index (GAPI), the Multi-color Assessment (MA) technique, and the Analytical Greenness metric (AGREE), confirming that the proposed approach exhibits superior greenness compared to reported techniques.

Introduction

Cloperastin fendizoate (CLP) is chemically known as 1-[2-[(4-chlorophenyl)-phenylmethoxy]ethyl]piperidine; 2-(4-hydroxy-3-phenylbenzoyl)benzoic acid, as shown in Fig. 1. It is a centrally acting antitussive reagent, primarily prescribed to manage the non-productive (dry) cough associated with conditions such as bronchitis, common cold, or certain allergic reactions. More recently, it has also been utilized to alleviate cough-related symptoms in patients with COVID-19 [1, 2]. Several analytical techniques have been employed for CLP determination, including UV spectroscopy [3], spectrofluorimetry [4], RP-HPLC [5, 6], and HPLC associated with tandem mass spectrometry [7]. However, chromatographic methods present certain limitations; they typically require multiple steps of sample preparation, separation, and detection, which can be labor-intensive and time-consuming, especially when dealing with complex mixtures. Moreover, these techniques often consume large volumes of samples and organic solvents, which are expensive, potentially hazardous, and environmentally unfriendly. Additionally, their implementation depends on sophisticated and costly detection equipment.

To the best of our knowledge, no voltammetric method has been previously reported for the determination of CLP. Voltammetric techniques have attracted considerable attention in pharmaceutical analysis due to their high sensitivity, simplicity, low cost, rapid response, and minimal sample preparation requirements, making them well-suited for the determination of electroactive compounds. Accordingly, the present study introduces the first voltammetric approach for the sensitive and selective analysis of CLP. The proposed method is based on a modified carbon paste electrode with nanomaterials, which offers several advantages, including enhanced electron transfer kinetics, increased effective surface area, and improved sensitivity and selectivity toward the target analyte, thereby improving the overall analytical performance of the developed method. Additionally, the application of green assessment is crucial, as it allows the evaluation of the environmental friendliness, sustainability, and safety of the analytical method, guiding researchers to develop methods that minimize chemical waste, energy consumption, and exposure to hazardous reagents. Therefore, the proposed method was evaluated using established green assessment techniques, such as Analytical Greenness Metric (AGREE), Complex Green Analytical Procedure Index (Complex-GAPI), and the Analytical Eco-Scale, to ensure minimal environmental impact.

Working electrode

Carbon Paste Electrode (CPE)

The CPE was fabricated by blending 0.5 g of graphite powder with approximately 0.3 mL of paraffin oil in a porcelain dish. The obtained paste was inserted into the electrode body, and its surface was subsequently polished to obtain a smooth finish with filter paper until a smooth, shiny finish was achieved.

MWCNT-Modified CPE (2% MWCNT/CPE)

To prepare the MWCNT-modified electrodes, the required proportions of MWCNTs and graphite powder were mixed with diethyl ether to ensure homogeneity. The mixture was air-dried, after which paraffin oil was incorporated and thoroughly blended until a consistent and well-wetted paste was formed, indicating uniform mixing of all components.

Nanomaterial-Modified CPEs

Modified sensors containing 2% MWCNT/CPE with additional modifiers such as 3% SiO₂ NPs, 3% ZrO₂ NPs, 3% chitosan, and 3% graphene were fabricated using the same procedure described above.

Standard solutions

A standard stock solution of CLP (1.5 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹) was formed by solubilized 10.0 mg of the compound in a 10 mL volumetric flask and completing the volume with double-distilled water.

Construction of calibration curves

Differential pulse (DP) voltammograms were obtained in the potential range of 0.0–1.60 V using a scan rate of 150 mV s⁻¹, with a pulse amplitude of 50 mV and a pulse width of 10 ms. Aliquots from the CLP stock solution (1.5 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹) were introduced into a 10 cm³ electrochemical cell containing 10 mL of 0.2 M phosphate buffer at pH 8, yielding final concentrations in the range of 7.5 × 10− 7 − 6.0 × 10− 5 mol L⁻¹ (0.5–40 µg mL⁻¹). A calibration curve was generated by plotting the current signal values against the corresponding drug concentrations, and the regression equation was subsequently determined.

Pharmaceutical preparations

A 14.1 mL portion of Notussil® suspension (labeled to contain 100 mg of CLP) was sonicated in an ultrasonic bath at 25 °C for 15 min, then filtered by a 0.45 μm disposable syringe filter. The filtrate was diluted to 100 mL with distilled water to obtain a 1 mg mL⁻¹ (≈ 1.5 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹) stock solution. Aliquots of this stock were transferred to a 10 cm³ electrolytic cell, and the electrochemical method described above was applied. CLP concentration was calculated using the linear regression equation obtained from the calibration curve.

Validation and green assessment

The analytical method was validated according to ICH Q2 (R1) guidelines. Validation parameters, including linearity, accuracy, precision, LOD, LOQ, and selectivity, were evaluated. In addition, the method was assessed using green analytical metrics (The Multi-color Assessment (MA) Tool, Analytical GREEnness Metric (AGREE), Complex Green analytical procedure index (Complex-GAPI), and the analytical Eco-scale to ensure environmentally friendly practices.

Factor optimization

Influence of pH and buffer types

The influence of pH on the electrochemical response of CLP (1.5 × 10⁻5 mol L⁻¹) was examined utilizing 0.04 M BR buffer solutions over the pH range 2–11 at a CPE with a scan rate of 150 mV s⁻¹, employing DP voltammetry (Fig. 3a). At pH 2, a single clearly distinguishable anodic signal was detected. Increasing the buffer pH resulted in a progressive rise in the current signal, achieving the highest value at pH 8. Beyond this value, a sharp decline in signal intensity was noted at pH 9, while at pH 11 the anodic peak completely disappeared. Based on these results, pH 8 was chosen for further investigations into the influence of buffer composition. The pKa of CLP is reported in the literature to be around 8–9, indicating that the drug exists in a partially protonated form near neutral to slightly alkaline media. At pH 8, the equilibrium between protonated and neutral species facilitates electron transfer at the electrode surface, resulting in enhanced oxidation current and improved analytical sensitivity. This explains why pH 8 was selected as the optimal condition for voltammetric determination.

When the potential signal was plotted against the solution pH, a shift to more negative values was observed, supporting the assumption of involvement of protons in the oxidation procedure of the investigated drug at the surface of the sensor. As illustrated in Fig. 3b, a linear correlation was obtained between the potential signal (E/V) and pH, which was expressed by the following regression Equation [10].

The slope of the potential signal –pH plot was determined to be 0.0311 V per pH unit, which is lower than the theoretical Nernstian value of 0.059 V per pH unit. This suggests that the number of protons participating in the oxidation process is lower than the number of electrons transferred. Such behavior implies that the oxidation of CLP at the surface of the sensor followed a proton–electron coupled mechanism, in which electron transfer is the dominant step, while proton involvement is limited.

Since pH 8 was identified as the optimum condition, 0.2 M phosphate buffer and 0.2 M borate buffer (both at pH 8) were employed to assess their influence on the electrochemical response of CLP, in comparison with 0.04 M BR buffer. Among the tested media, 0.2 M phosphate buffer (pH 8) produced the highest current signal (Fig. 4).

Different electrode types

CPE was selected for studying the electrochemical performance of the analyte owing to its simple preparation, easy surface renewal, wide potential window, and the possibility of modification with different materials to improve selectivity and sensitivity [11].

Since metal oxide nanoparticles are often reported to enhance electrochemical signals due to their catalytic properties, different types were tested. However, adding 2% of zirconia (ZrO₂), silica (SiO₂), or titanium dioxide (TiO₂) nanoparticles to the CPE did not improve the response. Instead, the current decreased noticeably, as shown in Fig. 5. Other modifiers, such as 2% graphene nanoparticles, were also examined; however, graphene resulted in lower current and higher background noise compared to the unmodified electrode. These results highlight the well-known advantage of MWCNTs. Adding 2% MWCNTs to the paste improved the electrode’s uniformity, increased its surface area, and enhanced its conductivity[12–14]. Therefore, the 2% MWCNT/CPE was selected for further electrode modification because it provided the best balance between improved signal and stable background current.

Next, several modifiers were tested in combination with the MWCNT/CPE. Adding 1% ZrO₂ or 1% SiO₂ nanoparticles did not enhance the response; instead, the signal decreased compared to the MWCNT/CPE alone (Fig. 6). On the other hand, incorporating chitosan produced a significant increase in the electrochemical signal. Thus, the electrode containing 2% MWCNT and 1% chitosan in the CPE was chosen as the optimal sensor for this study. The improved performance is likely due to chitosan’s ability to increase the effective surface area, enhance electron transfer, and form a uniform, biocompatible film that helps disperse the MWCNTs evenly. These properties make chitosan a valuable modifier for improving the sensitivity and stability of carbon-based electrochemical sensors[15–17].

Electrode characterization

As described above, functionalizing the bare CPE with MWCNTs and chitosan nanoparticles enlarges the electrode’s effective surface area and improves the electrical conductivity of the sensor. The larger surface provides more active sites, which facilitate greater adsorption and accumulation of the analyte on the modified sensor.

To confirm this hypothesis, the electroactive surface areas of three sensors CPE, 2% MWCNT/CPE, and 2%MWCNT/1%Chitosan/CPE, were determined utilizing the Randles–Sevcik equation:

The calculated electroactive surface areas were 0.03 cm² for the bare CPE, 0.05 cm² for the 2% MWCNT/CPE, and 0.14 cm² for the 2%MWCNT/1%Chitosan/CPE.

These results show that introducing MWCNTs increased the surface area by approximately 1.67 fold, and the subsequent incorporation of chitosan nanoparticles further enhanced it to about 4.67 fold compared with the unmodified electrode.

This marked enlargement of the effective surface area accounts for the high sensitivity observed for the designed electrode, as previously discussed.

Influence of accumulation time

The influence of accumulation time was investigated utilizing 1.5 × 10⁻⁵ M CLP solution in 0.2 M phosphate buffer of pH 8 at 2% MWCNT/1% Chitosan/CPE. The current signal was recorded against time for 25 s with 5 s intervals, utilizing DP at open circuit, as illustrated in Fig. 12. A sharp decline in the current signal was obtained as the accumulation time elevated. This behavior indicates that prolonged accumulation leads to surface saturation and possible adsorption of oxidation products on the active sites of the electrode, which hinders further electron transfer and favors a diffusion-controlled mass-transfer process of the analyte toward the sensor surface. It should be noted that the pre-accumulation step was employed solely as an auxiliary parameter to optimize the analytical signal and was not intended to imply an adsorption-controlled mass transport mechanism. The diffusion-controlled behavior was independently confirmed by the scan-rate study, as discussed in the previous section. Consequently, an accumulation time of 5 s was chosen as the optimum value for current signal measurements.

Green assessment

The environmental impact of the analytical methods was investigated according to four primary factors: elevated energy demand, excessive waste generation, intensive chemical consumption with related hazards, and overall chemical usage [22, 23]. In addition, the cost efficiency and eco-friendliness of the developed approach were examined utilizing four different assessment tools, as outlined below.

The Multi-color Assessment (MA) Tool

The Multi-color Assessment (MA) Tool [24] was utilized to investigate the environmental sustainability of the developed voltametric approach. This online platform combines four major evaluation tools, GEMAM, BAGI, RAPI, and VIGI, which yielded respective scores of 74.5%, 90.0%, 97.5%, and 35.0% (Table 7), providing an integrated evaluation of greenness, applicability, performance, and innovation. Method-specific information, including solvent type and volume, waste produced per analysis, instrument energy demand, sample preparation procedures, and analytical characteristics such as LOD, linear range, and precision, was entered into the MA-Tool interface. The system generated color-coded ratings for each assessment domain and an overall MA-score of 74.3 (Table 7), representing a high level of environmental compatibility in line with the principles of white analytical chemistry.

The Analytical Eco-Scale

The Analytical Eco-Scale is a metric utilized to evaluate the environmental friendliness of an analytical approach [25]. The system begins with a total score of 100, from which penalty points are subtracted. A final score above 75 indicates excellent environmental friendliness, a score above 50 reflects acceptable greenness, and anything below 50 is considered environmentally insufficient. It offers a quantitative way to evaluate analytical methods because it accounts for every reagent involved, not only the most hazardous ones, as some other evaluation systems do. Table 7 shows the calculated scores for the proposed method. It received 88 points, showing a high level of environmental friendliness.

Analytical GREEnness metric (AGREE)

The AGREE tool evaluates analytical approaches in accordance with the 12 principles of Green Analytical Chemistry (GAC), which encompass factors such as sample preparation, number of steps, waste generation, energy requirements, and toxicity [26, 27]. Each criterion is expressed on a unified scale ranging from 0 to 1. Within the pictogram, methods approaching a score of 1 and highlighted in dark green are considered more environmentally sustainable. Table 7 presents the pictograms of the proposed. The suggested approach achieved an AGREE score of 0.77.

Complex Green analytical procedure index (Complex-GAPI)

The GAPI tool offers a comprehensive investigation of analytical methodologies through fifteen parameters, each represented by a pentagram that reflects different stages of the procedure including sampling, sample preparation, instrumentation, solvents, and reagents employed, as well as the overall purpose of the method. The color coding in GAPI indicates the level of environmental impact: green denotes high ecological compatibility, yellow reflects moderate impact, and red signifies considerable environmental risk [28] As illustrated in the Table 7. The proposed approach obtained ten green regions. These findings, as demonstrated by the Comple-GAPI evaluation, indicate that the developed approach is environmentally sustainable.

To further emphasize the sustainability of the proposed voltammetric method, the obtained greenness metrics were compared with those of previously reported analytical methods for CLP determination, including spectrofluorimetric and HPLC techniques. As summarized in Table 7, the proposed approach demonstrated a superior or comparable environmental profile across all applied assessment tools. Although the spectrofluorimetric method exhibited a slightly higher Eco-Scale score (89) compared to the proposed method (88), both approaches fall within the category of excellent environmental friendliness. In contrast, the HPLC method showed a noticeably lower Eco-Scale score (74), reflecting increased solvent consumption, higher waste generation, and greater energy demand.

Conclusion

The proposed green method represents the first practical trial to characterize the electrochemical performance of CLP. A 2%MWCNT/1%Chitosan/CPE electrode was fabricated for the quantification of CLP in its pure form and pharmaceutical formulation. Differential pulse voltammetric measurements using the modified sensor demonstrated excellent selectivity, high accuracy, low detection and quantification limits, and a broad linear range. The method proved to be suitable for drug assay without the need for complex procedures or costly instrumentation. Moreover, the electrochemical results generated in this study provide a valuable reference for future investigations, given that no electrochemical approaches for CLP have been previously reported.

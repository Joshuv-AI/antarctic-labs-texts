Exact Anomalous Current Fluctuations in Quantum Many-Body Dynamics
Kazuya Fujimoto, Taiki Ishiyama, Taiga Kurose, Takato Yoshimura, Tomohiro Sasamoto

Abstract
Fluctuations of integrated currents have attracted considerable interest over the past decades in the context of statistical mechanics. Recently, anomalous current fluctuations, characterized by the M-Wright function, were obtained exactly in a classical automaton [Ž. Krajnik et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 128 , 160601 (2022)], and previous studies have shown that the anomalous behavior can arise in a variety of classical systems. Despite the rapidly growing interest in such anomalous behaviors, which capture a universal aspect of one-dimensional many-body transport, the exact derivation of the M-Wright function in quantum many-body systems has remained elusive. In this Letter, we present the first exact microscopic derivation of the M-Wright function in quantum many-body dynamics by analyzing the integrated spin current in a one-dimensional Fermi-Hubbard model with infinitely strong repulsive interactions. Our results lay the groundwork for exploring anomalous integrated currents in a broad class of quantum many-body systems.

Setup.–

Suppose that we have a one-dimensional lattice
\Lambda\coloneqq\{-N+1,-N+2,...,N\}
with a positive integer
N
and denote an annihilation operator for a fermion with spin
\sigma\in S\coloneqq\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}
at site
j\in\Lambda
by
\hat{f}_{\sigma,j}
. Then, the Hamiltonian of the
t0
model
Essler et al. 2005
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{H}\coloneqq-\hat{P}\sum_{\sigma\in S}\sum_{j=-N+1}^{N-1}\left(\hat{f}_{\sigma,j+1}^{\dagger}\hat{f}_{\sigma,j}+{\rm h.c.}\right)\hat{P},

(1)

where we introduce
\hat{P}\coloneqq\prod_{j\in\Lambda}\left(1-\hat{n}_{\uparrow,j}\hat{n}_{\downarrow,j}\right)
with
\hat{n}_{\sigma,j}\coloneqq\hat{f}_{\sigma,j}^{\dagger}\hat{f}_{\sigma,j}
, which is a projector to Hilbert space without any double occupancy. This model is the effective description of the Fermi-Hubbard model with strong repulsive interactions
Essler et al. 2005
. Let us denote a density matrix at time
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by
\hat{\rho}(t)
, which obeys the conventional von-Neumann equation. Then, the time-evolved density matrix is given by
\hat{\rho}(t)=\hat{U}(t)\hat{\rho}(0)\hat{U}(t)^{\dagger}
with
\hat{U}(t)\coloneqq e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}t}
, where the Dirac constant is set to unity. The initial density matrix
\hat{\rho}(0)
for the exact calculation is assumed to be

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}(0)=\frac{1}{4^{N}}\prod_{j\in\Lambda}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{2}\hat{n}_{j}\right)

(2)

with
\hat{n}_{j}\coloneqq\hat{n}_{\uparrow,j}+\hat{n}_{\downarrow,j}
. The term on a site
j
for
\hat{\rho}(0)
has the simple interpretation that a fermion can occupy the site
j
with a probability of
1/2
and its spin can take up (down) with a probability of
1/2
.

The physical quantity of interest is the probability
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]
of an integrated spin current
\Delta S_{R}^{z}
at the origin and time
t
.
To define this probability, we introduce an operator
\hat{S}^{z}_{R}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{s}^{z}_{j}
with
\hat{s}^{z}_{j}\coloneq\hat{n}_{\uparrow,j}-\hat{n}_{\downarrow,j}
, where we omit a factor
1/2
for the sake of simplicity.
Then,
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]
is defined by two-time measurements
Moriya et al. 2019
;
McCulloch et al. 2023
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{S^{z}_{R}=-N}^{N}{\rm Tr}\left[\hat{P}_{S}(S_{R}^{z}+\Delta S_{R}^{z})\hat{U}(t)\hat{P}_{S}(S_{R}^{z})\hat{\rho}(0)\hat{P}_{S}(S_{R}^{z})\hat{U}(t)^{\dagger}\right],

(3)

where
\hat{P}_{S}(S^{z}_{R})
is a projector for eigenspace with an eigenvalue
S^{z}_{R}
of
\hat{S}^{z}_{R}
.
We denote the corresponding generating function by
G_{S}(\lambda,t)\coloneqq\sum_{\Delta S_{R}^{z}=-N}^{N}\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]e^{\lambda\Delta S_{R}^{z}}
.
By definition, we can express the probability via the generating function as
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]=\int_{C_{r}}dz~z^{-1-\Delta S_{R}^{z}}G_{S}(\lambda,t)/(2\pi{\rm i})
, where we use a complex variable
z\coloneqq e^{\lambda}
and
C_{r}
is a contour with a radius
r
enclosing the origin in the complex plane. In this work, we first calculate
G_{S}(\lambda,t)
and subsequently derive
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]
by performing the contour integral.

Exact expression of
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]
.–

We derive the exact expression of
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]
that is convenient for implementing its asymptotic analysis. The fundamental point for our exact analysis is the spin-charge separation of the
t0
model
Gamayun et al. 2023
, for which the initial spin configuration of the fermions is independent of time and the spins can be transported only through the charge transport (see Sec.
I
of Supplemental Material (SM)
SM
). Thanks to this property, we exactly derive

\displaystyle G_{S}(\lambda,t)=\mathbb{P}_{C}[0,t]+2\sum^{N}_{\Delta N_{R}=1}\left(\cosh(\lambda)\right)^{\Delta N_{R}}\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t].

(4)

The derivation is given in Sec.
I
of SM
SM
.
Here, we define
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]\coloneqq\int_{C_{r}}dz~{\rm det}[\delta_{m,n}+\sinh(\lambda/2)^{2}D_{m,n}(t)]_{m,n=1}^{N}/(2\pi{\rm i}z^{\Delta N_{R}+1})
, where
D_{m,n}(t)~(m,n\in\Lambda)
is the solution of
{\rm i}dD_{m,n}(t)/dt=D_{m+1,n}(t)+D_{m-1,n}(t)-D_{m,n+1}(t)-D_{m,n-1}(t)
with the initial condition
D_{m,n}(0)=\delta_{m,n}\chi[m\in\{-N+1,...,0\}]
. Here,
\chi[\bullet]
is the indicator function and the boundary condition is open. The physical meaning of
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
is the probability that the transferred particle number from the left region (
-N+1,-N+2,...,0
) to the right region (
1,2,...,N
) at time
t
is
\Delta N_{R}
under the unitary time evolution for spinless free fermions. This interpretation is explained in Sec.
III
of SM
SM
.

We substitute Eq. (
4
) into the contour integral for
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S^{z}_{R},t]
, exactly deriving

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S^{z}_{R},t]=\delta_{\Delta S^{z}_{R},0}\mathbb{P}_{C}[0,t]

\displaystyle+2\sum^{N}_{\Delta N_{R}=1}\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]\sum_{n=0}^{\Delta N_{R}}\dfrac{~{}_{\Delta N_{R}}C_{n}}{2^{\Delta N_{R}}}\delta_{\Delta S^{z}_{R},\Delta N_{R}-2n}.

(5)

Before going to the asymptotic analysis, we note that the right hand side of Eq. (
5
) has a simple probabilistic interpretation. From the probabilistic interpretation of
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
, the contribution to
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S^{z}_{R},t]
comes only from the
\Delta N_{R}
fermions occupying the left region from the origin in the initial state. This fact is schematically displayed in Fig.
2
, where we consider the case with
\Delta N_{R}=3
. Considering this fact, we suppose that we have
\Delta N_{R}-n
up-spin fermions and
n
down-spin fermions in the left region of the initial state. At time
t
, all these fermions pass the origin because of
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
. Hence, the integrated spin current at time
t
becomes
\Delta N_{R}-2n
, which corresponds to the Kronecker delta of Eq. (
5
). The case with
\Delta N_{R}=3
and
n=1
is shown in Fig.
2
. Next, we consider possible initial spin configurations in the left region. In our initial density matrix, the positions of the particles and their spins are random via Eq. (
2
). Hence, the number of all possible configurations of up-spin and down-spin fermions in the left region is
2^{\Delta N_{R}}
and that for
\Delta N_{R}-n
up-spin fermions and
n
down-spin fermions is
~{}_{\Delta N_{R}}C_{n}
. Hence,
{}_{\Delta N_{R}}C_{n}/(2^{\Delta N_{R})}
is the probability of having
\Delta N_{R}-n
up-spin fermions and
n
down-spin fermions in the initial left region. Therefore, the right hand side of Eq. (
5
) is the summation of the probability with the integrated spin current
\Delta N_{R}-2n
under the equal probability for such fermion configurations.

Asymptotic analysis of
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]
.–

We shall study the long-time behavior of
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]
after taking the thermodynamic limit (
N\rightarrow\infty
) of Eq. (
5
).
The fundamental ingredient for this analysis is the probability
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
for the typical fluctuation of
\Delta N_{R}
, which is given by, for
t\gg 1
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{t}\mathbb{P}_{C}[\sqrt{t}\mathcal{J}_{C},t]\sim\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\exp\left(-\dfrac{\pi\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}}{2}\right).

(6)

This asymptotic form is derived in Sec.
III
of SM
SM
. Employing Eq. (
6
) and Stirling’s formula, we obtain
the limiting probability
\mathbb{P}_{S}^{\rm typ}[\mathcal{J}_{S}]\coloneqq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{1/4}\mathbb{P}_{S}[t^{1/4}\mathcal{J}_{S},t]
for the typical fluctuation of
\Delta S^{z}_{R}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{S}^{\rm typ}[\mathcal{J}_{S}]=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\dfrac{d\mathcal{J}_{C}}{{\sqrt{\mathcal{J}_{C}}}}\exp\left[-\dfrac{\pi\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}}{2}-\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}{2\mathcal{J}_{C}}\right],

(7)

which is derived in Sec.
IV
of SM
SM
. This expression is identical to the M-Wright function
\mathbb{P}_{\rm MW}[\mathcal{J}_{S},1/\sqrt{\pi}]
. Therefore, we derive the M-Wright function exactly in the quantum many-body dynamics.

We numerically check this asymptotic behavior. As detailed in Sec.
V
of SM
SM
, in our numerical calculation, we solve the equation of motion for

, then calculate the probability
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]

\mathbb{P}_{\rm MW}[\mathcal{J}_{S},1/\sqrt{\pi}]

Hydrodynamic derivation of the M-Wright function.–

Just above, we derive the M-Wright function exactly in the dynamics with the specific initial state of Eq. (
2
), but its extension to general initial states remains challenging. Here, we explore another approach based on hydrodynamics to tackle this task and consider a grand canonical density matrix as the initial state, which is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}(0)=\frac{1}{Z}e^{-\beta(\hat{H}-\mu\hat{N})},

(8)

where
\beta
,
\mu
, and
Z
are the inverse nonzero temperature, the chemical potential, and the normalization constant, respectively, and we define
\hat{N}\coloneqq\hat{P}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\left(\hat{n}_{\uparrow,j}+\hat{n}_{\downarrow,j}\right)\hat{P}
. As derived in End Matter, we apply GHD
Castro-Alvaredo et al. 2016
;
Bertini et al. 2016
;
Doyon 2020
;
Alba et al. 2021
;
Bouchoule and Dubail 2022
;
Essler 2023
and BMFT
Doyon et al. 2023a
;
Doyon et al. 2023b
to the
t0
model with Eq. (
8
), deriving the M-Wright function:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{S}^{\rm typ}[\mathcal{J}_{S}]=\dfrac{1}{\pi d(\beta,\mu)}\int^{\infty}_{0}\dfrac{d\mathcal{J}_{C}}{{\sqrt{\mathcal{J}_{C}}}}\exp\left[-\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}}{2d(\beta,\mu)^{2}}-\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}{2\mathcal{J}_{C}}\right]

(9)

with
d(\beta,\mu)\coloneqq\sqrt{2[1/(2+e^{-\beta(2+\mu)})-1/(2+e^{\beta(2-\mu)})]/(\pi\beta)}
.
This hydrodynamic result can reproduce the exact result of Eq. (
7
) by taking the limit
\beta\rightarrow 0
while fixing
\beta\mu=-\log 2
, for which Eq. (
8
) converges to the initial state of Eq. (
2
) for the exact analysis. This result implies the robustness of the M-Wright function in the
t0
model against the choice of the initial states.

Discussion.–

We shall discuss two topics: (i) the relation between the classical automaton and the
t0
model and (ii) the possibility of experimental observation for our theoretical prediction.

Conclusion and future prospects.–

We have theoretically studied the probability
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]
for the integrated spin current of the one-dimensional
t0
model. We have performed the exact microscopic calculation, demonstrating that the scaled probability
t^{1/4}\mathbb{P}_{S}[t^{1/4}\mathcal{J}_{S},t]
with the initial state of Eq. (
2
) converges to the M-Wright function
\mathbb{P}_{\rm MW}[\mathcal{J}_{S},\sigma]
in the long time limit. Furthermore, we have derived
\mathbb{P}_{\rm MW}[\mathcal{J}_{S},\sigma]
for the initial grand canonical state using GHD and BMFT. On the basis of our results, we have discussed the relation to the previous work
Krajnik et al. 2022b
on the classical automaton and the experimental possibility of observing our theoretical prediction.

As a promising future prospect, it is important to clarify the extent to which anomalous current fluctuations characterized by the M-Wright function are universal in quantum many-body systems. Our exact result suggests that the spin-charge, which renders the spin configuration static in time, is essential for the emergence of the M-Wright function. Motivated by this fact, it is highly intriguing to test whether the anomalous current fluctuation persists even in nonintegrable settings by introducing noisy spin-independent potentials into the
t0
model. Another natural extension is to consider the SU(
N
)
t0
model and investigate the robustness of the M-Wright function by systematically changing the internal spin degrees of freedom. These theoretical studies are promising, as they may admit exact solutions by extending our current analytic approach.

Beyond the
t0
model, an exact treatment of the XXZ spin chain is of particular interest. While Ref.
Yoshimura et al.
derived the M-Wright function from a hydrodynamic perspective, an exact microscopic analysis based on the Bethe ansatz is technically challenging. Such exact results would provide invaluable insights into universal aspects of quantum many-body dynamics, including the intriguing KPZ physics of spin chains

;
Das et al. 2019
;
Krajnik and Prosen 2020

Fava et al. 2020

(E-6)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}S^{z}(x,t)+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(v(x,t)S^{z}(x,t)\right)=0.

In the following, we calculate the probability for the typical fluctuations of the integrated spin current by using the hydrodynamic method developed in Refs.

First, we introduce the scaled charge density and spin defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,t)\coloneqq\rho^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,\sqrt{\tau}x,\tau t),\quad\tilde{S}^{z}(x,t)\coloneqq S^{z}(\sqrt{\tau}x,\tau t).

I.1
Spin-charge separation

The

\ket{\bm{x},\bm{s}}

\bm{x}\coloneqq(x_{1},...,x_{N_{p}})

\bm{s}\coloneqq(s_{1},...,s_{N_{p}})

and

t0

\displaystyle\bra{\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{s}^{\prime}}\hat{H}\ket{\bm{x},\bm{s}}=\delta_{\bm{s}^{\prime},\bm{s}}\bra{\bm{x}^{\prime}}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}\ket{\bm{x}},

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm ff}\coloneqq-\sum_{j=-N+1}^{N-1}\left(\hat{a}_{j+1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}+\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j+1}\right).

(S-2)

Here,
\hat{a}_{j}
is the annihilation operator of a spinless fermion at site

and
\ket{\bm{x}}
is a basis of the spinless fermions.
For the following calculations, we define the notation

\displaystyle\bra{\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{s}^{\prime}}\hat{U}(t)\ket{\bm{x},\bm{s}}=\langle\bm{s}^{\prime}|\bm{s}\rangle\bra{\bm{x}^{\prime}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\ket{\bm{x}}.

This matrix element clearly shows that the spin configuration does not change in time.

III

\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]

We shall derive the asymptotic expression of

First, we show that

Second, we demonstrate that the computation of
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]

Finally, we implement the asymptotic analysis for the expression obtained in the second part.

III.1

We consider the dynamics of the spinless fermions in the

The dynamics is generated by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{\varpi}(0)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=-N+1}^{N}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}+\dfrac{1}{2}(1-\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j})\right)

\bm{x}^{R,r}\end{subarray}}\sum_{N_{R}}\bra{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}\hat{P}_{C}(N_{R})e^{{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}e^{\lambda\left(\hat{N}_{R}-N_{R}\right)}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\hat{P}_{C}(N_{R})\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}

(S-43)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4^{N}}\sum_{l=0}^{N}\sum_{r=0}^{N}e^{-\lambda r}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}^{L,l}\\
\bm{x}^{R,r}\end{subarray}}\bra{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}e^{{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}e^{\lambda\hat{N}_{R}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}},

(S-44)

where we use
\hat{P}_{C}(N_{R})\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}=\delta_{N_{R},r}\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}
to derive the last line.
Next, we use the completeness relation
\displaystyle\sum_{N_{L}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{R}=0}^{N}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{y}^{L,N_{L}}\\
\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}\end{subarray}}\ket{\bm{y}^{L,N_{L}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}\bra{\bm{y}^{L,N_{L}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}=1
, obtaining

\displaystyle e^{\lambda\hat{N}_{R}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}

\displaystyle=e^{\lambda\hat{N}_{R}}\left(\sum_{N_{L}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{R}=0}^{N}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{y}^{L,N_{L}}\\
\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}\end{subarray}}\ket{\bm{y}^{L,N_{L}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}\bra{\bm{y}^{L,N_{L}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}\right)e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}

(S-45)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N_{L}=0}^{N}\sum_{N_{R}=0}^{N}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{y}^{L,N_{L}}\\
\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}\end{subarray}}e^{\lambda\hat{N}_{R}}\ket{\bm{y}^{L,N_{L}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}\bra{\bm{y}^{L,N_{L}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}

(S-46)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N_{R}=0}^{N}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}}\\
\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}\end{subarray}}e^{\lambda\hat{N}_{R}}\ket{\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}\bra{\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}

(S-47)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N_{R}=0}^{N}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}}\\
\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}\end{subarray}}e^{\lambda N_{R}}\ket{\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}\bra{\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}.

(S-48)

Here, to get the third line, we use
\bra{\bm{y}^{L,N_{L}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}\propto\delta_{N_{L}+N_{R},l+r}
, which can be derived by the particle number conservation for
\hat{H}_{\rm ff}
.
Plugging Eq. (
S-48
) into Eq. (
S-44
), we obtain

\displaystyle G_{\rm ff}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4^{N}}\sum_{l=0}^{N}\sum_{r=0}^{N}e^{-\lambda r}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}^{L,l}\\
\bm{x}^{R,r}\end{subarray}}\bra{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}e^{{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\left(\sum_{N_{R}=0}^{N}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}}\\
\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}\end{subarray}}e^{\lambda N_{R}}\ket{\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}\bra{\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}\right)

(S-49)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4^{N}}\sum_{N_{R}=0}^{N}\sum_{l=0}^{N}\sum_{r=0}^{N}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}^{L,l}\\
\bm{x}^{R,r}\end{subarray}}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}}\\
\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}\end{subarray}}e^{\lambda(N_{R}-r)}|\bra{\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}|^{2}.

(S-50)

Finally, we substitute Eq. (
S-50
) into Eq. (
S-40
), getting

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\rm ff}[\Delta N_{R},t]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4^{N}}\sum_{N_{R}=0}^{N}\sum_{l=0}^{N}\sum_{r=0}^{N}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}^{L,l}\\
\bm{x}^{R,r}\end{subarray}}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}}\\
\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}\end{subarray}}\delta_{\Delta{N_{R}},N_{R}-r}|\bra{\bm{y}^{L,l+r-N_{R}},\bm{y}^{R,N_{R}}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}|^{2}

(S-51)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4^{N}}\sum_{l=0}^{N}\sum_{r=0}^{N}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}^{L,l}\\
\bm{x}^{R,r}\end{subarray}}\sideset{}{{}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{y}^{L,l-\Delta{N_{R}}}\\
\bm{y}^{R,\Delta{N_{R}}+r}\end{subarray}}|\bra{\bm{y}^{L,l-\Delta{N_{R}}},\bm{y}^{R,\Delta{N_{R}}+r}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\ket{\bm{x}^{L,l},\bm{x}^{R,r}}|^{2}

(S-52)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t],

(S-53)

where we use Eq. (
S-29
). This equality means that it is sufficient to consider the generating function
G_{\rm ff}(\lambda,t)
of Eq. (
S-39
) when we study the asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
.

III.3
Exact expansion formula of
G_{\rm ff}(\lambda,t)

We describe how to obtain the exact expansion formula of
G_{\rm ff}(\lambda,t)
that is convenient for its asymptotic analysis with
t\gg 1
.

First, we derive a determinantal expression for Eq. (
S-39
).
By Eq. (
S-39
), we obtain

\displaystyle G_{\rm ff}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left[e^{{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}e^{\lambda\hat{N}_{R}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}e^{-\lambda\hat{N}_{R}}\sum_{N_{R}}\hat{P}_{C}(N_{R})\frac{\hat{I}}{4^{N}}\hat{P}_{C}(N_{R})\right]

(S-54)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(1+e^{-\lambda})^{N}}{2^{N}}{\rm Tr}\left[e^{{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}e^{\lambda\hat{N}_{R}}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\hat{\varpi}_{0}\right],

(S-55)

where we use
\hat{\varpi}(0)=\hat{I}/4^{N}
and
\sum_{N_{R}}\hat{P}(N_{R})=1
and define
\hat{\varpi}_{0}\coloneqq(1+e^{-\lambda})^{-N}2^{-N}e^{-\lambda\hat{N}_{R}}
.
Here we note that
\hat{\varpi}_{0}
satisfies
{\rm Tr}[\hat{\varpi}_{0}]=1
because we have

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[e^{-\lambda\hat{N}_{R}}]

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\lambda\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}}\right]

(S-56)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=-N+1}^{0}{\rm Tr}_{j}[1]\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N}{\rm Tr}_{j}\left[e^{-\lambda\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}}\right]\right)

(S-57)

\displaystyle=2^{N}(1+e^{-\lambda})^{N},

(S-58)

where
{\rm Tr}_{j}[\bullet]
is the trace operation at site
j
. Thus, we can apply the Wick theorem to Eq. (
S-55
), obtaining

\displaystyle G_{\rm ff}(\lambda,t)=\dfrac{(1+e^{-\lambda})^{N}}{2^{N}}\det\left(\delta_{m,n}+(e^{\lambda}-1)C_{m,n}(t)\right)_{m,n\in\{1,2,...,N\}},

(S-59)

where the function
D_{m,n}(t)
is given by

\displaystyle C_{m,n}(t)\coloneqq{\rm Tr}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{m}(t)\hat{a}_{n}(t)\hat{\varpi}_{0}\right)

(S-60)

with
\hat{a}_{n}(t)\coloneqq e^{{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}\hat{a}_{n}e^{-{\rm i}\hat{H}_{\rm ff}t}
.

Second, we calculate the concrete expression of
C_{m,n}(t)
.
Since the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm ff}
is given as the quadratic form of the spinless fermions, the annihilation operator becomes

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{n}(t)=\sum_{q=-N+1}^{N}\hat{a}_{q}U_{q,n}(t)

(S-61)

where
U_{q,n}(t)
is the unitary matrix determined by the Hamiltonian.
Thus, we substitute Eq. (
S-61
) into Eq. (
S-60
), getting

\displaystyle C_{m,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=-N+1}^{N}\sum_{q=-N+1}^{N}U_{p,m}(t)^{*}U_{q,n}(t){\rm Tr}\left(\hat{a}_{p}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{q}\hat{\varpi}_{0}\right)

(S-62)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{e^{\lambda}+1}\sum_{p=1}^{N}U_{p,m}(t)^{*}U_{p,n}(t)+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{p=-N+1}^{0}U_{p,m}(t)^{*}U_{p,n}(t)

(S-63)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{e^{\lambda}+1}\delta_{m,n}-\dfrac{1}{e^{\lambda}+1}\sum_{p=-N+1}^{0}U_{p,m}(t)^{*}U_{p,n}(t)+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{p=-N+1}^{0}U_{p,m}(t)^{*}U_{p,n}(t)

(S-64)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{e^{\lambda}+1}\delta_{m,n}+\dfrac{e^{\lambda}-1}{2e^{\lambda}+2}\sum_{p=-N+1}^{0}U_{p,m}(t)^{*}U_{p,n}(t)

(S-65)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{e^{\lambda}+1}\delta_{m,n}+\dfrac{e^{\lambda}-1}{2e^{\lambda}+2}D_{m,n}(t)

(S-66)

with
D_{m,n}(t)=\sum_{p=-N+1}^{0}U_{p,m}(t)^{*}U_{p,n}(t)
. Here we use
{\rm Tr}\left(\hat{a}_{p}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{q}\hat{\varpi}_{0}\right)=\delta_{p,q}/(e^{\lambda}+1)
.
We put Eq. (
S-66
) into Eq. (
S-59
), getting

\displaystyle G_{\rm ff}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(1+e^{-\lambda})^{N}}{2^{N}}\det\left(\delta_{m,n}+\dfrac{e^{\lambda}-1}{e^{\lambda}+1}\delta_{m,n}+\dfrac{(e^{\lambda}-1)(e^{\lambda}-1)}{2e^{\lambda}+2}D_{m,n}(t)\right)_{m,n\in\{1,2,...,N\}}

(S-67)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(1+e^{-\lambda})^{N}}{2^{N}}\det\left(\dfrac{2}{1+e^{-\lambda}}\delta_{m,n}+\dfrac{(e^{\lambda/2}-e^{-\lambda/2})^{2}}{2+2e^{-\lambda}}D_{m,n}(t)\right)_{m,n\in\{1,2,...,N\}}

(S-68)

\displaystyle=\det\left(\delta_{m,n}+\dfrac{(e^{\lambda/2}-e^{-\lambda/2})^{2}}{4}D_{m,n}(t)\right)_{m,n\in\{1,2,...,N\}}

(S-69)

\displaystyle=\det\left(\delta_{m,n}+\sinh\left(\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\right)^{2}D_{m,n}(t)\right)_{m,n\in\{1,2,...,N\}}

(S-70)

Here, we comment on the expression for
D_{m,n}(t)
.
Using Eq. (
S-61
), we derive

\displaystyle D_{m,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=-N+1}^{0}U_{p,m}(t)^{*}U_{p,n}(t)

(S-71)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=-N+1}^{0}\sum_{q=-N+1}^{0}U_{p,m}(t)^{*}U_{q,n}(t)\delta_{p,q}

(S-72)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=-N+1}^{N}\sum_{q=-N+1}^{N}U_{p,m}(t)^{*}U_{q,n}(t){\rm Tr}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{p}\hat{a}_{q}\hat{\varpi}_{\rm DW}\right)

(S-73)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{m}(t)\hat{a}_{n}(t)\hat{\varpi}_{\rm DW}\right),

(S-74)

where we define the domain-wall initial state as

\displaystyle\hat{\varpi}_{\rm DW}\coloneqq\left(\prod_{n=-N+1}^{0}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{n}\right)\ket{0}\bra{0}\left(\prod_{n=-N+1}^{0}\hat{a}_{n}\right)

(S-75)

with the vacuum
\ket{0}
for the spinless free fermions. Hence, we can calculate the time dependence of
D_{m,n}(t)
by solving the following differential equation:

\displaystyle{\rm i}\dfrac{d}{dt}D_{m,n}(t)=D_{m+1,n}(t)+D_{m-1,n}(t)-D_{m,n+1}(t)-D_{m,n-1}(t),

(S-76)

where the initial condition is
D_{m,n}(0)=\delta_{m,n}\chi[m\in\{-N+1,...,0\}]
with the indicator function
\chi[\bullet]
. The boundary condition is open, namely
D_{N+1,n}(t)=0
,
D_{m,N+1}(t)=0
,
D_{-N,n}(t)=0
, and
D_{m,-N}(t)=0
. Here, we emphasize that the calculation of
G_{\rm ff}(\lambda,t)
can be reduced to the full counting statistics for the free-fermion dynamics starting from the simple domain-wall initial state. Thanks to this simplification, we can perform the asymptotic analysis of
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
, as shown in the following subsection.

Third, we take the thermodynamic limit (
N\rightarrow\infty
).
Then, we obtain

\displaystyle D_{m,n}(t)={\rm i}^{n-m}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}J_{m+l}(2t)J_{n+l}(2t).

(S-77)

This can be derived as follows.
We define a function
\bar{D}(k_{1},k_{2},t)
as

\displaystyle\bar{D}(k_{1},k_{2},t)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}D_{m,n}(t)e^{{\rm i}(k_{1}m-k_{2}n)}.

(S-78)

Then, we can expand
D_{m,n}(t)
as

\displaystyle D_{m,n}(t)=\dfrac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk_{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk_{2}\bar{D}(k_{1},k_{2},t)e^{{\rm i}(-k_{1}m+k_{2}n)}.

(S-79)

After putting it into Eq. (
S-76
), we get
\bar{D}(k_{1},k_{2},t)=\bar{D}(k_{1},k_{2},0)e^{-{\rm i}2t\left(\cos(k_{1})-\cos(k_{2})\right)}
.
Here, the initial condition is given by

\displaystyle\bar{D}(k_{1},k_{2},0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}D_{m,n}(0)e^{{\rm i}(k_{1}m-k_{2}n)}

(S-80)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{0}e^{{\rm i}(k_{1}-k_{2})m}.

(S-81)

Combining all the results above, we obtain

\displaystyle D_{m,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{0}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk_{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk_{2}e^{{\rm i}(-k_{1}(m-l)-2t\cos(k_{1}))}e^{{\rm i}(k_{2}(n-l)+2t\cos(k_{2}))}

(S-82)

\displaystyle={\rm i}^{n-m}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}J_{n+l}(2t)J_{m+l}(2t),

(S-83)

where we use the integral formula for the Bessel function
J_{n}(x)
of the first kind:

\displaystyle J_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}^{n}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\exp\left({\rm i}x\cos\theta+{\rm i}n\theta\right)

(S-84)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{{\rm i}^{n}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\exp\left(-{\rm i}x\cos\theta-{\rm i}n\theta\right).

(S-85)

This completes the derivation of Eq. (
S-77
).

Finally, we consider the logarithm of the generating function, deriving the following expansion:

\displaystyle\log G_{\rm ff}(\lambda,t)=-\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{a}}{a}\omega^{a}{\rm Tr}E(t)^{a}

(S-86)

with
\omega\coloneqq{\rm sinh}^{2}(\lambda/2)
.
According to Appendix A in Ref.
Moriya et al. 2019
of the main text, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}E(t)^{a}=\dfrac{1}{4^{a}}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{2a}\int_{C_{r}}\dfrac{dz_{k}}{2\pi{\rm i}z_{k}}\int_{0}^{2t}dt_{k}\right]~\left\{\prod_{k=1}^{2a}{\rm exp}\left[\dfrac{\sqrt{t_{k}t_{k+1}}}{2}\left(z_{k}-\dfrac{1}{z_{k}}\right)\right]\right\}.

(S-87)

This expression is convenient for implementing the asymptotic analysis with
t\gg 1
because the functions in the complex integral, except for the exponential term, have simple poles only at the origin. In the next subsection, we apply the asymptotic analysis to this expression.

III.4
Asymptotic analysis of
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
via a saddle-node approximation

We analytically derive the asymptotic form of
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
with
t\gg 1
by applying the conventional saddle-node approximation to Eqs. (
S-86
) and (
S-87
). First, we obtain the asymptotic expression of
{\rm Tr}E(t)^{a}
. Second, we take the summation of Eq. (
S-86
), getting the asymptotic expression of
G_{\rm ff}(\lambda,t)
. Finally, we transform the generating function
G_{\rm ff}(\lambda,t)
to the probability
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
via Eq. (
S-40
) and then obtain the asymptotic expression of
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
. In what follows, we set
T\coloneqq 2t
to simplify the expressions derived here.

III.4.1
a=1
case

We shall derive the asymptotic form of
{\rm Tr}E(t)
.
By definition, we get

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}E(t)=\dfrac{1}{4}\int_{C_{r}}\dfrac{dz_{1}}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{C_{r}}\dfrac{dz_{2}}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{T}dt_{1}\int_{0}^{T}dt_{2}~\dfrac{1}{z_{1}z_{2}}{\rm exp}\left[\dfrac{\sqrt{t_{1}t_{2}}}{2}\left(f(z_{1})+f(z_{2})\right)\right]

(S-88)

with
f(z)\coloneqq z-z^{-1}
.
We use
z_{k}=e^{{\rm i}\theta_{k}}
, getting

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}E(t)=\dfrac{T^{2}}{4(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx_{1}\int_{0}^{1}dx_{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta_{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta_{2}e^{{\rm i}T\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}\left[\sin(\theta_{1})+\sin(\theta_{2})\right]}.

(S-89)

The extremum of
\theta
is
\theta=(2n+1)\pi/2~(n\in\mathbb{Z})
, and thus it is enough to consider two of them, which lead to the following expansions:

\displaystyle\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}+\Delta\right)\sim 1-\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta^{2},

(S-90)

\displaystyle\sin\left(\dfrac{3\pi}{2}+\Delta\right)\sim-1+\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta^{2}.

(S-91)

Thus, we have the asymptotic form given by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta_{1}e^{{\rm i}T\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}\sin(\theta_{1})}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta{\rm exp}\left({{\rm i}T\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}-{\rm i}t\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}\Delta^{2}/2}\right)+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta{\rm exp}\left(-{\rm i}T\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}+{\rm i}t\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}\Delta^{2}/2\right)

(S-92)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\dfrac{2\pi}{T\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}}}\cos\left(T\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right).

(S-93)

Putting this into
{\rm Tr}E(t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}E(t)

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{T^{2}}{4(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx_{1}\int_{0}^{1}dx_{2}\dfrac{8\pi}{T\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}}\cos\left(T\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)^{2}

(S-94)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2T}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{1}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{2}\cos\left(Ty_{1}y_{2}-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)^{2}

(S-95)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2T}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{1}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{2}\left[\dfrac{1}{2}+\cos\left(2Ty_{1}y_{2}-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\right]

(S-96)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{T}{\pi}.

(S-97)

III.4.2
a=2
case

We shall consider the asymptotic form of
{\rm Tr}E(t)^{2}
, but in this case, one will find a difference from the case of
a=1
.
By definition, we get

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}E(t)^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{T^{4}}{4^{2}}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{4}\int_{0}^{1}dx_{k}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dfrac{d\theta_{k}}{2\pi}\right]\left[\prod_{k=1}^{4}{\rm exp}\left({\rm i}T\sqrt{x_{k}x_{k+1}}\sin\theta_{k}\right)\right].

(S-98)

As in the previous asymptotic analysis, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dfrac{d\theta_{k}}{2\pi}e^{{\rm i}T\sqrt{x_{k}x_{k+1}}\sin(\theta_{k})}\sim\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{T\pi\sqrt{x_{k}x_{k+1}}}}\cos\left(T\sqrt{x_{k}x_{k+1}}-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right).

(S-99)

Then, combining them, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}E(t)^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{T^{4}}{4^{2}}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{4}\int_{0}^{1}dx_{k}\right]\left[\prod_{k=1}^{4}\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi T\sqrt{x_{k}x_{k+1}}}}\cos\left(T\sqrt{x_{k}x_{k+1}}-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)\right]

(S-100)

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{2T}{\pi}\right)^{2}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{4}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{k}\right]\left[\prod_{k=1}^{4}\cos\left(Ty_{k}y_{k+1}-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)\right],

(S-101)

where we introduce
y_{k}=\sqrt{x_{k}}
.
We can proceed with the further calculations as

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}E(t)^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\left(\dfrac{2T}{\pi}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{1}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{2}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{3}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{4}\cos\left(Ty_{1}y_{2}-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)\cos\left(Ty_{2}y_{3}-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 169.00934pt\times\cos\left(Ty_{3}y_{4}-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)\cos\left(Ty_{4}y_{1}-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)

(S-102)

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{2T}{\pi}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{1}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{2}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{3}\int_{0}^{1}dy_{4}\dfrac{1}{4}\left[\cos\left(T(y_{1}+y_{3})y_{2}-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)+\cos\left(T(y_{1}-y_{3})y_{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 176.46399pt\times\left[\cos\left(T(y_{3}+y_{1})y_{4}-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)+\cos\left(T(y_{3}-y_{1})y_{4}\right)\right]

(S-104)

where we neglect the rapidly oscillating terms to get the last line.

y_{2}

(S-108)

III.4.3

We generalize the above result to the general case.

\displaystyle I_{2m}(T)

Here, we introduce

Hence, we finally get

(S-117)

IV
Asymptotic analysis of
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S^{z}_{R},t]

We shall investigate the asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S^{z}_{R},t]
using Eqs. (
S-34
) and (
S-128
).
The exact expression of
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S^{z}_{R},t]
under the thermodynamic limit (
N\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S^{z}_{R},t]=\delta_{\Delta S^{z}_{R},0}\mathbb{P}_{C}[0,t]+2\sum^{\infty}_{\Delta N_{R}=1}\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]\sum_{n=0}^{\Delta N_{R}}\dfrac{~{}_{\Delta N_{R}}C_{n}}{2^{\Delta N_{R}}}\delta_{\Delta N_{R}-\Delta S^{z}_{R}-2n,0}.

(S-129)

One can readily show the relation
\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S^{z}_{R},t]=\mathbb{P}_{S}[-\Delta S^{z}_{R},t]
because of
~{}_{\Delta N_{R}}C_{n}=~_{\Delta N_{R}}C_{\Delta N_{R}-n}
and
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]=\mathbb{P}_{C}[-\Delta N_{R},t]
, and thus we can focus on the case with
\Delta S^{z}_{R}\geq 0
in the following.

To proceed with the asymptotic analysis of Eq. (
S-129
), we note that the typical fluctuation of
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
for
t\gg 1
is given by Eq. (
S-128
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{C}[\sqrt{t}\mathcal{J}_{C},t]\sim\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2t}}\exp\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}\right).

(S-130)

Then, it is convenient to introduce a small bin
\Delta\mathcal{J}_{C}=2/\sqrt{t}
, where the factor
2
is put since the summation of Eq. (
S-129
) over
\Delta N_{R}
is taken only for even or odd numbers.
As a result, we obtain, for
t\gg 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S^{z}_{R},t]

\displaystyle=\delta_{\Delta S^{z}_{R},0}\mathbb{P}_{C}[0,t]+\sqrt{t}\sum^{\infty}_{\Delta N_{R}=1}\Delta\mathcal{J}_{C}~\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]\dfrac{~{}_{\Delta N_{R}}C_{(\Delta N_{R}-\Delta S^{z}_{R})/2}}{2^{\Delta N_{R}}}\dfrac{1+(-1)^{\Delta N_{R}-\Delta S_{R}^{z}}}{2}\theta(\Delta N_{R}-\Delta S^{z}_{R})

(S-131)

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int^{\infty}_{\Delta S_{R}^{z}t^{-1/2}}d\mathcal{J}_{C}~\exp\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}\right)\sqrt{\dfrac{2\sqrt{t}\mathcal{J}_{C}}{\pi t\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}-\pi(\Delta S_{R}^{z})^{2}}}\left(1-\dfrac{(\Delta S^{z}_{R})^{2}}{t\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}}\right)^{-\sqrt{t}\mathcal{J}_{C}/2}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{t}\mathcal{J}_{C}-\Delta S_{R}^{z}}{\sqrt{t}\mathcal{J}_{C}+\Delta S_{R}^{z}}\right)^{\Delta S^{z}_{R}/2}.

(S-132)

Here, we use the asymptotic expression given by

~{}_{N}C_{n}\sim\sqrt{\dfrac{N}{2\pi n(N-n)}}\left(\dfrac{n}{N}\right)^{-n}\left(1-\dfrac{n}{N}\right)^{n-N}

(S-133)

for
N,n,~N-n\gg 1
.

We next introduce the scaled integrated spin current
\mathcal{J}_{S}\coloneqq\Delta S^{z}_{R}t^{-\eta}
with a real parameter
\eta\in(0,1/2)
.
The expression of Eq. (
S-132
) reads

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{S}[t^{\eta}\mathcal{J}_{S},t]

\displaystyle\sim\int^{\infty}_{\mathcal{J}_{S}t^{\eta-1/2}}d\mathcal{J}_{C}~\exp\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}\right)\sqrt{\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{C}}{\pi t^{1/2}\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}-\pi t^{2\eta-1/2}\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}}\left(1-\dfrac{t^{2\eta-1}\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}{\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}}\right)^{-\sqrt{t}\mathcal{J}_{C}/2}\left(\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{C}-t^{\eta-1/2}\mathcal{J}_{S}}{\mathcal{J}_{N}+t^{\eta-1/2}\mathcal{J}_{S}}\right)^{t^{\eta}\mathcal{J}_{S}/2}

(S-134)

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{\mathcal{J}_{S}t^{\eta-1/2}}d\mathcal{J}_{C}~\sqrt{\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{C}}{\pi t^{1/2}\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}-\pi t^{2\eta-1/2}\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}}\exp\left[-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}+\sqrt{t}g(\mathcal{J}_{C})+t^{\eta}\mathcal{J}_{S}h(\mathcal{J}_{C})\right],

(S-135)

where we define the two functions
g(\mathcal{J}_{C})
and
h(\mathcal{J}_{C})
as

\displaystyle g(\mathcal{J}_{C})\coloneqq\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{C}}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}}{\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}-t^{2\eta-1}\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}\right),

(S-136)

\displaystyle h(\mathcal{J}_{C})\coloneqq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{C}-t^{\eta-1/2}\mathcal{J}_{S}}{\mathcal{J}_{C}+t^{\eta-1/2}\mathcal{J}_{S}}\right).

(S-137)

Because the dominant contribution to the integral of Eq. (
S-135
) comes from values of
\mathcal{J}_{C}
of order unity, due to the factor
e^{-\pi\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}/2}
, we can expand
g(\mathcal{J}_{C})
and
h(\mathcal{J}_{C})
for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle g(\mathcal{J}_{C})\sim t^{2\eta-1}\frac{\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}{2\mathcal{J}_{C}},

(S-138)

\displaystyle h(\mathcal{J}_{C})\sim-t^{\eta-1/2}\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{S}}{\mathcal{J}_{C}},

(S-139)

where
t^{2\eta-1}
and
t^{\eta-1/2}
are small because of the assumption
0<\eta<1/2

Putting them together, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{S}[t^{\eta}\mathcal{J}_{S},t]\sim\int^{\infty}_{\mathcal{J}_{S}t^{\eta-1/2}}d\mathcal{J}_{C}~\sqrt{\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{C}}{\pi t^{1/2}\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}-\pi t^{2\eta-1/2}\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}}\exp\left[-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}-t^{2\eta-1/2}\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}{2\mathcal{J}_{C}}\right].

(S-140)

Finally, we substitute
\eta=1/4
into Eq. (
S-140

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{S}[t^{1/4}\mathcal{J}_{S},t]

\displaystyle\sim\int^{\infty}_{\mathcal{J}_{S}t^{-1/4}}d\mathcal{J}_{C}~\sqrt{\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{C}}{\pi t^{1/2}\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}-\pi\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}}\exp\left[-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}-\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}{2\mathcal{J}_{C}}\right]

\displaystyle\sim t^{-1/4}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\dfrac{d\mathcal{J}_{C}}{{\sqrt{\mathcal{J}_{C}}}}\exp\left[-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}-\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}{2\mathcal{J}_{C}}\right].

(S-142)

Therefore, we derive the following limiting expression for the scaled probability

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{1/4}\mathbb{P}_{S}[t^{1/4}\mathcal{J}_{S},t]=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\dfrac{d\mathcal{J}_{C}}{{\sqrt{\mathcal{J}_{C}}}}\exp\left[-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}-\dfrac{\mathcal{J}_{S}^{2}}{2\mathcal{J}_{C}}\right].

Our numerical calculation is based on the following equation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{S}[\Delta S^{z}_{R},t]=\delta_{\Delta S^{z}_{R},0}\mathbb{P}_{C}[0,t]+2\sum^{N}_{\Delta N_{R}=1}\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]\dfrac{~{}_{\Delta N_{R}}C_{(\Delta N_{R}+\Delta S^{z}_{R})/2}}{2^{\Delta N_{R}}}\dfrac{1+(-1)^{\Delta N_{R}+\Delta S^{z}_{R}}}{2}\theta\left(\Delta N_{R}\geq\Delta S_{R}^{z}\right).

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\oint_{C_{r}}dz~\bar{G}_{\rm ff}(z,t)z^{-\Delta N_{R}-1},

(S-145)

\displaystyle\bar{G}_{\rm ff}(z,t)

\displaystyle=\det\left(\delta_{m,n}+\dfrac{z+z^{-1}-2}{4}D_{m,n}(t)\right)_{m,n\in\{1,2,...,N\}},

(S-146)

\displaystyle{\rm i}\dfrac{d}{dt}D_{m,n}(t)

\displaystyle=D_{m+1,n}(t)+D_{m-1,n}(t)-D_{m,n+1}(t)-D_{m,n-1}(t)

(S-147)

with the initial condition

,
D_{m,N+1}(t)=0
,
D_{-N,n}(t)=0
, and
D_{m,-N}(t)=0
.

VI
Asymptotic analysis of the classical automaton

We shall review the result reported in Ref.
Krajnik et al. 2022b
of the main text, where the M-Wright function for the integrated spin current was derived exactly in a classical automaton. We can reproduce the previous result using an analytical method similar to our derivation of the M-Wright function for the
t0
model. This derivation is a bit different from the original one in the previous work and is convenient for discussing the difference between the
t0
model and the classical automaton.

VI.1
setup

We review the setup for the classical automaton in Ref.
Krajnik et al. 2022b
of the main text. Suppose that we have a one-dimensional lattice labeled by
\mathbb{Z}
and assign each site three states, namely a up-spin state (
\uparrow
), a down-spin state (
\downarrow
), a vacant state (
\varnothing
). Then a whole state is given by a sequence of these symbols such as
(\cdots\uparrow\varnothing\downarrow\uparrow\uparrow\varnothing\downarrow\uparrow\varnothing\cdots)
.
Note that the authors in Ref.
Krajnik et al. 2022b
of the main text use “charge” instead of “spin”, but we here use “spin” for a better comparison with the
t0
model.
The time-evolution rule is constructed by a two-site propagator
\hat{U}_{j,j+1}^{\rm CA}
which induces only the exchanges
(\uparrow\varnothing)\leftrightarrow(\varnothing\uparrow)
and
(\downarrow\varnothing)\leftrightarrow(\varnothing\downarrow)
. Then, the time-evolution propagator
\hat{U}^{\rm CA}
is defined by

\displaystyle\hat{U}^{\rm CA}\coloneqq\prod_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{U}_{2j,2j+1}^{\rm CA}\times\prod_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{U}_{2k-1,2k}^{\rm CA}.

(S-148)

We can obtain a time-evolved state by repeatedly applying
\hat{U}^{\rm CA}
to the initial state.

VI.2
Probability
\mathbb{P}_{S}^{\rm CA}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]
for the integrated spin current
\Delta S_{R}^{z}

We derive the explicit expression of
\mathbb{P}_{S}^{\rm CA}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]
for the integrated spin current
\Delta S_{R}^{z}
.
According to Eq. (5) with
b=0
in Ref.
Krajnik et al. 2022b
of the main text, the generating function
G_{S}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)
associated with
\mathbb{P}_{S}^{\rm CA}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]
is given by

\displaystyle G_{S}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{t}\sum_{r=0}^{t}~{}_{t}C_{l}~{}_{t}C_{r}\rho^{2t-l-r}\bar{\rho}^{l+r}\left(\cosh(\lambda)\right)^{|l-r|}

(S-149)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Delta N_{R}=-t}^{t}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{t}\sum_{r=0}^{t}~{}_{t}C_{l}~{}_{t}C_{r}\rho^{2t-l-r}\bar{\rho}^{l+r}\delta_{\Delta N_{R},r-l}\right)\left(\cosh(\lambda)\right)^{|\Delta N_{R}|},

(S-150)

where
\rho
and
\bar{\rho}
are the probabilities for the particles and the vacancies.

To make a better comparison between the
t0
model and the classical automaton, we define the following function:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\Delta N_{R},t]\coloneqq\sum_{l=0}^{t}\sum_{r=0}^{t}~{}_{t}C_{l}~{}_{t}C_{r}\rho^{2t-l-r}\bar{\rho}^{l+r}\delta_{\Delta N_{R},r-l}.

(S-151)

This quantity can be interpreted as the probability that the transferred particle number to the right region (
j\geq 1
) is
\Delta N_{R}
by definition.
Then, we can express
G_{S}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)
as

\displaystyle G_{S}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{\Delta N_{R}=-t}^{t}\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\Delta N_{R},t]\left(\cosh(\lambda)\right)^{|\Delta N_{R}|}

(S-152)

When the initial state has
\rho=1/2
and
\bar{\rho}=1/2
, we have the relation
\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\Delta N_{R},t]=\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[-\Delta N_{R},t]
.
Then, the generating function becomes

\displaystyle G_{S}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)=\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[0,t]+2\sum_{\Delta N_{R}=1}^{t}\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\Delta N_{R},t]\left(\cosh(\lambda)\right)^{\Delta N_{R}}.

(S-154)

In the remaining part of this section, we focus only on the case with
\rho=1/2
and
\bar{\rho}=1/2
.

We transform the generating function of Eq. (
S-154
) into the probability
\mathbb{P}_{S}^{\rm CA}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]
, obtaining

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{S}^{\rm CA}[\Delta S_{R}^{z},t]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{C_{r}}dz~G_{S}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)z^{-\Delta S_{R}^{z}-1}

(S-155)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\Delta S_{R}^{z},0}\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[0,t]+2\sum_{\Delta N_{R}=1}^{t}\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\Delta N_{R},t]\sum_{n=0}^{\Delta N_{R}}\dfrac{~{}_{\Delta N_{R}}C_{n}}{2^{\Delta N_{R}}}\delta_{\Delta S^{z}_{R},\Delta N_{R}-2n}

(S-156)

with
z=e^{\lambda}
.The expression of Eq. (
S-156
) is formally the same as Eq. (
5
) of the
t0
model in the main text.
This expression implies that the difference between the
t0
model and the classical automaton stems only from the charge probabilities, namely
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
and
\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\Delta N_{R},t]
. In the following, we derive the M-Wright function using Eq. (
S-156
) and subsequently clarify this difference.

VI.3
Asymptotic analysis of
\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\Delta N_{R},t]

To derive the M-Wright function, it is important to investigate the asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\Delta N_{R},t]
.
For this purpose, we use the generating function
G_{C}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)
corresponding to
\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\Delta N_{R},t]
, given by

\displaystyle G_{C}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{\Delta N_{R}=-t}^{t}\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\Delta N_{R},t]e^{\lambda\Delta N_{R}}

(S-157)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4^{t}}\sum_{\Delta N_{R}=-t}^{t}e^{\lambda\Delta N_{R}}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{t}\sum_{r=0}^{t}~{}_{t}C_{l}~{}_{t}C_{r}\delta_{\Delta N_{R},r-l}\right)

(S-158)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4^{t}}\sum_{l=0}^{t}\sum_{r=0}^{t}~{}_{t}C_{l}~{}_{t}C_{r}e^{\lambda\left(r-l\right)}.

(S-159)

First, we consider the long-time dynamics (
t\gg 1
) for the generating function
G_{C}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)
. Then, we have a small quantity
1/t
and then introduce discrete variables
x_{r}=r/t
and
y_{l}=l/t
. As a result, the summation of Eq. (
S-159
) can be transformed to be

\displaystyle G_{C}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)\sim\dfrac{t}{2\pi\cdot 4^{t}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1}dy\dfrac{e^{-tf(x,y)}}{\sqrt{xy(1-x)(1-y)}},

(S-160)

where
x
and
y
are the continuous variables corresponding to
x_{r}
and
x_{l}
, respectively, and
f(x,y)
is defined by

\displaystyle f(x,y)\coloneqq\log\left(x^{x}y^{y}(1-x)^{1-x}(1-y)^{1-y}\right)+\lambda(y-x).

(S-161)

To derive the above, we use the asymptotic formula given by

~{}_{t}C_{l}\sim\sqrt{\dfrac{t}{2\pi l(t-l)}}\left(\dfrac{l}{t}\right)^{-l}\left(1-\dfrac{l}{t}\right)^{l-t}

(S-162)

for
t\gg 1
,
l\gg 1
, and
t-l\gg 1
.

Second, we apply the method of steepest descent to Eq. (
S-160
).
Following the conventional procedure, we look for the local minimum point
(x_{0},y_{0})
of the function
f(x,y)
, which dominantly contributes to the integral.
After elementary calculations, we obtain

\displaystyle(x_{0},y_{0})=\left(\dfrac{1}{e^{-\lambda}+1},\dfrac{1}{e^{\lambda}+1}\right).

(S-163)

Then, the function
f(x,y)
can be expanded as

\displaystyle f(x,y)\sim f(x_{0},y_{0})+\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{(e^{\lambda}+1)^{2}}{e^{\lambda}}(x-x_{0})^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{(e^{\lambda}+1)^{2}}{e^{\lambda}}(y-y_{0})^{2}

(S-164)

Hence, we apply the method of steepest descent, getting the asymptotic expression of
G_{C}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)
for
t\gg 1
as

\displaystyle G_{C}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{\exp\left(-2t\log 2-tf(x_{0},y_{0})+\log t\right)}{2\pi\sqrt{x_{0}y_{0}(1-x_{0})(1-y_{0})}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\exp\left(-\dfrac{t}{2}\dfrac{(e^{\lambda}+1)^{2}}{e^{\lambda}}x^{2}-\dfrac{t}{2}\dfrac{(e^{\lambda}+1)^{2}}{e^{\lambda}}y^{2}\right)

(S-165)

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-2t\log 2-tf(x_{0},y_{0})+\log t-\log\left(2\pi\sqrt{x_{0}y_{0}(1-x_{0})(1-y_{0})}\right)+\log\left(\dfrac{2\pi e^{\lambda}}{t(e^{\lambda}+1)^{2}}\right)\right]

(S-166)

\displaystyle\sim\exp\left[-2t\log 2-tf(x_{0},y_{0})\right]

(S-167)

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}e^{\lambda}+\dfrac{1}{4}e^{-\lambda}\right)^{t},

(S-168)

where we leave only the leading term in the third line and the relation
x_{0}+y_{0}
in the last line. As a result, the generating function becomes

\displaystyle\log G_{C}^{\rm CA}(\lambda,t)\sim t\log\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}e^{\lambda}+\dfrac{1}{4}e^{-\lambda}\right)

(S-169)

for
t\gg 1
. From this expression, one can find that the first cumulant vanishes and the second one becomes
t/2
.

Third, we derive the probability
\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\Delta N_{R},t]
for the typical fluctuation using Eq. (
S-169
).
The detailed calculation is almost the same as that for the derivation of Eq. (
S-128
), so we do not repeat the calculation here. The resulting probability is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\sqrt{t}\mathcal{J}_{C},t]\sim\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\exp\left(-\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}\right)

(S-170)

for
t\gg 1
.

Finally, we comment on the difference between
\mathbb{P}_{C}^{\rm CA}[\Delta N_{R},t]
and
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
. The apparent difference is the factor of
\mathcal{J}_{C}^{2}
in the exponential.
This reflects the different microscopic dynamics for the classical automaton and the
t0
models. The classical automaton has only the two propagating modes, while the
t0
model has the infinite propagating modes as the eigenstates of the free fermions are characterized by the continuous wavenumber ranging from
-\pi
to
\pi
. This results in the difference of the factor, and one can understand this fact clearly by Sec.
VII
, where we derive the probability
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
under a hydrodynamic assumption by highlighting the role of the infinitely many modes.

We shall give another derivation of Eq. (

of the main text, and one can obtain a physically intuitive understanding of the emergence of the M-Wright function for the integrated spin current.

We now apply BMFT to the
t0
model to derive the asymptotic probability
\mathbb{P}_{C}[\Delta N_{R},t]
. As described in Sec.
III
, the probability is determined by the dynamics of free fermions. Thus, the application of BMFT to it is straightforward because solving the equations of motion for GHD in Refs.
Castro-Alvaredo et al. 2016

;
Doyon 2020
;
Alba et al. 2021
;
Bouchoule and Dubail 2022

of the main text is trivial. After applying BMFT to the free fermions, we obtain the following equations for the generating function

of

under the BMFT approximation:

\displaystyle\log G^{\rm BMFT}_{C}(\lambda,t)=\dfrac{t}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dkF^{\rm BMFT}_{C}(\lambda,k,t),

(S-172)

\displaystyle F^{\rm BMFT}_{C}(\lambda,k,t)=\dfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{\lambda}d\lambda^{\prime}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau v(k)n(k,0,\tau),

(S-173)

where we define
v(k)\coloneqq 2\sin k
and the occupation number as

\displaystyle n(k,x,t)\coloneqq\dfrac{1}{e^{\beta(k,x,t)}+1}

(S-174)

with the local inverse temperature
\beta(k,x,t)
.
The inverse temperature is determined by

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\beta(k,x,\tau)+v(k)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\beta(k,x,\tau)=0,

(S-175)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\tau}H(k,x,\tau)+v(k)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}H(k,x,\tau)=0,

(S-176)

\displaystyle H(k,x,0)=\lambda\theta(x)-\beta(x,k,0),

(S-177)

\displaystyle H(k,x,t)=\lambda\theta(x),

(S-178)

where
H(k,x,t)
is the auxiliary field for the inverse temperature and
\theta(x)
is the step function. These equations can be derived mainly using the results of Sec. 5.3 in Ref.
Doyon et al. 2023b
of the main text. Solving the equations for
\beta(k,x,\tau)
and
H(k,x,\tau)
, we can calculate the cumulant generating function
\log G^{\rm BMFT}_{C}(\lambda,t)
by Eqs. (
S-172
), (
S-173
), and (
S-174
).

First, we solve the equation of motion for
\beta(k,x,\tau)
.
To get the initial condition
\beta(k,x,0)
, we solve Eq. (
S-176
) and then obtain

\displaystyle H(k,x,\tau)=f(k,x-v(k)\tau)

(S-179)

with a unknown function
f(\bullet)
.
This equation with Eq. (
S-178
) leads to

\displaystyle H(k,x,\tau)=\lambda\theta(x+v(k)t-v(k)\tau).

(S-180)

Thus, we can derive the initial condition
\beta(x,k,0)
via Eq. (
S-177
) as

\displaystyle\beta(k,x,0)=\lambda\theta(x)-\lambda\theta(x+v(k)t).

(S-181)

Using this initial condition, we can solve Eq. (
S-175
), getting

\displaystyle\beta(k,x,\tau)=\lambda\theta(x-v(k)\tau)-\lambda\theta(x+v(k)t-v(k)\tau).

(S-182)

Second, we derive the expression of
\beta(k,0,\tau)
for
v(k)\geq 0
with Eq. (
S-182
) because the computation of Eq. (
S-173
) via Eq. (
S-174
) needs the inverse temperature only at the origin (
x=0
).
The detailed expression becomes

\displaystyle\beta(k,0,\tau)=\begin{cases}-\lambda&(0\leq\tau\leq t)\\
0&(t<\tau)\\
0&(\tau<0).\end{cases}

(S-183)

Thus, the definition of the occupation number of Eq. (
S-174
) leads to

\displaystyle n(k,0,\tau)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{e^{\lambda}}{1+e^{\lambda}}&(0\leq\tau\leq t)\\
1/2&(t<\tau)\\
1/2&(\tau<0).\end{cases}

(S-184)

Hence, we get, for
v(k)\geq 0
,

\displaystyle F^{\rm BMFT}_{C}(\lambda,k,t)=v(k)\log\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}e^{\lambda}\right).

(S-185)

Third, we consider the case with
v(k)<0
.
Then, using Eq. (
S-182
), we obtain, in the same manner as above,

\displaystyle\beta(k,0,\tau)=\begin{cases}\lambda&(0\leq\tau\leq t)\\
0&(t<\tau)\\
0&(\tau<0).\end{cases}

(S-186)

This readily leads to

\displaystyle n(k,0,\tau)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{e^{-\lambda}}{1+e^{-\lambda}}&(0\leq\tau\leq t)\\
1/2&(t<\tau)\\
1/2&(\tau<0).\end{cases}

(S-187)

As a result, we get, for
v(k)<0
,

\displaystyle F^{\rm BMFT}_{C}(\lambda,k,t)=-v(k)\log\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}e^{-\lambda}\right).

(S-188)

Finally, we substitute Eqs. (
S-185
) and (
S-188
) into Eq. (
S-172
), obtaining

\displaystyle\log G^{\rm BMFT}_{C}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{t}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}dkv(k)\log\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}e^{\lambda}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}e^{-\lambda}\right)

(S-189)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2t}{\pi}\log\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}e^{\lambda}+\dfrac{1}{4}e^{-\lambda}\right).

(S-190)

This is identical to the cumulant generating function obtained in the asymptotic analysis based on the exact expression (see Eq. (
S-121
)). Note that the essentially same generating function was reported in Ref.
Schönhammer 2007
of the main text, where scattering matrix techniques were used. Therefore, following the same calculations given in Sec.
III.4.5
, we can derive the probability of Eq. (
S-128
) for the typical fluctuations of the integrated charge current.

This hydrodynamic calculation clearly shows that the infinite number of propagating modes

contribute to the cumulant generating function

. This situation is very different from the classical automaton in Ref.

of the main text, where there are only two propagating modes. This point is explained in the main text and Sec.

VIII
Details for the hydrodynamic derivation of the M-Wright function

VIII.1
Derivation of the effective velocity via a Gaudin matrix

We derive the effective velocity of Eqs. (
E-8
) and (
E-9
) of End Matter using a Gaudin matrix. The method used here is based on Refs.
Borsi et al. 2020
;
Pozsgay 2020
;
Borsi et al. 2021
of the main text.

We consider the setup used in End Matter of the main text. Then, the Bethe equation of the
t0
model
Izergin et al. 1998
;
Abarenkova et al. 2001
;
Essler et al. 2005
is given by

\displaystyle e^{{\rm i}\lambda^{\rm(c)}_{a}L}

\displaystyle=e^{{\rm i}\Theta}~~~~~~~~~~(a\in\{1,2,...,N_{p}\}),

(S-191)

\displaystyle e^{{\rm i}\lambda^{\rm(s)}_{b}N_{p}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{M+1}~~~(b\in\{1,2,...,M\})

(S-192)

with
\Theta=\sum_{b=1}^{M}\lambda^{\rm(s)}_{b}
.
When taking the logarithm of the above, we get

\displaystyle\lambda^{\rm(c)}_{a}L-\sum_{b=1}^{M}\lambda^{\rm(s)}_{b}=2\pi I^{\rm(c)}_{a},

(S-194)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\rm(s)}_{b}N_{p}-\pi(M+1)=2\pi I^{\rm(s)}_{b},

(S-195)

where
I^{\rm(c)}_{a}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
I^{\rm(s)}_{b}\in\mathbb{Z}
are the Bethe quantum numbers.

According to Refs.
Borsi et al. 2020
;
Pozsgay 2020
;
Borsi et al. 2021
of the main text, there is a conjecture for the form factor of current operators in multicomponent systems.
To be specific, the averages of a current operator
\hat{J}_{m}
corresponding to a local conserved quantity
\hat{Q}_{m}
is given by

\displaystyle\bra{\{\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{a}\}}\hat{J}_{m}\ket{\{\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{a}\}}

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{e}^{\prime}\right)^{\rm T}\cdot G^{-1}\cdot\bm{h}_{m}

(S-196)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{L}\sum_{\alpha,a}v_{\rm eff}(\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{a})h_{m}^{(\alpha)}(\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{a}),

(S-197)

where
h_{m}^{(\alpha)}
is the one-particle eigenvalue of
\hat{Q}_{m}
and
G^{-1}
is the inverse of the Gaudin matrix
G_{(\alpha,j),(\beta,k)}~~(\alpha,\beta\in\{\rm c,s\})
defined by

\displaystyle G_{({\rm c},j),({\rm c},k)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\pi\dfrac{\partial I^{\rm(c)}_{j}}{\partial\lambda^{\rm(c)}_{k}}~~~~~(j,k\in\{1,2,...,L\}),

(S-198)

\displaystyle G_{({\rm c},j),({\rm s},k)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\pi\dfrac{\partial I^{\rm(c)}_{j}}{\partial\lambda^{\rm(s)}_{k}}~~~~~(j\in\{1,2,...,L\},~k\in\{1,2,...,N_{p}\}),

(S-199)

\displaystyle G_{({\rm s},j),({\rm c},k)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\pi\dfrac{\partial I^{\rm(s)}_{j}}{\partial\lambda^{\rm(c)}_{k}}~~~~~(j\in\{1,2,...,N_{p}\},~k\in\{1,2,...,L\}),

(S-200)

\displaystyle G_{({\rm s},j),({\rm s},k)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\pi\dfrac{\partial I^{\rm(s)}_{j}}{\partial\lambda^{\rm(s)}_{k}}~~~~~(j,k\in\{1,2,...,N_{p}\}).

(S-201)

Here, we assume that the order of the labels for the Gaudin matrix is defined as
({\rm c},1)<({\rm c},2)<....<({\rm c},L)<({\rm s},1)<({\rm s},2)<...<({\rm s},N_{p}).

The vector
\bm{e}^{\prime}
and the effective velocity
\bm{v}_{\rm eff}(\lambda_{a}^{\rm(\alpha)})
are defined by

\displaystyle\bm{e}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\underbrace{2\sin(\lambda^{\rm(c)}_{1}),...,2\sin(\lambda^{\rm(c)}_{N_{p}})}_{N_{p}},\underbrace{0,...,0}_{M})^{\rm T},

(S-202)

\displaystyle{v}_{\rm eff}(\lambda_{a}^{\rm(\alpha)})

\displaystyle\coloneqq L\sum_{b,\beta}e^{\prime}(\lambda_{b}^{\rm(\beta)})G^{-1}_{({\rm\beta},b),({\rm\alpha},a)}.

(S-203)

The detailed expression of the Gaudin matrix is given by

\displaystyle G=\begin{pmatrix}G_{1}&G_{3}\\
O&G_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(S-204)

where
O
is the
M\times N_{p}
the matrices whose entries are zero and
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
, and
G_{3}
are defined by

\displaystyle G_{1}\coloneqq{\rm diag}(\underbrace{L,L,...,L}_{N_{p}})\eqqcolon LE_{N},

(S-205)

\displaystyle G_{2}\coloneqq{\rm diag}(\underbrace{N_{p},N_{p},...,N_{p}}_{M})\eqqcolon N_{p}E_{M},

(S-206)

\displaystyle G_{3}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1&\cdots&-1\\
-1&-1&\cdots&-1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-1&-1&\cdots&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(S-207)

Here
G_{3}
is
N_{p}\times M
matrix, and
E_{N_{p}}
and
E_{M}
are the unit
N_{p}\times N_{p}
and
M\times M
matrices, respectively.
Thus, the inverse of
G
is given by

\displaystyle G^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}G_{1}^{-1}&-G_{1}^{-1}G_{3}G_{2}^{-1}\\
O&G_{2}^{-1}\\
\end{pmatrix}

(S-208)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}L^{-1}E_{N_{p}}&-L^{-1}{N_{p}}^{-1}G_{3}\\
O&{N_{p}}^{-1}E_{M}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(S-209)

where
G_{1}^{-1}
and
G_{2}^{-1}
are the inverses of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
.
As a result, we obtain

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{\rm eff}(\bm{\lambda})=\left(\underbrace{2\sin(\lambda^{\rm(c)}_{1}),...,2\sin(\lambda^{\rm(c)}_{N_{p}})}_{N_{p}},\underbrace{\dfrac{2}{N_{p}}\sum_{a}\sin(\lambda^{\rm(c)}_{a}),...,\dfrac{2}{N_{p}}\sum_{a}\sin(\lambda^{\rm(c)}_{a})}_{M}\right)^{T}.

(S-210)

Finally, we take the thermodynamic limit in Eq. (
S-210
), obtaining

\displaystyle v_{\rm eff}^{\rm c}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=2\sin(\lambda),

(S-211)

\displaystyle v_{\rm eff}^{\rm s}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\rm th}\dfrac{\displaystyle\dfrac{2}{L}\sum_{a}\sin(\lambda_{a})}{\displaystyle\dfrac{N_{p}}{L}}

(S-212)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\lambda~2\sin(\lambda)\rho^{\rm(c)}(\lambda)}{\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\lambda~\rho^{\rm(c)}(\lambda)}.

(S-213)

These expressions are identical to the effective velocities of Eqs. (
E-8
) and (
E-9
) in End Matter.

VIII.2
Detailed explanation for the initial correlations of Eqs. (
E-16
), (
E-17
), and (
E-18

We consider the initial correlations for the charge and spin of the
t0
model.
The initial state addressed here is the grand canonical density matrix of Eq. (
8
) of the main text, which is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}(0)=\frac{1}{Z}e^{-\beta(\hat{H}-\mu\hat{N})},

(S-214)

where
\beta
and

\hat{N}=\hat{P}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\left(\hat{n}_{\uparrow,j}+\hat{n}_{\downarrow,j}\right)\hat{P}
and

To simplify notions in the following calculations, we introduce

VIII.2.1
Initial correlation of Eq. (
E-16
)

The Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
does not have any terms with
\hat{s}^{z}_{j}
, and thus the spin state is described by the infinite temperature state.
As a result, we derive

\displaystyle\langle\hat{s}^{z}_{j}\rangle_{\rm ini}

\displaystyle=0,

(S-215)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{s}^{z}_{j}\hat{s}^{z}_{k}\rangle_{\rm ini}

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{\rm ini}\delta_{j,k},

(S-216)

where we use
\hat{s}^{z}_{j}=\hat{n}_{\uparrow,j}-\hat{n}_{\downarrow,j}
and the constraint
\hat{n}_{\uparrow,j}\hat{n}_{\downarrow,j}=0
for no double occupancy.
This readily leads to

\displaystyle\langle\hat{s}^{z}_{j}\hat{s}^{z}_{k}\rangle_{\rm ini}-\langle\hat{s}^{z}_{j}\rangle_{\rm ini}\langle\hat{s}^{z}_{k}\rangle_{\rm ini}=\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{\rm ini}\delta_{j,k}.

(S-217)

Then, we expect

\displaystyle\langle\delta\tilde{S}^{z}(x,0)\delta\tilde{S}^{z}(y,0)\rangle=C_{1}\delta(x-y)

(S-218)

with a constant
C_{1}
.

We next determine
C_{1}
using a microscopic calculation. The hydrodynamic variable
S^{z}(x,0)
is the averaged spin density at a position
x
and thus the operator counterpart is

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{z}\coloneqq\dfrac{1}{2N}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\hat{s}^{z}_{j}.

(S-219)

We can compute the spin fluctuation, getting

\displaystyle\langle(\hat{S}^{z})^{2}\rangle_{\rm ini}-\langle\hat{S}^{z}\rangle_{\rm ini}\langle\hat{S}^{z}\rangle_{\rm ini}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4N^{2}}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{\rm ini}

(S-220)

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{1}{2N}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\lambda\rho^{\rm(c)}(\lambda)~~(N\gg 1).

(S-221)

Here we note that
2N
in Eq. (
S-221
) is the system size and can be regarded as the typical spatial scale of hydrodynamics.
Thus,
2N
corresponds to
\sqrt{\tau}
in Eq. (
E-13
) and then we get

\displaystyle C_{1}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\lambda\rho^{\rm(c)}(\lambda).

(S-222)

This completes the explanation of Eq. (
E-16
).

VIII.2.2
Initial correlation of Eq. (
E-17
)

We can show that the correlation function
\langle\hat{n}_{j}\hat{n}_{k}\rangle_{\rm ini}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{\rm ini}\langle\hat{n}_{k}\rangle_{\rm ini}
at nonzero temperature decays exponentially as a function of
|j-k|
. Furthermore, the rapidity
\lambda
in the thermodynamic Bethe ansatz can be interpreted as the wavenumber of the free fermions.
Thus, in the description of hydrodynamics, we expect

\displaystyle\langle\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,0)\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\nu,y,0)\rangle=F_{\rm charge}(\lambda,\nu)\delta(x-y).

(S-223)

with a function
F_{\rm charge}(\lambda,\nu)
.

We next determine the form of
F(\lambda,\mu)
in Eq. (
S-223
) using the microscopic calculation of the
t0
model.
As commented above, the rapidity
\lambda
corresponds to the wavenumber of the free fermions, and thus the microscopic object of
{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda)
is
\hat{N}(\lambda)
defined by

\displaystyle\hat{N}(\lambda_{\alpha})\coloneqq\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\lambda_{\alpha})\hat{A}(\lambda_{\alpha}),

(S-224)

where the operator
\hat{A}(\lambda_{\alpha})
is defined by

\displaystyle\hat{A}(\lambda_{\alpha})\coloneqq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2N}}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\hat{a}_{j}e^{-{\rm i}\lambda_{\alpha}j}

(S-225)

with
\lambda_{\alpha}=\pi\alpha/N~(\alpha\in\Lambda)
. Using this notation, we can show

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2N}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{a}_{j}=\dfrac{1}{2N}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\lambda_{\alpha})\hat{A}(\lambda_{\alpha})\xrightarrow[N\rightarrow\infty]{}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\dfrac{d\lambda}{2\pi}\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\hat{A}(\lambda),

(S-226)

which explains the reason why
\hat{N}(\lambda_{\alpha})
corresponds to
{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda)
.
Under this setup, we can diagonalize the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm ff}
under the periodic boundary condition

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm ff}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}(-2\cos(\lambda_{\alpha}))\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\lambda_{\alpha})\hat{A}(\lambda_{\alpha}).

(S-227)

Then, by definition, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\hat{N}(\lambda_{\alpha})\rangle_{\rm ini}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left[\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\lambda_{\alpha})\hat{A}(\lambda_{\alpha})\exp\left({\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}(2\beta\cos(\lambda_{\alpha})+\beta\mu+\log 2)\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\lambda_{\alpha})\hat{A}(\lambda_{\alpha})}\right)\right]}{\displaystyle 2\pi{\rm Tr}\left[\exp\left({\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}(2\beta\cos(\lambda_{\alpha})+\beta\mu+\log 2)\hat{A}^{\dagger}(\lambda_{\alpha})\hat{A}(\lambda_{\alpha})}\right)\right]}

(S-228)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\dfrac{2}{\displaystyle e^{-\beta(2\cos(\lambda_{\alpha})+\mu)}+2}

(S-229)

\displaystyle=\rho^{\rm(c)}(\lambda_{\alpha}).

(S-230)

We follow a similar calculation with the Wick theorem, obtaining

\displaystyle\langle\hat{N}(\lambda_{\alpha})\hat{N}(\nu_{\beta})\rangle_{\rm ini}-\langle\hat{N}(\lambda_{\alpha})\rangle_{\rm ini}\langle\hat{N}(\nu_{\beta})\rangle_{\rm ini}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\delta_{\lambda_{\alpha},\nu_{\beta}}}{2\pi}\dfrac{1}{2\pi}n^{\rm(c)}(\lambda_{\alpha})(1-n^{\rm(c)}(\nu_{\beta}))

(S-231)

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{1}{2N}\delta(\lambda-\nu)\dfrac{1}{2\pi}n^{\rm(c)}(\lambda)(1-n^{\rm(c)}(\nu))~~(N\gg 1).

(S-232)

As explained in Eq. (
S-221
), the system size
2N
corresponds to the spatial scale
\sqrt{\tau}
of the hydrodynamics and thus we can get

\displaystyle F_{\rm charge}(\lambda,\nu)=\delta(\lambda-\nu)C_{2}(\lambda)

(S-233)

with

\displaystyle C_{2}(\lambda)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}n^{\rm(c)}(\lambda)(1-n^{\rm(c)}(\lambda)).

(S-234)

This completes the explanation of Eq. (
E-17
).

VIII.2.3
Initial correlation of Eq. (
E-18
)

The Hamiltonian of the
t0
model and the particle number operator
\hat{n}_{j}=\hat{n}_{\uparrow,j}+\hat{n}_{\downarrow,j}
are invariant under the transformation of
z\rightarrow-z
, while the spin operator
\hat{s}^{z}_{j}=\hat{n}_{\uparrow,j}-\hat{n}_{\downarrow,j}
is transformed into
-\hat{s}^{z}_{j}
.
Then, the correlation for the charge and spin becomes

\displaystyle\langle\hat{n}_{j}\hat{s}^{z}_{j}\rangle_{\rm ini}-\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{\rm ini}\langle\hat{s}^{z}_{j}\rangle_{\rm ini}=0.

(S-235)

Hence, we expect that the initial correlation in the hydrodynamics is given by Eq. (
E-18
) of the main text:

\displaystyle\langle\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,0)\delta\tilde{S}^{z}(y,0)\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

(S-236)

VIII.3
Detailed calculation for the functional integral of Eq. (
E-30
)

Our starting point is the functional integral of Eq. (
E-30
) in End Matter.
First, we rewrite the functional integral as follows:

\displaystyle\langle e^{zJ(\tau)}\rangle\sim\dfrac{1}{Z_{\rm charge}Z_{\rm spin}}\int\mathcal{D}\delta\tilde{S}^{z}(x,0)\mathcal{D}\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,0)\exp\bigg[

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\left(\dfrac{\delta\tilde{S}^{z}(x,0)^{2}}{C_{1}}+\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\lambda\dfrac{\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,0)^{2}}{C_{2}(\lambda)}\right)

\displaystyle+z\tau^{1/4}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\delta\tilde{S}^{z}(x,0)\chi[-|X_{1}|<x<0]\bigg].

(S-237)

Here, we use the relation
Z_{\rm hyd}=Z_{\rm charge}Z_{\rm spin}
with

\displaystyle Z_{\rm charge}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\mathcal{D}\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,0)\exp\bigg(-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\lambda\dfrac{\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,0)^{2}}{C_{2}(\lambda)}\bigg),

(S-238)

\displaystyle Z_{\rm spin}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\mathcal{D}\delta\tilde{S}^{z}(x,0)\exp\bigg(-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\dfrac{\delta\tilde{S}^{z}(x,0)^{2}}{C_{1}}\bigg).

(S-239)

Second, we implement the functional integral with respect to
\delta\tilde{S}^{z}
. Then, we introduce the new variable defined by
A(x)\coloneqq\delta\tilde{S}^{z}(x,0)-C_{1}z\tau^{1/4}\chi[-|X_{1}|<x<0]
with the indication function
\chi[\bullet]
.
As a result, we get

\displaystyle\langle e^{zJ(\tau)}\rangle\sim\dfrac{1}{Z_{\rm charge}}\int\mathcal{D}\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,0)\exp\bigg(

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\lambda\dfrac{\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,0)^{2}}{C_{2}(\lambda)}+\dfrac{C_{1}z^{2}\tau^{1/2}}{2}|X_{1}|\bigg).

(S-240)

Third, we put Eq. (
E-27
) with
t=1
into Eq. (
S-240
), calculating the functional integral with respect to
\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}
.
This substitution leads to

\displaystyle\langle e^{zJ(\tau)}\rangle\sim\dfrac{1}{Z_{\rm charge}}\int\mathcal{D}\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,0)

\displaystyle\exp\bigg(-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\lambda\dfrac{\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,0)^{2}}{C_{2}(\lambda)}\bigg)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\exp\bigg(\dfrac{z^{2}\tau^{1/4}}{2}\left|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\lambda\int_{0}^{2\sqrt{\tau}\sin\lambda}dx\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,-x,0)\right|\bigg).

(S-241)

To proceed with the functional integral of Eq. (
S-241

Y\coloneqq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\lambda\int_{0}^{2\sqrt{\tau}\sin\lambda}dx\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,-x,0)
and
B\coloneqq z^{2}\tau^{1/4}/2

\displaystyle\langle e^{zJ(\tau)}\rangle\sim\left\langle e^{B|Y|}\right\rangle_{\rm charge},

(S-242)

where we define

\displaystyle\left\langle\bullet\right\rangle_{\rm charge}\coloneqq\dfrac{1}{Z_{\rm charge}}\int\mathcal{D}\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,0)\exp\bigg(-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\lambda\dfrac{\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\rm(c)}(\lambda,x,0)^{2}}{C_{2}(\lambda)}\bigg)\bullet.

(S-243)

To evaluate Eq. (
S-242

\left\langle e^{BY}\right\rangle_{\rm charge}
.
Following almost the same calculation as the derivation of Eq. (
S-240
) and taking into account the sign of
\sin\lambda
for the upper limit of the integral, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle e^{BY}\right\rangle_{\rm charge}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\dfrac{B^{2}\sqrt{\tau}}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\lambda|\sin\lambda|n^{\rm(c)}(\lambda)(1-n^{\rm(c)}(\lambda))\right)=\exp\left(\dfrac{B^{2}}{2}(\tau^{1/4}d(\beta,\mu))^{2}\right),

(S-244)

where we use

\displaystyle d(\beta,\mu)=\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\pi\beta}\left(\dfrac{1}{2+e^{-\beta(2+\mu)}}-\dfrac{1}{2+e^{\beta(2-\mu)}}\right)}.

(S-245)

This result means that the probability of
Y
obeys the Gaussian distribution with the zero average and the variance
\tau^{1/2}d(\beta,\mu)^{2}
because the cumulant generating function
\log\left\langle e^{BY}\right\rangle_{\rm charge}
is the quadratic form of
B

\displaystyle\langle e^{zJ(\tau)}\rangle

\displaystyle\sim\left\langle e^{B|Y|}\right\rangle_{\rm charge}

(S-246)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau^{1/2}d(\beta,\mu)^{2}}}\exp\left(-\dfrac{y^{2}}{2\tau^{1/2}d(\beta,\mu)^{2}}+B|y|\right)

(S-247)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\dfrac{d(\beta,\mu)^{2}z^{4}\tau}{8}\right)\left[1+{\rm erf}\left(\dfrac{d(\beta,\mu)z^{2}\sqrt{\tau}}{2^{3/2}}\right)\right]

(S-248)

with the error function
{\rm erf}(\bullet)

E-31

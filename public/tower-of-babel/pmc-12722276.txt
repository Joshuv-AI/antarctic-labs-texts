Spatially resolved whole-transcriptomic profiling of EGFR-mutated lung adenocarcinomas stratified by PD-L1 expression
Authors: Chenxi Yu, Chenxi Shi, Hao Zhou, Na Li, Ruyi Ma, SongBai Liu, Zaixiang Tang, Xianling Zhang, Zhe Lei, Hualong Qin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeHigh programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression is associated with unfavorable clinical outcomes in epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-mutated lung adenocarcinomas (LUAD) patients treated with tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) or anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy, yet the underlying mechanisms are less explored.MethodsBulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) datasets were analyzed to investigate intertumoral transcriptional variations linked to PD-L1 expression. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) was utilized to quantify PD-L1 expression on tumor cells. Digital spatial profiling (DSP) was performed on 23 EGFR-mutated LUAD tissue samples to characterize transcriptomic differences in tumor cell (TC), immune cell (IM), and macrophage (MA) compartments between PD-L1 high and low groups. Furthermore, a publicly available DSP dataset was analyzed and IHC was conducted for validation.ResultsAnalysis of RNA-seq datasets identified differentially expressed genes, signaling pathways, and immune profiles associated with PD-L1 expression. Compared to low PD-L1 tumors, high PD-L1 tumors exhibited increased infiltration of T regulatory cells (Tregs) and enhanced immunosuppressive signatures. DSP analysis revealed compartment-specific molecular disparities: TC segment in high PD-L1 tumors showed upregulated signatures of cell proliferation, invasion, and metastasis. IM segment displayed increased infiltration of immunosuppressive cells, including Tregs and myeloid-derived suppressor cells and upregulated expression of inhibitory immunomodulators CD276, HAVCR2, and LGALS9C.ConclusionCombining bulk and spatial RNA-seq, this study characterized the molecular and immunological hallmarks of EGFR-mutated LUAD in the context of PD-L1 expression, providing new insights into the development of tailored therapeutic strategies for EGFR-mutated LUAD with high PD-L1 expression.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13402-025-01143-9.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with its global incidence projected to sustain a persistent upward trend over the next few decades [1–3]. Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), the most prevalent histologic subtype of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), is characterized by a high frequency of oncogenic driver alterations [1]. In the Asian population, over 50% of LUAD cases harbor epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations [4], which have conferred significant treatment advantages in the era of targeted therapy [5]. Among these, exon 19 deletion (p. E746-A750del) and exon 21-point mutation (p.L858R) are the most common. While most EGFR-mutated LUAD patients exhibit a positive response to EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR-TKIs), approximately 5–10% of those with sensitizing EGFR mutations demonstrate poor responsiveness to EGFR-TKIs [6–8], a phenomenon termed primary resistance. Previous studies have established that high programmed cell death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression (tumor proportional score ≥ 50%) is associated with primary resistance to EGFR-TKIs in treatment-naïve EGFR-mutated LUAD patients [9]. Additionally, several studies have confirmed that high PD-L1 expression correlates with unfavorable clinical outcomes in EGFR-mutated LUAD patients treated with TKIs [10–15]. Furthermore, current evidence indicates that anti-PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapy provides limited benefit to NSCLC patients with EGFR mutations, especially those with high PD-L1 expression [16, 17]. In this context, elucidating the underlying mechanisms is critical for optimizing personalized management strategies for EGFR-mutated LUAD patients with high PD-L1 expression, ultimately aiming to improve long-term survival and clinical outcomes.

Additionally, intratumoral heterogeneity of PD-L1 expression is a well-recognized hallmark of NSCLC [18, 19]. Given the heterogeneous distribution of the tumor microenvironment (TME) within individual tumors, the impact of PD-L1 expression on tumor cells (TCs) may vary across different regions. Therefore, investigation of TME has emerged as a powerful approach to decipher key cellular interactions underlying the clinical outcomes of TKIs in EGFR-mutated LUAD. The TME, a multifaceted ecosystem comprising tumor cells, immune cells, and stromal components, profoundly influences therapeutic response and clinical outcome [20]. Using multiplex immunohistochemistry, Yang et al. demonstrated a potential correlation between PD-L1 expression, immune cell composition, and EGFR-TKI response [12]. Notwithstanding these insights, the role of TME in shaping therapeutic sensitivity and prognosis in EGFR-mutated LUAD, particularly within the PD-L1 and spatial context, remains less understood.

Direct analysis of tumor specimens enables more precise characterization of TME and enhances the accuracy of therapeutic efficacy assessment and prognostic prediction [21]. Consequently, there is a critical need for advanced multiplexed and quantitative platforms, particularly those compatible with formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissues-a standard clinical specimen type. One such technique is NanoString digital spatial profiling (DSP), a cutting-edge technology that facilitates comprehensive mapping of tumor-immune landscapes. This platform allows high-plex quantification of mRNA and proteins for specific regions of interest (ROIs) or areas of illumination (AOIs) on a single FFPE tissue section, with a unique capacity to characterize spatial heterogeneity and dissect TME cellular interactions [22].

In this study, we employed a robust quantitative spatial molecular profiling approach to investigate the transcriptomic changes in the tumor cell, immune cell, and macrophage compartments according to PD-L1 expression levels using DSP in a cohort of EGFR-mutated LUAD patients. Comprehensive analysis of bulk RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics data provides critical molecular and cellular insights into the prognosis and therapeutic landscape of EGFR-mutated LUAD.

Materials and methods

Study population for digital spatial profiling (DSP) and immunohistochemistry (IHC) analysis

Out of the eligible 33 patients, 23 were randomly selected for Digital spatial profiling (DSP). We conducted DSP analysis using formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks from epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-mutated (L858R or exon 19 deletion [19Del]) lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) specimens. IHC analysis was performed on the consecutive sections of the same 10 lesions used for DSP (Validation Cohort 1) as well as on 10 additional (Validation Cohort 2) EGFR-mutated LUAD samples. These specimens were obtained at the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University and included immunohistochemistry (IHC) data for PD-L1 expression. Patients with other oncogenic driver alterations (e.g., ROS proto-oncogene 1 [ROS1] or anaplastic lymphoma kinase [ALK] fusions) or a history of prior anticancer therapy were excluded from the study.

Immunohistochemistry

IHC analysis of PD-L1 tumor proportion score (TPS) on pre-treatment samples was performed using the Dako Agilent PD-L1 IHC 22C3 pharmDx kit. Scoring was independently conducted by two experienced pathologists with expertise in thoracic oncology, who were blinded to clinical outcomes and study objects. PD-L1 expression was categorized as follows: high expression (TPS ≥ 50%) and low expression (TPS 1–49%), in accordance with established clinical criteria [23, 24]. Additionally, consecutive 5-µm-thick FFPE tissue sections were subjected to IHC staining following previously described protocols [22, 25]. The primary antibodies used were as follows: anti-FoxP3 (sc-53876, 1:500 dilution, Santa Cruz, USA), anti-CD276 (ab227670, 1:100 dilution, Abcam, USA), and anti-HAVCR2 (ab241332, 1:1000 dilution, Abcam, USA). After staining, slides were mounted using Micromount (Cat#3801731; Leica, Germany) and independently evaluated by two pathologists. For quantification, FoxP3-, CD276-, and HAVCR2-positive stromal cells were counted in ten randomly selected high-power fields (HPFs) across different tissue regions, and results were presented as the average number of positive cells per HPF as previously reported [25].

Digital spatial profiling of the whole transcriptome atlas

Tissues were fixed as FFPE blocks, and a tissue microarray (TMA) was constructed from FFPE blocks of 23 EGFR-mutated LUAD specimens included in the DSP analysis. For each patient, 2–3 cores (3 mm diameter) were arrayed to ensure representative sampling. The 5-µm-thick FFPE TMA slides underwent a standardized preparation protocol: initial baking at 60 °C for 1.5 h, followed by deparaffinization and rehydration through three 5-minute washes in CitriSolv, two 5-minute washes in 100% ethanol, a 5-minute wash in 95% ethanol, and a final 5-minute wash in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS); Subsequent brief immersion in diethylpyrocarbonate-treated water at 99 °C for 10 s, transfer to 1× tris-EDTA solution at 99 °C for 20 min of antigen retrieval, and a 5-minite 1×PBS wash. incubation with a 1 µg/mL proteinase K at 37 °C for 15 min to expose RNA, followed by another 5-minute 1×PBS wash; post-exposure fixation in 10% neutral buffered formalin (NBF) for 5 min, two 5-minute immersions in NBF stop buffer, and a 5-minute PBS wash. For RNA detection, UV-photocleavable oligo probes (whole transcriptome atlas, WTA) were used: hybridization solution was applied for in situ hybridization with overnight incubation at 37 °C, followed by immersion in 2× saline-sodium citrate (SSC) buffer, two 25-min washes in stringent wash solution (2×SSC) at 37 °C, two 2-min 2×SSC washes, and treatment with Buffer W to remove off-target probes (followed by buffer removal). The slides were labeled at 25 °C for 1 h with pan-cytokeratin (PanCK) (AE1 + AE3, Novus Biologicals, Cat# NBP2-33200 AF488) for tumor cells, CD45 (D9M8I from CST, Cat# 13917, internally conjugated) for immune cells, CD68 (KP1, SANTA, Cat# sc-20060 AF647) for macrophages, and SYTO13 (Cat# 121303303) for nuclei followed by two 5-minute 2× SSC washes. Slides were loaded onto the GeoMx instrument for region of interest (ROI) selection based on immunofluorescence images; with results validated by pathologists from the Department of Pathology, First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University; auto-segmentation used custom UV illumination masks to define areas of interest (AOIs) for photocleavable tag release in panCK + tumor cell, CD45 + immune cell, and CD68 + macrophage compartments, with cleaved barcodes collected from each AOI and quantified via sequencing.

Data preprocessing and bioinformatics analysis

The GeoMx NGS Pipeline (v2.2.0.2) was used to convert sequenced FASTQ files into DCC files. Data underwent quality control (QC) for technical signal, technical background, probe performance, and normalization. For technical signal QC, AOIs with reads aligning to the template sequence at < 80% were excluded. Technical background QC included three metrics: no template control (NTC) count, negative probe counts, and AOI parameters. NTC counts were used to identify template contamination, with AOIs exhibiting NTC values > 1000 removed. Negative probe counts defined the overall technical signal threshold (set at < 4 counts). AOI parameters were assessed by nucleus count and surface area; AOIs met QC criteria if they contained ≥ 100 nuclei or, had a surface area > 5000 µm2. AOI sizes were standardized via cell number and area normalization to ensure inter-sample consistency. High-quality data were normalized with Quantile 3 (Q3) normalization [26] and subjected to downstream analysis. Dimension reduction employed uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP; v0.2.8.0) and t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (tSNE). Differential gene expression analysis was performed using the edgeR package (v3.34.0), with significance thresholds set at false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and |log2 (fold change [FC])| > 1. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was conducted using the R package “clusterProfiler”, with FDR-corrected p < 0.05 defined as significant enrichment. The signature score was calculated using the formula: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\sqrt[\mathrm{n}]{{(\mathrm{X}}_{1}+1)\dots\:{(\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{i}}+1)...({\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{n}}+1)}$$\end{document}, where Xi represents the normalized expression value of the gene corresponding to the cells, and n is the number of genes in the signature [27]. Finally, intergroup differences in various signatures (i.e., cell function, signaling pathways, and metabolism), were assessed using the ingle sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm [28] applied to the gene expression matrix. Immune cell abundance in the TME was determined with ssGSEA and ImmuneCell, respectively.

Public RNA-seq and DSP cohorts

We utilized publicly available data of two cohorts of EGFR-mutated (L858R or 19Del; excluding ROS1 and ALK fusions) LUAD patients. In the RNA-seq cohort, RNA-seq data and clinical characteristics of 37 patients were sourced from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). To validate our DSP findings, areas of interest (AOIs) from 4 EGFR-mutated (L858R or 19Del) LUAD patients with available PD-L1 expression data [19] were analyzed. The clinicopathologic features of both cohorts are summarized in Table S1.

Public RNA-seq data analysis

The read count matrix was used to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) using the limma package (v3.54.2) in R. DEGs were defined as those with p-value < 0.05, and |log2 (fold change)| > 1. Volcano plots and heatmaps were generated using the ggpubr and Complexheatmap R packages, respectively. Enrichment analysis of Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways was conducted using the KOBAS-i web tool, with a significance threshold of an adjusted p-value corrected using the Benjamini & Hochberg method, set at < 0.05. ssGSEA was performed by “GSVA” package (v1.16.0) in R to explore the enrichment level of immune cells in tumor samples. The ImmuneCell algorithm was employed to assess the enrichment levels of 28 major immune cell types in tumor samples. In addition, ImmuneCellAI online tool (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/ImmuCellAI/#!/analysis) was utilized to infer the infiltration score of 22 immune cell types.

Gene set enrichment analysis

The log2-transformed normalized gene expression matrix was subjected to ssGSEA using Wikipathways gene sets (downloaded from the WikiPathways database, https://data.wikipathways.org/current/gmt/) [29]. Non-redundant biological pathways suitable for ssGSEA was extracted from the WikiPathways database [30]. Intergroup differences in immune function and immune cell activity were assessed via ssGSEA, with results visualized using heatmaps and boxplots.

Statistical analysis

The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to determine differences between groups. Heatmaps were constructed with the R package “Complex Heatmap” (v2.8.0) [31]. A p-value of less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Results

Characterization of differential expression genes and pathways in EGFR-mutated LUAD based on PD-L1 expression

To reveal the molecular differences across distinct PD-L1 expression groups, publicly available RNA sequencing datasets (for downloaded clinical information, see Table S1) were used. We initially analyzed the transcriptomic data from lung samples of 37 patients with EGFR-mutated LUAD (carrying L858R or 19Del) (RNA-seq cohort). These 37 patients were stratified into a high PD-L1 expression and a low PD-L1 expression group (Table S2). To further explore, we additionally divided the cohort into three subgroups, low (n = 9), intermediate (n = 19), and high (n = 9) PD-L1 expression groups (Table S2).

A total of 151 DEGs were identified between the high and low PD-L1 expression groups, including 108 upregulated and 43 downregulated genes in the PD-L1 high group (Fig. 1A; Table S3). KEGG pathway analysis revealed that the upregulated DEGs in the PD-L1 high group were significantly enriched in cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, transcriptional mis-regulation in cancer, cytosolic DNA-sensing, pathways in cancer, JAK-STAT signaling pathway, and PD-L1 expression and PD-1 checkpoint pathway in cancer (adjusted p-value ≤ 0.05) (Fig. 1B).

To comprehensively evaluate pathway differences across PD-L1 expression statuses, pathway enrichment analysis was performed using a curated dataset from the WikiPathways database [29]. Six distinct pathway clusters (C1-C6) were identified between PD-L1 high and low groups (Fig. 1C). C2-clustered pathways associated with mitochondrial function were preferentially active in the PD-L1 low-expression group (Fig. 1C). Three clusters (C4, C5, and C6) predominantly grouped pathways activated in the PD-L1 high-expression group (Fig. 1C). Specifically, pathways in clusters C4 and C5, including neovascularization, the Netrin-UNC5B signaling pathway, the p53 transcriptional gene network, and the PDGFR β pathway, were significantly enriched in the PD-L1 high-expression group (all p < 0.05; Fig. 1C and S1). Similarly, pathways clustered in C6, such as MUC1-altered glycosylation, the canonical NF-κB pathway, and signal transduction via IL1R, were also significantly enriched in this group (all p < 0.05; Fig. 1C and S1). All these pathways are closely associated with enhanced tumor malignancy.

Immune profile differences in EGFR-mutated LUAD based on PD-L1 expression

The clinical efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) in EGFR-mutated NSCLC has been explored [32–35]. However, the landscape of immunomodulators and the immune cell repertoires in EGFR-mutated LUAD, particularly in relation to PD-L1 expression, remains poorly characterized. We first analyzed the expression of 33 immunomodulatory genes associated with cancer immunity. Heatmap analysis revealed distinct expression patterns of these immunomodulators between PD-L1 high and low groups (Figs. 2A and S2A). Notably, apart from CD274, PD-L1 high expression samples exhibited significantly higher levels of three immunomodulatory genes, ICOS, PDCD1LG2, and SLAMF7 compared to low expression samples (all p < 0.01; Fig. 2B and C, S2B, and C). These heterogeneous expression profiles of immunomodulatory genes may provide cues for potential therapeutic targets to enhance immunotherapy responses or inhibit tumor progression and metastasis in EGFR-mutated LUAD in the context of PD-L1 expression.

Intrigued by the immune-related gene expression differences observed across PD-L1 status, we further evaluated alterations in the TME. Using the ImmuneCell and ImmuneCellAI algorithms, we estimated the relative proportions of specific immune cell subsets according to PD-L1 status (Figs. 2D-F and S3A-C). The ImmuneCell analysis confirmed that regulatory T cells (Tregs) were significantly more abundant in the PD-L1 high group than in the low group (p < 0.05; Fig. 2E and F, S3B, and C), whereas the low group exhibited higher proportions of B cells and monocytes (p < 0.05; Fig. 2E and F, S3B, and C). As previously established, CD4 + Tregs play a central role in immune response regulation, comprising two major subsets: thymus-derived naturally occurring Tregs (nTregs) and induced Tregs (iTregs) generated from CD4 + CD25- precursors in lymphoid tissues [36]. ImmuneCellAI analysis further revealed that the PD-L1 high group harbored significantly enriched populations of iTregs, nTregs, and Type 1 regulatory (Tr1) cells compared to the low group (all p < 0.05; Fig. 2E and F, S3B, and C). The ssGSEA technique was subsequently employed to evaluate the infiltration status of 16 immune cell types and calculate scores for 13 immunological functions to further dissect the association between immune infiltration and PD-L1 expression (Fig. 2G and H, and S4). The PD-L1 high expression group displayed increased infiltration of Tregs and elevated immunological function such as antigen-presenting cell (APC) co_inhibition and T cell co_inhibition (Fig. 2G and H, and S4).

Spatially resolved transcriptomic profiling of intratumoral area in EGFR-mutated LUAD based on PD-L1 expression using DSP

Bulk RNA-sequencing data lack the ability to reveal spatial relationships within tissues. To address this limitation, spatially resolved multiplexed DSP was employed to characterize the spatial heterogeneity of tumor cells and TME in EGFR-mutated LUAD, with a specific focus on their association with PD-L1 expression. Tumor samples were collected from 23 treatment-naïve patients with EGFR-mutated LUAD. The clinicopathologic characteristics of these enrolled patients are summarized in Table S4. Briefly, the cohort included 10 (43.5%) male and 13 (56.5%) female patients, with a median age of 66 years (range: 33–80 years). Among the patients, 13 harbored the EGFR L858R mutation and 10 carried the EGFR 19del mutation. The patients were distinguished into two groups based on tumor PD-L1 expression [high (n = 7) and low (n = 16)]. TMA comprising diverse intratumoral regions selected based on tumor PD-L1 expression levels was constructed, arrayed, and utilized for subsequent DSP analyses. TMA sections were simultaneously stained with fluorescently labeled antibodies against the tumor cell (TC) marker PanCK, immune cell (IM) marker CD45, macrophage (MA) marker CD68, and the nuclear stain DNA Syto13. A schematic representation of the DSP experimental design is depicted in Fig. 3A. For each tumor core, 2–3 ROIs were selected based on PD-L1 expression in tumor cells (Fig. 3B). Two groups were distinguished according to tumor PD-L1 expression in ROIs determined by IHC assay (Table S5). Each ROI was segmented into molecularly defined tissue compartments through fluorescent co-localization using a masking and segmentation strategy, enabling specific examination of transcriptional alterations within each compartment: TC (PanCK+/CD45-/SYTO13+), IM (PanCK-/CD68-/CD45+/SYTO13+), and MA (PanCK-/CD68+/SYTO13+) compartments (Fig. 3B). Following probe hybridization, ultraviolet-induced cleavage, and barcode collection, whole-transcriptome gene expression was quantitated by Illumina sequencing. After stringent QC (Table S6), the obtained data were normalized to the Q3 for downstream analysis (Figure S5A). The utility of the Q3 normalization approach resides in its capacity to reliably mitigate the impact of outliers on spatial transcriptomic data [37]. Dimension reduction analyses via tSNE and UMAP for data exploration revealed that TC AOIs were distinctly separated from IM and MA AOIs (Figure S5B and C).

Gene expression profiles and biological processes associated with tumor cell PD-L1 expression in each compartment

Characterizing the TME is critical for unraveling the tumor-immune interactions that underlie therapeutic responses and clinical outcomes. To this end, we identified DEGs and enriched signaling pathways in spatially distinct compartments between PD-L1 high and PD-L1 low groups. A total of 470 DEGs, including 374 upregulated and 96 downregulated genes in the TC compartment, were identified between the PD-L1 high and PD-L1 low groups (Fig. 4A, up panel; Table S7A). Within the IM segment, 166 DEGs were detected between PD-L1 high and PD-L1 low groups, with 144 highly expressed in PD-L1 high and 22 genes highly expressed in PD-L1 low (Fig. 4A, middle panel; Table S7B). In the MA compartment, 203 genes (175 upregulated, 28 downregulated) showed significant expression difference compared to the PD-L1 low group (Fig. 4A, down panel; Table S7C). GO biological process analysis revealed that the top enriched GO terms across all three segments were associated with extracellular matrix organization (Figure S6A and B), a biological process known to influence both classical and emerging hallmarks of cancer [38].

Spatial-level characterization of cancer-related cell function, signaling pathways, and metabolic signatures in EGFR-mutated LUAD, particularly in the context of PD-L1 expression, remains underexplored. Signature scores related to tumor cell proliferation, including E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, mitotic spindle, and MYC targets, were significantly higher in PD-L1 high tumor AOIs (all p < 0.05; Figs. 4B and S7A). The glycolysis signaling score linked to energy metabolism, was markedly higher in PD-L1 high tumor AOIs (p < 0.001; Figs. 4B and S7B), indicating enhanced tumor cell proliferation in the PD-L1 high group. Notably, signatures related to invasion and metastasis, including angiogenesis, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and KRAS and MTORC1 signaling pathways, were significantly upregulated in PD-L1 high tumor AOIs (all p < 0.05; Figs. 4B, S7C, and 7D).

To thoroughly evaluate transcriptional alterations occurring between PD-L1 high and PD-L1 low groups across compartments, we performed pathway enrichment analysis using the WikiPathways database [29]. Six distinct pathway clusters (C1-C6) with varying correlations to tumor cell PD-L1 expression were identified (Fig. 4C). Clusters C1 and C2 primarily comprised stromal-associated pathways, while clusters C5 and C6 included predominantly TC compartment pathways. In the TC compartment, C5 pathways associated with invasion and metastasis and C6 pathways linked to tumor cell proliferation were significantly enriched in the PD-L1 high group (all p < 0.05; Figs. 4C and S8). Additionally, the PD-L1 high group in IM and MA segments also showed enrichment in C5 cluster pathways. These findings highlight transcriptomic differences linked to PD-L1 expression, with compartment-specific enrichment of biological processes driving tumor progression.

Immune-related differences associated with tumor PD-L1 expression

Subsequently, we focused on characterizing TME differences between PD-L1 high and PD-L1 low groups. First, ssGSEA was employed to evaluate the infiltration status of 16 immune cell types and derive scores for 13 immunological functions to assess the association between immune infiltration and tumor cell PD-L1 expression in the IM segment (Figs. 5A and S9A). The PD-L1 low group exhibited a higher proportion of plasmacytoid dendritic cells and stronger immunological functions such as cytolytic activity, while the PD-L1 high expression group showed increased infiltration score of mast cells and Tregs (Figs. 5B and S9A). Next, immune cell deconvolution was performed to estimate the relative proportions of specific cell subsets in the IM compartment between the two groups (Figs. 5C and S9B). Neutrophil accounted for a higher proportion in the PD-L1 low group compared to the PD-L1 high group (Figs. 5D and S9B). Conversely, the scores of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and Tregs, two key immunosuppressive cell subsets in the TME, were higher in the IM segment of the PD-L1 high group than in those of the PD-L1 low group (Figs. 5D and S9B). These findings were validated using a public DSP dataset (Figs. 5E and S9C). Furthermore, IHC analysis further demonstrated significant increase in the expression level of FoxP3 (Treg marker) of PD-L1 high expression samples in both the Validation Cohort 1 and Validation Cohort 2 (Figure S10A; Table S8). Additionally, T helper 1 cells were found to be enriched in PD-L1 high AOIs (Figs. 5D and S9B). Collectively, these results indicate that the TME exhibits distinct profiles based on heterogeneous tumor PD-L1 expression, with the PD-L1 high TME displaying immunosuppression and exhaustion compared to the PD-L1 low TME.

Our subsequent analysis centered on evaluating the expression of immunomodulatory genes associated with cancer immunity. Notably, the inhibitory immune checkpoint genes CD276, HAVCR2, and LGALS9 were upregulated in the IM compartment of carcinoma regions in PD-L1 high group (Figs. 5F and S9D). IHC data further demonstrated these findings in both the Validation Cohort 1 and Validation Cohort 2 (Figures S10B; Table S8). These upregulated immune checkpoint genes represent potential therapeutic targets for EGFR-mutated LUAD with high PD-L1 expression.

Discussion

Recent evidence indicates that high PD-L1 expression may correlate with early resistance to TKIs, inferior response to anti-PD1/PD-L1 therapy and shorter survival in EGFR-mutated LUAD. This study aimed to characterize the molecular features of EGFR-mutated LUAD with divergent PD-L1 expression levels. Using bulk transcriptomic profiling, we first characterized intertumoral gene expression differences. We then employed a robust spatial molecular profiling approach to comprehensively evaluate intratumoral transcriptional alterations in TC, IM, and MA compartments, stratified by tumor PD-L1 expression in EGFR-mutated LUAD. This investigation leveraged the key attributes of DSP technology, which is particularly suited for analyzing spatial transcriptomic datasets with heterogeneous gene expression patterns [22, 25, 39].

Beyond re-capitulating established cancer hallmark pathways [31], GeoMx DSP offers a deeper understanding of the specific biological compartments where these alterations occur. Specifically, by directly implementing auto-segmentation, supports the analysis of compartment-specific pathways, eliminating the need for tissue manipulation or pathological delineation of tumor [40]. We delineated oncogenic, metabolic, and immune-related process differences between PD-L1 high and low groups across distinct compartments. EMT emerged as the top enriched pathway in PD-L1 high tumors. EMT is widely recognized as a critical initialing step in tumor invasion and metastasis [41], mediating tumor progression by remodeling the TME. Specifically, EMT induces an immunosuppressive TME that facilitates immune escape and metastatic dissemination [42, 43]. During EMT, tumor cells secret cytokines such as TGF-β and interleukin-10, which polarize T cells into Tregs [44, 45], thereby promoting tumor progression and metastasis [46, 47].

Stromal components within the TME are pivotal in tumor initiation, progression, and metastasis [48]. This study highlights extensive TME remodeling between PD-L1 high and low groups, characterized by increased proportions of immunosuppressive Tregs and MDSCS. These findings suggest the formation of a progressively immunosuppressive microenvironment that enables tumor cell adaptation. Tregs are classified into two major subsets: natural thymic-derived Tregs (nTreg) and peripherally induced Tregs (iTreg) [49]. The transcription factor FOXP3 is essential for Treg development; CD4 + CD25 + FOXP3 + Tregs, whether nTreg or iTreg-derived, suppress immune responses in the TME [50]. Multiple studies have demonstrated that tumor-infiltrating Tregs drive NSCLC progression and metastasis [51, 52]. Tumor-infiltrating Tregs suppress anti-tumor immune response through several pathways, including the secretion of immunosuppressive cytokines, upregulation of inhibitory checkpoint molecules [53, 54], ultimately facilitating tumor progression and metastasis [55]. Tregs also promote cancer cell invasion by modulating EMT [56, 57] and enhance tumor angiogenesis in the TME through the secretion of VEGF-A and IL-10, further supporting metastatic spread [58]. MDSCs represent another key immunosuppressive immune cell subset in the TME. Through mediators such as arginase 1, inducible nitric oxide synthase, reactive oxygen species, and peroxynitrite, MDSCs suppress the activity of effector T cells (Teffs) and natural killer cells [59], promote the differentiation of Tregs [60], and induce immunosuppressive phenotypes in macrophages [61]. Additionally, tumor-infiltrating MDSCs overexpress inhibitory checkpoints like PD-L1 across multiple cancer types [62]. Consistent with these findings, ICB has been shown to deplete MDSCs and increase the Teff: MDSC ratio [63], implicating MDSCs in the development of ICB resistance.

Our segmentation approach enabled us to define ligand and corresponding receptor relations of immunomodulator genes. IM segment in high PD-L1 tumors showed upregulated expression of CD276, HAVCR2, and LGALS9, indicating the activation of multiple distinct immunosuppressive signaling pathways within the tumor stroma. Conventional cancer treatment strategies, particularly in EGFR-mutated LUAD, have often overlooked stromal component modulation. However, these genes have been proposed as emerging targets for immune checkpoint or immunomodulatory therapies [64, 65]. Therefore, these immunomodulatory genes might represent interesting therapeutic candidates for EGFR-mutated LUAD with high PD-L1 expression.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to characterize the molecular features of EGFR-mutated LUAD with divergent PD-L1 expression levels using DSP.

The ability of DSP to isolate transcriptomic data from defined tissue areas without destroying or cutting the tissue is extremely valuable, thus providing a foundation for additional investigations. While this study yielded significant novel findings with clinical relevance, we acknowledge several potential limitations. First, the relatively small sample size limits the generalizability of our findings. Larger-scale studies are therefore needed to enhance statistical robustness, validate the observed results, and extend their applicability to broader populations or contexts. Additionally, although DSP enables highly multiplexed profiling, precisely defining cell boundaries and capturing transcripts at the single-cell level remains technically challenging. As such, future studies leveraging cutting-edge technologies like CosMx spatial molecular imaging [66] and 10× Xenium [67] to resolve RNA and protein spatial distributions at subcellular resolution are warranted.

Conclusions

Through spatial transcriptomic analysis, we deciphered distinct biological processes and cellular landscapes associated with varying tumor PD-L1 expression in EGFR-mutated LUAD. PD-L1 high regions exhibited more pronounced immunosuppressive and exhausted phenotypes compared to PD-L1 low regions. These findings may explain the importance of integrating tumor PD-L1 expression into therapy decisions for EGFR-mutated LUAD. Collectively, our findings represent a critical advance in understanding how PD-L1 expression sculpts the TME and hold significant promise for optimizing clinical management of EGFR-mutated LUAD patients.

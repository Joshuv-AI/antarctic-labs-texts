Extendability of group actions on K3 or Enriques surfaces
Tianchen Zhao

Abstract
. Let X X be a K3 or Enriques surface with good reduction. Let G G be a finite group acting (not necessarily linearly) on X X . We give a criterion for this group action to extend to a smooth model of X X in terms of the action of G G on the second ℓ \ell -adic cohomology groups. In particular, we generalize the result on the extendability of Galois actions on K3 surfaces by Chiarellotto, Lazda, and Liedtke. As an application, we prove that a symplectic linear group action is extendable if the residue characteristic does not divide its order. Lastly, we relate the good reduction of Enriques surfaces with that of their K3 double covers.



1.
Introduction

Throughout the whole article, we fix the following notations.

\begin{array}[]{ll}K&\mbox{a complete and discretely valued field}\\
\cal{O}_{K}&\mbox{the ring of integers of $K$}\\
k&\mbox{the residue field of $K$, which we assume to be perfect of characteristic $p$}\\
K^{\mathrm{s}}&\mbox{a fixed separable closure of $K$}\\
\overline{k}&\mbox{the residue field of $K^{\mathrm{s}}$, an algebraic closure of $k$}\\
G_{E/F}&\mbox{the Galois group of $E/F$, a Galois extension}\\
I_{E/F}&\mbox{the inertia group of $E/F$, a Galois extension of complete and}\\
&\mbox{discretely valued fields}\\
G_{F}&\mbox{an absolute Galois group of a field $F$}\\
I_{F}&\mbox{an absolute inertia group of a complete and discretely valued field $F$}\end{array}

Definitions 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper variety over
K
.

•

A model of
X
is an proper flat algebraic space over
\cal{O}_{K}
whose generic fibre is isomorphic to
X
.

•

We say
X
has good reduction if
X
has a smooth model. Also, we say
X
has potential good reduction if
X_{L}
has good reduction for some finite extension
L/K
.

In this article, we study when a group action on a smooth proper variety extends to a smooth model. In particular, the group actions are not assumed to be linear over the ground field unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper variety over
K
with good reduction. Let
G
be a group acting on
X
. We say the
G
-action on
X
is extendable if the
G
-action extends to some smooth model
\cal{X}
of
X
.

Note that we only require the action to extend to some smooth model rather than a particular smooth model.

Example 1.3
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
K
with good reduction. Its smooth model coincides with the Néron model by
[
2
, Corollary 1.4]
. Then every group action on
X
is extendable by the Néron mapping property.

1.1.
Good reduction and the extendability problem

The extendability of group actions plays an important role in the good reduction problem.

In
[
7
]
, Liedtke and Matsumoto prove that if a K3 surface
X
over
K
has good reduction after a finite and separable extension
L/K
and
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\text{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
is unramified, then
X
has good reduction after a finite and unramified Galois extension. The idea of the proof is as follows. By passing to the Galois closure, we assume
L/K
is Galois. We find a smooth model
\cal{X}
of
X_{L}
, such that the action of
I_{L/K}
on
X_{L}
extends to
\cal{X}
. Then
\cal{X}/I_{L/K}
gives a smooth model of
X
over the maximal unramified sub-extension of
L/K
.

Another example is in
[
4
]
. Let
X
be a K3 surface over
K
with good reduction after a finite and unramified extension
L/K
. Again, we may assume
L/K
is Galois. Chiarellotto, Lazda, and Liedtke derive a criterion for the extendability of the action of
G_{L/K}
on
X_{L}
. If the action does extend to
\cal{X}
, then
\cal{X}/G_{L/K}
gives a smooth model of
X
.

In addition to the extendability of Galois actions, the extendability of linear group actions also has applications to the good reduction problem. See Corollary
1.12
for an example.

1.2.
Main results

We focus on the case where
X
is a K3 or Enriques surface. In particular, for any smooth model
\cal{X}
of
X
, its special fibre
\cal{X}_{k}
is determined up to isomorphism by
X
by
[
7
, Proposition 4.7]
. We call it the canonical reduction of
X
, denoted by
X^{\circ}
. This is also a K3 or Enriques surface depending on
X
. Moreover, there is a canonical homomorphism

\mathrm{sp}:\Aut(X)\to\Aut(X^{\circ})\ \ \ g\mapsto g_{k},

called the specialization homomorphism. Any group action on
X
induces a group action on
X^{\circ}
via specialization. See Section
2.1
for more details.

Let
G
be a finite group acting on
X
on the right. This induces a
G
-action on
K
on the left whose fixed field is
K_{0}=K^{G}
(we call this the base field of the
G
-action as in Definition
3.11
). We restrict to the case where
K/K_{0}
is finite and unramified.

Remark 1.4
.

We will see why we want
G
to act on the right in Lemma
3.16
. This assumption is merely a formality. Indeed, suppose
G
acts on
X
on the left via
\rho:G\to\Aut(X)
. We can convert this to a right action by taking the anti-homomorphism
\rho^{\prime}:G\to\Aut(X)
,
g\mapsto\rho(g^{-1})
.

Let
\ell\neq p
be a prime number. We want to give a criterion for the extendability of group actions in terms of the
\ell
-adic cohomology of
X
and
X^{\circ}
. Since
X
has good reduction,
I_{K}
acts trivially on
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))
. Then
G_{k}\cong G_{K}/I_{K}
acts on
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))
. Denote by
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))^{G_{k}}
and
H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))^{G_{k}}
respectively the
G_{k}
-invariant subspaces of
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))
and
H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))
. As in Section
4.1
, we can define a
G
-action on
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))^{G_{k}}
and similarly on
H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))^{G_{k}}
via specialization.

By adapting the method in
[
4
]
, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be a K3 or Enriques surface over
K
with good reduction. Let
G
be a finite group acting on
X
with base field
K_{0}
. Assume
K/K_{0}
is finite and unramified. Then the
G
-action on
X
is extendable if and only if there is a
G
-isomorphism

H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))^{G_{k}}\cong H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))^{G_{k}}.

A further criterion is given in Theorem
4.7
. In Section
4.1
, we define a group
\overline{G}_{k}
that acts on
X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}}
and
X_{\overline{k}}
, which combines the action of
G
on
X
and the Galois action on
K^{\mathrm{s}}
and
\overline{k}
. Then we show that the condition in Theorem
1.5
is further equivalent to the existence of a
\overline{G}_{k}
-isomorphism
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))\cong H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))
.

Theorem
1.5
recovers the criterion for the extendability of Galois action in
[
4
]
. Let
L/K
be a finite and unramified extension. Let
k_{L}
be the residue field of
L
. Let
X
be a K3 surface over
K
such that
X_{L}
has good reduction. By passing to the Galois closure, we may assume
L/K
is Galois.

Corollary 1.6
.

[
4
, Theorem 1.6]

The following are equivalent.

(1)

X
has good reduction.

(2)

The action of
G_{L/K}
on
X_{L}
is extendable.

(3)

There is a
G_{K}
-isomorphism
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\text{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\cong H^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{k}}^{\circ},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
.

(4)

There is a
G_{L/K}
-isomorphism
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\text{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))^{G_{k_{L}}}\cong H^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{k}}^{\circ},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))^{G_{k_{L}}}
.

Proof.

Suppose the
G_{L/K}
-action on
X_{L}
extends to a smooth model
\cal{X}
. Then
\cal{X}/G_{L/K}
is a smooth model of
X
by
[
4
, Proposition 7.1]
. This proves
(2)\implies(1)
.

Next, suppose
X
has a smooth model
\cal{X}
. Then the comparison isomorphism
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\cong H^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{k}}^{\circ},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
is

-equivariant. This proves
(1)\implies(3)
.

For
(3)\implies(4)
, we simply restrict the
G_{K}
-isomorphism to the
G_{k_{L}}
-invariant subspace. Finally,
(4)\implies(2)
follows by Theorem

.
∎

In case of a
K
-linear action,
G
acts on

and on
H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
via specialization. We have the following criterion for extendability.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a K3 or Enriques surface over
K
with good reduction. Let
G
be a finite group acting
K
-linearly on
X
. Then the
G
-action on
X
is extendable if and only if there is a
G
-isomorphism

H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\cong H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}).

In particular, this implies that the extendability of
K
-linear group actions is a geometric property rather than an arithmetic property.

1.3.
Applications of the main results

We present some applications of our main results. We will prove them in Section
5
. The first application is a criterion for the extendability of a symplectic group action in terms of the order of the group and the residue characteristic.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
X
be a K3 surface over a field
F
. An
F
-linear automorphism of
X
is symplectic if the induced automorphism of
H^{0}(X,\omega_{X})
is the identity. An
F
-linear group action of
G
on
X
is symplectic if for every
g\in G
, the automorphism
g:X\to X
is symplectic.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
G
be a finite group acting linearly and symplectically on
X
. If
p
does not divide the order of
G
, then the
G
-action is extendable.

Remark 1.10
.

Matsumoto proves this in
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
with a weaker notion of extendability. In
[
9
]
, an automorphism
g
of a K3 surface
X
with good reduction is defined to be extendable if there exists a finite extension
L/K
such that
g
extends to a smooth model of
X_{L}
. This corresponds to potential extendability in our terminology

Remark 1.11
.

Our proof of Corollary
1.9
is different from that of
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
. Despite this, we need
[
9
, Lemma 2.13]
and
[
9
, Proposition 6.1]
. Neither of them depend on the main result of
[
9
]
. Indeed,
[
9
, Lemma 2.13]
is a general fact about K3 surfaces and
[
9
, Proposition 6.1]
is based on a mild generalization of the flops considered in
[
7
, Section 4]
.

Assume
\chara K=0
. This corollary can be applied to show that singular K3 surfaces have potential good reduction modulo
p\neq 2
with a scheme model. In
[
8
, Theorem 0.1]
, Matsumoto shows that if
p\neq 2,3
, every K3 surface with a Shioda-Inose structure of product type has good reduction after a field extension of ramification index
1,2,3,4,
or
6
. In particular, this holds for singular K3 surfaces.

This corollary generalizes the argument to
p=3
. The argument in
[
8
, Theorem 0.1]
fails for

: in step (4), Matsumoto shows that a symplectic involution on a K3 surface

extends to a smooth model

using a relative elliptic fibration, that is, a fibration

whose generic and special fibres are both elliptic fibrations. If

If we apply
[

, Theorem 1.1]
, we see that the symplectic involution is potentially extendable, so we see that

has good reduction after a field extension of ramification index

or

.

We can also relate the good reduction of Enriques surfaces with that of their K3 double covers. Recall that for every Enriques surface
Y
over a field

of characteristic different from

with a fixed-point-free involution

is a K3 double cover of

and
\iota

Corollary 1.12
.

Assume

. Let

be an Enriques surface over

. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

Y
has good reduction.

(2)

There exists a K3 double cover

of

(3)

There exists a K3 double cover

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review the preliminaries of this article, including the specialization homomorphism, RDP models, Weyl groups, and group cohomology with coefficients in a non-abelian group.

2.1.
Specialization homomorphisms

Let
X
be a K3 or Enriques surface over
K
with good reduction. Let
X^{\circ}
be the canonical reduction of
X
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
g:X\to X
be an automorphism. For every smooth model
\cal{X}
of
X
,
g
extends uniquely to a birational self-map
\cal{X}\dashrightarrow\cal{X}
defined away from finitely many curves on the special fibre.

Proof.

The proof is the same as
[
9
, Proposition 2.2(1)]
.
∎

By this lemma, every automorphism
g
on
X
extends to
U\subset\cal{X}
, the complement of finitely many curves on the special fibre. Then it restricts to a birational self-map of
X^{\circ}
. Since

is a K3 or Enriques surface, the self-map extends uniquely to a automorphism of
X^{\circ}
. This defines a group homomorphism
\mathrm{sp}_{\cal{X}}:\Aut(X)\to\Aut(X^{\circ})
called the specialization homomorphism.

Lemma 2.2
.

The specialization map
\mathrm{sp}_{\cal{X}}:\Aut(X)\to\Aut(X^{\circ})
is independent of the choice of
\cal{X}
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as
[
9
, Proposition 2.2(2)]
.
∎

Given this lemma, we may write

\mathrm{sp}:\Aut(X)\to\Aut(X^{\circ})\ \ \ g\mapsto g_{k}.

Then any group acting on
X
also acts on
X^{\circ}
via specialization. In particular, let
L/K
be a finite Galois extension. Let
k_{L}
be the residue field of
K
. The
G_{L/K}
-action on
X_{L}
specializes to a
G_{L/K}
-action on
X^{\circ}_{k_{L}}
. This factors through the
G_{k_{L}/k}
-action on
X^{\circ}_{k_{L}}
via the surjection

.

2.2.
RDP models and Weyl groups

Suppose
X
is a K3 or Enriques surface over
K
with good reduction. Although the canonical reduction of
X
is unique up to isomorphism, there is in general no canonical choice of smooth models of
X
. However, once a polarization
\cal{L}
of
X
is fixed, there is a canonical of model of
X
, called the RDP model
P(X,\cal{L})
. It may admit mild singularities on the special fibre but we can still recover the canonical reduction from its special fibre.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
X
be a K3 or Enriques surface with good reduction. Let
\cal{L}
be a polarization of
X
. Then there exists a projective model
P(X,\cal{L})
of
X
such that

(1)

P(X,\cal{L})
only depends on the pair
(X,\cal{L})
up to unique isomorphism.

(2)

The special fibre
P(X,\cal{L})_{k}
has at worst rational double points, and its minimal desingularization coincides with
X^{\circ}
.

Proof.

(2) is by
[
7
, Proposition 4.6]
and (1) follows from the proof of
[
7
, Proposition 4.7]
.
∎

Let
X
be a K3 or Enriques surface over
K
with good reduction. Fix a polarization
\cal{L}
of
X
. Then
P(X,\cal{L})_{k}
is a surface over
k
with at worst rational double points and
X^{\circ}\to P(X,\cal{L})_{k}
is its minimal desingularization. We denote the exceptional locus as
E_{X,\cal{L}}\subset X^{\circ}
.

Let
\Lambda_{X,\cal{L}}
be the subgroup of
\Pic(X^{\circ})
generated by the classes of all the integral components of

. For every such component
E_{i}
, denote
n_{i}=h^{0}(E_{i},\cal{O}_{E_{i}})
. Let
s_{E_{i}}
be the automorphism of either
\Lambda_{X,\cal{L}}
or

defined by

s_{E_{i}}:D\mapsto D+\frac{1}{n_{i}}(D.E_{i})E_{i}

Let

. The Weyl group
\cal{W}_{X,\cal{L}}

In

2.3.
Group cohomology with coefficients in a non-abelian group

We recall the construction of group cohomology with coefficients in a non-abelian group in
[
12
, Section 5.1, Chapter I]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Suppose
G
is a group acting on a possibly non-abelian group
A
on the left.

(1)

An
A
-valued 1-cocycle for
G
is a function
\phi:G\to A
such that for every
g,h\in G
,

\phi(gh)=\phi(g)g(\phi(h)).

(2)

Given two 1-cocycles
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
, we say
\phi\sim\phi^{\prime}
if there exists
a\in A
such that for all
g\in G
,

a\phi(g)=\phi^{\prime}(g)g(a).

(3)

Define
H^{1}(G,A)=\{\text{$A$-valued $1$-cocycles for $G$}\}/\sim
. This is a pointed set, with base point represented by the trivial cocycle
G\to A
, which maps every
g\in G
to the identity in
A
.

Next, suppose
M
is a left
G
-module, so
G
acts on the possibly non-abelian group
\Aut(M)
on the left by conjugation. Denote the action of
G
on
M
by
\rho:G\to\Aut(M)
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\phi:G\to\Aut(M)
be an
\Aut(M)
-valued 1-cocycle of
G
. Define
M^{\phi}
, the twist of
M
by
\phi
, as a
G
-module:

(1)

The underlying group is
M
.

(2)

The action
\rho^{\phi}:G\to\Aut(M^{\phi})
is defined for every
g\in G
,
m\in M
,

\rho^{\phi}(g)(m)=\phi(g)(\rho(g)(m)).

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\phi:G\to\Aut(M)
be an
\Aut(M)
-valued 1-cocycle of
G
. Then the cohomology class represented by
\phi
is trivial if and only if there exists a
G
-isomorphism

M\cong M^{\phi}.

Proof.

By definition, the cohomology class represented by
\phi
is trivial if and only if there exists
f\in\Aut(M)
such that for every
g\in G
,

\phi(g)=f^{-1}\cdot g(f)=f^{-1}\cdot\rho(g)\cdot f\cdot\rho(g)^{-1}.

Next, a
G
-isomorphism
h:M\to M^{\phi}
is a group automorphism of
M
such that for every
m\in M
,

\displaystyle\rho^{\phi}(g)(h(m))=h(\rho(g)(m))

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(\phi(g)\cdot\rho(g)\cdot h)(m)=(h\cdot\rho(g))(m)

Therefore,
\phi(g)=h\cdot\rho(g)\cdot h^{-1}\cdot\rho(g)^{-1}
. Then the two conditions are equivalent upon taking
f=h^{-1}
.
∎

To prove Theorem

3.1.
Extendability of birational maps

The constructions in this sub-section are based on
[
4
, Section 7.1]
. Let
X
be a K3 or Enriques surface over
K
with good reduction. Fix a smooth model
\cal{X}
with special fibre
X^{\circ}
.

Given a birational map
f:\cal{X}\dashrightarrow\cal{X}^{+}
between two smooth models of
X
, we construct an endomorphism of
\Pic(X^{\circ})_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Denote by
\Gamma_{f}\subset\cal{X}\times\cal{X}^{+}
the graph of
f
and by
\Gamma_{f,k}\subset X^{\circ}\times\cal{X}^{+}_{k}
its special fibre. Let
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
be the projections from
X^{\circ}\times\cal{X}_{k}^{+}
to
X^{\circ}
and
\cal{X}_{k}^{+}
respectively. Then we define a homomorphism

\Pic(\cal{X}^{+}_{k})\to\Pic(X^{\circ})\ \ \ D\mapsto p_{1*}(\Gamma_{f,k}\cap p_{2}^{*}D),

which extends by linearity to
\tilde{s}_{f}:\Pic(\cal{X}^{+}_{k})_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\Pic(X^{\circ})_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Also, by the uniqueness of the canonical reduction, we have an isomorphism
f_{k}:X^{\circ}\xrightarrow{\sim}\cal{X}^{+}_{k}
. We precompose
\tilde{s}_{f}
with the pullback via
f_{k}^{-1}
and get an endomorphism
s_{f}
of
\Pic(X^{\circ})_{\mathbb{Q}}
,

s_{f}:\Pic(X^{\circ})_{\mathbb{Q}}\xrightarrow{(f_{k}^{*})^{-1}}\Pic(\cal{X}^{+}_{k})_{\mathbb{Q}}\xrightarrow{\tilde{s}_{f}}\Pic(X^{\circ})_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Let
\ell\neq p
be a prime number. The cycle class map defines an injection
[-]:\Pic(X^{\circ})_{\mathbb{Q}}\to H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))
. We extend
s_{f}
to

{q_{\ell}}_{*}:H^{1}(G,\cal{W}_{X,\cal{L}})\to H^{1}(G,\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}(H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1)))).

Let
\gamma_{\cal{L},\ell}={q_{\ell}}_{*}(\alpha_{\cal{L},\ell})
.

Lemma 4.8
.

The pointed map
{q_{\ell}}_{*}
has trivial kernel.

Proof.

Denote the action of
\cal{W}_{X,\cal{L}}
on
\Lambda_{X,\cal{L},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
by
j^{\prime}_{\ell}:\cal{W}_{X,\cal{L}}\to\Aut(\Lambda_{X,\cal{L},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}})
. The kernel of
{j^{\prime}_{\ell}}_{*}
is trivial by
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
. It suffices to prove that
{q_{\ell}}_{*}
and
{j^{\prime}_{\ell}}_{*}
have the same kernel.

The cycle class map gives a
G
-injection

\Lambda_{X,\cal{L},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\to H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))
. Denote by
S_{\ell}
the quotient and consider the short exact sequence of
G
-representations

0\to\Lambda_{X,\cal{L},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\to H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))\to S_{\ell}\to 0.

Suppose
\alpha\in H^{1}(G,\cal{W}_{X,\cal{L}})
. By Proposition
2.8
,

{j^{\prime}_{\ell}}_{*}(\alpha)\text{ is trivial}\iff\Lambda_{X,\cal{L},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\cong\Lambda_{X,\cal{L},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}^{\alpha},

{q_{\ell}}_{*}(\alpha)\text{ is trivial}\iff H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))^{G_{k}}\cong H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))^{G_{k},\alpha}.

Next,
\cal{W}_{X,\cal{L}}
acts trivially on
S_{\ell}
by construction, so
S_{\ell}\cong S_{\ell}^{\alpha}
. Then we are done by the semi-simplicity of the representations of the finite group
G
.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

There is a
G
-isomorphism

H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))\cong H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))^{\gamma_{\cal{L},\ell}}.

Proof.

Since
X
has good reduction over
K
, we have an isomorphism

\phi:H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))\to H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1)).

We claim that this gives a
G
-isomorphism if we twist
H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))^{G_{k}}
by
\gamma_{\cal{L},\ell}
. This amounts to showing that

\displaystyle\phi\circ\gamma_{\cal{L},\ell}(g)\circ g_{k}^{*}=g^{*}\circ\phi.

This is much simpler than the proof of Proposition
4.5
since
g=g^{\#}
. By Proposition
4.3
and Lemma
3.2
,

\gamma_{\cal{L},\ell}(g)=(i_{\ell}\circ\alpha{L})(g)=i_{\ell}(s_{g_{\cal{X}}})=s_{g_{\cal{X}},\ell}=\phi^{-1}\circ g^{*}\circ\phi\circ((g_{k})^{*})^{-1}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
\cal{L}
be a polarization on
X
fixed by
G
. We have

\displaystyle H^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{k}}^{\circ},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\cong H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\text{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\text{ as $G$-representations}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle H^{2}_{\et}(X_{\overline{k}}^{\circ},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))\cong H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\text{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))\text{ as $G$-representations}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\gamma_{\cal{L},\ell}\text{ is trivial}\iff\alpha_{\cal{L},\ell}\text{ is trivial}\iff\text{the $G$-action is extendable}

by Proposition
4.9
, Lemma
4.8
, and Theorem
3.19
.
∎

5.
Applications

In this section, we prove Corollary
1.9
and Corollary
1.12
.

5.1.
Extendability of symplectic group actions

We prove Corollary
1.9
in this sub-section. Suppose
X
is a K3 surface over
K
with good reduction and
G
is a finite group acting
K
-linearly on
X
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
p\nmid|G|
, for every
g\in G
,
g
and
g_{k}
have the same order.

Proof.

Suppose
g
has order
n
and
g_{k}
has order
m
. The order of
g_{k}
divides the order of
g
so
n=mk
for some integer
k
. By
[
9
, Proposition 6.1]
,
k
is a power of
p
. Since
p\nmid|G|
, we have
k=1
so
m=n
as we want.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

If
g
is symplectic on
X
, so is
g_{k}
on
X^{\circ}
.

Proof.

By
[
7
, Proposition 4.7]
, there exists a smooth model
\cal{X}
such that
g
is defined over an open dense subset of
U\subset\cal{X}
whose complement consists of finitely many curves on the special fibres. Denote the intersection of
U
with
X^{\circ}
as
V
, an open dense subset of
X^{\circ}
. Denote the relative canonical divisor of
U
and
V
respectively as
\omega_{U}
and
\omega_{V}
.

We make the following claims.

(1)

The restriction map
H^{0}(X^{\circ},\omega_{X^{\circ}})\to H^{0}(V,\omega_{V})
is injective.

(2)

The restriction map
H^{0}(U,\omega_{U})\to H^{0}(X,\omega_{X})
is injective.

(3)

The image of the restriction map
H^{0}(U,\omega_{U})\to H^{0}(V,\omega_{V})
equals the image of
H^{0}(X^{\circ},\omega_{X^{\circ}})\to H^{0}(V,\omega_{V})
.

Indeed, since
X^{\circ}
is integral and
\omega_{X^{\circ}}
is trivial, (1) holds. Also,
U
is isomorphic to
\cal{X}
away from codimension
2
, so
H^{0}(U,\omega_{U})=H^{0}(\cal{X},\omega_{\cal{X}})=\cal{O}_{K}
, which is torsion-free. Since the restriction map in (2) is the base change to from
\cal{O}_{K}
to
K
, it is injective. Finally, we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{H^{0}(\cal{X},\omega_{\cal{X}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(X^{\circ},\omega_{X^{\circ}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(U,\omega_{U})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(V,\omega_{V})}

The vertical map on the left is an isomorphism. The horizontal map on the top corresponds to the quotient homomorphism
\cal{O}_{K}\to k
, therefore surjective. Then (3) holds by diagram chasing.

Suppose
g
acts trivially on
H^{0}(X,\omega_{X})
. By (2),
g
also acts trivially on
H^{0}(U,\omega_{U})
and thus on the image of
H^{0}(U,\omega_{U})\to H^{0}(V,\omega_{V})
. Then by (3),
g_{k}
acts trivially on the image of
H^{0}(X^{\circ},\omega_{X^{\circ}})\to H^{0}(V,\omega_{V})
. Finally, by (1),
g_{k}
acts trivially on
H^{0}(X^{\circ},\omega_{X^{\circ}})
. Therefore,
g_{k}
is symplectic on
X^{\circ}
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.9
.

By Theorem
1.7
, we need to prove that
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
and
H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
are isomorphic as
G
-representations. Since
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
has characteristic
0
, it suffices to compare their characters. Equivalently, we prove that for all
g\in G
,
g
and
g_{k}
have the same characteristic polynomial on
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
and
H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
respectively.

First,
g
and therefore
g_{k}
are symplectic by Lemma
5.2
. Then by
[
9
, Lemma 2.13]
, the characteristic polynomials of
g
and
g_{k}
only depend on their orders. Finally, by Lemma
5.1
,
g
and
g_{k}
have the same order. This finishes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Good reduction of Enriques surfaces

We prove Corollary
1.12
in this sub-section. First, we recall some facts about Enriques surfaces.

Suppose the ground field
F
has characteristic different from
2
. Then every K3 surface over
F
quotient by an involution without fixed points is an Enriques surface by
[
3
, Proposition 10.15]
.

Conversely, every Enriques surface over
F
is the quotient of a K3 double cover by a fixed-point-free involution. The construction is as follows. Suppose
Y
is an Enriques surface. We choose an isomorphism
\omega_{Y}^{\otimes 2}\cong\cal{O}_{Y}
and form the
\cal{O}_{Y}
-algebra
\cal{O}_{Y}\oplus\omega_{Y}
. We take the K3 double cover to be its relative spectrum

X=\underline{\Spec}_{Y}(\cal{O}_{Y}\oplus\omega_{Y})

and the associated involution
\iota
is the unique involution that acts as the identity on
\cal{O}_{Y}
and the
(-1)
-map on
\omega_{Y}
. This is a K3 surface by the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 10.14]
.

In fact, this construction can be done in families.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume
p\neq 2
. Let
Y
be an Enriques surface over
K
with good reduction. Let
\cal{Y}
be a smooth model of
Y
over
\cal{O}_{K}
. Then there exists a smooth proper algebraic space
\cal{X}
over
\cal{O}_{K}
with an involution
\iota
such that
\cal{X}_{K}
and
\cal{X}_{k}
are respectively K3 double covers of
Y
and
\cal{Y}_{k}
with associated involutions
\iota_{K}
and
\iota_{k}
.

Proof.

We mimic the construction of a K3 double cover of an Enriques surface. First, since
Y
is an Enriques surface,
\omega_{Y}
is nontrivial but
\omega_{Y}^{\otimes 2}\cong\cal{O}_{Y}
. The pullback induces an isomorphism
\Pic(\cal{Y})\to\Pic(Y)
that maps
\omega_{\cal{Y}}
to
\omega_{Y}
. Therefore,
\omega_{\cal{Y}}
is nontrivial but
\omega_{\cal{Y}}^{\otimes 2}\cong\cal{O}_{\cal{Y}}
. We choose such an isomorphism and construct the
\cal{O}_{\cal{Y}}
-algebra
\cal{O}_{\cal{Y}}\oplus\omega_{\cal{Y}}
. Let

\cal{X}=\underline{\Spec}_{\cal{Y}}(\cal{O}_{\cal{Y}}\oplus\omega_{\cal{Y}})

and
\iota
be the unique involution that acts as the identity on
\cal{O}_{\cal{Y}}
and the
(-1)
-map on
\omega_{\cal{Y}}
.

Since taking the relative spectrum commutes with base change,
\cal{X}_{K}
and
\cal{X}_{k}
are respectively the K3 double covers of
Y
and
\cal{Y}_{k}
determined by the base change of the isomorphism
\omega_{\cal{Y}}^{\otimes 2}\cong\cal{O}_{\cal{Y}}
to
K
and
k
. Also,
Y=\cal{X}_{K}/\iota_{K}
and
\cal{Y}_{k}=\cal{X}_{k}/\iota_{k}
. By construction,
\cal{X}
is proper and flat. It remains to show that
\cal{X}
is smooth. Indeed, since
\cal{X}_{K}
and
\cal{X}_{k}
are smooth,
\cal{X}
is smooth by flatness.
∎

Now we prove Corollary
1.12
.

Proof of Corollary
1.12
.

Suppose
Y
has good reduction with a smooth model
\cal{Y}
. Apply Lemma
5.3
to
Y
and
\cal{Y}
. Let
\cal{X}
and
\iota
be as in Lemma
5.3
. Then
\cal{X}_{K}
is a K3 double cover of
Y
with associated involution
\iota_{K}
. Its specialization
\iota_{k}
has no fixed points since
\cal{Y}_{k}=\cal{X}_{k}/\iota_{k}
is an Enriques surface. This proves
(1)\implies(2)
.

Also, suppose
X
is a K3 double cover of
Y
, with associated involution
\iota
, such that
X
has good reduction and
\iota_{k}
has no fixed points. Then
X^{\circ}/\iota_{k}
is an Enriques surface. By
[
3
, Section 10.10]
,

\dim H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})^{\iota_{k}=\mathrm{id}}=\dim H^{2}_{\et}((X^{\circ}/\iota_{k})_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})=10.

This proves that
(2)\implies(3)
.

It remains to prove that
(3)\implies(1)
. Suppose
X
is a K3 double cover of
Y
, with associated involution
\iota
, such that
X
has good reduction and
\dim H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})^{\iota_{k}}=10.
We claim that
\iota
is extendable. Indeed, let
G=\langle\iota\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
. By Theorem
1.7
, it suffices to prove that there is a
G
-isomorphism

H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\cong H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}).

Every finite dimensional representation of
G
decomposes as the direct sum of the subspace fixed by
\iota
and the subspace where
\iota
acts as the
(-1)
-map. Since
H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
and
H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
both have dimension 22, it suffices to show that

(5.4)

\dim H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})^{\iota=\mathrm{id}}=\dim H^{2}_{\et}(X^{\circ}_{\overline{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})^{\iota=\mathrm{id}}.

Since
Y
is an Enriques surface, by
[
3
, Section 10.10]
, the LHS of
5.4
is

\dim H^{2}_{\et}(X_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})^{\iota=\mathrm{id}}=\dim H^{2}_{\et}(Y_{K^{\mathrm{s}}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})=10.

On the other hand, the RHS of
5.4
is
10
by assumption. This shows that
\iota
is extendable.

Let
\cal{X}
be a smooth model of
X
to which
\iota
extends. We want to show that
\cal{Y}=\cal{X}/\iota
is a smooth model of
Y
. By construction,
\cal{Y}
is a model of
Y
. We need to prove that
\cal{Y}
is smooth. By flatness, it suffices to prove that
\cal{Y}_{k}
is smooth.

Since
p\neq 2
, we have
\cal{Y}_{k}\cong X^{\circ}/{\iota_{k}}
. We claim that
\iota_{k}
is non-symplectic. Suppose
\iota_{k}
is symplectic. By
[
5
, Proposition 3.11, Chapter 15]
,
\cal{Y}_{k}
is a surface with at worst RDPs whose minimal desingularization is a K3 surface. In particular, the Euler characteristic of
\cal{Y}_{k}
is
2
. However, the Euler characteristic of
\cal{Y}_{k}
should agree with that of
Y
, which is
1
. This is a contradiction, so
\iota_{k}
is non-symplectic.

Then by the classification in
[
11
, Remark 4.5.4]
,
\cal{Y}_{k}
is either a smooth rational surface or an Enriques surface (in fact, it must be an Enriques surface but we don’t need this). In particular,
\cal{Y}_{k}
is smooth as we want. This shows that
(5)\implies(1)
and finishes the proof.
∎

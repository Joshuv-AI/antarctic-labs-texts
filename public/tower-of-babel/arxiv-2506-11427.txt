In-Plane Ni–O–Ni Bond Angles as Structural Fingerprints of Superconductivity in Layered Nickelates: Effects of Pressure, Strain, Layering, and Correlations
Bipasa Samanta, Alexandru B. Georgescu

Abstract
We investigate the structural and electronic conditions conducive to superconductivity in layered nickelates using density functional theory with Hubbard corrections (DFT+ U U ). For both the bilayer and 1–3 polymorphs of La 3 ​ Ni 2 ​ O 7 \text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{7}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{7}}\text{}} , we find that the in-plane Ni–O–Ni bond angles under pressure strongly correlate with the experimentally observed superconducting transition temperature ( T c T_{c} ) dome, and may serve as a reasonable proxy. Under compressive strain, the bond angles straighten, peaking near 2% strain—consistent with experimental reports of superconductivity in strained bilayer thin films. However, the bond angles at this strain are more bent than those achieved under hydrostatic pressure, correlating with a lower T c T_{c} . We show that increasing the number of NiO 2 \text{NiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} layers, as in La 4 ​ Ni 3 ​ O 10 \text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{10}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{10}}\text{}} , or substituting heavier rare-earth elements (e.g., Pr) raises the pressure required to reach the structural configuration associated with superconductivity. Our results indicate that these systems require higher external pressure to achieve in-plane bond straightening. Varying the on-site Coulomb interaction U U reveals that stronger electronic correlations delay the structural transition and favor high-spin states. This suggests that moderate correlation strength may be optimal for superconductivity, with stronger correlation preventing the formation of favorable bond geometries. Electronic structure analysis shows that the Ni e g e_{g} orbitals dominate near the Fermi level and shift downward with pressure, enhancing Ni–O hybridization. These results highlight how pressure and strain tune structural features that may be essential for engineering high- T c T_{c} phases in nickelate superconductors.

To evaluate the role of electron correlations in
\text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{7}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{7}}\text{}}

\text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{7}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{7}}\text{}}

\sim

Antiferromagnetic Ordering Effect

The inclusion of on-site Coulomb interactions (

. The AFM configurations exhibit distinct stability regimes under pressure, with AFM-C consistently requiring the highest transition pressure. The inclusion of a finite

3
Conclusion

We have systematically investigated the pressure and strain induced structural and electronic evolution of layered nickelates using density functional theory with Hubbard
U
corrections (DFT+
U
). We found the Ni-O-Ni in plane bond angles versus pressure strongly correlate with the
T_{c}
, while versus strain we found that the bond angles are straightest at 2% compressive strain - the strain at which superconductivity was observed at ambient pressure. Analyzing lattice parameters, Ni–O bond lengths, and Ni–O–Ni bond angles over a range of pressures and
U
values, we capture the structural transition in
\text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{7}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{7}}\text{}}
from an orthorhombic to a high-symmetry tetragonal phase characterized by the straightening of the out-of-plane Ni–O–Ni bond angle to 180
∘
in agreement with experimental reports, coinciding with the suppression of NiO
6
octahedral tilting. Replacement of La with heavier Lanthanides (e.g. Pr) increases the transition pressure, and so does increasing the number of perovskite layers.

Our results reveal that the critical transition pressure increases with
U
, reflecting a competition between electron correlation and external pressure. This interplay also governs the stability of the spin states: at lower
U
(notably at
U
= 2 eV), a high-spin to low-spin transition emerges near the superconducting pressure regime, whereas higher
U
values stabilize the high-spin state across the full pressure range, shifting the superconducting window to higher pressures. This suggests that while correlations are known to be important to superconductivity in these materials, a just-right amount of correlation is key: correlations that are too strong will prevent the bond angles from straightening.

Electronic structure analysis shows that the Ni
e_{g}
orbitals dominate the density of states near the Fermi level. Under compression, these orbitals shift downward in energy, enhancing Ni–O hybridization and promoting electronic delocalization. Additionally, we find that the number of Ni–O–Ni interlayer linkages plays a key role in determining the critical pressure, emphasizing the importance of lattice connectivity in tuning superconducting behavior.

In summary, this work elucidates the competing roles of pressure and electronic correlations in governing the structural and electronic landscape of layered nickelates. These insights provide a foundation for guiding future experimental and theoretical strategies to engineer superconductivity through strain, pressure, and chemical doping in nickelate systems.

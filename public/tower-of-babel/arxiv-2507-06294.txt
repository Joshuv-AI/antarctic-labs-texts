Argyres-Douglas Theories, Macdonald Indices And Arc Space Of Zhu Algebra
George Andrews, Anindya Banerjee, Chinmaya Bhargava, Ranveer Kumar Singh, Runkai Tao

Abstract
In this paper, we relate the MacDonald index of a 4d 𝒩 = 2 {\cal N}=2 SCFT with the Hilbert series of the arc space of the Zhu algebra of the corresponding Schur VOA . Using this, we conjecture a simple formula for the MacDonald index of ( A 1 , D 2 ​ n + 1 ) (A_{1},D_{2n+1}) Argyres-Douglas theory. We perform checks of the formula against the known Schur limits and RG flows. To match the Schur limit, we prove new q q -series identities.

Introduction

We study some families of 4d
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal field theories known as Argyres-Douglas theories
Argyres:1995jj
;
Argyres:1995xn
;
Witten:1997sc
;
Cecotti:2010fi
;
Xie:2012hs
. These theories are characterized by the lack of a description in terms of any Lagrangian preserving the
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry, and fractional scaling dimensions of Coulomb branch generators (a subset of well-understood local operators that are expected to be universal in any interacting 4d
\mathcal{N}=2
theory). Despite the absence of a Lagrangian description, these theories are amenable to powerful methods of analysis that give us insight into the dynamics of the theory without recourse to any perturbative approximation. This is made possible by several special features of these theories. These include:

(1)

standard simplifying features of conformal symmetry like radial quantization, the state-operator correspondence and the finite radius of convergence of the operator product expansion
Rattazzi:2008pe

(2)

applicability of the representation theory of the superconformal algebra
Dolan:2002zh
;
Cordova:2016emh
in constraining and characterizing the local operator spectrum of the theory,

(3)

the presence of a continuous moduli space of vacua in the quantum theory that is not lifted by any radiative corrections
Argyres:1996eh
, and

(4)

the existence of subsectors of local operators (BPS operators) that are protected against (or transform in a tractable manner under) large classes of deformations of the theory. Such deformations include certain supersymmetry-preserving renormalization group flows (henceforth,
RG
flows) associated with spontaneous breaking of the global symmetry of these theories, and these in particular will be of interest to us
Maruyoshi:2016aim
;
Maruyoshi:2016tqk
.

In this context, we will make extensive use of a familiar counting function called the superconformal index
Kinney:2005ej
that counts (albeit with ambiguities) the
BPS
operators of a 4d
SCFT
and has good behavior under supersymmetry-preserving
RG
flows.

It was demonstrated in
Beem:2013sza
that a subset of the ring of
BPS
local operators (called the Schur ring) has close connections to a vertex operator algebra, the algebraic building block of a two-dimensional rational conformal field theory. Throughout this work, we will focus our analysis on this subring. The superconformal index on this subring also restricts to two special limits called the MacDonald index and the Schur index
Gadde:2011uv
;
Buican:2015tda
;
Cordova:2015nma
(see Appendix
B
for a brief review) that we will use to understand properties of this ring.

The goal of the present paper is twofold:

1.

One of the main technical obstacles in the evaluation of the Hilbert series of arc space of a variety is determining the dimension of varieties appearing in the arc space construction. We use the Gröbner basis algorithm

(95)

We choose the ordering of variables to be

With the degree lexicographic ordering of monomials, Mathematica calculations give is the leading terms of the Gröbner basis to be

(97)

where
I_{\ell,m}^{\left(A_{1},A_{2}\right)}

(98)

where for sets
A,B
we have introduced the notation
A\cdot B:=\{ab:a\in A,b\in B\}
,
F_{s}(\ell,m)
is the set of all monomials generated by
\left\{J_{i}:i\geq s\right\}
of weight
(\ell,m)
.
The set
F_{m}(\ell,m)
is thus the set of monomials of weight
(\ell,m)
. We now want to count the number of basis elements of
(A({\cal V}_{(A_{1},A_{2})})_{n}/I_{n})_{\ell,m}
obtained from Gröbner basis using the formula (
85
). The set
F(\ell,m)
of all monomials of weight
(\ell,m)
are given by

F(\ell,m):=\left\{J_{k_{1}}^{a_{1}}\cdots J_{k_{s}}^{a_{s}}:s\geq 0,k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq\dots\leq k_{s},\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}k_{i}=\ell,\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}=m\right\}\penalty\ .

(99)

From
F(\ell,m)
, we need to remove the monomials

\{J_{k}\}\cdot F_{k}(\ell-k,m-1)\penalty\ ,\quad k=1,\dots,m-1\penalty\ ,

(100)

because these monomials are part of the leading monomials of the Gröbner basis. This means that that

|(A({\cal V}_{(A_{1},A_{2})})_{n}/I_{n})_{\ell,m}|=|F_{m}(\ell,m)|\penalty\ .

(101)

(102)

We now claim that

\sum_{\ell\geq 0,m\geq 0}\left|F_{m}(\ell,m)\right|q^{\ell}t^{m}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{q^{m^{2}}}{(q)_{m}}t^{m}\penalty\ ,

(103)

where

(q)_{m}:=(q;q)_{m}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-q^{i})\penalty\ .

(104)

To prove this, let us first define

\mathcal{I}_{m}^{s}(q):=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\left|F_{s}(\ell,m)\right|q^{\ell}\penalty\ .

(105)

Then we claim that

\mathcal{I}_{m}^{s}(q)=\frac{q^{sm}}{(q)_{m}}\penalty\ .

(106)

The generating function for
|F(\ell,m)|
is given by

\sum_{\ell,m\geq 0}|F(\ell,m)|q^{\ell}t^{m}=\mathsf{Ser}_{t=0,q=0}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-q^{i}t\right)}\penalty\ .

(107)

It is also easy to see that

\mathsf{Ser}_{t=0,q=0}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=s}^{\infty}\left(1-q^{i}t\right)}=\sum_{\ell,m\geq 0}|F_{s}(\ell,m)|q^{\ell}t^{m}=\sum_{m\geq 0}{{\cal I}}_{m}^{s}(q)t^{m}\penalty\ .

(108)

Using the formula

\frac{1}{(z;q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{z^{m}}{(q;q)_{m}}\penalty\ ,

(109)

for

, we deduce that

{{\cal I}}^{1}_{m}(q)=\frac{q^{m}}{(q)_{m}}\penalty\ .

(110)

It is easy to see that

{{\cal I}}^{s}_{0}=1\penalty\ .

(111)

Next note that

\left(1-q^{s}\right)\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{I}_{m}^{s}(q)t^{m}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{I}_{m}^{s+1}(q)t^{m}\penalty\ .

(112)

This gives us the recursion relation

\mathcal{I}_{m}^{s+1}(q)=\mathcal{I}_{m}^{s}(q)-q^{s}\mathcal{I}_{m-1}^{s}(q)\penalty\ ,\quad m>0\penalty\ .

(113)

The solution to this recursion relation subject to initial conditions (
110
) and (
111

106

\mathcal{I}_{m}^{s+1}(q)=q^{sm}\left(\frac{1}{(q)_{m}}-\frac{1}{(q)_{m-1}}\right)=\frac{q^{(s+1)_{m}}}{(q)_{m}}\penalty\ .

(114)

This proves that

\sum_{\ell\geq 0,m\geq 0}\left|F_{m}(\ell,m)\right|q^{\ell}t^{m}=\sum_{m\geq 0}{{\cal I}}_{m}^{m}(q)t^{m}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{q^{m^{2}}}{(q)_{m}}t^{m}\penalty\ .

(115)

92
) of
(A_{1},A_{2})
theory.

4.2.1
Matching The Schur Limit

The first check is the matching of the Schur index obtained by taking the Schur limit. The Schur index of

;

(138)

{{\cal I}}_{\mathsf{S}}^{(A_{1},D_{2n+1})}(q,1)=\left(\frac{\left(q^{2n+1};q^{2n+1}\right)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\right)^{3}\penalty\ .

(139)

{{\cal I}}_{\mathsf{S}}^{(A_{1},D_{2n+1})}(q,1)=\mathsf{PE}\left[3\frac{q-q^{2n+1}}{(1-q)\left(1-q^{2n+1}\right)}\right]

(140)

\begin{split}\frac{3(q-q^{2n+1})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2n+1})}&=\left(\frac{3q}{1-q}-\frac{3q^{2n+1}}{1-q^{2n+1}}\right)\penalty\ .\end{split}

(141)

\mathsf{PE}\left[\frac{kq}{1-q}\right]=\frac{1}{(q;q)^{k}_{\infty}}\penalty\ ,\quad\mathsf{PE}[f-g]=\frac{\mathsf{PE}[f]}{\mathsf{PE}[g]}\penalty\ ,

(142)

{{\cal I}}_{\mathsf{S}}^{(A_{1},D_{2n+1})}(q,1)=\mathsf{PE}\left[3\frac{q-q^{2n+1}}{(1-q)\left(1-q^{2n+1}\right)}\right]=\left(\frac{\left(q^{2n+1};q^{2n+1}\right)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\right)^{3}\penalty\ .

(143)

{{\cal I}}_{\mathsf{S}}^{(A_{1},D_{3})}(q)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{m}}{(q;q)_{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\binom{2m}{k}_{q}\penalty\ .

(144)

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{m}}{(q;q)_{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\binom{2m}{k}_{q}=\left(\frac{\left(q^{3};q^{3}\right)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\right)^{3}\penalty\ .

{thm}

We have the identity

\frac{\left(z;q^{3}\right)_{\infty}\left(q^{3}/z;q^{3}\right)_{\infty}\left(q^{3};q^{3}\right)_{\infty}}{(z;q)_{\infty}(q/z;q)_{\infty}(q;q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}\sum_{k=-n}^{n}{2n\choose n+k}_{q}z^{k}\penalty\ .

(146)

of (

Appendix B
Conventions For Superconformal Index And Various Limits

There exist multiple conventions for the superconformal index in the literature, so in this appendix, we lay out our choice of conventions. See Table 1 of
Gadde:2011uv
for other choices.

The superconformal index evaluates the partition function of a 4d
\mathcal{N}=2

SCFT
on
S^{3}\times S^{1}_{\beta}
with certain fugacities for background fields turned on. In the Hamiltonian perspective, this involves four commuting supercharges and takes the form,

{{\cal I}}_{\mathsf{SCI}}(\rho,\sigma,\tau)=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}(S^{3})}[(-1)^{F}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\delta}^{1}_{\dot{-}}}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\delta}^{1}_{\dot{+}}}\tau^{\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}_{+}}e^{-\beta\delta^{1}_{-}}]\penalty\ ,

(297)

where,

\begin{split}&\delta^{1}_{-}=E-2R+r-2j\penalty\ ,\\
&\tilde{\delta}^{1}_{\dot{+}}=E-2R-r+2\bar{j}\penalty\ ,\\
&\tilde{\delta}^{1}_{\dot{-}}=E-2R-r-2\bar{j}\penalty\ ,\\
&\delta^{2}_{+}=E+2R+r+2j\penalty\ .\\
\end{split}

(298)

We can change to a different set of variables as follows,

\begin{split}&t:=\tau^{2}\penalty\ ,\\
&p:=\sigma\tau\penalty\ ,\\
&q:=\rho\tau\penalty\ .\\
\end{split}

(299)

In these new variables, we have,

{{\cal I}}_{\mathsf{SCI}}(p,q,t)=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}(S^{3})}[(-1)^{F}p^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\delta}^{1}_{\dot{-}}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\delta}^{1}_{\dot{+}}}t^{\frac{1}{4}(\delta^{2}_{+}-\tilde{\delta}^{1}_{\dot{+}}-\tilde{\delta}^{1}_{\dot{-}})}e^{-\beta\delta^{1}_{-}}]\penalty\ .

(300)

The index is automatically independent of
\beta
, i.e., it counts
1/8
-BPS operators that satisfy,

E=2R-r+2j\penalty\ .

(301)

Manually substituting this condition into the index, we can write it in a simplified form,

{{\cal I}}_{\mathsf{SCI}}(p,q,t)=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}(S^{3})}[(-1)^{F}p^{j-\bar{j}-r}q^{j+\bar{j}-r}t^{R+r}]\penalty\ .

(302)

&r=j-\bar{j}\penalty\ .\\
\end{split}

(303)

Only
\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{R}
,
\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{R(j,0)}
and
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{R(j,\bar{j})}

Appendix C
Gröbner Basis

In this appendix, we record some definitions from commutative algebra, specifically the Gröbner basis of an ideal of a polynomial ring following
loustaunau1994introduction
. This terminology and some standard results recorded here are used in Section
3.3
.

Let
\mathds{F}
be a field and
P:=\mathds{F}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring in
n
-variables with coefficients in
\mathds{F}
.
Recall that given
s

f_{1},\dots,f_{s}
, the ideal generated by
I
is given by

I=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\rangle:=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}g_{i}f_{i}\penalty\ :g_{i}\in P\right\}\penalty\ .

(310)

Our goal is to get a basis (or at least the dimension) of the vector space
P/I
. This is achieved by finding
the
Gröbner basis for
I
which we now explain. The first step is to choose a
monomial ordering
on the monomials in
P
. A monomial in
P
is of the form

X^{\vec{a}}:=x_{1}^{a^{1}}\dots x_{n}^{a^{n}}\penalty\ ,\quad\vec{a}=(a^{1},\dots,a^{n})\in({\mathds{Z}}_{\geq 0})^{n}\penalty\ .

(311)

A monomial ordering on
P
is a total order
<
on the set of monomials such that

(1)

X<Y\implies XZ<YZ
for any monomials
X,Y,Z
.

(2)

1<X
for any monomial
X
.

There are several standard choices of monomial ordering, lexicographic (lex), degree lexicographic (deglex), degree reverse lexicographic (degrevlex) and so on. We will use the deglex ordering, which we now define.

Lexicographic ordering.

Choose an ordering on the variables. For example:

x_{1}>x_{2}>\dots x_{n}\penalty\ .

(312)

Then to compare two monomials, lex first compares the exponent of
x_{1}
(i.e., the largest variables), if equal then compares the exponent of
x_{2}
(i.e., the second largest variables) and so forth. For example

x_{1}^{2}>x_{1}x_{2}>x_{1}>x_{2}^{2}>x_{2}>1\penalty\ .

(313)

Degree lexicographic ordering.

Deglex first compares the degree of the monomials. If the degrees are equal then, deglex compares the monomials using lex. For example

x_{1}^{2}>x_{1}x_{2}>x_{2}^{2}>x_{1}>x_{2}>1\penalty\ .

(314)

Once we have chosen a monomial ordering, we can rearrange any polynomial in the order of decreasing order.

{defn}

For any
0\neq f\in P
of degree
r
given by

f=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}X^{\vec{a}_{i}}\penalty\ ,

(315)

such that
X^{\vec{a}_{1}}>X^{\vec{a}_{2}}>\dots>X^{\vec{a}_{r}}

(1)

\mathsf{LM}(f)=X^{a_{1}}
, the leading monomial of
f
,

(2)

\mathsf{LC}(f)=c_{1}
, the leading coefficient of
f
,

(3)

\mathsf{LT}(f)=c_{1}X^{\alpha_{1}}
, the leading term of
f
.

{defn}

(1)

Given polynomials
f,g,h\in P
, we say that
f
is reducible to
h
by
g
if
\mathsf{LM}(g)
divides a nonzero term
X

f

h=f-\frac{X}{\mathsf{LM}(g)}g\penalty\ .

(316)

We symbolically write the reduction as

f\overset{g}{\longrightarrow}h\penalty\ .

(317)

(2)

Given polynomials
f,h\in R
and a set
G=\left\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{s}\right\}\subset R

f
is reducible to

(318)

if there exists a subset

(319)

(3)

A polynomial

is called reduced with respect to

G

is divisible by any one of the
\mathsf{LM}\left(g_{i}\right)

(4)

The remainder of a polynomial
f
with respect to
G
is a polynomial

such that
f
is reducible to
h
modulo
G
and

is reduced with respect to
G
.

{defn}

A set of non-zero polynomials
G=\left\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{s}\right\}
is called a Gröbner basis for an ideal
I\subset P
if for all nonzero
f\in I
,
\mathsf{LM}\left(g_{i}\right)
divides
\mathsf{LM}(f)
for some
g_{i}\in G
.

{thm}
(
loustaunau1994introduction
, Theorem 1.6.7)

G
is a Gröbner basis if and only if for all

the remainder of reduction of

by
G
is unique.

Let

G

\mathds{F}
-vector space

Appendix H
Explicit Formula For
f^{n}_{m,l}(q)

In this appendix, we find an explicit form of the polynomials
f_{m,l}^{n}(q)
.
Recall that

\frac{\left(z;q^{2n+1}\right)_{\infty}\left(q^{2n+1}/z;q^{2n+1}\right)_{\infty}\left(q^{2n+1};q^{2n+1}\right)_{\infty}}{(z;q)_{\infty}(q/z;q)_{\infty}(q;q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{m}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\frac{f^{n}_{m,l}(q)}{(q;q)_{l}}\sum_{k=-l}^{l}{2l\choose l+k}_{q}z^{k}\penalty\ ,

(398)

where
f^{n}_{m,l}
is a polynomial satisfying

1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}q^{m}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\frac{f^{n}_{m,l}(q)}{(q;q)_{l}}(q^{N};q)_{l}(q^{1-N};q)_{l}=q^{nN(N-1)}\penalty\ .

(399)

Our goal is to find a closed form expression for the polynomials
f_{m,l}^{n}(q)
. Let us begin by proving a lemma.

{lemma}

For each
k\in\mathds{N}
, define the sum

\begin{split}S_{k}:=\sum_{i_{k},i_{k-1},\dots,i_{3},i_{2},i_{1}\geq 0}&q^{i_{k}^{2}+(i_{k}+i_{k-1})^{2}+\dots+(i_{k}+\dots+i_{3}+i_{2})^{2}}q^{ki_{k}+(k-1)i_{k-1}+\dots+2i_{2}+i_{1}}\\
\times&\frac{(q^{N};q)_{i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}+i_{1}}(q^{1-N};q)_{i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}+i_{1}}}{(q;q)_{i_{k}}(q;q)_{i_{k-1}}\dots(q;q)_{i_{3}}(q;q)_{i_{2}}(q;q)_{i_{1}}}\penalty\ .\end{split}

(400)

Then we have

S_{k}=q^{kN(N-1)}\penalty\ .

(401)

{proof}

We prove this by induction on
k
. For
k=1
, we have

S_{1}=\sum_{i_{1}\geq 0}q^{i_{1}}\frac{(q^{N};q)_{i_{1}}(q^{1-N};q)_{i_{1}}}{(q;q)_{i_{1}}}\penalty\ .

(402)

This is the hypergeometric sum in (
160
) and is equal to
q^{N(N-1)}
. We now assume that
S_{k-1}=q^{(k-1)N(N-1)}
and show that
S_{k}=q^{kN(N-1)}
. We have

\begin{split}S_{k}=\sum_{i_{k},i_{k-1},\dots,i_{3},i_{2}\geq 0}&q^{i_{k}^{2}+(i_{k}+i_{k-1})^{2}+\dots+(i_{k}+\dots+i_{3}+i_{2})^{2}}q^{ki_{k}+(k-1)i_{k-1}+\dots+2i_{2}}\\
\times&\frac{(q^{N};q)_{i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}}(q^{1-N};q)_{i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}}}{(q;q)_{i_{k}}(q;q)_{i_{k-1}}\dots(q;q)_{i_{3}}(q;q)_{i_{2}}}\\
\times&\sum_{i_{1}\geq 0}q^{i_{1}}\frac{(q^{N+i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}};q)_{i_{1}}(q^{1-N+i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}};q)_{i_{1}}}{(q;q)_{i_{1}}}\penalty\ ,\end{split}

(403)

where we used

(a;q)_{n+k}=(a;q)_{n}(aq^{n};q)_{k}\penalty\ .

(404)

The inner sum can again be evaluated using the hypergeometric sum (
158
) to be

\begin{split}\sum_{i_{1}\geq 0}q^{i_{1}}\frac{(q^{N+i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}};q)_{i_{1}}(q^{1-N+i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}};q)_{i_{1}}}{(q;q)_{i_{1}}}&=q^{(N+i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2})(N-i_{k}-i_{k-1}-\dots-i_{2}-1)}\\
&=q^{N(N-1)-(i_{k}+\dots+i_{2})^{2}-i_{k}-i_{k-1}-\dots-i_{2}}\penalty\ .\end{split}

(405)

Plugging in
S_{k}
, we get

\begin{split}S_{k}=\sum_{i_{k},i_{k-1},\dots,i_{3},i_{2}\geq 0}&q^{i_{k}^{2}+(i_{k}+i_{k-1})^{2}+\dots+(i_{k}+\dots+i_{3}+i_{2})^{2}}q^{ki_{k}+(k-1)i_{k-1}+\dots+2i_{2}}\\
\times&\frac{(q^{N};q)_{i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}}(q^{1-N};q)_{i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}}}{(q;q)_{i_{k}}(q;q)_{i_{k-1}}\dots(q;q)_{i_{3}}(q;q)_{i_{2}}}\\
\times&q^{N(N-1)-(i_{k}+\dots+i_{2})^{2}-i_{k}-i_{k-1}-\dots-i_{2}}\\
=q^{N(N-1)}&\sum_{i_{k},i_{k-1},\dots,i_{3},i_{2}\geq 0}q^{i_{k}^{2}+(i_{k}+i_{k-1})^{2}+\dots+(i_{k}+\dots+i_{3})^{2}}q^{(k-1)i_{k}+(k-2)i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}}\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\times\frac{(q^{N};q)_{i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}}(q^{1-N};q)_{i_{k}+i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}}}{(q;q)_{i_{k}}(q;q)_{i_{k-1}}\dots(q;q)_{i_{3}}(q;q)_{i_{2}}}\penalty\ .\end{split}

(406)

Renaming the dummy variables
i_{j}\to i_{j-1},\penalty\ 2\leq j\leq k
in the last sum, we see that

S_{k}=q^{N(N-1)}S_{k-1}=q^{(k-1)N(N-1)}q^{N(N-1)}=q^{kN(N-1)}\penalty\ .

(407)

Now in
S_{k}
we change variable to

i_{1}+i_{2}+\dots+i_{k}=:i_{1}^{\prime}\penalty\ .

(408)

Then the sum becomes

\begin{split}S_{k}=\sum_{i_{k},i_{k-1},\dots,i_{3},i_{2},i_{1}^{\prime}\geq 0}&q^{Q(i_{2},i_{3},\dots,i_{k})}q^{(k-1)i_{k}+(k-2)i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}+i_{1}^{\prime}}\\
\times&\frac{(q^{N};q)_{i^{\prime}_{1}}(q^{1-N};q)_{i^{\prime}_{1}}}{(q;q)_{i_{k}}(q;q)_{i_{k-1}}\dots(q;q)_{i_{3}}(q;q)_{i_{2}}(q;q)_{i^{\prime}_{1}-i_{2}-\dots-i_{k}}}\penalty\ ,\end{split}

(409)

where we have defined

Q(i_{2},i_{3},\dots,i_{k}):=i_{k}^{2}+(i_{k}+i_{k-1})^{2}+\dots+(i_{k}+\dots+i_{3}+i_{2})^{2}\penalty\ .

(410)

Next we change variables to

(k-1)i_{k}+(k-2)i_{k-1}+\dots+i_{2}+i_{1}^{\prime}=:i_{2}^{\prime}\penalty\ .

(411)

Noting that

\frac{1}{(q;q)_{k}}=\frac{(q^{k+1};q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}=0,\quad\text{for}\quad k<0\penalty\ ,

(412)

we get

\begin{split}S_{k}&=\sum_{i_{k},i_{k-1},\dots,i_{3},i_{2}^{\prime},i_{1}^{\prime}\geq 0}\frac{q^{Q(i_{2}^{\prime}-(k-1)i_{k}-(k-2)i_{k-1}-\dots-i_{1}^{\prime},i_{3},\dots,i_{k})}q^{i_{2}^{\prime}}}{(q;q)_{i_{k}}(q;q)_{i_{k-1}}\dots(q;q)_{i_{3}}}\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\times\frac{(q^{N};q)_{i^{\prime}_{1}}(q^{1-N};q)_{i^{\prime}_{1}}}{(q;q)_{i_{2}^{\prime}-(k-1)i_{k}-(k-2)i_{k-1}-\dots-i_{1}^{\prime}}(q;q)_{2i^{\prime}_{1}+i_{3}+2i_{4}+\dots+(k-2)i_{k}-i_{2}^{\prime}}}\\
&=\sum_{i_{2}^{\prime}\geq 0}q^{i_{2}^{\prime}}\sum_{i_{1}^{\prime}\geq 0}\frac{(q^{N};q)_{i^{\prime}_{1}}(q^{1-N};q)_{i^{\prime}_{1}}}{(q;q)_{i_{1}^{\prime}}}\\
&\times\sum_{i_{k},i_{k-1},\dots,i_{3}\geq 0}\frac{q^{Q(i_{2}^{\prime}-(k-1)i_{k}-(k-2)i_{k-1}-\dots-i_{1}^{\prime},i_{3},\dots,i_{k})}(q;q)_{i_{1}^{\prime}}}{(q;q)_{i_{k}}\dots(q;q)_{i_{3}}(q;q)_{i_{2}^{\prime}-(k-1)i_{k}-(k-2)i_{k-1}-\dots-i_{1}^{\prime}}(q;q)_{2i^{\prime}_{1}+i_{3}+2i_{4}+\dots+(k-2)i_{k}-i_{2}^{\prime}}}\penalty\ .\end{split}

(413)

Note that the second sum is bounded above by
i_{2}^{\prime}
since

\frac{1}{(q;q)_{i_{2}^{\prime}-(k-1)i_{k}-(k-2)i_{k-1}-\dots-i_{1}^{\prime}}}=0\quad\text{unless}\quad i_{1}^{\prime}\leq i_{2}^{\prime}\penalty\ .

(414)

Thus we have

S_{k}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{m}f^{k}_{m,l}(q)\frac{(q^{N};q)_{l}(q^{1-N};q)_{l}}{(q;q)_{l}}\penalty\ ,

(415)

where

f^{k}_{m,l}(q)=\sum_{i_{k},i_{k-1},\dots,i_{3}\geq 0}\frac{q^{Q(m-(k-1)i_{k}-(k-2)i_{k-1}-\dots-2i_{3}-l,i_{3},\dots,i_{k})}(q;q)_{l}}{(q;q)_{i_{k}}\dots(q;q)_{i_{3}}(q;q)_{m-(k-1)i_{k}-(k-2)i_{k-1}-\dots-2i_{3}-l}(q;q)_{2l+i_{3}+2i_{4}+\dots+(k-2)i_{k}-m}}\penalty\ .

(416)

By Lemma
H
, we see that

in (
416
) satisfies (
399

(417)

Clearly, the term

\frac{q^{(k-1)l^{2}}(q;q)_{l}}{(q;q)_{l}}=q^{(k-1)l^{2}}\penalty\ .

f^{2}_{m,l}=q^{l}f^{2}_{m-1,l}+f^{2}_{m-1,l-1}-q^{2m-l-3}f^{2}_{m-3,l-1}\penalty\ .

(419)

f^{2}_{m,l}(q)=q^{(m-l)^{2}}\frac{(q;q)_{l}}{(q;q)_{m-l}(q;q)_{2l-m}}=q^{(m-l)^{2}}{l\choose m-l}_{q}\penalty\ .

(420)

We have

\begin{split}q^{l}f^{2}_{m-1,l}&+f^{2}_{m-1,l-1}-q^{2m-l-3}f^{2}_{m-3,l-1}\\
&=\frac{q^{(m-l-1)^{2}+l}(q;q)_{l}}{(q;q)_{m-l-1}(q;q)_{2l-m+1}}+\frac{q^{(m-l)^{2}}(q;q)_{l-1}}{(q;q)_{m-l}(q;q)_{2l-m-1}}-\frac{q^{(m-l-2)^{2}+2m-l-3}(q;q)_{l-1}}{(q;q)_{m-l-2}(q;q)_{2l-m+1}}\\
&=\frac{q^{(m-l)^{2}}(q;q)_{l}}{(q;q)_{m-l}(q;q)_{2l-m}}\left[q^{l-2(m-l)+1}\frac{1-q^{m-l}}{1-q^{2l-m+1}}+\frac{1-q^{m-2l}}{1-q^{l}}\right.\\
&\left.\hskip 170.71652pt-q^{-2m+3l+1}\frac{(1-q^{m-l-1})(1-q^{m-l})}{(1-q^{l})(1-q^{2l-m+1})}\right]\penalty\ .\end{split}

(421)

Straightforward calculation gives

q^{l-2(m-l)+1}\frac{1-q^{m-l}}{1-q^{2l-m+1}}+\frac{1-q^{m-2l}}{1-q^{l}}-q^{-2m+3l+1}\frac{(1-q^{m-l-1})(1-q^{m-l})}{(1-q^{l})(1-q^{2l-m+1})}=1\penalty\ .

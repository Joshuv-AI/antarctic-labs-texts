Electrodynamics of vortices in quasi-two-dimensional scalar Bose-Einstein condensates
Seong-Ho Shinn, Adolfo del Campo

Abstract
In two spatial dimensions, vortex-vortex interactions approximately vary with the logarithm of the inter-vortex distance, making it possible to describe an ensemble of vortices as a Coulomb gas. We introduce a duality between vortices in a quasi-two-dimensional (quasi-2D) scalar Bose-Einstein condensates (BEC) and effective Maxwell’s electrodynamics. Specifically, we address the general scenario of inhomogeneous, time-dependent BEC number density with dissipation or rotation. Starting from the Gross-Pitaevskii equation (GPE), which describes the mean-field dynamics of a quasi-2D scalar BEC without dissipation, we show how to map vortices in a quasi-2D scalar BEC to 2D electrodynamics beyond the point-vortex approximation, even when dissipation is present or in a rotating system. The physical meaning of this duality is discussed.

Vortex-vortex interactions are known to scale logarithmically with the inter-vortex distance within some approximations.
In the limit of a homogeneous BEC number density, excluding the core region of each vortex, an ensemble of static vortices in a nonrotating quasi-two-dimensional (quasi-2D) scalar BEC without dissipation can be mathematically regarded as a 2D Coulomb gas
[
9
]

For nonrotating three-dimensional (3D)
{}^{4}\textrm{He}
superfluid without dissipation,
a duality between vortices in a thin cylindrical system and 2D electrodynamics has been reported in the limit where the superfluid density is constant outside vortices

Such a duality has been extended to the general 3D case by using

35

In the absence of an external potential, the asymptotic vortex dynamics in 2D nonlinear Schrödinger equation has been studied
[

[
37

For the nonrotating scalar BEC without dissipation,

38

,
and the effective Maxwell’s equations in a quasi-2D system

39

However, the BEC number density is zero at the core of a vortex, and thus, a vortex has a finite core size, which is about the order of the healing length (coherence length)

As a result, the fluctuation of the BEC number density cannot be neglected, especially around the core of the vortex.
To simplify the problem, the point-vortex model (PVM) has been widely used. Yet, such description cannot account for the dynamic vortex creation and annihilation processes if one uses the mean-field Gross-Pitaevskii equation (GPE) under nonrotating quasi-2D scalar BEC without dissipation

It thus remains to be established whether the connection between vortices and effective Maxwell’s electrodynamics is valid in a quasi-2D scalar BEC with dissipation, beyond the PVM or under rotation.

In this paper, starting from the microscopic Hermitian Hamiltonian of a nonrotating quasi-2D scalar bosons in the s-wave scattering limit

46

48
,
49
,

,
51

,
53

Using that condition, we show how vortices in a quasi-2D scalar BEC can be connected to the effective Maxwell’s equations even beyond the PVM and without assuming a time-independent BEC number density.
From such duality, we show that the

[

56
]

can be
alternatively induced
and generalized beyond the PVM description and present how to calculate the temporal change of the circulation.
Under the GPE + PVM, we show that the logarithmic vortex interaction may need correction if vortices move. We also show that one can recover previously known results when the fluctuations of the BEC number density are negligible.

II.1
Case 1: Gross-Pitaevskii equation for nonrotating scalar BEC

Using second quantization and the
s
-wave scattering limit, the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}\left(t\right)
in the Heisenberg picture of a nonrotating quasi-2D scalar Bose gas on the
xy
plane in a region
\mathcal{A}
can be expressed as
[
11
,
46
]

\displaystyle\hat{H}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{A}}d^{2}r\;\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2M}\nabla^{2}+V\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

(1)

\displaystyle+\frac{g}{2}\int_{\mathcal{A}}d^{2}r\;\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right),\qquad

where

\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
is the bosonic field operator (in Heisenberg picture) that annihilates a boson at the position
\bm{r}
and at time
t
,

\hbar
is the reduced Planck constant,

M
is the mass of the boson,

V\left(\bm{r},t\right)
is a local
external potential (with no singularity)
satisfying

\left[V\left(\bm{r},t\right),\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]=0
,

g=2\sqrt{2\pi}\hbar^{2}a_{B}a_{s}/\left(Ml_{\perp}\right)
is the density-density interaction coefficient in the quasi-2D BEC
[
57
,
53
]
,

a_{B}
is the Bohr radius,
a_{s}
is the
s
-wave scattering length in units of
a_{B}
,
l_{\perp}\coloneqq\sqrt{\hbar/\left(M\omega_{\perp}\right)}
is the harmonic oscillator length in
z
axis, and
\omega_{\perp}
is the harmonic trap frequency in
z

By defining the number density operator
\hat{n}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
and the probability current operator
\hat{\bm{J}}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
as

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{J}}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq\frac{\hbar}{2Mi}\left[\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right)-h.c.\right],

\displaystyle\hat{n}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right),

(2)

where
\hat{A}\pm h.c.\coloneqq\hat{A}\pm\hat{A}^{\dagger}
for any quantum operator
\hat{A}
, one can show that the integrated density operator
\int_{\mathcal{A}}d^{2}r\;\hat{n}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
commutes with
\hat{H}\left(t\right)
and thus

N\coloneqq\left\langle\int_{\mathcal{A}}d^{2}r\;\hat{n}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right\rangle
is constant in time
t
, with
\left\langle\hat{A}\right\rangle
denoting the expectation value of the quantum operator
\hat{A}
.
From the Heisenberg equations of motion, it follows that

\frac{\partial\hat{n}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\hat{\bm{J}}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0,

(3)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{J}}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4M^{2}}\left\lgroup\left\{\nabla\left[\nabla^{2}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\right\}\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+h.c.\right\rgroup

(4)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{M}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\left\{\nabla\left[V\left(\bm{r},t\right)+g\hat{n}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\right\}\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4M^{2}}\left\{\left[\nabla^{2}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\nabla\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+h.c.\right\}.

To simplify the problem, we focus on the mean-field limit where
the BEC order parameter is given by

\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq\left\langle\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right\rangle
,
satisfying

\psi^{\dagger}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)\psi^{\dagger}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
as we consider a scalar (single-component) BEC.
In this mean-field limit,
one can introduce the BEC number density

n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq\left|\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right|^{2}
and the mean-field probability current

\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq\frac{\hbar}{2Mi}\left[\psi^{*}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)-c.c.\right],

(5)

where
A\pm c.c.\coloneqq A\pm A^{*}
for any complex function
A
.

In the zero temperature limit, the integrated density is normalized as

\int_{\mathcal{A}}d^{2}r\;n\left(\bm{r},t\right)=N
and the mean-field order parameter
\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)
obeys the GPE
[
58
,
59
,
46
]

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}=\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2M}\nabla^{2}+V\left(\bm{r},t\right)+gn\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right),

(6)

which takes the same form as the Heisenberg equation of motion for
\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
upon the replacement
\hat{\psi}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\rightarrow\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)
.
From the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
), we define the mean-field energy
E\left(t\right)
of the nonrotating quasi-2D scalar BEC as

E\left(t\right)=\int_{\mathcal{A}}d^{2}r\;\left[\Phi_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+n\left(\bm{r},t\right)V\left(\bm{r},t\right)+\frac{g}{2}n^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right],

(7)

i.e., the energy obtained by neglecting the noncondensed bosonic particles.
Here,

\displaystyle\Phi_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4M}\left[\psi^{*}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla^{2}\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)+c.c.\right]

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\left[\Phi_{Q}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+\frac{M}{2}\frac{J^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{n^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}\right],

is the mean-field kinetic energy density, and

\Phi_{Q}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2M}\frac{\nabla^{2}\sqrt{n\left(\bm{r},t\right)}}{\sqrt{n\left(\bm{r},t\right)}},

(9)

is the quantum potential
[
60
,
61
]
.
Note that
\Phi_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
is well-defined even for
\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0
, whereas
\Phi_{Q}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
is singular where
\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0
.

From the GPE in Eq. (
6
), it follows that

\frac{\partial n\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0,

(10)

and

\displaystyle Mn\left(\bm{r},t\right)\frac{\partial\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\quad=\Phi_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla n\left(\bm{r},t\right)-n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla\Phi_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-n^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla\left[V\left(\bm{r},t\right)+gn\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-M\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla\cdot\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right).

(11)

Following Refs.
[
62
,
63
,
64
]
, we define the superfluid velocity
\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
as

n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right),

(12)

so that Eq. (
10
) can be interpreted as the continuity equation for the superfluid number density

.
However, care must be taken since
\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
itself is well-defined even when
\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0
, whereas
\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
is not well-defined at the location where the order parameter vanishes,

As a result of this feature, previous studies
[

,

,

,
38

neglected the fluctuations on

\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\left(\hbar/M\right)\nabla\varphi\left(\bm{r},t\right)

62
,

,

,

,

]

for any case.
The proper treatment when one defines

\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\left(\hbar/M\right)\nabla\varphi\left(\bm{r},t\right)
can be seen in, e.g., Refs.
[

,

,

,

,

]

\nabla\times\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\frac{\hbar}{Mi}\left[\nabla\psi^{*}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\times\left[\nabla\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right],

\displaystyle n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla\times\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\left[\nabla n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\times\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+n^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla\times\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right).\qquad

(14)

Since

\displaystyle\left[\nabla n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\times\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\hbar}{2Mi}\left[\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla\psi^{*}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+c.c.\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\times\left[\psi^{*}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)-c.c.\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\hbar}{Mi}n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\left[\nabla\psi^{*}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\times\left[\nabla\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right],\qquad

wherever the superfluid density is finite

, the vorticity vanishes,

(17)

where we introduce the effective kinetic force
\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
and the effective potential
U_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
, defined as

\displaystyle n^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\Phi_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla n\left(\bm{r},t\right)

(18)

\displaystyle-n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla\Phi_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right),

and

U_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq V\left(\bm{r},t\right)+gn\left(\bm{r},t\right).

(19)

For finite density
n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\neq 0
, one can see that

\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=-\nabla\left[\Phi_{Q}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+Mv^{2}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)/2\right]
. However, the effective kinetic force

\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
is singular at
n\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0
.
In Sec.
III
, we will show how to determine the curl of

\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
, leading to

\nabla\times\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\neq 0
when vortices are moving.

Equation (
16
) is the continuity equation for the fluid with flow velocity
\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
.
The absence of a drag force for
n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\neq 0
underlines the superfluid character, justifying to call
\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
as the superfluid velocity.
We kept
n^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
in Eq. (
17
) to emphasize that the superfluid velocity
\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
and the effective kinetic force
\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
are singular in the region where
n\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0
.

II.2
Case 2: beyond the
nonrotating
Gross-Pitaevskii equation

The Gross-Pitaevskii equation in Eq. (
6
) is obtained by neglecting the bosonic field operator of the noncondensed particles
[
46
]

in the nonrotating case.
However, at
nonzero temperature, a dissipation term emerges (e.g.,
[
47
,
48
,
49
,
50
,
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
]
).
To go beyond the nonrotating dissipationless case,
let
us generalize Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
) as

\frac{\partial n\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=G\left(\bm{r},t\right),

(20)

and

\displaystyle Mn\left(\bm{r},t\right)\frac{\partial\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\quad=n^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\left[\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)-\nabla U_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-M\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\nabla\cdot\bm{J}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+\bm{F}\left(\bm{r},t\right),\qquad

(21)

where
G\left(\bm{r},t\right)
and
\bm{F}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
are real functions to be determined by the equation
i\hbar\partial\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)/\partial t
in the model under consideration. Said differently, they are model-dependent.
We shall focus on the specific forms of
\bm{F}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
or
G\left(\bm{r},t\right)
for a rotating quasi-2D scalar BEC without dissipation in Sec.
II.2.1
.
Nevertheless, in Sec.
IV
, we will show that one can still build the effective Maxwell’s equations without specifying them.

From the definition of the superfluid velocity in Eq. (
12
), Eq. (
21
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle Mn^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\frac{\partial\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\quad=n^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\left[\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)-\nabla U_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\bm{F}\left(\bm{r},t\right)-MG\left(\bm{r},t\right)n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right),\qquad

(22)

which implies that

\bm{F}\left(\bm{r},t\right)-MG\left(\bm{r},t\right)n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
may be regarded as some kind of
\left(\textrm{force}\right)\times\left(\textrm{area}\right)^{-2}
acting on the fluid at position
\bm{r}
at time
t
.

Note that
-MG\left(\bm{r},t\right)\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)/n\left(\bm{r},t\right)
corresponds to the drag force, and thus, going beyond the GPE description, the system may no longer be a superfluid. However, for notational convenience, we will continue to use the word “superfluid”, referring to
\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
as the superfluid velocity whether the drag force exists or not.
For notational convenience, we introduce

\bm{F}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq\frac{\bm{F}\left(\bm{r},t\right)-MG\left(\bm{r},t\right)n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{n^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)},

(23)

which might be singular in the region where
n\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0
.
By using Eq. (
22
), one can determine

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left[\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+\bm{F}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]

(24)

\displaystyle+\nabla^{2}U_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right).

It can be shown that so far, every result in this Sec.
II
is also valid in 3D space.
In the following subsection, we will consider a rotating quasi-2D scalar BEC without dissipation as an example.

II.2.1
Example: rotating quasi-2D scalar BEC without dissipation

Motivated by the experiment with two concentric counter-rotating superfluids
[
70
]
, we will consider a rotating quasi-2D scalar BEC with angular velocity

\bm{\Omega}_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\Omega_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\bm{e}_{\perp}
that depends on position or time.
In the rotating frame, the GPE in Eq. (
6
) becomes
[
57
]

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2M}\nabla^{2}+V\left(\bm{r},t\right)+gn\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right)

(25)

\displaystyle-\bm{\Omega}_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\cdot\left(\bm{r}\times\frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla\right)\psi\left(\bm{r},t\right).

Therefore one can see that

G\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\left[\bm{\Omega}_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\times\bm{r}\right]\cdot\nabla n\left(\bm{r},t\right),

(26)

and after some calculations,

\displaystyle\bm{F}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}MG\left(\bm{r},t\right)n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle-Mn^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\bm{\Omega}_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\times\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle+Mn^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\left[\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\bm{\Omega}_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\times\bm{r}

\displaystyle+Mn\left(\bm{r},t\right)\left[\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\cdot\nabla n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\bm{\Omega}_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\times\bm{r}

\displaystyle-Mn^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\bm{\Omega}_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\times\left[\left(\bm{r}\cdot\nabla\right)\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]

\displaystyle+Mn^{2}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\left[\left(\bm{e}_{\perp}\times\bm{r}\right)\cdot\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\nabla\Omega_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}Mn\left(\bm{r},t\right)\left\{\bm{\Omega}_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\cdot\left[\bm{r}\times\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\right\}\nabla n\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}Mn\left(\bm{r},t\right)\left[\bm{r}\cdot\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\bm{\Omega}_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\times\nabla n\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}Mn\left(\bm{r},t\right)\left[\bm{r}\cdot\nabla n\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\bm{\Omega}_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\times\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right).

Then, it can be shown that

\bm{F}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=M\nabla\left\{\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\cdot\left[\bm{\Omega}_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\times\bm{r}\right]\right\},

(28)

which is the generalization of Eq. (14.6) in Ref.
[
57
]

(see Appendix
A
for the derivation).
When
\Omega_{\perp}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
is constant, Eq. (
26
) is identical to Eq. (14.5) in Ref.
[
57
]
.

Note that

\bm{F}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
is singular in the region where
n\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0
.
Hence, one should be careful not to assume that

\nabla\times\bm{F}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0
.
As already advanced, we will consider that curl in the next section.

III
Vortex quantization

A vortex with topological charge
q_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfies

\oint d\bm{l}\cdot\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\left(2\pi\hbar/M\right)q_{j}
around its core at position

\bm{r}=\bm{r}_{\alpha_{j}}
, where

\mathbb{Z}
is the set of integers
[
66
,
41
]
(energetic and stability considerations generally restrict the values of
q_{j}
to
\pm 1
).
Using Stokes’ theorem, Eqs. (
13
), (
14
), and (
15
),
if we assume that (within the system) the superfluid density is zero only at the core of the vortex,
one can infer that

\displaystyle n\left(\bm{r}_{\alpha_{j}}\left(t\right),t\right)=0.

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow\nabla\times\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\bm{e}_{\perp}\frac{2\pi\hbar}{M}q_{j}\delta\left(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{\alpha_{j}}\left(t\right)\right),

(29)

where

\delta\left(\bm{r}\right)
is the Dirac delta function, and

\bm{r}=\bm{r}_{\alpha_{j}}\left(t\right)
is the position of the core of the vortex with topological charge
q_{j}
at time
t
.
In BEC experiments or numerical simulations, the typical size of the
vortex core
is about the order of the healing length (coherence length)
\xi_{h}\coloneqq\hbar/\sqrt{2Mgn_{m}}
, where
n_{m}
is the mean BEC number density
[
41
]
.

In general, even for nonrotating BEC, the topological charge of the vortex may change over time.
It may flip sign (e.g., from
q=1
to
q=-1
), turning a vortex into an antivortex, in a nonrotating quasi-2D scalar BEC under anisotropic trap potential and small
g

[
71
]
.
The nonconservation of the number of vortices is observed in nonrotating scalar bosons under the second-order phase transition
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
. Its growth can be explained via the Kibble-Zurek mechanism
[
5
]
, while its decay can result from coarsening
[
72
,
73
]
.
Also, this change in the number of vortices is numerically shown in the stochastic GPE
under the periodic boundary conditions

[
13
]
and in the stochastic projected GPE
[
74
]
.

Considering the case of time-dependent topological charges, for multiple vortices in the nonrotating quasi-2D scalar BEC in the region
\mathcal{A}
, Eqs. (
29
) can be generalized to

\displaystyle\oint_{\partial\mathcal{A}}d\bm{l}\cdot\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\frac{2\pi\hbar}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}q_{j}\left(t\right),

\displaystyle n\left(\bm{r}_{\alpha_{j}}\left(t\right),t\right)=0\quad\textrm{ for $j=1,2,\cdots,N_{v}\left(\mathcal{A};t\right)$. }

\displaystyle\;\Rightarrow\nabla\times\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\bm{e}_{\perp}\frac{2\pi\hbar}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}q_{j}\left(t\right)\delta\left(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{\alpha_{j}}\left(t\right)\right),\qquad

(30)

where

q_{j}\left(t\right)\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\partial\mathcal{A}
represents the boundary of the region
\mathcal{A}
,

\oint_{\partial\mathcal{A}}d\bm{l}\cdot\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
represents the closed line integration of
\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
along the closed curve
\partial\mathcal{A}
,

N_{v}\left(\mathcal{A};t\right)
is the number of vortices in the region
\mathcal{A}
at time
t
,
and

q_{j}\left(t\right)\neq 0
for
j=1,2,\cdots,N_{v}\left(\mathcal{A};t\right)
, whereas
q_{j}\left(t\right)=0
for
j>N_{v}\left(\mathcal{A};t\right)
.

As we consider a quasi-2D system,

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left\{\bm{e}_{\perp}\cdot\left[\nabla\times\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\right\}=\bm{e}_{\perp}\cdot\left[\nabla\times\frac{\partial\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}\right],

(31)

and from Eq. (
22
),

\displaystyle M\bm{e}_{\perp}\cdot\left[\nabla\times\frac{\partial\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\bm{e}_{\perp}\cdot\left\{\nabla\times\left[\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+\bm{F}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\right\}.

(32)

Thus, combining Eqs. (
31
) and (
32
)
and using Stokes’ theorem,
it follows that

\nabla\times\left[\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+\bm{F}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]=M\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\nabla\times\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right],

(33)

and for special cases where

n\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0
only at vortex cores,

\displaystyle\nabla\times\left[\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+\bm{F}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad=2\pi\hbar\bm{e}_{\perp}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}q_{j}\left(t\right)\delta\left(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{\alpha_{j}}\left(t\right)\right)\right],

(34)

implying that the quantum curl dynamics can describe the system.
Though it is not always possible to build effective Maxwell’s equations in the quantum curl dynamics
[
75
]
,
Eq. (
33
) will be used in Sec.
IV

to define the effective free electric current density in the effective Maxwell’s equations, and in Sec.
VI
to present how it is related to the time change of the circulation of
\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
.

IV
The connection between vortices in quasi-2D BEC and electrodynamics

The mathematical connection between static vortices in a nonrotating quasi-2D scalar BEC and 2D electrostatics within the GPE and PVM is well established
[
9
]
.
In this section, we will derive the duality between vortices in a quasi-2D BEC and 2D electrodynamics in general.
Motivated by Ref.
[
39
]
, let us define the vortex charge density
\rho_{v}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
such that

\int_{\mathcal{A}}d^{2}r\;\rho_{v}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{N_{v}\left(\mathcal{A};t\right)}q_{j}\left(t\right).

(35)

We define
\rho_{v}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
in this way because the curl of
\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
need not equal the Dirac delta function if
the superfluid density
n\left(\bm{r},t\right)
is zero within some finite region around the core of the vortex.
For example, the nonlinearity of the field equation for superfluid
{}^{4}\textrm{He}

changes the curl of the superfluid velocity
\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

to the smeared delta function (see Eq. (1.64) in Part II of Ref.
[
34
]
).
Note that setting

\rho_{v}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}q_{j}\left(t\right)\delta\left(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{\alpha_{j}}\left(t\right)\right)

corresponds to the PVM, so we also consider beyond the PVM.

Now, let us introduce the effective electric field
\bm{E}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
defined as

\bm{E}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq\frac{M}{2\pi\hbar\epsilon_{\rm sf}}\bm{v}_{P}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\times\bm{e}_{\perp},

(36)

where

\bm{v}_{P}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

satisfies

\nabla\times\bm{v}_{P}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\bm{e}_{\perp}\frac{2\pi\hbar}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}q_{j}\left(t\right)\delta\left(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{\alpha_{j}}\left(t\right)\right),

(37)

as if

\bm{v}_{P}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

corresponds to the superfluid velocity in the PVM up to some irrotational vector (we will show later in Eq. (
52
) that

\bm{v}_{P}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

is the velocity of the vortex core),
and the effective vacuum permittivity
\epsilon_{\rm sf}
is some constant. Similarly, we define the effective electric displacement field
\bm{D}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
and the effective polarization density
\bm{P}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
as

\displaystyle\bm{D}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\frac{M}{2\pi\hbar}\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\times\bm{e}_{\perp},

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\bm{D}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)-\epsilon_{\rm sf}\bm{E}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

(38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M}{2\pi\hbar}\left[\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)-\bm{v}_{P}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]\times\bm{e}_{\perp},

whence it follows that

\nabla\cdot\bm{D}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\rho_{v}\left(\bm{r},t\right).

(39)

Further, note that

\nabla\cdot\bm{P}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\rho_{v}\left(\bm{r},t\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}q_{j}\left(t\right)\delta\left(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{\alpha_{j}}\left(t\right)\right),

(40)

represents the deviation from the PVM.

From Eq. (
33
), we introduce the effective free electric current density
\bm{J}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
as

\bm{J}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq\frac{\bm{e}_{\perp}}{2\pi\hbar}\times\left[\bm{f}_{K}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+\bm{F}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]-\frac{\partial\bm{P}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t},

(41)

so that Eq. (
33
) can be written as a continuity equation for the vortex charge density

\frac{\partial\rho_{v}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\bm{J}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0.

(42)

However, Eq. (
42
) does not imply vortex charge conservation; the vortex charge may not be conserved as
q\left(t\right)
may depend on time
t
.
This consideration, including the non-conservation of the vortex charge, is one of the main differences between our work and the previous works relying on the conservation of the vortex charge
[
32
,
69
,
76
,
77
]
.
In Sec.
VI
, Eq. (
42
) will be used to show how to calculate the change of the circulation of
\bm{v}_{s}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
in time.

From Eqs. (
22
), (
36
), (
38
), and (
41
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{D}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M}{2\pi\hbar}\frac{\partial\bm{v}_{P}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}\times\bm{e}_{\perp}+\frac{\partial\bm{P}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bm{J}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+\nabla\times\left[-\frac{U_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{2\pi\hbar}\bm{e}_{\perp}\right].\quad

If
U_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
is constant in space, Eq. (
43
) cannot be distinguished from Maxwell’s equations with the effective magnetic field strength
\bm{H}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0
.
Therefore let us define
\bm{H}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
as

\bm{H}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\coloneqq-\frac{U_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)-\bar{U}_{\rm sf}\left(t\right)}{2\pi\hbar}\bm{e}_{\perp},

(44)

where

\bar{U}_{\rm sf}\left(t\right)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}\int_{\mathcal{A}}d^{2}r\;U_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right),

(45)

is the spatial average of
U_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
and

\left|\mathcal{A}\right|

denotes the area of the region
\mathcal{A}
.
Then, Eq. (
43
) can be written as

\nabla\times\bm{H}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=\bm{J}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+\frac{\partial\bm{D}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}.

(46)

Additionally, as our system is in a quasi-2D,

\nabla\cdot\bm{H}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=0,

(47)

meaning that there is no effective free magnetic monopole.

Using the definitions in Eqs. (
36
) and (
38
),

\nabla\times\bm{D}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=-\frac{M}{2\pi\hbar}\bm{e}_{\perp}\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{P}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+\nabla\times\bm{P}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right).

(48)

By defining the effective free magnetic current density
\bm{J}_{m,\rm{sf}}\left(\bm{r},t\right)
as

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{m,\rm{sf}}\left(\bm{r},t\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle c^{2}_{\rm sf}\left[\frac{M}{2\pi\hbar}\bm{e}_{\perp}\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{P}\left(\bm{r},t\right)-\nabla\times\bm{P}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)\right]

(49)

\displaystyle+\frac{\bm{e}_{\perp}}{2\pi\hbar}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[U_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)-\bar{U}_{\rm sf}\left(t\right)\right],

where the effective speed of light in vacuum
c_{\rm sf}
is some positive constant, Eq. (
48
) can be expressed as

\nabla\times\bm{D}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)=-\frac{1}{c^{2}_{\rm sf}}\left[\bm{J}_{m,\rm{sf}}\left(\bm{r},t\right)+\frac{\partial\bm{H}_{\rm sf}\left(\bm{r},t\right)}{\partial t}\right].

(50)

From the
Landau’s criterion of superfluidity
[
78
,
57
,
11
]
,
we will set
c_{\rm sf}
to be the maximum value of the
speed of sound
in the scalar BEC, i.e.,
c_{s}\coloneqq\sqrt{gn_{\rm max}/M}
, where
n_{\rm max}
is the maximum value of
n\left(\bm{r},t\right)
.

Using Eq. (
49
), it is straightforward to check that

Towards a general subtraction formula for NNLO QCD corrections to processes at hadron colliders: final states with quarks and gluons
Federica Devoto, Kirill Melnikov, Raoul Röntsch, Chiara Signorile-Signorile, Davide Maria Tagliabue, Matteo Tresoldi

Abstract
We describe the calculation of integrated subtraction terms in the nested soft-collinear subtraction scheme for hadron collider processes with quarks and gluons, thereby extending the results presented in Ref. [ 1 ] . Although this extension eventually proves to be straightforward, it requires a more careful treatment of certain collinear limits to achieve a compact and physically-transparent final result. We also show that the cancellation of infrared divergences can be organized in such a way that, once soft contributions are removed, it occurs independently for each of the external partons. We consider these results to be important stepping stones on the way to deriving finite remainders of the integrated subtraction terms for fully-general hadron collider processes in the context of the nested soft-collinear subtraction scheme.

One of the main obstacles in constructing a generalized NNLO subtraction scheme is the proliferation of terms required at different stages of the calculation and the need to combine multiple contributions to obtain the physically-transparent structure of the final result.
In the recent paper
[
1

, we employed the so-called

scheme
[
17

to show how this problem can be overcome.
The key element in the construction described in Ref.
[
1
]
is the iterative nature of IR subtraction terms that emerges at the level of color-correlated matrix elements.
Using this feature as a guiding principle allows us to treat the problem in an almost process-independent way, and significantly simplifies the intermediate steps required to demonstrate the cancellation of IR poles.
As a result, it became possible to show the cancellation of these poles for the process
q{\bar{q}}\to X+{N_{g}}\,g
, where the number of final-state gluons
{N_{g}}
is a parameter, and to produce relatively compact formulas for the finite remainder of the integrated subtraction terms.

Although the results reported in Ref.
[
1
]
are very general, we did take advantage of the symmetries of the final state and the simple structure of the collinear limits between final-state gluons and incoming partons.
Hence, to generalize the calculation of Ref.
[
1
]
to an arbitrary process, we need to do two things: first, consider less symmetric final states and, second, account for flavor-changing initial-state collinear emissions.
The goal of this paper is to address
the first issue and we do this by considering a process where an initial
gq
state produces an arbitrary number of
gluons and a quark.
Furthermore, we also consider corrections in which an additional quark-antiquark pair is radiated, and extract the contributions
to the integrated subtraction terms that depend on the number of light quark flavors
n_{\mathrm{f}}
.
We show that the general approach presented in Ref.
[
1
]
can be
applied to such processes with small modifications which mostly concern the interconnection of final-state collinear splittings
as these may be affected by the choice of unresolved partons in different ways.
We find that, if the subtraction terms described by these collinear limits are combined before integration, they lead to universal, physically-transparent structures.
In fact, these features can already be seen at NLO, providing a guide as to how to solve this problem at NNLO.

In general, we find that the method introduced in Ref.
[
1
]
is sufficiently robust to provide the description of more complex final and initial states. Indeed, as we will see, the soft part is unchanged, while the collinear part can be treated leg-by-leg.
Hence, in spite of the fact that we still work with a particular partonic process, we believe that the present paper is an important step towards providing integrated NNLO subtraction terms and finite remainders for
generic
multi-jet processes at hadron and lepton colliders.

2
The leading order process

We are interested in understanding higher-order QCD corrections to the inclusive production of
N
jets at a hadron collider in association with a color-neutral system
X
,
pp\to X+N~{\rm jets}
.
This process receives contributions from many partonic channels.
In this paper, we will focus on the following one

{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}:~a_{g}+b_{q}\to X+{N_{g}}\,g+q\,,

(1)

where
{N_{g}}=N-1
, and
a
and
b
label initial state partons
with momenta
p_{a,b}
.
We write the cross section for process
{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
as

\begin{split}2s_{ab}\,{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathrm{LO}}^{gq}&=\bigg\langle\frac{1}{{N_{g}}!}F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{gq}[\{g\}_{{N_{g}}},\{q\}_{1}]\bigg\rangle\\
&=\frac{N_{\rm av}}{{N_{g}}!}\int{\mathrm{d}}\Phi\,(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(P_{\rm fin}-P_{\rm in})\,\absolutevalue{\calM_0(P_{\rm fin}, P_{\rm in} )}^{2}\,{\mathcal{O}}(P_{\rm fin})\,,\end{split}

(2)

and we note that, in order to obtain its contribution to the hadronic process, we need to convolute the partonic cross section with parton distribution functions (PDF)
f_{g}
and
f_{q}
. In Eq. (
2
),

{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
is the matrix element of the process in
Eq. (
1
),
{\mathrm{d}}\Phi
is the Lorentz-invariant phase space for the final-state particles, and
{\mathcal{O}}
is an IR-safe observable which ensures that the final state contains at least
N
resolved jets.
The total momenta of the initial- and final-state particles are denoted as
P_{\rm in}
and
P_{\mathrm{fin}}
, respectively, and the delta function in Eq. (
2
) enforces energy-momentum conservation.
Summation over spins and colors of final-state partons, and averaging over spins and colors of initial-state partons are assumed in Eq. (
2
).
The corresponding spin and color averaging factors are described by the factor

N_{\rm av}
.

Furthermore,

\{g\}_{{N_{g}}}
and
\{q\}_{1}

in Eq. (
2
)
denote lists of
{N_{g}}
final-state gluons, and of
a single final-state quark, respectively.
This notation is redundant for the process that we consider, but we introduce it with an eye on future generalizations. The factor
1/{N_{g}}!
corresponds to the symmetry factor arising from having
{N_{g}}
indistinguishable gluons in the final state.
It is convenient to absorb this symmetry factor and omit the initial-state partons and lists of final-state
partons by defining

F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}=\frac{1}{{N_{g}}!}F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{gq}[\{g\}_{{N_{g}}},\{q\}_{1}]\,.

(3)

Then, the differential cross section for
the process
{\cal A}_{0}
reads

2s_{ab}\,{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathrm{LO}}^{gq}=\big\langle F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}\big\rangle\,,

(4)

and we will use this notation in what follows.

3
NLO QCD corrections

Having fixed the notation, we continue by discussing the NLO QCD corrections to the partonic process
{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
in Eq. (
1
).
As usual, one has to compute virtual corrections to
{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
and account for real-emission contributions that are comprised of processes where the number
of final-state partons is increased by one. For the complete calculation, many real-emission processes need to be considered.
However, since we would like to discuss the cancellation of IR singularities in higher-order corrections to
{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
, we can omit contributions that do not lead to this process when soft or collinear limits are taken.
There are also processes which lead to
{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
under collinear limits but which are convoluted with different PDFs; we do not consider such processes either.

With this clarification, it is easy to convince oneself that the following three processes should be considered

\begin{split}{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}:\quad&a_{g}+b_{q}\to X+({N_{g}}+1)g+q\,,\\
{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}:\quad&a_{g}+b_{q}\to X+({N_{g}}-1)g+q+{q^{\prime}}{{\bar{q}}^{\prime}}\,,\quad{q^{\prime}}\neq q\,,\\
{{\mathcal{A}}_{3}}:\quad&a_{g}+b_{q}\to X+({N_{g}}-1)g+q+q{\bar{q}}\,.\end{split}

(5)

However, further simplifications are possible.
Indeed, in order to provide a singular contribution to the process
{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
, each of the three processes
{\cal A}_{1,2,3}
should “lose” a parton in such a way that one gets both the initial and the final state of
{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
.
There are several ways in which this can happen in the process
{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}
, but in processes
{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}
and
{{\mathcal{A}}_{3}}
this can only occur if
a quark-antiquark pair,
q^{\prime}{\bar{q}}^{\prime}
or
q{\bar{q}}
, respectively, is clustered into a gluon.
Since these collinear limits are identical for processes
{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}
and
{{\mathcal{A}}_{3}}
, we do not need to consider the latter since we can retrieve all the required information from the former.

We begin with the discussion of process
{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}
in Eq. (
5
), and write the corresponding differential cross section as

2s_{ab}\,{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}_{\mathrm{R}}=\bigg\langle\frac{1}{({N_{g}}+1)!}F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{gq}[\{g\}_{{N_{g}}+1},\{q\}_{1}]\bigg\rangle\,.

(6)

Following Ref.
[
1
]
, we introduce partitions of unity
\Delta^{(i)}
and split the cross section into a sum of terms such that in each term only one of the partons can lead to a singular limit once it becomes unresolved.
We find

2s_{ab}\,{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}_{\mathrm{R}}=\sum_{i}\bigg\langle\frac{\Delta^{(i)}}{({N_{g}}+1)!}F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{gq}[\{g\}_{{N_{g}}+1},\{q\}_{1}]\bigg\rangle\,.

(7)

The index
i
in the above formula runs over all final-state partons, and a possible choice of partitions
\Delta^{(i)}
is described in Appendix B of Ref.
[
1
]
.

The potentially-unresolved parton
i
can be either a (anti)quark or a gluon.
Accounting for the symmetry properties of
F_{\mathrm{LM}}
, we rename the final state momenta and call the unresolved parton
\mathfrak{m}
.
There are
{N_{g}}+1
ways to choose an unresolved gluon and a single choice for an unresolved quark.
Hence, we can write

2s_{ab}\,{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}_{\mathrm{R}}=\big\langle\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}F_{\mathrm{R}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}]\big\rangle\,,

(8)

where

F_{\mathrm{R}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}]=F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}_{g}]+F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}_{q}]\,,

(9)

and the functions
F_{\rm LM}
are defined as

\begin{split}F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}_{g}]&=\frac{1}{{N_{g}}!}F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{ab}[\{g\}_{{N_{g}}},\{q\}_{1}|\mathfrak{m}_{g}]\,,\\
F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}_{q}]&=\frac{1}{({N_{g}}+1)!}F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{ab}[\{g\}_{{N_{g}}+1}|\mathfrak{m}_{q}]\,.\end{split}

(10)

We note that in the last
F_{\mathrm{LM}}
function the quark list has disappeared, and that the symmetry factors that multiply various functions
F_{\mathrm{LM}}
are defined by the number of
resolved
gluons.

Following the discussion in Ref.
[
1
]
, we
extract soft and collinear divergences that affect the real emission contribution, and obtain

2s_{ab}\,{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}_{\mathrm{R}}=\big\langle S_{\mathfrak{m}}F_{\mathrm{R}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}]\big\rangle+\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{H}}}\big\langle\overline{S}_{\mathfrak{m}}C_{i\mathfrak{m}}\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}F_{\mathrm{R}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}]\big\rangle+\big\langle\mathcal{O}_{\text{NLO}}^{(\mathfrak{m})}\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}F_{\mathrm{R}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}]\big\rangle\,.

(11)

We denote the list of
N
final-state resolved partons contained in a given
F_{\mathrm{LM}}
as
{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}
, and the list that combines
{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}
and the initial-state particles as
{\mathcal{H}}=\{a,b\}\cup{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}
.
The soft and collinear subtraction operators in Eq. (
11
) read

\overline{S}_{\mathfrak{m}}=\mathbb{1}-S_{\mathfrak{m}}\,,\qquad\overline{C}_{i\mathfrak{m}}=\mathbb{1}-C_{i\mathfrak{m}}\,,\qquad\mathcal{O}_{\text{NLO}}^{(\mathfrak{m})}=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{H}}}\overline{S}_{\mathfrak{m}}\overline{C}_{i\mathfrak{m}}\,\omega^{\mathfrak{m}i}\,.

(12)

The partition functions
\omega^{\mathfrak{m}i}
are defined in Ref.
[
1
]
, and allow us to treat one collinear singularity at a time.
We note that, in Eq. (
11
), the soft-limit operator
S_{\mathfrak{m}}
is only needed when
\mathfrak{m}
is a gluon.
Hence, to apply Eq. (
11
) for generic processes, we have to set the soft operator to zero when the unresolved parton is
a quark or an antiquark, i.e.,

S_{q}=S_{\bar{q}}=0\,.

(13)

Upon integrating over the phase space of the soft gluon,
the first term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
11
) can be written as

\big\langle S_{\mathfrak{m}}F_{\mathrm{R}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}]\big\rangle\equiv\big\langle S_{\mathfrak{m}}F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}_{g}]\big\rangle=[\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}]\big\langle I_{\rm S}(\epsilon)\cdot F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}\big\rangle\,,

Collinear limits require more attention.
As stated above, we are interested in those limits that lead to singularities proportional to the matrix element of
{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
, and we isolate such limits using the
projection operator

\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{P}}}_{\!\!{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}
.
Terms that involve the soft operator
S_{\mathfrak{m}}
are unaffected by
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{P}}}_{\!\!{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}
,
but some non-vanishing collinear limits are removed, e.g. the limit where the final state quark
\mathfrak{m}_{q}
becomes collinear to the initial state gluon
a_{g}
; thus

\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{P}}}_{\!\!{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}\big\langle C_{a\mathfrak{m}}\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}_{q}]\big\rangle=0\,.

(15)

This procedure allows us to select a subset of limits of the NLO matrix element that do not change the Born level configuration and whose combination is free of
1/\epsilon
singularities. This subset of limits does not remove
all
the singularities of the hard N(N)LO matrix element; in particular, those singularities whose limits include a different Born-level matrix element to that of
{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
are not subtracted. We do this because our main aim is to study the structure of final-state collinear limits in the presence of quarks rather than provide complete formulas for the physical real correction to the cross section.

With these preliminary remarks out of the way,
we can analyze collinear singularities related to the initial-state radiation.
The only unresolved parton that can develop a collinear singularity with the incoming gluon
a_{g}
and still contribute to
{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
is the final-state gluon
\mathfrak{m}_{g}
.
Then, following the steps detailed in Ref.
[
1
]
, we obtain

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{P}}}_{\!\!{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}\big\langle\overline{S}_{\mathfrak{m}}C_{a\mathfrak{m}}\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}F_{\mathrm{R}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}]\big\rangle&=\big\langle\overline{S}_{\mathfrak{m}}C_{a\mathfrak{m}}\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}_{g}]\big\rangle\\
&=\frac{[\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}]}{\epsilon}\Big[\big\langle\Gamma_{a,g}\,F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}\big\rangle+\big\langle{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathrm{gen}}}_{gg}\otimes F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}\big\rangle\Big]\;,\end{split}

(16)

where we have used the fact that
C_{a\mathfrak{m}}\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}=1

{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathrm{gen}}}_{\alpha\beta}\otimes F_{\mathrm{LM}}=\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}z\,{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathrm{gen}}}_{\alpha\beta}(z)\,\frac{F_{\mathrm{LM}}[z\cdot p_{a},p_{b};{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}]}{z}\,.

\Gamma_{a,f_{a}}
arising from the soft-collinear limits and of the (azimuthal averaged) collinear splitting functions
{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathrm{gen}}}_{\alpha\beta}
are reported in Eqs (

b_{q}
is analogous. We
find

\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{P}}}_{\!\!{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}\big\langle\overline{S}_{\mathfrak{m}}C_{b\mathfrak{m}}\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}F_{\mathrm{R}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}]\big\rangle=\frac{[\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}]}{\epsilon}\Big[\big\langle\Gamma_{b,q}\,F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}\big\rangle+\big\langle F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}\otimes{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathrm{gen}}}_{qq}\big\rangle\Big]\,,

(18)

where the right convolution
is defined as

F_{\mathrm{LM}}\otimes{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathrm{gen}}}_{\alpha\beta}=\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}z\,{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathrm{gen}}}_{\alpha\beta}(z)\,\frac{F_{\mathrm{LM}}[p_{a},z\cdot p_{b};{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}]}{z}\,.

We continue with the discussion of the final-state collinear limits, which correspond to
i\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}
in Eq. (
11
).
We note that, in this case,
C_{i\mathfrak{m}}\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}=E_{i}/(E_{i}+E_{\mathfrak{m}})=z_{i,\mathfrak{m}}

E_{i,\mathfrak{m}}
are energies of the corresponding partons.

[

.

To address it, we will consider the two contributions to the function
F_{\mathrm{R}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}]
in Eq. (

We start with
F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}_{g}]

\sum_{i\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}}\big\langle\overline{S}_{\mathfrak{m}}C_{i\mathfrak{m}}\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}_{g}]\big\rangle=\frac{[\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}]}{\epsilon}\sum_{i\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}}\big\langle\Gamma_{i,f_{i}\to f_{i}g}\,F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}\big\rangle\,,

(20)

where we have used the fact that clustering a collinear gluon with any parton does not change that parton’s flavor.
The
generalized final-state anomalous dimension

\Gamma_{i,f_{i}\to f_{i}g}
can be found in Eq. (
143
).
We note that these quantities generalize the final-state anomalous dimensions
\gamma_{z,f_{i}\to f_{i}g}^{22}(L_{i})
defined in Eq. (

)
and used in Ref.

\Gamma_{i,g\to gg}

Additionally,
\gamma_{z,f_{i}\to f_{i}g}^{22}(L_{i})
carries a subscript

to indicate that it is

.
We drop this subscript in the definition of
\Gamma_{i,f_{i}\to f_{i}f_{\mathfrak{m}}}
to lighten the notation.

We emphasize that the generalized final-state anomalous dimensions differ from the conventional collinear anomalous dimensions that arise from integrals of the collinear splitting functions without additional factors
z_{i,\mathfrak{m}}
.
To see how conventional collinear anomalous dimensions emerge, we need to consider the other contribution to the function
F_{\mathrm{R}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}]
, where a

quark
is potentially-unresolved.
Since in
F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}_{q}]
the only resolved final-state partons are gluons, the unresolved quark
\mathfrak{m}_{q}
has to be clustered with one of them.
The clustered parton is a quark, so that the number of resolved gluons decreases by one and the number of resolved quarks increases by one.
Working out the symmetry factors, we obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{i\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}}\big\langle C_{i\mathfrak{m}}\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}_{q}]\big\rangle=\frac{[\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}]}{\epsilon}\sum_{i\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}},q}}}\!\!\big\langle\Gamma_{i,q\to gq}\,F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}\big\rangle\,,\end{split}

(21)

where, on the right-hand side,
{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}},q}}
denotes the list of resolved final-state quarks.
In the current case, this notation is redundant as this list consists of a single parton, but we note that Eq. (
21
) holds independently of the number of final-state quarks.

Combining Eqs (
20
) and (
21
), we obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{i\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}}\big\langle\overline{S}_{\mathfrak{m}}C_{i\mathfrak{m}}\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}F_{\mathrm{R}}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}[\mathfrak{m}]\big\rangle=\frac{[\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}]}{\epsilon}\bigg\langle\bigg[\sum_{i\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}},g}}}\!\!{\Gamma_{i,g\to gg}}+\sum_{i\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}},q}}}\!\!\Gamma_{i,q}\bigg]F_{\mathrm{LM}}^{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}\bigg\rangle\,,\end{split}

(22)

where we define

\Gamma_{i,q}=\Gamma_{i,q\to qg}+\Gamma_{i,q\to gq}\,.

(23)

We emphasize that the quantity
\Gamma_{i,q}
in Eq. (

4
NNLO QCD corrections: general framework

In this section, we study the NNLO QCD corrections to the process

in Eq. (
1

Similarly to the NLO case, we discard all singular contributions

To compute the NNLO QCD corrections to

and
{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}

&{{\mathcal{A}}_{5}}:~a_{g}+b_{q}\to X+{N_{g}}\,g+q+{q^{\prime}}{{\bar{q}}^{\prime}}\,,\\

In order to cancel the

{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}_{\rm NNLO}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}={\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}_{\rm VV}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}+\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{P}}}_{\!\!{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}\Big[{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}}_{\mathrm{RV}}+n_{\mathrm{f}}{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}}_{\mathrm{RV}}+{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{4}}}_{\mathrm{RR}}+{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{5}},{\rm int}}_{\mathrm{RR}}+n_{\mathrm{f}}{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{5}}}_{\mathrm{RR}}+n_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{6}}}_{\mathrm{RR}}\Big]+{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\sigma}^{{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}_{\rm pdf}\,.

We note that the only singular limits of the process
{\cal A}_{5}
that will project onto

are those in which

and
\bar{q}^{\prime}
are either simultaneously soft, or are clustered together in the collinear regime (see the corresponding discussion after Eq. (

and
\bar{q}^{\prime}

, which does not appear with a factor of

{\cal A}_{0}

{{\mathcal{A}}_{n}}
under consideration.

.
Following Ref.
[
1
]
, we will refer to such partons as

and

[

]

[

]

5
Simplifications of the double-unresolved contributions

The formulas presented in the previous section
express the contributions to the NNLO corrections in terms of soft and collinear operators acting on the functions

In this section, we simplify the formulas obtained in the previous sections, focusing on the double-unresolved contributions, which contain the strongest singularities. This preliminary work will enable us to dramatically simplify the demonstration of the cancellation of
singularities, which we discuss in Sec.

In our analysis below, we follow Ref.

8
Conclusions

In this paper we discussed the calculation of the integrated
NNLO QCD subtraction terms
for the process
gq\to X+{N_{g}}\,g+q
, where the number of hard gluons
{N_{g}}
is

a parameter
and
X
is an arbitrary colorless final state. We worked
in the context of the nested soft-collinear subtraction scheme
[
17
]
and followed
closely a similar study of the all-gluon final state reported
in Ref.
[
1
]
.

We have found that the approach
described in Ref.
[
1
]

is sufficiently robust, and can be applied to a complex final state that contains both quarks and gluons.
In doing so, we have seen that physical quantities such as collinear anomalous dimensions arise when different singular configurations are properly combined before the subtraction terms are integrated over the unresolved phase space – a feature that is important for making computations not only more feasible but also physically transparent.
Finally, since
i) soft limits at NNLO depend only on the color charges of hard partons, ii) the collinear singularities factorize on the external parton legs and iii) an improved understanding of
the interplay of the different collinear limits has been achieved in this paper,
we believe that it
should be straightforward to extend these results to processes with an arbitrary number of jets at a hadron collider.

This section

We denote the color-charge operators with
\boldsymbol{T}_{i}
.
Squares of these operators are the Casimir operators of the corresponding representations of the QCD gauge group SU(3), i.e.

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{T}_{q}^{2}=\boldsymbol{T}_{\bar{q}}^{2}=C_{\mathrm{F}}=\frac{N_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}-1}{2N_{\mathrm{c}}}\,,\qquad\boldsymbol{T}_{g}^{2}=C_{\mathrm{A}}=N_{\mathrm{c}}\,,\end{split}

(126)

where
N_{\mathrm{c}}
is the number of colors.
Quark and gluon anomalous dimensions read

\gamma_{q}=\frac{3}{2}C_{\mathrm{F}}\,,\qquad\gamma_{g}=\frac{11}{6}\,C_{\mathrm{A}}-\frac{2}{3}T_{\mathrm{R}}n_{\mathrm{f}}\,,

(127)

where
T_{\mathrm{R}}=1/2
, and
n_{\mathrm{f}}
is the number of massless quark flavors.

The strong coupling is renormalized in the
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme. The relation between the bare and renormalized coupling constants reads

\displaystyle g_{\rm s,b}^{2}=g_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}S_{\epsilon}\mu^{2\epsilon}\left[1-\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)}{2\pi}\frac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)}{2\pi}\right)^{\!\!2}\left(\frac{\beta_{0}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{2\epsilon}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}^{3})\right]\,,

(128)

where
S_{\epsilon}=(4\pi)^{-\epsilon}e^{\epsilon\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}}
, and

\beta_{0}=\frac{11}{6}C_{\mathrm{A}}-\frac{2}{3}T_{\mathrm{R}}n_{\mathrm{f}}\,,\qquad\beta_{1}=\frac{17}{6}C_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}-\frac{5}{3}C_{\mathrm{A}}T_{\mathrm{R}}n_{\mathrm{f}}-C_{\mathrm{F}}T_{\mathrm{R}}n_{\mathrm{f}}\,.

(129)

We note that, at leading order, the gluon anomalous dimension and the coefficient of the QCD
\beta
-function coincide,
\gamma_{g}=\beta_{0}
.
Furthermore, it is convenient to define the following quantity

\displaystyle[\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}]\equiv{}

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)}{2\pi}\frac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\,,

(130)

related to the coupling constant.

In the main body of this paper, we have used
\epsilon
-dependent constants

\displaystyle N_{\epsilon}^{(b,d)}=\frac{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)\,\Gamma(1+2\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1+\epsilon)}=1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\epsilon^{2}+\order{\ep^3}\,,

Figure 1:
Graphical representation of the convention adopted for collinear factorization.
The top figure describes the final-state splitting process
[i\mathfrak{m}]^{*}\to i(z)+\mathfrak{m}({\bar{z}})
, where
z=1-E_{\mathfrak{m}}/E_{[i\mathfrak{m}]}
, and
{\bar{z}}=1-z
.
The bottom figure corresponds to the initial-state splitting process
i\to[i\mathfrak{m}]^{*}+\mathfrak{m}
, with
z=1-E_{\mathfrak{m}}/E_{i}
.
We note that in the case of FSR, the action of
C_{i\mathfrak{m}}
on
\Delta^{(\mathfrak{m})}
produces an extra factor of
z
, which does not occur in ISR.

Consider the final-state splitting process
[i\mathfrak{m}]^{*}\to i(z)+\mathfrak{m}(1-z)
, where
i
and
\mathfrak{m}
are two partons with flavors
f_{i}
and
f_{\mathfrak{m}}
, respectively.
The variable
z=1-E_{\mathfrak{m}}/E_{[i\mathfrak{m}]}
represents the energy fraction carried by parton
i
in the collinear splitting, while parton
\mathfrak{m}
takes the energy fraction
1-z
.
As for
[i\mathfrak{m}]
, it is the clustered “mother” parton.
We denote the spin-averaged final-state splitting functions as
P_{f_{[i\mathfrak{m}]}f_{i}}(z)
(cf. Figure
1
, above).
These functions read

\begin{split}P_{qq}(z)&=C_{\mathrm{F}}\left[\frac{1+z^{2}}{1-z}-\epsilon(1-z)\right]\,,\\
P_{qg}(z)&=C_{\mathrm{F}}\left[\frac{1+(1-z)^{2}}{z}-\epsilon z\right]\equiv P_{qq}(1-z)\,,\\
P_{gq}(z)&=T_{\mathrm{R}}\left[1-\frac{2z(1-z)}{1-\epsilon}\right]\,,\\
P_{gg}(z)&=2C_{\mathrm{A}}\left[\frac{z}{1-z}+\frac{1-z}{z}+z(1-z)\right]\,.\end{split}

(135)

Next, we consider the initial-state splitting process
i\to[i\mathfrak{m}]^{*}+\mathfrak{m}
, where
i
is now the incoming parton,
[i\mathfrak{m}]
is the parton that enters the hard scattering, and
\mathfrak{m}
is an outgoing parton.
The variable
z
corresponds to
z=1-E_{\mathfrak{m}}/E_{i}
.
In this case, we denote the initial-state splitting function, averaged over initial-state color and polarizations, as
P_{[i\mathfrak{m}]i,{\mathrm{i}}}(z)
(cf. Figure
1
, below).
These functions are related to the splittings in Eq. (
135
) by the following relations

\displaystyle P_{qq,\rm i}(z)

\displaystyle=-zP_{qq}(1/z)\equiv P_{qq}(z)\,,

\displaystyle P_{qg,\rm i}(z)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{2N_{c}}{2(1-\epsilon)(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\right]zP_{qg}(1/z)\equiv P_{gq}(z)\,,

(136)

\displaystyle P_{gq,\rm i}(z)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{2(1-\epsilon)(N_{c}^{2}-1)}{2N_{c}}\right]zP_{gq}(1/z)\equiv P_{qg}(z)\,,

\displaystyle P_{gg,\rm i}(z)

\displaystyle=-zP_{gg}(1/z)\equiv P_{gg}(z)\,.

We observe that the notation just described for initial- and final-state splittings, and illustrated in Figure
1
, applies to all tree-level and one-loop splittings used in this article.
Therefore, it must be applied to all functions defined in the remainder of this appendix, as well as those collected in
FinalResult.m
(cf. Table
1
) and
UsefulFunctions.m
(cf. Table
2
).

Finally, we report the conventional leading-order Altarelli-Parisi splitting functions that we use throughout the paper.
They read
[
94
]

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{qq}(z)

\displaystyle=C_{\mathrm{F}}\left[2{\mathcal{D}}_{0}(z)-(1+z)+\frac{3}{2}\delta(1-z)\right]\,,

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{qg}(z)

\displaystyle=T_{\mathrm{R}}\Big[(1-z)^{2}+z^{2}\Big]\,,

(137)

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{gq}(z)

\displaystyle=C_{\mathrm{F}}\left[\frac{1+(1-z)^{2}}{z}\right]\,,

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{gg}(z)

\displaystyle=2C_{\mathrm{A}}\left[{\mathcal{D}}_{0}(z)+z(1-z)+\frac{1}{z}-2\right]+\beta_{0}\delta(1-z)\,.

We also require the one-loop Altarelli-Parisi splitting functions; they can be found in Sec. 4.3 of Ref.
[
94
]
.
They are also given in the ancillary file
UsefulFunctions.m
, see Table
2
.
We point out that, for the
qq
splitting, we need the
non-singlet
splitting function from which the analog of the interference contribution of identical quarks has been subtracted.

Generalized splitting functions and anomalous dimensions

A.3.1
Tree-level

Phase-space remnants and
damping factors introduce
additional factors to integrands over splitting functions that we require.
For this reason, it is convenient to define generalized splitting functions and their integrals.

For the initial-state splitting where parton
\mathfrak{m}
becomes collinear to an initial-state parton
a
of flavor
f_{a}
, the following integral appears

E_{a}^{-k\epsilon}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}z\,(1-z)^{-k\epsilon}P_{f_{[a\mathfrak{m}]}f_{a},{\mathrm{i}}}(z)\,F(z)\,.

(138)

The initial-state splitting functions
P_{\alpha\beta,\rm i}
are given in Eq. (
136
), and the function
F(z)
typically contains the relevant matrix element squared.
Such integrals may contain infrared divergences which need to be extracted.
To facilitate this, we define a new splitting function

{\mathcal{P}}_{f_{[a\mathfrak{m}]}f_{a}}^{(k)}(z,E_{a})=\overline{S}_{z}\Big[(1-z)^{-k\epsilon}P_{f_{[a\mathfrak{m}]}f_{a},{\mathrm{i}}}(z)\Big]-2\delta_{f_{[a\mathfrak{m}]}f_{a}}\,\boldsymbol{T}_{f_{[a\mathfrak{m}]}}^{2}\frac{1-e^{-k\epsilon L_{a}}}{k\epsilon}\delta(1-z)\,.

(139)

Here,
\overline{S}_{z}=\mathbb{1}-S_{z}
, where
S_{z}
subtracts the soft singularity at
z=1
.
The relevant powers of phase-space factors
(1-z)^{-\epsilon}
are
k=2
at NLO, and
k=4
at NNLO.
Explicit expressions for the functions
{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha\beta}^{(k)}
in Eq. (
139
) are provided in the ancillary file
UsefulFunctions.m
, see Table
2
.
We note that we omit the superscript for
k=2
when writing the generalized splitting functions and their integrals to simplify the notation.
Furthermore, we point out that functions
{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha\beta}^{(k)}(z,E_{a})
in Eq. (
139
) are, in fact, energy-independent if
\alpha\not=\beta
.

For the manipulations described in the main body of the paper, it is
convenient to rewrite the generalized splitting functions as follows

-\bigg[\left(\frac{2E_{a}}{\mu}\right)^{\!\!-2\epsilon}\frac{\Gamma^{2}(1-\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}\bigg]^{\frac{k}{2}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha\beta}^{(k)}(z,E_{a})=\Gamma_{a,\alpha}^{(k)}\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}\;\delta(1-z)+{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha\beta}^{(k),\text{gen}}(z,E_{a})\,,

(140)

with

\displaystyle\Gamma_{a,\alpha}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\bigg[\left(\frac{2E_{a}}{\mu}\right)^{\!\!-2\epsilon}\frac{\Gamma^{2}(1-\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}\bigg]^{\frac{k}{2}}\left[\gamma_{\alpha}+2\boldsymbol{T}_{\alpha}^{2}\frac{1-e^{-k\epsilon L_{a}}}{k\epsilon}\right]\,,

(141)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha\beta}^{(k),\text{gen}}(z,E_{a})

\displaystyle=\bigg[\left(\frac{2E_{a}}{\mu}\right)^{\!\!-2\epsilon}\frac{\Gamma^{2}(1-\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}\bigg]^{\frac{k}{2}}\left[-\hat{P}^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta}(z)+\epsilon\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha\beta}^{(k),\text{fin}}(z)\right]\,.

(142)

Here,
\hat{P}^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta}
are the Altarelli-Parisi splitting functions given in Eq. (
137
), while
{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha\beta}^{(k),\text{fin}}(z)
corresponds to the
\epsilon
-expansion of
-{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha\beta}^{(k)}(z,E_{a}=0)
starting with
\order{\ep}
.
Explicit expressions for quantities that appear in Eqs (
141
) and (
142
) are provided in
UsefulFunctions.m
, see Table
2
.

We continue with the discussion of the final-state splittings. Then, if the final-state parton
\mathfrak{m}
becomes collinear to the final-state parton
i
of flavour
f_{i}
, the generalized final-state anomalous dimension reads

\Gamma_{i,f_{[i\mathfrak{m}]}\to f_{i}f_{\mathfrak{m}}}^{(k)}=\bigg[\left(\frac{2E_{i}}{\mu}\right)^{\!\!-2\epsilon}\frac{\Gamma^{2}(1-\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}\bigg]^{\frac{k}{2}}\gamma_{z,f_{[i\mathfrak{m}]}\to f_{i}f_{\mathfrak{m}}}^{2k}(L_{i})\,,

(143)

where

\begin{split}\gamma_{g(z),f_{[i\mathfrak{m}]}\to f_{i}f_{\mathfrak{m}}}^{nk}(L_{i})=&-\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}z\,\overline{S}_{z}\Big[z^{-n\epsilon}(1-z)^{-k\epsilon}g(z)\,P_{f_{[i\mathfrak{m}]}f_{i}}(z)\Big]\\
&+2\delta_{f_{[i\mathfrak{m}]}f_{i}}\,\boldsymbol{T}^{2}_{f_{[i\mathfrak{m}]}}\frac{1-e^{-k\epsilon L_{i}}}{k\epsilon}g(1)\,.\end{split}

(144)

The splitting functions
P_{\alpha\beta}
can be found in Eq. (
135
), while the explicit expressions for the quantities defined in Eqs (
143
) and (
144
) are provided in
UsefulFunctions.m
, see Table
2
.

Finally, we define “physical” combinations of quark and gluon collinear anomalous dimensions

\Gamma_{i,q}^{(k)}=\Gamma_{i,q\to qg}^{(k)}+\Gamma_{i,q\to gq}^{(k)}\,,\qquad\Gamma_{i,g}^{(k)}=\Gamma_{i,g\to gg}^{(k)}+2n_{\mathrm{f}}\,\Gamma_{i,g\to q{\bar{q}}}^{(k)}\,,

(145)

which appear in the operators
I_{\rm C}
and
I_{\rm T}
.

A.3.2
Spin-correlations

To describe the spin-correlated contributions arising from sectors
\theta^{(b)}
and
\theta^{(d)}
, we require the following
integrals

\begin{split}\delta_{\mathfrak{m}}(\epsilon)&=\frac{N_{\epsilon}^{(b,d)}E_{\rm max}^{4\epsilon}}{2}\int_{E_{\rm max}}^{2E_{\rm max}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}E_{[\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}]}}{E_{[\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}]}^{1+4\epsilon}}\int_{1-\xi}^{\xi}{\mathrm{d}}z\,[z(1-z)]^{-2\epsilon}\big[P_{g\mathfrak{m}}(z)+\epsilon\,P_{g\mathfrak{m}}^{\perp,{\mathrm{r}}}(z)\big]\,,\\
\delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\perp}(\epsilon)&=\frac{N_{\epsilon}^{(b,d)}E_{\rm max}^{4\epsilon}}{2}\int_{E_{\rm max}}^{2E_{\rm max}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}E_{[\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}]}}{E_{[\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}]}^{1+4\epsilon}}\int_{1-\xi}^{\xi}{\mathrm{d}}z\,[z(1-z)]^{-2\epsilon}P_{g\mathfrak{m}}^{\perp}(z)\,,\end{split}

(146)

where
\xi=E_{\rm max}/E_{[\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}]}
,

\mathfrak{m}
can be either a quark or a gluon, and
the transversal splitting functions are defined as follows

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} P_{gg}^{\bot}(z)&=4C_{\mathrm{A}}(1-\epsilon)\,z(1-z)\,,&\quad P_{gg}^{\bot,{\mathrm{r}}}(z)&=2C_{\mathrm{A}}\,z(1-z)(1-2\epsilon)\,,\\
P_{gq}^{\bot}(z)&=-4T_{\mathrm{R}}\,z(1-z)\,,&\quad P_{gq}^{\bot,{\mathrm{r}}}(z)&=-2T_{\mathrm{R}}\,z(1-z)\frac{1-2\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\,.\end{aligned}

(147)

The
\epsilon
-expansions of the
\delta
-integrals in Eq. (
146
) are reported in
UsefulFunctions.m
, see Table
2
.
Note that we combine the gluon and quark components as

\delta(\epsilon)=\delta_{g}(\epsilon)+2n_{\mathrm{f}}\,\delta_{q}(\epsilon)\,,\qquad\delta^{\perp}(\epsilon)=\delta_{g}^{\perp}(\epsilon)+2n_{\mathrm{f}}\,\delta_{q}^{\perp}(\epsilon)\,.

(148)

Additionally, we find it convenient to introduce the following notation for the integrals

\gamma_{\bot,g\to\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}^{22}=-\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}z\;\frac{P_{g\mathfrak{m}}^{\bot}(z)}{[z(1-z)]^{2\epsilon}}\,,\qquad\gamma_{\bot,g\to\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}}^{22,{\mathrm{r}}}=-\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}z\;\frac{P_{g\mathfrak{m}}^{\bot,{\mathrm{r}}}(z)}{[z(1-z)]^{2\epsilon}}\,,

(149)

where
(\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n})=(gg)
or
(q{\bar{q}})
. “Physical” combinations of anomalous dimensions are defined as follows:

\gamma_{\bot,g}^{22}=\gamma_{\bot,g\to gg}^{22}+2n_{\mathrm{f}}\,\gamma_{\bot,g\to q{\bar{q}}}^{22}\,,\qquad\gamma_{\bot,g}^{22,{\mathrm{r}}}=\gamma_{\bot,g\to gg}^{22,{\mathrm{r}}}+2n_{\mathrm{f}}\,\gamma_{\bot,g\to q{\bar{q}}}^{22,{\mathrm{r}}}\,.

(150)

In the
\epsilon=0
limit, they read

\gamma_{\bot,g}^{22}=-\frac{2}{3}(C_{\mathrm{A}}-2n_{\mathrm{f}}T_{\mathrm{R}})\,,\qquad\gamma_{\bot,g}^{22,{\mathrm{r}}}=-\frac{1}{3}(C_{\mathrm{A}}-2n_{\mathrm{f}}T_{\mathrm{R}})\,,

(151)

and are reported in
FinalResult.m
, see Table
1
.

One-loop

A.4
Definitions and relevant properties of
I
-operators

In Ref.
[
1
]
, we have introduced
virtual, soft, and collinear operators, and we employ similar operators in this paper as well. The virtual operator is defined as

I_{\rm V}(\epsilon)=\overline{I}_{1}(\epsilon)+\overline{I}_{1}^{\dagger}(\epsilon)\,,

(160)

where
[
87
]

\overline{I}_{1}(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(ij)}\frac{{\mathcal{V}}_{i}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\epsilon)}{\boldsymbol{T}_{i}^{2}}\,\boldsymbol{T}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{T}_{j}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{2p_{i}\cdot p_{j}}\right)^{\!\!\epsilon}e^{i\pi\lambda_{ij}\epsilon}\,,\qquad{\mathcal{V}}_{i}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\epsilon)=\frac{\boldsymbol{T}_{i}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\epsilon}\,.

(161)

In Eq. (
161
),
\gamma_{i}
are the anomalous dimensions that can be found in (
127
), the sum goes over the unordered pairs of external particles
i,j
with
i\neq j
,
and
\lambda_{ij}
are constants which are equal to 1 if both
i
and
j
are incoming or outgoing, and to
0
otherwise.
The soft operators which appear in the double-real and in the real-virtual corrections read

\begin{split}I_{\rm S}(\epsilon)&=-\frac{(2E_{\rm max}/\mu)^{-2\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{(ij)}\eta_{ij}^{-\epsilon}K_{ij}\,(\boldsymbol{T}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{T}_{j})\,,\\
\widetilde{I}_{\rm S}(2\epsilon)&=-\frac{(2E_{\rm max}/\mu)^{-4\epsilon}}{(2\epsilon)^{2}}\sum_{(ij)}\eta_{ij}^{-2\epsilon}\widetilde{K}_{ij}\,(\boldsymbol{T}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{T}_{j})\,,\end{split}

(162)

where
\eta_{ij}=(1-\cos\theta_{ij})/2
, and

\begin{split}K_{ij}&=\frac{\Gamma^{2}(1-\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}\eta_{ij}^{1+\epsilon}{{}_{2}F_{1}}(1,1,1-\epsilon,1-\eta_{ij})\,,\\
\widetilde{K}_{ij}&=\frac{\Gamma^{2}(1-2\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-4\epsilon)}\,\eta_{ij}^{1+3\epsilon}{{}_{2}F_{1}}(1+\epsilon,1+\epsilon,1-\epsilon,1-\eta_{ij})\,.\end{split}

(163)

A useful relation between
I_{\rm S}
and
\widetilde{I}_{\rm S}
is

\widetilde{I}_{\rm S}(2\epsilon)=I_{\rm S}(2\epsilon)+\order{\ep}\,.

(164)

The Laurent expansions of
I_{\rm V}(\epsilon)
,
I_{\rm S}(\epsilon)
, and
\widetilde{I}_{\rm S}(2\epsilon)
can be found in Appendix A.5 of Ref.
[
1
]
.

The collinear operators which appear in the double-real and in the real-virtual corrections read, respectively,

I_{\rm C}^{(k)}(\epsilon)=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{H}}}\frac{\Gamma_{i,f_{i}}^{(k)}}{\epsilon\,(k/2)}\,,\qquad\widetilde{I}_{\rm C}(2\epsilon)=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{H}}}\frac{\Gamma_{i,f_{i}}^{\rm 1L}}{2\epsilon}\,,

(165)

where generalized initial- and final-state anomalous dimensions are given in Eqs (
141
) and (
143
) for
I_{\rm C}^{(k)}
, and in Eqs (
154
) and (
157
) for
\widetilde{I}_{\rm C}
.
We note that the following relations hold

\widetilde{I}_{\rm C}(2\epsilon)=I_{\rm C}(2\epsilon)+\order{\ep^0}\,,\qquad I_{\rm C}^{(4)}(\epsilon)=I_{\rm C}(2\epsilon)+\order{\ep^0}\,.

(166)

Finally, the
\epsilon
-finite operator
I_{\rm T}

is defined as follows

I_{\rm T}(\epsilon)=I_{\rm V}(\epsilon)+I_{\rm S}(\epsilon)+I_{\rm C}(\epsilon)\,.

(167)

Its expansion in powers of
\epsilon
reads

I_{\rm T}(\epsilon)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\epsilon^{n}I_{\rm T}^{(n)}\,,

(168)

where the
n=0,1
coefficients are given by

\displaystyle I_{\rm T}^{(0)}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{(ij)}(\boldsymbol{T}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{T}_{j})\bigg[\left(2L_{\rm max}+\frac{1}{2}\log\eta_{ij}\right)\log\eta_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}L_{ij}\left(L_{ij}+\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\boldsymbol{T}_{i}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{Li}_{2}(1-\eta_{ij})+\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\lambda_{ij}\bigg]+\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{H}}}\boldsymbol{T}_{i}^{2}\bigg[2L_{\rm max}^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\Big(2\widetilde{L}_{i}\gamma_{i}^{22,(0)}-\gamma_{i}^{22,(1)}\Big)\frac{\theta_{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}}{\boldsymbol{T}_{i}^{2}}

(169)

\displaystyle-2\bigg(L_{i}^{2}+2L_{i}\widetilde{L}_{i}+\widetilde{L}_{i}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\boldsymbol{T}_{i}^{2}}\bigg)\bar{\theta}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle I_{\rm T}^{(1)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{(ij)}(\boldsymbol{T}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{T}_{j})\bigg[\frac{1}{6}\big(L_{ij}^{3}+\log^{3}\eta_{ij}\big)+2L_{\rm max}^{2}\log\eta_{ij}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\lambda_{ij}L_{ij}

\displaystyle+\bigg(L_{\rm max}-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-\eta_{ij})\bigg)\log^{2}\eta_{ij}+2L_{\rm max}\,\mathrm{Li}_{2}(1-\eta_{ij})-\mathrm{Li}_{3}(\eta_{ij})

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Li}_{3}(1-\eta_{ij})+\frac{\gamma_{i}}{2\boldsymbol{T}_{i}^{2}}\big(L_{ij}^{2}-\pi^{2}\lambda_{ij}\big)\bigg]+\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{H}}}\boldsymbol{T}_{i}^{2}\bigg\{-\frac{4}{3}L_{\rm max}^{3}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}L_{\rm max}-3\zeta_{3}

(170)

\displaystyle+\bigg[\bigg(2\widetilde{L}_{i}^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\bigg)\gamma_{i}^{22,(0)}-2\widetilde{L}_{i}\gamma_{i}^{22,(1)}+\gamma_{i}^{22,(2)}\bigg]\frac{\theta_{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}}{\boldsymbol{T}_{i}^{2}}+\bigg[\frac{4}{3}L_{i}^{3}+4L_{i}^{2}\widetilde{L}_{i}+4L_{i}\widetilde{L}_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}L_{i}+\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\boldsymbol{T}_{i}^{2}}\bigg(2\widetilde{L}_{i}^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\bigg)\bigg]\bar{\theta}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}\bigg\}\,.

To write the above equations, we have used

\theta_{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\,,\end{cases}\qquad\qquad\bar{\theta}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}=1-\theta_{{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{f}}}}\,,

(171)

together with the expansion coefficients of the collinear anomalous dimensions
\gamma_{i}^{22}(L_{i})
, defined as

\gamma_{i}^{22}(L_{i})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\epsilon^{n}\,\gamma_{i}^{22,(n)}(L_{i})\,,\qquad i=g,q\,.

(172)

We note that in Eqs (
169
) and (
170
), the dependence of the coefficients
\gamma_{i}^{22,(n)}
on the logarithms
L_{i}
is not shown explicitly.

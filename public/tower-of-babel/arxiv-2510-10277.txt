L L -functions of elliptic curves in ring class extensions of real quadratic fields via regularized theta liftings
Jeanine Van Order

Abstract
. We derive new integral presentations for central derivative values of L L -functions of elliptic curves defined over the rationals, basechanged to a real quadratic field K K , twisted by ring class characters of K K in terms of sums along “geodesics” corresponding to the class group of K K of automorphic Green’s functions for certain Hirzebruch-Zagier-like arithmetic divisors on Hilbert modular surfaces. We also relate these sums to Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer constants and periods.

\displaystyle\operatorname{Spin}(V_{A,j})({\bf{Q}})\backslash\operatorname{Spin}(V_{A,j})({\bf{A}})\cong\operatorname{SO}(V_{A,j})({\bf{Q}})\backslash\operatorname{SO}(V_{A,j})({\bf{A}})\cong{\bf{A}}_{K}^{\times}/{\bf{A}}^{\times}K^{\times}.

Hence, we can view the ring class character
\chi:{\bf{A}}_{K}^{\times}/{\bf{A}}^{\times}K^{\times}\rightarrow{\bf{C}}^{\times}
as an automorphic
representation of
\operatorname{SO}(V_{A,j})({\bf{A}})
. In a similar way, we have natural identifications

\displaystyle\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A,j})({\bf{Q}})\backslash\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A,j})({\bf{A}})\cong\operatorname{GO}(V_{A,j})({\bf{Q}})\backslash\operatorname{GO}(V_{A,j})({\bf{A}})\cong{\bf{A}}_{K}^{\times}/K^{\times}.

Here, strictly speaking, we fix one of the two connected components
\operatorname{GO}^{\pm}(V_{A,j})
of
\operatorname{GO}(V_{A,j})
so that

\displaystyle\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A,j})({\bf{Q}})\backslash\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A,j})({\bf{A}})\cong\operatorname{GO}^{\pm}(V_{A,j})({\bf{Q}})\backslash\operatorname{GO}^{\pm}(V_{A,j})({\bf{A}})\cong{\bf{A}}_{K}^{\times}/K^{\times}.

We refer to the discussion in
[
38
, Theorem 2.3.3]
for more background leading to this identification.

4.2.
Hermitian symmetric domains

The symmetric spaces associated to each quadratic space
(V_{A},Q_{A})
are hermitian symmetric domains,
i.e. have a complex structure. We have the following equivalent realizations.

4.2.1.
The Grassmannian model

Recall we let
D(V_{A})=D^{\pm}(V_{A})=\left\{z\subset V_{A}({\bf{R}}):\dim(z)=2,Q_{A}|_{z}<0\right\}

denote the Grassmannian of oriented hyperplanes of
V_{A}({\bf{R}})
on which
Q_{A}
is negative definite.

4.2.2.
The projective model

Note that
D(V_{A})
can be identified with the complex surface

\displaystyle Q(V_{A})

\displaystyle=\left\{w\in V_{A}({\bf{C}}):(w,w)_{A}=0,(w,\overline{w})_{A}<0\right\}/{\bf{C}}^{\times}\subset{\bf{P}}(V_{A}({\bf{C}}))

via the map

(13)

\displaystyle D^{\pm}(V_{A})\longrightarrow Q(V_{A}),\quad z\longmapsto v_{1}-iv_{2}=w,

for
v_{1},v_{2}
a properly-oriented standard basis of
D^{\pm}(V_{A})
with
(v_{1},v_{1})_{A}=(v_{2},v_{2})_{A}=-1

and
(v_{1},v_{2})_{A}=0
. We refer to this identifications
D^{\pm}(V_{A})\cong Q(V_{A})
as the
projective model
.

4.2.3.
The tube domain model

Fix a Witt decomposition
V_{A}({\bf{R}})=V_{A,0}+{\bf{R}}\cdot e+{\bf{R}}\cdot f
,
with
e
and
f
chosen so that
(e,e)_{A}=(f,f)_{A}=0
and
(e,f)_{A}=1
,
and
C(V_{A})=\left\{y\in V_{A,0}:(y,y)_{A}<0\right\}
its negative cone.
We can then identify
D^{\pm}(V_{A})\cong Q(V_{A})
with the corresponding tube domain

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(V_{A})

\displaystyle:=\left\{z\in V_{A,0}({\bf{C}}):\Im(z_{0})\in C(V_{A})\right\}\cong\mathfrak{H}^{2}

via the map
\mathcal{H}(V_{A})\longrightarrow V_{A}({\bf{C}})
sending
z\longmapsto w(z):=z+e-q_{A}(z)f

composed with the projection to
Q(V_{A})
. We call
\mathcal{H}(V_{A})\subset V_{0,A}({\bf{C}})\cong{\bf{C}}^{2}
the
tube domain model
.

4.3.
Spin Shimura varieties

We now describe the Shimura varieties associated with each group
\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})
.
Here, we take
U_{A}\subset\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})
to be any compact open subgroup.

4.3.1.
Orbifolds

Consider the Shimura varieties
X_{U_{A}}=\operatorname{Sh}_{U_{A}}(D^{\pm}(V_{A}),\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A}))
with complex points

\displaystyle X_{U_{A}}({\bf{C}})=\operatorname{Sh}_{U_{A}}(D^{\pm}(V_{A}),\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A}))({\bf{C}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{Q}})\backslash\left(D^{\pm}(V_{A})\times\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})/U_{A}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{Q}})\backslash\left(\mathfrak{H}^{2}\times\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})/U_{A}\right).

Note that this is a quasiprojective surface defined over
{\bf{Q}}
.
Via the exceptional isomorphism
(\ref{accidentV_A})
with choice of level
(\ref{level})
, we obtain the identification

X_{U_{A}}({\bf{C}})\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2}({\bf{Q}})^{2}\backslash\left(\mathfrak{H}^{2}\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}({\bf{A}}_{f})^{2}/\zeta(U_{A})\right)

with the two-fold product
Y_{0}(N)\times Y_{0}(N)
of the noncompactified modular curve
Y_{0}(N)=\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathfrak{H}
.

4.3.2.
Decompositions

Fix a (finite) set of representatives
h_{j}\in\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{Q}})\backslash\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})/U_{A}
so that

(14)

\displaystyle\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}})

\displaystyle=\coprod_{j}\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{Q}})\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{R}})^{0}h_{j}U_{A},

where
\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{R}})^{0}
denotes the identity component of

\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{R}})\cong\operatorname{GSpin}(2,2)
.
This gives us the corresponding decomposition of the Shimura variety as

(15)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sh}_{U_{A}}(D^{\pm}(V_{A}),\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A}))

\displaystyle=\coprod_{j}X_{A,j},\quad\text{where}~~X_{A,j}=\Gamma_{j}\backslash D^{\pm}(V_{A})

for the arithmetic subgroup

\Gamma_{A,j}=\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{Q}})\cap\left(\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{R}})^{0}h_{j}Uh_{j}^{-1}\right)
.
Chosing
U_{A}
according to
(\ref{level})
via
(\ref{accidentV_A})
, this simply recovers the
decomposition
X_{U_{A}}=\operatorname{Sh}_{U_{A}}(\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A}),D^{\pm}(V_{A}))\cong Y_{0}(N)\times Y_{0}(N)
.

4.3.3.
Special divisors

We now consider special (arithmetic) divisors on
X_{U_{A}}=\operatorname{Sh}_{U_{A}}(D^{\pm}(V_{A}),\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A}))
.
Given a vector
x\in V_{A}({\bf{Q}})
with
Q_{A}(x)>0
, let
V_{A,x}:=x^{\perp}\subset V_{A}
denote the orthogonal complement, with

\displaystyle D(V_{A})_{x}=D^{\pm}(V_{A})_{x}=\{z\in D^{\pm}(V_{A}):x\perp z\}.

Let
\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A,x})({\bf{A}}_{f})
denote the stabilizer in
\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})
of

.
We have a natural map defined on
h\in\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})
by

(16)

\displaystyle\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A,x})({\bf{Q}})\backslash D^{\pm}(V_{A})_{x}\times\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A,x})({\bf{A}}_{f})/\left(\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A,x})({\bf{A}}_{f})\cap hU_{A}h^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sh}_{U_{A}}(\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A}),D^{\pm}(V_{A}))

\displaystyle[z,h_{1}]

\displaystyle\longmapsto[z,h_{1}h].

Definition 4.1
.

Given
x\in V_{A}({\bf{Q}})
with
Q_{A}(x)>0
and
h\in\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})
, let

Z_{A}(x,h)=Z_{A}(x,h,U_{A})
denote the image of the map
(\ref{natural-x})
. Here, we drop the compact open subgroup

U_{A}\subset\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})
from the notation when the context is clear.

This image
Z_{A}(x,h)=Z_{A}(x,h,U_{A})
determines a special codimension-
1
cycle on
X_{U_{A}}
defined over
{\bf{Q}}
.
As explained in
[

\lx@sectionsign 1
]
and
[

, these cycles satisfy many nice functorian properties.
To illustrate a couple of relevant properties, let is for a given
m\in{\bf{Q}}_{>0}
write
\Omega_{A,m}({\bf{Q}})
to denote the quadric

\displaystyle\Omega_{A,m}({\bf{Q}})

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in V_{A}:Q_{A}(x)=m\right\}.

If
\Omega_{A,m}({\bf{Q}})
is not empty, we fix a point
x_{0}\in\Omega_{A,m}({\bf{Q}})
.
The corresponding finite adelic points
\Omega_{A,m}({\bf{A}}_{f})
determine a closed subgroup of
V_{A}({\bf{A}}_{f})
.
Given a Schwartz function
\varphi_{f}=\otimes_{v<\infty}\varphi_{v}\in\mathcal{S}(V_{A}({\bf{A}}_{f}))^{U_{A}}

(17)

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{f})\cap\Omega_{A,m}({\bf{A}}_{f})

\displaystyle=\coprod_{r}U_{A}\cdot\zeta_{r}^{-1}\cdot x_{0}

for some finite set of representatives
\zeta_{r}\in\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})
.
Via
(\ref{support})
, we define the analytic divisor

(18)

\displaystyle Z_{A}(\varphi_{f},m,U_{A})

\displaystyle=\sum_{r}\varphi_{f}(\zeta_{r}^{-1}\cdot x_{0})Z_{A}(x_{0},\zeta_{r},U_{A}).

If

is an inclusion of compact open subgroups of
\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})
with
\operatorname{pr}:X_{U_{A}^{\prime}}\rightarrow X_{U_{A}}

the corresponding covering of Shimura varieties, we have the projection formula

\displaystyle\operatorname{pr}^{*}Z_{A}(\varphi_{f},m,U_{A})

\displaystyle=Z_{A}(\varphi_{f},m,U_{A}^{\prime}).

Hence, the analytic divisor is defined on the Shimura variety
X=\varprojlim_{U_{A}}X_{U_{A}}
,
and so we are justified in dropping the reference to the compact open subgroup

from the notation.
We can also consider the right multiplication by

which determines a morphism

\displaystyle[h]:X_{U_{A}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow X_{hU_{A}h^{-1}}.

This morphism
[h]
is defined over
{\bf{Q}}
, and its pushforward
[h]_{*}
satisfies the relation

\displaystyle[h]_{*}:Z_{A}(\varphi_{f},m,U_{A})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow Z(\omega_{L_{A}}(h)\varphi_{f},m,hU_{A}h^{-1}),\quad~~~\text{ where $\omega_{L_{A}}(h)\varphi_{f}(x)=\varphi_{f}(h^{-1}x)$}.

In this way, we can deduce that these analytic divisors
(\ref{AD})
are compatible with Hecke operators on
X_{U_{A}}
.
Moreover, with respect to the decomposition
(\ref{Sh-decomp})
, the result of
[

(cf.
[

\lx@sectionsign 1

decomposes as

\displaystyle Z_{A}(\varphi_{f},m,U_{A})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}Z_{A,j}(\varphi_{f},m,U_{A}),

where for each factor

we write

\displaystyle Z_{A,j}(\varphi_{f},m,U_{A})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{x\in\Omega_{A,m}({\bf{Q}})\bmod\Gamma_{A,j}}\varphi_{f}(h_{j}^{-1}x)\operatorname{pr}_{j}(D^{\pm}(V_{A})_{x})

for
\operatorname{pr}_{j}:D^{\pm}(V_{A})\longrightarrow\Gamma_{A,j}\backslash D^{\pm}(V_{A})
the natural projection.

Definition 4.2
.

Given a positive rational number

4.3.4.
Relation to Hirzebruch-Zagier divisors

Suppose we fix the level

Note that these special divisors

can be viewed as embeddings of modular curves into the surface

\displaystyle T_{m}=T_{m}(L_{A})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\mu\in L_{A}^{\vee}/L_{A}}Z_{A}(\mu,m/\Delta).

4.3.5.
Geodesic spaces

Each of the subspaces
(V_{A,j},Q_{A,j})
of signature
(1,1)
gives rise to a geodesic set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}(V_{A,j})

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A,j})({\bf{Q}})\backslash\left(D^{\pm}(V_{A,j})\times\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A,j})({\bf{A}}_{f})/U_{A,j}\right),\quad U_{A,j}:=U_{A}\cap\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A,j})({\bf{A}}_{f}).

To describe this, we again fix the level structure
U_{A}\subset\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})
as in Corollary
3.4
.
We can embed each subset
\mathfrak{G}(V_{A,2})
as geodesic on some modular curve
C\subset X_{U_{A}}\cong Y_{0}(N)^{2}
, and in this way

(20)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}(V_{A,2})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow C\subset X_{U_{A}}=\operatorname{Sh}_{U_{A}}(D^{\pm}(V_{A}),\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A}))\cong Y_{0}(N)\times Y_{0}(N).

That is, let us now consider the norm form
Q_{\mathfrak{a}}(z)={\bf{N}}_{K/{\bf{Q}}}(z)/{\bf{N}}\mathfrak{a}
as a binary quadratic form

\displaystyle Q_{A,2}(X,Y):={\bf{N}}_{K/{\bf{Q}}}(X+z_{\mathfrak{a}}Y)/{\bf{N}}\mathfrak{a}=a_{\mathfrak{a}}X^{2}+b_{\mathfrak{a}}XY+c_{\mathfrak{a}}Y^{2}.

The roots

\mathfrak{Z}^{\pm}_{\mathfrak{a}}=(-b_{\mathfrak{a}}\pm\sqrt{\Delta})/2a_{\mathfrak{a}}

of the quadratic polynomial

or

determine endpoints of a geodesic arc
\gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}
in
\mathfrak{H}
.
Hence via

, we can view

as a “geodesic” subset of

\displaystyle Y_{0}(N)\hookrightarrow Y_{0}(N)\times Y_{0}(N)\cong\operatorname{Sh}_{U_{A}}(\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A}),D^{\pm}(V_{A})).

In the same way, viewing each of the Hirzebruch-Zagier special divisors

as a modular curve
C_{\overline{U}_{A}}
, we view the geodesic sets
\mathfrak{G}(V_{A,2})
as subsets embedded through this modular curve

\displaystyle C_{\overline{U}_{A}}=Z_{A}(\mu,m)\subset X_{U_{A}}\cong Y_{0}(N)\times Y_{0}(N).

4.3.6.
Arithmetic automorphic forms

Let
\mathcal{L}_{D(V_{A})}=\mathcal{L}_{D^{\pm}(V_{A})}
denote the restriction to
D(V_{A})\cong Q(V_{A})
of the tautological bundle on
{\bf{P}}(V_{A}({\bf{C}}))
.
The natural action of the orthogonal group
\operatorname{O}(V_{A})({\bf{R}})
on
V_{A}({\bf{C}})
induces one of the connected
component of the identity
\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{R}})^{0}
of
\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{R}})

on
\mathcal{L}_{D(V_{A})}
. Hence, there is a holomorphic line bundle

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{A}=\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{Q}})\backslash\left(\mathcal{L}_{D(V_{A})}\times\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})/U_{A}\right)\longrightarrow X_{U_{A}}.

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{A}
has a canonical model over
{\bf{Q}}
by
[
24
]
.
We define a hermitian metric
h_{\mathcal{L}_{D(V_{A})}}
on
\mathcal{L}_{D(V_{A})}
by

\displaystyle h_{\mathcal{L}_{D(V_{A})}}(w_{1},w_{2})_{A}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\cdot(w_{1},\overline{w}_{2})_{A}.

This metric is invariant under the action by
\operatorname{O}(V_{A})({\bf{R}})
, and hence descends to
\mathcal{L}_{A}
.
The map
z\mapsto w(z)
used to identify
D(V_{A})\cong\mathcal{H}_{\pm}(V_{A})\cong\mathfrak{H}^{2}

can be viewed as a nowhere vanishing section of
\mathcal{L}_{D(V_{A})}
of norm

\displaystyle||w(z)||_{A}=-\frac{1}{2}\cdot(w(z),\overline{w}(z))_{A}=-(y,y)_{A}=:|y|^{2}_{A}.

For
h\in\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{R}})
, we have that
h\cdot w(z)=w(hz)\cdot j(h,z)
for a holomorphic automorphy factor

\displaystyle j:\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{R}})\times D(V_{A})\longrightarrow{\bf{C}}^{\times}.

In this way, holomorphic sections of
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{\otimes l}
for
l\in\frac{1}{2}{\bf{Z}}
can be viewed as holomorphic functions

\displaystyle\Psi:D(V_{A})\times\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})\longrightarrow{\bf{C}}

of

and
h\in\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})
satisfying the transformation propeties

•

\Psi(z,hu)=\Psi(z,h)
for all
u\in U_{A}
,

•

\Psi(\gamma z,\gamma h)=j(\gamma,z)^{l}\cdot\Psi(z,h)
for all
\gamma\in\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{Q}})
.

We define the norm of a section
(z,h)\rightarrow\Psi(z,h)\cdot w(z)^{\otimes l}
to be

\displaystyle||\Psi(z,h)||_{A}^{2}

\displaystyle=|\Psi(z,h)|_{A}^{2}\cdot|y|_{A}^{2l},

we refer to this as the Petersson norm of the holomorphic section
\Psi
.
Note that under the isomorphism
(\ref{Sh-decomp})
, such a section
\Psi
corresponds
to the collection
\{\Psi(\cdot,h_{j})\}_{j}
of holomorphic functions on
D(V_{A})=D^{\pm}(V_{A})\cong\mathfrak{H}^{2}

which are holomorphic of weight
l
for the corresponding arithmetic group
\Gamma_{A,j}=\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{Q}})\cap h_{j}^{-1}U_{A}h_{j}
.

4.4.1.
Gaussian functions

Given

\operatorname{pr}_{z}:V_{A}({\bf{R}})\longrightarrow z

denote the projection, whose kernel defines the orthogonal complement
z^{\perp}:=\ker(\operatorname{pr}_{z})
.
Given
x\in V_{A}({\bf{R}})
, we then define the resultant

\displaystyle R(x,z)_{A}

\displaystyle:=-\left(\operatorname{pr}_{z}(x),\operatorname{pr}_{z}(x)\right)=\left|(x,w(z))_{A}\right|_{A}^{2}\cdot|y|_{A}^{2}.

Using this resultant, we can associate to a hyperplane
z\in D(V_{A})
and vector
x\in V_{A}({\bf{R}})
a majorant

\displaystyle\left(x,x\right)_{A,z}

\displaystyle:=(x,x)_{A}+2\cdot R(x,z)_{A}.

Writing
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(D(V_{A}))
to denote the space of smooth functions on
D(V_{A})

we use this majorant to define a Gaussian function

\varphi_{\infty}(x,z)\in\mathcal{S}(V_{A}({\bf{R}}))\otimes\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(D(V_{A}))
by the rule

\displaystyle\varphi_{\infty}(x,z)

\displaystyle:=\exp\left(-\pi\cdot\left(x,x\right)_{A,z}\right).

It is known that
\varphi_{\infty}(hx,hz)=\varphi_{\infty}(x,z)
for all
h\in\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{R}})
,
and also that
\varphi_{\infty}
has weight
0
for the action of the maximal compact subgroup

\operatorname{SO}_{2}({\bf{R}})
of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\bf{R}})
.

4.4.2.
Theta kernels

Given
z\in D(V_{A})
,
g\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\bf{A}})
, and
h_{f}\in\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{A}}_{f})
,
we write
\theta_{L_{A}}^{\star}
to denote the linear functional on
\varphi_{f}\in\mathcal{S}(V_{A}({\bf{A}}_{f}))
defined by

(21)

\displaystyle\varphi_{f}\longmapsto\theta_{L_{A}}^{\star}(g,z,h_{f};\varphi_{f})

\displaystyle:=\sum\limits_{x\in V_{A}({\bf{Q}})}\omega_{L_{A}}(g)\left(\varphi_{\infty}(\cdot,z)\otimes\omega_{L_{A}}(h_{f})\varphi_{f}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{x\in V_{A}({\bf{Q}})}\omega_{L_{A}}(g,1)\left(\varphi_{\infty}(\cdot,z)\otimes\omega_{L_{A}}(1,h_{f})\varphi_{f}\right)(x).

It is easy to see that for all
\gamma\in\operatorname{GSpin}(V_{A})({\bf{Q}})
, we have

\displaystyle\theta_{L_{A}}^{\star}(g,\gamma z,\gamma h_{f};\varphi_{f})

\displaystyle=\theta_{L_{A}}^{\star}(g,z,h_{f};\varphi_{f}).

By Poisson summation (see
[

Preclinical assessment of a novel ablation catheter specific to robotic magnetic navigation
Authors: Qingzhi Luo, Xiang Li, Yangyang Bao, Changjian Lin, Yun Xie, Yue Wei, Tianyou Ling, Ning Zhang, Kang Chen, Wenqi Pan, Liqun Wu, Qi Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundA novel catheter (Magbot) is needed to enable seamless integration with robotic magnetic navigation systems and third-party mapping platforms.ObjectiveThe goals of this study were to assess the contact force (CF) and lesion characteristics of the Magbot catheter under robotic magnetic navigation guidance and to compare these parameters with those of the RMT catheter solely for the CARTO system.MethodsA total of 1200 CF measurements were collected for both the Magbot and RMT catheters when spanning different vector orientations, and catheter extensions were obtained in a simulation study. Subsequently, radiofrequency energy was delivered to an ex vivo porcine heart to create ablation lesions using 3 power settings: 30 W for 30 seconds, 40 W for 20 seconds, and 50 W for 15 seconds.ResultsCF values were highly dependent on both vector orientation and catheter extension. The Magbot catheter consistently yielded more favorable CF values across all anatomical configurations than did the RMT catheter. While no statistically significant difference in lesion size was observed between the 2 catheters across all tested energy settings, a consistent trend favoring the Magbot catheter was noted. Moreover, high-power ablation (50 W/15 seconds) generated lesions characterized by a significant increase in superficial width but a concomitant reduction in depth and overall volume. Notably, compared with the Magbot catheter, the RMT catheter was associated with a significantly higher incidence of steam pops, with risk escalating sharply at higher power levels.ConclusionThe findings indicate that the Magbot catheter not only matched the RMT catheter in core lesion formation efficacy but also offered distinct advantages in terms of CF stability and safety profile.

Key Findings ▪We first conducted a comprehensive evaluation of the Magbot catheter’s performance using an ex vivo porcine heart model under robotic magnetic navigation guidance.▪In comparison with the RMT catheter, the Magbot catheter demonstrated comparable efficacy in creating core lesions while achieving enhanced contact force values and a notably improved safety profile.▪Compared with low-power ablation (30 W/30 seconds), high-power ablation (50 W/15 seconds) increased superficial lesion width but reduced depth and total volume for both catheters.

Introduction

Robotic magnetic navigation (RMN)–guided catheter ablation has been extensively used in the treatment of cardiac arrhythmias. The primary advantages of this technology include enhanced catheter stability, improved lesion formation, and an optimized safety profile.1, 2, 3 Among the key determinants of ablation efficacy, catheter-tissue contact force (CF) plays a pivotal role in governing lesion dimensions (diameter, depth, and volume).4,5 Despite these advances, a critical limitation of current RMN systems lies in their lack of integrated real-time quantitative CF monitoring capabilities. Emerging evidence further underscores the complexity of CF dynamics in RMN-guided procedures: recent studies have demonstrated that CF measurements obtained with the RMT catheter are substantially modulated by variations in magnetic field vector orientation and differences in catheter extension length relative to the long sheath.6,7 In an in vitro phantom model, Bhaskaran et al8 reported larger lesion dimensions with RMN compared to non-CF–guided manual irrigated radiofrequency (RF) ablation lesions, yet comprehensive data on CF quantification and lesion metrics specific to RMN systems remain scarce. The Magbot catheter, a novel device (MicroPort EP Medtech, Shanghai, China) compatible with RMN, has recently been approved by the National Medical Products Administration in China and addresses this gap, though its CF performance and lesion-generating characteristics have not been systematically investigated. Notably, no prior studies have directly compared the Magbot catheter with the RMT catheter, leaving critical knowledge gaps regarding their relative efficacy in CF delivery and lesion formation.

Against this backdrop, the present study aimed to (1) quantify CF values generated by the Magbot and RMT catheters under 4 distinct magnetic field vector orientations and 4 catheter extension configurations (relative to the long sheath) using an RMN system (operating at a magnetic field strength of 0.08 T), (2) create RF ablation lesions in isolated porcine myocardial tissue and systematically document lesion dimensions across varying ablation power settings and procedural durations for each catheter, and (3) perform comparisons of CF values and lesion metrics between the 2 catheters.

Methods

The Magbot catheter’s structural design and innovation

The distal deflectable segment of the Magbot catheter comprises 5 integrated components: 1 distal electrode and 4 magnetic blocks of varying dimensions (Figure 1A). Magnetic block 1 (length 2.3 mm; volume 5.06 mm3) is fully encased and rigidly bonded within the distal electrode (length 3.5 mm; diameter 8.2 F; surface area 33.06 mm2); this intimate integration is a primary contributor to the higher CF achievable by the Magbot relative to the RMT catheter. Magnetic blocks 2, 3, and 4 (volumes 8.85 mm3, 7.87 mm3, and 10.8 mm3, respectively) are independently positioned along the distal, middle, and proximal portions of the rigid segment. Collectively, the total magnetic component volume at the Magbot catheter tip is significantly larger than that of the RMT catheter (32.6 mm3 vs 18.8 mm3). Experimental model and CF measurements

The measurement methodology used was consistent with that used in a prior study evaluating CF for the RMT catheter.6 CF measurements were performed using the Genesis RMN system (Stereotaxis, St. Louis, MO), an 8.2-F RF ablation Magbot catheter with magnetic navigation (MicroPort EP Medtech, Shanghai, China), and an 8-F RF RMT catheter (Biosense Webster, Irvine, CA) introduced via an 8.5-F long sheath (SR0, MicroPort EP Medtech, Irvine, CA). CF data were recorded with a precision pressure sensor (FUTEK, Guangdong, China) calibrated to 0.01-g resolution. The experimental setup (Figure 2I) positioned the anatomical model on an adjustable table within the electrophysiology laboratory, centrally aligned between 2 permanent magnets at an approximate height of 8 cm above the table surface. The long sheath remained stationary, while the scale was repositioned to assess CF at predetermined anatomical sites. Catheter manipulation was achieved through RMN using a controlled magnetic field strength of 0.08 T. For both catheter types, measurements were obtained at 4 distinct magnetic field vector orientations: transverse, caudal, cranial, and sagittal. The scale was positioned orthogonally to each respective plane, with the catheter maintained in perpendicular alignment relative to the scale. After each measurement, the catheter was withdrawn, and the scale was verified to return to a baseline reading of 0 g to exclude potential electromagnetic interference artifacts. At each of the 4 scale positions, multiple CF measurements were performed for 4 standardized catheter extension lengths from the long sheath (position I = minimal extension; position IV = maximal extension). A total of 1200 measurements were performed—600 for each catheter group. Ablation protocol and lesion metrics assessment

The experimental setup was analogous to those described in previous studies.9,10 The ex vivo model (Figure 2II) consisted of a saline-filled container including a thermostat, a circulating pump, and an indifferent electrode. The electric impedance of the experimental setup was adjusted by the ratio of saline and water to 100 Ω; the temperature was held at 37°C. Fresh porcine myocardial slabs (thickness 2–4 cm, longitudinally incised parallel to the left ventricular long axis) were procured from local butchers and immersed in a thermostatically regulated saline bath. Ablation catheter navigation to the myocardial slabs was performed under real-time RMN system guidance by using fixed CF (Magbot 11.4 g; RMT 7.6 g) and magnetic vector orientation. These CF targets were not predetermined; rather, they were derived from the mean CF values recorded for each catheter during the experimental stimulation setup, specifically within a magnetic field aligned to the sagittal vector and with the catheter extended to position II. Under these identical tested conditions, the Magbot catheter demonstrated a significantly higher mean CF (11.44 ± 0.01 g) than did the RMT catheter (7.57 ± 0.01 g). These values represent the characteristic performance profiles of each device within this stimulation model and were then used to evaluate lesion formation at the CF levels in an ex vivo model.

To achieve a range of lesion sizes, myocardial lesions were created via power-controlled RF ablation using different ablation power and duration settings—30 W for 30 seconds, 40 W for 20 seconds, and 50 W for 15 seconds—for both catheter models. 30 replicates were generated for each protocol. During ablation, the Magbot catheter underwent continuous saline irrigation at 8 ml/min (for 30 W) and 15 mL/min (for 40 and 50 W). Conversely, the RMT catheter received continuous saline irrigation at 17 mL/min (for 30 W) and 30 mL/min (for 40 and 50 W) during RF energy delivery. After each ablation, the ablation electrode and electrode-tissue interface were inspected for char formation and audible pops were documented. Post-RF application, myocardial lesions were longitudinally sectioned, and the maximal depth, maximal diameter, depth at the maximal diameter, and lesion surface diameter were measured using a digital caliper with a resolution of 0.01 mm. All lesion measures were performed by 2 operators. In case of a steam pop, the lesion was discarded for size quantification. The incidence of steam pops was calculated by dividing the total number of events by 30 lesions. The lesion volume was calculated using the formula as previously described by Nakagawa et al.11

Intercatheter comparisons between the Magbot and RMT catheters

In this study, CF measurements for different vectors and extensions, along with lesion characteristics and procedural safety from the 3 ablation protocols described above, were compared between the 2 catheters.

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables are described as mean ± standard deviation. Comparisons between continuous variables were made using the Student t test (2-group comparisons) or the analysis of variance (ANOVA) test (>2-group comparisons) for normally distributed variables. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test (2 groups) or the Kruskal–Wallis H test (>2 groups). Categorical variables were described as count (percentage) and compared using the χ2 test. 2-tailed P value <.05 were considered statistically significant. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 23 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY).

Effects of magnetic vector differences on CF values

Significant intervector variability in CF values was observed for both catheters. The largest disparities occurred at position I, with a progressive reduction in this vector-dependent difference across subsequent positions. For both catheters, the greatest CF difference between vectors was seen between the caudal and cranial vectors: Magbot: 17.49 ± 0.14 g (caudal) vs 6.62 ± 0.08 g (cranial), P < .001; RMT: 15.61 ± 0.11 g (caudal) vs 4.03 ± 0.05 g (cranial), P < .001.

Effects of catheter extension from the long sheath on CF values

Analysis of data across vectors at positions I–IV revealed a consistent trend: increased catheter extension from the sheath correlated with lower CF values. Notably, this pattern diverged in the caudal vector for the Magbot catheter, where CF values increased from position I to position III (6.62 ± 0.08 g → 7.55 ± 0.05 g → 7.98 ± 0.02 g) before a slight decline at position IV (5.28 ± 0.02 g). In contrast, the RMT catheter demonstrated a distinct behavior in the caudal vector, with a reduction in CF values at position IV.

Comparison of CF values between the 2 catheters

CF values were directly compared between the Magbot and RMT catheters across 4 distinct magnetic vector orientations and 4 catheter extension configurations. A statistically significant increase in CF values (minimum 3 g difference; P < .001) was observed for the Magbot catheter compared with the RMT catheter in all experimental scenarios.

Part 2: Lesion size measurements and inter- and intracatheter comparisons

A total of 180 lesions were generated: 90 lesions per catheter, with 30 lesions allocated to each power setting. Among these, 42 lesions (23.3%) could not be analyzed because of the occurrence of steam pops. Specifically, 9 steam pop–related lesions occurred with the Magbot catheter: 1 at 30 W for 30 seconds, 2 at 40 W for 20 seconds, and 6 at 50 W for 15 seconds. The remaining 33 lesions with steam pops occurred with the RMT catheter: 4 at 30 W for 30 seconds, 9 at 40 W for 20 seconds, and 20 at 50 W for 15 seconds. Therefore, an additional 9 lesions were performed with the Magbot catheter compared with 33 with the RMT catheter because of steam pop. Notably, among lesions affected by steam pops, the mean time to onset was 13.9 ± 5.1 seconds (range 9–29 seconds).

Lesion dimensions and morphology

Figure 5 illustrates the dimensions and morphology of the mean lesion generated under distinct power configurations for each catheter. For both catheters, the data consistently revealed that high-power settings (50 W for 15 seconds) produced lesions characterized by a marked increase in superficial width, coupled with a decrease in depth and total volume. Intra- and intercatheter comparisons of safety across different power settings

Regarding procedural safety metrics (Figure 6), the incidence of steam pops did not differ significantly across the tested power-time protocols when using the Magbot catheter (ANOVA: 30 W/30 seconds, 3.3%; 40 W/20 seconds, 6.7%; 50 W/15 seconds, 20%; P = .075). In contrast, a distinct dose-dependent increase in the incidence of steam pops was observed with the RMT catheter, demonstrating a strong positive correlation between power setting and complication rate (ANOVA: 30 W/30 seconds, 13.3%; 40 W/20 seconds, 30%; 50 W/15 seconds, 66.7%; P < .0001). Furthermore, a direct comparison between catheters revealed that for equivalent power-time settings, the RMT catheter was associated with a significantly higher risk of steam pops. This difference reached statistical significance at 40 W/20 seconds (P = .021) and 50 W/15 seconds (P < .0001). Discussion

Main findings

This study provides the first comprehensive characterization of the Magbot catheter’s performance in an ex vivo porcine heart model under RMN guidance. Our findings offer critical insights into its CF dynamics, lesion-generating capability, and its safety profile when benchmarked against the established ThermoCool RMT platform (Biosense Webster). The principal findings are as follows:1.Superior CF profile: The Magbot catheter consistently delivered more favorable CF values across all evaluated anatomical configurations relative to the RMT catheter.2.Anisotropic CF behavior: CF measurements were highly dependent on the orientation of the applied magnetic field vector. A progressive decline in CF was observed with increasing distance of the catheter from the long sheath.3.Power-dependent lesion morphology (intracatheter analysis): High-power ablation (50 W/15 seconds) generated lesions characterized by a significant increase in superficial width but a concomitant reduction in depth and total lesion volume, as compared with lesions created with a low-power protocol (30 W/30 seconds).4.Comparative efficacy (intercatheter analysis): No statistically significant differences were detected in lesion characteristics between the Magbot and RMT catheters across all tested energy settings, indicating comparable efficacy.5.Safety disparity: A critical safety distinction emerged at equivalent power-time settings: the RMT catheter was associated with a significantly elevated incidence of steam pops, with risk escalating markedly at higher power levels.

Factors influencing CF achievement using RMN and their clinical implications

The attainment of stable and appropriate CF during RMN-guided ablation is highly dependent on 2 primary factors: the selected vector and the degree of catheter extension beyond the sheath, potentially resulting in heterogeneous pressure distribution across cardiac structures at different anatomical sites.6,7 Pioneering work by Bessière et al12 examined CF generated by the RMN system, revealing an average CF of 6 g in the absence of a sheath, which surged to 22 g when a long sheath was used. However, exceeding this threshold often precipitated catheter buckling due to excessive mechanical stress. In this study, the Magbot catheter achieved consistently more favorable CF values across all tested conditions compared with the RMT catheter. This performance advantage is likely attributed to its increased number of magnets, which enhances navigational stability. Notably, a key finding of this investigation is that all Magbot-derived CF measurements remained within clinically established therapeutic windows (range 5.28–17.49 g). Elevated CF can augment ablation efficacy, and it concurrently heightens the risk of steam pops. However, the incidence of pops was consistently lower across all the tested power settings, thereby underscoring the superior safety profile of the Magbot catheter.

Significant intra- and intervector differences in CF measurements were observed across anatomical positions (I–IV), a finding with substantial clinical relevance—particularly for patients with corrected congenital heart disease. This cohort is notably susceptible to cavotricuspid isthmus atrial flutter, necessitating meticulous CF optimization during ablation in the caudal vector configuration. This specific orientation mandates a pronounced 180° catheter deflection, presenting unique technical challenges. Our data revealed that CF values in position I (caudal vector) were lower than those in other orientations. The RMT catheter demonstrated a marked drop in CF at position IV under this vector, whereas the Magbot system maintained more consistent performance. Consequently, strategic advancement of the Magbot catheter beyond the long sheath at position III may represent an effective strategy to optimize CF delivery during cavotricuspid isthmus ablation, with peak values approaching 8 g in this specific setup.

Inter- and intracatheter differences in lesion metrics across different power settings and their clinical relevance and strategic considerations

Lesion formation during RF ablation is governed by multiple factors, including CF and its stability, catheter type, energy dynamics, and tissue characteristics.13 The intercatheter comparisons yielded 2 salient insights. First, a consistent but nonsignificant trend toward larger lesions, encompassing maximal width, depth, and volume metrics, was noted with the Magbot catheter compared with the RMT catheter under identical energy parameters, indicating that both devices have comparable fundamental lesion-generating capabilities. Of note, increased CF achieved by the Magbot catheter at a given magnetic vector was not associated with increased lesion size, possibly reflecting differences in distal electrode architecture. Specifically, the Magbot catheter incorporates a substantially greater total magnetic component volume at the tip (32.6 mm3 vs 18.8 mm3 for RMT) and a larger distal electrode surface area (33.06 mm2 vs 28.88 mm2 for RMT). Furthermore, the proximal magnetic element within the Magbot tip is integrated more tightly with the electrode, contributing to enhanced CF without proportionally enlarging lesion dimensions. This phenomenon may be partly explained by the fact that, at constant power output, a larger contact area reduces overall current density, thereby potentially limiting lesion depth and volume and yielding comparable lesion sizes between devices.

Second, and of greater clinical relevance, the RMT catheter exhibited a markedly elevated incidence of steam pops, particularly at higher power settings. As described above, the enlarged distal electrode of the Magbot catheter reduces current density, resulting in a lower risk of steam pop formation. In addition, prior literature indicates that catheter tip irrigation plays a pivotal role in modulating both lesion geometry and steam pop frequency.14 The RMT catheter employs a conventional irrigation configuration, using 6 larger orifices (0.4 mm each) positioned at the distal tip. In contrast, the Magbot catheter features a proprietary microporous irrigation design, comprising 66 uniformly distributed micropores (0.1 mm each) across its distal electrode. This configuration facilitates more homogeneous cooling with reduced irrigation volume, thereby mitigating steam pop occurrence. Consistent with this, steam pop rates were highest in the 50-W RMT cohort (66.7%), intermediate the 40-W RMT group (30%), and lowest in the 50-W Magbot group (20%). These rates exceed those previously reported in in vivo studies involving ST (Biosense Webster) and STSF (Biosense Webster) catheters. A plausible contributing factor is the relatively low baseline impedance (∼100 Ω) observed in this study, which may drive excessive current flow, thereby increasing lesion size and propensity for vaporization events.15

In alignment with previous biophysical models,16,17 our intracatheter analysis of lesion morphology showed that lesions generated using a high-power protocol (50 W for 15 seconds) exhibited significantly larger superficial width but reduced depth and volume compared with those produced by a low-power regimen (30 W for 30 seconds) for both catheters. This trade-off reflects a greater degree of lateral heat dissipation at the tissue surface rather than deep conductive heating. This finding reinforces the critical importance of power calibration for lesion-based efficacy and highlights a trade-off between creating durable transmural lesions and minimizing collateral thermal injury.18 Accumulating clinical evidence has suggested that high-power, short-duration RF applications achieve acute and long-term efficacy comparable to traditional low-power, long-duration approaches in atrial fibrillation ablation, without compromising safety.19, 20, 21 Notably, the Magbot catheter did not exhibit the same elevation in steam pop risk at matched energy levels, positioning it as a potentially safer alternative—particularly in scenarios demanding aggressive power delivery where the RMT system’s risk-benefit profile becomes less favorable. Consequently, a tailored RF strategy may be warranted: using high-power settings for posterior wall ablation procedures could enhance efficacy while preserving safety, whereas anterior wall targets may benefit from conservative power titration to ensure adequate depth and minimize thermal complications.

Limitations

There are several limitations inherent in the present study. First, the ex vivo ablation procedures were performed on isolated porcine hearts, which fail to replicate the dynamic physiological conditions encountered in clinical settings—specifically, the continuous motion associated with cardiac contraction and respiration. Second, lesion dimensions were assessed on the basis of visual demarcation rather than electrophysiological mapping; consequently, the actual electrical lesion size may be considerably smaller than what is observed visually. Third, the use of fixed catheter deployment length and vector configuration, while standardizing comparisons between the Magbot and RMT catheters, fails to reflect the individualized multimodal positioning based on electroanatomical and imaging data used clinically. Therefore, the direct comparisons of CF and lesion size at a fixed length may not fully capture the clinical relevance of our findings, and further studies in vivo or using patient-specific models are warranted to validate our results.

Conclusion

The findings of this study revealed that while the Magbot catheter achieved comparable efficacy in forming core lesions relative to the RMT catheter, it distinguished itself through enhanced CF stability and a significantly improved safety profile—most notably, a reduced tendency for steam pops. These attributes underscore its promise as a next-generation solution for robotic navigation ablation. Further in vivo investigations are needed to confirm these ex vivo observations and to evaluate their influence on key long-term clinical metrics, including procedural duration, first-pass isolation success, and overall complication rates.

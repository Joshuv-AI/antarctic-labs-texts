The local Turnpike Property in Mean Field Control and Games with quadratic Hamiltonian
Marco Cirant and Nicolò De Bernardi

Abstract
We study the local stability properties of solutions to ergodic and discounted mean field games systems in the long time horizon, around stationary equilibria, when the Hamiltonian is quadratic. We replace the usual monotonicity of the coupling term with a weaker, local assumption on the stationary equilibrium (that need not be unique), stemming from a second-order strict positivity condition. This new stability assumption, together with a symmetry property of the system, allows us to derive an exponential turnpike property for those solutions that are close to the stationary one. Finally, through a fixed-point argument, we establish the actual existence of stable solutions, both on the finite horizon [ 0 , T ] [0,T] and on the infinite horizon, provided that the initial (and terminal) data are close enough to the stationary equilibrium.

Introduction

This paper is devoted to the study of the long time behavior of solutions to discounted Mean Field Games systems of PDEs with quadratic Hamiltonian, of the following form:

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\frac{|Du|^{2}}{2}-f(x,m)+\delta u=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
\partial_{t}m-\Delta m-\text{div}(mDu)=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
m(0,x)=m_{0}(x),\qquad u(T,x)=u_{T}(x),&\text{ on }\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
T>0
is the time-horizon,
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{2}
and has bounded first and second derivatives with respect to the second component,
\delta\geq 0
is the discount factor. Here,
\Omega
can be either the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
(in which case, data are periodic in the
x
variable);
m_{0},u_{T}
are the initial distribution and the terminal cost, respectively, and they are assumed to be of class
C^{2,\alpha}
.

This system of PDEs has been introduced in
[
26
]
to describe
Mean Field Nash equilibria
among a population of players with density
m(t,x)
, where a typical player controls his own state

{\rm d}X_{s}=\alpha_{s}{\rm d}s+\sqrt{2}\,{\rm d}B_{s}\qquad\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

with the aim of minimizing the cost

J(\alpha)=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\delta s}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\alpha_{s}|^{2}+f\big(X_{s},m(s,X_{s})\big)\right){\rm d}s+e^{-\delta T}u_{T}(X_{T}).

Within this framework,
u
is the so-called value function of the typical player. Moreover, the same PDE system constitutes the optimality conditions for the
Mean Field optimal control problem
of the functional (see again
[
26
]
)

\mathcal{F}(w)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}e^{-\delta s}\left(\frac{1}{2}m(s,y)|w(s,y)|^{2}+F\big(y,m(s,y)\big)\right){\rm d}y{\rm d}s+e^{-\delta T}\int_{\Omega}u_{T}(y)m(T,y){\rm d}y

subject to

\partial_{t}m-\Delta m+\text{div}(mw)=0,\quad\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\qquad m(0,x)=m_{0}(x)

where
F(x,m)=\int_{0}^{m}f(x,\nu){\rm d}\nu
.

The main purpose of this paper is to address the following problem. Consider a solution
(\bar{u},\bar{m})
to the
stationary
system

\begin{cases}\Delta\bar{u}-\frac{|D\bar{u}|^{2}}{2}+f(x,\bar{m})-\delta\bar{u}=0,&\text{ on }\Omega\\
\Delta\bar{m}+\text{div}(\bar{m}D\bar{u})=0,&\text{ on }\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}=1,\end{cases}

(1.2)

then
under what conditions can we guarantee that
(\bar{u},\bar{m})
attracts dynamic equilibria
(u,m)
as
T\to\infty
?
The main point of our work is not to assume any kind of global uniqueness of stationary solutions
(\bar{u},\bar{m})
. Therefore, we will focus on
local
stability properties, that is, the stabilization of dynamic equilibria that are close in a suitable sense to a given stationary one. Note that if
\delta=0
, then we shall look at the so-called
ergodic
system, where the additional constant
\lambda
appears in the HJ equation:

\begin{cases}\Delta\bar{u}-\frac{|D\bar{u}|^{2}}{2}+f(x,\bar{m})-\lambda=0,&\text{ on }\Omega\\
\Delta\bar{m}+\text{div}(\bar{m}D\bar{u})=0,&\text{ on }\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}=1.\end{cases}

(1.3)

In such case, we will assume throughout the paper that
\lambda=0
. More precisely, given a triple
(\bar{u},\bar{m},\lambda)
satisfying the previous system, we tacitly replace
f
by
f-\lambda
.
The previous question is formulated in terms of stability properties of PDE systems, but it can be rephrased within the context of optimal control, in particular when
\delta=0
. In such case, if we look at the
static
Mean Field optimal control problem, that is, of the functional

\overline{\mathcal{F}}(w)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}m(y)|w(y)|^{2}+F\big(y,m(y)\big){\rm d}y

subject to

-\Delta m+\text{div}(mw)=0,\quad\text{ on }\Omega\qquad\int_{\Omega}m(y){\rm d}y=1,

we want to investigate conditions on static minimizers (or critical points)
(\bar{w},\bar{m})
ensuring their ability to attract minimizers (or critical points) of
\mathcal{F}
as
T\to\infty
.

Being the PDE system under consideration at the crossroad of Mean Field Games and (infinite dimensional) optimal control, we can borrow intuitions from both sides. In the optimal control literature, the phenomenon that solutions spend most of the time close to a steady state is often referred to as the
turnpike property
. Its analysis has a long history that started in the ’60s, though the first observations in applied sciences go back to works of von Neumann. A fundamental observation coming from the optimal control literature is that local stability of stationary states originates from the (local) saddle point structure of the optimality conditions (or extremal equations, or Pontryagin system), that takes the form of a Hamiltonian system. This fact was already suggested in
[
37
]
in the LQ setting, and it has been more recently developed in general frameworks in
[
31
,
33
,
32
,
34
]
. We refer to the very nice survey
[
35
]
for historical insights, recent developments and further references.

The renewed interest in the turnpike phenomenon in optimal control has grown in parallel with the study of long-time behavior in Mean Field Game theory. Within this context, we mention the pioneering works
[
10
,
7
]
and the more recent ones
[
3
,
9
,
12
,
16
,
21
]
. The common thread in these contributions is the presence of some assumption that guarantees in fact a
global
turnpike phenomenon: both dynamic and stationary solutions are
unique
, and the former converge in long time to the latter independently of the initial / terminal conditions. These assumptions typically take the form of
monotonicity
(or “smallness” of the data in some sense).

The main purpose of this work is to investigate problems with absence of monotonicity, where multiple stationary states are likely to coexist. The stability analysis of stationary equilibria in non-monotone frameworks has been addressed in few works
[
2
,
11
,
13
,
29
]
, but it seems at this stage still limited to quite specific models. See also
[
8
]
for the application of Weak KAM methods to MFG. In fact, without monotonicity, dynamic phenomena that are far from the turnpike one have been observed, such as periodic behavior
[
17
]
or heteroclinic connections
[
15
]
.

It is important to observe that, once specialized to the MFG setting, the methods developed in optimal control theory seem to require assumptions that resemble the ones of monotonicity (at least in a local sense). That is why, though we are taking inspiration from those methods here, some ideas will be introduced to address truly non monotone situations. This crucial point will be discussed in details in Section
1.1
.

The presence of the quadratic Hamiltonian simplifies the analysis: the method presented here exploits a sort of
symmetry
property of the MFG, which looks clear in the quadratic case. This symmetry property will be clarified and formalized in a general sense in Section
1.1
below.

\bullet

(1.4)

. We anticipate here that

.

Assume that
(
S) holds. Let

) and (
1.2
) respectively, such that

The turnpike property of

1.1
Discussion on the method

Consider systems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), with solutions
(m,u)
and

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+\langle Dv,D\bar{u}\rangle+\delta v=f(x,\bar{m}+\mu)-f(x,\bar{m})-\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{2},&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\\

\mu
can be rewritten as

\partial_{t}\mu-\text{div}(\bar{m}Dv)-\text{div}\left(\bar{m}D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right)=0\Longrightarrow\partial_{t}\mu=\text{div}\left(\bar{m}\left(Dv+D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right)\right),

hence the vector field
Dv+D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)
, appearing in the “dynamic” part of (
1.9
), drives in fact the evolution of
\mu
.

Note that we have focused here on linear stability only. In the general, nonlinear case, the proof of the exponential decay of the quantity
\Phi
goes along similar lines, but one needs to handle the presence of lower order terms. There is also some technical work that is needed to derive decay bounds on weighted
L^{2}
norms, then on
L^{\infty}
norms. Moreover, the presence of
\delta>0
also needs to be handled with care; in a sense, this is a further perturbation of the problem. Indeed, the previous argument gives the exponential decay of
\tilde{\Phi}(t)=e^{-\delta t}\Phi(t)
. To recover the exponential decay of
\Phi(t)
, one then needs
\delta
to be small enough, since the exponential decay for
\Phi
from the one of
\tilde{\Phi}
takes the form

e^{-(\sigma-\delta)t}+e^{-(\sigma+\delta)(T-t)}.

1.2
The assumption
(
S)

We discuss in this section the assumption
(
S) .

First, the identity
D\bar{m}=-\bar{m}D\bar{u}
is true if
\bar{m}(x)
is proportional to
e^{-\bar{u}(x)}
: this ansatz gives in fact a solution to the stationary Fokker-Planck equation, so any given solution
\bar{m}
is actually proportional to
e^{-\bar{u}}
provided that uniqueness for the Fokker-Planck equation holds. This is a topic on itself, that has been largely explored, see for example
[
5
]
; when
\Omega
is unbounded, uniqueness is more delicate, and it is somehow connected to the validity of Poincaré type inequalities.

Similarly, there is a large body of literature on the Poincaré inequality for
\bar{m}
if
\Omega
is bounded (that goes under the name of weighted Poincaré inequality); this is valid for example if
\bar{m}
is regular enough and bounded away from zero (which is our case, by elliptic regularity and the strong maximum principle). When
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, an additional care is needed: the inequality is somehow related also to the behavior of
\bar{m}
as
x\to\infty
, and it is true in many situations, see for example
[
1
]
.

The main restriction in
(
S) is the inequality (
1.4
). We first observe that, under mild assumptions on
f
, such inequality has equivalent formulations.

Lemma 1.3

(Equivalent formulations of
(
S) )
.

Assume that
\bar{m},f_{m}(\cdot,\bar{m}(\cdot))\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and that
\bar{m}
satisfies a Poincaré inequality. Then, the following properties are equivalent :

(a)

\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\geq\eta\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}{\rm d}x;

(b)

\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\geq\eta^{\prime}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x;

(c)

\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\geq\eta^{\prime\prime}\int_{\Omega}\mu^{2}{\rm d}x,

for some
\eta,\eta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime\prime}>0
and for all
\mu\in L^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
\int_{\Omega}\mu(x){\rm d}x=0
,
\mu/\bar{m}\in W^{1,2}(\bar{m}{\rm d}x)
.

Proof.

Suppose that (a) holds. The first implication is a simple application of the weighted-Poincaré inequality for
\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}
. In fact, we know that

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\bar{m}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}\mu\,{\rm d}x=0,

hence

\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\overset{(a)}{\geq}\eta\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}{\rm d}x\\
\geq\frac{\eta}{C_{P}}\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}^{2}}{\rm d}x=\frac{\eta}{C_{P}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x,

where
C_{P}
is the Poincaré constant of
\bar{m}
. Therefore, (b) holds with
\eta^{\prime}=\frac{\eta}{C_{P}}
.

Next, let (b) hold. Since
\bar{m}
is bounded from above and non-negative, we have that

\eta^{\prime}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\geq\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\int_{\Omega}\mu^{2}{\rm d}x,

hence (c) holds with
\eta^{\prime\prime}=\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}}
.

Finally, let (c) hold. Take
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. We can split the left hand side of (c) into two pieces, as follows

\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\\
=(1-\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x+\\
+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\geq\\
\geq(1-\varepsilon)\eta^{\prime\prime}\int_{\Omega}\mu^{2}{\rm d}x+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x=\\
=\int_{\Omega}\left[(1-\varepsilon)\eta^{\prime\prime}+\varepsilon f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\right]\mu^{2}{\rm d}x+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\geq\\
\geq\int_{\Omega}\left[(1-\varepsilon)\eta^{\prime\prime}-\varepsilon\|f_{m}(\cdot,\bar{m})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right]\mu^{2}{\rm d}x+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x=\blacktriangle

Now, choosing
\varepsilon=\frac{\eta^{\prime\prime}}{\eta^{\prime\prime}+\|f_{m}(\cdot,\bar{m})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}
, we have

(1-\varepsilon)\eta^{\prime\prime}-\varepsilon\|f_{m}(\cdot,\bar{m})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=0.

Hence, we proceed from
\blacktriangle
to get

\blacktriangle\geq\frac{\eta^{\prime\prime}}{\eta^{\prime\prime}+\|f_{m}(\cdot,\bar{m})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}{\rm d}x,

so that (a) holds with
\eta:=\frac{\eta^{\prime\prime}}{\eta^{\prime\prime}+\|f_{m}(\cdot,\bar{m})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}
.
∎

Remark 1.4

(The principal eigenvalue)
.

The inequalities (a), (b), (c) of the previous lemma can be interpreted as bounds from below of certain quotients of Rayleigh type. For instance (b) reads as

\inf_{\int_{\Omega}\mu=0}\frac{\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x}{\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x}\geq\eta^{\prime}>0.

If such infimum is attained at some
\mu
, then (formally) the following equality holds

f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu-\frac{1}{\bar{m}}{\rm div}\left(\bar{m}D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right)+\delta\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}=\eta_{1}\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}+\ell,

(1.10)

where
\eta_{1}\geq\eta^{\prime}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
(
\ell
can be characterized in terms of
\mu,\bar{m}
). Via the change of variables
v=-\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}
and the identity
D\bar{u}=-\frac{D\bar{m}}{\bar{m}}
one finds the PDE system solved by the triple
(v,\mu,\ell)
:

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+\langle Dv,D\bar{u}\rangle+\delta v-f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu=\eta_{1}v+\ell\\
\Delta\mu+\text{div}(\bar{m}Dv)+\text{div}(\mu D\bar{u})=0,\\
\int_{\Omega}\mu=0.\end{cases}

(1.11)

So, another way of seeing
(
S) is that

the first (or principal) eigenvalue
\eta_{1}
, that is the smallest number
\eta_{1}

such that (
1.11
) has nontrivial solutions, must be positive.

Arguing similarly with the inequality (c) yields an eigenvalue problem of the form

\begin{cases}-\Delta v+\langle Dv,D\bar{u}\rangle+\delta v-f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu=-\hat{\eta}_{1}\mu+\ell\\
\Delta\mu+\text{div}(\bar{m}Dv)+\text{div}(\mu D\bar{u})=0,\\
\int_{\Omega}\mu=0.\end{cases}

Though all considerations are made here in a merely formal way, it would be interesting to develop a systematic spectral theory for the present problems.

Remark 1.5

(The Lasry-Lions monotone case)
.

In this standard monotonicity regime, that is when
f_{m}\geq\ell>0
everywhere, it is clear that Lemma
1.3
(c) holds with
\eta^{\prime\prime}=\ell
, and so
(
S) holds.

In addition, by means of the Poincaré inequality,

\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\geq\int_{\Omega}\left(f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\bar{m}+\frac{1}{C_{P}}\right)\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x,

hence one can actually allow for some mild non-monotonicity
\inf f_{m}(x,\bar{m}(x))\bar{m}(x)>-C_{P}^{-1}
, as in
[
21
]
.

We now point out that (
1.4
) can be also related to local minimality properties of
\bar{m}
for a static optimization problem, at least when
\delta=0
. This is motivated by the fact, already mentioned in the introduction, that the stationary PDE system (
1.2
) constitutes the optimality conditions of the minimization of the functional
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(m,w)
under the constraints
-\Delta m+\text{div}(mw)=0
and
\int_{\Omega}m\,{\rm d}x=1
. In the quadratic case, it is possible to further simplify the static optimization problem by introducing a functional
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
, which depends only on the variable
m
, defined as follows

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m):=\int_{\Omega}F(x,m)+\frac{|Dm|^{2}}{2m}{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}F(x,m)+2|D\sqrt{m}|^{2}{\rm d}x,\qquad\forall\sqrt{m}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}m=1.

The next Lemma explains that (
1.4
) holds whenever
\bar{m}
is a strict (local) minimum of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
.

Lemma 1.6
.

If
\bar{m}
is a minimum for
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
such that
\bar{m}
is continuous and everywhere positive, then (
1.4
) holds with
\eta=0
. If, moreover, there exists
\eta^{\prime\prime}>0
such that

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m)-\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{m})\geq\frac{\eta^{\prime\prime}}{2}\int_{\Omega}(m-\bar{m})^{2}{\rm d}x\qquad\forall\sqrt{m}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega),

(1.12)

then assertion (c) of Lemma
1.3
holds (hence (
1.4
) holds for some
\eta>0
).

Proof.

Take
\mu
such that
\mu\in L^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\int_{\Omega}\mu(x){\rm d}x=0
,
\mu/\bar{m}\in W^{1,2}(\bar{m}{\rm d}x)
. Assume also that it has compact support. We take a small variation around the minimum
\bar{m}
, namely
\bar{m}+\varepsilon\mu
, for
\varepsilon>0
small enough so that
\bar{m}+\varepsilon\mu>0
(recall that
\bar{m}
is continuous and everywhere positive, and
\mu\in L^{\infty}
). Then, we compute variations of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
as follows

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m+\varepsilon\mu)=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m)+\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m)[\varepsilon\mu]+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m)[\varepsilon\mu,\varepsilon\mu]\\
-\varepsilon^{3}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\mu\bar{m}}{2(\bar{m}+\varepsilon\mu)}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}-\int_{0}^{1}f_{mm}(x,m+s\varepsilon\mu)\mu^{2}(1-s)^{2}{\rm d}s\right]{\rm d}x,

where

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m)[\varepsilon\mu]=\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\frac{\langle D\bar{m},D\mu\rangle}{\bar{m}}-\mu\frac{|D\bar{m}|^{2}}{2\bar{m}^{2}}+f(x,\bar{m})\mu\,{\rm d}x,

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m)[\varepsilon\mu,\varepsilon\mu]=\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Since
\bar{m}
is a minimum of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
, then letting
\varepsilon\to 0
implies that
\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m)[\mu]=0
and
\delta^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m)[\mu,\mu]\geq 0
for all
\mu
(with compact support). Note that, if
\mu
has not compact support, one can construct a sequence
\mu_{R}=(\mu-c_{R})\phi(x/R)
, where
\phi
is a cutoff function,
c_{R}=\int\mu\phi/\int\phi
, so that
\delta^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m)[\mu_{R},\mu_{R}]\to\delta^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m)[\mu,\mu]
as
R\to\infty
; therefore, (
1.4
) holds with
\eta=0
.

For the second assertion, if we assume the validity of (
1.12
), then
\bar{m}
is still a minimizer of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
, and

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{m}+\varepsilon\mu)-\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{m})\geq\frac{\eta\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mu^{2}{\rm d}x\Longleftrightarrow\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m)[\varepsilon\mu,\varepsilon\mu]+o(\varepsilon^{2})\geq\frac{\eta\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mu^{2}{\rm d}x,

from which we derive Lemma
1.3
(c).
∎

Remark 1.7
.

Note that if
\bar{m}
is a minimizer of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
, then
\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(m)[\mu]=0
for all test functions
\mu
such that
\int_{\Omega}\mu=0
; this means that

-{\rm div}\left(\frac{D\bar{m}}{\bar{m}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{D\bar{m}}{\bar{m}}\right|^{2}+f(x,\bar{m})=\lambda

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. If we let
\bar{u}=-\log\bar{m}
, then the triple
(\bar{u},\bar{m},\lambda)
solves (
1.3
). Note also that the left-hand side of (
1.10
) is nothing but the linearization of the previous equation.

Remark 1.8
.

The minimization of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
can be recast, after the change of variables
m=\varphi^{2}
into the minimization of

\varphi\mapsto\int_{\Omega}F(x,\varphi^{2})+2|D\varphi|^{2}{\rm d}x,\qquad\forall\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\,{\rm d}x=1,

that is related to the normalized solutions of the (stationary) nonlinear Schrödinger equation.

In light of the previous lemmata, the following approach to the construction of stationary solutions satisfying
(
S) becomes quite natural, at least in the case where
\delta=0
. First of all, if one considers minimizers of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
, these satisfy (
1.4
) only in a “weak” sense. Such minimizers are not necessarily stable for the evolutionary problem. However, if in specific cases one can show that these minimizers are strong (or nondegenerate), so that (
1.4
) is fully satisfied, then the results of the paper apply. In any case, given
any
minimizer
\bar{m}
, it is possible to perturb the problem by replacing
f
with
f+\eta(m-\bar{m})
, in such a way that (
1.4
) is satisfied. This perturbation is clearly artificial, but it shows in some way that the minimizers of
(
S) are stable, at least in a generic sense.

The construction of such minimizers deserves a separate discussion that goes beyond the scope of this work. We mention that, besides the convex case (that is when
f
is not monotone), it has been carried out in previous works, see for example
[
14
,
18
,
20
,
24
,
23
,
28
]
. The study of their
nondegeneracy
properties is, to our knowledge, open even in special cases. We believe that this is an interesting research direction, and much can be borrowed from the literature on nonlinear Schrödinger equations, e.g.
[
30
,
36
]
.

1.3
Final remarks

Remark 1.9

(More general Hamiltonians)
.

The presence of a quadratic Hamiltonian allows to present the approach proposed in this paper in a more transparent way, but we believe that more general situations can be addressed similarly. If we revisit the discussion of Section
1.1
replacing the Hamiltonian
|Du|^{2}
by
H(x,Du)
, we first observe that the linearized parabolic system reads

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mu=\Delta\mu+\text{div}(\mu H_{p}(D\bar{u}))+\text{div}(\bar{m}H_{pp}(D\bar{u})Dv),\\
\partial_{t}v=-\Delta v+\langle Dv,H_{p}(D\bar{u})\rangle-f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu+\delta v\\
\end{cases}

so we have the same operator form with

\displaystyle A\mu=\Delta\mu+\text{div}(\mu H_{p}(D\bar{u})),

\displaystyle BB^{*}v=-\text{div}(\bar{m}H_{pp}(D\bar{u})Dv).

Heuristically, our approach can be developed following almost identical lines provided that the symmetry condition

A^{*}(BB^{*})^{-1}=(BB^{*})^{-1}A

holds. There are situations besides the purely quadratic one where the previous identity is satisfied, but those will be explored in future work.

Remark 1.10

(The potential structure)
.

In the MFG framework, the variational point of view on the problem under consideration is related with the structure of the Lagrangian cost
|\alpha|^{2}+f(x,m)
. It is known that, given a more general Lagrangian
L(x,\alpha,m)
, a potential description may not be available. Nevertheless, the approach presented here may still apply, since it works at the level of PDE systems; indeed, one would need to linearize the parabolic problem, and address the symmetry and nondegeneracy conditions discussed in Section
1.1
.

Remark 1.11

(Small discount)
.

The approach presented in the previous section requires
\delta
to be small; if one looks at the main statements, the presence of the discount “unbalances” the exponential decay terms, and the arguments break down if
\delta
is too large. The presence of the turnpike phenomenon is not clear when
\delta
is large. In fact, even if we look at the simple finite dimensional minimization problem

\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\delta t}\left(\frac{|\dot{x}(t)|^{2}}{2}+V(x(t))\right){\rm d}t,

with Euler-Lagrange equation

\ddot{x}_{\delta}(t)-\delta\dot{x}_{\delta}(t)-V^{\prime}(x_{\delta}(t))=0,

whose solution approaches a constant in time function as
\delta
becomes large, since

\frac{\ddot{x}_{\delta}(t)}{\delta}-\dot{x}_{\delta}(t)-\frac{V^{\prime}(x_{\delta}(t))}{\delta}=0\quad\underset{\delta\to+\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\quad-\dot{x}_{\infty}(t)=0.

The stationary problem does not depend on
\delta
, because it is just
V^{\prime}(\bar{x})=0
; hence, if
x_{\delta}(0)\neq\bar{x}
, where
\bar{x}
is such that
V^{\prime}(\bar{x})=0
, then the solution itself remains near
x_{\delta}(0)
, which implies that the turnpike property cannot be satisfied.

Remark 1.12

(Dissipativity)
.

The approach presented here can be also revisited under the lens of
dissipativity
, a notion that is known to be connected to the turnpike property in optimal control theory (see for instance
[
33
]
). In particular, integrating (
1.8
) on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
gives

\Phi(t_{1})\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\langle\mu,Q\mu\rangle+\langle\mu,A^{*}(BB^{*})^{-1}A\mu\rangle{\rm d}t+\Phi(t_{2}),

and whenever the right-hand side controls from above
\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}|\mu|^{2}
, it is known that the turnpike property can be proven, at least in an integral sense (this kind of control from above is in fact our assumption
(
S) ). Comparing the previous inequality with
[
33
, Definition 1]
, one may recognize that
\Phi
plays the role of a
storage function
, though it is time-dependent (and not state-dependent) and the inequality is reversed.

The results in this section are the crucial steps towards the proof of Theorem

, and concern the

and

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+\langle Dv,D\bar{u}\rangle-f(\bar{m}+\mu)+f(\bar{m})+\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{2}+\delta v=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\\

(NL)

Now, consider a solution to the previous system, i.e. a couple
(v,\mu)
which satisfies the two coupled equations. We Taylor expand

\bar{m}
with integral remainder, so that the first equation becomes

-\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+\langle Dv,D\bar{u}\rangle-f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu-\xi\mu^{2}+\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{2}+\delta v=0,\\
\text{where \quad$\xi(x,t)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-z)^{2}f_{mm}(x,\bar{m}(x)+z\mu(x,t))\,{\rm d}z$}

We need to assume some integrability conditions for
\mu

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mu(t),\mu(t)\in L^{1}(\Omega),\quad\mu(t),D\mu(t),D^{2}\mu(t),\mu(t)/\bar{m}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v(t),v(t),Dv(t),D^{2}v(t),D^{3}v(t)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mu(t)=0

for a.e.

.

Remark 2.1
.

It is worth noting that, as all the estimates in this section are either monotone with respect to the initial and terminal data or independent of them, they extend to the following PDE system:

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+\langle Dv,D\bar{u}\rangle-f(\bar{m}+\nu\mu)+f(\bar{m})+\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{2}+\delta v=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
\partial_{t}\mu-\Delta\mu-\text{div}(\bar{m}Dv)-\text{div}(\mu D\bar{u})-\text{div}(\mu Dv)=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
\mu(0,\cdot)=\nu\mu_{0}(\cdot)\quad v(T,\cdot)=\nu v_{T}(\cdot),&\text{ on }\Omega,\\
\end{cases}

(2.2)

for all
\nu\in[0,1]
; this will be important to conclude the fixed point argument in Section
4
. Indeed, it will be crucial below to use the inequality in
(
S) in the more general form

\int_{\Omega}\nu f_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}+\frac{\delta\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}\,{\rm d}x\geq\eta\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}\left|D\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}\right)\right|^{2}{\rm d}x,

where
\eta
is independent of
\nu\in[0,1]
. This is true because the inequality is assumed in
(
S) to hold when
\nu=1
, while for
\nu=0
it is obviously satisfied with
\eta=1
. The general case follows by convex combination.

) and

We proceed directly with the computation of the derivative of

D\bar{m}=-\bar{m}D\bar{u}
. Hypothesis (

) ensures the operation of integration by parts (in particular when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and that derivatives can be moved into integrals.

\dot{\tilde{\Phi}}(t)=\int_{\Omega}e^{-\delta t}\left[(\partial_{t}\mu)v+\mu(\partial_{t}v)-\delta\mu v+2\frac{\mu(\partial_{t}\mu)}{\bar{m}}-\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}\right]{\rm d}x=

From now on, for the sake of simplicity, we will denote by

z:=\frac{\mu}{\bar{m}}.

Changing all the signs, we get the following equality:

-\dot{\tilde{\Phi}}(t)=\int_{\Omega}e^{-\delta t}\Big[\frac{\mu|Dv|^{2}}{2}+\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}+f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\xi\mu^{3}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}+\\
+2\bar{m}|Dz|^{2}+2\bar{m}\langle Dv,Dz\rangle+2\mu\langle Dv,Dz\rangle\Big]{\rm d}x.

(2.4)

We now use the hypothesis
(
S) with
\mu
.
Hence, for all

,

\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu^{2}+\bar{m}|Dz|^{2}+\delta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\geq\eta\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}|Dz|^{2}{\rm d}x,

Then, if one plugs (

\begin{split}-\dot{\tilde{\Phi}}(t)&\geq\int_{\Omega}e^{-\delta t}\left[\frac{\mu|Dv|^{2}}{2}+\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}+\xi\mu^{3}+(1+\eta)\bar{m}|Dz|^{2}+2\bar{m}\langle Dv,Dz\rangle+2\mu\langle Dv,Dz\rangle\right]{\rm d}x=\\

Since it can be easily shown that (as

\bar{m}|Dv+Dz|^{2}+\eta\bar{m}|Dz|^{2}\geq\frac{\eta}{4}\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{4}\bar{m}|Dz|^{2},

we can rewrite the whole inequality as follows:

-\dot{\tilde{\Phi}}(t)\geq\int_{\Omega}e^{-\delta t}\left[\frac{\eta}{4}\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{4}\bar{m}|Dz|^{2}+\xi\mu^{3}+2\mu\langle Dv,Dz\rangle+\frac{\mu|Dv|^{2}}{2}\right]{\rm d}x.

(2.6)

Now, we deal with the last three terms of (

\mu
and
Dv

•

2\mu\langle Dv,Dz\rangle\geq-2|\mu||Dv||Dz|\geq-2\Theta|\mu||Dz|\geq-\Theta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}-\Theta\bar{m}|Dz|^{2};

•

\xi\mu^{3}\geq-C_{f}\left\|\mu(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\bar{m}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}\geq-C_{f}\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Theta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}};

•

\frac{1}{2}\mu|Dv|^{2}\geq-\frac{1}{2}|\mu||Dv|^{2}\geq-\frac{1}{2}\Theta|\mu||Dv|\geq-\frac{1}{4}\Theta\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}-\frac{1}{4}\Theta\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}.

-\dot{\tilde{\Phi}}(t)\geq\\
\int_{\Omega}e^{-\delta t}\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}\left[\frac{\eta}{4}-\frac{\Theta}{4}\right]+e^{-\delta t}\bar{m}|Dz|^{2}\left[\frac{\eta}{4}-\Theta\right]+e^{-\delta t}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}\left[-\Theta C_{f}\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\frac{5\Theta}{4}\right]{\rm d}x\geq

Now, since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bar{m}(x)z(t,x){\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}\mu(t,x){\rm d}x=0
for all
t
, by the Poincaré inequality we have that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}z^{2}\bar{m}{\rm d}x\leq C_{P}\int_{\Omega}|Dz|^{2}\bar{m}{\rm d}x,

hence

-\dot{\tilde{\Phi}}(t)\geq\\
\int_{\Omega}e^{-\delta t}\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}\left[\frac{\eta}{4}-\frac{\Theta}{4}\right]+e^{-\delta t}\bar{m}|Dz|^{2}\left[\frac{\eta}{8}-\Theta\right]+e^{-\delta t}\left[\frac{\eta}{8C_{P}}-\Theta C_{f}\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\frac{5\Theta}{4}\right]\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x.

Now, if

the following inequality follows:

\begin{split}-\dot{\tilde{\Phi}}(t)&\geq\int_{\Omega}e^{-\delta t}\left[\frac{3\eta}{16}\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{16}\bar{m}|Dz|^{2}+\left(\frac{\eta}{8C_{P}}-\Theta C_{f}\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\frac{5\Theta}{4}\right)\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}\right]{\rm d}x\\

where
\hat{v}=v-\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}(y)v(t,y)\,{\rm d}y
.

Hence, by means the Young’s and the triangular inequality, we get that, for
\sigma=\frac{\eta}{32C_{P}}
,

-\dot{\tilde{\Phi}}(t)\geq\sigma\int_{\Omega}e^{-\delta t}|\mu\hat{v}|+e^{-\delta t}\left|\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}\right|{\rm d}x\geq\sigma e^{-\delta t}\left|\int_{\Omega}\mu\hat{v}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\right|\geq\sigma e^{-\delta t}\left|\int_{\Omega}\mu\hat{v}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\right|=\sigma|\tilde{\Phi}(t)|,

since

\int_{\Omega}\mu(t,x)\hat{v}(t,x){\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}\mu v\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}\mu(t,x)\underbrace{\left(\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}(y)v(t,y){\rm d}y\right)}_{=\text{constant w.r.t. }x}{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}\mu(t,x)v(t,x){\rm d}x.

Then, we finally obtain that

\begin{cases}\dot{\tilde{\Phi}}(t)\leq\sigma\tilde{\Phi}(t)\\
\dot{\tilde{\Phi}}(t)\leq-\sigma\tilde{\Phi}(t),\end{cases}

from which, after integration on
[t,T]
and
[0,T]

\tilde{\Phi}(T)e^{-\sigma(T-t)}\leq\tilde{\Phi}(t)\leq\tilde{\Phi}(0)e^{-\sigma t},

(2.8)

which implies (
2.3

Finally, note that integrating (

) on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
gives

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}e^{-\delta t}\left[\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}+\bar{m}|Dz|^{2}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{\bar{m}}\right]{\rm d}x{\rm d}t\leq c[\tilde{\Phi}(t_{1})-\tilde{\Phi}(t_{2})],

(2.9)

where

depends on
\eta,C_{P}
.

1\leq e^{\sigma}e^{-\sigma(T-t)}
)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\bar{m}}{2}|Dv|^{2}(t,x)e^{-\delta t}{\rm d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{\bar{m}}{2}|Dv|^{2}(T,x)e^{-\delta T}{\rm d}x+c\left(\tilde{\Phi}(0)e^{-\sigma t}-\tilde{\Phi}(T)\right)\\
\leq e^{\sigma}e^{-\delta T-\sigma(T-t)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\bar{m}}{2}|Dv|^{2}(T,x){\rm d}x+c\tilde{\Phi}(0)e^{-\sigma t}+ce^{\sigma}|\tilde{\Phi}(T)|e^{-\sigma(T-t)}.

Putting all together, we deduce that

\left\|\sqrt{\bar{m}}|Dv|(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}e^{-\delta t}\leq 2c|\tilde{\Phi}(0)|e^{-\sigma t}+2ce^{\sigma}|\tilde{\Phi}(T)|e^{-\sigma(T-t)}+e^{\sigma}e^{-\delta T-\sigma(T-t)}\left\|\sqrt{\bar{m}}|Dv|(T,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

from which we derive the conclusion, after recalling that
\tilde{\Phi}(t)=e^{-\delta t}\Phi(t)
.
∎

3
L^{\infty}
turnpike a priori estimates

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem

. To this aim, we need to improve the

decay estimates of the previous Section into
L^{\infty}
ones; this will require some technical work, and some further conditions on the initial / terminal data.

Throughout the section,
v,\mu,\Phi
are as in the previous one; the constants
\sigma,\Theta
,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4}
are those appearing in Propositions
2.2
,
2.3
,
2.4
. Finally, we set

\sigma_{1}=\frac{\sigma-\delta}{n+1},\qquad\sigma_{2}=\frac{\sigma+\delta}{n+1}.

Remark 3.1
.

As in Section
2
, we specify that all the estimates in this section hold also for solutions to (
2.2
) for all
\nu\in[0,1]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
are in force. Then, there exist
\bar{\varepsilon}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\bar{\lambda}>0
, depending on
\eta,\bar{m},C_{f}
, but independent of
T
, such that the following is true: for all
\lambda\leq\bar{\lambda}
,
\varepsilon\leq\bar{\varepsilon}
, if

\|\mu(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon\left(e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}\right)\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],

(3.1)

\|\mu_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|v_{T}(\cdot)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}<\lambda

(3.2)

then any solution
(v,\mu)
to the non-linear system (
NL
) satisfies the following estimates:

\|\mu(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\Theta\qquad\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}<\Theta,

(3.3)

for all
t\in[0,T]
, where
\Theta
is as in Proposition
2.2
.

We divide the proof of Proposition
3.2
into two Lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
(v,\mu)
is a solution to (
NL
) and that the assumptions of Proposition
3.2
are in force. Then, the following estimate holds:

\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|v_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}}\right)C_{f}

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{v}(t,x):=v(t,x)e^{-\delta t}
. First, we have to establish the equation satisfied by
\tilde{v}
:

-\partial_{t}\tilde{v}-\Delta\tilde{v}+\langle D\tilde{v},D\bar{u}\rangle-f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu e^{-\delta t}-\xi\mu^{2}e^{-\delta t}+\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{2}e^{-\delta t}=0.

(3.4)

Then, consider
\tilde{\Gamma}
solution to

\begin{cases}\partial_{s}\tilde{\Gamma}-\Delta\tilde{\Gamma}-\text{div}(\tilde{\Gamma}D\bar{u})=0,&\text{ on }(t,T)\times\Omega\\
\tilde{\Gamma}(t,\cdot)=\delta_{x},&\text{ on }\{s=t\}\times\Omega\\
\end{cases}

multiply it by
\tilde{v}
and then multiply (
3.4
) by
\tilde{\Gamma}

After integration with respect to time and space, one gets

\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\Omega}-\partial_{s}\tilde{v}\tilde{\Gamma}-\left(\Delta\tilde{v}\right)\tilde{\Gamma}+\langle D\tilde{v},D\bar{u}\rangle\tilde{\Gamma}-f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu e^{-\delta s}\tilde{\Gamma}-\xi\mu^{2}e^{-\delta s}\tilde{\Gamma}\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}s+\\
+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|Dv|^{2}e^{-\delta s}\tilde{\Gamma}-\partial_{s}\tilde{\Gamma}\tilde{v}+\Delta\tilde{\Gamma}\tilde{v}+\text{div}(\tilde{\Gamma}D\bar{u})\tilde{v}\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}s=0.

Integration by parts in space yields

\int_{\Omega}-\tilde{v}\tilde{\Gamma}\bigg|_{t}^{T}{\rm d}y=\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu e^{-\delta s}\tilde{\Gamma}+\xi\mu^{2}e^{-\delta s}\tilde{\Gamma}-\frac{1}{2}|Dv|^{2}\tilde{\Gamma}e^{-\delta s}{\rm d}y{\rm d}s.

Using the initial condition on
\tilde{\Gamma}
, the fact that
\tilde{\Gamma}
is a flow of probability measures and the square norm of the gradient is positive, we derive that

\tilde{v}(t,x)\leq\int_{\Omega}\tilde{v}(T,y)\tilde{\Gamma}(T,y){\rm d}y+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu e^{-\delta s}\tilde{\Gamma}+\xi\mu^{2}e^{-\delta s}\tilde{\Gamma}\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}s\leq

\leq\|\tilde{v}_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2C_{f}\int_{t}^{T}\|\mu(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}e^{-\delta s}{\rm d}s,

where we used the fact that
\|\mu(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1
and the Hölder’s inequality. Now, we use assumption (
3.1
) to get

\tilde{v}(t,x)\leq\|\tilde{v}_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2C_{f}\int_{t}^{T}\varepsilon\left(e^{-\sigma_{1}s}+e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-s)}\right)e^{-\delta s}{\rm d}s.

(3.5)

To get the other side of the inequality, we define a second flow of probability, called
\bar{\Gamma}
, such that

\begin{cases}\partial_{s}\bar{\Gamma}-\Delta\bar{\Gamma}-\text{div}(\bar{\Gamma}(D\bar{u}+Dv))=0,&\text{ on }(t,T)\times\Omega\\
\bar{\Gamma}(t,\cdot)=\delta_{x},&\text{ on }\{s=t\}\times\Omega\\
\end{cases}

and we do the same computations as before. We get

\tilde{v}(t,x)=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{v}_{T}(y)\bar{\Gamma}(T,y){\rm d}y+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu e^{-\delta s}\bar{\Gamma}+\xi\mu^{2}e^{-\delta s}\bar{\Gamma}-\frac{1}{2}|Dv|^{2}e^{-\delta s}\bar{\Gamma}+\langle Dv,D\tilde{v}\rangle\bar{\Gamma}\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}s.

Recall that
\tilde{v}(t,x)=v(t,x)e^{-\delta t}
, hence from the previous equation we get

\tilde{v}(t,x)=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{v}_{T}(y)\bar{\Gamma}(T,y){\rm d}y+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu e^{-\delta s}\bar{\Gamma}+\xi\mu^{2}e^{-\delta s}\bar{\Gamma}+\frac{1}{2}|Dv|^{2}e^{-\delta s}\bar{\Gamma}\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}s\geq

\geq\int_{\Omega}\tilde{v}_{T}(y)\bar{\Gamma}(T,y){\rm d}y+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\Omega}f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu e^{-\delta s}\bar{\Gamma}+\xi\mu^{2}e^{-\delta s}\bar{\Gamma}{\rm d}y{\rm d}s.

Changing all signs yields

-\tilde{v}(t,x)\leq-\int_{\Omega}\tilde{v}_{T}(y)\bar{\Gamma}(T,y){\rm d}y+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\Omega}-f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu e^{-\delta s}\bar{\Gamma}-\xi\mu^{2}e^{-\delta s}\bar{\Gamma}\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}s\leq

\leq\|\tilde{v}_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2C_{f}\int_{t}^{T}\varepsilon\left(e^{-\sigma_{1}s}+e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-s)}\right)e^{-\delta s}{\rm d}s.

(3.6)

Putting (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) together, we finally get

\|\tilde{v}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|\tilde{v}_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2C_{f}\int_{t}^{T}\varepsilon\left(e^{-\sigma_{1}s}+e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-s)}\right)e^{-\delta s}{\rm d}s,

(3.7)

from which, by definition of
\tilde{v}
, we have

\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|v_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}e^{-\delta(T-t)}+2C_{f}\int_{t}^{T}\varepsilon\left(e^{-\sigma_{1}s}+e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-s)}\right)e^{-\delta(s-t)}{\rm d}s.

Since
s\geq t
, we have that
e^{-\delta(s-t)}\leq 1
, hence

\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|v_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}}\right)C_{f},

which is the thesis.
∎

Now, let us define

C_{v}:=\|v_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}}\right)C_{f}.

(3.8)

It’s clear that it tends to
0
as both
\|v_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and
\varepsilon
approach
0
.
Then, we have to get a "smallness" estimate also for
Dv
. Since the equation for
v
is quadratic in the gradient variable, we can linearize it by using the so-called Cole-Hopf transform, i.e. by defining

w(t,x):=e^{-\frac{v(t,x)}{2}}.

Now, we prove the following

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
(v,\mu)
is a solution to (
NL
) and that the assumptions of Proposition
3.2
are in force. Then, the following estimate holds:

\|Dv(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 4e^{\frac{C_{v}}{2}}\|Dv_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2e^{\frac{C_{v}}{2}}C^{\prime}\left(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}+\delta C_{v}+C_{v}\right),

for all
t\in[0,T]
,
C_{v}
is as in (
3.8
) and
C^{\prime}
depends on
\bar{u},C_{v}
.

Proof.

From the definition of
w
we derive that
v=-2\text{ log}(w)
, hence by the chain rule,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v=-\frac{2}{w}\partial_{t}w,

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}v=-\frac{2}{w}\partial_{x_{i}}w,

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}v=-\frac{2}{w}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}w+\frac{2}{w^{2}}\partial_{x_{i}}w\cdot\partial_{x_{j}}w,

\displaystyle\Delta v=-\frac{2}{w}\Delta w+\frac{2}{w^{2}}|Dw|^{2}

\displaystyle Dv=-\frac{2}{w}Dw.

After substituting the previous expressions into the equation satisfied by
v
, we get that
w
satisfies the following Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}w-\Delta w+\langle Dw,D\bar{u}\rangle+\frac{w}{2}\left(f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu+\xi\mu^{2}-\delta v\right)=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
w(T,\cdot)=e^{-\frac{v_{T}(\cdot)}{2}},&\text{ on }\Omega\\
\end{cases}

We have shown in Lemma
3.3
that
\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{v}
for all
t
, with
C_{v}
independent of
T
.
We now turn our attention to the function
\tilde{w}:=w-1
, that we expect to be close to zero; indeed, since by Lemma
3.3
we can choose
C_{v}
as small as we need (in particular smaller than
1
), we can derive the following estimates on
\tilde{w}
.

e^{-\frac{C_{v}}{2}}\leq w(t,x)\leq e^{\frac{C_{v}}{2}},\qquad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega,

hence

e^{-\frac{C_{v}}{2}}-1\leq w(t,x)-1\leq e^{\frac{C_{v}}{2}}-1,\qquad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega.

It’s true that

e^{\frac{C_{v}}{2}}-1\leq C_{v}\quad\text{ and }\quad e^{-\frac{C_{v}}{2}}-1\geq-\frac{C_{v}}{2}\geq-C_{v},

provided that
C_{v}\leq 1
. We conclude that

-C_{v}\leq w(t,x)-1\leq C_{v}\Rightarrow\|\tilde{w}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\|w(t,\cdot)-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{v}\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

Such function
\tilde{w}
satisfies the following equation:

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}\tilde{w}-\Delta\tilde{w}+\langle D\tilde{w},D\bar{u}\rangle+\frac{w}{2}\left(f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu+\xi\mu^{2}-\delta v\right)=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
\tilde{w}(T,\cdot)=e^{-\frac{v_{T}(\cdot)}{2}}-1,&\text{ on }\Omega.\\
\end{cases}

We can apply Lemma
5.4
with
H=-D\bar{u}
and
g=-\frac{w}{2}\left(f_{m}(\bar{m})\mu+\xi\mu^{2}-\delta v\right)
and we get the following estimate:

\begin{split}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|D\tilde{w}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}&\leq 2\|D\tilde{w}_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&+C\left[\|w\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\left(\|f_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}+\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\mu\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\right)\right]\\
&+C\left(\delta\|w\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}+\|\tilde{w}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\right)\leq\\
&\leq 2\|D\tilde{w}_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C^{\prime}\left(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}+\delta C_{v}+C_{v}\right),\end{split}

(3.9)

for a mighty different
C^{\prime}
, which depends also on
C_{f}
.

Recall that
D\tilde{w}=Dw
, and that
Dv=-2e^{\frac{v}{2}}Dw
. Estimate (
3.9
) can be rewritten as follows:

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|Dv(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 4e^{\frac{C_{v}}{2}}\|Dv_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2e^{\frac{C_{v}}{2}}C^{\prime}_{H}\left(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}+\delta C_{v}+C_{v}\right),

which is the thesis.
∎

We are ready to show Proposition
3.2
.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Gathering all the estimates from Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
and assumption (
3.1
) and using hypothesis (
3.2
), we have that

\|\mu(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon\left(e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}\right)\leq 2\varepsilon,

\begin{split}\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}&\leq C_{v}=\|v_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}}\right)C_{f}\leq\\
&\leq\lambda+2\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}}\right)C_{f},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\|Dv(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}&\leq 4e^{\frac{C_{v}}{2}}\|Dv_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2e^{\frac{C_{v}}{2}}C^{\prime}_{H}\left(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}+\delta C_{v}+C_{v}\right)\leq\\
&\leq 4e^{\frac{C_{v}}{2}}\lambda+2e^{\frac{C_{v}}{2}}C^{\prime}_{H}\left(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}+\delta C_{v}+C_{v}\right),\end{split}

for all

Since we can choose

,

We now state the core of the fixed point argument. First of all, we have to derive some useful estimates for
\Phi(0)
and

Lemma 3.5
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
3.2
, one has the following estimate

|\Phi(0)|+|\Phi(T)|\leq\left\|\frac{\mu_{0}}{\sqrt{\bar{m}}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\frac{\mu_{0}}{\sqrt{\bar{m}}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{C_{P}}{2}\left\|\sqrt{\bar{m}}|Dv|(T,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(3.10)

where
C_{P}
is the Poincaré-Wirtinger constant of
\bar{m}
.

Proof.

Before starting, we notice that, by Proposition
3.2
, we have that in particular

\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\Theta\leq 1.

(3.11)

We start from
\Phi(0)
, by applying Holder’s inequality:

\Phi(0)=\int_{\Omega}\mu_{0}\cdot v(0)+\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{\bar{m}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}\underbrace{v^{2}(0)}_{\leq 1}{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{\bar{m}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{\bar{m}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{\bar{m}}\,{\rm d}x,

where we used (
3.11
).
Then, we observe that, if we define
\hat{v}:=v-\int_{\Omega}v
, since
\int_{\Omega}\mu=0
, we have the following

\Phi(T)=\int_{\Omega}\mu(T,x)v(T,x)+\frac{\mu^{2}(T,x)}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x=\\
=\int_{\Omega}\mu(T,x)\hat{v}(T,x){\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\mu(T,x)\left(\int_{\Omega}v(T,y){\rm d}y\right){\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu^{2}(T,x)}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x=\\
=\int_{\Omega}\mu(T,x)\hat{v}(T,x)+\frac{\mu^{2}(T,x)}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\geq\int_{\Omega}-\frac{\mu^{2}(T,x)}{2\bar{m}}-\frac{\bar{m}\hat{v}^{2}(T,x)}{2}+\frac{\mu^{2}(T,x)}{\bar{m}}{\rm d}x\geq\\
\geq\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu^{2}(T,x)}{2\bar{m}}-\frac{C_{P}}{2}\bar{m}|D\hat{v}|^{2}(T,x){\rm d}x\geq-\int_{\Omega}\frac{C_{P}}{2}\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}(T,x){\rm d}x,

where we used Young’s inequality and we applied the weighted Poincaré inequality.
To get the reverse inequalities, we use (
2.3
) and we obtain

\Phi(0)\geq\Phi(T)e^{-(\sigma+\delta)(T-t)}\geq-e^{-(\sigma+\delta)(T-t)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{C_{P}}{2}\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}(T,x){\rm d}x\geq-\int_{\Omega}\frac{C_{P}}{2}\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}(T,x){\rm d}x,

and

\Phi(T)\leq\Phi(0)e^{-(\sigma-\delta)t}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{\bar{m}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{\bar{m}}\,{\rm d}x.

Putting all the previous estimates together, we get (
3.10
).
∎

Proof.

Therefore, (

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|\mu(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|\mu_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C\left(\|\bar{m}Dv\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n+1}(\Omega))}+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n}(\Omega))}\right),

\begin{split}\|\bar{m}Dv\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n+1}(\Omega))}&=\underset{s\in(t-\tau,t)}{\sup}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\bar{m}|^{n+1}|Dv|^{n+1}(s,y){\rm d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\\
&\leq\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\|Dv\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}\underset{s\in(t-\tau,t)}{\sup}\left(\int_{\Omega}\bar{m}|Dv|^{2}(s,y){\rm d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\\
&\leq\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\|Dv\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}\underset{s\in(t-\tau,t)}{\sup}\left(\lambda_{3}e^{-(\sigma-\delta)s}+\lambda_{4}e^{-(\sigma+\delta)(T-s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\\
&\leq\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\|Dv\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}\underset{s\in(t-\tau,t)}{\sup}\left(\lambda_{3}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}e^{-\sigma_{1}s}+\lambda_{4}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-s)}\right)\\
&\leq\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\|Dv\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}\left(\lambda_{3}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}e^{-\sigma_{1}(t-\tau)}+\lambda_{4}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}\right)\\
&=\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\|Dv\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}\left(\lambda_{3}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}e^{\sigma_{1}\tau}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+\lambda_{4}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}\right).\end{split}

(3.17)

Now we proceed with the other term in brackets.

\begin{split}\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n}(\Omega))}&=\underset{s\in(t-\tau,t)}{\sup}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\mu|^{n}(s,y){\rm d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
&\leq\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\|\bar{m}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\underset{s\in(t-\tau,t)}{\sup}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu^{2}(s,y)}{\bar{m}(y)}{\rm d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
&\leq\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\|\bar{m}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\underset{s\in(t-\tau,t)}{\sup}\left(\lambda_{1}e^{-(\sigma-\delta)s}+\lambda_{2}e^{-(\sigma+\delta)(T-s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
&\leq\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\|\bar{m}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\underset{s\in(t-\tau,t)}{\sup}\left(\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{-\frac{\sigma-\delta}{n}s}+\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{-\frac{\sigma+\delta}{n}(T-s)}\right)\\
&\leq\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\|\bar{m}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{-\sigma_{1}(t-\tau)}+\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}\right)\\
&=\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\|\bar{m}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{\sigma_{1}\tau}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}\right)\\
\end{split}

(3.18)

Plugging estimates (
3.17
) and (
3.18
) into (
3.16
) we get

\begin{split}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|\mu(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}&\leq 2\|\mu_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\|Dv\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}\left(\lambda_{3}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}e^{\sigma_{1}\tau}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+\lambda_{4}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}\right)\\
&+C\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\|\bar{m}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{\sigma_{1}\tau}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}\right)\\
&\leq 2\|\mu_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\left(\lambda_{3}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}e^{\sigma_{1}\tau}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+\lambda_{4}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}\right)\\
&+C\|\bar{m}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{\sigma_{1}\tau}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}\right)\end{split}

Since
\lambda_{i}
, for all
i=1,...,4
, go to
0
as
\lambda
goes to
0
, and the same happens for
\|\mu_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, we find that there exists
\tilde{\lambda}>0
(possibly smaller than
\bar{\lambda}
) such that

\|\mu(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{2}\left(e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}\right)\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],

i.e. (
3.15
).
∎

4
Fixed point theorem and existence of solutions

Throughout this section, we restrict ourselves to the periodic case
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{n}
. We are going to build a solution
(u^{T},m^{T})
to (
1.1
) which satisfies the turnpike property with respect to a stationary solution
(\bar{u},\bar{m})
to (
1.2
), for every
T>0
. Despite the estimates shown in Sections
2
and
3
hold also for
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we restrict ourselves here to the torus to avoid further technicalities that come from the non compact nature of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We point out that solutions
(u^{T},m^{T})
are not
a priori
unique, but our claim is that we are able to find at least one solution which is exponentially close to
(\bar{u},\bar{m})
.

Then, in the second part of the present section, we will prove also an existence result for the infinite horizon problem

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\frac{|Du|^{2}}{2}-f(x,m)+\delta u=0,&\text{ on }(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\\
\partial_{t}m-\Delta m-\text{div}(mDu)=0,&\text{ on }(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\\
m(0,x)=m_{0}(x),\qquad u\text{ bounded },&\text{ on }\mathbb{T}^{n},\end{cases}

(4.1)

such that
m
is exponentially close to
\bar{m}
for large
t
.

Our first theorem (that is Theorem
1.2
in the introduction) guarantees the existence of a solution to (
1.1
) which satisfies the exponential turnpike decay, for each
T>0
, under some smallness assumptions on the initial / terminal data and
(
S) , that allow to apply the estimates in Section
3
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{n}
and
T>0
be fixed. Suppose that
(\bar{u},\bar{m})
is a (classical) solution to (
1.2
) and that
(
S) holds. Then, there exist constants
\bar{\varepsilon}>0,\lambda>0,\sigma>0
(which depend only on
\bar{m},\bar{u},\eta,C_{f}
but not on the time horizon
T
) such that, if

\delta<\sigma,\quad\|m_{0}(\cdot)-\bar{m}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\|u_{T}(\cdot)-\bar{u}(\cdot)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}<\lambda,

then there exists a (classical) solution
(u^{T},m^{T})
to (
1.1
) satisfying the following properties:

\displaystyle\|m^{T}(t,\cdot)-\bar{m}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{2}\left(e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}\right),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{\bar{m}(\cdot)}\left|Du^{T}(t,\cdot)-D\bar{u}(\cdot)\right|\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq\lambda_{3}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+\lambda_{4}e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)},

(4.3)

for all
t\in[0,T]
, where
\sigma_{1}=\frac{\sigma-\delta}{n+1}
,
\sigma_{2}=\frac{\sigma+\delta}{n+1}
,
\lambda_{3}
and
\lambda_{4}
come from Lemma
3.7
.

The proof relies on a fixed point argument; more precisely, it makes use of the following nonlinear version of the so-called
Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem
:

Theorem 4.2

(
[
22
]
, Theorem 11.6)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
T
be a compact mapping of
X\times[0,1]
into
X
such that
T(x,0)=0
for all
x\in X
. Suppose there exists a constant
M
such that

\|x\|_{X}<M

(4.4)

for all
(x,\nu)\in X\times[0,1]
satisfying
x=T(x,\nu)
. Then, the mapping
T(x,1)
has a fixed point.

The idea is to build an operator in a suitable Banach space and to exploit the
a priori
estimates of Sections
2
and
3
. Note also that, from the proof of Theorem
4.2
, in order to find a fixed point, it suffices to check (
4.4
) for fixed points such that
(x,\nu)\in\overline{B}_{M}(0)\times[0,1]
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
T>0
be fixed. To simplify the notation, throughout the proof, we drop the superscript
T
; thus, the triple
(\rho,u,m)
should be intended as
(\rho^{T},u^{T},m^{T})
. The ambient Banach space is defined as follows:

X:=\mathcal{C}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}).

We look for a solution in this space. We equip it with the norm

\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\rho\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{X}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\frac{\|\rho(t,\cdot)\|_{{\infty},\mathbb{T}^{n}}}{e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+e^{-\sigma_{2}(T-t)}}\right),

(4.5)

where
\sigma_{1}=\frac{\sigma-\delta}{n+1}
and
\sigma_{2}=\frac{\sigma+\delta}{n+1}
and
\sigma
comes from Proposition
2.2
.

Notice that
\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\rho\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{X}
and

\|\rho\|_{X}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\rho(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty,\mathbb{T}^{n}}

(4.6)

are equivalent (
T
is fixed). In particular,
(X,\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{X})
is a Banach space, since
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
is.
Therefore, to prove that a sequence is converging in
X
, we can use equivalently either the
\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{X}
norm or the
\|\cdot\|_{X}
one.
Now, let

U:=\left\{\rho\in X:\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\rho\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{X}\leq\bar{\varepsilon}\right\},

where
\bar{\varepsilon}
comes from Proposition
3.2
. We define
\mathcal{F}:U\times[0,1]\to X
such that
\mathcal{F}(\rho,\nu)=\mu
, where
\mu
is the solution to the following PDE system:

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+\langle Dv,D\bar{u}\rangle-f(x,\bar{m}+\nu\rho)+f(x,\bar{m})+\delta v+\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{2}=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\\
\partial_{t}\mu-\Delta\mu-\text{div}(\bar{m}Dv)-\text{div}(\mu D\bar{u})-\text{div}(\mu Dv)=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\\
\mu(0,\cdot)=\nu\mu_{0}(\cdot)\qquad v(T,\cdot)=\nu v_{T}(\cdot).&\text{ on }\mathbb{T}^{n},\end{cases}

(4.7)

where
\mu_{0}=m_{0}-\bar{m}
and
v_{T}=u_{T}-\bar{u}
.

Let us briefly describe how the operator acts. It takes
\rho\in U
, sends it to the solution
v
to the first equation in (
4.7
), with the corresponding terminal condition, and then maps it to
\mu
, solution to the second equation of the same PDE system, with the related initial datum. Notice that the fixed points
\mu=\mathcal{F}(\mu,1)
are precisely the solutions to (
NL
).

We need to prove that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
4.2
.

Step 1:
\mathcal{F}
is continuous.

Let
\{(\rho_{k},\nu_{k})\}_{k}
be a sequence in
U\times[0,1]
such that
(\rho_{k},\nu_{k})\to(\rho,\nu)
as
k\to+\infty
, for some
\rho\in X
and some
\nu\in[0,1]
, i.e.

\|\rho_{k}-\rho\|_{X}\to 0\qquad|\nu_{k}-\nu|\to 0,

as
k\to+\infty
. We have to prove that
\mu_{k}\to\mu
in
X
, where
\mu_{k}:=\mathcal{F}(\rho_{k},\nu_{k})
and
\mu:=\mathcal{F}(\rho,\nu)
. We can do it by using any of the equivalent norms (
4.5
) and (
4.6
); in particular, we wish to prove that

\|\mu_{k}-\mu\|_{X}=\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|\mu_{k}(t,\cdot)-\mu(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\to 0,\qquad\text{ for }k\to+\infty.

Recall that, by assumption,
\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\rho_{k}\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\leq\bar{\varepsilon}
, hence the same procedure described in Proposition
3.2
allows us to say that

\|Dv_{k}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq\Theta<1,

for all
t\in[0,T]
and for all
k
.
The same reasoning holds also for
\rho
and
Dv
, since
\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\rho\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\leq\bar{\varepsilon}
.

First of all, we define
\hat{v}_{k}:=v_{k}-v
. While computing the difference between the equations satisfied by
v_{k}
and
v
, the following term involving
f
appears:

-f(x,\bar{m}+\nu_{k}\rho_{k})+f(x,\bar{m}+\nu\rho)=-\xi_{\nu}(t,x)(\nu_{k}\rho_{k}-\nu\rho),

where
\xi_{\nu}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-z)f_{m}(x,\bar{m}+\nu\rho+z(\nu_{k}\rho_{k}-\nu\rho))\,{\rm d}z
. Hence, the equation satisfied by
\hat{v}_{k}
is

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}\hat{v}_{k}-\Delta\hat{v}_{k}+\langle D\hat{v}_{k},D\bar{u}\rangle-\xi_{\nu}(\nu_{k}\rho_{k}-\nu\rho)+\delta\hat{v}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\langle D\hat{v}_{k},Dv_{k}+Dv\rangle=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{n},\\
\hat{v}_{k}(T,\cdot)=(\nu_{k}-\nu)v_{T}(\cdot),&\text{ on }\mathbb{T}^{n}.\end{cases}

In the same way as (
3.7
) is obtained in Lemma
3.3
, we get the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|\hat{v}_{k}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq|\nu_{k}-\nu|\|v_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\int_{t}^{T}\|\xi_{\nu}\|_{X}\cdot\|(\nu_{k}\rho_{k}-\nu\rho)(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq|\nu_{k}-\nu|(\|v_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}+TC_{f}\|\rho_{k}\|_{X})+TC_{f}\|\rho_{k}-\rho\|_{X},

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Since the sequence
\rho_{k}
converges to
\rho\in X
,
\nu_{k}\to\nu
in
[0,1]
by hypothesis and
v_{T}
are bounded, we have that

\|v_{k}-v\|_{X}\to 0,\qquad\text{ for }k\to+\infty.

Let us define
F_{k}:=Dv_{k}+Dv
. We apply Lemma
5.4
to the equation in (
3.4
), with
H=-D\bar{u}-\frac{1}{2}F_{k}
and
g=\xi_{\nu}(\nu_{k}\rho_{k}-\nu\rho)-\delta(v_{k}-v)
. Note that
H
is bounded uniformly in
k
because both
Dv_{k}
and
Dv
are. Therefore, we get:

\displaystyle\|D\hat{v}_{k}\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|D\hat{v}_{k}(T,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}+C\left(\|\xi_{\nu}\|_{X}\cdot\|\nu_{k}\rho_{k}-\nu\rho\|_{X}+(\delta+1)\|v_{k}-v\|_{X}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(|\nu_{k}-\nu|\|Dv_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}+C\cdot C_{f}\|\rho_{k}\|_{X}\right)+C\left(C_{f}\|\rho_{k}-\rho\|_{X}+(\delta+1)\|v_{k}-v\|_{X}\right),

where
C
depends on
\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}
and
\Theta
, with the right hand side going to
0
as
k\to+\infty
.

Finally, let
\hat{\mu}_{k}:=\mu_{k}-\mu
. This function satisfies the following PDE:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\hat{\mu}_{k}-\Delta\hat{\mu}_{k}-\text{div}(\hat{\mu}_{k}D\bar{u})-\text{div}(\bar{m}D\hat{v}_{k})-\text{div}(\hat{\mu}_{k}Dv)-\text{div}(\mu_{k}D\hat{v}_{k})=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\\
\hat{\mu}_{k}(0,\cdot)=(\nu_{k}-\nu)\mu_{0}(\cdot),&\text{ on }\mathbb{T}^{n}.\end{cases}

A direct application of Lemma
5.2
requires to control the spatial
L^{n}
norm of
\hat{\mu}_{k}
, at least on
[\tau,T]
. To avoid it, we take advantage of the fact that
T
is fixed, of estimate (
5.3
) in Remark
5.3
and the boundedness of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
to get:

\begin{split}\|\hat{\mu}_{k}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}&\leq C\left(|\nu_{k}-\nu|\|\mu_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\|\bar{m}D\hat{v}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))}+\|\mu_{k}D\hat{v}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(|\nu_{k}-\nu|\|\mu_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\|\bar{m}D\hat{v}_{k}\|_{X}+\|\mu_{k}\|_{X}\cdot\|D\hat{v}_{k}\|_{X}\right),\end{split}

(4.8)

where
C
depends on
\tau,T,\|D\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}
. Moreover, since
\|Dv_{k}\|_{X}
are uniformly bounded in
k
, a further application of estimate (
5.3
) in Remark
5.3
, we observe that also
\|\mu_{k}\|_{X}
are uniformly bounded in
k
. This enables us to improve (
4.8
) as follows:

\|\hat{\mu}_{k}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C\left(|\nu_{k}-\nu|\|\mu_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\|\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\cdot\|D\hat{v}_{k}\|_{X}+\tilde{C}\|D\hat{v}_{k}\|_{X}\right),\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

The right hand side goes to
0
, hence we conclude that

\|\mu_{k}-\mu\|_{X}\to 0,

for
k\to+\infty
.

Step 2:
\mathcal{F}
is compact.

At this stage of the proof, we take advantage of the compactness of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
to deduce compactness for
\mathcal{F}
. To reach this aim, we wish to prove that, given a sequence
\mu_{k}:=\mathcal{F}(\rho_{k},\nu_{k})
, for some
\{(\rho_{k},\nu_{k})\}\subseteq U\times[0,1]
, it admits a limit point
\mu\in X
with respect to the convergence given by either the
\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{X}
or the
\|\cdot\|_{X}
norm.
By the previous step, we already know that both
\mu_{k}
and
v_{k}
are bounded with respect to the norm on
X
uniformly in
k
.
Then, since
D\bar{u}
,
\bar{m}
,
D\bar{m}
,
v_{k}
,
\rho_{k}
are uniformly (in
k
) bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
for any
p\geq 1
, from standard results in parabolic regularity theory (see for instance
[
25
]
), we deduce that
\mu_{k}
is bounded in
\mathcal{C}^{\theta,\theta/2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
independent of
k
.
As a consequence, the whole sequence
\{\mu_{k}\}
is equi-continuous in
X
, thus one can apply Ascoli-Arzelà theorem. Therefore, there exists
\mu\in X
and a subsequence
\{\mu_{k_{j}}\}
such that

\|\mu_{k_{j}}-\mu\|_{X}\to 0\qquad\text{ for }j\to+\infty,

hence the compactness of
\mathcal{F}
.

Step 3: Fixed points satisfy
\mu=\mathcal{F}(\mu,\nu)\not\in\partial U
, i.e.
\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\mu\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{X}<\bar{\varepsilon}
.

In this final step we will use the
a priori
estimates of Sections
2
and
3
.

Note that Proposition
3.9
requires the fundamental hypothesis
(
S) and the integrability assumptions (
2.1
) (condition (
3.14
) is a consequence of (
3.13
) on the torus). To get the latter, we show first that
(v,\mu)
possess further regularity properties, by means of a bootstrap argument. First of all, starting from
\mu\in\mathcal{C}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})
, we immediately observe that both
v
and
Dv
are bounded with respect to the norm on
X
, hence they belong to any
L^{p}
space, for
p\geq 1
; thus, standard parabolic regularity theory, for instance
[
25
,
27
]
, yields that
\mu\in\mathcal{C}^{\theta,\theta/2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
, as in the previous compactness step. Again, making use of the extra regularity just derived, we see that
v\in\mathcal{C}^{2+\theta,1+\theta/2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Computing the divergence terms in the second equation yields that also
\mu\in\mathcal{C}^{2+\theta,1+\theta/2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Actually,
v
has at least one additional continuous spatial derivative, hence the regularity assumptions in (
2.1
) are satisfied because
\bar{m}
has a strictly positive minimum on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Note also that,
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{n}
and the structure of the equation for
\mu
has only terms in divergence form, we notice that, by integration by parts,

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mu(t,x)\,{\rm d}x=0,

hence

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mu(t,x)\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mu_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x=0.

We finally apply Proposition
3.9
(note that the estimate holds for all
\nu
in view of Remarks
2.1
and
3.1
) to get that

\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\mu\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{X}\leq\bar{\varepsilon}\Longrightarrow\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\!\left\lvert\mu\right\rvert\!\right\rvert\!\right\rvert_{X}\leq\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{2}<\bar{\varepsilon},

i.e.
\mu\not\in\partial U
, hence the fixed points of
\mathcal{F}(\rho,\nu)
cannot lie on the boundary of
U
for all
\nu\in[0,1]
.

Lastly, we need to verify that
\mathcal{F}(\cdot,0)=0
on
U
. Note that
\mu:=\mathcal{F}(\rho,0)
satisfies the following PDE system

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+\langle Dv,D\bar{u}\rangle+\delta v+\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{2}=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\\
\partial_{t}\mu-\Delta\mu-\text{div}(\bar{m}Dv)-\text{div}(\mu D\bar{u})-\text{div}(\mu Dv)=0,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\\
\mu(0,\cdot)=0\qquad v(T,\cdot)=0.&\text{ on }\mathbb{T}^{n}\end{cases}

Since
v\equiv 0
is the unique solution of the first equation, we deduce that
\mu\equiv 0
, by uniqueness for the second equation.

Therefore, the operator
\mathcal{F}(\rho,1)
admits a fixed point
\mu\in\bar{U}
, which means a couple
(v,\mu)
that solves system (
NL
) and satisfies the turnpike property.

Finally, if we take
m^{T}:=\mu+\bar{m}
and
u^{T}:=v+\bar{u}
, we get a solution to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
which satisfies the turnpike property (
4.2
). As regards property (
4.3
), it follows from Lemma
3.7
.
∎

Estimate (
4.2
) plays a fundamental role in the proof of the existence of solutions to the infinite horizon problem. More precisely, the fact that the constant
\bar{\varepsilon}
is independent of
T
allows us to pass to the limit as
T\to+\infty
and to preserve the same estimate also for the limit function. We are able to state the existence theorem for (
4.1
).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Suppose that
(\bar{u},\bar{m})
is a (classical) solution to (
1.2
) and that
(
S) holds. Then, there exist constants
\lambda>0,\bar{\varepsilon}>0,\sigma>0
(which depend only on
\bar{m},\bar{u},\eta,C_{f}
) such that if

\delta<\sigma,\quad\|m_{0}(\cdot)-\bar{m}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}<\lambda,

then there exists a (classical) solution
(u^{\infty},m^{\infty})
to (
4.1
) satisfying the following properties:

\displaystyle\|m^{\infty}(t,\cdot)-\bar{m}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{2}e^{-\sigma_{1}t},

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{\bar{m}(\cdot)}\left|Du^{\infty}(t,\cdot)-D\bar{u}(\cdot)\right|\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq\lambda_{3}e^{-\sigma_{1}t},

for all
t>0
, where
\sigma_{1}=\frac{\sigma-\delta}{n+1}
and
\lambda_{3}
comes from Lemma
3.7
.

Proof.

The proof relies on a diagonal argument.
From Theorem
4.1
, we already know that, for each
T>0
, there exists a solution
(v^{T},\mu^{T})
to (
NL
) which satisfies the turnpike property, if the initial / terminal data are small enough.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and consider a solution
(v_{k},\mu_{k})
to the following MFG system

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}v_{k}-\Delta v_{k}+\langle Dv_{k},D\bar{u}\rangle-f(\bar{m}+\mu_{k})+f(\bar{m})+\frac{|Dv_{k}|^{2}}{2}+\delta v_{k}=0,&\text{ on }(0,k)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\\
\partial_{t}\mu_{k}-\Delta\mu_{k}-\text{div}(\bar{m}Dv_{k})-\text{div}(\mu_{k}D\bar{u})-\text{div}(\mu_{k}Dv_{k})=0,&\text{ on }(0,k)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\\
\mu_{k}(0,\cdot)=\mu_{0}(\cdot)\quad v_{k}(k,\cdot)=0,&\text{ on }\mathbb{T}^{n},\\
\end{cases}

(4.9)

where
\mu_{0}=m_{0}-\bar{m}
.

Fix now
j=1
. Each couple of functions
(v_{k},\mu_{k})
admits a restriction to
[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
(if
k\geq 1
) which is again a solution to the equations in (
4.9
). Moreover, since by Theorem
4.1

\|\mu_{k}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{2}\left(e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+e^{-\sigma_{2}(k-t)}\right)\leq\bar{\varepsilon}\qquad\forall t\in[0,k],

it is true that for all
k>1
(i.e. if
k
is large enough)

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\|\mu_{k}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq\bar{\varepsilon},

which means that
\{\mu_{k}\}
is equibounded on
[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
. The same regularity argument used in Theorem
4.1
enables us to establish that the sequence
\{\mu_{k}\}
is also equi-continuous on
[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Therefore, Ascoli-Arzelà theorem yields that there exists a subsequence
\{\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{(1)}\}\subseteq\{\mu_{k}\}
such that

\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{(1)}\to\mu^{(1)},

for some
\mu^{(1)}
defined on
[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
, and where the convergence is uniform in time (more precisely, it is the convergence with respect to one of the equivalent norms on
X
, where
X
is as in Theorem
4.1
with
T=1
).

We now need to do the same for
v_{k}
. We consider the sequence
\{\tilde{v}_{k}^{(1)}\}
, corresponding to
\{\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{(1)}\}
. As a first step, we know that, by the previous Theorem and Lemmata in Section
3
,

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\|\tilde{v}_{k}^{(1)}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq\sup_{t\in[0,k]}\|\tilde{v}_{k}^{(1)}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C,

for some
C
independent of
T
and
k
. Moreover, standard parabolic regularity theory yields that
\tilde{v}_{k}^{(1)}
are Hölder continuous, with constants independent of
k
. This implies that the whole sequence is equi-continuous. We can apply again Ascoli-Arzelà theorem to extract a uniformly converging subsequence
v_{k}^{(1)}\to v^{(1)}
for some
v^{(1)}
defined on
[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Thus, because of the global estimates for
v_{k}^{(1)}
on
[0,k]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
, also
\|v^{(1)}(1,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C
, where
C
is the same as above.
Finally, we take the subsequence
\{\mu_{k}^{(1)}\}
, where each
\mu_{k}^{(1)}
corresponds to
v_{k}^{(1)}
in the couple
(v_{k}^{(1)},\mu_{k}^{(1)})
which solves both equations in (
4.9
). Again, by uniqueness of limit,
\mu_{k}^{(1)}\to\mu^{(1)}
on
[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
as
k\to+\infty
.

Summing up, so far we have obtained subsequences
\{\mu_{k}^{(1)}\}
and
\{v_{k}^{(1)}\}
converging to
\mu^{(1)}
and
v^{(1)}
respectively, on
[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

To go on, fix now
j=2
. By repeating the same argument as above, one gets that there exists a subsequence
\{\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{(2)}\}\subseteq\{\mu_{k}^{(1)}\}
such that

\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{(2)}\to\mu^{(2)},

for some
\mu^{(2)}
defined on
[0,2]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Notice that

\mu^{(2)}\big|_{[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}}=\mu^{(1)},

by uniqueness of limit, since
\{\tilde{\mu}_{k}^{(2)}\}
is a subsequence of
\{\mu_{k}^{(1)}\}
.

Analogously, we get that from the corresponding
\{\tilde{v}_{k}^{(2)}\}
, one can extract a subsequence
\{v_{k}^{(2)}\}
which converges to some
v^{(2)}
on
[0,2]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

We iterate the procedure; in the end, we obtain that there exist subsequences
\{\mu_{k}^{(j)}\}\subseteq\{\mu_{k}\}
,
\{v_{k}^{(j)}\}\subseteq\{v_{k}\}
and sequences
\{\mu^{(j)}\}
,
\{v^{(j)}\}
such that
\mu_{k}^{(j)}\to\mu^{(j)}
,
v_{k}^{(j)}\to v^{(j)}
(uniformly), as
k\to+\infty
,
\mu^{(j)}\big|_{[0,l]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}}=\mu^{(l)}
and
v^{(j)}\big|_{[0,l]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}}=v^{(l)}
for all
l<j
. As a consequence of these facts, one takes diagonal subsequences
\{\mu^{(k)}_{k}\}
and
\{v^{(k)}_{k}\}
, which converge (respectively) to the (well-posed) functions

\displaystyle\mu(t,x):=\mu^{(j)}(t,x),\qquad\text{ if }t\in[0,j]\text{ for some }j,

\displaystyle v(t,x):=v^{(j)}(t,x),\qquad\text{ if }t\in[0,j]\text{ for some }j,

where the convergence is local in time.

The previous functions form a solution to the infinite horizon problem with initial condition given by
\mu_{0}=m_{0}-\bar{m}
and with
v
bounded for all
t>0
. In particular, the latter is true since
v
is built by means of the functions
v^{(j)}
, which are as well bounded by a constant independent of
T
.

Finally, since each
\mu_{k}^{(k)}
satisfies the turnpike property

\|\mu_{k}^{(k)}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{2}\left(e^{-\sigma_{1}t}+e^{-\sigma_{2}(k-t)}\right)\qquad\forall t\in[0,k],

if we fix
t\in[0,k]
, then

\|\mu(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\left\|\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mu_{k}^{(k)}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\mu_{k}^{(k)}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{2}e^{-\sigma_{1}t}.

The previous inequality holds for all
t>0
, hence the thesis, after having defined
m^{\infty}:=\mu+\bar{m}
and
u^{\infty}:=v+\bar{u}
. In fact, also
u^{\infty}
is bounded since both
v
and
\bar{u}
are. As regards property (
4.3
), it follows from Lemma
3.7
, passing to the limit as
T\to+\infty
.

∎

Appendix

This final section is devoted to present the proof of some useful inequalities used in Section
3
to get the
a priori
estimates. We point out that similar estimates can be found also in
[
19
]
; however, we need more precise ones, with constants independent of the time horizon
T
, and to do this we have to perform a slightly more refined work.

Moreover, we specify that the estimates in this Section hold for both
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{n}
and
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

First of all, we recall that the heat kernel is defined as the function

\Gamma(t,x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{n}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}},&\text{ if }t>0\\
0,&\text{ if }t\leq 0,\end{cases}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recall that the
L^{1}
norm of
\Gamma(t,\cdot)
over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is constant and equal to 1, for
t>0
.
We briefly recall here some very well-known results about the
L^{p}
norms of
\Gamma
and its gradient both on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. The heat kernel on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
has a similar structure, and in particular it has the same behavior as on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exist two constants
C_{1}(n,p)
and
C_{2}(n,p)
such that, for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
,

\|\Gamma(t,x)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\frac{1}{p^{\frac{n}{2p}}\cdot(4\pi t)^{\frac{n}{2p^{\prime}}}}=:C_{1}(n,p)t^{-\frac{n}{2p^{\prime}}}

and

\left\||D\Gamma|(t,x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=C_{2}(n,p)t^{-\frac{n}{2p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}},

where
p^{\prime}
is such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
(with
p^{\prime}=+\infty
if
p=1
).

We now prove two Lemmas used in Section
3
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
z
be the unique (mild) solution to the following linear forward PDE:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}z-\Delta z={\rm div}(zF)+{\rm div}(G),&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
z(0,\cdot)=z_{0}(\cdot),&\text{ on }\Omega.\\
\end{cases}

Suppose that
F\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)
,
F\neq 0
,
G\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{n+1}(\Omega))
.
Assume moreover that
z\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{n}(\Omega))
. Then, the following estimate holds:

\|z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|z_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{F}\left(\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n+1}(\Omega))}+\mathbbm{1}_{[\tau,T]}(t)\|z\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n}(\Omega))}\right),

(5.1)

for all
t\in[0,T]
, where
\tau=\frac{1}{16\|F\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}C_{2}(n)^{2}}
and
C_{F}
depends on
F,n,\tau
.

Proof.

First of all, let
C_{1}(n):=C_{1}(n,\frac{n}{n-1})
,
C_{2}(n):=C_{2}(n,1)
and
\tilde{C}_{2}(n):=C_{2}(n,\frac{n+1}{n})
, where
C_{1}(n,p)
and
C_{2}(n,p)
are as in Lemma
5.1
. We consider the PDE as a heat equation with an external source; therefore, we use Duhamel representation formula to get the following expression

\begin{split}z(t,x)&=\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(t,x-y)z_{0}(y){\rm d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(t-u,x-y)\left[\text{div}(zF)+\text{div}(G)\right](u,y){\rm d}y{\rm d}u\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(t,x-y)z_{0}(y){\rm d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\langle D\Gamma(t-u,x-y),-z(u,y)F(u,y)-G(u,y)\rangle{\rm d}y{\rm d}u.\end{split}

Thus, by means of Hölder’s inequality for convolutions,

\|z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|\Gamma(t-u,x-\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\cdot\|z_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\|D\Gamma(t-u,x-\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\|z(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}{\rm d}u+\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\|D\Gamma(t-u,x-\cdot)\|_{L^{\frac{n+1}{n}}(\Omega)}\|G(u)\|_{L^{n+1}(\Omega)}{\rm d}u\\
\leq\|z_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{2}(n)\|F\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\underset{u\in[0,\tau]}{\sup}\|z(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\rm d}u+\\
+\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{-1+\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G(u)\|_{L^{n+1}(\Omega)}{\rm d}u,

where
\tau
is to be fixed and we consider only
t\in[0,\tau]
.
After performing the change of variables
r:=t-u
, we get

\begin{split}&\leq\|z_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{2}(n)\|F\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\underset{u\in[0,\tau]}{\sup}\|z(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{0}^{t}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r\\
&+\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\int_{0}^{t}r^{-1+\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G(t-r)\|_{L^{n+1}(\Omega)}{\rm d}r\\
&\leq\|z_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{2}(n)\|F\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\underset{u\in[0,\tau]}{\sup}\|z(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}2\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}+\\
&+\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\int_{0}^{\tau}r^{-1+\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G(t-r)\|_{L^{n+1}(\Omega)}{\rm d}r\\
&\leq\|z_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{2}(n)\|F\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\underset{u\in[0,\tau]}{\sup}\|z(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}2\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}+\\
&+(2n+2)\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\tau^{\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n+1}(\Omega))}.\end{split}

Choose
\tau=\frac{1}{16C_{2}(n)^{2}\|F\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}}
and take the supremum over
[0,\tau]
in the left hand side. Therefore,

\underset{t\in[0,\tau]}{\sup}\|z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|z_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\underset{u\in[0,\tau]}{\sup}\|z(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+(2n+2)\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\tau^{\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n+1}(\Omega))},

from which

\underset{t\in[0,\tau]}{\sup}\|z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|z_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2(2n+2)\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\tau^{\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n+1}(\Omega))}.

which gives the first part of the estimate (
5.1
).

For larger times, we have to use a cut-off method. Fix
s\in(0,T-1)
and let

\eta(t):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }t\in[0,s]\\
t-s,&\text{ if }t\in[s,s+1]\\
1,&\text{ if }t\in[s+1,T]\\
\end{cases}

and let
\tilde{z}(t,x)=\eta(t)z(t,x)
. We have that

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\tilde{z}=\eta\partial_{t}z+\eta^{\prime}z&=\eta(\Delta z+\text{div}(zF)+\text{div}(G))+\eta^{\prime}z=\\
&=\Delta\tilde{z}+\text{div}(\tilde{z}F)+\eta\text{ div}(G)+\eta^{\prime}z,\end{split}

with initial condition
\tilde{z}(0,\cdot)=\eta(0)z(0,\cdot)=0
.
Hence,
\tilde{z}
satisfies (a.e.) the following Cauchy problem:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{z}-\Delta\tilde{z}=\text{div}(\tilde{z}F)+\eta\text{ div}(G)+\eta^{\prime}z,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
\tilde{z}(0,\cdot)=0,&\text{ on }\Omega\end{cases}

Using Duhamel representation formula, we get

\begin{split}\tilde{z}(t,x)&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(t-u,x-y)\left(\text{div}(\tilde{z}F)+\eta\text{ div}(G)+\eta^{\prime}z\right)(u,y){\rm d}y{\rm d}u\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\langle D\Gamma(t-u,x-y),-\tilde{z}F(u,y)-\eta(u)G(u,y)\rangle+\Gamma(t-u,x-y)\eta^{\prime}(u,y)z(u,y){\rm d}y{\rm d}u\\
&=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\langle D\Gamma(t-u,x-y),-\tilde{z}F(u,y)-\eta(u)G(u,y)\rangle+\Gamma(t-u,x-y)\eta^{\prime}(u,y)z(u,y){\rm d}y{\rm d}u,\end{split}

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\eta
(and consequently
\tilde{z}
) has support, with respect to
t
, on
[s,T]
. Consider
s\leq t\leq s+\tau
, where
\tau
is defined as before. Then, again by Hölder’s inequality for convolutions,

\begin{split}\|\tilde{z}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}&\leq\int_{s}^{t}\|D\Gamma(t-u,x-\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\|\tilde{z}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\rm d}u\\
&+\int_{s}^{t}\|D\Gamma(t-u,x-\cdot)\|_{L^{\frac{n+1}{n}}(\Omega)}\|G(u)\|_{L^{n+1}(\Omega)}+\|\Gamma(t-u,x-\cdot)\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}(\Omega)}\|z(u)\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}{\rm d}u\\
&\leq C_{2}(n)\|F\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\underset{u\in[s,s+\tau]}{\sup}\|\tilde{z}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{s}^{t}(t-u)^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}u\\
&+\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\int_{s}^{t}(t-u)^{-1+\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G(u)\|_{L^{n+1}(\Omega)}{\rm d}u+C_{1}(n)\int_{s}^{t}(t-u)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|z(u)\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}{\rm d}u\\
&\overset{r:=t-u}{\leq}2C_{2}(n)\|F\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\underset{u\in[s,s+\tau]}{\sup}\|\tilde{z}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&+\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\int_{0}^{t-s}r^{-1+\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G(t-r)\|_{L^{n+1}(\Omega)}{\rm d}r+C_{1}(n)\int_{0}^{t-s}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|z(t-r)\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}{\rm d}r\\
&\leq 2C_{2}(n)\|F\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\underset{u\in[s,s+\tau]}{\sup}\|\tilde{z}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&+\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\int_{0}^{\tau}r^{-1+\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G(t-r)\|_{L^{n+1}(\Omega)}{\rm d}r+C_{1}(n)\int_{0}^{\tau}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|z(t-r)\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}{\rm d}r\\
&\leq 2C_{2}(n)\|F\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\underset{u\in[s,s+\tau]}{\sup}\|\tilde{z}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&+(2n+2)\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\tau^{\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n+1}(\Omega))}+2\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{1}(n)\|z\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n}(\Omega))}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\underset{u\in[s,s+\tau]}{\sup}\|\tilde{z}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+(2n+2)\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\tau^{\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n+1}(\Omega))}\\
&+2\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{1}(n)\|z\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n}(\Omega))}.\end{split}

Taking the supremum over
[s,s+\tau]
, we get

\begin{split}\underset{u\in[s,s+\tau]}{\sup}\|\tilde{z}(u,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}&\leq 2(2n+2)\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\tau^{\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n+1}(\Omega))}\\
&+4\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{1}(n)\|z\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n}(\Omega))}.\end{split}

Recalling the definition of
\tilde{z}
, we get that

\|z(s+\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\\
=\frac{1}{\tau}\|\tilde{z}(s+\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{2(2n+2)\tilde{C}_{2}(n)\tau^{\frac{1}{2n+2}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n+1}(\Omega))}}{\tau}\\
+\frac{4\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{1}(n)\|z\|_{L^{\infty}((t-\tau,t);L^{n}(\Omega))}}{\tau},

for all
0<s<T-1
.
This can be extended also for
s>T-1
(but
s<T-\tau
), by getting a different cut-off function

\eta_{2}(t):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }t\in[0,s]\\
t-s,&\text{ if }t\in[s,T]\\
\end{cases}

and again
\tilde{z}(t,x)=\eta_{2}(t)z(t,x)
. The computations are the same as before. Gathering all the estimates, we finally get (
5.1
).
∎

Remark 5.3
.

We notice that in the proof of (
5.1
) the last term arises from the cut-off argument. This means that the estimate on
[0,\tau]
reads as follows:

\|z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|z_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{F}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\tau);L^{n}(\Omega))}.

(5.2)

A rougher bound than (
5.1
) can be obtained by iterating (
5.2
)
m
times, where
m
is the integer part of
\frac{T}{\tau}
. In this way,

\begin{split}\|z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}&\leq 2^{m}\|z_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{F}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m}2^{k}\right)\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\tau);L^{n}(\Omega))}\\
&=2^{m}\|z_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{F}(2^{m+1}-1)\|G\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\tau);L^{n}(\Omega))},\end{split}

(5.3)

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

The second estimate is about backward equations with terminal condition.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
z
be the unique (mild) solution to the following linear backward PDE:

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}z-\Delta z=\langle Dz,H\rangle+g,&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\\
z(T,\cdot)=z_{T}(\cdot),&\text{ on }\Omega.\\
\end{cases}

(5.4)

Suppose that
H,g\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)
. Assume moreover that
z(t,\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Then,

\|Dz(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|Dz_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{H}\left(\|g\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}+\mathbbm{1}_{[\tau,T]}(t)\|z\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\right),

(5.5)

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

Let
C_{2}(n):=C_{2}(n,1)
, where
C_{2}(n,p)
is as in Lemma
5.1
. We consider again, as in Lemma
5.2
, the PDE as a heat equation with an external source. We recast the equation in (
5.4
) as a forward PDE by introducing the following change of (time) variable:

\tilde{z}(t,x):=z(T-t,x).

Then, we determine the equation satisfied by
\bar{z}
.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{z}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{s}z(T-t,x)

\displaystyle=\Delta z(T-t,x)+\langle Dz(T-t,x),H(T-t,x)\rangle+g(T-t,x)

\displaystyle=\Delta\tilde{z}(t,x)+\langle D\tilde{z}(t,x),H(T-t,x)\rangle+g(T-t,x),

with initial condition
\tilde{z}(0,\cdot)=z_{T}(\cdot)
. Therefore, by using Duhamel representation formula, we get

\tilde{z}(t,x)=\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(t,x-y)z_{T}(y){\rm d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(t-u,x-y)\left[\langle D\tilde{z}(u,y),H(T-u,y)\rangle+g(T-u,y)\right]{\rm d}y{\rm d}u,

hence, recalling the definition of
\tilde{z}
,

z(t,x)=\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(T-t,x-y)z_{T}(y){\rm d}y\\
+\int_{0}^{T-t}\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(T-t-u,x-y)\left[\langle Dz(T-u,y),H(T-u,y)\rangle+g(T-u,y)\right]{\rm d}y{\rm d}u.

Now, let us compute the
i
-th spatial derivative of
z
, exploiting the properties of the convolution with respect to differentiation. We get

\partial_{x_{i}}z(t,x)=\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(T-t,x-y)\partial_{y_{i}}z_{T}(y){\rm d}y+\\
+\int_{0}^{T-t}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x_{i}}\Gamma(T-t-u,x-y)\langle Dz(T-u,y),H(T-u,y)\rangle{\rm d}y{\rm d}u+\\
+\int_{0}^{T-t}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x_{i}}\Gamma(T-t-u,x-y)g(T-u,y){\rm d}y{\rm d}u,

from which, by Young’s inequality for convolutions, we get

\begin{split}\|\partial_{x_{i}}z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}&\leq\|\Gamma(T-t,x-\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\|Dz_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&+\int_{0}^{T-t}\|D\Gamma(T-t-u,x-\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\|Dz(T-u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|H(T-u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\rm d}u\\
&+\int_{0}^{T-t}\|D\Gamma(T-t-u,x-\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\|g(T-u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\rm d}u\\
&=\|Dz_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{2}(n)\int_{0}^{T-t}(T-t-u)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|Dz(T-u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|H(T-u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\rm d}u\\
&+C_{2}(n)\int_{0}^{T-t}(T-t-u)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g(T-u)\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm d}u\\
&\overset{u^{\prime}:=T-u}{=}\|Dz_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\\
&+C_{2}(n)\int_{t}^{T}(u^{\prime}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|Dz(u^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|H(u^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|g(u^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right){\rm d}u^{\prime}.\end{split}

Let
T-\tau\leq t\leq T
, with
\tau
to be fixed later. From the previous inequality, with the change of variables
r:=u^{\prime}-t
, we deduce

\begin{split}&\leq\|Dz_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{2}(n)\int_{t}^{T}(u^{\prime}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\underset{u^{\prime}\in[T-\tau,T]}{\sup}\|Dz(u^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|H(u^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\rm d}u^{\prime}\\
&+C_{2}(n)\int_{0}^{T-t}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g(t+r)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\rm d}r\\
&\leq\|Dz_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{2}(n)\|H\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\underset{u^{\prime}\in[T-\tau,T]}{\sup}\|Dz(u^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{0}^{\tau}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}u^{\prime}\\
&+C_{2}(n)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\int_{0}^{\tau}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r\\
&\leq\|Dz_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{2}(n)\|H\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\underset{u^{\prime}\in[T-\tau,T]}{\sup}\|Dz(u^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}}+2\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{2}(n)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}.\end{split}

Choose
\tau=\frac{1}{16\|H\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}C_{2}^{2}(n)}
. Then, taking the supremum on both sides on
[T-\tau,T]
and over all spatial derivatives of
z
, we get

\underset{t\in[T-\tau,T]}{\sup}\|Dz(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|Dz_{T}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{H}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)},

for some
C_{H}
depending on
\|H\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)},n
.

To extend the estimate for further times, we have to apply a cut-off method. Let
s>0
and
s<T-1
and define

\omega(t)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }t\in[0,T-s-1]\\
T-t-s,&\text{ if }t\in[T-s-1,T-s]\\
0,&\text{ if }t\in[T-s,T]\\
\end{cases}

The support of
\omega(T-t)
is
[s,T]
. Define
\bar{z}(t,x)=\omega(t)z(t,x)
and we compute the equation satisfied (a.e.) by
\bar{z}
, which is:

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\bar{z}

\displaystyle=-\omega\partial_{t}z-\omega^{\prime}z

\displaystyle=\omega\Delta z+\omega\langle Dz,H\rangle+\omega g-\omega^{\prime}z

\displaystyle=\Delta\bar{z}+\langle D\bar{z},H\rangle+\omega g-\omega^{\prime}z,

with boundary condition
\bar{z}(T,\cdot)=0
. Duhamel representation formula, together with the previous change of time variable and the property of the support of
\omega
, gives:

\begin{split}\bar{z}(t,x)&=\int_{s}^{T-t}\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(T-t-u,x-y)\langle D\bar{z}(T-u,y),H(T-u,y)\rangle{\rm d}y{\rm d}u\\
&+\int_{s}^{T-t}\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(T-t-u,x-y)\left[\omega(T-u)g(T-u,y)-\omega^{\prime}(T-u)z(T-u,y)\right]{\rm d}y{\rm d}u=\\
&\overset{u^{\prime}:=T-u}{=}\int_{t}^{T-s}\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(u^{\prime}-t,x-y)\left[\langle D\bar{z}(u^{\prime},y),H(u^{\prime},y)\rangle+\omega(u^{\prime})g(u^{\prime},y)-\omega^{\prime}(u^{\prime})z(u^{\prime},y)\right]{\rm d}y{\rm d}u^{\prime}.\end{split}

Computing the
i
-th spatial derivative and exploiting the properties of the convolution with respect to differentiation yields:

\partial_{x_{i}}\bar{z}(t,x)=\int_{t}^{T-s}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x_{i}}\Gamma(u-t,x-y)\left[\langle D\bar{z}(u,y),H(u,y)\rangle+\omega(u)g(u,y)-\omega^{\prime}(u)z(u,y)\right]{\rm d}y{\rm d}u.

Take now
t\in[T-s-\tau,T-s]
, where
\tau
is the same as before. Taking the sup norm and using the Young’s inequality for convolutions, we obtain

\begin{split}\|\partial_{x_{i}}\bar{z}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}&\leq\int_{t}^{T-s}\|D\Gamma(u-t,x-\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\left[\|D\bar{z}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|H\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}+\|g(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right]{\rm d}u\\
&+\int_{t}^{T-s}\|D\Gamma(u-t,x-\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\|z(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\rm d}u\\
&\overset{r:=u-t}{=}C_{2}(n)\underset{u\in[T-s-\tau,T-s]}{\sup}\|D\bar{z}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|H\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\int_{0}^{T-s-t}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r\\
&+C_{2}(n)\int_{0}^{T-s-t}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|z(t+r)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\rm d}r+C_{2}(n)\int_{0}^{T-s-t}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g(t+r)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\rm d}r\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\underset{u\in[T-s-\tau,T-s]}{\sup}\|D\bar{z}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{2}(n)\|z\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\int_{0}^{\tau}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r\\
&+C_{2}(n)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\int_{0}^{\tau}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\underset{u\in[T-s-\tau,T-s]}{\sup}\|D\bar{z}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{2}(n)\left(\|z\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\right).\end{split}

Taking the supremum over all derivatives and over
[T-s-\tau,T-s]
, we get

\underset{u\in[T-s-\tau,T-s]}{\sup}\|D\bar{z}(u,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{H}\left(\|g\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}+\|z\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\right).

Recalling the definition of
\bar{z}
, we have that

\|Dz(T-s-\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\frac{1}{\tau}\|D\bar{z}(T-s-\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C_{H}}{\tau}\left(\|g\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}+\|z\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\right),

for all
0<s<T-1
. The same reasoning holds for
s\in[T-1,T-\tau]
, if one chooses the cut-off function

\omega_{2}(t)=\begin{cases}T-t-s,&\text{ if }t\in[0,T-s]\\
0,&\text{ if }t\in[T-s,T].\\
\end{cases}

Gathering all the estimates we obtained, we finally get (
5.5
).
∎

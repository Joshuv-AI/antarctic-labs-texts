Pathogenic signatures and therapeutic evaluation of emergent MPXV Clade Ib in low-susceptibility and immunocompromised mouse models
Authors: Hongyu Han, Xiaowei Wang, Yongchang Wu, Xiaorong Yang, Peng Qian, Xiaoqing Xie, Dongmei Jiang, Shan Wu, Xiaoping Tang, Quanhai Pang, Haisheng Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMonkeypox (Mpox) is a zoonotic disease that threatens global public health. Different clades of monkeypox virus (MPXV) vary in transmissibility and pathogenicity. In 2023, a Clade Ib MPXV variant emerged in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, continued to spread in parts of Africa, and subsequently spilled over to other regions, posing new challenges for outbreak prevention and control.ResultsWe established stable mouse models of MPXV Clade Ib infection by intranasally infecting C57BL/6/STAT1−/−, AGB6 (C57BL/6-Ifngr1−/−Ifnar1−/−), C57BL/6, and BALB/c mice. Susceptible strains showed marked body weight loss, high viral loads in tissues, and severe histopathological lesions. RNA-seq analysis of spleens at the early stage of infection showed that differentially expressed genes were mainly enriched in interferon-mediated antiviral pathways and inflammatory cytokine-related pathways, whereas genes associated with adaptive immune responses were downregulated. Comparative analysis showed that MPXV Clade Ib caused more severe disease phenotypes than Clade IIb under the same experimental conditions, which is consistent with reported differences in clinical severity.ConclusionWe established reproducible mouse models for MPXV Clade Ib infection and demonstrated that Clade Ib showed greater replication capacity and pathogenicity than Clade IIb in these models. This study also provides a foundation for subsequent research on the pathogenesis of MPXV and the evaluation of antiviral efficacy.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12866-026-05273-4.Keywords: Mpox, Monkeypox virus, MPXV Clade Ib, Mouse model, Pathogenicity

Introduction

Monkeypox (Mpox) is a zoonotic disease caused by the Monkeypox virus (MPXV). Phylogenetically, MPXV diverges into two primary lineages: Clade I (subclades Ia and Ib) and Clade II (subclades IIa and IIb) [1, 2]. Historically, mpox has been primarily endemic to Central and West Africa [3]. In 2022, a global outbreak driven by Clade IIb rapidly spread to non-endemic countries, prompting the World Health Organization (WHO) to declare it a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) in July of that year [4]. In September 2023, the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) reported the emergence of a novel Clade Ib variant, which has subsequently spread to regions outside Africa [5]. In view of the potential risk of global spread, the WHO re-declared mpox a PHEIC in August 2024, underscoring the persistent and severe challenges facing global public health security [6].

Transmission patterns and clinical outcomes vary across different MPXV clades [7]. The Clade IIb variant responsible for the 2022 outbreak was primarily transmitted through sexual contact among men who have sex with men (MSM) and has generally been associated with a low case fatality rate [8]. In contrast, Clade Ib has been associated with more severe clinical outcomes and sustained human-to-human transmission. It frequently infects children through close household contact, while heterosexual transmission and sexual networks involving female sex workers also represent major routes of spread [9–11]. Clinically, although both clades present with typical symptoms such as fever, rash, and lymphadenopathy, Clade Ib is more often associated with severe generalized rashes and persistent genital lesions. These manifestations substantially increase the disease burden and pose a particularly severe threat to children, pregnant women, and immunocompromised individuals [12]. However, while mucocutaneous lesions are the typical symptoms, the occurrence of respiratory involvement and its complications also warrants attention. A review of epidemiological and clinical data from past mpox outbreaks indicates that, in addition to direct contact with patients’ skin lesions or bodily fluids, respiratory exposure may contribute to transmission under certain close-contact or high-exposure conditions [13, 14]. A clinical analysis of mpox patients by Hugh Adler et al. [15] revealed that MPXV DNA was detectable in upper respiratory tract swabs. In some patients, the virus remained detectable in the upper respiratory tract even after skin lesions had crusted over. These findings suggest that the upper respiratory tract may be involved in viral shedding or replication. Regarding clinical features, the spectrum of mpox-associated respiratory manifestations includes pharyngitis and mucosal ulcers, with some severe cases developing complications such as bronchopneumonia [16, 17]. Histological analysis of some severe or fatal cases by Jana M. Ritter et al. revealed that bronchopneumonia and acute lung injury were relatively common pathological findings; immunohistochemistry (IHC) confirmed the presence of MPXV antigens within the pulmonary lesions [18]. In non-human primate models, Gary M. et al. reported that following aerosol exposure to MPXV, the lower respiratory epithelium was infected, leading to bronchopneumonia [19]. These clinical and pathological data indicate that respiratory involvement is an important component in the pathogenesis of mpox.

Vaccines and antiviral drugs are essential countermeasures against mpox [20, 21]. However, variation in their efficacy among different viral clades remains unclear, highlighting an urgent need for reliable animal models to support their evaluation. Given the high costs and ethical constraints associated with non-human primate models [22], mouse models have become the primary tools for research. During the early stages of the 2022 Clade IIb outbreak, in the absence of clade-specific infection models, vaccinia virus (VACV) was frequently used as a surrogate strain in research [23]. Studies have shown that wild-derived inbred mouse strains (CAST/EiJ, MOLF/EiJ, and PERA/EiJ) are highly susceptible to MPXV [24], and that mouse susceptibility varies significantly among different MPXV Clades [25]. Recent studies have utilized the CAST mouse model to demonstrate differences in virulence between MPXV Clade Ib and Clade IIb [26]. However, the low docility, breeding difficulties, and limited availability of wild-derived inbred mice restrict their widespread application during sudden viral outbreaks [27, 28].

Therefore, there is a need for alternative mouse models that offer a stable supply, are easy to handle, and can effectively reflect the outcomes of clinical therapeutic interventions. Furthermore, considering the respiratory involvement in human cases, establishing a mouse model via the intranasal challenge route facilitates the observation of mpox-associated respiratory pathological changes under experimental conditions. To this end, this study evaluated the susceptibility and associated pathological characteristics of mice from four different genetic backgrounds (STAT1−/−, AGB6, C57BL/6, and BALB/c) following intranasal infection with MPXV Clade Ib, and examined host gene expression changes post-infection via transcriptomic analysis. Building upon this, we conducted a preliminary comparison of Clade Ib and Clade IIb infections in these mouse models and evaluated the in vivo therapeutic efficacy of the antiviral drug tecovirimat. This study aims to provide supplementary experimental data and model references for research on the pathogenesis of MPXV Clade Ib and the evaluation of antiviral interventions.

Materials and methods

Cells, viruses, and antiviral compounds

Vero E6 cells were purchased from Wuhan Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd. and maintained in high-glucose Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) (Gibco: C11995500CR) supplemented with L-glutamine, sodium pyruvate, 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Cytiva: DH30406.05), and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco: 15140-122) at 37 ℃ in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. For virus infection assays, medium containing 2% FBS was used. MPXV Clade Ib (strain MPXV Ib gz8h-s01) and Clade IIb (strain hMpxV/China/GD-GZ8H-0613-5/2023) were isolated from skin swabs collected at Guangzhou Eighth People’s Hospital. Tecovirimat was obtained from MedChemExpress (MCE: HY-14805). A 62.5 mg/mL stock solution was prepared in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (Sigma: D2650) and stored at -20 ℃. For in vivo experiments, tecovirimat was formulated in 2% DMSO, 40% PEG 300 (MCE: HY-Y0873), 5% Tween 80(MCE: HY-Y1891), and 53% physiological saline.

Virus amplification

The MPXV Clade Ib and Clade IIb strains used in this study were propagated in Vero E6 cells. Briefly, Vero E6 cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. For viral amplification, cell monolayers at approximately 80–90% confluence were infected with MPXV at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.01 in infection medium (DMEM supplemented with 2% FBS). The infected cells were monitored daily and incubated until extensive cytopathic effect (CPE) was observed, typically at 3 to 4 days post-infection. Subsequently, the cell culture supernatant and the infected cells were harvested together. To effectively release the intracellular virions, the suspension was subjected to three consecutive freeze-thaw cycles (alternating between − 80 °C and 37 °C). The cell lysate was then centrifuged at 4,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C to remove cellular debris. The clarified viral supernatant was aliquoted and stored at -80 °C until further use in vivo. The viral titer of the stock was determined using a standard plaque assay on Vero E6 cells. All procedures involving infectious MPXV were strictly conducted within the Biosafety Level 3 (BSL-3) facility at Guangzhou Eighth People’s Hospital, in full compliance with institutional biosafety regulations.

Virus titer determination

On the day prior to the experiment, 24-well plates were seeded with Vero E6 cells to reach approximately 90% confluence. Viral samples were serially diluted (e.g., 10-fold dilutions) in DMEM. The serially diluted viral suspensions were inoculated onto the Vero E6 cell monolayers and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h to allow for viral adsorption, with gentle rocking every 15 min. Following adsorption, the viral inoculum was removed. The cells were then overlaid with a pre-warmed semi-solid medium consisting of equal volumes of 1.5% agar (Sigma: A9414) and DMEM supplemented with 2% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. After allowing approximately 15 min for the agar to solidify at room temperature, the plates were inverted and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2. After 72 h of incubation, an equal volume of a combined fixative and staining solution (comprising 0.5 g crystal violet, 25 mL of 75% ethanol, and 475 mL of paraformaldehyde) was added directly to the wells. The plates were then left at room temperature for 24 h. Subsequently, the fixative solution was aspirated using a Pasteur pipette, the solidified agar overlay was carefully peeled off, and the wells were gently washed with water. Visible plaques were manually counted, and the viral titers were calculated and expressed as plaque-forming units per milliliter (PFU/mL).

Mice

C57BL/6 and BALB/c mice were purchased from Guangdong GemPharmatech Co., Ltd. STAT1−/− mice were purchased from Kunshan Mingqian Microbiology Research Institute Co., Ltd. AGB6 mice were purchased from Shanghai Model Organisms Center, Inc. After arrival, mice were acclimated for 2–3 days, randomly housed, and maintained in an Animal Biosafety Level 3 (ABSL-3) laboratory standard mouse housing cage systems at 22 ± 2 ℃ temperature with water, feed, and bedding provided.

Mouse challenge and drug administration

On the day of infection, mice were anesthetized using isoflurane (RWD: R510), and the depth of anesthesia was confirmed by monitoring respiratory rate and assessing muscle relaxation. Intranasal (i.n.) inoculation was performed by administering 50 µL of the viral suspension into a single nostril. Control groups received an equivalent volume of PBS. Following infection, animals were monitored daily for changes in general health status and survival outcomes. Body weight was measured and recorded regularly. Mice exhibiting a 25% reduction in baseline body weight were humanely euthanized in accordance with ethical guidelines. For the drug treatment groups, 200 µL of tecovirimat (10 mg/kg) was administered via oral gavage 2 h prior to infection, followed by daily gavage for 5 consecutive days beginning on the day of viral challenge. Control groups received corresponding volumes of the vehicle solution under the same administration schedule. For the euthanasia procedure at the experimental endpoints or upon reaching the humane endpoint, all animals were first rendered deeply unconscious through an intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection of 1.25% tribromoethanol at a dosage of 0.2 mL/10 g body weight. Deep anesthesia and complete unconsciousness were strictly confirmed by the absence of the pedal withdrawal reflex (toe pinch). Once verified, the mice were humanely euthanized via cervical dislocation prior to dissection. This specific dual-stage method was selected to ensure a rapid and painless death while optimally preserving tissue integrity and avoiding chemical artifacts for downstream pathogenic and therapeutic analyses. All animal care and experimental procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Institute of Infectious Diseases, The Eighth Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University (Animal Ethics Number: IACUC-YHS-2024-08).

Viral load quantification

On the day prior to the experiment, 96-well plates were seeded with Vero E6 cells at a density of 1 × 104 cells per well. Tissues stored in DMEM containing 2% FBS were homogenized using a low-temperature tissue grinder at a frequency of 80 Hz for 30 s, and this process was repeated three times. The homogenates were then centrifuged at 4,000 rpm for 10 min, and the resulting supernatants were serially diluted from 10− 1 to 10− 7. Each dilution was added to Vero E6 cell monolayers in six replicate wells. After 3 days of incubation, cytopathic effects (CPE) were recorded, and infectious viral titers were calculated as TCID50/g of tissue using the Reed-Muench method. For consistency with the inoculation dose reported in plaque-forming units (PFU), TCID50-derived titers were converted to PFU-equivalent values using the Poisson-based approximation [29]: 1 TCID50 = 0.69 PFU. The final values are presented as log10(PFU equivalents/g) of tissue. These values represent PFU-equivalent estimates and were not directly determined by plaque assay.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) and Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) staining

Tissue samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (Biosharp: BL539A) for 48 h and subsequently removed from the biosafety level 3 laboratory. Following paraffin embedding, the samples were processed for hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) and immunohistochemistry (IHC) experiments. For H&E staining, lung tissue sections were sequentially stained with hematoxylin (Solarbio: G1080) and eosin (Solarbio: G1100). For IHC analysis, antigen retrieval was carried out using Tris-EDTA buffer (10 mM Tris Base, 1 mM EDTA, 0.05% Tween 20, pH 9.0) for 30 min. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked using a 3% hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) solution for 10 min at room temperature. Subsequently, nonspecific antibody binding was blocked with goat serum (ZSGB-BIO: ZLI9056) for 30 min at room temperature. The sections were then incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with primary rabbit anti-vaccinia virus antibody (Abcam: ab35219, dilution 1:2000). After washing, sections were incubated with an enzyme-labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG polymer (ZSGB-BIO: PV-6001) for 30 min at room temperature. Immunoreactivity was visualized using freshly prepared DAB chromogen solution, followed by counterstaining with hematoxylin. The sections were dehydrated through graded alcohol and xylene, cleared, and mounted under coverslips for microscopic examination.

Histopathology and image analysis

Lung tissues were the primary organs examined in this study for histopathological changes and viral antigen expression. For the quantification of immunohistochemical (IHC) signals, images were analyzed using ImageJ software (Fiji). The “Color Deconvolution” plugin (H DAB vector) was employed to separate the DAB signal (brown) from the hematoxylin counterstain (blue). A consistent threshold was applied to the isolated DAB channel, and the positive staining area (Area) was measured to quantify the viral antigen levels. To assess the severity of lung lesions, H&E-stained sections were evaluated by an experienced pathologist who was blinded to the experimental groups. The histopathological changes were graded using a semi-quantitative 0–3 scoring system based on five parameters: alveolar damage, edema, inflammation, necrosis and hemorrhage. The general scoring criteria were based on the percentage of the affected area per microscopic field of view: 0 (normal tissue structure with no lesions); 1 (mild lesions, affecting < 20% of the field); 2 (moderate lesions, affecting 20%-50% of the field); and 3 (severe lesions with significant structural damage, affecting > 50% of the field). Scores from randomly selected fields per section were averaged for each sample. The detailed scoring criteria are provided in Supplementary Table 1.

RNA extraction, library preparation and sequencing

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, 15596-026) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA integrity was qualitatively assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis prior to library preparation. The gel images showed clear 28 S, 18 S, and 5 S rRNA bands without obvious degradation smearing. RNA Integrity Number (RIN) values were not determined using a Bioanalyzer, which is acknowledged as a limitation of the RNA quality assessment. All samples successfully passed library construction and sequencing quality control, and the sequencing data showed high base-calling quality, with Q30 values exceeding 95% across all samples. Poly(A) mRNA was enriched using oligo(dT) magnetic beads and fragmented, followed by cDNA synthesis using the NEBNext Ultra RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NEB; Cat. No. E7530). The resulting cDNA was end-repaired, A-tailed, and ligated to sequencing adapters. Libraries were purified with AMPure XP beads (1.0×), size-selected, and PCR-amplified, and then sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq X Plus platform (Guangzhou Banyan Medical Technology Co., Ltd.).

RNA-seq data analysis

Raw sequencing data were subjected to quality control using FastQC, and summary reports were generated with MultiQC. Adapter sequences and low-quality bases were removed using Trim Galore. Clean paired-end reads were aligned to the mouse reference genome (mm10) using STAR. Gene-level read counts were quantified with featureCounts to generate a count matrix for downstream analyses. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using DESeq2 (v1.44.0) with thresholds of |log2 fold change|≥1 and Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted P value (padj) ≤ 0.05. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed using clusterProfiler (v4.12.6).

Statistical information

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (v8.0.2). Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Differences between groups were assessed using two-tailed Student’s t tests or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), as appropriate, unless otherwise stated. Exact P values or significance levels are indicated in the figures. The specific statistical tests used and the number of replicates are provided in the corresponding figure legends and/or relevant sections of the text. Replicates are reported as either technical replicates (e.g., replicate wells) or biological replicates (defined as the number of independent subjects per group).

Result 1: differential susceptibility of mice with distinct genetic backgrounds to MPXV Clade Ib infection

Previous studies have demonstrated that wild-type mice typically develop mild, self-limiting disease following MPXV infection, whereas deficiencies in the interferon signaling pathway markedly increase susceptibility [30–33]. To investigate strain-dependent differences in susceptibility, we established infection models using four mouse strains (STAT1−/−, AGB6, C57BL/6, and BALB/c) and compared their responses to MPXV Clade Ib (Fig. 1a). Following intranasal inoculation with an identical dose (2 × 105 PFU/mouse), all four groups exhibited weight loss, but their clinical outcomes differed substantially. STAT1−/− mice showed the most pronounced weight loss (Fig. 1b) and reached 100% mortality by 8 days post-infection (dpi) (Fig. 1c), whereas AGB6 mice exhibited a 40% mortality rate (Fig. 1c). In contrast, although C57BL/6 and BALB/c mice experienced transient weight loss of approximately 20%, all animals survived and subsequently recovered, consistent with a self-limiting disease course (Fig. 1b and c). Viral load quantification and pathological analysis revealed that at 7 dpi, viral loads in the nasal turbinates remained high across all groups, with no significant differences observed. In lung tissues, viral loads in STAT1−/− mice were slightly higher than those in the other groups (Fig. 1d), whereas viral loads in the blood and other visceral organs were below the detection limit (data not shown). Immunohistochemistry (IHC) confirmed that viral antigens were predominantly localized to the bronchial epithelium and surrounding inflammatory cells in the lungs, with STAT1−/− mice exhibiting the strongest positive signals (Fig. 1e and f). Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining further revealed lung pathological changes, including peribronchial epithelial hyperplasia, necrosis, and inflammatory cell infiltration (Fig. 1g). Quantitative pathology scores were consistent with the clinical phenotypes observed in the mice (Fig. 1h). Further investigation showed that STAT1−/− mice remained highly susceptible to low-dose infection (2,000 PFU), as evidenced by significant weight loss (Fig. 1i and j). In addition, dynamic monitoring of C57BL/6 and BALB/c mice demonstrated that the virus replicated efficiently in both the nasal turbinates and lungs, with viral loads peaking at 5 dpi (Fig. 1l and m). Taken together, STAT1−/− mice exhibited a highly susceptible and lethal phenotype, whereas wild-type mice developed a non-lethal infection characterized by efficient viral replication. These experiments were independently replicated and showed excellent reproducibility.

Result 2: temporal dynamics of the splenic transcriptome induced by MPXV Clade Ib infection

To elucidate the characteristics of the host immune response to MPXV Clade Ib infection, we performed RNA-seq analysis on spleens collected from C57BL/6 and BALB/c mice at multiple time points post-infection (Fig. 2a). UpSet and volcano plot analyses revealed marked differences in the breadth and temporal dynamics of the transcriptional responses between the two strains (Fig. 2b and c). C57BL/6 mice exhibited rapid and robust transcriptional remodeling, with the number of DEGs peaking at 1,877 at 5 dpi. Notably, 999 DEGs were already detectable by 3 dpi, accompanied by significant upregulation of antiviral genes such as Oas1a, Rtp1, and Ligp1, indicating early initiation of the immune response. In contrast, the host response in BALB/c mice was relatively delayed and attenuated. The number of DEGs peaked at only 451 at 5 dpi, with only 14 DEGs detected at 3 dpi, and the overall magnitude of the response was lower than that observed in C57BL/6 mice.

Hierarchical clustering heatmap (Fig. 2d) and pathway enrichment analysis (Fig. 2e) elucidated the dynamics of key immune modules. Regarding innate immunity, C57BL/6 mice showed robust activation of the “antiviral interferon response” and “inflammatory cytokine-related modules” as early as 3–5 dpi. In contrast, the corresponding responses in BALB/c mice were markedly delayed, which may be associated with their reduced capacity for early viral control. Notably, despite the activation of innate immunity, both mouse strains showed varying degrees of downregulation of genes associated with adaptive immunity (Fig. 2d). Core genes (e.g., Cd4, Cd22, Igkc, Pax5, Tap1) were consistently downregulated throughout the course of infection. Pathway enrichment analysis (Fig. 2e) confirmed that, in C57BL/6 mice, pathways related to early lymphocyte differentiation and function were significantly suppressed. In BALB/c mice, pathways involved in MHC antigen presentation were preferentially downregulated. As infection progressed to the recovery phase (10 dpi), C57BL/6 mice exhibited significant upregulation of genes associated with regeneration and proliferation (e.g., Col4a3, Col5a1, Pdgfb), along with enrichment of pathways related to “chromosome segregation” and “extracellular matrix organization”. These changes indicate that the host had transitioned beyond the acute inflammatory phase and initiated transcriptional programs for tissue repair and restoration of homeostasis. In contrast, BALB/c mice activated the “immunoglobulin-mediated immune response” pathway during the late phase of infection (10 dpi), suggesting a delayed but detectable humoral immune-related response that may contribute to recovery.

Result 3: comparative analysis of disease progression and transcriptomic profiles between MPXV Clade Ib and Clade IIb in mice

Previous clinical reports indicate that MPXV Clade Ib is associated with more severe clinical outcomes than Clade IIb [12]. We compared the pathogenicity of the two clades using the STAT1−/− mouse model under identical conditions (intranasal infection with 2 × 105 PFU)(Fig. 3a). The results indicated that the Clade Ib group exhibited an earlier onset of weight loss (4 dpi) with a more rapid disease progression (Fig. 3b). Virological and pathological assessments showed that at 7 dpi, both the viral load and the extent of viral antigen distribution in lung tissues, as assessed by IHC, were significantly higher in the Clade Ib group than in the Clade IIb group (Fig. 3c, d and f). Both infection groups developed pulmonary inflammatory infiltration and tissue necrosis; however, there was no significant difference in pathology scores between the two groups (Fig. 3e and g).

To investigate the host responses accompanying these distinct infection kinetics, we compared the splenic transcriptomes at 7 dpi. Although the Clade IIb group induced a slightly higher total number of DEGs (Fig. 3h), volcano plot analysis revealed that Clade Ib infection was associated with more pronounced transcriptional changes. Specifically, the magnitude of gene regulation (Fold Change) and statistical significance were notably higher in the Clade Ib group(Fig. 3i). In particular, the Clade Ib group exhibited high expression of the tissue-injury-associated gene Mmp8 and the inflammatory mediator S100a3, along with a marked downregulation of T cell receptor-related genes (such as Trbv30). Further pathway analysis (Fig. 3j) revealed that among the upregulated pathways, both Ib and IIb groups activated processes related to chromosome segregation. However, while the IIb group enriched transcripts associated with the “T cell receptor signaling pathway” and “immune response-activating signaling pathway”, the Ib group exhibited a broader downregulation of key immune-related pathways, including “response to interferon-β” and “antigen processing and presentation”. In summary, within this highly permissive STAT1−/− mouse model, MPXV Clade Ib infection was associated with earlier disease progression, higher lung viral loads, and stronger viral antigen signals than Clade IIb infection under the same experimental conditions. These virological differences were accompanied by transcriptional signatures associated with tissue injury, inflammation, and reduced activation of selected immune-related pathways.

Result 4: validation of the mouse model for antiviral drug evaluation using Tecovirimat

Tecovirimat is a well-established orthopoxvirus inhibitor commonly used as a reference compound to validate the drug screening potential of animal model [34]. To assess the suitability of the mouse models established in this study for antiviral drug evaluation, we examined the in vivo efficacy of Tecovirimat in three mouse strains with distinct susceptibility profiles: C57BL/6, AGB6, and STAT1−/−. As outlined in the experimental design (Fig. 4a), following infection with MPXV Clade Ib, drug treatment by oral gavage was initiated on the same day (day 0) and continued for 5 consecutive days. Compared with the vehicle control group, Tecovirimat treatment significantly attenuated infection-induced weight loss in all three strains (Fig. 4b, d and f). Virological analysis further showed a marked reduction in viral loads in the lung tissues of treated mice (Fig. 4c, e and g), indicating that the drug effectively inhibited viral replication in the primary target organ. Histopathological assessment showed better preservation of lung architecture in the treated group, with substantially ameliorated infection-related lesions (Fig. 4h). In addition, IHC revealed a pronounced decrease in MPXV antigen staining in the lungs of Tecovirimat-treated mice (Fig. 4i-l). Transcriptomic analysis of spleens from STAT1−/− mice showed that Tecovirimat treatment attenuated MPXV-induced transcriptional remodeling compared with the DMSO control group (Supplementary Fig. 1). The Tecovirimat-treated group showed enrichment of wound healing-related pathways, suggesting a shift toward recovery-associated transcriptional programs (Supplementary Fig. 1e). In contrast, nucleotide metabolism-related pathways were downregulated, which may reflect reduced infection-associated metabolic perturbation and cellular stress (Supplementary Fig. 1e). In summary, we successfully validated the in vivo antiviral efficacy of Tecovirimat across three mouse genotypes. These findings support the use of these mouse models as preclinical systems for evaluating antiviral interventions against MPXV Clade Ib and provide an experimental basis for future screening of additional antiviral candidates.

Discussion

With the continued spread of MPXV Clade Ib in Africa and the risk of international importation, tractable animal models are needed to investigate pathogenesis and evaluate countermeasures [35]. Here we established a mouse infection platform spanning multiple genetic backgrounds via intranasal inoculation and, together with transcriptomic profiling, delineated pathogenic features of Clade Ib while demonstrating the utility of these models for antiviral assessment.

We observed that wild-type mice (C57BL/6 and BALB/c) exhibited only self-limiting, transient weight loss after infection. In contrast, both STAT1−/− and AGB6 mice developed lethal disease. This difference indicates that although MPXV encodes multiple immune-evasion proteins that antagonize the host interferon (IFN) system [36, 37], viral replication remains, to some extent, constrained in hosts with an intact IFN response. Notably, AGB6 mice showed milder weight loss and lower mortality than STAT1−/− mice. This suggests that, in the absence of type I/II IFN signaling, type III IFN or other STAT1-activating pathways independent of the canonical receptors may still confer residual protection. By contrast, complete loss of STAT1 abolishes downstream interferon-stimulated gene (ISG) transcription, enabling the virus to overwhelm host defenses and undergo rapid expansion. Transcriptomic data further supported this interpretation: wild-type mice mounted a robust early ISG response, which likely curtailed initial viral dissemination and prevented a fatal outcome.

In addition, comparisons between C57BL/6 and BALB/c mice highlighted the influence of host genetic background on the response to MPXV Clade Ib infection. C57BL/6 mice mounted a rapid and strong immune response, whereas BALB/c mice exhibited a delayed and weaker response. Despite these differences, both strains showed early suppression of adaptive immune-related pathways, although different components were affected. C57BL/6 mice predominantly exhibited impaired lymphocyte differentiation, while BALB/c mice were characterized by downregulation of MHC-mediated antigen presentation. These findings indicate that MPXV Clade Ib infection induces host-background-dependent patterns of immune dysregulation. Meanwhile, such differences may also be influenced by viral replication efficiency, tissue viral burden, or infection kinetics.

Given the high susceptibility of STAT1−/− mice to MPXV, we used this model to corroborate the clinically observed accelerated disease progression of Clade Ib compared to Clade IIb and to characterize the distinct transcriptomic and pathogenic profiles of these clades in the absence of intact interferon signaling. Our results indicate that the rapid disease progression associated with Clade Ib in this immunodeficient host is characterized by a distinct transcriptional signature showing elevated inflammation and suppressed adaptive immune pathways. Specifically, Clade Ib infection induced high expression of the tissue-remodeling gene Mmp8 and the inflammatory mediator S100a3. These transcriptional changes were consistent with the pulmonary lesions observed in infected mice, although the pathology scores did not differ significantly between the two clades at the examined time point. In parallel, transcriptomic analysis revealed that the Clade Ib group exhibited a broader downregulation of adaptive immune programs than Clade IIb, including antigen processing/presentation and T-cell receptor signaling (e.g., Trbv30). These data suggest that in a STAT1-deficient background, the combination of strong inflammatory responses and the downregulated expression of antigen presentation-related genes may compromise effective viral clearance. This observation aligns with the higher viral loads and more severe disease progression associated with Clade Ib in the mouse model.

To evaluate the translational value of our intranasal mouse model, it must be compared with human mpox and existing animal models. While Our model captures certain features of severe pulmonary involvement reported in severe or fatal human cases, including lung pathology and inflammatory infiltration [18, 38]. However, it does not fully reproduce systemic viral dissemination or the characteristic cutaneous and mucocutaneous lesions of human mpox. In this study, a 50 µL intranasal inoculation volume was used to promote consistent lower respiratory tract exposure and improve experimental reproducibility [39, 40]. Nevertheless, this approach likely bypasses the initial nasal mucosal replication that occurs during natural upper respiratory exposure. Compared with NHP and prairie dog models, which better recapitulate systemic disease and skin lesions [41, 42], our mouse model provides a cost-effective, genetically defined, and reproducible platform for characterizing host responses during severe pulmonary infection and for preclinical antiviral efficacy evaluation.

Finally, although our study supports the utility of these mouse models for antiviral evaluation, recent clinical trial data from the Democratic Republic of the Congo suggest that tecovirimat may have limited clinical benefit in patients with Clade I MPXV infection [43]. This differs from the clear antiviral effect observed in our models. One possible explanation is the timing of treatment initiation. In our experiments, tecovirimat was administered on the day of infection, before extensive immune dysregulation and tissue injury had developed. In clinical practice, however, treatment is often initiated only after the onset of overt symptoms, when viral replication may no longer be the sole driver of disease progression. These observations suggest that early intervention may be critical for antiviral efficacy against MPXV Clade Ib. They also highlight the need for further studies to define the therapeutic window of antiviral treatment and to explore whether antiviral therapy combined with modulation of host inflammatory responses may provide greater benefit in more advanced stages of infection.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we established a reproducible mouse platform for MPXV Clade Ib infection across multiple genetic backgrounds and characterized the major in vivo features of infection in these models. Disease severity was closely associated with host interferon responsiveness, with severe or lethal outcomes observed in interferon-deficient mice and non-lethal but productive infection in wild-type mice. Transcriptomic analyses identified early antiviral and inflammatory responses accompanied by suppression of adaptive immune-related programmes, with distinct temporal patterns between mouse strains. In the STAT1−/− model, MPXV Clade Ib infection was associated with earlier disease progression and higher lung viral loads than Clade IIb infection under the same experimental conditions. In addition, tecovirimat showed clear antiviral activity in several of the established models. Collectively, these findings expand the available experimental resources for MPXV Clade Ib research and provide a useful basis for future studies of infection characteristics and in vivo antiviral evaluation.

Limitations and future directions

This study has several limitations. First, although we used mice with diverse genetic backgrounds, we did not include a fully immunocompetent, highly susceptible model like the CAST/EiJ mouse. This limits our conclusions regarding natural viral immune evasion. Because our susceptible STAT1−/− mice inherently lack functional interferon signaling, the observed downregulation of adaptive immunity might primarily reflect accelerated viral replication, rather than specific viral evasion strategies against an intact immune system. Second, while our bulk transcriptomic data indicate immune suppression, these findings lack validation at the single-cell level. Future studies employing single-cell and spatial profiling, combined with reverse genetics, are needed to identify the specific cell populations and viral factors involved. Third, our intranasal challenge model primarily mimics respiratory infection and does not reproduce the cutaneous or genital lesions typical of human disease. Future work should incorporate intradermal and genital mucosal routes to better define tissue tropism. Additionally, observation periods should be extended beyond the acute phase to evaluate long-term lung lesion recovery. Finally, tecovirimat was evaluated under a single fixed regimen. Future studies should explore broader therapeutic windows, dose-response effects, and rational combinations with immunomodulators. Ultimately, integrating cross-species multi-omics data, broader viral isolates, and humanized mouse models will help uncover clinical biomarkers and guide precision treatments for MPXV Clade Ib infection.

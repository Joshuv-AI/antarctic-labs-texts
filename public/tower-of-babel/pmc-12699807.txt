Prediction of spontaneous preterm birth before 34 weeks in twin pregnancies using first-trimester cervical length
Authors: Magdalena Litwińska, Izabela Walasik Szewczyk, Tomasz Szajner, Marcin Pasternok, Tomasz Góra, Krzysztof Mędrek, Paweł Stanirowski, Artur Ludwin, Ewelina Litwińska
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe aim of this retrospective cohort study with a case-control analysis, based on routinely collected data was to examine whether cervical length (CL) measurement in the first trimester independently contributes to the prediction of spontaneous preterm birth (PTB) before 34 weeks’ gestation in twin pregnancies.MethodsIn the study period, 2781 twin pregnancies were booked for routine pregnancy care in five fetal medicine centres in Poland in the years 2015–2023. The inclusion criteria were monochorionic or dichorionic twin pregnancy with two life fetuses at the time of the 11–13 weeks’ scan without any major abnormality. Univariate and multivariate regression logistic analysis was conducted to define which maternal and pregnancy characteristics provided an independent contribution in the prediction of spontaneous PTB before 34 weeks’ gestation.ResultsAmong 2781 twin pregnancies booked for routine pregnancy care at 11–13 weeks, 2004 met qualification criteria for the study. Univariable regression analysis showed that the most significant biophysical risk factor of spontaneous PTB in twins was CL below 30 mm (OR = 4.85; 3.16–7.43). Also, CL < 35 mm was associated with an increased risk of PTB (OR = 3.42; 95%CI: 2.37–4.54). The most important maternal risk factor of PTB was obstetric history of PTD among multiparas (OR = 19.15, 95%CI: 8.22–44.58) and medical history of diabetes mellitus type 1 and 2 (OR = 3.49, 95%CI: 1.98–6.17) in the index pregnancy. However, multivariable regression analysis showed that the only factor that significantly increased the risk of spontaneous PTB in twins was obstetric history of PTB.ConclusionsWe showed that CL measurement in the first trimester of pregnancy in twin gestations may be useful in predicting preterm birth before 34 weeks of gestation.Keywords: Cervical length, Twin gestation, Spontaneous preterm birth, First-trimester ultrasound

Introduction

Preterm birth (PTB) remains the leading cause of neonatal and childhood morbidity and mortality [1]. The risk of spontaneous PTB is approximately 1% in singleton pregnancies and about ten times higher in twin pregnancies [2]. Given that the twinning rate has increased by one-third since the 1980 s, complications related to PTB in twin gestations have become a significant global health concern. Several risk factors for PTB in twins have been investigated, including maternal characteristics, obstetric history, and biophysical markers such as mid-trimester cervical length (CL) measurement [3–6].

Evidence indicates that, in twin pregnancies before 24 weeks’ gestation, a CL ≤ 25 mm measured by mid-trimester transvaginal ultrasound is associated with a significantly increased risk of PTB [7]. Interventions aimed at reducing the incidence of spontaneous PTB in twin gestations—including vaginal progesterone, cervical cerclage, and pessary placement—have shown limited efficacy [8–11]. In twin pregnancies presenting with asymptomatic cervical dilation before 24 weeks’ gestation, a recent randomized controlled trial (RCT) demonstrated that a combination of physical examination–indicated cerclage, indomethacin, and antibiotic therapy significantly reduced the risk of PTB, with an approximately 50% relative reduction in early PTB before 28 weeks [12]. Similarly, an RCT investigating vaginal progesterone use in unselected twin pregnancies from 12 weeks’ gestation reported no overall benefit; however, a secondary analysis suggested a reduction in PTB rates among women with a CL < 30 mm measured during the first-trimester scan [13].

In this study, we aimed to determine whether first-trimester CL measurement provides an independent contribution to predicting spontaneous PTB before 34 weeks’ gestation in twin pregnancies.

Methods

Study design

This was a retrospective multicenter cohort study with a case-control analysis of women with twin gestations booked for routine pregnancy care in five fetal medicine units in Poland in the years 2015–2023. The data for this study was derived from the databases of the participating centers. The inclusion criteria were monochorionic or dichorionic twin pregnancy with two life fetuses at the time of the 11–13 weeks’ scan without any major abnormality. In the participating centres all patients are routinely offered an ultrasound scan at 11–13 weeks’ gestation in order to calculate the gestational age on the basis of the crown-rump length (CRL) of the bigger twin, determine chorionicity, perform the first-trimester combine test for aneuploidies and measure CL using a transvaginal approach (TVUS).

Cervical length was assessed using TVUS in accordance with the guidelines established by the International Society of Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology (ISUOG). All measurements were performed by physicians experienced in obstetric imaging (FMF certificate). Patients were instructed to empty their bladder prior to the examination to avoid distortion of pelvic anatomy. Each patient was positioned in the dorsal lithotomy position to allow optimal visualization of the cervix. A high-frequency (5–9 MHz) endovaginal transducer was used for all assessments. The probe was covered with a sterile sheath and lubricated with ultrasound gel before insertion. The transducer was gently inserted into the anterior vaginal fornix with care to avoid excessive pressure on the cervix. The cervix was imaged in the sagittal plane, ensuring visualization of the entire endocervical canal from the internal os to the external os. The internal os was identified as the point where the endocervical canal widens into the uterine cavity, and the external os as the distal end opening into the vagina. Cervical length was measured as the linear distance between the internal and external os along the endocervical canal. Calipers were placed at the internal and external os, taking care to avoid including cervical mucus or funneling in the measurement. At least three measurements were obtained for each patient, and the shortest, best-quality measurement was recorded for analysis, in line with clinical best practices. Cervical length cut-offs of 30 mm and 35 mm were selected based on their predictive value for preterm birth risk in twin gestations. Because the data were collected prospectively across five different centers and measurements were performed by five different operators, inter-observer variability was not formally assessed; however, all practitioners were experienced in obstetric ultrasonography and adhered to the same sandarised measurment protocol. The results of PAPP-A protein measurements were expressed as multiples of the median (MoM).

Maternal demographic characteristics recorded at the time of the booking visit included maternal age, height, weight, method of conception, smoking status, parity as well as history of PTB and medical disorders. The study was approved by the Ethics Commitee of the Medical University of Warsaw (approval number AKBE/293/2024).

For data analysis we excluded cases of monochorionic twins with early signs of twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome or selective fetal growth restriction such as discordance in CRL of more than 20% between the fetuses or nuchal translucency above 3.5 mm. Also, iatrogenic deliveries due to maternal or fetal complications such as pregnancy induced hypertension, pre-eclampsia, placental abruption, as well as fetal growth restriction and twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome were excluded from further analysis. We excluded patients who experienced surgical trachelectomy or conization. After excluding patients who delivered before 24 weeks’ gestation, a total number of 2004 cases met qualification criteria for the study. Patients were divided into two groups: patients who delivered at 24 + 0 to 33 + 6 weeks and patients who delivered after 34 weeks’ gestation. Only pregnancies resulting in the delivery of two live born infants were included in the study.

Statistical analysis

Statistical comparison of the maternal and pregnancy characteristics in the two groups was performed using Kruskal-Wallis test and post-hoc analysis for continuous variables and chi-square test and Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. We conducted univariable and multivariable logistic regression analysis to determine which maternal and pregnancy characteristics provided a significant independent contribution in the prediction of spontaneous PTB before 34 weeks’ gestation. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant and post hoc Bonferroni correction was used to adjust for multiple comparisons where necessary.

Discussion

Spontaneous PTB in multiple pregnancies remains a multifactorial problem [2]. The possible mechanisms responsible for the beginning of labor are uterine stretch, intrauterine infection, cervical insufficiency or maternal stressors [3]. Among twin gestations, the rate of PTB is 10 times higher than among singletons [2]. Above mentioned factors classify twin pregnancy as high risk for maternal and fetal complications. Defining a group of women in twin pregnancy at high risk of PTB is crucial. The first trimester prediction model can help to implement closer follow up and prevent prematurity.

Principal findings of the study

The mean first trimester CL was 36 mm [32–39] in the group of patients who delivered before 34 weeks’ gestation. The first trimester CL of 39 mm [36–43] was a predictor of labour after 34 weeks of gestation (p < 0.01). Almost half of women who delivered preterm had a first trimester CL of less than 35 mm, another 16.4% had cervix shorter than 30 mm.

Analysing a group of patients with first trimester CL of less than 35 mm almost 22% delivered preterm. Among women with CL less than 30 mm almost 35% delivered preterm (p < 0.01). The first trimester CL cut-off of 35 mm was predictive of PTB before 34 weeks of gestation (p < 0.01).

Comparison with previous studies

Several studies have determined that screening for PTB in the second trimester by CL measurement in twin pregnancies can be effectively achieved using a defined cut-off value [10, 14]. Roman et al. proved that transvaginal measurement of the CL of less than 25 mm in the second trimester predicts preterm birth in twin pregnancies, independently from other risk factors. Cervical length is also a good predictor of PTB in symptomatic women carrying twins with CL of less than 10 mm [2].

The use of CL measurement in the first trimester in twins is rarely described in literature. The reference ranges of CL measurement in the first trimester were reported by Fujit et al. In the group of 144 unselected woman in twin pregnancies who delivered after 34 weeks gestation, the mean CL at 13 weeks was 47 mm [range 44–50] [15]. In the presented study involving 1778 patients who delivered after 34 weeks of gestation, the mean first trimester CL measurement was 39 mm [range 36–43]. The difference between the measurements results most probably from the discrepancy in the sample size between both studies.

We hypothesize that transferring cervical measurement to the first trimester of pregnancy can identify a group of twins at high risk of preterm birth.

Aboulgar et al. in a study presented at World Congress on Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology in 2011 showed a relation between the first trimester CL in twins conceived by IVF/ICSI and preterm delivery [16]. In this study involving 197 twins, the first trimester CL of less than 38.5 mm was predictive of preterm labour before 34 weeks (p = 0.033). These results are similar to ours with the mean CL of 36 mm (range 32–39 mm) in a group of 226 women who delivered before 34 weeks. This data can help to evaluate which patients may require closer CL follow-up.

One of a very few studies on preterm delivery in twins that involved assessment of the first trimester CL was the EVENTS study by Rehal et al. In this randomized double-blind trial authors evaluated the use of vaginal progesterone from 11 to 34 weeks’ gestation to reduce the incidence of spontaneous preterm birth. Secondary analysis showed that progesterone may reduce the risk of preterm birth in women with CL of less than 30 mm [17]. In this study, in the group of patients who delivered before 34 weeks of gestation, the prevalence of CL below 30 mm was 4.2 times higher than in those who delivered after 34 weeks. Above mentioned data suggest that progesterone can be beneficial after the first trimester cervical measurement in a specific group of patients.

Literature reports that CL measurement is important in assessing the risk of PTB among patients in single gestation. Large sample size studies from 2011 to 2012 were created by Greco et al. [18, 19]. The first study consisted of 1492 respondents that delivered after 34 weeks and 16 women who had spontaneous delivery before 34 weeks of gestation. The mean CL was 32.4 mm, moreover compared to unaffected pregnancies, the median endocervical length was significantly shorter (27.5 vs. 32.5 mm, p < 0.0001). The study by Greco from 2012 was a screening study for spontaneous preterm delivery in singleton pregnancies from CL measured by transvaginal ultrasound at 11–13 weeks’ gestation. The median cervical length multiple of the median (MoM), corrected for maternal characteristics, was significantly lower in the preterm (0.892 MoM, 95% CI 0.829–0.945) than in the term delivery group (0.994 MoM, 95% CI 0.919–1.082; p < 0.0001). A study from 2021 by Feng et al. proved that first trimester CL measurement is effective in identification of women at risk of PTB. Moreover the method of two-line measurements provided better prediction for spontaneous PD at < 32 weeks, with a larger area under the curve, than the single-line technique (AUROC: 0.72 vs. 0.61, p < 0.01) [20]. There are still conflicting results in assessment of first trimester CL; large sample sizes are needed to prove the CL measurement as effective. In 2025 Mahale et al. found that cervical length measured at 18 to 22 weeks is more sensitive than cervical length measured at the time of 11 to 13 weeks scan in predicting preterm labor. Moreover, reduction of cervical length between midtrimester and first trimester ultrasound was not effective in predicting PTB (p = 0.001) [21].

Prediction model of preterm delivery

The screening for preterm delivery by first trimester CL measurement in twin pregnancies can be achieved. The preterm birth prediction model for twins should include maternal characteristics as well as first trimester CL measurement. The assessment of CL in the first trimester, helps to distinguish the group of twins which need a close follow up. Maternal characteristics are also important, considering the history of preterm deliveries and co-morbidities.

Strengths and limitations of the study

The strength of this study is the sample size. To our knowledge this is the largest study on CL measurement in the first trimester in twin gestations. The data existing in the literature is very limited. We proved that assessment of the cut-off value for CL can be useful in the prediction of PTB in twins.

The limitations of this study relate to its retrospective design and incomplete data on maternal characteristics. Also, inter-observer variability was not assessed, as measurements were performed by operators across five different centers; however, all were experienced sonographers following the same standardized protocol. Another limitation relates to the fact that CL measurements were performed within a two-week timeframe, which could have influenced the results. Additionally, the measurements were not adjusted for gestational age. Randomized controlled trials are needed to properly establish a customized CL standard in the first trimester and then evaluate the cut-off value for CL predicting preterm delivery.

Conclusion

We showed that CL measurement in the first trimester of pregnancy in twin gestations may be useful in predicting preterm birth before 34 weeks of gestation. However, prospective studies are needed to confirm this hypothesis.

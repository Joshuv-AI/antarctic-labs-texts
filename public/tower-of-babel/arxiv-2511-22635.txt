Abstract
. We give a geometric account of Harish-Chandra’s principle that a tempered irreducible representation of a real reductive group is either square-integrable modulo center, or embeddable in a representation that is parabolically induced from such a representation. Our approach uses the Satake compactification, an associated groupoid that was constructed in the first paper of this series, and its C ∗ C^{*} -algebra.



1.
Introduction

A basic organizing principle in the tempered representation theory of real reductive groups, discovered by Harish-Chandra, is that a tempered irreducible representation of a real reductive group is either square-integrable modulo center, or embeddable in a representation that is parabolically induced from a representation that is square-integrable modulo center. This is one of the two foundational principles underpinning Harish-Chandra’s Plancherel formula, the other being that a reductive group possesses irreducible square-integrable representations precisely when it possesses a compact Cartan subgroup.

The discrete series representations have been analyzed in detail from a geometric point of view—more precisely from an index-theoretic point of view—starting with the work of Parthasarathy
[
26
]
and Atiyah and Schmid
[
4
]
, and culminating in the work of Lafforgue
[
21
]
. Harish-Chandra’s first principle has received less attention.

We shall use a
C^{*}
-algebra constructed from the Satake compactification
[
30
,
6
]
to give a conceptual, noncommutative-geometric proof of Harish-Chandra’s first principle. Our main results are stated precisely in Section
6.1
.

C^{*}
-algebras play two roles in our argument. First, Harish-Chandra’s tempered irreducible unitary representations correspond precisely to those irreducible unitary representations of
G
that integrate to irreducible representations of the reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(G)
, and every irreducible representation of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
is so-obtained
[
10
]
. Second,
C^{*}
-algebra theory provides a simple tool to separate the space of all these irreducible representations into two parts: indeed if
A
is any
C^{*}
-algebra, and if
J
is any ideal in
A
, then there is a partition the spectrum
\widehat{A}
(the set of irreducible representations, up to equivalence) into those representations that vanish on all elements of
J
, and those that don’t, and this partition takes the simple form

\widehat{A}=\widehat{A/J}\,\,\sqcup\,\,\widehat{J};

see for instance
[
12
, Sec. 3.2]
.

In broad terms, glossing over some details for now, our argument will be as follows. We shall introduce in Section
2
an ideal
I
in
A{=}C^{*}_{r}(G)
for which

\widehat{I}=\{\,\text{discrete series representations of $G$}\,\}.

This is a very general construction that may be applied to any unimodular locally compact group. Then, in Section
3
, we shall define a second ideal
J\triangleleft A
such that

\widehat{A/J}=\left\{\,\parbox{245.71754pt}{\begin{center}tempered irreducible representations of $G$ that embed in a principal series representation\end{center}}\,\right\}.

The definition will be specific to real reductive groups, of course, but it will be otherwise very elementary, using only the definition of parabolic induction, as viewed from the perspective of
C^{*}
-algebra theory
[
9
,
8
]
. Harish-Chandra’s principle amounts to the assertion that
I=J
.

In Section
6
we shall prove that the ideals
I
and
J
coincide using the Satake compactification
\mathscr{X}
of the symmetric space associated to
G
, and a groupoid
\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}
that we constructed and studied in
[
7
]
, following ideas of Omar Mohsen
[
24
]
. The reduced
C^{*}
-algebra of the groupoid,
C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}})
fits into an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\operatorname{int}\mathscr{X}})\longrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}})\longrightarrow C^{*}(\mathscr{G}_{\partial\mathscr{X}})\longrightarrow 0

according to the decomposition of
\mathscr{X}
into its interior and boundary. We shall prove that
I{=}J
by relating
I
to the image of the inclusion morphism in the exact sequence, and
J
to the kernel of the quotient morphism; obviously the two ideals in the groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra are the same. Crucial to the argument is a
C^{*}
-algebra morphism

C^{*}_{r}(G)\longrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}})

that was introduced by Mohsen in
[
24
]
and that is studied here first in Section
4
, and then in a slightly generalized form in Section
5
.

It is a pleasure to thank Alain Connes and Omar Mohsen for illuminating conversations that directly inspired our work.

2.
The topological discrete series

In this section we shall investigate what might be called the topological discrete series of a locally compact and unimodular group; these are the irreducible unitary representations that are isolated in the reduced unitary dual of the group. Our treatment of the topic is influenced by the approach of Bernstein
[
5
]
to the smooth representation theory of
p
-adic groups.

For real reductive groups with compact center, the topological discrete series representations turn out to be the precisely Harish-Chandra’s discrete series (they are in any case obviously included in the discrete series). But for our approach to the Harish-Chandra principle is is more appropriate to use the topological discrete series from the outset.

2.1.
The compact ideal in the reduced group C*-algebra

Throughout Section
2
we shall denote by
G
a unimodular, locally compact and Hausdorff topological group.

The following two definitions are appropriate only for groups with compact center (see Remark
2.1.8
), but they can nevertheless be given for any
G
.

2.1.1 Definition
.

An irreducible unitary representation
\pi
of
G
is a
topological discrete series representation
if the singleton set
\{\pi\}
is an open and closed subset of the reduced unitary dual of
G
— which is by definition the spectrum of the reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(G)
.

For the rest of this section, fix a compact subgroup
K\subseteq G
. The definition below depends on the choice of
K
, or rather on the conjugacy class of
K
. But we shall avoid inserting
K
into the terminology because when we turn to reductive groups it will be natural to pick a maximal compact subgroup, obviating the need to mention
K
explicitly.

Let
V
be (the underlying finite-dimensional Hilbert space of) a finite-dimensional unitary representation of
K
. We shall be interested in the Hilbert space

(2.1.2)

L^{2}(G/K;V)=\bigl[L^{2}(G)\otimes V\bigr]^{K},

where the fixed-point space is defined using the right-translation action of
K
on
G
, and of course the given action of
K
on
V
. More geometrically, this is the space of
L^{2}
-sections of the vector bundle over
G/K
that is induced from
V
. The group
G
acts on
L^{2}(G/K;V)
via the left-translation action of
G
on itself.

2.1.3 Definition
.

The
compact ideal

C^{*}_{\operatorname{cpt}}(G)\triangleleft C^{*}_{r}(G)
is

\Bigl\{\,f\in C_{r}^{*}(G):\parbox{270.0pt}{\begin{center}$f$ acts as a compact operator on $L^{2}(G/K;V)$, for every \\
finite-dimensional unitary representation $V$ of $K$\end{center}}\,\Bigr\}

Our goal is to determine the relationship between the topological discrete series and the compact ideal. For this we need one more definition.

2.1.4 Definition
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and let
K
be a compact subgroup of
G
. A unitary representation
\pi
of
G
is
K
-admissible
if the restriction of
\pi
to
K
includes each irreducible representation of
K
with at most finite multiplicity.

Recall now that if
I
is any closed, two-sided ideal in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, then every irreducible representation of
I
extends in a unique way to an irreducible representation of
A
, and in this way the spectrum of
I
is identified with an open subset of the spectrum of
A
, namely the open set of those irreducible representations of
A
that are non-zero on
I
. With this, the main result of Section
2
is as follows:

2.1.5 Theorem
.

Fix
G
and
K
as above. The spectrum of the ideal
C^{*}_{\mathrm{cpt}}(G)
consists of all
(
equivalence classes of
)
irreducible unitary representations of
G
such that

(i)

\pi
is a topological discrete series representation, and

(ii)

\pi
is
K
-admissible.

To begin we shall show that every
K
-admissible topological discrete series representation belongs to the spectrum of the compact ideal (the converse requires a bit more work).

For computations, it will be convenient to recast Definition
2.1.3
as follows. Corresponding to each irreducible unitary representation
V
of
K
there is a central projection
p\in C^{*}(K)
, which acts as the projection onto the
V
-isotypical subspace in any unitary representation of
K
, and every central projection in
C^{*}(K)
is an orthogonal sum of such. Since, when
V
is irreducible,

L^{2}(G){\cdot}p\cong\bigl[L^{2}(G)\otimes V^{*}\bigr]^{K}\otimes V=L^{2}(G/K;V^{*})\otimes V,

where here we are using the right action of
C^{*}(K)
on
L^{2}(G)
, the compact ideal can be described as the ideal of those elements in
C^{*}_{r}(G)
that act as compact operators on
L^{2}(G){\cdot}p
, for every central projection
p\in C^{*}(K)
.

2.1.6 Lemma
.

If an irreducible unitary representation
\pi
is a
K
-admissible topological discrete series representation, then
\pi
is square-integrable.

Proof.

The union of all subalgebras

p{\cdot}C^{*}_{r}(G){\cdot}p\subseteq C^{*}_{r}(G)
,
as
p
ranges over the central projections in
C^{*}(K)
, acting as multipliers of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
, is a dense subalgebra of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
. Under the assumption of
K
-admissibility, each
\pi(p{\cdot}C^{*}_{r}(G){\cdot}p)
is a
C^{*}
-algebra of finite-rank operators on
H_{\pi}
. Therefore, assuming that
\pi
is irreducible,

\pi(C^{*}_{r}(G))=\mathfrak{K}(H_{\pi}),

the
C^{*}
-algebra of compact operators on
H_{\pi}
.

Now if
A
is any
C^{*}
-algebra, and if
\pi
is an irreducible representation of
A
such that
\{\pi\}
is an open and closed subset of
\widehat{A}
, then
A
decomposes as a direct sum of closed, two-sided ideals
A=I(\pi)\oplus\operatorname{ker}(\pi)
, where

(2.1.7)

I(\pi)=\{\,a\in A:a\cdot\operatorname{ker}(\pi)=\operatorname{ker}(\pi)\cdot a=0\,\},

and obviously the restriction of
\pi
to
I(\pi)
is an isomorphism onto
\pi(A)
. Thus

A=I(\pi)\oplus\operatorname{ker}(\pi)\cong\pi(A)\oplus\operatorname{ker}(\pi).

In the present case, because of the direct sum decomposition above, the closed subspace

\overline{I(\pi)\cdot L^{2}(G)}\subseteq L^{2}(G)

is both a subrepresentation of the regular representation of
G
and a representation of
I(\pi)\cong\mathfrak{K}(H_{\pi})
. As a representation of the compact operators, it is a direct sum of irreducible subrepresentations. Each subrepresentation is also a representation of
G
, equivalent to
\pi
, since the action of
G
factors through the projection
C^{*}_{r}(G)\to I(\pi)
. So
\pi
is embeddable into
L^{2}(G)
, and is therefore square-integrable; see for instance
[
12
, Lem. 14.1.2]
.
∎

2.1.8 Remark
.

If
G
fails to have compact center, then it has no square-integrable irreducible unitary representations, and therefore no
K
-admissible discrete series representations. In addition, if
G
fails to have compact center, then
C^{*}_{\mathrm{cpt}}(G){=}0
. Indeed, if
G
fails to have compact center, then the representation of the center on
[L(G)\otimes V]^{K}
has no finite-dimensional subrepresentations, whereas each nonzero self-adjoint element of
C^{*}_{\mathrm{cpt}}(G)
would have a finite-dimensional eigenspace on some
[L(G)\otimes V]^{K}
that would be such a finite-dimensional subrepresentation. So in the noncompact center case, Theorem
2.1.5
is correct but vacuous.

2.1.9 Lemma
.

Every
K
-admissible topological discrete series representation is included in the spectrum of the compact ideal.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be a
K
-admissible topological discrete series representation of
G
. By Lemma
2.1.6
it is square-integrable, and therefore every subrepresentation of
L^{2}(G)
that is equivalent to
\pi
has the form

(2.1.10)

H_{w}=\{\,\varphi_{w,v}\in L^{2}(G):v\in H_{\pi}\,\}

for some nonzero
w\in H_{\pi}
, where
\varphi_{w,v}
is the square-integrable matrix coefficient function

(2.1.11)

\varphi_{w,v}(g)=\langle w,\pi(g)^{-1}v\rangle_{H_{\pi}}.

It follows from the admissibility of
\pi
that for every finite-dimensional unitary representation
V
of
K
the space

\overline{I(\pi)\cdot L^{2}(G/K;V)}=\bigl[\overline{I(\pi)\cdot L^{2}(G)}\otimes V\bigr]^{K},

with
I(\pi)
the annihilator ideal in (
2.1.7
), is a finite-direct sum of copies of
\pi
, and that therefore
I(\pi)
acts compactly on it. Therefore
I(\pi)\subseteq C^{*}_{\mathrm{cpt}}(G)
.
∎

2.2.
A spectral radius-type formula

In order to complete the proof of Theorem
2.1.5
we need the following simple but remarkable formula due to Cowling, Haagerup and Howe.

2.2.1 Theorem

(
[
10
, Remark (d), p.103]
)
.

Let
G
be any locally compact Hausdorff topological group. If
f\in C_{c}(G)
then

\|f\|_{C^{*}_{r}(G)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(f^{*}f)^{n}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{{1}/{2n}},

where the multiplication in
C_{c}(G)
is the convolution product, defined using a choice of Haar measure
(
we use the same choice for both sides of the formula
)
, and the adjoint is the convolution adjoint.

2.2.2 Lemma
.

Let
p\in C^{*}(K)
be a central projection.
The ideal

C_{r}^{*}(G)pC_{r}^{*}(G)\triangleleft C^{*}_{r}(G)

acts isometrically on
L^{2}(G){\cdot}p
.

Proof of Lemma
2.2.2
.

Let us write
\lambda:C^{*}_{r}(G)\to\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(G){\cdot}p)
for the left regular representation of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
on
L^{2}(G){\cdot}p
. It suffices to show that

(2.2.3)

\|\lambda(a)\|_{\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(G){\cdot}p)}\geq\|a\|_{C^{*}_{r}(G)}

for
a\in C_{r}^{*}(G)pC_{r}^{*}(G)
, since the reverse inequality is automatic. Moreover, it suffices to prove this for a dense subset of elements, so let us consider elements of the form

a=\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}py_{j}\in C_{r}^{*}(G){\cdot}p{\cdot}C_{r}^{*}(G),

with
x_{j},y_{j}\in C_{c}(G)
.
If we denote by
\rho
the right action of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
on
L^{2}(G)
, then

\displaystyle\|(a^{*}a)^{n}\|_{L^{2}(G)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|(a^{*}a)^{n-1}a^{*}x_{j}py_{j}\|_{L^{2}(G)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|(a^{*}a)^{n-1}a^{*}x_{j}p\|_{L^{2}(G)}\,\|\rho(y_{j})\|_{\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(G))}

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda(a)\|^{2n-1}_{\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(G){\cdot}p)}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|x_{j}p\|_{L^{2}(G){\cdot}p}\,\|\rho(y_{j})\|_{\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(G))}.

Taking
2n^{\mathrm{th}}
roots, and then taking the limit as
n
tends to infinity, we obtain (
2.2.3
) from Theorem
2.2.1
.
∎

2.3.
Spectrum of the compact ideal

We can now complete the proof of Theorem
2.1.5
:

2.3.1 Lemma
.

Every irreducible unitary representation in the spectrum of the compact ideal, when viewed as an irreducible unitary representation of
G
, is a square-integrable representation.

Proof.

For each central projection
p\in C^{*}(K)
write

(2.3.2)

J_{p}=\overline{C_{\operatorname{cpt}}^{*}(G){\cdot}p{\cdot}C_{\operatorname{cpt}}^{*}(G)}.

This is a closed, two-sided ideal in
C^{*}_{\operatorname{cpt}}(G)
and in
C^{*}_{r}(G)
. Moreover the union of the direct system of all
J_{p}
is dense in
C^{*}_{\operatorname{cpt}}(G)
, and as a result

(2.3.3)

\widehat{C_{\operatorname{cpt}}^{*}(G)}=\bigcup_{p}\widehat{J_{p}}.

So it suffices to prove that each irreducible representation of each
J_{p}
is a square-integrable representation of
G
.

It follows from Lemma
2.2.2
that

J_{p}
is faithfully represented on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(G){\cdot}p
, where, according to the definition of the compact ideal, it acts as compact operators:

(2.3.4)

J_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{1-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{K}(L^{2}(G){\cdot}p).

This means that
J_{p}
is abstractly isomorphic to a
c_{0}
-direct sum of compact operator algebras, and that all of its irreducible representations occur as subrepresentations of the action of
J_{p}
on
L^{2}(G){\cdot}p
; see
[
2
, Thm. 1.4.4]
. In particular, every irreducible representation of
J_{p}
occurs as a subrepresentation of
L^{2}(G)
, and so, viewed as a representation of
G
, it is a square-integrable representation.
∎

2.3.5 Lemma
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. If
\pi(A)=\mathfrak{K}(H_{\pi})
, then the singleton
\{\pi\}
is a closed subset of the spectrum of
A
.

Proof.

This is well known; since
A/\operatorname{ker}(\pi)\cong\mathfrak{K}(H_{\pi})
, and since
\mathfrak{K}(H_{\pi})
has a unique irreducible representation up to equivalence, the singleton
\{\pi\}
is equal to
\{\tau\in\widehat{A}:\tau(\operatorname{ker}(\pi))=0\}
which is by definition a closed set in the spectrum.
∎

2.3.6 Theorem
.

Every irreducible unitary representation in the spectrum of the compact ideal is a
K
-admissible topological discrete series representation.

Proof.

We shall use the ideals
J_{p}
from (
2.3.2
) in the proof of Lemma
2.3.1
, along with the formula (
2.3.3
). Because of the latter, it suffices to prove that each irreducible representation of each
J_{p}
is a
K
-admissible topological discrete series representation.

In the proof of Lemma
2.3.1
we showed that
J_{p}
is a
c_{0}
-direct sum of compact operator algebras, and as a result, its spectrum carries the discrete topology, in which every singleton set
\{\pi\}
is open. Since
J_{p}
is an ideal in
C^{*}_{r}(G)
, its spectrum is an open subset of the spectrum of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
—- which is the reduced dual of
G
— and so
\{\pi\}
is an open subset of the reduced dual, too.

Let us show next that every irreducible representation
\pi
of the compact ideal is
K
-admissible. According to Lemma
2.3.1
,
\pi
, viewed as an irreducible unitary representation of
G
, is square-integrable, and as we noted in (
2.1.10
) it follows that
L^{2}(G)
includes the subrepresentations

H_{w}=\{\,\varphi_{w,v}\in L^{2}(G):v\in H_{\pi}\,\}

with
w\in H_{\pi}
and
\varphi_{w,v}
the matrix coefficient function in (
2.1.11
), all of which are equivalent to
\pi
. They generate a closed subspace of
L^{2}(G)
that is isomorphic to
H_{\pi}\otimes\overline{H}_{\pi}
as a left and right representation of
G
. We obtain, therefore inclusions

(2.3.7)

H_{\pi}\otimes[\overline{H}_{\pi}\otimes V]^{K}\longrightarrow[L^{2}(G)\otimes V]^{K}

of representations of
G
for all finite-dimensional unitary representations
V
of
K
. Now, there is some
f
in the compact ideal with
\pi(f)\neq 0
. Because
f
acts as a compact operator on the right-hand side of (
2.3.7
), it must act as a compact operator on the left-hand side, too, which implies the finite-dimensionality of
[\overline{H}_{\pi}\otimes V]^{K}
and so the admissibility of
\pi
.

Finally,
K
-admissibility implies that
\pi(C^{*}_{r}(G))=\mathfrak{K}(H_{\pi})
, as explained in the proof of Lemma
2.1.6
, so by Lemma
2.3.5
the singleton
\{\pi\}
is closed.
∎

2.4.
The compact ideal and real reductive groups

If
G
is a real reductive group with compact center, then the result about the compact ideal that we shall require is precisely Theorem
2.1.5
above (in the context of real reductive groups we shall always choose
K
to be a maximal compact subgroup). If
G
has noncompact center, then we shall need to modify Theorem
2.1.5
, as described in this section.

If
G
is any real reductive group, then there is a direct product decomposition

(2.4.1)

G={}^{\circ}\hskip-1.0ptG\times A_{\Sigma}

called the
split decomposition
of
G
, in which:

(i)

A_{\Sigma}
consists of all elements in the center of
G
that are inverted by the Cartan involution (the notation is taken from
[
7
, Sec. 3]
and will be reviewed here in Section
3.1
), and

(ii)

{}^{\circ}\hskip-1.0ptG
is generated, as a closed subgroup of
G
, by all of the compact subgroups of
G
.

See for instance
[
19
, Prop. 7.22(f)]
, and see Section
3.1
for more information about our conventions regarding real reductive groups. The group
{}^{\circ}\hskip-1.0ptG
has compact center, and if
G
has compact center then
{}^{\circ}\hskip-1.0ptG=G
.

From (
2.4.1
) we obtain an isomorphism

C^{*}_{r}(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}C^{*}_{r}({}^{\circ}\hskip-1.0ptG)\otimes C^{*}_{r}(A_{\Sigma}),

using which we may define a
compact modulo center
ideal in
C^{*}_{r}(G)
by

(2.4.2)

C^{*}_{\operatorname{cmc}}(G)\triangleleft C^{*}_{r}(G),\qquad C^{*}_{\operatorname{cmc}}(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}C^{*}_{\operatorname{cpt}}({}^{\circ}\hskip-1.0ptG)\otimes C^{*}_{r}(A_{\Sigma}).

Theorem
2.1.5
implies that:

2.4.3 Theorem
.

Under the split decomposition in (
2.4.1
) spectrum of the closed, two-sided ideal
C^{*}_{\operatorname{cmc}}(G)\triangleleft C^{*}_{r}(G)
consists precisely of the tensor products of admissible topological discrete series representations of
{}^{\circ}\hskip-1.0ptG
with unitary characters of the abelian group
A_{\Sigma}
. ∎

2.4.4 Definition
.

We shall say that an irreducible tempered representation
\pi
is an
admissible topological discrete series representation, modulo center
if it has the form given in the statement of Theorem
2.4.3
above.

The group
A_{\Sigma}
acts freely and properly on
G/K
(we shall think of this as a right action, although of course
A_{\Sigma}
is central, so it is also the left action). It therefore acts diagonally on
G/K\times G/K
and we shall write the quotient manifold as

\mathscr{G}_{\Sigma}=G/K\times_{A_{\Sigma}}G/K;

the notation is related to the structure of the Satake compactification, as will be reviewed later. If we view functions
k\in C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\Sigma})
as continuous functions on
G/K\times G/K
that are
A_{\Sigma}
-invariant for the diagonal action, then
C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\Sigma})
generates a
C^{*}
-algebra of operators

C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\Sigma})\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(G/K))

using the usual formula for integral operators on
L^{2}(G/K)
; it is of course a groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra.

More generally, if
V
is a finite-dimensional unitary representation of
K
, then we may form the Hilbert space
[L^{2}(G)\otimes V]^{K}
, which is the Hilbert space of
L^{2}
-sections of the vector bundle over
G/K
induced from
V
, and we may also form the space

C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\Sigma};V)=\bigl[C_{c}(G\times_{A_{\Sigma}}G)\otimes\End(V)\bigr]^{K\times K},

where the first copy of
K
acts on the right of the first copy of
G
and on the left of
\End(V)
; and the second copy of
K
acts on the right of the second copy of
G
and on the right of
\End(V)
. This identifies with the space of continuous compactly supported sections of the bundle over
\mathscr{G}_{\Sigma}
that is obtained by pulling back the vector bundles associated to
V
and
V^{*}
along the two projection maps
\mathscr{G}_{\Sigma}\rightrightarrows G/K
, and then forming the tensor product, as we shall discuss in more detail later. But in any case we may form the completion

C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\Sigma};V)\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(G;V)).

2.4.5 Lemma
.

The unitary isomorphism

L^{2}(G;V)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}L^{2}({}^{\circ}\hskip-1.0ptG/K;V)\otimes L^{2}(A_{\Sigma})

given by the split decomposition (

) conjugates the

-algebra

Proof.

This follows from the right
(K{\times}K)

\begin{gathered}G\times_{A_{\Sigma}}G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}({}^{\circ}\hskip-1.0ptG\times{}^{\circ}\hskip-1.0ptG)\times A_{\Sigma}\\

and the usual characterization of the compact operators as the norm closure of the compactly supported smoothing operators.
∎

The following result offers a characterization of the compact modulo center ideal, along the lines of our definition of the compact ideal, that is more conceptual than the

2.4.6 Theorem

for every finite-dimensional unitary representation

.

2.4.7 Remark
.

Lemma

shows that the

Proof of the Theorem.

This follows from the commuting diagram

\textstyle{C^{*}_{\operatorname{cmc}}(G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

3.
Parabolic induction

The purpose of this section is to record a
C^{*}
-algebraic characterization (in terms of ideals in the reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra) of the tempered irreducible representations of a real reductive group
G
that may be embedded in parabolically induced representations. The material below is quite well known; for instance it is implicit in
[
8
]
.

3.1.
Parabolic subgroups

We shall use the definition of real reductive group from
[
19
, Sec. VII.2]
, which includes all the groups in Harish-Chandra’s class
[
17
, Sec. 3]
; the two definitions coincide for linear groups
[
19
, pp. 447-449]
.

Choose an Iwasawa decomposition
G{=}KAN

[
19
, Prop. 7.31]
. The
standard parabolic subgroups
of
G
are the parabolic subgroups of
G

[
19
, Sec. VII.7]
that include the
minimal parabolic subgroup

P_{\min}{=}MAN
, where
M
is the centralizer of
A
in
K
. Our choice of Iwasawa decomposition determines a system of positive restricted roots
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
, and within this a system of simple roots
\Sigma
. Associated to each subset
I\subseteq\Sigma
is a standard parabolic subgroup

(3.1.1)

P_{I}=M_{I}A_{I}N_{I}\subseteq G.

The groups
P_{I}
are all of the standard parabolic subgroups, and every other parabolic subgroup is conjugate to some
P_{I}
. For all this, see e.g.
[
19
, Sec. VII.7]
, or see
[
7
, Sec. 3.1]
for a summary adapted to the perspectives of this paper.

3.1.2 Example
.

If
G=SL(n,\mathbb{R})
, and if
P_{\min}
is the subgroup of upper-triangular matrices in
G
, then the standard parabolic subgroups are the various block upper-triangular subgroups of
G
, including both
P_{\emptyset}=P_{\min}
and
P_{\Sigma}=G
itself.

3.2.
Parabolic induction via Hilbert C*-modules

For the rest of Section
3
we shall fix a real reductive group
G=KAN
and a standard parabolic subgroup
P_{I}=M_{I}A_{I}N_{I}
.

The Cartesian product group
L_{I}=M_{I}A_{I}
is the associated
Levi factor
, and if
\sigma
is a unitary representation of
L_{I}
, then we may form the unitarily induced representation

\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}\sigma

[
18
, Ch. VII]
by first extending
\sigma
to a representation of
P_{I}
that is trivial on the normal subgroup
N_{I}
, and then applying the functor of unitary induction. We shall always assume that
\sigma
is weakly contained in the regular representation of
L_{I}
, in which case
\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}\sigma
is weakly contained in the regular representation of
G
.

3.2.1 Remark
.

We shall use the notion of weak containment of representations, see
[
12
, Def. 3.4.5]
, extensively below. For most purposes (such as the one above) the definition of weak containment is conveniently formulated in terms of matrix coefficient functions. But it will be important to us that at the
C^{*}
-algebra level there is an equivalent formulation in terms of ideals:
\pi
is weakly contained in
\rho
if and only if
\operatorname{ker}(\rho)\subseteq\operatorname{ker}(\pi)
; see
[
12
, Thm. 3.4.3]
.

The functor of parabolic induction has been recast in terms of Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules by Pierre Clare
[
9
]
, and his perspective will be very convenient for us. What follows is a summary of the variation on Clare’s definitions that is appropriate for tempered representations; see
[
8
, Sec. 4]
for more details.

The Banach space
C^{*}_{r}(G/N_{I})
is a certain norm completion of the space of continuous, compactly supported functions on
G/N_{I}
. It carries a Hilbert
C^{*}_{r}(L_{I})
-module structure associated to the free and proper right-translation action of
L_{I}
on
G/N_{I}
, and a unitary action of
G
that integrates to a left action of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
by adjointable operators on
C^{*}_{r}(G/N_{I})
. Clare’s main observation is as follows (see
[
22
, Ch. 4]
for the tensor product involved in the statement, and for other information about Hilbert modules):

3.2.2 Proposition

(See
[
9
, Cor. 1]
])
.

The functor of parabolic induction
(
from unitary representations of
L_{I}
that are weakly contained in the regular representation to unitary representations of
G
that are weakly contained in the regular representation
)
is equivalent to the tensor product functor
H_{\sigma}\mapsto C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I})\otimes_{C_{r}^{*}(L_{I})}H_{\sigma}
.

3.3.
A C*-algebra characterization of parabolically induced representations

We shall use Clare’s perspective on parabolic induction to prove the following result:

3.3.1 Theorem
.

The following two classes of irreducible tempered representations
G
are equal to one another:

(i)

The irreducible tempered representations of
G
that may be embedded as subrepresentations of some parabolically induced representation
\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}\sigma
, where
\sigma
is some irreducible tempered representation of
L_{I}
.

(ii)

The irreducible tempered representations of
G
that are weakly contained in the regular representation of
G
on
L^{2}(G/N_{I})
.

Denote by
\mathfrak{K}(C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I}))
the
C^{*}
-algebra of compact (in the Hilbert module sense) adjointable Hilbert module operators on
C^{*}_{r}(G/N_{I})

[
22
, p.10]
.

3.3.2 Lemma

(See
[
8
, Prop. 4.5]
)
.

The
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(G)
acts on the Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
C^{*}_{r}(G/N_{I})
through the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{K}(C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I}))
of compact Hilbert
C^{*}
-module operators on
C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I})
.

3.3.3 Lemma
.

Up to unitary equivalence, the
irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{K}(C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I}))
are precisely those of the form

\displaystyle\mathfrak{K}(C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I}))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathfrak{K}\bigl(C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I})\otimes_{C^{*}_{r}(L_{I})}H_{\sigma}\bigr)

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\longmapsto T\otimes I,

where
\sigma
is a tempered irreducible unitary representation of
L_{I}
.

Proof.

The Hilbert
C^{*}
-module

C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I})
is a full Hilbert
C_{r}^{*}(L_{I})
-module (meaning that the closed two-sided ideal generated by all inner products is
C^{*}_{r}(L_{I})
itself). Therefore, considered as a left
\mathfrak{K}(C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I}))
-module, the Hilbert
C^{*}_{r}(L_{I})
-module

C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I})
is a strong Morita equivalence between
\mathfrak{K}(C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I}))
and
C_{r}^{*}(L_{I})
; see
[
22
, Ch. 7]
. The lemma follows from this.
∎

3.3.4 Lemma
.

The regular representations of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
on
C^{*}_{r}(G/N_{I})
and on
L^{2}(G/N_{I})
have the same kernels:

\ker\bigl(C_{r}^{*}(G)\to\mathfrak{K}(C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I}))\bigr)=\ker\bigl(C_{r}^{*}(G)\to\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(G/N_{I}))\bigr).

Proof.

On continuous, compactly supported functions
\varphi,\psi\colon G/N\to\mathbb{C}
, the
C^{*}_{r}(L_{I})
-valued inner product on
C^{*}_{r}(G/N_{I})
is given by the formula

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{C^{*}_{r}(G/N_{I})}(\ell)=\langle\varphi,\psi\cdot\ell^{-1}\rangle_{L^{2}(G/N_{I})},

where the dot indicates the right unitary action of
L_{I}
on
L^{2}(G/N_{I})

[
8
, p. 10]
. If we write this as an identity of continuous, compactly supported functions on
L_{I}
,

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{C^{*}_{r}(G/N_{I})}=\langle\varphi,\boldsymbol{\psi}\rangle_{L^{2}(G/N_{I})},

where
\boldsymbol{\psi}
denotes the function
\ell\mapsto\psi\cdot\ell^{-1}
, and if
\theta
is any continuous, compactly supported function on
L_{I}
, then we obtain

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{C^{*}_{r}(G/N_{I})}*\theta=\langle\varphi,\boldsymbol{\psi}\rangle_{L^{2}(G/N_{I})}*\theta.

From this we obtain

(3.3.5)

\langle\lambda_{C^{*}_{r}(G/N_{I})}(f)\varphi,\psi\rangle_{C^{*}_{r}(G/N_{I})}*\theta=\langle\lambda_{L^{2}(G/N_{I})}(f)\varphi,\boldsymbol{\psi}\rangle_{L^{2}(G/N_{I})}*\theta,

first for
f\in C_{c}(G)
, but then by continuity for all
f\in C^{*}_{r}(G)
, if we view (
3.3.5
) as an identity of continuous functions on
G
(not necessarily compactly supported). The lemma follows from this.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.3.1
.

The inclusion of class (i) in the theorem into class (ii) is clear from Clare’s description of parabolic induction and Lemma
3.3.4
.

Suppose that
\pi
is weakly contained in
L^{2}(G/N_{I})
. By Lemma
3.3.4
,
\pi
is trivial on the kernel of the representation

C_{r}^{*}(G)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{K}(C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I})),

and so it can be viewed as a representation of the image, which is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{K}(C_{r}^{*}(G/N_{I}))
. But now every irreducible representation of a subalgebra
A
of a
C^{*}
-algebra
B
can be embedded into the restriction to
A
of an irreducible representation of
B
; see
[
12
, Prop. 2.10.2]
. It therefore follows from Lemma
3.3.3
that
\pi
may be embedded into some
\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}\sigma
, as required.
∎

3.4.
The cuspidal ideal

Let us borrow a term from Harish-Chandra (see e.g.
[
17
, Sec. 18]
) and make the following definition:

3.4.1 Definition
.

The
cuspidal ideal

C^{*}_{\operatorname{cusp}}(G)\triangleleft C^{*}_{r}(G)
is the intersection of ideals

\operatorname{ker}\bigl(\lambda_{G/N_{I}}\colon C^{*}_{r}(G)\to\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(G/N_{I}))\bigr)

as
I
ranges over all subsets of
\Sigma
other than
\Sigma
itself.

3.4.2 Theorem
.

A tempered irreducible unitary representation of
G
vanishes on cuspidal ideal, and so determines an irreducible representation of the quotient
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(G)/C^{*}_{\operatorname{cusp}}(G)
, if and only if it embeds in a unitary representation of the form

\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}\sigma
, where
I\subseteq\Sigma
is a proper subset of the set of simple restricted roots, and
\sigma
is a tempered irreducible unitary representation of
L_{I}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be a tempered irreducible unitary representation of
G
, viewed as an irreducible representation of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
. Suppose first that
\pi
is
nonzero
on the cuspidal ideal. Then of course
\pi
is nonzero on each individual
\operatorname{ker}(\lambda_{G/N_{I}})
, and so by Theorem
3.3.1
, it can embed in no parabolically induced representation, as in the statement of the theorem.

Suppose next that
\pi
is
zero
on the cuspidal ideal.
It is a general fact about
C^{*}
-algebras that if
\rho
is any irreducible representation of a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, if
J_{1},\dots J_{k}
are closed, two-sided ideals in
A
, and if
\rho
is zero on
J_{1}\cap\cdots\cap J_{k}
, then
\rho
is zero on at least one of
J_{1},\dots,J_{k}
; see
[
12
, Lem. 2.11.4]
. So
\pi
vanishes on at least one of the ideals
\operatorname{ker}(\lambda_{G/N_{I}})
in Definition
3.4.1
, and therefore by Theorem
3.3.1
it embeds in some
\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}\sigma
, as required.
∎

4.
The Satake groupoid and its groupoid C*-algebra

In this section we shall introduce and study the
C^{*}
-algebra of the Satake groupoid. The groupoid was constructed in
[
7
]
, and we shall begin, in Section
4.1
, with a review of some of the topics there. After that and some further preliminaries, we shall construct a morphism from the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra of
G
to the groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra that is central to our approach to the Harish-Chandra principle.

4.1.
The Satake compactification and the Satake groupoid

Let
G=KAN
be a real reductive group. Any two maximal compact subgroups of
G
are conjugate, and it follows from the
KAK
-decomposition
[
19
, Thm. 7.39]
that the normalizer of the distinguished maximal compact subgroup
K
is
K{\cdot}A_{\Sigma}
. So the set of all maximal compact subgroups of
G
may be identified with
G/K{\cdot}A_{\Sigma}
, which gives it a topology, and indeed a smooth
G
-manifold structure.

The (maximal)
Satake compactification
for
G
is a
G
-compactification of the space of all maximal compact subgroups. See
[
30
]
for the original construction, and
[
6
,
15
]
, as well as
[
7
]
, for other accounts.

The Satake compactification
\mathscr{X}
may be constructed in various ways, but for our purposes it is best approached via the method of Oshima
[
25
]
, who embedded
\mathscr{X}
into a smooth closed
G
-manifold
\mathscr{M}
. This
Oshima space

\mathscr{M}
includes a finite family of smooth, closed hypersurfaces, each invariant under
G
, that intersect one another in normal crossings, and the space
G/K{\cdot}A_{\Sigma}
is embedded in
\mathscr{M}
as a single component in the mutual complement of these hypersurfaces. This gives
\mathscr{X}
the structure of a smooth compact
G
-manifold with corners. See
[
7
]
for a review tailored to our purposes.

The Satake compactification has finitely many
G
-orbits. They are in bijection with the subsets of the set
\Sigma
of simple restricted roots (including
\Sigma
itself), as follows: for each
I\subseteq\Sigma
there is a unique point
x_{I}\in\mathscr{X}
whose isotropy group is

(4.1.1)

G_{x_{I}}=K_{I}A_{I}N_{I},

where
A_{I}
and
N_{I}
are the factors in the standard parabolic subgroup
P_{I}{=}M_{I}A_{I}N_{I}
in (
3.1.1
) and
K_{I}=K\cap M_{I}
. The set
\{\,x_{I}:I\subseteq\Sigma\,\}
is a complete set of representatives of the
G
-orbits.

4.1.2 Definition
.

For
I\subseteq\Sigma
we shall denote by
\mathscr{X}_{I}\subseteq\mathscr{X}
the orbit of the point
x_{I}
above.

4.1.3 Example
.

The orbit
\mathscr{X}_{\Sigma}\subseteq\mathscr{X}
is the interior of the Satake compactification (
K_{\Sigma}=K
and
A_{\Sigma}=A_{\Sigma}
).

In
[
7
]
we constructed a Lie groupoid
\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{M}}
whose object space is
\mathscr{M}
, following a method that was introduced by Omar Mohsen in
[
24
]
in the context of foliations. The construction is carried out in two stages. First a family of closed subgroups
H_{m}\subseteq G
, parametrized by the points
m\in\mathscr{M}
, is constructed in such a way that

(i)

H_{m}
is a subgroup of the isotropy group of
m\in\mathscr{M}
,

(ii)

if
m\in\mathscr{M}
and
g\in G
then
H_{g{\cdot}m}=\Ad_{g}(H_{m})
, and

(iii)

the total space of the family,

\mathscr{H}_{\mathscr{M}}=\{\,(h,m):h\in H_{m},\,\,\,m\in\mathscr{M}\,\},

is a closed subset and a smooth submanifold of the Cartesian product space
G{\times}\mathscr{M}
, for which the projection to
\mathscr{M}
is a submersion.

See
[
7
, Sec. 3.5]
. Second, the Oshima groupoid is constructed as a quotient of the transformation groupoid

G\ltimes\mathscr{M}=\{\,(m_{2},g,m_{1}):m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathscr{M},\,\,\,g\in G,\,\,\,gm_{1}=m_{2}\,\}

by
\mathscr{H}_{\mathscr{M}}
, upon viewing
\mathscr{H}_{\mathscr{M}}
as the normal subgroupoid consisting of all those morphisms
(m,h,m)
for which
h\in H_{m}
. This is a Lie groupoid
[
7
, Def. 4.2.2]
.

The Satake compactification
\mathscr{X}\subseteq\mathscr{M}
, being a
G
-invariant subset of
\mathscr{M}
, is a saturated subset for both the transformation and Oshima groupoids, and we define the
Satake groupoid

\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}
to be the reduction of
\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{M}}
to
\mathscr{X}
, or in other words the subgroupoid comprised of morphisms with sources and targets in
\mathscr{X}
. This is not a Lie groupoid, because the object space is not a smooth manifold (without boundary or corners). But it is smooth in the source and target fiber directions (it is a
continuous family groupoid
in the terminology of
[
27
]
), and this suffices for our purposes. Compare
[
11
, Remark 2.6]
.

4.2.
Haar system on the Satake groupoid

Because it is the reduction of the Lie groupoid
\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{M}}
to a closed saturated subset, the Satake groupoid is guaranteed to possess a Haar system, which may be constructed using densities.

As we mentioned, our main objective in Section
4
is the construction of a morphism from the
C^{*}
-algebra of
G
to the
C^{*}
-algebra of the Satake groupoid
\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}
. In doing so we shall be closely following ideas of Omar Mohsen
[
24
]
, but whereas Mohsen uses the density approach to Haar systems, here we shall use explicit measures, constructed as follows.

Fix a smooth family of Haar measures on the subgroups
H_{m}\subseteq G
that comprise the family
\mathscr{H}_{\mathscr{M}}
over the Oshima space
\mathscr{M}
(these are unimodular Lie groups, incidentally).

4.2.1 Lemma
.

There is a unique smooth function

\delta\colon G\times\mathscr{M}\longrightarrow(0,\infty)

such that if
g\in G
and
m\in\mathscr{M}
, and if
f\colon H_{m}\to\mathbb{C}
is any continuous, compactly supported function, then

\int_{H_{m}}f(h)\,dh=\delta(g,m)\int_{H_{g{\cdot}m}}f(g^{-1}hg)\,dh.

Proof.

This is a consequence of usual uniqueness theorem for Haar integrals, since the right-hand side above may be viewed as a Haar integral on
H_{m}
.
∎

Now fix a Haar measure on
G
. With this, and with the chosen Haar measures on the groups
H_{m}
, there are unique right-
G
-invariant smooth measures on the homogeneous spaces

H_{m}\backslash G
for which

(4.2.2)

\int_{G}f(g)\,dg=\int_{H_{m}\backslash G}\left(\int_{H_{m}}f(hg)\,dh\right)d\dot{g}

for all continuous, compactly supported
f\colon G\to\mathbb{C}
. The dot in
\dot{g}
is a reminder that we are integrating over cosets.

Now recall that a Haar system on
\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}
is a smooth family of smooth measures on the target fibers

\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}^{x}=\{\,\gamma\in\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}:\operatorname{target}(\gamma)=x\,\},

such that

(4.2.3)

\int_{\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}^{x_{1}}}f(\alpha\circ\beta)\,d\mu^{x_{1}}(\beta)=\int_{\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}^{x_{2}}}f(\gamma)\,d\mu^{x_{2}}(\gamma)

for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}
with

\operatorname{source}(\alpha)=x_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{target}(\alpha)=x_{2},

and all
f\in C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}})
. See
[
28
, Sec. I.2]
or
[
11
, Sec. 2.2]
.
A straightforward computation now shows that:

4.2.4 Lemma
.

The family of integrals

\int_{\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}^{x}}f\,d\mu^{x}=\int_{H_{x}\backslash G}f(x,g,g^{-1}x)\delta(g^{-1},x)\,d\dot{g}\qquad(x\in\mathscr{X},\,\,\,f\in C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}))

is a Haar system on the Satake groupoid. ∎

4.2.5 Remark
.

Here and below we are representing elements of the Satake groupoid as equivalence classes of triples
(x_{2},g,x_{1})
with
gx_{1}=x_{2}
. The
the equivalence relation is

(x_{2},g,x_{1})\sim(x_{2},gh,x_{1})\qquad\forall h\in H_{x_{1}}

and the groupoid structures are

\displaystyle\operatorname{source}(x_{2},g,x_{1})

\displaystyle=x_{1}

\displaystyle\operatorname{target}(x_{2},g,x_{1})

\displaystyle=x_{2}

\displaystyle(x_{3},g_{2},x_{2})\circ(x_{2},g_{1},x_{1})

\displaystyle=(x_{3},g_{2}g_{1},x_{1}).

4.3.
Regular representations of the groupoid algebra

Using Haar system, we may define a convolution multiplication and
*
-operation on
C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}})
in the usual way, see
[
28
, Sec. II.1]
or
[
11
, Sec. 2.2]
, and so equip
C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}})
with the structure of a
*
-algebra. This may be completed to obtain the
reduced
C^{*}
-algebra

C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}})
using the regular representations of the groupoid, which we briefly review.

For
x\in\mathscr{X}
, denote by
\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X},x}
the source fiber

\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X},x}=\{\,\gamma\in\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}:\operatorname{source}(\gamma)=x\,\}.

The Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X},x})
is the completion of
C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X},x})
in the norm given by the formula

\|\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X},x})}=\int_{\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}^{x}}|\xi(\gamma^{-1})|^{2}\,d\mu^{x}(\gamma),

and
C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}})
is represented as bounded operators on this Hilbert space via the
regular representation

(4.3.1)

6.
The Harish-Chandra principle

6.1.
Statement of the principle

Here is our main result:

6.1.1 Theorem

(Harish-Chandra principle)
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group with Iwasawa decomposition
G{=}KAN
and corresponding system of simple restricted roots
\Sigma
, and let

\pi
be a tempered irreducible representation of
G
. Exactly one of the following is true:

(i)

either
\pi
is an admissible topological discrete series representation, modulo center, in the sense of of Definition
2.4.4
, or

(ii)

\pi
may be embedded as a subrepresentation of some parabolically induced representation
\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}\sigma
, where
I
is a proper subset of
\Sigma
and
\sigma
is a tempered irreducible representation of
L_{I}
.

This is basically due to Harish-Chandra, although a precise statement does not appear in his work. For that, see
[
23
]
or
[
31
]
. By applying the same principle again to the representation
\sigma
in the statement of the theorem, and by using the principle of induction in stages
[
18
, (7.5)]
, one obtains from the theorem the following well-known consequence:

6.1.2 Corollary
.

Let
\pi
tempered irreducible representation of a real reductive group
G
with Iwasawa decomposition
G=KAN
. There is a standard parabolic subgroup
P_{I}=M_{I}A_{I}N_{I}
of
G
, an admissible topological discrete series representation
\sigma
of
M_{I}
, a unitary character
e^{i\nu}
of
A_{I}
, and an embedding of
\pi
into the parabolically induced representation
\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}\sigma\otimes e^{i\nu}
. ∎

The fact that admissibility is built into our approach to the discrete series from the beginning, as in Theorem
2.1.5
, has another interesting consequence:

6.1.3 Corollary
.

Every tempered irreducible unitary representation of a real reductive group is admissible.

Proof.

Frobenius reciprocity shows that the parabolically induced representation obtained from an admissible representation is again admissible; compare
[
18
, Prop. 8.4]
.
∎

This is again a well-known result of Harish-Chandra
[
16
, Thm. 6]
, who proved the same for all irreducible unitary representations. The approach to the result presented here resembles Bernstein’s proof of the admissibility of smooth, irreducible representations of reductive
p
-adic groups
[
5
]
.

6.2.
Proof of the main theorem

The Harish-Chandra principle, as stated in Theorem
6.1.1
, is equivalent to the following identity:

6.2.1 Theorem
.

If
G
is any real reductive group, then
C^{*}_{\operatorname{cmc}}(G)=C^{*}_{\operatorname{cusp}}(G)
.

We shall use the integration morphisms in (
5.2.8
) to reduce the proof of Theorem
6.2.1
to a much easier assertion about ideals in the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra of the Satake groupoid.

6.2.2 Definition
.

If
K
is a finite-dimensional unitary representation of
K
, and if
I
is any subset of
\Sigma
, then we shall write

L^{2}(G/H_{I};V)=[L^{2}(G/N_{I})\otimes V]^{K_{I}}.

Recall that
H_{I}=K_{I}N_{I}
; see (
4.5.1
); in the display, we restrict the representation
V
from
K
to
K_{I}
.

6.2.3 Lemma
.

If
G{=}KAN
is any real reductive group, then

C^{*}_{\operatorname{cusp}}(G)=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subsetneqq\Sigma\\
\text{\rm$V$\,f.d.\,rep.\,of\,$K$}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{ker}\bigl(C^{*}_{r}(G)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(G/H_{I};V)\bigr)

where the intersection is over all subsets of
\Sigma
other than
\Sigma
itself, and over all finite-dimensional unitary representations of
K
.

Proof.

This follows from the
K_{I}
-isotypical decomposition

L^{2}(G/N_{I})\cong\bigoplus_{W\in\widehat{K_{I}}}\bigl[L^{2}(G/N_{I})\otimes W\bigr]^{K_{I}}\otimes W^{*}

together with the fact that every irreducible unitary representation of
K_{I}
occurs within some finite-dimensional unitary representation of
K
, upon restriction.
∎

6.2.4 Lemma
.

The source fiber representation

\lambda_{x_{\Sigma}}\colon C_{r}^{*}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}};V)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{B}\bigl(L^{2}(G/K;V)\bigr)

is faithful. Under the identification

L^{2}(G/K;V)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}L^{2}(G/H_{\Sigma};V)\otimes L^{2}(A_{\Sigma})

the
restriction of
\lambda_{x_{\Sigma}}
to the ideal
C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\operatorname{int}\mathscr{X}};V)
is an isomorphism

C_{r}^{*}(\mathscr{G}_{\operatorname{int}\mathscr{X}};V)\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathfrak{K}(L^{2}(G/H_{\Sigma};V))\otimes C^{*}(A_{\Sigma})

Proof.

The source-fiber representations

\lambda_{x}\colon C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}};V)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X},x}))\qquad(x\in\mathscr{X})

have the semicontinuity property

\|\lambda_{x}(f)\|\leq\liminf_{y\to x}\|\lambda_{y}(f)\|\qquad\forall f\in C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}}).

The lemma follows from this, since
\operatorname{int}\mathscr{X}
is dense in
\mathscr{X}
.
∎

6.2.5 Lemma
.

The compact modulo center and cuspidal ideals in
C^{*}_{r}(G)
may described using the integration morphisms in (
5.2.8
) as follows:

C^{*}_{\operatorname{cmc}}(G)=\bigl\{\,f\in C^{*}_{r}(G):f_{!}\in C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\operatorname{int}\mathscr{X}};V)\,\,\,\forall\,\text{\rm$V$\,f.d.\,rep.\,of\,$K$}\,\bigr\}

and

C^{*}_{\operatorname{cusp}}(G)=\bigl\{\,f\in C^{*}_{r}(G):f_{!}|_{\partial\mathscr{X}}=0\in C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\partial\mathscr{X}};V)\,\,\,\forall\,\text{\rm$V$\,f.d.\,rep.\,of\,$K$}\,\bigr\}.

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma
6.2.4
and the commutativity of the diagram in (

), the assertion that
f_{!}\in C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\operatorname{int}\mathscr{X}};V)
is equivalent to the assertion that under the regular representation of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
on
L^{2}(G/K;V)
,

maps to an element in the image of the regular representation of
C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\operatorname{int}\mathscr{X}};V)
(we are identifying Hilbert spaces here as in Lemma
5.2.6
). But this image is precisely the
C^{*}
-algebra of operators described in Lemma
2.4.5
. So by Theorem
2.4.6
the assertion that
f_{!}\in C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\operatorname{int}\mathscr{X}};V)
for all

is equivalent to the assertion that

, as required.

The second assertion is a consequence of the commutativity of the diagram in (

) again, together with Lemma
6.2.3
and the fact that, by definition of the norm on

, this

-algebra is represented faithfully on the direct sum of all

(so that an element of the

-algebra is zero if and only if it is zero as an operator on each Hilbert space summand).
∎

6.2.6 Lemma
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional unitary representation of
K
. The inclusion and restriction morphisms

C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\operatorname{int}\mathscr{X}};V)\to C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}};V)\quad\text{and}\quad C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}};V)\to C_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\partial\mathscr{X}};V)

This is fairly easy case of a well-known argument using the notion of amenability; see for instance
[

. First, if

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C^{*}_{\max}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}\setminus\mathfrak{Z}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C^{*}_{\max}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C^{*}_{\max}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathfrak{Z}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}\setminus\mathfrak{Z}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C^{*}_{r}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{X}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

is an isomorphism. Third, since

has only a finite number of

is a single

as in (

is amenable; indeed it is Morita equivalent to the abelian group

∎

Proof of Theorem

In view of the formulas in Lemma

by an equivalence relation. In the first stage a smooth family

\mathbf{H}=\{\,(h,t)\in\mathbf{G}:h\in H_{t}\,\}

of closed subgroups
H_{t}\subseteq G
indexed by
\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}
is constructed, and the associated smooth family of homogeneous spaces
\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{H}
is formed. In the second stage, an action of
A
on
\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}
is defined by

(a\cdot t)_{\alpha}=e^{\alpha(\log(a))}t_{\alpha}\qquad\forall\alpha\in\Sigma.

The groups
H_{t}
have the property that

\Ad_{a}H_{t}=H_{a\cdot t}\qquad\forall a\in A,\,\,\,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma},

and so a right-action of
A
on
\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{H}
may be defined by

(gH_{t},t)\cdot a=(gH_{t}a,a^{-1}\cdot t)=(gaH_{a^{-1}\cdot t},a^{-1}\cdot t)\qquad\forall g\in G,\,\,\,\forall a\in A,\,\,\,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}.

The Oshima space is the quotient

\mathscr{M}=(\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{H})/A.

It carries a unique smooth
G
-manifold structure for which the quotient map from
\mathbf{G}
(with the left-translation action of
G
) is a
G
-equivariant submersion. See
[
7
, Thm. 3.3.12]
.

Thinking of
\mathscr{M}
as a quotient of
\mathbf{G}
by an equivalence relation, the Satake compactification is the image in
\mathscr{M}
of the space

\mathbf{G}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}=G{\times}\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}
,
where
\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}
is the space of sequences with zero or positive entries. We may therefore describe the Satake compactification as

\mathscr{X}=(\mathbf{G}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}/\mathbf{H}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}})/A.

A.2.
A continuous family of finite-dimensional unitary representations

If
I
is a subset of
\Sigma
, let us define
t_{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}
by

(A.2.1)

t_{I,\alpha}=\begin{cases}1&\alpha\in I\\
0&\alpha\notin I.\end{cases}

The group
H_{t_{I}}
has a semidirect product structure

H_{t_{I}}=K_{I}\ltimes\theta(N_{I}),

where
K_{I}\subseteq K
and
N_{I}\subseteq N
; see after
[
7
, Def. 3.2.20]
. So
K_{I}
is a quotient of
H_{I}
.

A.2.2 Lemma
.

Let
\pi\colon K\to U(V)
be a finite-dimensional unitary representation of
K

There is a unique family of continuous unitary representations

\pi_{t}\colon H_{t}\longrightarrow U(V)\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}})

such that

(i)

for every
I\subseteq\Sigma
, the representation
\pi_{t_{I}}
is the composition

H_{I}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm quot.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K_{I}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm incl.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}U(V),

(ii)

if
t\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}
,
h\in H_{t}
and
a\in A
, then
\pi_{t}(h)=\pi_{a\cdot t}(aha^{-1})
, and

(iii)

the map

\mathbf{H}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}\ni(h,t)\longmapsto\pi_{t}(h)\in U(V)

is continuous.

Proof.

The elements
t_{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}
constitute a complete set of orbit representatives for the
A
-action on
\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}
, so the family
\{\pi_{t}\}
is certainly uniquely determined by (i) and (ii), assuming it exists.

As for existence, given
t\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}
, choose
a\in A
and
I\subseteq\Sigma
such that
t=a\cdot t_{I}
, and then define

\pi_{t}(h)=\pi_{t_{I}}(a^{-1}ha)\qquad\forall h\in H_{t},

where
\pi_{t_{I}}
is defined as in (i). Now the Lie algebra of
H_{t}
has the form

\mathfrak{h}_{t}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})}\bigl\{\,t^{2\gamma}X{+}\theta(X):X_{\gamma}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}\,\bigr\},

where the direct sum is over the positive restricted roots, and
\mathfrak{m}
is the Lie algebra of the centralizer of
\mathfrak{a}
in
K
. See
[
7
, Def. 3.2.7]
. One computes that the Lie algebra representation of
\mathfrak{h}_{t}
associated to
\pi_{t}
has the form

\begin{gathered}d\pi_{t}\big|_{\mathfrak{m}}=d\pi\big|_{\mathfrak{m}}\\
d\pi_{t}:t^{2\gamma}X_{\gamma}+\theta(X_{\gamma})\mapsto t^{\gamma}d\pi\bigl(X_{\gamma}+\theta(X_{\gamma})\bigr),\end{gathered}

where
t^{\gamma}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma}t_{\alpha}^{n_{\gamma,\alpha}}
, with the nonnegative integers
n_{\gamma,\alpha}
being the coefficients of the expression
\gamma=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma}n_{\gamma,\alpha}\alpha
, as in
[
7
, Def. 3.2.5]
.
This shows that
\pi_{t}
is independent of the choices made in its definition, and that the family
\{\pi_{t}\}
is continuous in
t\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}
, at least at the Lie algebra level.

Now, every element of the group
H_{t}
is a product of an element of
M
and an element of the connected Lie subgroup with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{t}
, see
[
7
, Def. 3.2.20]
. Since
\pi_{t}|_{M}=\pi|_{M}
, for all
t
, the family of Lie group representations
\{\pi_{t}\}
is continuous at the group level, as in item (iii) above. This completes the proof.
∎

A.3.
Construction of the vector bundle

We are now ready to construct our
G
-equivariant Hermitian vector bundles on
\mathscr{X}
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1.1
.

We shall construct the vector bundle in the same way that Oshima constructed the Satake compactification. Thus we begin by forming the topological space
(\mathbf{G}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}{\times}V)/\mathbf{H}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}
, which is the quotient of
\mathbf{G}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}{\times}V
by the equivalence relation

(g,t,v)\sim(gh,t,\pi_{t}(h^{-1})v)\qquad\forall g\in G,\,\,\,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}},\,\,\,\forall h\in H_{t},\,\,\,\forall v\in V.

Then we equip the quotient space with the
A
-action

[g,t,v]\cdot a=[ga,a^{-1}t,v]

and form the further topological quotient space

V_{\mathscr{X}}=((\mathbf{G}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}{\times}V)/\mathbf{H}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}})/A.

This is a family of vector spaces over the Satake compactification, in the sense of
[
3
, Sec. 1.1]
, and the left-translation action of
G
preserves this structure. Local triviality of the family may be checked using the coordinate map

\begin{gathered}N\times\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{X}\\
(n,t)\longmapsto[\![n,t]\!]\end{gathered}

(the double brackets indicate equivalence classes) which is a homeomorphism onto an open subset of
\mathscr{X}
; see
[
7
, Lem. 3.4.3]
. The inner product on
V
gives the equivariant vector bundle we have just defined a
G
-invariant Hermitian structure. Finally, if
I\subseteq\Sigma
and if
t_{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}_{{\hskip-0.8pt\circ\hskip-0.7pt\plus}}
is as in (
A.2.1
), then the map

\displaystyle V_{\mathscr{X}}\big|_{\mathscr{X}_{I}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow G/A_{I}H_{I}\times_{K_{I}}V

\displaystyle[\![g,at_{I},v]\!]

\displaystyle\longmapsto[\![ga,v]\!]

is a
G
-equivariant vector bundle isomorphism over
\mathscr{X}_{I}\cong G/A_{I}H_{I}
, as required.
∎

A.4.
Remark about vector bundles on the Oshima space

One might ask if the construction in the previous section can be improved so as to produce a
smooth
,
G
-equivariant Hermitian vector bundle on the Oshima space
\mathscr{M}
? The answer is yes in a range of cases.

One approach is to examine the family of Lie algebra representations

\mathfrak{h}_{t}\ni t^{2\gamma}X_{\gamma}+\theta(X_{\gamma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d\pi_{t}}}{{\longmapsto}}t^{\gamma}d\pi\bigl(X_{\gamma}+\theta(X_{\gamma})\bigr)\in\mathfrak{u}(V)

with
d\pi_{t}\big|_{\mathfrak{m}}=d\pi\big|_{\mathfrak{m}}
that was encountered in the proof Lemma
A.2.2
. It extends using the same formula to a smooth family of infinitesimally unitary representations
\pi_{t}\colon\mathfrak{h}_{t}\to\mathfrak{u}(V)
parametrized by
t\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}
. If each of these Lie algebra representations integrates to a Lie group representation
\pi_{t}\colon H_{t}\to U(V)
with
\pi_{t}\big|_{M}=\pi\big|_{M}
, then the arguments in the previous two sections will construct a smooth,
G
-equivariant Hermitian vector bundle on
\mathscr{M}
.

The Lie algebra representations
\pi_{t}
do not always integrate to Lie group representations; counterexamples can be found among the finite covers of the group
SL(2,\mathbb{R}){\times}SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, or even of
SO(2){\times}SL(2,\mathbb{R})
. But if
G
has a complexification
G_{\mathbb{C}}
, then this problem does not arise. Indeed, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}^{\Sigma}
there is an element of
A_{\mathbb{C}}
that conjugates
H_{t}
into itself and
conjugates the Lie algebra representation
d\pi_{t}
to the representation
d\pi_{|t|}
. So integrability of
\pi_{t}
follows from the proof of Lemma
A.2.2
.

As a result, if
G
is a linear real reductive group, then each of the
G
-equivariant Hermitian vector bundles
V_{\mathscr{X}}
constructed in the proof of Theorem
5.1.1
is the restriction to
\mathscr{X}
of a smooth,
G
-equivariant Hermitian vector bundle over the Oshima space
\mathscr{M}
.

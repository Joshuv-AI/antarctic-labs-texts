1.
Introduction

This note is intended to elucidate the comment in
[
KV11
, Remark 5.3.4]

concerning the chiral de Rham complex
\Omega_{X}^{\textup{ch}}
of a smooth algebraic variety
X

and the factorization structure of the global formal loop space
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{S}_{C}X

over a
1|1
-dimensional smooth supercurve
C
.
Let us start the explanation with the background.
We work over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers throughout this note.

Background

In
[
BD04
]
, Beilinson and Drinfeld constructed a geometric theory of vertex algebras
by introducing (Beilinson-Drinfeld) chiral algebras and factorization algebras.
The theory is build on the language of
\mathcal{D}
-modules, and gives a coordinate-free formulation.
See also
[
FB04
, Chap. 19,20]
and
[
FG12
]
for the details.

In
[
MSV99
]
, Malikov, Schechtman and Vaintrob introduced the chiral de Rham complex
\Omega_{X}^{\textup{ch}}

of a smooth algebraic variety
X
.
It is a sheaf of dg (differential graded) vertex algebras on
X
,
and can be naively regarded as a loop space analogue, or a semi-infinite analogue,
of the de Rham complex
\Omega_{X}
.

This naive analogy is clarified by Kapranov and Vasserot in
[
KV04
]
,
where the notion of a factorization space is introduced
as a non-linear analogue of Beilinson-Drinfeld factorization algebras.
In
[
KV04
]
, the formal loop space
\mathcal{L}X
of a smooth algebraic variety
X
and
the global formal loop space
\mathcal{L}_{C_{0}}X
over a smooth curve
C_{0}
are constructed as
(tamely-behaving) ind-schemes, and a structure of a factorization space on
\mathcal{L}_{C_{0}}X
is introduced.
Moreover, the de Rham complex
\CDR_{X}
(associated to the right
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module
\Omega_{X}^{\dim X}
)
is constructed from the ind-scheme
\mathcal{L}_{C_{0}}X
as a sheaf of complexes on
X\times C_{0}
.
The fiber of
\CDR_{X}
over a point of
C_{0}
, which is a sheaf of dg vertex algebras on
X
,
is identified with the chiral de Rham complex
\Omega_{X}^{\textup{ch}}
.
Finally, the factorization structure on
\mathcal{L}_{C_{0}}X
induces a chiral algebra
structure on
\CDR_{X}
over
C_{0}
,
which coincides with the vertex algebra structure on
\Omega_{X}^{\textup{ch}}
.
Thus, we have gained some insight into the geometric origin of
the vertex algebra structure of
\Omega_{X}^{\textup{ch}}
.

The chiral de Rham complex
\Omega_{X}^{\textup{ch}}
enjoys many interesting properties,
as studied extensively in these 25 years.
One of them is the supersymmetry.
To explain that, we recall the notion of a SUSY vertex algebra introduced by Heluani and Kac
[
HK07
]
,
which is a superfield formulation of a vertex superalgebra equipped with supersymmetry.
There are two types of SUSY vertex algebras:
N_{W}=N
and
N_{K}=N
algebras, and what is called
an
N
-supersymmetry in physics (and mathematics) literatures corresponds to the
N_{K}=N
one.
Let us also mention the work of Heluani
[
H07
, §4]

which constructed the theory of chiral algebras on supercurves
and investigated the relation to SUSY vertex algebras.

It is known that the chiral de Rham complex
\Omega_{X}^{\textup{ch}}
has a natural structure
of an
N_{K}=1
SUSY vertex algebra
[
HK07
, Example 5.13]
,
[
BHS08
, §4]
.
At this point, one may ask if this SUSY structure on
\Omega_{X}^{\textup{ch}}
can be understood
in terms of some geometric argument, which would be an extension of the argument in
[
KV04
]
.

In
[
KV11
]
, Kapranov and Vasserot generalized the formal loop space construction in
[
KV04
]

to the super setting.
As we will review in
§​

3
, they constructed the formal superloop space
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{S}Y

for a smooth superscheme
Y
of finite type,
and the global formal superloop space
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{S}_{C}Y
for
Y
and a smooth supercurve
C
.
As will be reviewed in
Fact

4.6
,
they also introduced a structure of a factorization space on
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{S}_{C}Y
.

As will be explained in
§​

4.3
, these results in
[
KV11
]
and some materials
from
[
BD04
,
KV04
]
imply that the chiral de Rham complex
\Omega_{X}^{\textup{ch}}

of a smooth algebraic variety
X
can be identified with the fiber of the sheaf
\CDR_{X}

over a point of a smooth supercurve
C
of dimension
1|1
,
and also with the fiber of the structure sheaf of the global formal superloop space
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{S}_{C}X
.
The factorization structure on
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{S}_{C}X
induces on the last one
a natural structure
\mu
(
4.6
) of a chiral algebra over
C
.
According to
[
H07
, §4]
, such
\mu
corresponds to an
N_{W}=1
SUSY vertex algebra structure.
So, there is a slight difference on SUSY structures:
the natural one is
N_{K}=1
, but what is obtained is
N_{W}=1
.

Now, in
[
KV11
, Remark 5.3.4]
, it is commented that the factorization space structure on
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{S}_{C}X

gives “a geometric reason” for the natural
N_{K}=1
SUSY structure on
\Omega_{X}^{\textup{ch}}
.
However, as we mentioned above, a straightforward argument only yields an
N_{W}=1
structure.
As mentioned in the beginning, our goal is to elucidate this problem.

Main argument

Our solution of this problem is given in
§​

5
. The idea is simple:
As the base supercurve
C
of
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{S}_{C}X
, we take a superconformal curve
(C,\mathcal{S})
.
Then, the
N_{W}=1
SUSY chiral product
\mu
(
4.6
) over
C

constructed from the factorization structure can be naturally modified to
a superconformal one
\mu^{\textup{sc}}
(
5.2
) over
(C,\mathcal{S})
.
The modification is made by simply replacing the diagonal
\Delta\subset C^{2}

with the superdiagonal
\Delta^{\textup{sc}}\subset C^{2}
(see
Definition

2.6
).
Note that we used the superconformal structure
\mathcal{S}
to get the well-defined
\Delta^{\textup{sc}}
.

2.1.
Preliminary of notations

Following
[

, §1.1]

The

Given a superscheme

, its

\mathcal{J}_{X}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}

(2.1)

\displaystyle X_{\textup{red}}\coloneqq(\underline{X},\mathcal{O}_{X,\overline{0}}/\sqrt{\mathcal{O}_{X,\overline{0}}}).

As given in
[
KV11
, §1.2, p.1085]

smooth
(

)
supervariety

X
over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
.
The structure morphism
X\to\Spec\mathbb{C}
is then a smooth morphism, and the dimension
\dim X

(relative over
\Spec\mathbb{C}
) is in
\mathbb{N}^{2}
, denoted as
p|q
.
For example, the
affine superspace
of dimension
p|q

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{p|q}_{\mathbb{C}}\coloneqq\Spec(\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{p}]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\dotsc,\xi_{q}])

is a smooth supervariety,
where
x_{i}
’s are even and
\xi_{j}
’s are odd coordinate functions.

Hereafter we suppress the word “over
\mathbb{C}
” if confusion may not occur.
As explained in
[
KV11
, §1.1, (1.1.2)]
,
for a morphism
A\to B
of supercommutative algebras,
we have the
module of Kähler differentials

\Omega_{B/A}^{1}\coloneqq I/I^{2},\quad I\coloneqq\Ker(B\otimes_{A}B\xrightarrow{m}B),

where
m
denotes the multiplication map.
It is a
B
-module equipped with the universal differential
d\colon B\to\Omega_{B/A}^{1}
.
We understand that
d
is
even
(see
[
Y22
, §1.2, p.103, Notation]
for the detail).
As a global counterpart, for a morphism
X\to Y
of superschemes,
we have the
sheaf
\Omega_{X/Y}^{1}
of Kähler differentials
on
X
.
It is a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module equipped
with an even universal differential
d\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\Omega_{X/Y}^{1}
.
We also have the de Rham (super)complex

(2.3)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{\ d\ }{}\Omega_{X/Y}^{1}\xrightarrow{\ d\ }{}\Omega_{X/Y}^{2}\xrightarrow{\ d\ }{}\cdots,\quad\Omega_{X/Y}^{p}\coloneqq\Wedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{p}\Omega_{X/Y}^{1}.

Note that this complex is unbounded in general even if
X
is smooth over
Y
.
If
Y=\Spec\mathbb{C}
, we denote

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Omega_{X}^{1}\coloneqq\Omega_{X/\Spec\mathbb{C}}^{1},\quad\Omega_{X}^{p}\coloneqq\Wedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{p}\Omega_{X}^{1}\quad(p\in\mathbb{N}).

Let
X
be a smooth supervariety of dimension
p|q
.
By
[
KV11
, §1.2]
, the sheaf
\Omega_{X}^{1}
is locally free of rank

.
The
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-dual of
\Omega_{X}^{1}

\Theta_{X}
of
X
,
and it is isomorphic to the sheaf
\shDer_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{O}_{X})

\mathcal{O}_{X}

\mathbb{C}

\Theta_{X}\coloneqq\shHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\Omega_{X}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{X})\cong\shDer_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{O}_{X}).

The tangent sheaf is a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
(regarded as
\shHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\Omega_{X}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{X})
)
and a sheaf of Lie

-superalgebras on

(regarded as
\shDer_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{O}_{X})

It is moreover a Lie superalgebroids on

[

Let
X
be as before.
By

(2.5)

\displaystyle Z=(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{p},\zeta_{1},\dotsc,\zeta_{q})\colon U\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{p|q}

containing

,
with

’s odd, such that they generate the maximal ideal

(2.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{Z}=(\partial_{z_{1}},\dotsc,\partial_{z_{p}},\partial_{\zeta_{1}},\dotsc,\partial_{\zeta_{q}}),

.

(or the pair

.

2.2.
The differential operators
\mathcal{D}
in the super setting

Let
X
be a smooth supervariety over
\mathbb{C}
,
and
\shEnd_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
be the endomorphism sheaf of the superalgebra
\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
The
sheaf of differential operators on
X
is the subsheaf

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{X}\subset\shEnd_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{O}_{X})

generated by
\mathcal{O}_{X}
(considered as multiplication operators)
and
\Theta_{X}\cong\shDer_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
(considered as derivations).

In this note, a
quasi-coherent left
(resp.
right
)
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module means
a left (resp. right)
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module which is quasi-coherent as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
We denote the category of quasi-coherent left, resp. right,
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-modules by

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},l}_{X},\quad\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},r}_{X}.

In particular,
\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},l}_{X}
is the category of quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
equipped with (left) integrable connections along
X
.

As in the non-super case (
[
HTT08
, §1.2]
,
[
BD04
, 2.1.1]
), if
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},l}_{X}
,
then
\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{L}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is a left
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module.
Thus
\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},l}_{X}
is a monoidal category with unit object
\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
Also, if
\mathcal{L}\in\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},l}_{X}
and
\mathcal{M}\in\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},r}_{X}
,
then
\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{L}
is a right
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module on which

\tau\in\Theta_{X}\subset\mathcal{D}_{X}
acts as

(m\otimes l)\tau=(m\tau)\otimes l-m\otimes(\tau l
).
We denote it by
\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{L}\in\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},r}_{X}
.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},l}_{X}
, then
\shHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}^{\prime})\in\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},l}_{X}
by

(\tau f)(m)=-f(m)\tau+f(m\tau)
.

We denote the
Berezinian sheaf of
X
by

(2.8)

\displaystyle\omega_{X}\coloneqq\shBer(\Omega_{X}^{1}).

It is a right
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module by
\nu\tau\coloneqq-\Lie_{\tau}(\nu)
for
\nu\in\omega_{X}
and

\tau\in\Theta_{X}
, where
\Lie
denotes the Lie derivative.
See
[
DM99
, §1.10, §1.11]
and
[
N23
]
for the details of
\omega_{X}
.
As shown in
[
P83
]
, the categories
\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},l}_{X}
and
\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},r}_{X}
are equivalent by

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},l}_{X}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }{}\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},r}_{X},\quad&\mathcal{L}\longmapsto\mathcal{L}^{r}\coloneqq\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{L},\\
\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},r}_{X}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }{}\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},l}_{X},\quad&\mathcal{M}\longmapsto\mathcal{M}^{l}\coloneqq\omega_{X}^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}\cong\shHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\omega_{X},\mathcal{M}),\end{split}

If
q=0
, i.e.,
X
is even, then
\omega_{X}
is equal to the top differential forms
\Omega_{X}^{p}
.
(c.f. (
2.3
)).
The category
\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},r}_{X}
is a monoidal category with

\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigl((\mathcal{M}^{l})\otimes(\mathcal{M}^{\prime l})\bigr)^{r}\in\mathsf{D}^{\textup{qc},r}_{X}

and unit object
\omega_{X}
.

For a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of smooth superschemes, we denote the (underived) direct and
inverse image functors for (left or right)
\mathcal{D}
-modules by
f_{*}
and
f^{*}
, respectively.
For a left
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-module
\mathcal{L}
, the
\mathcal{D}
-module inverse image

-module, equipped with a natural left

The right

-module inverse image is then given by the left-right equivalence (

).
For a right
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{M}
, the

-module direct image

-module

\displaystyle f_{\cdot}(\mathcal{L}\otimes_{\mathcal{D}_{X}}\mathcal{D}_{X\to Y}),\quad\mathcal{D}_{X\to Y}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{Y}}f^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{Y},

Here

denotes the

2.3.
Supercurves and superconformal curves

Definition 2.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
A
smooth supercurve of dimension
1|N
is a smooth supervariety
C
with
\dim(C)=1|N
.
A
superconformal curve of dimension
1|N
is
a smooth supercurve
C
of dimension
1|N
equipped with a locally free subsheaf

\mathcal{S}\subset\Theta_{C}
of rank
0|N
such that the composition map

\mathcal{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{S}\xrightarrow{[\cdot,\cdot]}\Theta_{C}\xrightarrow{\bmod\mathcal{S}}\Theta_{C}/\mathcal{S}

induces an
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-module isomorphism
\mathcal{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{S}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Theta_{C}/\mathcal{S}
.

As explained in
[
H07
, §4]
, we can consider an
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebras
as a chiral algebra on a smooth supercurve of dimension
1|N
,
and an
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebra as one on a superconformal curve of dimension
1|N
.

In this note, we only consider superconformal curves of dimension
1|1
,
and suppress the term “of dimension
1|1
” if no confusion may occur.
They are also called SUSY curves (in
[
BH21
,
BHP
]
for example),
and called
\text{SUSY}_{1}
curves in
[
M91
]
.
In the complex analytic setting, they are called super Riemann surfaces (in
[
W19
]
for example).

Let us recall local properties of superconformal curves.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(C,\mathcal{S})
be a superconformal curve.

(1)

For each point
x
of
C
, there is a local coordinate system
Z=(z,\zeta)

at
x
such that
\mathcal{S}
is locally generated as an
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-module by the odd derivation

(2.11)

\displaystyle\not{\partial}_{Z}\coloneqq\partial_{\zeta}+\zeta\partial_{z}.

(2)

The
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-dual
\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\coloneqq\shHom_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{O}_{C})

satisfies
\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\cong\omega_{C}
.

(3)

Let
\tau\colon\Omega_{C}^{1}\to\mathcal{S}^{\vee}
be the dual of the injection
\mathcal{S}\hookrightarrow\Theta_{C}
.
Then the composition

\Ker\tau\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\Omega_{C}^{1}\xrightarrow{\ d\ }{}\Omega_{C}^{2}\xrightarrow{\ \tau\wedge\tau\ }{}(\mathcal{S}^{\vee})^{\otimes 2}

yields an isomorphism

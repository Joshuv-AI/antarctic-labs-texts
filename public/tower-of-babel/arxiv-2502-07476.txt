Persistent bundles over configuration spaces
and obstructions for regular embeddings

Abstract




1
Introduction

Configuration spaces
attract attention in various areas of mathematics.
For instance,
the configuration spaces of hard spheres
(for example,
[
1
,
2
,
8
]
) is a geometric model of
the sphere packing problem and have applications in
statistical mechanics
(for example,
[
26
]
);
the topology of configuration spaces has intrinsic
connections with iterated loop spaces
(for example,
[
11
,
16
,
31
]
)
and braid groups (for example,
[
11
,
12
]
);
etc.

Let
X
be a CW-complex. Let
k
be a positive integer. The
ordered configuration space

{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)
is the subspace of the Cartesian product
X^{k}
consisting of the points

(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{k})
such that
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
if
i\neq j
.
The symmetric group
\Sigma_{k}
on
k
-letters acts on
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)
from the left by

\displaystyle\sigma(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{k})=(x_{\sigma(1)},x_{\sigma(2)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k)}),\text{\ \ \ }\sigma\in\Sigma_{k}.

(1.1)

This induces a covering map from
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)
to
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)/\Sigma_{k}
.
The associated vector bundle of this covering map is

\displaystyle\xi(X,k):\mathbb{R}^{k}\longrightarrow{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)\times_{\Sigma_{k}}\mathbb{R}^{k}\longrightarrow{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)/\Sigma_{k}

(1.2)

where
\Sigma_{k}
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
from the right by

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k})\sigma=(a_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},a_{\sigma^{-1}(2)},\cdots,a_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}),\text{\ \ \ }\sigma\in\Sigma_{k}.

(1.3)

The complexification of
\xi(X,k)
is a complex vector bundle

\displaystyle\xi(X,k)\otimes\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{C}^{k}\longrightarrow{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)\times_{\Sigma_{k}}\mathbb{C}^{k}\longrightarrow{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)/\Sigma_{k}.

(1.4)

Let
\mathbb{F}
be the real numbers
\mathbb{R}
or the complex
numbers
\mathbb{C}
.
We write (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) uniformly as

\xi(X,k;\mathbb{F})
where
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
gives (
1.2
)
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
gives (
1.4
).

The bundles
\xi(X,k;\mathbb{F})

have various applications in topology and geometry and
have been extensively studied since 1970’s (for example,
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
13
,
14
,

,

,

).
Among these applications,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
) is interesting and attracts
attention during the last half century.

The regular embedding problem is initially studied
by K. Borsuk
[

]
in 1957 and later developed
by F.R. Cohen and D. Handel
[
15
]
in 1978,
M.E. Chisholm
[

]
in 1979,
D. Handel and J. Segal

]
in 1980,
D. Handel
[

]
in 1996,
P. Blagojević, W. Lück and G. Ziegler

]

P. Blagojević, F.R. Cohen, W. Lück and G. Ziegler
[

]
in 2016,

A map
f:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}^{N}
is called

(real or complex)
k
-regular

if for any distinct
k
points in
X
,
their images are linearly independent in
\mathbb{F}^{N}
.
When
k\geq 2
, such a map is an embedding.
By the Haar-Kolmogorov-Rubinstein Theorem (cf.
[
19
]
and
[
32
, pp. 237-242]
),
one of the motivations to study
k
-regular maps is
the Čebyšev approximation.

The existence problems of
k
-regular maps
as well as their obstructions
are investigated.
For example, D. Handel studied
obstructions for
3
-regular maps
[
20
]

in 1979 and studied
some existence and non-existence problems of

k
-regular maps
[
21
]
in 1980.

The bundles
\xi(X,k;\mathbb{F})

can be
applied to give obstructions for the existence of
k
-regular maps.
Some obstructions using the Stiefel-Whitney classes of
\xi(X,k)

for the existence of
k
-regular maps were proved by
F. Cohen and D. Handel
[
15
]
, restated in
[
29
, Lemma 4.2]
.
Some obstructions using the Chern classes of
\xi(X,k;\mathbb{C})

for the existence of complex
k
-regular maps were proved by
P. Blagojević, F. Cohen, W. Lück and G. Ziegler
[
5
]
,
restated in
[
29
, Lemma 4.3]
.

In this paper, we apply the method of persistent homology to
the bundles over configuration spaces.
Then we weaken the
k
-regularity to define a
(k,r)
-regularity
condition for embeddings (cf. Definition
21
)
and
give obstructions for the
(k,r)
-regular embedding problem.

Precisely,
we give a filtration
of
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)
by the configuration spaces of hard spheres
and construct a family of pull-back
bundles of
\xi(X,k;\mathbb{F})
.
As the persistent version of
\xi(X,k;\mathbb{F})
,
we define the persistent bundles

\boldsymbol{\xi}(X,k,-;\mathbb{F})
(cf. eq. (
4.7
)).
We generalize the
k
-regularity and define the
(k,r)
-regular embeddings
for any
r\geq 0
.
We equip a metric
d
on
X
and call a map

f:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}^{N}
to be
(k,r)
-regular

if for any
k
points in
X
whose pairwise distances are greater than
2r
,
their images are linearly independent (cf. Definition
21
).
We prove some obstructions for the existence of the
(k,r)
-regular maps
by using the characteristic
classes of
\boldsymbol{\xi}(X,k,-;\mathbb{F})
,
which are elements in the persistent cohomology rings
of filtrations of the unordered configuration space
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)/\Sigma_{k}
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. For any positive integer
k

and any nonnegative number
r
,
the
ordered configuration space of hard spheres of radius
r
,
denoted by
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,r)
,
is the subspace of
X^{k}
consisting of the points

(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{k})
whose coordinates satisfy
d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r
for any
i\neq j
.
In particular, the space
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,0)
is
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)
.
The symmetric group

\Sigma_{k}
acts on
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,r)

freely and properly discontinuously.
The orbit space
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,r)/\Sigma_{k}

is called the
unordered configuration space
of hard spheres of radius
r
.
The family
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,r)
for all
r\geq 0
, denoted by
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,-)
,
gives a filtration of
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)
.
The
\Sigma_{k}
-action on
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X)

is equivariant with respect to the filtration thus it induces
a filtered orbit space

{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,-)/\Sigma_{k}
and a covering map of filtered spaces
from
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,-)
to
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,-)/\Sigma_{k}
.

Let
{\rm Conf}_{\bullet}(X,-)
be the collection of
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,-)
and
let
{\rm Conf}_{\bullet}(X,-)/\Sigma_{\bullet}
be the collection of

{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,-)/\Sigma_{k}
for
k\geq 1
.
A persistent double complex for

{\rm Conf}_{\bullet}(X,-)

will be constructed in Theorem
4.5
.
In particular, if we let
X
be a Riemannian manifold
M
,
then the differential forms on
{\rm Conf}_{\bullet}(M,-)

will give a persistent double complex. The
\Sigma_{\bullet}
-invariant forms
on
{\rm Conf}_{\bullet}(M,-)
, or alternatively the differential forms on

{\rm Conf}_{\bullet}(M,-)/\Sigma_{k}
,
will give a persistent double complex as well (cf. Subsection
4.1
).
Moreover, if we let
X
be a graph
G
and let

d
be the canonical geodesic distance of
G
,
then
{\rm Conf}_{\bullet}(V_{G},-)/\Sigma_{\bullet}
will
give a filtration of the independence complex of
G

(cf. Subsection
4.2
).

Embed the discrete fibre of the covering map
from
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,-)
to
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,-)/\Sigma_{k}

into
\mathbb{F}^{k}
as the unit vectors of the
k

We obtain a persistent vector bundle
\boldsymbol{\xi}(X,k,-;\mathbb{F})

(cf. Definition
4
and Theorem
4.2
)
over the filtered space
{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,-)/\Sigma_{k}
.
The Stiefel-Whitney class of
\boldsymbol{\xi}(X,k,-;\mathbb{R})

is a persistent cohomology class in the persistent cohomology ring

H^{*}({\rm Conf}_{k}(X,-)/\Sigma_{k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and the
Chern class of
\boldsymbol{\xi}(X,k,-;\mathbb{C})

is a persistent cohomology class in the persistent cohomology ring

H^{*}({\rm Conf}_{k}(X,-)/\Sigma_{k};\mathbb{Z})
.

\displaystyle N\geq\sup\{t(k)\mid\bar{w}_{t(k)}(\boldsymbol{\xi}(X,k,-+r;\mathbb{R}))\neq 0\}+k;

(1.5)

(2)

-regular map,

\displaystyle N\geq\sup\{t(k)\mid\bar{c}_{t(k)}(\boldsymbol{\xi}(X,k,-+r;\mathbb{C}))\neq 0\}+k.

If

,

In Section

and the characteristic classes of persistent bundles.
In Section

Both Section

In Section

In Section

Persistent fibre bundles

In this section, we introduce the definitions of persistent covering maps (cf. Definition

Definition 1
.

A
persistent CW-complex

\mathbf{A}

is a family of CW-complexes
A(t)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}

together with a family of maps

\alpha(t_{1},t_{2}):A(t_{1})\longrightarrow A(t_{2})
for
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
such that

(1)

\alpha(t,t)
is the identity map for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(2)

\alpha(t_{1},t_{3})=\alpha(t_{2},t_{3})\circ\alpha(t_{1},t_{2})
for any
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq t_{3}
.

Definition 2
.

The
persistent cohomology ring

H^{*}(\mathbf{A})
of a persistent CW-complex
\mathbf{A}

is the family of cohomology rings
H^{*}(A(t))
for
t\in\mathbb{R}

together with the family of induced homomorphisms

\alpha(t_{1},t_{2})^{*}:H^{*}(A(t_{2}))\longrightarrow H^{*}(A(t_{1}))
for
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
.

The persistent cohomology ring
is an analog of the persistent homology (cf.
[
34
]
)
with extra consideration of cup products.
For the persistent cohomology ring

of a persistent CW-complex

\mathbf{A}
, it follows from Definition
1

and Definition

that

(1)

\alpha(t,t)^{*}
is the identity map for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(2)

\alpha(t_{1},t_{3})^{*}=\alpha(t_{1},t_{2})^{*}\circ\alpha(t_{2},t_{3})^{*}
for any
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq t_{3}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f:{\bf A}\longrightarrow{\bf B}
be a persistent fibre bundle.
Then the family of fibre bundles

F(t)\longrightarrow A(t)\overset{f(t)}{\longrightarrow}B(t)

has a uniform fibre
F(t)=F
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

For any
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
, since the diagram (
3
)
is a pull-back, we have that

F(t_{1})\longrightarrow A(t_{1})\overset{f(t_{1})}{\longrightarrow}B(t_{1})

is a pull-back fibre bundle
of

F(t_{2})\longrightarrow A(t_{2})\overset{f(t_{2})}{\longrightarrow}B(t_{2})
.
Hence

F(t_{1})=F(t_{2})
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
f:{\bf A}\longrightarrow{\bf B}
be a persistent covering map.
Then the family of covering maps

Proof.

For any

the discrete fibres

Hence they have the same cardinality.

5.1
Obstructions for strong totally geodesic embeddings of
Riemannian manifolds

Let
M^{\prime}
be a Riemannian manifold.
A Riemannian submanifold
M\subseteq M^{\prime}
is called
totally geodesic
if every
geodesic of
M
is also a geodesic of
M^{\prime}
(cf.
[
27

We give a condition stronger
than the totally geodesic condition in the next definition.

Definition 19
.

We call a Riemannian submanifold
M\subseteq M^{\prime}

strong totally geodesic

if for any
p,q\in M
, every geodesic of
M^{\prime}
connecting
p
and
q

is also a geodesic of
M
.

Example 5.1
.

Let
m_{1}<m_{2}
be positive integers.
The unit sphere
S^{m_{1}}
is a totally geodesic submanifold
of the unit sphere
S^{m_{2}}
.
However,
S^{m_{1}}
is not a strong totally geodesic submanifold of
S^{m_{2}}

since
any pair of antipodal points in
S^{m_{1}}

can be connected by the semi-circles of any great circles
in
S^{m_{2}}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
M^{\prime}
be a complete Riemannian manifold.
Then
any strong totally geodesic submanifold
M\subseteq M^{\prime}
is totally geodesic.

Proof.

Let
M\subseteq M^{\prime}
be a strong totally geodesic submanifold.
Let
\gamma
be a geodesic of
M
.
It suffices to prove
\gamma
is a geodesic of

Lemma 5.4

Let

Suppose

Let

For any geodesic

of

to

is strong totally geodesic.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
M^{\prime}
be a Riemannian manifold.
Let

be a Riemannian
submanifold of
M^{\prime}
.
Then

\displaystyle d_{M}(p,q)\geq d_{M^{\prime}}(p,q)

for any

Let

\displaystyle d_{M}(p,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{\gamma:[0,1]\longrightarrow M\atop\gamma(0)=p{\rm~and~}\gamma(1)=q}{\rm length}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{\gamma^{\prime}:[0,1]\longrightarrow M^{\prime}\atop\gamma^{\prime}(0)=p{\rm~and~}\gamma^{\prime}(1)=q}{\rm length}(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle d_{M^{\prime}}(p,q).

.
Then by Definition

of

\displaystyle d_{M^{\prime}}(p,q)

\displaystyle\inf_{\gamma^{\prime}:[0,1]\longrightarrow M^{\prime}{\rm~is~a~geodesic~of~}M^{\prime}\atop\gamma^{\prime}(0)=p{\rm~and~}\gamma^{\prime}(1)=q}{\rm length}(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle\inf_{\gamma:[0,1]\longrightarrow M{\rm~is~a~geodesic~of~}M\atop\gamma(0)=p{\rm~and~}\gamma(1)=q}{\rm length}(\gamma)

\displaystyle d_{M}(p,q).

(2) Suppose the equality of (

of

(3) Suppose for any

By (2),

is strong totally geodesic.

for some

, then

\displaystyle N\geq t(k)+k.

Proof.

Suppose

is a
(k,r)
-regular map.
Let

in (

).
Then
the induced map

\displaystyle\iota^{*}:H^{*}(G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{\infty});\mathbb{Z}_{2})\longrightarrow H^{*}(G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{N});\mathbb{Z}_{2})

an epimorphism

\displaystyle\iota^{*}:\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{k}]\longrightarrow\frac{{\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{k}]}}{{(\bar{w}_{N-k+1},\bar{w}_{N-k+2},\cdots,\bar{w}_{N})}},

\displaystyle(\bar{w}_{N-k+1},\bar{w}_{N-k+2},\cdots,\bar{w}_{N})

,
\bar{w}_{N-k+2}
,
\cdots
,
\bar{w}_{N}

By substituting (

), we have
homomorphisms of persistent cohomology algebras
such that the diagram commutes

\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{k}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota^{*}}
\scriptstyle{(\iota\circ f)^{*}}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{k}]}/{(\bar{w}_{N-k+1},\bar{w}_{N-k+2},\cdots,\bar{w}_{N})}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

It follows that

and

It follows from (
6.14
) and (
6.15
) that
either

or

. Suppose

. For any

(k,r)
-regular map

from

into

into

.

is finite.

).
∎

7
Applications of the regular embeddings to geometric realizations of
the independence complexes

The geometric realizations of simplicial complexes attract attention in
computer science (cf.
[
28
]
).
Let
V
be a discrete set with a total order
\prec
.
An
abstract simplicial complex

\mathcal{K}
with vertices from
V

is a family of nonempty subsets of
V
such that
for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
and any nonempty subsets
\tau
of
\sigma
,
it holds
\tau\in\mathcal{K}
(cf.
[
24
, p. 107]
).
An element
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
is called a
simplex
(cf.
[
24
, p. 103]
).
Let
\sigma=\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
be a simplex
where
v_{0}\prec v_{1}\prec\cdots\prec v_{n}
.
A
geometric realization
of
\sigma

is a map
i_{\sigma}:\sigma\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that

i_{\sigma}(v_{k})=e_{k}
for
0\leq k\leq n
, where

e_{0}
,
e_{1}
,
\ldots
,
e_{n}
is a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Given a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
,
let
V_{\mathcal{K}}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{K}}\sigma

be the set of vertices of
\mathcal{K}
.
A
geometric realization
of
\mathcal{K}

is a map
i_{\mathcal{K}}:V_{\mathcal{K}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N+1}

such that for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
,
the restriction

i_{\sigma}=i_{\mathcal{K}}\mid_{\sigma}
induces
a geometric realization of
\sigma
in an affine subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space.
Consider the
persistent independence complex

\displaystyle{\rm Ind}(X,-)=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}{\rm Conf}_{k}(X,-)/\Sigma_{k}

(7.1)

where the parameter is
r\in[0,+\infty]

such that (
7.1
) is a filtered simplicial complex.
In particular,

(1)

if
X
is a Riemannian manifold with a Riemannian metric
g
,
then
{\rm Ind}(X,-)
is a persistent
\Delta
-manifold with the induced
Riemannian metric
g^{k}/\Sigma_{k}
;

(2)

if
X
is the vertex set of a graph, then
{\rm Ind}(X,-)
is a persistent simplicial complex
such that the vertices are discrete.

The
k
-regularity can be weakened to
the affine
k
-regularity (cf.
[
4
, Definition 2.4]
).
We call a map
f:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}

affine
(k,r)
-regular
if for any distinct

k
-points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in X
such that
d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r
for any
i\neq j
,
their images
f(x_{1})
,
\ldots
,
f(x_{k})
are affinely independent in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Note that the affine
(k,0)
-regularity implies the affine
(l,0)
-regularities for any

2\leq l\leq k
; and the affine
(k,r)
-regularity implies the affine
(k,s)
-regularity for
any
s\geq r
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Suppose an embedding
f:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
is
affine
(l,r)
-regular for any
2\leq l\leq k
.
Then
f

induces a persistent geometric realization of
the
(k-1)
-skeleton of
{\rm Ind}(X,-+r)
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

Let
s\geq r
.
Then

f:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
is
affine
(l,s)
-regular for any
2\leq l\leq k
.
Let
\sigma\in{\rm sk}^{k-1}({\rm Ind}(X,s))
.
Then the number of vertices in
\sigma
is smaller than or equal to
k

and
d(u,v)>2s
for any distinct vertices
u
and
v
in
\sigma
.
Thus
f(\sigma)=\{f(v)\mid v\in\sigma\}
, i.e.
the images of the vertices in
\sigma
,
is affinely independent in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Hence
f\mid_{\sigma}
is a geometric realization of
\sigma
.
Therefore,
f
induces a geometric realization of
{\rm sk}^{k-1}({\rm Ind}(X,s))

in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Taking the persistence, we obtain a
persistent geometric realization of

{\rm sk}^{k-1}({\rm Ind}(X,-+r))
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
∎

Corollary 7.2
.

Suppose an embedding
f:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
is

(l,r)
-regular for any
2\leq l\leq k
.
Then
f

induces a persistent geometric realization of
the
(k-1)
-skeleton of
{\rm Ind}(X,-+r)
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

The linear independence of
l
-vectors implies the affine independence of these vectors.
Thus the
(l,r)
-regularity of a map

f:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
implies the affine
(l,r)
-regularity of
f
.
The proof follows from Proposition
7.1
.
∎

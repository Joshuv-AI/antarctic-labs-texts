Involution on the Graded Grothendieck Ring of Varieties and 𝔻 \mathbb{D} -Singularities
Andrew Burke

Abstract
. We realize a graded variant K 0 ​ ( Var k dim ) K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim}) of the Grothendieck ring of varieties as a quadratic extension of the subring K 0 ​ ( Var k sp ) K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr}) spanned by classes of smooth and proper varieties. As such, there exists a natural involution 𝔻 \mathbb{D} on K 0 ​ ( Var k dim ) K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim}) . We show that 𝔻 \mathbb{D} commutes with the symmetric power operations Sym m \Sym^{m} up to zero divisors. Moreover, we study varieties which are smooth up to cut-and-paste relations, which we call 𝔻 \mathbb{D} -singular varieties, and we give applications to compactifications of varieties and the irrationality of Kapranov zeta functions.



1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a field, assumed to be of characteristic zero. The Grothendieck ring of varieties
K_{0}(\Var_{k})
is the abelian group generated by classes
[X]
of varieties
X
over
k
, modulo the cut-and-paste relations

\displaystyle[X]=[Y]+[X-Y]

for each closed subvariety
Y
of
X
. It is a ring, with product
[X]\cdot[Y]=[X\times Y]
.

This ring was first mentioned by Grothendieck in a
1964
letter to Serre in relation to motives. It features prominently in the theory of motivic integration, introduced by Kontsevich in
1995
and developed by Denef and Loeser. Here, integrals are valued in a completed localization of
K_{0}(\Var_{k})
.

Despite its simple definition, the Grothendieck ring of varieties
K_{0}(\Var_{k})
remains largely mysterious. It is a complicated ring with many zero divisors; see
[
Poo02
]
,
[
Kol03
]
,
[
Bor18
]
,
[
HL18
]
. Nevertheless, these zero divisors seem to witness important geometric information; see
[
KS18
]
,
[
LS24
]
.

The Grothendieck ring was connected to birational geometry in
[
LL03
]
via an isomorphism
K_{0}(\Var_{k})/([\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}])\cong\mathbb{Z}[\SB_{k}]
, where
\SB_{k}
is the monoid of stable birational equivalence classes. This isomorphism is exploited in
[
NS19
]
to prove that stable rationality specializes in smooth families. A modified argument in
[
KT19
]
removes the adjective ‘stable.’ The two perspectives are unified in
[
NO21
]
by considering a Grothendieck ring of varieties
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
, graded by dimension, which can detect birational type.

In this paper, we study
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
as an object worthy of independent interest. Explicitly, it is the graded abelian group generated in degree
d
by classes
[X]_{d}
of varieties
X
of dimension at most
d
, modulo the cut-and-paste relations

\displaystyle[X]_{d}=[Y]_{d}+[X-Y]_{d},

for each closed subvariety
Y
of
X
. It is a graded ring, with
[X]_{d}\cdot[Y]_{e}=[X\times Y]_{d+e}
.

There is a graded subring
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr})
of
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
spanned by the classes
[X]_{n}
of smooth and proper varieties
X
of dimension
n
. Note that
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr})
is a proper subring, as, for instance, it does not contain the classes
[\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}k]_{1}
or
[\mathbb{A}^{1}]_{1}
. Nevertheless, these two classes are solutions of the integral quadratic equation

\displaystyle\big(\hskip 2.84544ptx^{2}-[\mathbb{P}^{1}]_{1}\cdot x+\big([\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}]_{2}-[\mathbb{P}^{2}]_{2}\big)\hskip 2.84544pt\big)\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr})[x].

Our main structural result says that
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
is a quadratic extension of
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an isomorphism of graded rings

\displaystyle K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})\cong\frac{K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr})\big[\tau,\mathbb{L}\big]}{\big(\hskip 2.84544pt\tau+\mathbb{L}-[\mathbb{P}^{1}]_{1},\hskip 5.69046pt\tau\cdot\mathbb{L}-\big([\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}]_{2}-[\mathbb{P}^{2}]_{2}\big)\hskip 2.84544pt\big)}.

We view
\tau
and
\mathbb{L}
as indeterminates of degree one on the right side; they map to
[\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}k]_{1}
and
[\mathbb{A}^{1}]_{1}
, respectively.

Put more simply, as an abelian group, we have

\displaystyle K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})\cong K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr})\oplus\tau\cdot K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr}).

Moreover, there exists an involution

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}:K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim}),

which fixes
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr})
pointwise and interchanges
\tau=[\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}k]_{1}
and
\mathbb{L}=[\mathbb{A}^{1}]_{1}
, as well as projections
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr})
according to the above direct sum decomposition.

Though our formalism only works in the graded setting, one finds reflections of it on
K_{0}(\Var_{k})
. For example, Bittner’s involution on
K_{0}(\Var_{k})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]
(see
[
Bit04
]
) is an ungraded manifestation of
\mathbb{D}
. Our setup is more flexible because
\mathbb{D}
fixes the classes of smooth and proper varieties, and it does not require the inversion of
\mathbb{L}
, thereby losing birational data. Our formalism also yields simple proofs of the main structural results of
[
LL03
]
and
[
Zak19
]
on
K_{0}(\Var_{k})
(see Subsection
2.4
).

For the remainder of the introduction, we discuss three main ways we use the involution
\mathbb{D}
and projections
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
defined via Theorem
1.1
.

1.1.
Symmetric Powers

The graded Grothendieck ring of varieties
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
is equipped with symmetric power operations

\displaystyle\Sym^{m}:K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim}),\hskip 14.22636pt[X]_{d}\mapsto[\Sym^{m}X]_{md},

for each
m\geq 0
, where
\Sym^{m}X=X^{m}/S_{m}
.

By Proposition
4.5
, the classes
\tau
and
\mathbb{L}
behave well with respect to
\Sym^{m}
:
\Sym^{m}(\tau\alpha)=\tau^{m}\Sym^{m}(\alpha)
and
\Sym^{m}(\mathbb{L}\alpha)=\mathbb{L}^{m}\Sym^{m}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
. Motivated by the fact that
\mathbb{D}
interchanges
\tau
and
\mathbb{L}
, we conjecture that symmetric powers commute with involution.

Conjecture 1.2
.

\Sym^{m}\circ\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}\circ\Sym^{m}
for each
m\geq 0

One reduces to checking whether
\mathbb{D}[\Sym^{m}X]=[\Sym^{m}X]
for smooth and proper varieties
X
. This is really a question about the singularities of symmetric powers
\Sym^{m}X
, of which we ask a more refined version in Conjecture
5.24
. It is clearly true for curves and holds for surfaces by the formula of
[
Göt01
]
.

We have the following partial answer to Conjecture
1.2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

The operations
\Sym^{m}
and
\mathbb{D}
commute, up to zero divisors. More precisely, the image of
\Sym^{m}\circ\mathbb{D}-\mathbb{D}\circ\Sym^{m}
lies in

\displaystyle\ker\bigg(K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})\big[(\tau\mathbb{L})^{-1},(\mathbb{L}^{n}-\tau^{n})^{-1}_{n\geq 1}\big]\bigg).

Our proof identifies the localization
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})\big[\mathbb{L}^{-1},(\mathbb{L}^{n}-\tau^{n})^{-1}_{n\geq 1}\big]
with a suitable graded Grothendieck ring of stacks
\Kstk
. Following arguments of Ekedahl
[
Eke09a
]
,
[
Eke09b
]
, we show the class of
\Sym^{m}X
is equal to the class of the stack quotient
[X^{m}/S_{m}]
in
\Kstk
. We conclude by showing the class of any smooth and proper Deligne-Mumford stack is fixed by an extension of the involution
\mathbb{D}
to
\Kstk[\tau^{-1}]
.

The generating function for the classes of symmetric powers of a variety
X
of dimension
n
is called the Kapranov zeta function of
X
:

\displaystyle Z_{X}^{\dim}(t)=\sum_{m\geq 0}[\Sym^{m}X]_{mn}\cdot t^{m}\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})[[t]].

If
k
is finite, then
Z_{X}^{\dim}(t)
specializes under the point counting measure to the zeta function of
X
; this is a rational function by a theorem of Dwork. It is natural to ask whether
Z_{X}^{\dim}(t)
is also rational. Dwork’s argument shows
Z_{X}^{\dim}(t)
is rational when
X
is a curve, but
[
LL04
]
shows when
X
is a surface that
Z_{X}^{\dim}(t)
is rational if and only if
\kappa(X)=-\infty
. We extend one implication to arbitrary dimension.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth and projective variety of dimension
>1
and non-negative Kodaira dimension
\kappa(X)\geq 0
. Then the graded Kapranov zeta function
Z_{X}^{\dim}(t)\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})[[t]]
of
X
is (pointwise) irrational.

In fact, the ungraded Kapranov zeta function
Z_{X}(t)\in K_{0}(\Var_{k})[[t]]
of
X
is also (pointwise) irrational, if we assume a positive answer to Conjecture
1.2
.

In fact, a recently posted preprint
[
She25
]
independently proves irrationality unconditionally in the ungraded setting.

Our proof of Theorem
1.3
suggests that symmetric power operations may be better behaved in
\Kstk
. It would be interesting to know whether the stacky Kapranov zeta function
Z_{X}^{\Stk}(t)\in\Kstk[\tau^{-1}][[t]]
is irrational. Our argument fails in this setting.

1.2.
Gluing Morphisms

One can define a relative graded Grothendieck group of varieties
K_{0}(\Var_{X}^{\dim})
over a base scheme
X
. Here, the classes are of varieties equipped with a morphism to
X
, which the cut-and-paste relations are required to respect. We use relative dimension to measure graded degree. Our structural results extend immediately to this setting. In fact, the involution
\mathbb{D}_{X}
is part of a six-functor formalism on
K_{0}(\Var_{X}^{\dim})
.

Suppose we have a closed subscheme
Y\subset X
, with complement
U=X-Y
. Then there exists a canonical direct sum decomposition

\displaystyle K_{0}(\Var_{X}^{\dim})=K_{0}(\Var_{U}^{\dim})\oplus K_{0}(\Var_{Y}^{\dim}).

The relationship between the involutions
\mathbb{D}_{X}
,
\mathbb{D}_{U}
, and
\mathbb{D}_{Y}
is more involved. It is captured by a map

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{U}^{Y}:K_{0}(\Var_{U}^{\spr})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{Y}^{\spr}),

which we call the gluing morphism of
Y
to
U
via
X
. More specifically,
\mathfrak{g}_{U}^{Y}=-i^{*}\pi_{2}j_{!}
is defined in terms of the projection
\pi_{2}
from earlier.

We explore the geometric data witnessed by gluing morphisms.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
U
be a smooth variety. Choose a smooth compactification
X
of
U
whose boundary
D=X-U
is an snc divisor
D=\cup_{i\in I}D_{i}
. Then the class

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\neq J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|-1}[D_{J}\times\mathbb{P}^{|J|-1}]\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr}).

depends only on
U
, where we denote
D_{J}=\cap_{j\in J}D_{j}
for
J\subset I
.

In particular, if
D
is smooth, then the birational type and Hodge numbers of
D
are independent of the choice of compactification.

This result is likely already known to experts and can be proved more directly by careful application of the weak factorization theorem. Nevertheless, our approach is more systematic and allows for generalizations, as in Section
3
.

Example 1.6
.

If
X=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}k[[t]]
with
U=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}k((t))
and
Y=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}k
, the gluing map

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{U}^{Y}:K_{0}(\Var_{k((t))}^{\spr})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr})

extends to recover the volume morphism
\Vol:K_{0}(\Var_{k((t))}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
constructed by hand in
[
NO21
]
. It is the key ingredient in their proof that birational type specializes in smooth families, and it is exploited in
[
NO22
]
and
[
Mo23
]
to establish new examples of irrational hypersurfaces.

1.3.
\mathbb{D}
-Singularities

We study the following condition, which captures what it means for a variety to be smooth from the perspective of the graded Grothendieck group. Its definition, like that of the gluing morphism, relies on Theorem
1.1
.

Definition 1.7
.

We say a variety
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities if the identity morphism
\mathbbm{1}_{X}=[X\xrightarrow{id}X]_{0}
of
K_{0}(\Var_{X}^{\dim})
lies in the subgroup
K_{0}(\Var_{X}^{\spr})
. Equivalently,
\pi_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{X})=0
.

This is a local condition. If
X
is proper and has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities, then
\mathbb{D}[X]=[X]
, an identity which automatically holds if
X
is also smooth.

Concretely, a variety
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities if it can be constructed by cutting into pieces and then gluing back together a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of smooth varieties that are proper over
X
and of the same dimension as
X
.

Our definition of
\mathbb{D}
-singularities refines the more ad hoc notions of
\mathbb{L}
-rational singularities
[
NS19
]
and B-rational singularities
[
KT19
]
found in the literature (see Examples
5.2
and
5.3
). Moreover, it can be computed effectively, provided we understand a log resolution of
X
. We present a few examples here to help with intuition. More can be found in Section
5
.

Example 1.8
.

A curve
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities if and only if it is unibranched at each point.

Example 1.9
.

Let
X
be a cone over a smooth and proper variety
Y
of dimension
n
. Then
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities if and only if

\displaystyle[Y]=[\mathbb{P}^{n}]\hskip 8.5359pt\text{in}\hskip 8.5359ptK_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim}).

For example, a cone over a Veronese embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
or over an odd-dimensional complex quadric has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities.

In each of these cases, if
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities, then it is a rational homology manifold, i.e. the rational homology of the link at each singular point is equal to that of a sphere. More generally, Proposition
5.13
shows that if
X
is a local complete intersection with isolated singularities and
\mathbb{D}
-singularities, then
X
is a rational homology manifold. Nevertheless, there exist
\mathbb{D}
-singularities which are not rational homology manifolds, as in Example
5.14
.

Adapting arguments of Esser-Scavia
[
ES25
]
and Bittner
[
Bit05
]
, we prove:

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group acting on a smooth quasi-projective variety
X
. Then the quotient variety
X/G
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities.

Not all quotient singularities are
\mathbb{D}
-singularities; this is related to the failure of Noether’s question. Conjecture
5.24
is a refined version of Conjecture
1.2
which asks, nevertheless, whether the symmetric powers
\Sym^{m}X
of a smooth variety
X
have
\mathbb{D}
-singularities. We prove in Theorem
5.23
that this is true, up to potential zero divisors. A recent preprint
[
She25
]
provides further evidence by proving
\Sym^{m}X
has
\mathbb{L}
-rational singularities.

2.
Graded Grothendieck groups

2.1.
Preliminaries

Let
S
be a base scheme.

Here and throughout, we require that our base schemes be noetherian and excellent of characteristic zero. We are primarily interested in the case when
S
is a quasi-projective variety or the spectrum of a discrete valuation ring over a field
k
of characteristic zero. We impose such conditions on
S
to access results on resolution of singularities, the weak factorization theorem, and a well-behaved notion of relative dimension.

Namely, let
f:X\rightarrow S
be a morphism of finite type and assume that
X
is irreducible. Then we set

\displaystyle\dim_{S}X=\tr\deg(k(X),k(V))-\codim(V,S),

where
V
is the closure of
f(X)
in
S
. In particular, if
S
is a variety over a field
k
, we have
\dim_{S}X=\dim X-\dim S
. We will use the following basic properties of relative dimension without further comment.

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)

If
U
is an open subscheme of
X
, then
\dim_{S}U=\dim_{S}X
.

(2)

If
Y\subset X
is a closed irreducible subscheme, then

\displaystyle\dim_{S}X=\dim_{S}Y+\codim(Y,X).

(3)

If
Y\rightarrow X
is a dominant morphism of irreducible schemes over
S
, then

\displaystyle\dim_{S}Y=\dim_{S}X+\tr\deg(k(Y),k(X)).

(4)

If
X\xrightarrow{f}S\rightarrow T
, then
\dim_{T}X=\dim_{S}X+\dim_{T}S
.

(5)

If
X\rightarrow S
is flat of relative dimension
d
, then
\dim_{S}X=d
.

Proof.

The first three properties hold by
[
Ful13
, Lemma
20.1
]
.

For
(4)
, denoting by
V
the closure of the image
f(X)
in
S
, we have
\dim_{S}X=\dim_{S}V+\tr\deg(k(X),k(V))
and
\dim_{T}X=\dim_{T}V+\tr\deg(k(X),k(V))
by
(3)
. We therefore reduce to the case where
X=V
is a closed subscheme of
S
. The result is then immediate from
(2)
.

Property
(5)
is clear.
∎

If
X
is possibly reducible, let
\dim_{S}X=\sup\dim_{S}X_{i}
be the maximum relative dimension, taken over the irreducible components
X_{i}
of
X
.

Definition 2.2
.

The graded Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
of
S
-varieties is the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded abelian group with:

•

generators in degree
d
the isomorphism classes
[X]_{d}
of
S
-schemes
X
of finite type, with
d\geq\dim_{S}X
, and

•

relations
[X]_{d}=[Y]_{d}+[X-Y]_{d}
for
X
an
S
-scheme of finite type with
d\geq\dim_{S}X
and
Y
a closed subscheme of
X
.

We will call a relation as above a cut-and-paste or gluing relation. We sometimes write
[X]_{d}
as
[X\xrightarrow{f}S]_{d}
to emphasize the morphism
f
.

Denote by
\mathbbm{1}_{S}=[S\xrightarrow{id}S]_{0}\in K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
the identity morphism of
S
. We will also write

\displaystyle\tau=[\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}k]_{1}\hskip 11.38092pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 11.38092pt\mathbb{L}=[\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}]_{1}\hskip 11.38092pt\mathrm{in}\hskip 5.69046ptK_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim}).

2.2.
Functoriality

Consider a morphism
\phi:S\rightarrow T
of finite type. There is a pushforward homomorphism

\displaystyle\phi_{!}:K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{T}^{\dim}),\hskip 14.22636pt[X\xrightarrow{f}S]_{d}\mapsto[X\xrightarrow{\phi\circ f}T]_{d+\dim_{T}S}

of graded degree
\dim_{T}S
.

We also have a pullback

\displaystyle\phi^{*}:K_{0}(\Var_{T}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim}),\hskip 14.22636pt[X\rightarrow T]_{d}\mapsto[X\times_{T}S\rightarrow S]_{d+\deg\phi^{*}},

defined if
\phi
is flat or a closed embedding. We set
\deg\phi^{*}=0
if
\phi
is flat and
\deg\phi^{*}=k
if
\phi
is a closed embedding of codimension
k
.

If
S
and
T
are of finite type over a field
k
, there is an exterior product

\displaystyle\boxtimes:K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})\times K_{0}(\Var_{T}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{S\times T}^{\dim})

defined by

\displaystyle\big([X\xrightarrow{f}S]_{d},[Y\xrightarrow{g}T]_{e}\big)\mapsto[X\times Y\xrightarrow{f\times g}S\times T]_{d+e}.

Thus,
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
naturally inherits the structure of a graded ring, and
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
is a graded
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
-module via

\displaystyle[Y]_{d}\cdot[X\rightarrow S]_{e}=[X\times Y\rightarrow S]_{d+e}.

Moreover, in this situation,
\phi_{!}
and
\phi^{*}
are both
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
-module homomorphisms.

Even if
S
is not of finite type over a field, we view
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
as a graded module over
\mathbb{Z}[\tau,\mathbb{L}]
, where
\tau
acts by
\tau\cdot[X]_{d}=[X]_{d+1}
and
\mathbb{L}
acts by
\mathbb{L}\cdot[X]_{d}=[X\times_{S}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}]_{d+1}
.

2.3.
Involution

We will construct an involution
\mathbb{D}
on
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
by carefully studying an alternative Bittner-style presentation.

Theorem 2.3
.

The graded Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
of
S
-varieties admits a presentation with:

•

generators in degree
d
the isomorphism classes
[X]_{d}
of regular, connected
S
-schemes
X
, proper and of finite type over
S
, with
d\geq\dim_{S}X
, and

•

relations

\displaystyle[\Bl_{Y}X]_{d}-[E]_{d}=[X]_{d}-[Y]_{d},

for
\Bl_{Y}X
the blow-up of a regular, connected
S
-scheme
X
, proper and of finite type over
S
, with
d\geq\dim_{S}X
along a regular and connected subscheme
Y
, and with
E
the exceptional divisor.

Proof.

Our argument closely mirrors the analogous ungraded argument in
[
Bit04
, Theorems
3.1
and
5.1
]
. We provide it for the convenience of the reader and for future reference.

Denote by
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\bit})
the graded abelian group with presentation as in the statement of the Theorem. The natural map
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\bit})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
factors as a composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\bit})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\reg})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}

where
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\reg})
is the graded Grothendieck group of regular
S
-schemes, defined exactly as in Definition
2.2
, except that all schemes are required to be regular.

Step 1:
We show
\Phi:K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\reg})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
is an isomorphism of graded abelian groups by constructing an inverse
\Phi^{-1}
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow S
be a morphism of finite type and let
d\geq\dim_{S}X
. If necessary, replace
X
with its reduction; this is harmless, as
[X]_{d}=[X_{\red}]_{d}
. Choose a finite stratification

\displaystyle X=\coprod_{i\in I}X_{i}

of
X
by regular subschemes such that each of their closures
\overline{X_{i}}
is a union of strata. This is possible, as
S
being a J
2
scheme implies that the regular locus of
X
is open. Then set
\Phi^{-1}([X]_{d})=\sum_{i\in I}[X_{i}]_{d}
.

We need to show that this expression is well-defined. If
X
is itself smooth, we have
[X]_{d}=\sum_{i\in I}[X_{i}]_{d}\in K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\reg})
, as follows by induction on the size of the index set
I
. More generally, consider two stratifications
\coprod_{i\in I}X_{i}
and
\coprod_{j\in J}X_{j}
of arbitrary
X
. We can find a common refinement
\coprod_{k\in K}X_{k}
. Then, by the above, we deduce
[X_{i}]_{d}=\sum_{X_{k}\subset X_{i}}[X_{k}]_{d}
for each
i\in I
. Taking the sum over
i\in I
, we obtain
\sum_{i\in I}[X_{i}]_{d}=\sum_{k\in K}[X_{k}]_{d}
. Combining this with the same argument involving the index set
J
, we deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}[X_{i}]_{d}=\sum_{k\in K}[X_{k}]_{d}=\sum_{j\in J}[X_{j}]_{d}.

If
Y\subset X
is a closed subscheme, we can find a stratification of
X
such that
Y
is a union of strata. This implies that
\Phi^{-1}([X]_{d})=\Phi^{-1}([X-Y]_{d})+\Phi^{-1}([Y]_{d})
, so
\Phi^{-1}
defines a group homomorphism
\Phi^{-1}:K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\reg})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
. It is inverse to
\Phi
by construction.

Step 2:
We show
\Psi:K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\bit})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\reg})
is an isomorphism of graded abelian groups by constructing an inverse
\Psi^{-1}
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow S
be a morphism of finite type, with
X
regular. By Nagata compactification, there exists an open embedding
j:X\rightarrow\overline{X}
and proper morphism
\overline{f}:\overline{X}\rightarrow S
such that
f=\overline{f}\circ j
. By resolution of singularities as in
[
Tem08
]
, we may assume
\overline{X}
is regular and
D=\overline{X}-X
is an snc divisor. Write
D=\cup_{i\in I}D_{i}
and set
D_{J}=\cap_{j\in J}D_{j}
for
J\subset I
. Then we define

\displaystyle\Psi^{-1}([X]_{d})=\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|}[D_{J}]_{d}

for
d\geq\dim_{S}X
.

We need to show that the expression for
\Psi^{-1}([X]_{d})
is independent of choices. Consider two such regular compactifications
X\rightarrow\overline{X}
and
X\rightarrow\overline{X}^{\prime}
. By the weak factorization theorem,
[
AT19
]
, Theorem
1.2.1
, they differ by a sequence of blow-up and blow-downs of regular subvarieties with normal crossings. Thus we reduce to the case of
\overline{X}^{\prime}=\Bl_{Z}\overline{X}
, where
Z
is a regular connected subscheme having normal crossings with
D
.

Write
D^{\prime}=\overline{X}^{\prime}-X
as
\cup_{i\in I\cup\{0\}}D_{i}^{\prime}
, where
D_{0}^{\prime}
is the exceptional divisor over
Z
and
D_{i}^{\prime}
is the strict transform of
D_{i}
for
i\in I
. In fact, for
J\subset I
, we see
D_{J}^{\prime}=\cap_{j\in J}D_{j}^{\prime}
is the blow-up of
D_{J}
along
Z_{J}=Z\cap D_{J}
; the exceptional divisor is
E_{J}=D_{J}^{\prime}\cap D_{0}^{\prime}
. We deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{J\subset I\cup\{0\}}(-1)^{|J|}[D_{J}^{\prime}]_{d}=

\displaystyle\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|}\big([D_{J}]_{d}-[Z_{J}]_{d}+[E_{J}]_{d}\big)+\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|+1}[E_{J}]_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|}[D_{J}]_{d}-\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|}[Z_{J}]_{d}.

Thus we need to show
\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|}[Z_{J}]_{d}=0
. As
Z
has normal crossings with
D
, certainly
Z\subset D_{i_{0}}
for some
i_{0}\in I
. But then
Z_{J}=Z_{J\cup\{i_{0}\}}
for each
J\subset I-\{i_{0}\}
. The vanishing follows by splitting the alternating sum into those terms containing
i_{0}
and those not containing
i_{0}
.

Let
Y\subset X
be a regular closed subscheme of
X
. We need to show

\displaystyle\Psi^{-1}([X]_{d})=\Psi^{-1}([X-Y]_{d})+\Psi^{-1}([Y]_{d}).

Choose an open embedding
X\rightarrow\overline{X}
where
\overline{X}
is regular and
D=\overline{X}-X
is an snc divisor such that the closure
\overline{Y}
of
Y
is smooth and has normal crossings with
D
. We can achieve this, for example, by picking a regular compactification of
X
with snc boundary, and then replacing it with an embedded resolution of the closure of
Y
, compatible with the boundary. Set
D=\cup_{i\in I}D_{i}
and denote
D_{J}=\cap_{j\in J}D_{j}
and
Y_{J}=Y\cap D_{J}
for
J\subset I
.

Consider the blow-up
\Bl_{\overline{Y}}\overline{X}
of
\overline{X}
along
\overline{Y}
. It is a regular compactification of
X-Y
with boundary
\cup_{i\in I\cup\{0\}}D_{i}^{\prime}
; here
D_{i}^{\prime}
is the strict transform of
D_{i}
for
i\in I
and
D_{0}
is the exceptional divisor. Viewing
D_{J}^{\prime}=\cap_{j\in J}D_{j}^{\prime}
as the blow-up of
D_{J}
with center
Y_{J}
, we compute

\displaystyle\Psi^{-1}([X-Y]_{d})=

\displaystyle\sum_{J\subset I\cup\{0\}}(-1)^{|J|}[D_{J}^{\prime}]_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|}\big([D_{J}]_{d}-[Y_{J}]_{d}+[E_{J}]_{d}\big)-\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|+1}[E_{J}]_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi^{-1}([X]_{d})-\Psi^{-1}([Y]_{d}).

Therefore
\Psi^{-1}
defines a group homomorphism
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\reg})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\bit})
. It is clearly inverse to
\Psi
.

Putting Steps
1
and
2
together, we conclude that the composition
\Phi\circ\Psi:K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\bit})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
is a graded group isomorphism.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Observe that the geometric inputs to this Bittner presentation are resolution of singularities, and the weak factorization theorem. We will later need a Bittner presentation for an appropriate Grothendieck group of Deligne-Mumford stacks. Essentially the same proof works in this context, as the geometric ingredients remain valid.

[cf. Theorem

2.4.
Graded Motivic Measures

It can be difficult to deduce geometric results by working directly with Grothendieck groups of varieties because they are so large. Rather, one probes them by motivic measures; by this we mean homomorphisms
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})\rightarrow A
. We might ask that the map preserve other structures on
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
, for example that it be graded, commute with mutliplication, with involution, or with
\lambda
-structures (see Proposition
4.5
). Let us introduce some relevant motivic measures.

Ungraded Grothendieck group:
The quotient
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})/(\tau-1)K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
recovers the usual ungraded Grothendieck group of varieties
K_{0}(\Var_{S})
. This is the abelian group generated by isomorphism classes
[X]
of
S
-schemes
X
of finite type, modulo the relations
[X]=[Y]+[X-Y]
for every closed subscheme
Y
of
X
.

Pre-composing with reduction modulo
\tau-1
, we see all motivic measures on
K_{0}(\Var_{S})
are also motivic measures on
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
. Over a finite field
k
, for example, there is the point count measure

\displaystyle K_{0}(\Var_{k})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z},\hskip 14.22636pt[X]\mapsto|X(k)|.

We also have the Hodge-Deligne polynomial
H:K_{0}(\Var_{\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[u,v]
, characterized by the relation

\displaystyle H([X])=H_{X}(u,v)=\sum_{p,q\geq 0}(-1)^{p+q}h^{p,q}(X)\cdot u^{p}v^{q}

for every smooth and projective complex variety
X
.

Birational equivalence classes:
Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero and let
\mathbb{Z}[\Bir_{k}]
(resp.
\mathbb{Z}[\SB_{k}]
) be the monoid ring of the monoid of (resp. stable) birational equivalence classes over
k
. There is a quotient map
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\Bir_{k}]
which sends
[X]_{d}
to the birational equivalence class of
X
if
d=\dim X
and to zero otherwise. It is easily seen to be surjective with kernel generated by
\tau
.

Let us now pause to give a conceptual proof of a theorem of Larsen-Lunts
[
LL03
]
.

Proposition 2.11
.

K_{0}(\Var_{k})/(\mathbb{L})\cong\mathbb{Z}[\SB_{k}]

The proof of Larsen-Lunts involves careful manipulation of the weak factorization theorem and resolution of singularities. Our argument hides such an analysis behind the existence of the involution
\mathbb{D}
(which still relies on the Bittner presentation).

Proof.

We have the following chain of isomorphisms:

\displaystyle K_{0}(\Var_{k})/(\mathbb{L})\cong

\displaystyle K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})/(\tau-1,\mathbb{L})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})/(\mathbb{L}-1,\tau)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[\Bir_{k}]/([\mathbb{P}^{1}]-[\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}k])

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[\SB_{k}],

where the second is deduced by pre-composing with the involution
\mathbb{D}
.
∎

The following simple Lemma also has important connections to the literature.

Lemma 2.12
.

Suppose
\alpha\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
satisfies
\tau\alpha=0
. Then
\alpha=\mathbb{L}\beta
for a unique class
\beta\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr})
.

Proof.

We may uniquely write
\alpha=\gamma+\mathbb{L}\beta
, where
\beta,\gamma\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\spr})
. Then

\displaystyle 0=\pi_{1}(\tau\alpha)=\pi_{1}(\tau\mathbb{L}\beta+\tau\gamma)=\gamma.

∎

Remark 2.13
.

In

Remark 2.14
.

We also recover
[
Zak19
, Theorems
C
and

]
. Indeed, suppose
0\neq\alpha\in K_{0}(\Var_{k})
satisfies
\mathbb{L}\alpha=0
. We may lift
\alpha
to an element
\tilde{\alpha}\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
with
\mathbb{L}\tilde{\alpha}=0
. But then Lemma
2.12
says we may write
\tilde{\alpha}=\tau([X]_{n}-[Y]_{n})
, with

\displaystyle 0\neq\mathbb{D}(\tilde{\alpha})=[X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}]_{n+1}-[Y\times\mathbb{A}^{1}]_{n+1}

Mixed Hodge modules:
Let
S
be a complex variety. The Hodge-Deligne polynomial generalizes to a map

\displaystyle\chi_{S}^{c}:K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\MHM(S)),\hskip 14.22636pt\chi^{c}_{S}([X\xrightarrow{f}S]_{d})=[f_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}],

called the graded motivic Hodge-Grothendieck characteristic. Here,
K_{0}(\MHM(S))
is the Grothendieck group of the abelian category of mixed Hodge modules on
S
, and
\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}=(a_{X})^{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}
is the constant Hodge module on

. To see that
\chi_{S}^{c}
respects cut-and-paste relations, one utilizes the exact triangle
j_{!}j^{*}\rightarrow\id\rightarrow i_{*}i^{*}\xrightarrow{+1}
for a closed embedding

.

In fact, the derived category, and hence also the Grothendieck group, of mixed Hodge modules admits a six functor formalism. It is clear that if
\phi:S\rightarrow T
is a morphism of varieties, then we have the commutativity relations
\chi^{c}_{T}\phi_{!}=\phi_{!}\chi^{c}_{S}
and
\chi^{c}_{S}\phi^{*}=\phi^{*}\chi^{c}_{T}
. The respective dualities also commute up to a Tate twist:
\chi_{S}^{c}\circ\mathbb{D}=(\mathbb{D}\circ\chi^{c}_{S})(d+\dim S)
on degree

K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
.

2.5.
Spreading Out

Let
S
be a base scheme and let
\eta\in S
be a point with local ring
A=\mathscr{O}_{\eta,S}

Consider the inverse system
\big(\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A_{j}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A_{i}\big)_{j\geq i\in I}
of open affine subschemes
\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A_{i}
of
S
containing the point
\eta
. The inverse limit of this system is
\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A
. Alternatively,
A
is the direct limit of the corresponding direct system
\big(A_{i}\rightarrow A_{j}\big)_{i\leq j\in I}
of noetherian rings.

Taking the relative graded Grothendieck groups of the system of open immersions, we obtain a direct system
\big(K_{0}(\Var_{A_{i}}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{A_{j}}^{\dim})\big)_{i\leq j\in I}
, each map of graded degree zero.

Theorem 2.15
.

The natural graded ring homomorphism

\displaystyle\phi:\varinjlim_{i\in I}K_{0}(\Var_{A_{i}}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{A}^{\dim})

is an isomorphism. Moreover, it restricts to a map
\varinjlim_{i\in I}K_{0}(\Var_{A_{i}}^{\spr})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{A}^{\spr})
, which is an isomorphism.

See
[
NS11
, Section
3.4
]
for an ungraded spreading out principle in the context of an arbitrary direct system of rings. In the graded context, one needs to be more careful keeping track of dimensions.

Proof.

If
X_{i}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A_{i}
is a finite type morphism with
\dim_{A_{i}}X_{i}=d
, then the pullback
X_{i}\times_{A_{i}}A\rightarrow A
also satisfies
\dim_{A}(X_{i}\times_{A_{i}}A)=d
. It follows that the pullback
K_{0}(\Var_{A_{i}}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{A}^{\dim})
and hence also the direct limit
\phi
are of graded degree zero. Note that this is false if we had used the naive notion of relative dimension
\dim X_{i}-\dim A_{i}
instead.

We now argue as in
[
NS11
]
. Surjectivity of
\phi
follows from the existence, for any
X\rightarrow A
, of an
A_{i}
-model
X_{i}\rightarrow A_{i}
for some
i\in I
by
[
EGA60
, IV
.8.8.2
]
.

For injectivity, we use the results that if
X_{i}
and
Y_{i}
are finite type schemes over
A_{i}
, then the natural map

\displaystyle\varinjlim_{j\geq i}\Hom_{A_{j}}(Y_{i}\times_{A_{i}}A_{j},X_{i}\times_{A_{i}}A_{j})\rightarrow\Hom_{A}(Y_{i}\times_{A_{i}}A,X_{i}\times_{A_{i}}A)

is a bijection. Moreover, given
f_{i}:Y_{i}\rightarrow X_{i}
, then the induced morphism
f:Y_{i}\times_{A_{i}}A\rightarrow X_{i}\times_{A_{i}}A
is a closed (resp. open) immersion if and only if there exists some
j\geq i
for which the induced morphism
f_{j}:Y_{i}\times_{A_{i}}A_{j}\rightarrow X_{i}\times_{A_{i}}A_{j}
is closed (resp. open immersion), by
[
EGA60
, IV
.8.8.2
and IV
.8.10.5
]
.

For the final statement, we need simply note that the map
K_{0}(\Var_{A_{i}}^{\dim})\rightarrow K_{0}(\Var_{A}^{\dim})
sends
K_{0}(\Var_{A_{i}}^{\spr})
to
K_{0}(\Var_{A}^{\spr})
.
∎

2.6.
Stacks

Let
S
be a base scheme. We introduce a graded Grothendieck group
\KstkS
of algebraic stacks over
S
, as studied by Ekedahl
[
Eke09a
]
in the ungraded situation. The Grothendieck group
\KstkS
will be a localization of
K_{0}(\Var_{S}^{\dim})
.

All stacks in this section are assumed to be of finite type over
S
, with affine automorphism group schemes.

Let
\mathscr{X}\rightarrow S
be a stack as above. Then
\mathscr{X}

Lemma 2.17

Let

It is straightforward to verify that there is a split short exact sequence of graded abelian groups
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(\Var_{U}^{\dim})}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(\Var_{Y}^{\dim}).}

\displaystyle K_{0}(\Var_{X}^{\spr})=\big\{\hskip 2.84544ptj_{!}(\alpha)+i_{!}(\mathfrak{g}_{U}^{Y}(\alpha)+\beta)\hskip 4.26773pt|\hskip 4.26773pt\alpha\in K_{0}(\Var_{U}^{\spr}),\hskip 2.84544pt\beta\in K_{0}(\Var_{Y}^{\spr})\hskip 2.84544pt\big\}.

3.2.

In this subsection, we parse the geometric data captured by gluing morphisms

We start with the identity
\mathbbm{1}_{U}
of a smooth variety

Proposition 3.4

This follows immediately from the following decomposition of

\displaystyle[U\rightarrow X]=

\displaystyle\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|}\tau^{|J|}[\tilde{D}_{J}]

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|-1}\tau\mathbb{L}\cdot[\tilde{D}_{J}\times\mathbb{P}^{|J|-2}]\bigg)

\displaystyle-\tau\cdot\bigg(\sum_{\emptyset\neq J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|-1}[\tilde{D}_{J}\times\mathbb{P}^{|J|-1}]\bigg).

)

4.1.
Basic Theory

We first provide a rapid introduction to
\lambda
-rings. For more information, see
[
AT69
]
or
[

]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A
\lambda
-structure on a ring
A
is a sequence of maps
\lambda^{m}:A\rightarrow A
for
m\geq 0
such that, for all
x,y\in A
and
m\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{0}(x)=1,\hskip 14.22636pt\lambda^{1}(x)=x,\hskip 14.22636pt\lambda^{m}(x+y)=\sum_{i+j=m}\lambda^{i}(x)\cdot\lambda^{j}(y).

A
\lambda
-ring is a ring equipped with a
\lambda
-structure. A
\lambda
-ring
A
is graded if
A
is a graded ring and
\lambda^{m}(A_{d})\subset A_{md}
for all
m
and
d
. A (graded)
\lambda
-homomorphism between (graded)
\lambda
-rings is a (graded) ring homomorphism which commutes with the
\lambda
-operations on the source and target.

Example 4.2
.

The integers
\mathbb{Z}
admit a
\lambda
-structure
\lambda^{i}(n)=\binom{n+i-1}{i}
.

Example 4.3
.

Consider, more generally, the Grothendieck group
K(X)
of vector bundles on a variety
X
. This is the abelian group generated by isomorphism classes of vector bundles on
X
, modulo the relations
[E]=[F]+[G]
for every short exact sequence
0\rightarrow F\rightarrow E\rightarrow G\rightarrow 0
. It admits a
\lambda
-structure
\lambda^{i}([E])=[\Sym^{i}E]
.

We will use a variant of this construction.

Example 4.4
.

For a variety
X
, consider the abelian group
\overline{K}(X)
generated by isomorphism classes of vector bundles on
X
, modulo the relations
[E]=[F]+[G]
when

and

are isomorphic vector bundles. It admits a
\lambda
-structure
\lambda^{i}([E])=[\Sym^{i}E]
. Unlike

, it also admits a global sections map

\displaystyle h^{0}:\overline{K}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z},\hskip 14.22636pt[E]\mapsto\dim H^{0}(X,E)

Proposition 4.5
.

The graded Grothendieck ring of varieties

admits a graded
\lambda
-ring structure defined by

\displaystyle\lambda^{m}([X]_{d})=[\Sym^{m}X]_{md}

for quasi-projective varieties

and integers

. Moreover,

\displaystyle\lambda^{m}(\tau\alpha)=\tau^{m}\lambda^{m}(\alpha),\hskip 14.22636pt\lambda^{m}(\mathbb{L}\alpha)=\mathbb{L}^{m}\lambda^{m}(\alpha)

for all
\alpha\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})

Proof.

Let
Y\subset X
be a closed subscheme with complement

. Stratifying the symmetric power variety

according to how many of its points lie in

and

respectively, we see

\displaystyle[\Sym^{m}X]_{md}=\sum_{i+j=m}[\Sym^{i}U]_{id}\cdot[\Sym^{j}Y]_{jd}.

This identity shows both that
\lambda^{m}
is well-defined and that it is a
\lambda
-structure on
K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
.

The identity
\lambda^{m}(\tau\alpha)=\tau^{m}\lambda(\alpha)
is clear, and
\lambda^{m}(\mathbb{L}\alpha)=\mathbb{L}^{m}\lambda(\alpha)
follows from an argument of Totaro, as in
[
Göt01

4.4
]
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

If

isn’t quasi-projective, then the quotient

need not be defined as a scheme. It is, however, an algebraic space. As any algebraic space is generically a scheme, the naive Grothendieck ring

of algebraic spaces can be naturally identified with

as equal to
[\Sym^{m}X]_{md}

Let
A
be a
\lambda
-ring. An ideal
I
of

is called a
\lambda
-ideal if it is preserved by the
\lambda
-operations, in which case
A/I
is naturally a
\lambda
-ring, and the quotient map

a
\lambda
-homomorphism.

For example

\lambda

is given by taking symmetric powers of birational types.

There exist Adams operations

for each

. These are natural operations which are defined by universal polynomials in the
\lambda
-operations

.

We can package together some of the axioms of a

-structure by saying that

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}:A\rightarrow(1+tA[[t]])^{\times},\hskip 14.22636pta\mapsto\sum_{m\geq 0}\lambda^{m}(a)\cdot t^{m}.

In fact,

admits a natural
\lambda
-ring structure. Here, addition is given by multiplication of power series. Multiplication

and the
\lambda
-operations are uniquely characterized by the identities

\displaystyle\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+a_{i}t)\bigg)\circ\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+b_{j}t)\bigg)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+a_{i}b_{j}t),

and

\displaystyle\lambda^{m}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+a_{i}t)\bigg)=\prod_{J\subset\{1\ldots n\},\|J\|=m}(1+t\prod_{j\in J}a_{j}).

Nevertheless,

is not a

-homomorphism unless

is a so-called special
\lambda
-ring. For example,

is not special.

A=K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})
,
r_{1}=\mathbb{L}
,

, and
S
the multiplicative set generated by
x_{1}
and
x_{1}^{m}-x_{2}^{m}
for all

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
A
be a
\lambda
-ring such that
\lambda_{t}(1)=(1-t)^{-1}
and let
r_{1},\ldots r_{n}\in A
be such that
\lambda_{t}(r_{i}x)=\lambda_{r_{i}t}(x)
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and all
x\in A
. If
S
is a set of integer polynomials in
n
variables closed under multiplication and substitution
(x_{1},\ldots x_{n})\mapsto(x_{1}^{m},\ldots x_{n}^{m})
for all
m\geq 1
and
S^{\prime}=\{f(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})|f\in S\}
, then there is a unique
\lambda
-structure on the localization
A^{\prime}=(S^{\prime})^{-1}A
for which
A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
is a
\lambda
-ring homomorphism and for which
\lambda_{t}(r_{i}x)=\lambda_{r_{i}t}(x)
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and all
x\in A^{\prime}
.

Moreover, if
A
is a graded
\lambda
-ring and each
r_{i}
and each
f(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})
is homogeneous, then
A^{\prime}
is a graded
\lambda
-ring.

For ease of language, we will hereafter say that
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
are
\lambda
-linear in
A
if
\lambda_{t}(r_{i}x)=\lambda_{r_{i}t}(x)
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and all
x\in A
.

Proof.

Special care is required to keep track of notation in the ring
(1+tA[[t]])^{\times}
, which we will make use of throughout the proof. The symbol
\circ
denotes multiplication in the ring, the usual multiplicative symbols
\cdot
,
\bullet^{-1}
, and
\prod
refer to addition in the ring (multiplication of power series), and the usual addition symbols
+
and
\Sigma
refer to addition of power series.

We compute, for
x\in A
and
i=1,\ldots,n
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}(r_{i}x)=\lambda_{r_{i}t}(x)=(1+r_{i}t)\circ\lambda_{t}(x)=(\lambda_{-t}(r_{i})^{-1})\circ\lambda_{t}(x)=\sigma_{t}(r_{i})\circ\lambda_{t}(x),

where we denote
\sigma_{t}(r_{i})=\lambda_{-t}(r_{i})^{-1}
. In fact, if
R
is the subring of
A
generated by
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
, we can immediately show that the map

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}:R\rightarrow(1+tA[[t]])^{\times},\hskip 14.22636ptr\mapsto\lambda_{-t}(r)^{-1}

is a ring homomorphism which makes

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}:A\rightarrow(1+tA[[t]])^{\times}

into a
R
-module homomorphism. To prove the lemma, we need to show that
\lambda_{t}
extends uniquely to a
(S^{\prime})^{-1}R
-module homomorphism

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}:A^{\prime}\rightarrow(1+tA^{\prime}[[t]])^{\times}.

For this, it suffices to show that the image of
\sigma_{t}(f(r_{1},\ldots r_{n}))
in
(1+tA^{\prime}[[t]])^{\times}
is invertible for all
f\in S
..

Write
f(x)=\sum_{I}a_{I}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{i_{n}}
. We construct an inverse
\phi(t)=1+\sum_{n>0}b_{n}t^{n}
to
\sigma_{t}(f(r_{1},\ldots r_{n}))
by determining the coefficients
b_{n}
inductively. For this, let’s compute

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}(f(r_{1},\ldots r_{n}))\circ\phi(t)=

\displaystyle\prod_{I}\sigma_{t}(r_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots r_{n}^{i_{n}})^{a_{I}}\circ\phi(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{I}\phi(r_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots r_{n}^{i_{n}}t)^{a_{I}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{I}(1+\sum_{n>0}b_{n}r_{1}^{ni_{1}}\ldots r_{n}^{ni_{n}}t^{n})^{a_{I}}.

For the second equality, we used repeatedly the identity

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}(r_{i})\circ\phi(t)=(1+r_{i}t)\circ\phi(t)=\phi(r_{i}t).

The coefficient for
t^{n}
in this expression is a sum, consisting of
\sum_{I}a_{I}b_{n}r_{1}^{ni_{1}}\ldots r_{n}^{ni_{n}}=b_{n}f(r_{1}^{n},\ldots r_{n}^{n})
and other terms, each of which contains
b_{i}
for some
i<n
. Assuming
b_{i}
for
i<n
have already been chosen uniquely, we may solve uniquely for
b_{n}
inside of
A^{\prime}
so as to force this coefficient to vanish.

The final assertion is clear from our proof if we keep track of graded degree.
∎

5.
\mathbb{D}
-Singularities

5.1.
Definition and Properties

Let
X
be a base scheme.

Definition 5.1
.

We say
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities if the identity morphism
\mathbbm{1}_{X}=[X\xrightarrow{id}X]
of
K_{0}(\Var_{X}^{\dim})
lies in the submodule
K_{0}(\Var_{X}^{\spr})
.

Equivalently,
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities if
\pi_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{X})=0
in
K_{0}(\Var_{X}^{\spr})
.

More explicitly,
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities if and only if it can be built by cutting into pieces a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of regular schemes, proper over
X
of relative dimension zero, and then gluing back together.

Our notion of
\mathbb{D}
-singularities refines ad hoc definitions in the literature, when
X
is a variety over
k
.

Example 5.2
.

Recall that
X
is said to have
\mathbb{L}
-rational singularities
[
NS19
]
if there exists a resolution of singularities
f:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
such that, for every point
x\in X
, we have
[f^{-1}(x)]\equiv 1_{k(x)}
mod
\mathbb{L}
in
K_{0}(\Var_{k(x)})
.

Suppose
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities. Then given a resolution
f:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
, we have

\displaystyle[X\rightarrow X]=[\tilde{X}\rightarrow X]+\tau\mathbb{L}\cdot\alpha

for some
\alpha\in K_{0}(\Var_{X}^{\spr})
. Pulling back along any point
x:\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}k(x)\rightarrow X
, we obtain
[f^{-1}(x)]\equiv 1_{k(x)}
modulo
\mathbb{L}
in
K_{0}(\Var_{k(x)})
. Hence
X
has
\mathbb{L}
-rational singularities.

Example 5.3
.

The authors of
[
KT19
]
associate an invariant
\partial_{Z}(X)
to each closed subvariety
Z
of
X
containing the singular locus of
X
. Using our terminology, it is immediate to check that one can identify
\partial_{Z}(X)
with

\displaystyle-i^{!}(\pi_{2}[U\rightarrow X])\in K_{0}(\Var_{Z}^{\dim})/(\tau),

where
U=X-Z
and
i:Z\rightarrow X
. Assume that
Z
is an irreducible divisor. Then, in this terminology, the pair
(X,Z)
is said to have B-rational singularities if
[Z]=-i^{!}(\pi_{2}[U\rightarrow X])\in K_{0}(\Var_{Z}^{\dim})/(\tau)
.

If
Z
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities, then one checks
[Z\rightarrow X]=-\pi_{2}[U\rightarrow X]\in K_{0}(\Var_{X}^{\dim})
, so
(X,Z)
indeed has B-rational singularities.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
X
be a
k
-scheme. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities,

(2)

there exists an open cover
\cup_{i\in I}U_{i}
of
X
such that
U_{i}
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities for each
i\in I
,

(3)

\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathscr{O}_{x,X}
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities for each closed point
x\in X
.

Proof.

By spreading out, Theorem
2.15
, we see that
\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathscr{O}_{x,X}
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities if and only if
U
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities for some (affine) open subset
U
containing
s
. Thus properties
(2)
and
(3)
are equivalent. Clearly
(1)
implies
(2)
. For the implication
(2)
implies
(1)
, we use inclusion-exclusion:

\displaystyle\pi_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{X})=\sum_{\emptyset\neq J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|-1}(j_{U_{J}})_{!}j_{U_{J}}^{*}\pi_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{X}).

Since
\pi_{2}
commutes with pullback along a smooth morphism, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{X})=\sum_{\emptyset\neq J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|-1}(j_{U_{J}})_{!}\pi_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{U_{J}}).

Each term on the right side is zero, hence so is
\pi_{2}(\mathbbm{1}_{X})
.
∎

Let’s look at some example to get a better sense of the meaning of
\mathbb{D}
-singularities for complex varieties
X
.

Example 5.5
.

If
X
is smooth, it has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities.

Example 5.6
.

If
C
is a nodal curve with a node at
p\in C
, then

\displaystyle[C]=[\tilde{C}]-\tau\cdot[p],

where
\tilde{C}\rightarrow C
is the normalization. Thus
\pi_{2}[C]=-[p]\neq 0
, so
C
does not have
\mathbb{D}
-singularities.

On the other hand, if
C
has a cusp at
p\in C
, then
[C]=[\tilde{C}]
, so
C
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities.

More generally, a curve
C
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities if and only if it is unibranched at each point.

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose a variety
X
of dimension
n+1
has isolated singularities, supported at a point
p\in X
. Choose a log resolution
f:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
, so
f^{-1}(p)=\cup_{i\in I}d_{i}D_{i}
is a snc divisor. Then
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\neq J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|-1}[D_{J}\times\mathbb{P}^{|J|-1}]=[\mathbb{P}^{n}]\in K_{0}(\Var_{\mathbb{C}}^{\dim}).

Proof.

We compute, by inclusion-exclusion,

\displaystyle[X]=\sum_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|}\tau^{|J|}[D_{J}]+\tau^{n+1},

hence

\displaystyle-\pi_{2}[X]=\sum_{\emptyset\neq J\subset I}(-1)^{|J|-1}[D_{J}\times\mathbb{P}^{|J|-1}]-[\mathbb{P}^{n}].

∎

Example 5.8
.

Let
X
be a cone over a smooth proper

Example 5.9

Example 5.10
.

Let

be a smooth hypersurface of degree

. Embed
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
in

as a hyperplance and consider the blow-up

The class

of the strict transform of a line in

\displaystyle K_{Y}\cdot\big(\ell-de\big)=\big(-(n+1)H+E\big)\cdot\big(\ell-de\big)=-(n+1)+d<0.

\displaystyle[X]=

\displaystyle[Y]-\tau\cdot[\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\rightarrow p]+\tau^{n}[p\rightarrow p]

\displaystyle[Y]-\tau\mathbb{L}\cdot[\mathbb{P}^{n-2}\rightarrow p].

Example 5.11
.

One can show directly that:

(2)

-singularities if and only if

In this example,

-singularities if and only if

shows that

\displaystyle\frac{b_{1}}{a_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{b_{n}}{a_{n}},\hskip 11.38092pt\mathrm{where}\hskip 5.69046ptb_{i}\in\{1,2,\ldots,a_{i}-1\}

Definition 5.12
.

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety of dimension
n
. Say

is a rational homology manifold if for any point

,

\displaystyle H^{i}_{\{x\}}(X,\mathbb{Q})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q}&\text{if }i=2n\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

A rational homology manifold should be thought of as being smooth for the purposes of rational (co)homology theories. For example, rational homology manifolds satisfy Poincaré duality.

There is a relationship between
\mathbb{D}
-singularities and rational homology manifolds more generally.

Proposition 5.13
.

Suppose
X
is a local complete intersection with isolated singularities. If
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities, then
X
is a rational homology manifold.

Proof.

We may assume
X
has dimension
n>1
by Example
5.6
. Let
i_{x}:\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathbb{C}\rightarrow X
be a singular point. Using the notation of Subsection
2.4
, we compute

\displaystyle[i_{x}^{!}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}]

\displaystyle=i_{x}^{!}\chi\mathbbm{1}_{X}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}i_{x}^{*}\mathbb{D}\chi\mathbbm{1}_{X}

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{D}\chi i_{x}^{*}\mathbb{D}\mathbbm{1}_{X})(-n)

\displaystyle=[\mathbb{Q}^{H}(-n)]

inside
K_{0}(\MHS(\mathbb{C}))
, where the last step uses our assumption that
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities.

By
[

]
,
H^{i}(i_{x}^{!}\mathbb{Q}_{X})=H^{i}_{\{x\}}(X,\mathbb{Q})=0
unless
i=n,n+1
, or
i=2n
, as

is a local complete intersection. Combining this with the above, we deduce

\displaystyle[H^{n}_{\{x\}}(X,\mathbb{Q})]=[H^{n+1}_{\{x\}}(X,\mathbb{Q})]\in K_{0}(\MHS(\mathbb{C})).

But
H^{n}_{\{x\}}(X,\mathbb{Q})
has weights
\leq(n-1)
and
H^{n+1}_{\{x\}}(X,\mathbb{Q})
has weights
\geq(n+1)
by

]
. We conclude that

, and hence

is a rational homology manifold.
∎

Nevertheless, there is no direct implication between

]
for details. See also the following example.

Example 5.14
.

Let

be a surface with an isolated cuspidal singularity, i.e. such that the exceptional divisor of the minimal resolution is a cycle of rational curves. Let

5.2.
Toric Orbifolds

The following Proposition is crucial in the proof of the more general Thereom
5.17
.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
X
be a simplicial toric variety (or, equivalently, a toric variety with finite abelian quotient singularities). Then
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities.

This result is essentially proved by Bittner
[
Bit05
, Corollary
4.2
]
, using different language. For the convenience of the reader, we will sketch the proof. It relies on the following combinatorial lemma, which is a consequence of the Dehn-Somerville equations.

Lemma 5.16
.

[cf.
[
Bit05
, Lemma
4.6
]
]
Let
\Sigma
be a simplicial refinement of the cone
\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
spanned by the standard basis vectors. For each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, let
\phi(\sigma)\in\Delta
be the smallest cone in
\Delta
containing
\sigma
. Then, denoting by
|\sigma|
the dimension of the simplex
\sigma
, the degree
n
polynomial

\displaystyle p_{\Delta}(s,t)=\sum_{\tau\in\Delta}\sum_{\phi(\sigma)=\tau}(-1)^{|\tau|}(s-t)^{|\tau|-|\sigma|}t^{n-|\tau|+|\sigma|},

is symmetric in
s
and
t
.

If
Y
is a toric variety defined by a fan
\Sigma
of dimension
n
and if
\sigma\in\Sigma
, denote by
\mathscr{O}_{\sigma}
the open orbit corresponding to
\sigma
and by
V_{\sigma}
its closure. From the identity
V_{\sigma}=\coprod_{\sigma\subset\sigma^{\prime}}\mathscr{O}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
, we deduce, by inclusion-exclusion,

\displaystyle[\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}]=\sum_{\sigma\subset\sigma^{\prime}}(-\tau)^{|\sigma^{\prime}|-|\sigma|}[V_{\sigma^{\prime}}].

Proof of Proposition
5.15
.

Take a toric resolution
Y\rightarrow X
of
X
. Denote by
\Sigma
and
\Delta
the fans defining
Y
and
X
respectively. For each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, denote by
\phi(\sigma)\in\Delta
the smallest cone in
\Delta
containing
\sigma
. Each
\sigma\in\Sigma
determines a
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{|\phi(\sigma)|-|\sigma|}
-bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\phi(\sigma)}
. We thus compute

\displaystyle[Y]=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}[\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}]\tau^{|\sigma|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in\Delta}\sum_{\phi(\sigma)=\tau}(\mathbb{L}-\tau)^{|\tau|-|\sigma|}[\mathcal{O}_{\phi(\sigma)}]\tau^{|\sigma|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in\Delta}\bigg(\sum_{\tau\subset\tau^{\prime}}\sum_{\phi(\sigma)=\tau}(-1)^{|\tau|}(\mathbb{L}-\tau)^{|\tau|-|\sigma|}\tau^{|\tau^{\prime}|-|\tau|+|\sigma|}\bigg)(-1)^{|\tau^{\prime}|}[V_{\tau^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in\Delta,|\tau^{\prime}|\geq 1}\bigg(\sum_{\tau\subset\tau^{\prime}}\sum_{\phi(\sigma)=\tau}(-1)^{|\tau|}(\mathbb{L}-\tau)^{|\tau|-|\sigma|}\tau^{|\tau^{\prime}|-|\tau|+|\sigma|}\bigg)(-1)^{|\tau^{\prime}|}[V_{\tau^{\prime}}]+[X]

This last line is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in\Delta,|\tau^{\prime}|\geq 1}(-1)^{|\tau^{\prime}|}p_{\tau^{\prime}}(\mathbb{L},\tau)[V_{\tau^{\prime}}]+[X].

by Lemma
5.16
.

But
V_{\tau^{\prime}}
is a simplicial toric variety of dimension less than
n
, so we can assume by induction on
n
that it has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities. Combined with Lemma
5.16
, which says that
p_{\tau^{\prime}}(\mathbb{L},\tau)
is symmetric in
\tau
and
\mathbb{L}
, we see that

\displaystyle[X]=[Y]-\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in\Delta,|\tau^{\prime}|\geq 1}(-1)^{|\tau^{\prime}|}p_{\tau^{\prime}}(\mathbb{L},\tau)[V_{\tau^{\prime}}]

is a sum of terms, all of which lie in
K_{0}(\Var_{X}^{\spr})
. Hence
X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities.
∎

5.3.
Abelian Quotient Singularities

Theorem 5.17
.

[cf. Theorem
1.10
]
Let
G
be a finite abelian group acting on a smooth quasi-projective variety
X
over a field
k
of characteristic zero. Then the quotient variety
X/G
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities.

For the proof, we will require some setup.

Lemma 5.18
.

Let
V
be a vector field of dimension
n
over a field
k
of characteristic zero and let
G
be a finite abelian group acting linearly on
V
. Then

\displaystyle[\mathbb{P}(V)/G]=[\mathbb{P}^{n-1}]\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim}).

Proof.

Diagonalize the action of
G
on
V
. This provides a decomposition of
V-\{0\}
as a disjoint union of
G
-equivariant standard coordinate tori
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell+1}
. Taking the quotient by the scalar action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, we obtain a
G
-equivariant decomposition of
\mathbb{P}V
into tori
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell}
, with
G
acting diagonally. Each of the quotients of the tori
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell}
by the
G
-action remain isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell}
. Therefore
[\mathbb{P}(V)/G]=[\mathbb{P}V]
.
∎

Lemma 5.19
.

Let
Z
be a variety over a field
k
of characteristic zero and let
\alpha\in K_{0}(\Var_{Z}^{\dim})
. Then
\alpha=0
if
i_{p}^{*}\alpha=0
in
K_{0}(\Var_{k(p)}^{\dim})
for every (possibly non-closed) point
i_{p}:\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}k(p)\rightarrow Z
.

Proof.

We induct on the dimension of
Z
. First reduce to the case where
Z
is irreducible by considering the components separately. Now let
\eta\in Z
be the generic point. Then
i_{\eta}^{*}\alpha=0
implies by spreading out, Theorem
2.15
, that
j_{U}^{*}\alpha=0
for some open subset
U
of
Z
. If
W\subset Z
is the complement of
U
, then
\alpha=(i_{W})_{!}\beta
for some
\beta\in K_{0}(\Var_{W}^{\dim})
, which vanishes when pulled back to any point. By the inductive hypothesis,
\beta=0
, and hence we conclude that
\alpha=0
.
∎

Proposition 5.20
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group acting on a quasi-projective variety
X
over a field
k
of characteristic zero, and let
E
be a
G
-equivariant vector bundle of rank
n
over
X
. Then

\displaystyle[\mathbb{P}(E)/G]=[\mathbb{P}^{n-1}]\cdot[X/G]\in K_{0}(\Var_{X/G}^{\dim}).

Proof.

Denote by
\pi:X\rightarrow X/G
the quotient map. Let
p\in X/G
be a point and denote
W=\pi^{-1}(p)
. By Lemma
5.19
, it suffices to show that
[\mathbb{P}E_{W}/G]=[\mathbb{P}_{k(p)}^{n-1}]
in
K_{0}(\Var_{k(p)}^{\dim})
, where
E_{W}
is the restriction of
E
to
W
.

If we pick a point
x\in W
, we have
\mathbb{P}E_{W}/G\cong\mathbb{P}E_{x}/G_{x}
, where
G_{x}\subset G
is the stabilizer of
x\in W
and
E_{x}
is the restriction of
E
to

. By Lemma
5.18
applied to

, considered as a vector space over the field

, we indeed find

\displaystyle[\mathbb{P}E_{W}/G]=[\mathbb{P}E_{x}/G_{x}]=[\mathbb{P}_{k(p)}^{n-1}]\in K_{0}(\Var_{k(p)}^{\dim}).

∎

Corollary 5.21
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group acting on a smooth quasi-projective variety

over a field

\displaystyle[\Bl_{Y}X/G]-[X/G]=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\mathbb{L}\cdot[Y/G]\cdot[\mathbb{P}^{k-1}],

6.
Kapranov Zeta Functions

6.1.
Irrationality Result

Theorem 6.1
.

[cf. Theorem
1.4
]
Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
>1
and non-negative Kodaira dimension
\kappa(X)\geq 0
. Then the graded Kapranov zeta function
Z_{X}^{\dim}(t)\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})[[t]]
of
X
is (pointwise) irrational.

In fact, the ungraded Kapranov zeta function
Z_{X}(t)\in K_{0}(\Var_{k})[[t]]
of
X
is also (pointwise) irrational, if we assume a positive answer to Question
5.24
.

If
R
is a ring, we say a power series
Z(t)\in R[[t]]
is pointwise irrational if there is a ring homomorphism
\mu:R\rightarrow F
, where
F
is a field, such that
\mu(Z(t))\in F[[t]]
is irrational.

We focus on the graded Kapranov zeta function
Z_{X}^{\dim}(t)\in K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})[[t]]
. We first give a simpler proof of Theorem
6.1
when
\kappa(X)\geq 1
. Then we prove the general case by making use of Adams operations, and we discuss the adjustments for the ungraded version, assuming a positive answer to Question
5.24
.

Larsen and Lunts proved that the Kapranov zeta function
Z_{X}(t)\in K_{0}(\Var_{k})[[t]]
of a smooth projective surface of Kodaira dimension
\kappa(X)\geq 0
is pointwise irrational in
[
LL04
]
. We follow their line of argument, which extends to arbitrary dimension by crucially using the following result of Arapura-Archava
[
AA03
]
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n>1
. If
Z_{m}
is a resolution of
\Sym^{m}X
, then

\displaystyle H^{0}(Z_{m},\omega_{Z_{m}}^{\otimes n})\cong\Sym^{m}H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\otimes d}),

whenever
nd
is even.

6.2.
Proof when
\kappa(X)\geq 1

Choose
d\geq 1
so that
h=h^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\otimes d})\geq 2
. Replacing
d
by
2d
if necessary, we assume that
d
is even.

Consider the map of monoids

\displaystyle\Bir_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{N},\hskip 14.22636pt[Z]\mapsto h^{0}(Z,\omega_{Z}^{\otimes d}).

By Theorem
6.2
, this map commutes with
\lambda
-operations. Hence the corresponding map of group rings
\mathbb{Z}[\Bir_{k}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}]
is a
\lambda
-homomorphism. Consider now the composite
\lambda
-homomorphism

\displaystyle\mu=\mu_{d}:K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\Bir_{k}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}].

We show that

\displaystyle\mu(Z_{X}^{\dim}(t))=\sum_{m\geq 0}\bigg[\binom{m+h-1}{h-1}\bigg]t^{m}.

is not rational in
F[[t]]
, where
F
is the fraction field of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}]
.

We use the following Lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

[cf.
[
LL03
, Section
3
]
]
If
F
is a field, a power series
\sum_{m}a_{m}t^{m}
is rational if and only if there exists
j>0
and
j_{0}>0
such that for each
m>j_{0}
, the determinant of the matrix

\begin{bmatrix}a_{m}&\dots&a_{m+j}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{m+j}&\dots&a_{m+2j}\end{bmatrix}

is zero.

Spelling this out, we need to show that for each
j>0
, there exists
m
sufficiently large so that

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{j+1}}\sgn(\sigma)\bigg[\prod_{i=1}^{j+1}\binom{m+\sigma(i)+i+h-3}{h-1}\bigg]\neq 0.

Each of the summands on the left hand side, considered as a polynomial in
m
, determines the multiset
\{\sigma(i)+i\}
. The multiset corresponding to the identity permutation is clearly unique, so there cannot be any cancellation in the monoid ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}]
for all
m
. The result follows.

Note that this proof uses the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}]
crucially. The power series

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 0}\binom{m+h-1}{h-1}t^{m}=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{h}}

is certainly rational in
\mathbb{Z}[[t]]
.

Moreover, this proof fails completely in the ungraded situation, as
K_{0}(\Var_{k})
only admits
\mathbb{Z}[\SB_{k}]
as a quotient, and plurigenera are not stably birational invariants.

6.3.
Proof when
\kappa(X)\geq 0

To attack the general case of Theorem
6.1
, we will need to keep track of more information. We use the Adams operations on the Grothendieck group
\overline{K}(X)
as in Example
4.4
, together with the global sections map

\displaystyle h^{0}:\overline{K}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}.

Fix
d\geq 1
and denote
h_{d}^{i}(Z)=h^{0}(\Psi^{d}[\Omega_{Z}^{i}])
if
Z
is a smooth projective variety.

Let
M\subset\mathbb{Z}[s]
be the monoid of polynomials with constant term one under multiplication, and consider the map
\nu=\nu_{d}
which assigns to a smooth projective variety
Z
, the element

\displaystyle\nu(Z)=1+h_{d}^{1}(Z)s+\ldots+h_{d}^{n}(Z)s^{d}\in M,

where
n=\dim Z
. Note that
\Psi^{d}\omega_{Z}=\omega_{Z}^{\otimes d}
, so the highest degree coefficient of
\nu(Z)
is the plurigenus
h^{0}(Z,\omega_{Z}^{\otimes d})
.

The following is proved in
[
LL04
, Proposition
6.1
]
.

Lemma 6.4
.

The map
\nu
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\nu(Z)=\nu(Z^{\prime})
if
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
are birational,

(2)

\nu(Z\times Z^{\prime})=\nu(Z)\nu(Z^{\prime})
,

(3)

\nu(\mathbb{P}^{1})=1
.

In particular, we can view
\nu
as a map of monoids
\Bir_{k}\rightarrow M
.

Consider now the composite ring homomorphism

\displaystyle\mu=\mu_{d}:K_{0}(\Var_{k}^{\dim})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\Bir_{k}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[M].

Remark 6.5
.

The monoid ring
\mathbb{Z}[M]
admits a natural
\lambda
-structure, but
\mu
is not a
\lambda
-homomorphism for
n>1
.

Now let’s prove Theorem
6.1
. As before, choose an even integer
d
such that
h=h^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\otimes d})\geq 1
.

Note that
M
is a free abelian monoid, so
\mathbb{Z}[M]
is an integral domain with fraction field
F
. We show that the image
f(t)
of

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 0}\mu([\Sym^{m}X])t^{m}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\mu([Z_{m}])t^{m}

in
F[[t]]
is irrational, where
Z_{m}
is a resolution of
\Sym^{m}X
.

We now require a few lemmas from
[
LL04
]
. The first is proved only for the resolution
\Hilb^{m}X\rightarrow\Sym^{m}X
when
X
is a surface, but the argument goes through in general.

Lemma 6.6
.

[cf.
[
LL04
, Proposition
7.5
]
]
Fix
d\geq 1
and
i\geq 1
. Then
h_{d}^{i}(Z_{m})=h^{0}(\Psi^{d}\Omega_{Z_{m}}^{i})
is bounded, independent of
m
.

Lemma 6.7
.

[cf.
[
LL04
, Proposition
2.6
]
]
Let
F
be the field of fractions of the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[G]
of a free abelian group
G
. Let
f(t)=\sum_{i\geq 0}g_{i}t^{i}\in F[[t]]
be a power series such that, for each
i
, either
g_{i}\in G
or
g_{i}=0
. Then
f(t)
is rational if and only if there exists
d\geq 1
and
i_{0}\geq 1
, and
\{h_{i}\in G\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
periodic with period
d
, such that

\displaystyle g_{i+d}=h_{i}g_{i}

for all
i\geq i_{0}
.

Suppose that
f(t)
is rational in
F[[t]]
. Apply Lemma
6.7
with
G
the group completion of
M
. In particular, there exists
h\in G
and integers
d\geq 1
and
i_{0}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\mu([Z_{i_{0}+\ell d}])=h^{\ell}\cdot\mu([Z_{i_{0}}])

for all
\ell\geq 0
.

Note that each
\mu([Z_{m}])
is a polynomial with positive leading term
h^{0}(Z_{m},\omega_{Z_{m}}^{\otimes d})
. Hence
h
is a nonconstant polynomial in
s
. But then, looking at the powers of
h
, we see the coefficients of fixed powers of
s
in the
\mu([Z_{m}])
cannot all stay bounded, contradicting Lemma
6.6
. This completes the proof of Theorem
6.1
in the graded setting.

The only change we make when working with the ungraded Grothendieck ring
K_{0}(\Var_{k})
is to use the reduction
r:K_{0}(\Var_{k})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\SB_{k}]
modulo
\mathbb{L}
, as opposed to modulo
\tau
. We then define
\mu:K_{0}(\Var_{k})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[M]
by composing
r
with the morphism
\mathbb{Z}[\SB_{k}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[M]
, which is well-defined by Lemma
6.4
.

However, it is now unclear whether the class
r([\Sym^{m}X])\in\mathbb{Z}[\SB_{k}]
reflects the stable birational type of
\Sym^{m}X
, i.e. whether
r([\Sym^{m}X])=[Z_{m}]\in\mathbb{Z}[\SB_{k}]
. This is not true for a resolution of a general singular variety. We can guarantee it only if we assume that
\Sym^{m}X
has
\mathbb{D}
-singularities or, more weakly, that
\Sym^{m}X
has
\mathbb{L}
-rational singularities. Shein
[
She25
]
recently showed that
\Sym^{m}X
does have
\mathbb{L}
-rational singularities and so proved Theorem
6.1
unconditionally in the ungraded setting.

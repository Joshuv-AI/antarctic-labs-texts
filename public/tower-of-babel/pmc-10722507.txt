Tandem Isomerization/α,β-Site-Selective and Enantioselective Addition Reactions of N-(3-Butynoyl)-3,5-dimethylpyrazole Induced by Chiral π–Cu(II) Catalysts
Authors: Weiwei Guo, Masahiro Hori, Yoshihiro Ogura, Kazuki Nishimura, Kosuke Oki, Tomoyuki Ikai, Eiji Yashima, Kazuaki Ishihara
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractAllenes are important building blocks, and derivatization of products via cycloadditions of allenes could become a powerful strategy for constructing carbocyclic and heterocyclic rings. However, the development of catalytic site-selective and enantioselective cycloaddition reactions of allenes still presents significant challenges. Here, we report chiral π–Cu(II)-complex-catalyzed isomerization of N-(3-butynoyl)-3,5-dimethyl-1H-pyrazole to generate N-allenoylpyrazole in situ and subsequent α,β-site-selective and enantioselective [3 + 2], [4 + 2], or [2 + 2] cycloaddition or conjugate addition reactions. The asymmetric environment created by the intramolecular π–Cu(II) interactions provides the corresponding adducts in moderate to high yield with excellent enantioselectivity. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first successful method for chiral-Lewis-acid-catalyzed tandem isomerization/α,β-site-selective and enantioselective cycloaddition or conjugate addition reactions of latent non-γ-substituted allenoyl derivative.

Introduction

Allenes are very versatile building blocks in organic chemistry due to the presence of two mutually perpendicular π-orbitals. Among their many applications in chemistry, cycloaddition reactions of activated allenes represent a powerful strategy for the synthesis of a variety of carbocycles and heterocycles.1,2 More importantly, asymmetric versions have been developed that greatly broaden their applications to include the synthesis of bioactive compounds. In recent years, 3-alkynoates have been explored as activated γ-substituted allenes in asymmetric cycloaddition reactions.3−5 For example, in 2010, Loh’s group developed the tandem isomerization of 3-alkynoates to allenoates/α,γ-site-selective and enantioselective [3 + 2] cycloaddition via vinylphosphonium dipolar intermediates using chiral phosphine catalyst4a (Scheme 1a). In contrast, in 2009, Huang, Tan et al. developed a tandem chiral guanidine-catalyzed enantioselective isomerization of 3-alkynoates to chiral allenoates/α,β-site-selective and diastereoselective [4 + 2] cycloaddition (Scheme 1b).5a In 2017, Brown’s group reported a tandem chiral quinine-derived thiourea-catalyzed enantioselective isomerization/β,γ-site-selective and diastereoselective [2 + 2] cycloaddition (Scheme 1b).5c Similar types of tandem reactions have also been developed by several other groups.4−6 However, to the best of our knowledge, reports of a tandem isomerization of 3-butynoyl derivatives to achiral latent non-γ-substituted allenes and their α,γ-site-selective and enantioselective cycloaddition reactions with 1,3-dipoles, dienes, or arenes (Scheme 1c) remain elusive so far, most likely because the reactivity and enantio-, stereo-, and site-selectivities of latent allenes that do not carry γ-substituents are hard to control due to their instability, multiple reactive sites, and structural simplicity. As shown in Schemes 1a,b, generally, the in situ isomerization of 3-alkynoates to the corresponding allenes is triggered by bases, which could limit their applications to some extent. If chiral Lewis-acid catalysts were effective for the in situ isomerization, the subsequent enantioselective cycloaddition reactions using the latent allenes as 1,3-dipolarophiles, dienophiles, or arenophiles would potentially be possible. Here, we report the first tandem isomerization/α,γ-site-selective and enantioselective [3 + 2], [4 + 2], or [2 + 2] cycloaddition or conjugate addition reactions of N-(3-butynoyl)-3,5-dimethylpyrazole induced by chiral π–Cu(II) catalysts (Scheme 1c).

Use of 3-Alkynoates and Their Analogues in Site and Asymmetric Tandem Isomerization/Cycloaddition or Conjugate Addition Reactions

Our previous work on the enantioselective α-halogenation of N-acyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazoles catalyzed by chiral π–Cu(II) or π–Cu(II)−π complexes has shown that chelation between Cu(II) and N-acylpyrazole can significantly increase the acidity of the α-proton of the carbonyl moiety.7,8 According to DFT calculations at the B3LYP/6-31+G* level of theory,7a the MEP value of the pseudo-Z conformer of N-(3-butynoyl)-3,5-dimethylpyrazole 1 is 157.3 kJ/mol, which corresponds to pKa = 17.0. This pKa value is much lower than those of acetone (pKa = 26.5) and tert-butyl acetate (pKa = 20.3) (see Supprting Information section 13 for details). Based on these results, we speculated that the relatively high acidity of Ha in CuX2L-coordinated 1 could result in isomerization to generate N-allenoyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazole 2 (Scheme 2). The allenoyl species 2 activated by CuX2L could then be directly captured by nucleophiles (Nu) such as 1,3-dipoles, dienes, and indoles which would result in α,β-site-selective and enantioselective [3 + 2], [4 + 2], and [2 + 2] cycloaddition reactions to give five-, six-, and four-membered cycloadducts, respectively (Scheme 1c).

Results and Discussion

α,β-Site- and Enantioselective [3 + 2] Cycloaddition of 3-Butynoyl-1H-pyrazole 1 with Azomethine Imines 3

To verify our speculation, the α,β-site-selective and enantioselective 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition of 3-butynoyl-1H-pyrazole 1 with azomethine imine 3a was initially investigated in dichloromethane at −40 °C in the presence of MS 4A (Table 1).9 In line with our hypothesis, when our previously developed ligand L3 was employed,71 worked well as a precursor of a latent activated allene (see Supporting Information section 16 for details) and α,β-site-selectively afforded endo-product 4a in 76% yield, albeit with a low ee value (entry 2). Given that the N-R1 group of ligand L can greatly influence the enantioselectivity of the asymmetric reaction, we then screened the N-R1 group of (2-naphthyl)-l-alanine-derived ligands. Although several primary and secondary alkyl groups were examined, only poor to moderate enantioselectivity was observed (entries 1–5). The best result was 84% yield and 77% ee when using the ligand that bears an N-methyl group (entry 1). Interestingly, the use of N-isopropyl and N-cyclohexyl groups furnished (5R,6S)-4a with opposite enantioselectivity (entry 4, 31% ee; entry 5, 50% ee). When L6 with an N-dibenzosuberyl (Dbs) group was employed as a ligand, the reaction afforded (5R,6S)-4a in 98% yield with significantly improved enantioselectivity (entry 6, 98% ee).

In order to understand these results, transition-state assemblies for the Cu(OTf)2·L1- and Cu(OTf)2·L6-catalyzed reactions are proposed in Figure 1 based on our previous reports on asymmetric π–Cu(II)-catalyzed reactions.7,8 These cycloaddition reactions proceed stepwise through Michal addition and subsequent cyclization. First, we confirmed that the preferred conformation of Cu(II)-chelated 2 is close to s-cis due to steric reasons using DFT calculations at the B3LYP/6-31G* level of theory: The s-cis conformer of N-allennoyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazpole 2 is by 4.32 kJ/mol more stable than its s-trans conformer. Furthermore, the distorted conformer of 2 (∼40°) is by 0.44 kJ/mol more stable than its s-cis conformer (see Supporting Information section 14 for details). As shown in Scheme 2, Cu(OTf)2L·1 prefers trans chelation between the nitrogen atom of L and the nitrogen atom of 1 based on the positive Cotton effect which was observed via CD analysis of Cu(NTf2)2·(S)-L2·2-acetylpyridine (see the Supporting Information section 15 for details). In the case of Cu(OTf)2·L1 (cf. TS-A1 and endo-TS-A2 in Figure 1), the π–Cu(II) interactions between the 2-naphthyl moiety of L1 and Cu(II) can be expected to block the si-face of the allenyl moiety of 2, which would result in azomethine imine 3a approaching the re-face to give (5S,6R)-4a as the major product. The steric bulkiness of N-R1 would prevent 3a from approaching the re-face in the order of entries 1–5. In entries 4 and 5, the opposite enantioselectivity was observed as a result of blocking the re-face by N-R1 overcoming the blocking of the si-face by π–Cu(II) interactions. On the other hand, in the case of Cu(OTf)2·L6, the close contact between the Cu(II) center and the benzene ring of the N-Dbs moiety, rather than the 2-naphthyl group, generates the π–Cu(II) interactions that induce the asymmetric environment by blocking the si face of the coordinated 2 (cf. TS-B1 and endo-TS-B2 in Figure 1).

Proposed transition-state assemblies for the [3 + 2] cycloaddition of 1 with 3a catalyzed by Cu(OTf)2·L1 and Cu(OTf)2·L6.

With the N-Dbs group as the R1 group on the chiral ligand L in hand, we then briefly evaluated the R2 group (entries 7 and 8 in Table 1). Using L7 bearing a benzyl moiety and L8 bearing a cyclohexylmethyl moiety furnished (5R,6S)-4a with 85% ee and 93% ee, respectively. These results indicate that a sufficiently bulky R2 group on L might assist in stabilizing the π–Cu(II) complex in TS-B1 and endo-TS-B2. Moreover, when L6 was used, π–π interactions between the naphthyl group and the benzene ring of N-Dbs may also contribute to the stabilization of TS-B1 and endo-TS-B2.7b

Using L6 as the chiral ligand of the π–Cu(II) Lewis-acidic catalyst, we then evaluated the generality of the tandem [3 + 2] cycloaddition. Azomethine imines 3 with both electron-withdrawing and electron-donating groups on the benzene ring underwent the cycloaddition efficiently to afford 4a–t in high yield and excellent enantiomeric excess (Table 2). Furthermore, substituents at the para- or meta-positions (4a–k, 4l–o, 4s, and 4t) of the benzene group were all tolerated. In addition, an ortho-fluoro substituent (4p) was also tolerated. However, ortho-bromo-substituted product 4q was generated in 88% yield with a slightly decreased ee of 78%. This can probably be attributed to the steric hindrance of the ortho-bromo substituent.

To show the utility of this tandem reaction, a gram-scale conversion was performed in the presence of 5 mol % of Cu(OTf)2·L6, which delivered 4a in 96% yield with 95% ee (Scheme 3). Furthermore, L6 was recovered in 96% yield, which is suitable for an environmentally friendly protocol. Moreover, the transformation of 4a offers opportunities to increase molecular diversity. For example, the double bond was easily isomerized to afford 5a in 98% yield. Reduction and alcoholysis were able to furnish 6a and 7a in 85% and 90% yield, respectively.

Gram-Scale Synthesis and Transformation

α,β-Site-Selective and Enantioselective Dearomative [3 + 2] Cycloaddition of 3-Butynoyl-1H-pyrazole 1 with Isoquinolinium Ylides 8

The utility of bench-stable isoquinolinium ylides in the enantioselective 1,3-dipolar cycloadditions has attracted substantial attention from the scientific community in very recent years.10 This stems from their proven convenient and efficient role for constructing pyrroloisoquinoline scaffolds, which are crucial moieties in numerous natural products and pharmaceutically active compounds.11 The first example of a regioselective dearomative [3 + 2] cyclization involving allenoates with isoquinolinium ylides was reported by Waldmann’s group in 2015.10e Subsequently, Guo’s group further expanded its application by incorporating phthalazinium ylides.12 Notably, both catalyzed methods yielded β,γ-selective products. Despite the successful development of the application of allenoates in the dearomative [3 + 2] cycloaddition with structurally valuable isoquinolinium ylides, no efficient enantioselective version has been developed to date, despite dedicated attempts.10e Thus, we then tried the tandem isomerization/α,β-site-selective and enantioselective dearomative [3 + 2] cycloaddition reactions of isoquinolinium ylides 8 with 1. Optimization was initially performed as shown in Supporting Information section 5, and finally, L9 was selected as the chiral ligand. As shown in Figure 3, a variety of substituted isoquinolinium methylides 8 could react with 1 efficiently, in most cases affording enantioriched exo-cycloadducts 9 (up to >99% ee) in excellent yield. Different substituents at the different positions of the isoquinolinium moieties all could be tolerated (9b–9f). Notably, 8f bearing the electron-donating group also worked well (9f: 97% yield, 91% ee). Using phthalazinium ylide (X = “N”) as a dipole, the reaction also proceeded smoothly and furnished the enantiopure 9g of >99% ee in 93% yield. The relative and absolute configuration of 9g was determined to be (1R,10bR) by X-ray crystallography analysis, and the configurations of all other products were assigned accordingly (Figure 3).

α,β-Site-Selective and Enantioselective Diels–Alder Reaction of 3-Butynoyl-1H-pyrazole 1 with Cyclic Dienes 10

The Diels–Alder reaction is among the most powerful reactions in organic chemistry because it allows for the formation of two C–C bonds and the construction of up to four contiguous stereogenic centers in a single step.13 Catalytic enantioselective Diels–Alder reactions of various activated alkenes have been explored with increasing intensity over the past several decades. However, only a few examples of applications of allene dienophiles have been reported, despite the potential utility in the total synthesis of natural products.5a,5b,14,15b One of the reasons may be that it is difficult to get suitable allenes (for example, in the total synthesis of laurenditerpenol, Corey’s group utilized trifluoroethyl allenoate as the dienophile,15b but which was synthesized in only 2% overall yield using a four-step procedure16). Thus, we investigated the feasibility of our in situ generated allene dienophiles for tandem [4 + 2] reactions.

As shown in Table 3, the tandem isomerization/α,β-site-selective and enantioselective [4 + 2] cycloaddition reactions of 1 with cyclopentadiene 10a was examined in the presence of L6 (11 mol %) and Cu(OTf)2 (10 mol %) in dichloromethane (entry 1). Although product 11a was formed in 42% yield, its endo-selectivity and enantioselectivity were only moderate. Fortunately, when L2 was used instead of L6, the chemical yield of 11a increased to 83%, and the endo- and enantioselectivity were also dramatically improved to 96% endo and 98% ee (entry 2).

To evaluate the generality of the present α,β-site-selective and enantioselective [4 + 2] cycloaddition reaction of in situ generated N-allenoylpyrazole 2, several dienes were examined under the optimized conditions using L2. As shown in Table 3, the [4 + 2] cycloaddition of 1 proceeded smoothly with cyclic dienes such as substituted cyclopentadienes, fulvenes, and furans to give the bicyclic endo-adducts 11 in high yield with high endo- and enantioselectivity. It should also be noted here that even when using the sterically more demanding 10b and 10d, the tandem reactions proceeded smoothly to furnish tricyclic 11b and 11d in quantitative yield with high diastereo- and enantioselectivity (entries 3 and 5). Surprisingly, Lewis-basic furans 10e and 10f also reacted with 1 in high yield with high diastereo- and enantioselectivity (entries 6 and 7). Furans have recently received particular attention in [4 + 2] cycloaddition reactions, as they are crucial renewable-platform molecules with great potential for the more sustainable production of chemical building blocks and materials.17 In contrast, the reaction of 2 with acyclic dienes gave no desired cycloadducts probably due to the instability of 2 and low reactivity of acyclic dienes.

Further transformations of cycloadducts 11 were then performed, as shown in Scheme 4. The ethanolysis of 11a, obtained in entry 2 of Table 3, gave the corresponding ester (12a) in 80% yield; 12a could be used for the synthesis of the (+)-enantiomer of the natural product (−)-β-santalene.14 The absolute configuration of (1S,2R,4R)-11a (entry 2) was determined from the [α]D value of 12a derived from 11a.14a,15,18 The reduction of 11f with NaBH4 gave 13f, which is an intermediate for the synthesis of the (+)-enantiomer of the bioactive natural product (−)-laurenditerpenol.14,15,18

The absolute asymmetric induction in the enantioselective [4 + 2] cycloaddition of 1 with 11 can be interpreted based on endo-TS-C, which is analogous to endo-TS-A (Figure 4). Notably, although N-allenoyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazole 2 could not be isolated due to instability, in situ generation of 2 was confirmed by isolation of 4. The isomerization of 1 to 2 with basic ligand L or azomethine imine 3 did not occur in the absence of Cu(OTf)2 at −40 °C. These control experiments have revealed that the activation of 1 with Cu(OTf)2 was crucial for the isomerization to 2.

α,β-Site-Selective and Enantioselective Dearomative 1,4-Addition and [2 + 2] Cycloaddition Reactions of 3-Butynoyl-1H-pyrazole 1 with 2,3-Disubstituted Indoles 14

To demonstrate the further applicability of the present catalytic isomerization of 1, we examined nucleophilic additions of 2,3-disubstituted indoles 14 to in situ generated N-allenoylpyrazole 2 (Scheme 5). Tandem isomerization/α,β-site-selective and enantioselective dearomative 1,4-addition of 2-aryl-3-methylindoles 14a and 14b to 1 smoothly furnished the desired products 15a and 15b, bearing all-carbon quaternary chiral centers, in high yield with high enantioselectivity. Interestingly in the reactions of 1 with 2,3-dialkylindoles such as 14c–14f, [2 + 2] cycloadducts 16c–16f, featuring three stereogenic centers, were generated in moderate yield with high enantioselectivity. The relative configuration of 16c was determined by NOE analysis. The absolute configurations of products 15a and 16d were assigned as (S) and (S,S,S) based on a circular-dichroism analyses combined with time-dependent-density functional theory calculations (Figures S3 and S4), respectively. Furthermore, although the [2 + 2] cycloadditions of 1 with N-methylated-14c and -14d were also examined, no reaction occurred even at −40 °C, and 1 was decomposed at −20 °C.

Tandem Isomerization/α,β-Site-Selective and Enantioselective Dearomative 1,4-Addition or [2 + 2] Cycloaddition Reactions of 1 with 14

We can rationally explain that the α,β-site-selective and enantioselective 1,4-addition of 2,3-disubstituted indole 14 to 1 occurs via TS-D, which is analogous to TS-B, to give the common intermediate 17 (Scheme 6). When 2-aryl-3-methylindole is used, the subsequent cyclization is suppressed by the steric hindrance of the 2-aryl moiety and/or the conjugational stability of the iminium cation with a 2-aryl group (see Supporting Information section 17). Thus, acyclic (S)-15 is obtained as a major product. In contrast, when 2,3-dalkylindole is used, the successive cyclization occurs α-site-selectively (>99% for the α-site of 2) and diastereoselectively (>99% de for 16) to give [2 + 2] cycloadduct (S,S,S)-16 via the folded, zwitterionic TS-E. On the other hand, (S,S,R)-16 is not produced via the extended, zwitterionic TS-F which is disfavored due to the steric hindrance between the 2,3-dialkyl groups and the pyrazoylcarbonyl group. Interestingly, the γ-site-selective cyclization via 17 to give (S,S)-18 did not proceed at all. In sharp contrast, there have been only examples of β,γ-site-selective dearomative (cyclo)addition reactions of 14 to electron-rich allenamides.19b−19e Besides, it is also known that thermal [2 + 2] cycloaddition of alkenes to electron-deficient allenoates proceeds β,γ-site-selectively through concerted, asynchronous [π2s + (π2s + π2s)] mechanism (Scheme 1b).20 Therefore, α,β-site-selective reactions shown in Scheme 5 are very rare and valuable results.19a,19f,19g

Conclusions

In summary, we have developed the first examples of a chiral-Lewis-acid-catalyzed tandem isomerization of N-(3-butynoyl)-3,5-dimethyl-1H-pyrazole to generate N-allenoylpyrazole/α,β-site-selective and enantioselective [3 + 2], [4 + 2], and [2 + 2] cycloadditions and 1,4-addition. Thus, the utility of chiral π–Cu(II) catalysts has been expanded to include enantioselective reactions of N-allenoylpyrazole as well as N-acryloyl-,8a,8b,8d,8hN-propioloyl-,8c,8d,8f and N-alkanoylpyrazoles.7

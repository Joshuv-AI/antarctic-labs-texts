Dualities and trialities in
𝒩

Abstract




We study new Seiberg-like dualities for 1d
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric gauge theories – that is, supersymmetric gauged quantum mechanics – with unitary gauge group and (anti)fundamental matter in chiral and fermi multiplets, and with non-zero Fayet–Iliopoulos parameter. Similarly to its higher-dimensional analogues, this 1d Seiberg duality is an infrared duality: the supersymmetric ground states of the dual gauge theories match exactly. We provide strong evidence for the dualities, including the matching of the flavoured Witten indices, a Higgs-branch derivation in terms of dual Grassmannian manifolds, and a detailed study of the Coulomb-branch ground states in the abelian case. We study how the supersymmetric ground states, in either dual description, depend on the sign of the Fayet–Iliopoulos parameter, and we explore the corresponding wall-crossing phenomenon. For some special values of the discrete parameters defining the unitary gauge theory, the dualities, combined with trivial wall-crossing, enhances to a triality. This includes, as a special case, the dimensional reduction to 1d of the 2d
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
Gadde–Gukov–Putrov triality.

Introduction

Infrared dualities are central to our understanding of supersymmetric gauge theories. Two distinct quantum field theories (QFT) are said to be infrared dual if they are isomorphic in the low-energy limit
Seiberg:1994pq
. This is to be contrasted with exact dualities, where two theories that might look distinct semi-classically are actually exactly the same quantum system at all scales — the prototypical example being the Montonen–Olive duality of 4d
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM
Montonen:1977sn
;
Sen:1994yi
. To avoid calling any effective field theory description an ‘infrared dual theory’, one generally reserves the ‘duality’ monicker to those pairs of theories which are both well-defined in the UV (
i.e.
at least asymptotically free). Those pairs are often known as Seiberg-like dual theories, since Seiberg duality in 4d
\mathcal{N}=1
SQCD is the prototypical infrared duality
Seiberg:1994pq
. By now, there are many well-established examples of Seiberg-like dualities between supersymmetric field theories in four, three and two space-time dimensions (see
Intriligator:1995ne
;
Kutasov:1995np
;
Aharony:1997gp
;
Giveon:2008zn
;
Hori:2006dk
for a very partial list), and even between non-supersymmetric 3d gauge theories
Aharony:2012nh
;
Seiberg:2016gmd
;
Karch:2016sxi
.

In this paper, we introduce and study new infrared dualities in 1d
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric gauge theories — that is, in gauged
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric quantum mechanics (SQM). In the context of quantum mechanics, an infrared duality is simply the claim that two distinct systems have isomorphic ground states. Here, the low-energy limit singles out the supersymmetric ground states, which have zero energy. For this notion of an infrared duality in SQM to really make sense, it is crucial that the SQM be gapped --- there is a finite energy gap between the zero-energy states and any other excited state.

In supersymmetric quantum mechanics, we should be able to understand the ground states directly, which should give us a physics proof of the duality — in this sense, SQM gives us tractable toy models of infrared dualities in supersymmetric QFT. In this paper, we carry out this ground-state analysis using two complementary approaches, which we dub
Higgs branch
and
Coulomb branch
approaches, following standard terminology in higher dimensions. Recall that we do not have a moduli space of vacua in quantum mechanics — the ground-state wavefunction spreads over the semi-classical target space instead. What the target space looks like depends on tunable parameters, and the Higgs and Coulomb descriptions appear in two distinct scaling limits.

Higgs phase geometry and dualities from Grassmannians.
The Higgs phase is obtained in the limit
|\zeta|\rightarrow\infty
, wherein the 1d gauge group is fully Higgsed and we flow to a 1d non-linear sigma model (NLSM) onto the Higgs branch. For
\varGamma
-SQCD with
y_{F}=1
, the target space of the NLSM is a particular vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\pm}
over the complex Grassmannian:

\mathcal{E}_{\pm}\rightarrow X_{\pm}\penalty\ ,\qquad\text{with }\,X_{+}\equiv{\rm Gr}(N_{c},n_{1})\,\text{ and }\,X_{-}\equiv{\rm Gr}(N_{c},n_{2})\penalty\ .

(1.4)

Here
\pm=\sign(\zeta)
. The detailed form of
\mathcal{E}_{\pm}
will be derived in the main text. The supersymmetric ground states correspond to cohomology classes of the target space
Witten:1982im
;
Alvarez-Gaume:1983zxc
:

{\mathscr{H}_{0}}\cong H^{\bullet}(X_{\pm},\mathcal{E}_{\pm})\penalty\ .

(1.5)

It turns out that these sheaf cohomology groups can be computed very explicitly using the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem
bott1957homogeneous
. This gives us, in particular, a completely explicit formula for the flavoured Witten index of
\varGamma
-SQCD as a polynomial in
y_{F}^{\pm}
. (Turning on
y_{F}\neq 1
gives us the equivariant cohomology.) We will then show, following
Jia:2014ffa
, that the mutation dualities follow directly from the Grassmannian duality, which is the geometric isomorphism between
N_{c}
-planes in
\mathbb{C}^{n_{f}}
and the orthogonal
(n_{f}-N_{c})
-planes:

{\rm Gr}(N_{c},n_{f})\cong{\rm Gr}(n_{f}-N_{c},n_{f})\penalty\ .

(1.6)

This proves our Seiberg-like dualities for
\varGamma
-SQCD in the Higgs phase. Note that this approach naturally derives subtle but important shifts of the fermion number and of background 1d Chern–Simons levels for the flavour symmetry that are induced by the mutations.

Coulomb phase and perturbative ground states.
The Coulomb phase is obtained in the limit
\zeta\rightarrow 0
while keeping the Coulomb-branch scale
M_{C}\equiv\zeta^{2}g^{2}
fixed, where
g
is the 1d gauge coupling
Hori:2014tda
. In this limit, we obtain an effective description in terms of the Coulomb-branch scalar
\sigma
and gauginos that are partnered to the 1d gauge field by supersymmetry. In this paper we will focus on the abelian case,
N_{c}=1
, for simplicity. Then the Coulomb branch is a line
\mathbb{R}\cong\{\sigma\}
, and we generally have several perturbative sectors on the
\sigma
-line separated by singular loci at which some chiral multiplet become massless. We work out the perturbative ground states in each region, incorporating the
E
-term potential associated with the
\varGamma
fields at first order in perturbation theory. We carefully match these perturbative ground states to the flavoured Witten, pointing out that distinct perturbative sectors on the
\sigma
-line correspond to taking specific residues of the matter integrand that appears in the JK-residue formula for the Witten index. In general, there are non-trivial cancellations in the full index between states in distinct perturbative sectors. These cancellations occur precisely betweeen pairs of states
\lvert\Psi\rangle
,
\overline{\lambda}\lvert\Psi^{\prime}\rangle
that would be lifted by SQM instantons if the effective field theory had a smooth potential with several zero-energy minima
Witten:1982im
. In our model, we have a singular potential with the singularities due to massless chiral multiplets, and we therefore conjecture that the gauge-theory high-energy completion leads to a physically equivalent effect.

It would be interesting to extend this Coulomb-phase analysis to the non-abelian theory, in which case we have a
N_{c}
-dimensional Coulomb branch, and the gauginos play a more crucial role. We leave this as an interesting challenge for future work. More generally, one can presumably extend our analysis to many other infrared dualities between 1d gauge theories with other gauge groups. Wall-crossing and trialities.
An interesting aspect of 1d
\mathcal{N}=2
gauge theories is that they can undergo wall-crossing
Hori:2014tda
;
Kim:2011sc
;
Cordova:2014oxa
. That is, the Hilbert space of ground states can change discontinuously as the FI parameters are varied, which is efficiently detected as a jump in the value of the flavoured Witten index. Throughout, we explore some aspects of wall-crossing in our 1d SQCD models. While wall-crossing is generic, some of the most interesting 1d theories are those for which the wall-crossing is trivial, so that we can smoothly interpolate between positive and negative FI parameter
\zeta
. We identify a sufficient condition for trivial wall-crossing, which we also conjecture to be necessary (except in trivial cases). Combining our dualities with a trivial wall-crossing transition (that is, flipping the sign of
\zeta
at no cost), we can extend dualities to trialities — that is, a set of three theories with isomorphic supersymmetric ground states. In a very special set of cases with
q_{c}=0
, this triality becomes precisely the naive dimensional reduction to SQM of the Gadde–Gukov–Putrov triality of 2d
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
supersymmetric gauge theories
Gadde:2013lxa
. More generally, dualities and trialities allow us to easily identify models which are ‘confining’, by which we mean that they can be described by free fields. This has interesting applications to the study of line defects in 3d
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric gauge theories
Hwang:2017kmk
;
Hosomichi:2021gxe
;
Closset:2023bdr
;
CGKSZZ25
; in particular, confinement trialities elucidate some duality relations first discussed in that context
Closset:2023bdr
.

The existence of many unitary gauge theories with trivial wall-crossing also opens a tantalising possibility, that there exists genuine 1d fixed points on the wall. Indeed, at
\zeta=0
the Coulomb-branch scalar potential of such theories looks like

U(\sigma)={1\over\sigma^{2}}\penalty\ ,

(1.7)

which is the potential for the best-known conformal model in quantum mechanics
deAlfaro:1976vlx
. It should be possible to directly establish some version of superconformal invariance in some ‘infrared’ scaling limit of the 1d gauge theories considered here. We leave this very important question for future work.

Gauged supersymmetric quantum mechanics

In this section, we review 1d
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric models – that is, supersymmetric quantum mechanics (SQM) with two real supercharges
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}

2.1
Supersymmetry algebra, superfields and Lagrangians

In terms of the complex supercharges
\mathcal{Q}=Q_{1}+iQ_{2}
and
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}=Q_{1}-iQ_{2}

\mathcal{Q}^{2}\penalty\ ,\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{2}=0\penalty\ ,\qquad\{\mathcal{Q},\overline{\mathcal{Q}}\}=2(H+Z)\penalty\ .

Here

For our purpose, it will be convenient to work in terms of the component fields of supermultiplets. Let us denote a generic supersymmetry variation by:

\delta=\delta_{\lunateepsilon}+\delta_{\overline{\lunateepsilon}}=i\lunateepsilon\mathcal{Q}-i\overline{\lunateepsilon}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}\penalty\ ,

(2.2)

A general, unconstrained supermultiplet contains two bosonic and two fermionic components:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lunateepsilon\left(\partial_{t}C-iv\right)\penalty\ ,

We are most interested in the matter multiplets, called chiral and fermi multiplets, and in the vector multiplets under which the matter can be charged.

Vector multiplet.
The 1d

\mathcal{V}=(A_{t},\sigma,\lambda,\overline{\lambda},D)\penalty\ .

(2.5)

Their supersymmetry transformations in Wess-Zumino (WZ) gauge read:

\displaystyle\delta A_{t}=i\lunateepsilon\overline{\lambda}+i\overline{\lunateepsilon}\lambda\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle\delta\sigma=i\lunateepsilon\overline{\lambda}+i\overline{\lunateepsilon}\lambda\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle\delta\lambda=\lunateepsilon\left(D_{t}\sigma-iD\right)\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle\delta\overline{\lambda}=\overline{\lunateepsilon}\left(D_{t}\sigma+iD\right)\penalty\ ,

\delta(A_{t}-\sigma)=0\penalty\ .

(2.7)

Chiral and fermi multiplets.
The 1d
\mathcal{N}=2
chiral multiplet consists of a complex scalar and a complex fermion,

\Phi=(\phi,\psi)\penalty\ ,\qquad\overline{\Phi}=(\overline{\phi},\overline{\psi})\penalty\ .

(2.9)

The 1d
\mathcal{N}=2

\mathbb{H}=(\eta,G)\penalty\ ,\qquad\overline{\mathbb{H}}=(\overline{\eta},\overline{G})\penalty\ .

Each fermi multiplet comes equipped with two holomorphic functions of the chiral multiplets,

The GLSM action.
The off-shell supersymmetric action of the 1d GLSM,

S=\int dt\,L_{\rm GLSM}\penalty\ ,

(2.16)

is given in term of a sum of supersymmetric Lagrangians:

L_{\rm GLSM}=L_{\mathcal{V}}+L_{\rm FI}+L_{\rm CS}+L_{\overline{\Phi}\Phi}+L_{\overline{\mathbb{H}}\mathbb{H}}+L_{J}+L_{\overline{J}}\penalty\ .

(2.17)

The first three terms govern the vector multiplet. The 1d ‘super-Yang-Mills’ term reads:

L_{\mathcal{V}}={1\over g^{2}}\text{Tr}\left({\frac{1}{2}}D_{t}\sigma D_{t}\sigma-i\overline{\lambda}D_{t}\lambda+{\frac{1}{2}}D^{2}+\overline{\lambda}[\sigma,\lambda]\right)\penalty\ .

(2.18)

Note that the gauge coupling
g^{2}
has mass dimension
[g^{2}]=3
.
The next two terms in (
2.17
) are the Fayet–Iliopoulos and 1d Chern–Simons terms, respectively. They are given schematically by:

L_{\rm FI}=\zeta\,\text{Tr}(D)\penalty\ ,

(2.19)

and

L_{\rm CS}=Q\,\text{Tr}(A_{t}-\sigma)\penalty\ .

(2.20)

More precisely, there will be as many FI parameters
\zeta
and 1d CS levels
Q
as there are distinct abelian factors inside
G
. In this paper we will focus on
G=U(N_{c})
, which has a single abelian factor. Note that
[\zeta]=-1
and
[Q]=0
. The bare CS level
Q
, also known as the bare gauge charge, needs to be integer-quantised:

Q\in\mathbb{Z}\penalty\ .

(2.21)

This ensures that the exponentiated action of the theory on a circle (in Euclidean time) is invariant under large gauge transformations. The existence of a supersymmetric 1d CS term is specific to
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry (in particular, it is not allowed by
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetry). The effect of the bare CS level is to modify Gauss’s law for the
U(1)\subset G
factors:

{\delta S\over\delta A_{t}}={\bf J}_{\rm matter}+Q=0\penalty\ .

(2.22)

Here
{\bf J}_{\rm matter}
is the ordinary
U(1)
gauge charge built out of the matter fields. Note that
A_{t}
enters the 1d theory as an auxiliary field. We will discuss important vacuum contributions to this expectation value in subsection
2.2
.

The matter sector consists of the kinetic terms for the chiral and fermi multiplets:

\displaystyle L_{\overline{\Phi}\Phi}

\displaystyle=D_{t}\overline{\phi}D_{t}\phi-\overline{\phi}(\sigma^{2}+D)\phi-i\overline{\psi}(D_{t}-i\sigma)\psi+i\sqrt{2}\overline{\phi}\lambda\psi-i\sqrt{2}\overline{\psi}\overline{\lambda}\phi\penalty\ ,

(2.23)

\displaystyle L_{\overline{\mathbb{H}}\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle=-i\overline{\eta}(D_{t}+i\sigma)\eta+\overline{G}G-\overline{E}E+\overline{\psi}_{E}\eta+\overline{\eta}\psi_{E}\penalty\ ,

where the vector-multiplet fields act on the chiral and fermi multiplets in their respective gauge representation, and with the overall traces left implicit. Finally, we have the
J
-terms:

L_{J}+L_{\overline{J}}=-GJ+\eta\psi_{J}-\overline{G}\overline{J}-\overline{\eta}\overline{\psi}_{\overline{J}}\penalty\ ,

(2.24)

with
\psi_{J}
defined similarly to
\psi_{E}
above.
Integrating out the auxiliary fields
G
,
\overline{G}
, the full action for the fermi multiplets becomes

L_{\rm fermi}=-i\overline{\eta}(D_{t}+i\sigma)\eta-\overline{E}E-\overline{J}J+\overline{\psi}_{E}\eta+\overline{\eta}\psi_{E}+\eta\psi_{J}+\overline{\psi}_{\overline{J}}\overline{\eta}\penalty\ ,

(2.25)

which exhibits the symmetry (
2.15
). We also have the standard
D
-term equations

D=\overline{\phi}\phi-\zeta\penalty\ ,

(2.26)

schematically. The scalar potential thus reads:

V=|E|^{2}+|J|^{2}+{{\frac{1}{2}}}D^{2}\penalty\ ,

(2.27)

and therefore supersymmetric ground states must satisfy
E=J=D=0
.

2.2
Quantising free superparticles and one-loop determinants

Consider a GLSM with chiral multiplets
\Phi^{i}
and fermi multiplets
\mathbb{H}^{I}
of charges
q_{i}
and
q_{I}
, respectively, under some
G=U(1)
vector multiplet (without much loss of generality). Here we first consider the free chiral and fermi multiplets in a supersymmetric background for the vector multiplet. The dynamics of the vector multiplet itself will be discussed later.

2.2.1
Canonical quantisation of chiral and fermi multiplets

We consider the supersymmetric background for the abelian vector multiplet:

A_{t}\pm\sigma=\text{constant}\penalty\ ,\qquad\lambda=\overline{\lambda}=0\penalty\ ,\qquad D=0\penalty\ .

(2.28)

The canonical momenta for the complex scalar and fermions is defined as
\Pi_{\varphi}={\delta S\over\delta D_{t}\varphi}
, hence we have:

\Pi_{\phi^{i}}=D_{t}\overline{\phi}_{i}\penalty\ ,\qquad\overline{\Pi}_{\overline{\phi}_{i}}=D_{t}\phi^{i}\penalty\ ,\qquad\Pi_{\psi^{i}}=-i\overline{\psi}_{i}\penalty\ ,\qquad\Pi_{\eta^{I}}=-i\overline{\eta}_{I}\penalty\ .

(2.29)

where we used the indices
i,j,...
and
I,J,...
for the chiral and fermi multiplets, respectively.
In our conventions, the canonical commutation relations are:

[\Pi_{\phi^{i}},\phi^{j}]=i\delta_{i}^{j}\penalty\ ,\qquad\quad[\overline{\Pi}_{\overline{\phi}_{i}},\overline{\phi}_{j}]=i\delta_{j}^{i}\penalty\ ,\qquad\quad\{\overline{\psi}_{i},\psi^{j}\}=\delta_{i}^{j}\penalty\ ,\qquad\quad\{\overline{\eta}_{I},\eta^{J}\}=\delta_{I}^{J}\penalty\ .

(2.30)

Note that this corresponds to the Heisenberg equations:

D_{t}\mathcal{O}=-i[{\bf H},\mathcal{O}]\penalty\ ,

(2.31)

and to weighting the path integral by the factor
e^{-iS}
. Here the Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle\bf{\displaystyle H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left(\Pi_{\overline{\phi}_{i}}\overline{\Pi}_{\phi^{i}}+\overline{\phi}_{i}(q_{i}\sigma)^{2}\phi^{i}-\psi_{i}(q_{i}\sigma)\overline{\psi}^{i}\right)

(2.32)

\displaystyle+\sum_{I}\left(\eta^{I}(q_{I}\sigma)\overline{\eta}_{I}+|E_{I}|^{2}+|J^{I}|^{2}+\psi_{J_{I}}\eta^{I}+\psi_{E^{I}}\overline{\eta}_{I}+\overline{\eta}_{I}\overline{\psi}_{\overline{J}^{I}}+\eta^{I}\overline{\psi}_{\overline{E}_{I}}\right)\penalty\ .

The electric charge (that is, the background
U(1)
gauge charge) is given by:

{\bf J}=\sum_{i}q_{i}\left(i\overline{\Pi}_{\overline{\phi}_{i}}\overline{\phi}_{i}-i\Pi_{\phi^{i}}\phi^{i}-\overline{\psi}_{i}\psi^{i}\right)+\sum_{I}q_{I}\,\eta^{I}\overline{\eta}_{I}\penalty\ .

(2.33)

Note that we have
[{\bf J},\mathcal{O}]=q[\mathcal{O}]\mathcal{O}
. Finally, we have the supercharges:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=-\sqrt{2}\sum_{i}\left(\Pi_{\phi^{i}}\psi^{i}-i\overline{\phi}^{i}(q_{i}\sigma)\psi^{i}\right)-\sqrt{2}\sum_{I}\left(i\overline{\eta}_{I}\overline{J}^{I}+i\eta^{I}\overline{E}_{I}\right)\penalty\ ,

(2.34)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Q}}=-\sqrt{2}\sum_{i}\left(\overline{\psi}_{i}\overline{\Pi}_{\overline{\phi}_{i}}+i\overline{\psi}_{i}(q_{i}\sigma)\phi^{i}\right)-\sqrt{2}\sum_{I}\left(-iJ_{I}\eta^{I}-i\overline{\eta}_{I}E^{I}\right)\penalty\ ,

which commute with both
{\bf H}
and
{\bf J}
.
These operators realise the on-shell supersymmetry algebra:

\mathcal{Q}^{2}=0\penalty\ ,\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{2}=0\penalty\ ,\qquad\{Q,\overline{Q}\}=2\left({\bf H}-\sigma{\bf J}\right)\penalty\ .

(2.35)

Note that the condition (
2.13
) is necessary for the supercharges to be nilpotent. The supersymmetric ground states are annihilated by both supercharges. They contribute to the Witten index (
1.3
), which now reads:

{\bf I}_{\rm W}=\text{Tr}((-1)^{\rm F}x^{\bf J})\penalty\ ,

(2.36)

with
x
acting as a fugacity for the electric charge. In the above, the normal-ordering constants in
{\bf H}
and
{\bf J}
were fixed by supersymmetry together with our conventions for defining the ground states, which we now explain.

Quantising the chiral multiplet.
Consider a single chiral multiplet
\Phi
of
U(1)
charge
q
, with the supercharges, gauge charge and Hamiltonian given as above. Assuming
E=J=0
for now, this is a free theory — we simply have bosonic and fermionic harmonic oscillators related by supersymmetry.
The two harmonic oscillators from the complex scalar
\phi
are best expressed in terms of ladder operators:

\displaystyle\phi={1\over\sqrt{2\omega}}(a^{\dagger}+b)\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}={1\over\sqrt{2\omega}}(b^{\dagger}+a)\penalty\ ,

(2.37)

\displaystyle\pi_{\phi}=i\sqrt{\omega\over 2}(a-b^{\dagger})\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle\overline{\pi}_{\overline{\phi}}=i\sqrt{\omega\over 2}(b-a^{\dagger})\penalty\ ,

with the non-trivial commutators being

[a,a^{\dagger}]=1\penalty\ ,\qquad\qquad[b,b^{\dagger}]=1\penalty\ .

(2.38)

Here we defined the frequency:

\omega=|q\sigma|\penalty\ .

(2.39)

Note that we have the electric charges
{\bf J}=-1,1,1,-1
for
a
,
a^{\dagger}
,
b
, and
b^{\dagger}
, respectively. In these variables, the supercharges take the form:

\mathcal{Q}=\begin{cases}2i{\sqrt{\omega}}\,b^{\dagger}\psi\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q\sigma>0\penalty\ ,\\
-2i{\sqrt{\omega}}\,a\psi\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q\sigma<0\penalty\ ,\\
\end{cases}\qquad\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Q}}=\begin{cases}-2i{\sqrt{\omega}}\,b\overline{\psi}\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q\sigma>0\penalty\ ,\\
2i{\sqrt{\omega}}\,a^{\dagger}\overline{\psi}\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q\sigma<0\penalty\ ,\\
\end{cases}

(2.40)

which depends on the sign of the real mass
q\sigma
. The Hamiltonian and electric charge become:

{\bf H}=|q\sigma|(a^{\dagger}a+b^{\dagger}b+1)-q\sigma\psi\overline{\psi}\penalty\ ,\qquad{\bf J}=q(a^{\dagger}a-b^{\dagger}b-\overline{\psi}\psi)\penalty\ .

(2.41)

Choosing the supersymmetric vacuum to be annihilated by
a
and
b

q\sigma
. Namely, we define a supersymmetric vacuum
\lvert 0\rangle_{\pm}
for
\sign{(q\sigma)}=\pm
that satisfy:

a\lvert 0\rangle_{\pm}=0\penalty\ ,\qquad b\lvert 0\rangle_{\pm}=0\penalty\ ,\qquad\begin{cases}\psi\lvert 0\rangle_{+}=0\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q\sigma>0\penalty\ ,\\
\overline{\psi}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=0\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q\sigma<0\penalty\ ,\end{cases}

(2.42)

so that
\lvert 0\rangle_{\pm}
is annihilated by both supercharges. Note that we chose the normal-ordering of the Hamiltonian such that:

{\bf H}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}=0\penalty\ ,\qquad{\bf J}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}=0\penalty\ ,\qquad(-1)^{\rm F}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}=\lvert 0\rangle_{+}\penalty\ ,

(2.43)

for
q\sigma>0
. The ground state
\lvert 0\rangle_{-}
for
q\sigma<0
will then necessarily have non-zero energy and charge, as well as non-trivial fermion parity:

{\bf H}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=|q\sigma|\lvert 0\rangle_{-}\penalty\ ,\qquad{\bf J}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=-q\lvert 0\rangle_{-}\penalty\ ,\qquad(-1)^{\rm F}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=-\lvert 0\rangle_{-}\penalty\ .

(2.44)

We still have

({\bf H}-\sigma{\bf J})\lvert 0\rangle_{\pm}=0\penalty\ ,

(2.45)

consistently with supersymmetry.
The non-trivial vacuum charges (
2.44
) are entirely due to the fermion; this phenomenon is generally known as a ‘parity anomaly’ — in quantum mechanics, it is rather a ‘charge conjugation anomaly’
Elitzur:1985xj
. In the path integral language, this is related to the statement that, when integrating out a free 1d fermion with the real mass
q\sigma
, we obtain an empty theory for
q\sigma>0
but generate a non-trivial contact terms (
2.20
) with
Q=-1
for
q\sigma<0
.

The supersymmetric ground states of the chiral multiplet take the form:

(a^{\dagger})^{s}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}\penalty\ ,\qquad\qquad(b^{\dagger})^{t}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}\penalty\ ,\qquad\text{with }s,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\penalty\ ,

(2.46)

for
q\sigma>0

q\sigma<0

(a^{\dagger})^{s}(b^{\dagger})^{t}\overline{\psi}^{f}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}
for
q\sigma>0

. Then we have
{\bf H}=(s+t+f)q\sigma
and
{\bf J}=q(s-t-f)
, and supersymmetry requires
{\bf H}-\sigma{\bf J}=2q\sigma(t+f)=0
which implies

2.36
) then takes the form:

{\bf I}_{\rm W}=1+x^{q}+x^{2q}+x^{3q}+\cdots=\sum_{s\geqslant 0}x^{qs}={1\over 1-x^{q}}\penalty\ .

(2.47)

Similarly, if
q\sigma<0
we have the states
(a^{\dagger})^{s}(b^{\dagger})^{t}\psi^{f}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}
with
{\bf H}=(s+t+f+1)|q\sigma|

{\bf H}-\sigma{\bf J}=2|q\sigma|(s+f)=0

The Witten index now reads:

{\bf I}_{\rm W}=-x^{-q}\left(1+x^{-q}+x^{-2q}+x^{-3q}+\cdots\right)=-x^{-q}\sum_{t\geqslant 0}x^{-tq}={1\over 1-x^{q}}\penalty\ ,

(2.48)

where the overall factor in the first equality is the contribution from the vacuum
\lvert 0\rangle_{-}
as given in (

2.47

Quantising the fermi multiplet.
Consider now a single fermi multiplet
\mathbb{H}
of
U(1)

q
, with
E=J=0

L_{\rm fermi}=-i\overline{\eta}(D_{t}+i\sigma)\eta
, with vanishing supercharges and with:

{\bf H}=\eta(q\sigma)\overline{\eta}\penalty\ ,\qquad\qquad{\bf J}=q\eta\overline{\eta}\penalty\ ,

(2.49)

so that
{\bf H}-\sigma{\bf J}=0
identically–hence, there are only two states by the Fermi exclusion principle, and they are all supersymmetric ground states. We choose to quantise the theory according to the sign of the real mass:

\begin{cases}\overline{\eta}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}=0\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q\sigma>0\penalty\ ,\\
\eta\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=0\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q\sigma<0\penalty\ ,\end{cases}

(2.50)

which gives us
{\bf H}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}=0
and
{\bf J}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}=0
, but also:

{\bf H}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=q\sigma\lvert 0\rangle_{-}\penalty\ ,\qquad{\bf J}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=q\lvert 0\rangle_{-}\penalty\ ,\qquad(-1)^{\rm F}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=-\lvert 0\rangle_{-}\penalty\ .

(2.51)

Thus, for
q\sigma>0
, we have the ground states
\eta^{f}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}
for
f=0,1
, while for
q\sigma<0
we have the ground states
\overline{\eta}^{f}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}
for

{\bf I}_{\rm W}=1-x^{q}=(-x^{q})(1-x^{-q})\penalty\ .

(2.52)

2.2.2
Witten index, path integral and parity anomaly

For the general set of free chiral multiplets
\Phi^{i}
and fermi multiplets
\mathbb{H}^{I}
charged under some background

{\bf I}_{\rm W}=x^{Q}\prod_{i}{1\over 1-x^{q_{i}}}\,\prod_{I}(1-x^{q_{I}})\penalty\ ,

(2.53)

where we allow for a bare CS level
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
.
This can also be computed in the path integral formalism, as a partition function of the 1d model on the circle. This can be done using the Euclidean time
\tau=-i\tau
(with
\tau\sim\tau+2\pi
), which gives us the Euclidean path integral weighted
e^{-S_{E}}
with:

S_{E}=\int d\tau\left(\sum_{i}\left(D_{\tau}\overline{\phi_{i}}D_{\tau}\phi^{i}+\overline{\phi}_{i}(q_{i}\sigma)^{2}\phi^{i}+\overline{\psi}_{i}(D_{\tau}+q_{i}\sigma)\psi^{i}\right)+\sum_{I}\overline{\eta}^{I}(D_{\tau}+q_{I}\sigma)\psi_{I}\right)\penalty\ .

The Witten index is then computed as the Gaussian path integral on the circle:

{\bf I}_{\rm W}=Z_{S^{1}}=\prod_{i}{\det(D_{\tau}+q_{i}\sigma)\over\det(-D_{\tau}^{2}+(q_{i}\sigma)^{2})}\,\prod_{I}\det(D_{\tau}-q_{I}\sigma)={\prod_{I}\det(D_{\tau}-q_{I}\sigma)\over\prod_{i}\det(-D_{\tau}+q_{i}\sigma)}\penalty\ ,

(2.54)

with periodic boundary conditions for the fermions. Let us define the complexified mass:

u\equiv-A_{\tau}+i\sigma=-i(A_{t}-\sigma)\penalty\ ,

(2.55)

and the fugacity:

x\equiv e^{2\pi iu}=\exp{\left(-i\int d\tau(A_{\tau}-i\sigma)\right)}\penalty\ .

(2.56)

Large gauge transformation along the circle corresponds to
u\rightarrow u+1
.
We immediately notice that the 1d CS contact term (
2.20

to the result — this is a contact term for the background gauge field that we are free to add, with the constraint that

to preserve background gauge invariance. We still have to compute the infinite products that appear in (
2.54

Z_{\eta}(x)\equiv\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(n+u)=\exp\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\log(n+u)\right)=\exp\left(c_{0}+c_{1}u+\log(\sin(\pi u))\right)\penalty\ .

(2.57)

A classic
\zeta

formally converges to

Further comments on 1d CS levels and parity anomaly.
Our conventions for quantising 1d fermions can be summarised as follows. In the UV, which corresponds to having massless chiral and fermi multiplets, we have an effective background charge:

q_{\rm uv}=\langle{\bf J}\rangle_{\rm uv}=\langle Q+{\bf J}_{\rm matter}\rangle_{\rm uv}=Q-{\frac{1}{2}}\sum q_{i}+{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{I}q_{I}\penalty\ .

(2.59)

The classical contribution
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
from (
2.20
) is supplemented by one-loop effects from the massless fermions
\psi_{i}
and
\eta_{I}
(running in a tadpole diagram). Note that the combination
q_{\rm uv}
is the one that is generally called ‘the 1d CS level’ of the theory–we call it UV effective CS level to distinguish it from the bare (classical) level
Q
. The two parameters
q_{\rm uv}
and
Q
are simply related by (
2.59
), and we shall characterise our theory using one or the other depending on convenience. In the actual GLSM, the 1d CS parameter
q_{\rm uv}
or
Q
is part of the definition of the theory. In particular, it directly affects Gauss’s law (
2.22
).

Once we turn on
\sigma\neq 0
, we have the two choices of vacua
\lvert 0\rangle_{\pm}
for
\sign(\sigma)=\pm
, with:

\displaystyle\sigma>0\;:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\psi^{i}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}=0\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q_{i}>0\penalty\ ,\\
\overline{\psi}_{i}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}=0\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q_{i}<0\penalty\ ,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\begin{cases}\overline{\eta}_{I}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}=0\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q_{I}>0\penalty\ ,\\
\eta^{I}\lvert 0\rangle_{+}=0\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q_{I}<0\penalty\ ,\end{cases}

(2.60)

\displaystyle\sigma<0\;:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\overline{\psi}_{i}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=0\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q_{i}>0\penalty\ ,\\
\psi^{i}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=0\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q_{i}<0\penalty\ ,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\begin{cases}\eta^{I}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=0\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q_{I}>0\penalty\ ,\\
\overline{\eta}_{I}\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=0\qquad&\text{if}\;\;q_{I}<0\penalty\ .\end{cases}

In these vacua, we then have:

\langle{\bf J}\rangle_{\pm}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\,\text{such that}\\
\sign(q_{i})=\mp\end{subarray}}q_{i}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\,\text{such that}\\
\sign(q_{I})=\mp\end{subarray}}q_{I}\penalty\ .

(2.61)

The fact that this is an integral charge is important, since this computes the 1d CS level

Q_{\rm ir}=\langle Q+{\bf J}\rangle_{\pm}\in\mathbb{Z}

(2.62)

in the empty effective theory in the infrared.
The statement of the parity anomaly is that
q_{\rm uv}\neq 0
, in general. However, if we are willing to choose
Q
half-integer, one could pick

q_{\rm uv}=0\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad Q=Q^{\rm HKY}\equiv{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i}q_{i}-{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{I}q_{I}\penalty\ ,

(2.63)

in which case we have

\langle Q^{\rm HKY}+{\bf J}\rangle_{\pm}={\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i}\sign(q_{i}\sigma)q_{i}-{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i}\sign(q_{I}\sigma)q_{I}\penalty\ ,

(2.64)

which matches the quantisation condition generally chosen in the literature
Hori:2014tda
, although this expectation value is not an integer in general. Indeed (
2.63
) is the only parity-preserving quantisation, but it is not the one we shall use. We prefer a parity-violating quantisation which preserves gauge invariance.

Effective theories for the abelian vector multiplet

\mathcal{F}^{0}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\mathcal{F}^{0}_{\rm phys}=\left\{\lvert\Psi\rangle\in\mathcal{F}^{0}\;|\;{\bf J}\lvert\Psi\rangle=0\right\}\penalty\ .

2.

For each would-be ground state obtained in the first step, write down the effective theory for the scalar
\sigma
and the gaugino
\lambda,\overline{\lambda}
, and determine whether the exact ground state of that effective theory actually exists.

3.

Add in the effect of the
E
- and
J
-term interactions. This can be challenging to do, in general. In our examples, we will treat these interactions in perturbation theory.

This three-step procedure allows us to obtain Coulomb-branch states which are candidates for the true ground states of the GLSM. In general, however, the true ground states are fewer than the number of perturbative ground states obtained from this Coulomb-branch analysis. In simpler settings without gauge interactions, one also finds that perturbative ground states of a SQM with a scalar potential can be lifted by non-perturbative effects mediated by instantons
Witten:1982im
. A completely analogous phenomenon occurs here, as we shall see in later sections.

For now, let us focus on the perturbative ground states of the effective field theory for the Coulomb-branch scalar. This theory takes the form
Witten:1981nf
;
Hori:2014tda
:

L_{\rm eff}={\frac{1}{2}}\dot{\sigma}^{2}-i\overline{\lambda}\dot{\lambda}-{\frac{1}{2}}(h^{\prime}(\sigma))^{2}+h^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)\overline{\lambda}\lambda\penalty\ ,

(2.67)

with
h^{\prime}(\sigma)\equiv{dh\over d\sigma}
. Here the real superpotential
h(\sigma)
is obtained at one loop, as we will review momentarily, giving us the real potential
U={\frac{1}{2}}(h^{\prime})^{2}
. We also rescaled the fields
\sigma
,
\lambda
and
\overline{\lambda}
by a factor of
g
. The supersymmetry transformations read:

\displaystyle\delta\sigma=i\lunateepsilon\overline{\lambda}+i\overline{\lunateepsilon}\lambda\penalty\ ,\qquad

\displaystyle\delta\lambda=\lunateepsilon\left(\dot{\sigma}-i{h^{\prime}(\sigma)}\right)\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle\delta\overline{\lambda}=\overline{\lunateepsilon}\left(\dot{\sigma}+i{h^{\prime}(\sigma)}\right)\penalty\ .

(2.68)

Canonical quantisation gives us the momentum
\Pi_{\sigma}=\dot{\sigma}
and the commutation relations:

[\Pi_{\sigma},\sigma]=i\penalty\ ,\qquad\quad\{\lambda,\overline{\lambda}\}=1\penalty\ .

(2.69)

The supercharges and the Hamiltonian are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=-i(\Pi_{\sigma}-i{h^{\prime}(\sigma)})\overline{\lambda}\penalty\ ,\qquad\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Q}}=i(\Pi_{\sigma}+i{h^{\prime}(\sigma)})\lambda\penalty\ ,

(2.70)

\displaystyle\bf{\displaystyle H}={\frac{1}{2}}\Pi_{\sigma}^{2}+{\frac{1}{2}}({h^{\prime}(\sigma)})^{2}-{\frac{1}{2}}{h^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)}(\overline{\lambda}\lambda-\lambda\overline{\lambda})\penalty\ .

Let us define:

\Sigma(\sigma)=e^{h(\sigma)}\penalty\ ,\qquad\quad\widetilde{\Sigma}(\sigma)=e^{-h(\sigma)}\penalty\ ,

(2.71)

which are the wavefunctions satisfying the first-order equations:

\left({d\over d\sigma}-h^{\prime}\right)\Sigma=0\penalty\ ,\qquad\quad\left({d\over d\sigma}+h^{\prime}\right)\widetilde{\Sigma}=0\penalty\ ,

(2.72)

respectively. Let us choose
\lambda
to be the fermionic annihilation operator, so it annihilates any ground state. Then, we find the two candidate supersymmetric ground states
\lvert\Sigma\rangle
and
\overline{\lambda}\lvert\widetilde{\Sigma}\rangle
— indeed, they are annihilated by both supercharges. Last but not least, we must check whether these states are square-normalisable. While this might depend on the details of the superpotential, in the case at hand we should only trust
U(\sigma)
near critical points (that is, near
\sigma=\sigma_{\ast}
so that
h^{\prime}(\sigma_{\ast})=0
) or, more generally, for energies
U<M_{C}
.
Performing a local analysis near a single critical point, one finds that:

\displaystyle{\lvert\Sigma\rangle}\;\text{ is normalisable}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}(\sigma_{\ast})}<0\penalty\ ,

(2.73)

\displaystyle{\overline{\lambda}\lvert\widetilde{\Sigma}\rangle}\;\text{ is normalisable}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}(\sigma_{\ast})}>0\penalty\ .

This gives us a good handle (or, least, as good a handle as one can hope for) on the perturbative ground states in the
U(1)
gauge theory.

Finally, let us discuss the explicit form of the superpotential obtained by integrating out the chiral multiplets. In a given state
\lvert\Psi\rangle\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm phys}^{0}
, we define the positive integers
N_{\phi_{i}}
counting the number of
a_{i}^{\dagger}
or
b_{i}^{\dagger}
oscillators:

{\bf N}_{\phi_{i}}\lvert\Psi\rangle=N_{\phi_{i}}\lvert\Psi\rangle\penalty\ ,\qquad\qquad{\bf N}_{\phi_{i}}\equiv a_{i}^{\dagger}a_{i}+b_{i}^{\dagger}b_{i}+1\penalty\ .

(2.74)

In other words, we have

N_{\phi_{i}}=s_{i}+t_{i}+1

(2.75)

if
\lvert\Psi\rangle
is obtained using the creation operators
(a_{i}^{\dagger})^{s_{i}}(b_{i}^{\dagger})^{t_{i}}
.
A one-loop computation then gives us the superpotential:

h(\sigma)=-\widetilde{\zeta}\sigma+{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i}N_{\phi_{i}}q_{i}\sign(q_{i}\sigma+m_{i})\log|q_{i}\sigma+m_{i}|\penalty\ ,

(2.76)

and hence the scalar potential:

U(\sigma)={\frac{1}{2}}\left(-\widetilde{\zeta}+\sum_{i}{q_{i}N_{\phi_{i}}\over 2|q_{i}\sigma+m_{i}|}\right)^{2}\penalty\ .

(2.77)

Here we introduced the rescaled FI parameter
\widetilde{\zeta}\equiv g\zeta=\sqrt{M_{C}}
. Note that this shape of the potential is only reliable for
U(\sigma)<M_{C}
. The divergence in the potential energy near the loci
q_{i}\sigma+m_{i}=0
has a simple and standard physical explanation — these are the loci at which chiral multiplets become massless and therefore where the effective description is not reliable.

\displaystyle(E_{\varGamma^{\prime\prime}})^{k}_{j}={{\widetilde{\Phi}}{}^{\prime\prime}}^{k}_{\beta}{\Phi^{\prime\prime}}^{\beta}_{j}-{\partial(J_{\mathbb{H}})^{k}_{\alpha}\over\partial\widetilde{\Phi}^{j}_{\alpha}}\penalty\ ,\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle(J_{\varGamma^{\prime\prime}})^{j}_{k}=-M^{j}_{i}{\partial(E_{\mathbb{H}})^{\alpha}_{k}\over\partial\Phi^{\alpha}_{i}}\penalty\ ,

(3.26)

\displaystyle(E_{\mathbb{H}^{\prime\prime}})^{\beta}_{i}={{\widetilde{\Phi}}{}^{\prime\prime}}^{b}_{j}M^{j}_{i}\penalty\ ,\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle(J_{\mathbb{H}^{\prime\prime}})^{i}_{\beta}={\partial(E_{\mathbb{H}})^{\alpha}_{k}\over\partial\Phi^{\alpha}_{i}}{\widetilde{\Phi}}^{{}^{\prime\prime}k}_{\beta}\penalty\ .

Supersymmetry is ensured by the same condition (
3.19
) as for the right mutation.

Finally, note that the right and left mutations are simply related to each other by a CP transformation of the full theory -- that is, the right mutation of the original SQCD theory gives us the same theory as the one obtained by a left mutation of the CP-conjugated SQCD theory.
11
11

11

A CP transformation is equivalent to a time-reversal transformation of the 1d
\mathcal{N}=2
gauge theory. It simply amounts to flipping the sign of all the gauge and flavour vector superfields simultaneously.
Moreover, the two mutation dualities are inverse of each other: a right mutation followed by a left mutation lands us back on the original theory.

4
Ground states and dualities from the Higgs branch

For large FI parameter, the SQCD theory is a 1d GLSM that flows to a supersymmetric NLSM onto the Grassmannian manifold. In this section, we describe the supersymmetric ground states from this geometric perspective. This leads to an explicit expansion of the Witten index in terms of characters of the flavour group. It also nicely explains the mutation duality presented above as originating simply from the Grassmannian duality:

{\rm Gr}(N_{c},n_{1})\cong{\rm Gr}(n_{1}-N_{c},n_{1})\penalty\ .

(4.1)

We note that such a geometric perspective was first proposed in
Jia:2014ffa
to explain the 2d
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
triality. Here we extend this approach to the more general target geometry allowed by our
\mathcal{N}=2
SQM.

4.1
Ground states from sheaf cohomology for positive FI parameter

Consider the case of large positive FI parameter,
\zeta>0
, with
n_{1}\geqslant N_{c}
. Then we have a target space

\mathcal{E}_{+}\longrightarrow X_{+}\penalty\ ,\qquad\qquad X_{+}={\rm Gr}(N_{c},n_{1})\penalty\ ,

(4.2)

where
X_{+}
is the complex Grassmannian of
N_{c}
-planes inside
\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{+}
is a particular vector bundle over
X_{+}
to be discussed momentarily.
By abuse of notation, we may call either
X_{+}
or the total space of the bundle
\mathcal{E}_{+}
the ‘Higgs branch’ of the GLSM, by contrast to the Coulomb-branch description that we will consider in later sections. By standard arguments, the supersymmetric ground states
\lvert\Psi\rangle
are in one-to-one correspondence with the sheaf cohomology classes:

\lvert\Psi\rangle\qquad\longleftrightarrow\qquad[\Psi]\in H^{\bullet}(X_{+},\mathcal{E}_{+})\penalty\ .

(4.3)

Using the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem, we can explicitly compute this cohomology for any
GL(n_{1},\mathbb{C})
-equivariant bundle
fulton2013representation
;
weyman2003cohomology
.

The vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{+}
for
\varGamma
-SQCD.
The geometry (
4.2

\varGamma
-SQCD, in which case the superpotential interactions render the bosonic target space effectively compact. Recall that the rank-
N_{c}
tautological bundle
\mathcal{S}
and the rank-
(n_{1}-N_{c})
quotient bundle
\mathcal{Q}
over the Grassmannian
X_{+}
are related by the Euler sequence:

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}\longrightarrow 0\penalty\ .

(4.4)

Recall that the constant modes of the fundamental chiral multiplets are essentially the homogeneous coordinates of
X_{+}
; they form an
N_{c}\times n_{1}
matrix
(\phi^{\alpha}_{i})
which, up to
{\rm GL}(N_{c})
transformations (
i.e.
complexified gauge transformations), parameterise points on
X_{+}
. The gauge theory construct

as the Kähler quotient

X_{+}\cong\mathbb{C}^{N_{c}n_{1}}/\!/_{\zeta}\,U(N_{c})\penalty\ .

(4.5)

To understand the bundle
\mathcal{E}_{+}
, we must understand the geometric interpretation of the
n_{2}
antifundamental fields
\widetilde{\phi}^{j}_{\alpha}
, of the
n_{3}
fundamental fermi multiplets
\mathbb{H}^{\alpha}_{k}
, and of the integer 1d Chern–Simons level
Q_{c}
. The effect of the latter is most readily explained: it modifies Gauss’s law, which allows states that are generated by homogenous polynomials in the
\phi^{i}_{\alpha}

12
12

12

More precisely, they are polynomials in the
SU(N_{c})

The net effect is to introduce a factor of
\det(\mathcal{S})^{Q_{c}}

\mathcal{E}_{+}
. The gauge variables

that appear in the localisation formula (
3.27

\mathcal{S}
, and we then have the Chern character:

{\rm ch}(\det(\mathcal{S}))=\det(x)\equiv\prod_{\alpha=1}^{N_{c}}x_{\alpha}\penalty\ .

(4.6)

Next, we note that the fundamental fermi multiplets
\mathbb{H}^{\alpha}_{j}
are valued in the tautological vector bundle, by their very definition. Their quantisation with
\zeta>0
leads to a fermionic Fock space generated by the modes
\mathbb{H}^{\alpha}_{j}
, in our conventions.
We then have a factor

\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathcal{S}^{\oplus n_{3}})=\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n_{3}})\penalty\ .

(4.7)

in the bundle
\mathcal{E}_{+}
. Here
\Lambda^{\bullet}F
denotes the full exterior algebra of a vector bundle
F
, and this should be understood in K-theory — namely, we are taking into account formal signs keeping track of the exterior-algebra degree.

The role played by the antifundamental chiral multiplets when
\zeta>0
is more subtle. Considered in isolation, the fields
\widetilde{\phi}^{j}=(\phi^{j}_{\alpha})
are sections of the dual tautological vector bundle
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
, and the
\varGamma^{j}_{i}

\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}n_{2}}

In
\varGamma
-SQCD, the antifundamental chiral multiplets
\widetilde{\phi}^{j}_{\alpha}

(E_{\varGamma})^{j}_{i}=\widetilde{\phi}^{j}_{\alpha}\phi^{\alpha}_{i}=0

(4.8)

on the constant modes. The full interactions can be deduced from the superpotential (

W=\phi^{\alpha}_{i}\overline{\varGamma}^{i}_{j}\widetilde{\phi}^{j}_{\alpha}\penalty\ .

(4.9)

On the Grassmannian, the
N_{c}\times n_{1}

\phi^{\alpha}_{i}
has rank-
N_{c}

of maximal rank, the superpontential (

\widetilde{\phi}^{j}_{\alpha}
and to all the gauge-neutral fermions
\varGamma^{j}_{i}
that are parallel to the
N_{c}

\phi^{i}_{\alpha}

N_{c}
distinct vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}}

\varGamma^{i}_{j}
orthogonal to the

\mathcal{S}

\varGamma

(n_{1}-N_{c})

\mathbb{C}^{N_{c}}
plane corresponding to

. That means that the massless
\varGamma
fields are valued in
\mathcal{Q}^{\ast}

\mathcal{Q}
defined by (

13

13

That we have

In summary, for
\varGamma
-SQCD with positive FI parameter, we have a target space which is the total space of the vector bundle

over the Grassmannian, with:

\mathcal{E}_{+}=\det(\mathcal{S})^{Q_{c}}\otimes\Lambda^{\bullet}((\mathcal{Q}^{\ast})^{\oplus n_{2}})\otimes\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathcal{S}^{\oplus n_{3}})\penalty\ .

(4.10)

As mentioned above, we consider
\mathcal{E}_{+}
as a K-theory class, with formal signs introduced when taking the exterior algebra.

Ground states from the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem.

Let us compute the group states (

(4.11)

Then, let

{\rm GL}_{N_{c}}

then decomposes into Schur-functor vector bundles corresponding to irreducible representations of

\mu\in Y(N_{c},n_{3})\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|\lambda|+|\mu|}\,\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mu}(-Q_{c})\otimes S_{\lambda^{T}}(\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}})\otimes S_{\mu^{T}}(\mathbb{C}^{n_{3}})\penalty\ ,

where we defined the vector bundle:

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda,\mu}(-Q_{c})\equiv\det(\mathcal{S})^{Q_{c}}\otimes S_{\lambda}(\mathcal{Q}^{\ast})\otimes S_{\mu}(\mathcal{S})\penalty\ ,

(4.13)

and

denotes the transpose of the Young tableau

\mathcal{E}_{+}
is taken as a K-theory class.

Now, given a pair of weights

\omega=[-Q_{c}-\mu_{N_{c}},-Q_{c}-\mu_{N_{c}-1},\cdots,-Q_{c}-\mu_{1},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n_{1}-N_{c}}]\penalty\ ,

and we denote by

H^{\bullet}(X_{+},\mathcal{E}_{+})=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in Y(n_{1}-N_{c},n_{2})\\
\mu\in Y(N_{c},n_{3})\\
\omega\;\text{regular}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|\lambda|+|\mu|}\,S_{\nu}(\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}})\otimes S_{\lambda^{T}}(\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}})\otimes S_{\mu^{T}}(\mathbb{C}^{n_{3}})\penalty\ .

Here, any given pair of weights

(-1)^{\rm F}=(-1)^{\ell(\sigma)+|\lambda|+|\mu|}

4.1.1
The Witten index from sheaf cohomology

The Witten index of
\varGamma
-SQCD with
\zeta>0
straightforwardly follows from (
4.17
). Indeed, the Witten index (
3.31
) in the geometric phase is simply given by the holomorphic Euler characteristic of the bundle
\mathcal{E}_{+}
:

\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}[N_{c},q_{c},\boldsymbol{n}^{F}]_{+}^{\varGamma}=\chi_{T}(X_{+},\mathcal{E}_{+})\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{{\rm dim}_{T}(X_{+})}(-1)^{k}\,{\rm dim}_{T}\,H^{k}(X_{+},\mathcal{E}_{+})\penalty\ .

(4.18)

More precisely, the flavoured Witten index is given here in terms of the equivariant Euler characteristic
\chi_{T}(X_{+},\mathcal{E}_{+})
, where
T
is a maximal torus for the flavour group (
3.3
), with equivariant parameters
(y_{I})=(y_{1,i},y_{2,j},y_{3,k})
. Given any of the complex vector spaces
S_{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{n_{I}})
appearing in (
4.17
), we define its ‘equivariant dimension’ to be the Schur polynomial
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}
in the
n_{I}
variables
y_{I}
:

{\rm dim}_{T}(S_{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{n_{I}}))\equiv\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}(y_{I})\penalty\ .

(4.19)

Therefore, given the decomposition (
4.17
), we find a completely explicit expression for the Witten index:

{\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}}_{+}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in Y(n_{1}-N_{c},n_{2})\\
\mu\in Y(N_{c},n_{3})\\
\omega\;\text{regular}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|\lambda|+|\mu|+\ell(\sigma)}\,\mathfrak{s}_{\nu}(y_{1}^{-1})\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda^{T}}(y_{2})\mathfrak{s}_{\mu^{T}}(y_{3}^{-1})\penalty\ .

(4.20)

Unlike the Coulomb-branch formula, this gives us the index directly as a polynomial

\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{I}^{\pm}]\penalty\ ,

(4.21)

and it elucidates the exact flavour-symmetry representations spanned by the ground states. Recall also that the non-equivariant Euler characteristic can be written as an integral over
X_{+}
according to the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch theorem:

\chi(X_{+},\mathcal{E}_{+})=\int_{X_{+}}{\rm Td}(X_{+})\wedge{\rm ch}(\mathcal{E}_{+})\penalty\ .

(4.22)

The formula (
4.20
) gives us an explicit expression for this integer:

\chi(X_{+},\mathcal{E}_{+})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in Y(n_{1}-N_{c},n_{2})\\
\mu\in Y(N_{c},n_{3})\\
\omega\;\text{regular}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|\lambda|+|\mu|+\ell(\sigma)}\,\dim(\nu;n_{1})\dim(\lambda^{T};n_{2})\dim(\mu^{T};n_{3})\penalty\ ,

(4.23)

where
\dim(\lambda;n)=\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}(1)
denotes the dimension of the representation
\mathfrak{R}_{\lambda}
of
{\rm GL}_{n}
.

\mathcal{H}^{(Q_{c}\leqslant 0)}\cong H^{0}(X_{+},\det(\mathcal{S})^{Q_{c}})={\rm Sym}^{|Q_{c}|}(\Lambda^{N_{c}}\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}})\penalty\ .

(4.27)

This corresponds to a rectangular Young tableau of size

in the Plücker coordinates of the Grassmannian (modulo the Plücker relations). For

using the fact that the canonical line bundle is given by

. The flavoured Witten index is then given by:

\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{s}_{[(-Q_{c})^{N_{c}},0^{n_{1}-N_{c}}]}(y_{1}^{-1})\qquad\quad&\text{if }Q_{c}\leqslant 0\penalty\ ,\\
0&\text{if }0<Q_{c}<n_{1}\penalty\ ,\\

\end{cases}

(4.30)

where we used the notation
\det(y_{1})\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}y_{1,i}
.

\varGamma
-SQED.
In the abelian case,
N_{c}=1

and
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{O}(-1)

\mathcal{E}_{+}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in Y(n_{1}-N_{c},n_{2})\\

(4.31)

where
\mathcal{F}(n)\equiv\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}(n)

\mu=[p]
, and we have

(4.32)

In this case, the non-flavoured Witten index is well-defined and given by the residue formula:

\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}=-\oint{dx\over 2\pi ix}x^{Q_{c}}{(1-x)^{n_{3}}\over(1-x)^{n_{1}}}\penalty\ ,

where we pick the residue at

Finally, the Euler characteristic

in (

the ‘empty’ ground state at
\sigma>0
, and we then have

\lvert 0\rangle_{-}=\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\overline{\psi}_{1,i}\prod_{k=0}^{n_{3}}\eta_{3,k}\lvert 0\rangle

(5.19)

at
\sigma<0
, generalising (
5.19
).
The Coulomb branch analysis is straightforward in this case because we do not have any superpotential interactions and the supercharges remain the same as in (
5.4
). We then find the free-field ground states:

\mathcal{F}^{0}=\left\{\begin{cases}\lvert s_{1},f_{3}\rangle_{+}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s_{1,i}!}}(a^{{\dagger}}_{1,i})^{s_{1,i}}\prod_{k=0}^{n_{3}}\eta_{3,k}^{f_{3,k}}\lvert 0\rangle\qquad&\text{if }\sigma>0\penalty\ ,\\
\lvert t_{1},f_{3}\rangle_{-}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{1,i}!}}(b^{{\dagger}}_{1,i})^{t_{1,i}}\overline{\psi}_{1,i}\prod_{k=0}^{n_{3}}\eta_{3,k}^{f_{3,k}}\lvert 0\rangle\qquad\quad&\text{if }\sigma<0\penalty\ .\end{cases}\right\}\penalty\ ,

(5.20)

where
f_{3,k}\in\{0,1\}
. Here we used the fact that, for
\sigma<0
, all the oscillators
\eta_{3,k}
should be turned on (in our conventions), but we obviously have that:

\prod_{k=1}^{n_{3}}\overline{\eta}_{3,k}^{\widetilde{f}_{3,k}}\eta_{3,k}\lvert 0\rangle=\prod_{k=0}^{n_{3}}\eta_{3,k}^{f_{3,k}}\lvert 0\rangle\qquad\text{for }\,f_{3,k}=\widetilde{f}_{3,k}+1\;\text{mod }2\penalty\ .

(5.21)

Imposing Gauss’s law gives us the states:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{0}_{\rm phys}=\left\{\begin{cases}\lvert s_{1},f_{3}\rangle_{+}\quad|\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}s_{1,i}+\sum_{k=1}^{n_{3}}f_{3,k}+Q_{c}=0\qquad&\text{if }\sigma>0\penalty\ ,\\
\lvert t_{1},f_{3}\rangle_{-}\quad|\quad-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}t_{1,i}+\sum_{k=1}^{n_{3}}f_{3,k}-n_{1}+Q_{c}=0\quad&\text{if }\sigma<0\penalty\ .\end{cases}\right\}\penalty\ .

(5.22)

The scalar potential
U(\sigma)
is unchanged compared to the
n_{3}=0
case, hence we only find ground states for
\zeta>0
. We thus have
n_{3}+1
distinct instances of the
n_{3}=0
result, with
Q_{c}
shifted to
Q_{c}+p
with:

p\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{n_{3}}f_{3,k}\in\{0,\cdots,n_{3}\}\penalty\ .

(5.23)

The true supersymmetric ground states are therefore

\lvert\Psi\rangle\cong\begin{cases}\lvert\Psi_{+};s_{1},f_{3}\rangle=\lvert s_{1}\rangle_{+}\quad&\text{if }Q_{c}+p\leqslant 0\penalty\ ,\\
\lvert\Psi_{+};t_{1},f_{3}\rangle=\overline{\lambda}\lvert t_{1}\rangle_{-}\quad&\text{if }Q_{c}+p\geqslant n_{1}\penalty\ ,\end{cases}

(5.24)

with no ground states whenever
0<Q_{c}+p<n_{1}
. (In particular, the theory has no supersymmetric ground states at all if
|q_{c}|<{n_{1}-n_{3}\over 2}
.)
Note the
U(n_{3})
fugacities:

Y_{3}\lvert\Psi\rangle=\prod_{k=1}^{n_{3}}y_{3,k}^{-f_{3,k}}\lvert\Psi\rangle\penalty\ .

(5.25)

At fixed
p
, we find states indexed by elements
f_{3}
of the set:

F(p,n_{3};f_{3})\equiv\left\{(f_{3,k})\in\{0,1\}^{n_{3}}\;\Big|\;\sum_{k=1}^{n_{3}}f_{3,k}=p\right\}\penalty\ ,\qquad\text{with }\;\left|F(p,n_{3};f_{3})\right|=\binom{n_{3}}{p}\penalty\ ,

(5.26)

which thus contribute to the flavoured index a factor of the Schur polynomial for the fully antisymmetric representation
[1^{p}]
of
U(n_{3})
, namely:

\sum_{f_{3}\in F(p,n_{3};f_{3})}\prod_{k=1}^{n_{3}}y_{3,k}^{-f_{3,k}}=\mathfrak{s}_{[1^{p},0^{n_{3}-p}]}(y_{3}^{-1})\penalty\ .

(5.27)

We thus find the Witten index:

\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}[1,Q_{c},(n_{1},0,n_{3})]=\sum_{p=0}^{n_{3}}(-1)^{p}\mathfrak{s}_{[1^{p},0^{n_{3}-p}]}(y_{3}^{-1})\;\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}[1,Q_{c}+p,(n_{1},0,0)]\penalty\ ,

(5.28)

in terms of the
n_{3}=0
index computed above. This exactly reproduces the Higgs-phase computation.

5.3
U(1)_{q_{c}}
with
n_{1}
fundamentals,
n_{2}
antifundamentals and no
\varGamma
fields

We turn next to the theory with both fundamental and antifundamental multiplets, while setting
n_{3}=0
for simplicity. Let us first consider the SQED theory without
\varGamma
field. In the case of vanishing flavour masses, we expect to have a gapless sector related to the non-compact target space due to the gauge-invariant field
\widetilde{\phi}\phi
. In order to lift this flat direction, we turn on a real mass
m
along
U(n_{1})\times U(n_{2})
so that the fundamental and antifundamental chiral multiplets obtain a common mass
\pm\sigma+m
, respectively, on the Coulomb branch. Equivalently, we turn on a complexified fugacity:

y_{1,i}=\boldsymbol{y}^{-1}\penalty\ ,\qquad y_{2,j}=\boldsymbol{y}\penalty\ ,\qquad\forall i,j\penalty\ ,

(5.29)

with
m\propto-\log\boldsymbol{y}
. The distinct frequencies of the fundamental and antifundamental chiral multiplets are then denoted by:

\omega=|\sigma+m|\penalty\ ,\qquad\qquad\widetilde{\omega}=|-\sigma+m|\penalty\ ,

(5.30)

respectively. Let us choose
m>0
for definiteness. The supercharges of the free theory are now given by:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}={\begin{cases}2i\sqrt{\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}b_{1,i}^{\dagger}\psi_{1,i}-2i\sqrt{\widetilde{\omega}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}a_{2,j}\psi_{2,j}\qquad&\text{if }\,\sigma>m\penalty\ ,\\
2i\sqrt{\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}b_{1,i}^{\dagger}\psi_{1,i}+2i\sqrt{\widetilde{\omega}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}b^{\dagger}_{2,j}\psi_{2,j}\qquad&\text{if }|\sigma|<m\penalty\ ,\\
-2i\sqrt{\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}a_{1,i}\psi_{1,i}+2i\sqrt{\widetilde{\omega}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}b^{\dagger}_{2,j}\psi_{2,j}\qquad&\text{if }\sigma<-m\penalty\ ,\end{cases}}

(5.31)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Q}}={\begin{cases}-2i\sqrt{\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}b_{1,i}\overline{\psi}_{1,i}+2i\sqrt{\widetilde{\omega}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}a^{\dagger}_{2,j}\overline{\psi}_{2,j}\qquad&\text{if }\sigma>m\penalty\ ,\\
-2i\sqrt{\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}b_{1,i}\overline{\psi}_{1,i}-2i\sqrt{\omega}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}b_{2,j}\overline{\psi}_{2,j}\qquad&\text{if }|\sigma|<m\penalty\ ,\\
2i\sqrt{\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}a^{\dagger}_{1,i}\overline{\psi}_{1,i}-2i\sqrt{\omega}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}b_{2,j}\overline{\psi}_{2,j}\qquad&\text{if }\sigma<-m\penalty\ .\end{cases}}

There are now three distinct regions on the Coulomb branch. Similarly to the previous cases, we now define

\lvert 0\rangle=\lvert 0\rangle_{++}\qquad\text{such that}\quad\psi_{1,i}\lvert 0\rangle=\psi_{2,j}\lvert 0\rangle=0\penalty\ ,

(5.32)

which is the ‘empty’ state in the region
|\sigma|<m
.
The ground states of the free theory, before imposing Gauss’s law, are given by:

\mathcal{F}^{0}=\left\{\begin{cases}\lvert s_{1},t_{2}\rangle_{+-}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\frac{(a_{1,i}^{\dagger})^{s_{1,i}}}{\sqrt{s_{1,i}!}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{2}}\frac{(b_{2,j}^{\dagger})^{t_{2,j}}}{\sqrt{t_{2,j}!}}\overline{\psi}_{2,j}\lvert 0\rangle\qquad&\text{if }\,\sigma>m\\
\lvert s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{++}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\frac{(a_{1,i}^{\dagger})^{s_{1,i}}}{\sqrt{s_{1,i}!}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{2}}\frac{(a_{2,j}^{\dagger})^{s_{2,j}}}{\sqrt{s_{2,j}!}}\lvert 0\rangle\qquad&\text{if }\,|\sigma|<m\\
\lvert t_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{-+}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\frac{(b_{1,i}^{\dagger})^{t_{1,i}}}{\sqrt{t_{1,i}!}}\overline{\psi}_{1,i}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{2}}\frac{(a_{2,j}^{\dagger})^{s_{2,j}}}{\sqrt{s_{2,j}!}}\lvert 0\rangle\quad\qquad&\text{if }\,\sigma<-m\end{cases}\right\}\penalty\ ,

(5.33)

and we then obtain the would-be physical states:

\mathcal{F}^{0}_{\rm phys}=\left\{\begin{cases}\lvert s_{1},t_{2}\rangle_{+-}\quad\,|\quad|s_{1}|+|t_{2}|+n_{2}+Q_{c}=0\qquad\quad&\text{if }\,\sigma>m\\
\lvert s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{++}\quad|\quad|s_{1}|-|s_{2}|+Q_{c}=0\qquad&\text{if }\,|\sigma|<m\\
\lvert t_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{-+}\quad\,|\quad-|t_{1}|-|s_{2}|-n_{1}+Q_{c}=0\quad\qquad&\text{if }\,\sigma<-m\end{cases}\right\}\penalty\ .

(5.34)

Here we introduced the notation:

|s_{1}|\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}s_{1,i}\penalty\ ,\qquad|s_{2}|\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}s_{2,j}\penalty\ ,\qquad|t_{1}|\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}t_{1,i}\penalty\ ,\qquad|t_{2}|\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}t_{1,j}\penalty\ .

(5.35)

Note that the states at
\sigma>0
can only exist if
Q_{c}\leqslant-n_{2}
, and those at
\sigma<-m
can only exist if
Q_{c}\geqslant n_{1}
. In particular, we never have states at the same time on both external regions of the
\sigma
line.

Last but not least, let us see which perturbative ground states can arise depending on the sign of the FI parameter. The scalar potential reads:

U(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{N_{\phi}}{2|\sigma+m|}-\frac{N_{\widetilde{\phi}}}{2|-\sigma+m|}-\zeta\right)^{2}\penalty\ ,

(5.36)

with
N_{\phi}\geqslant n_{1}
and
N_{\widetilde{\phi}}\geqslant n_{2}
for all states in each region. We analyse this potential in the limit of large
\zeta
, which means that we consider some arbitrarily large Coulomb-branch scale
M_{C}
. We then find that, for
\zeta>0
, there can be one vacuum in the region
\sigma<-m
and one vacuum in the region
|\sigma|<0
, while for
\zeta<0
there is one vacuum with
\sigma>0
and one with
|\sigma|<0
. Let us explore these perturbative ground states in more detail.

5.36
) with

5.3.1
Perturbative ground states with
|\sigma|>m

We begin by analysing the ‘exterior’ regions at
|\sigma|>m
.
In the region
\sigma>m
and in the limit
|\zeta|\rightarrow\infty
, we have a critical point if and only if
\zeta
is negative:

\sigma_{\ast}\approx m-{N_{\widetilde{\phi}}\over 2\zeta}\penalty\ ,\qquad h^{\prime\prime}(\sigma_{\ast})>0\penalty\ .

(5.37)

Similarly, in the region
\sigma<-m
, a critical point exists if and only if
\zeta
is positive:

\sigma_{\ast}\approx-m-{N_{\phi}\over 2\zeta}\penalty\ ,\qquad h^{\prime\prime}(\sigma_{\ast})>0\penalty\ .

(5.38)

We therefore find the perturbative ground states:

\lvert\Psi\rangle\cong\begin{cases}\lvert\Psi_{+-};s_{1},t_{2}\rangle=\overline{\lambda}\lvert s_{1},t_{1}\rangle_{+-}\quad&\text{if }\,\zeta<0\,\text{ and }\,Q_{c}\leqslant-n_{2}\penalty\ ,\\
\lvert\Psi_{-+};t_{1},s_{2}\rangle=\overline{\lambda}\lvert t_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{-+}\quad&\text{if }\,\zeta>0\,\text{ and }\,Q_{c}\geqslant n_{1}.\end{cases}

(5.39)

Note the gaugino insertion due to the fact that
h^{\prime\prime}(\sigma_{\ast})>0
.
There are only a finite number of perturbative ground states in the external regions, and we easily compute their contribution to the Witten index of the full theory. For simplicity of presentation, we only turn on the specific flavour fugacity (
5.29
). The ground states located at
\sigma>m
for
\zeta<0
then contribute:

\displaystyle(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})_{+-}^{\zeta<0}=

(5.40)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle(-1)^{n_{2}+1}\sum_{|s_{1}|,|t_{2}|=0}^{\infty}\delta_{|s_{1}|+|t_{2}|+n_{2}+Q_{c},0}\binom{n_{1}+|s_{1}|-1}{n_{1}-1}\binom{n_{2}+|t_{2}|-1}{n_{2}-1}\boldsymbol{y}^{|s_{1}|-|t_{2}|-n_{2}}}\penalty\ .

This Witten index for these perturbative vacua can be usefully written as a contour integral. Let us define the matter integrand:

Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})\equiv{x^{Q_{c}}\over(1-x\boldsymbol{y})^{n_{1}}(1-x^{-1}\boldsymbol{y})^{n_{2}}}\penalty\ .

(5.41)

Then one easily checks that:

(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})_{+-}^{\zeta<0}=-\oint_{(x=0)}{dx\over 2\pi ix}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})\penalty\ ,

(5.42)

where we pick the residue at
x=0
. Indeed, picking out the
x^{0}
factor in the integrand precisely imposes Gauss’s law on the free-field partition function expanded at small
x
, which indeed corresponds to the
\sigma>m
region.
Similarly, the ground states located at
\sigma<-m
for
\zeta>0
contribute:

\displaystyle(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})_{-+}^{\zeta>0}=

(5.43)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle(-1)^{n_{1}+1}\sum_{|t_{1}|,|s_{2}|=0}^{\infty}\delta_{|t_{1}|+|s_{2}|+n_{1}-Q_{c},0}\binom{n_{1}+|t_{1}|-1}{n_{1}-1}\binom{n_{2}+|s_{2}|-1}{n_{2}-1}\boldsymbol{y}^{-|t_{1}|+|s_{2}|-n_{1}}}\penalty\ ,

which can also be written as the contour integral:

(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})_{-+}^{\zeta>0}=\oint_{(x=\infty)}{dx\over 2\pi ix}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})\penalty\ ,

(5.44)

where we pick the residue at infinity. This naturally imposes Gauss’s law in the
\sigma<-m
region by picking out the
(1/x)^{0}
factor when expanding the integrand in
1/x
.

5.3.2
Perturbative ground states with
|\sigma|<m

In the interior region at large
\zeta
, we find the critical points

\sigma_{\ast}\approx\begin{cases}-m+{N_{\phi}\over 2\zeta}\quad&\text{if }\,\zeta>0\penalty\ ,\\
m+{N_{\widetilde{\phi}}\over 2\zeta}\quad&\text{if }\,\zeta<0\penalty\ ,\end{cases}\qquad\text{with }\;h^{\prime\prime}(\sigma_{\ast})<0\penalty\ .

(5.45)

Therefore, for any
\zeta\neq 0
, we find the perturbative ground states:

\lvert\Psi_{++};s_{1},s_{2}\rangle\equiv\lvert s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{++}\penalty\ ,\qquad\text{with }\quad|s_{1}|-|s_{2}|+Q_{c}=0\penalty\ ,

(5.46)

giving us an infinite number of ground states. They contribute to the index as:

(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})_{++}=\sum_{|s_{1}|,|s_{2}|=0}^{\infty}\delta_{|s_{1}|-|s_{2}|+Q_{c},0}\binom{n_{1}+|s_{1}|-1}{n_{1}-1}\binom{n_{2}+|s_{2}|-1}{n_{2}-1}\boldsymbol{y}^{|s_{1}|+|s_{2}|}\penalty\ .

(5.47)

This infinite series can be resummed explicitly by writing it as a hypergeometric function. For
Q_{c}<n_{1}
, let us define:

\displaystyle F_{-}(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{n_{1}+l-Q_{c}-1}{n_{1}-1}\binom{n_{2}+l-1}{n_{2}-1}\boldsymbol{y}^{2l-Q_{c}}}

(5.48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\binom{n_{1}-Q_{c}-1}{n_{1}-1}}\boldsymbol{y}^{-Q_{c}}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(n_{2},n_{1}-Q_{c};-Q_{c}+1;\boldsymbol{y}^{2}\right)\penalty\ .

while for
Q_{c}>-n_{2}
, we define:

\displaystyle F_{+}(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{n_{1}+l-1}{n_{1}-1}\binom{n_{2}+l+Q_{c}-1}{n_{2}-1}\boldsymbol{y}^{2l+Q_{c}}}

(5.49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\binom{n_{2}+Q_{c}-1}{n_{2}-1}}\boldsymbol{y}^{Q_{c}}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(n_{1},n_{2}+Q_{c};Q_{c}+1;\boldsymbol{y}^{2}\right)\penalty\ .

The two functions coincide for the values of
Q_{c}
for which they are both defined:

F_{-}(\boldsymbol{y})=F_{+}(\boldsymbol{y})\qquad\text{iff}\;\quad-n_{2}<Q_{c}<n_{1}\penalty\ .

(5.50)

We can then rewrite the Witten index (
5.47
) as:

(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})_{++}=\begin{cases}F_{-}(\boldsymbol{y})\qquad&\text{if }\;Q_{c}<n_{1}\penalty\ ,\\
F_{+}(\boldsymbol{y})\qquad&\text{if }\;Q_{c}>-n_{2}\penalty\ ,\\
\end{cases}

(5.51)

Note that this expression holds for either sign of
\zeta\neq 0
.
The Witten index for these perturbative ground states can again be written as a contour integral of the same integrand (
5.41
) as for the external regions. Indeed, one easily checks that:

F_{-}(\boldsymbol{y})=-\oint_{(x=\boldsymbol{y}^{-1})}{dx\over 2\pi ix}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})\penalty\ ,\qquad\qquad F_{+}(\boldsymbol{y})=\oint_{(x=\boldsymbol{y})}{dx\over 2\pi ix}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})\penalty\ .

(5.52)

where one picks the residue at
x=\boldsymbol{y}^{-1}
and
x=\boldsymbol{y}
, respectively.

5.3.3
Compairing to the JK residue: perturbative versus true ground states

Adding up the perturbative contributions for the exterior and interior regions, we find the Witten index for the full theory. It is interesting to check this explicitly against the expected full result. First, recall that the full index can be obtained by the JK residue formula (
3.27
), namely:

(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})^{\zeta>0}=-\oint_{(x=\boldsymbol{y}^{-1})}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})=F_{-}(\boldsymbol{y})\penalty\ ,\qquad\quad(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})^{\zeta<0}=\oint_{(x=\boldsymbol{y})}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})=F_{+}(\boldsymbol{y})\penalty\ ,

(5.53)

for positive or negative
\zeta
, respectively. For
\zeta>0
, adding up the perturbative contributions gives us:

(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})^{\zeta>0}=\begin{cases}-\oint_{(x=\boldsymbol{y}^{-1})}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})\qquad&\text{if }\;Q_{c}\leqslant-n_{2}\penalty\ ,\\
\oint_{(x=\boldsymbol{y})}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})\qquad&\text{if }\;-n_{2}<Q_{c}<n_{1}\penalty\ ,\\
\oint_{(x=\infty)}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})+\oint_{(x=\boldsymbol{y})}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})\qquad&\text{if }\;Q_{c}\geqslant n_{1}\penalty\ .\end{cases}

(5.54)

Similarly, the sum of the perturbative Witten indices for
\zeta<0
gives us:

(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})^{\zeta<0}=\begin{cases}-\oint_{(x=\boldsymbol{y}^{-1})}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})-\oint_{(x=0)}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})\qquad&\text{if }\;Q_{c}\leqslant-n_{2}\penalty\ ,\\
-\oint_{(x=\boldsymbol{y}^{-1})}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})\qquad&\text{if }\;-n_{2}<Q_{c}<n_{1}\penalty\ ,\\
\oint_{(x=\boldsymbol{y})}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})\qquad&\text{if }\;Q_{c}\geqslant n_{1}\penalty\ .\end{cases}

(5.55)

This reproduces the exact index (
5.53
), as expected. The non-trivial identities are those where we need to sum over perturbative ground states from two different regions (that is, for
\zeta>0
with
Q_{c}\geqslant n_{1}
and for
\zeta<0
with
Q_{c}\leqslant-n_{2}
). The identity between the perturbative approach and the JK residue formula is easily shown by deforming the contour in the
x
-plane.

The key physics, in these cases, is that we have more perturbative ground states than true ground states, resulting in non-trivial cancellations between distinct perturbative contributions to the index. If the effective theory for the
(\sigma,\lambda)
supermultiplet were exact with a finite scalar potential, the lifting of any pair of states with the same flavour quantum numbers in the interior and exterior region would be expected, because the two states in such a pair differ by a
\overline{\lambda}
insertion. This is precisely the condition for the existence of a SQM instanton contribution lifting the pair to non-zero energy
Witten:1982im
. In the present situation, we can only trust the scalar potential
U(\sigma)
up to
|U(\sigma)|\approx M_{C}
, yet it is natural to conjecture that a similar phenomenon occurs; here, it must be the case that the relevant ‘instantons’ are really the 1d gauge theory effects which smoothen out the singularities of the effective theory at
\sigma=\pm m
. Similar cancellations between perturbative states are a common occurrence in supersymmetric QFT — see
e.g.

Bullimore:2019qnt
for some 3d GLSM related to the present work, and
Pedder:2007ff
for a discussion in
\mathcal{N}=4
SQM.

Incidentally, the above discussion also gives us an explicit expression for the wall-crossing of the Witten index between positive and negative FI parameter:

(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})^{\zeta>0}-(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})^{\zeta<0}=(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})^{\zeta>0}_{-+}-(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})^{\zeta<0}_{+-}=\oint_{(x=\infty)}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})+\oint_{(x=0)}Z(x,\boldsymbol{y})\penalty\ .

(5.56)

Indeed the perturbative states in the interior region exist for any
\zeta\neq 0
, and therefore the wall-crossing comes entirely from the perturbative states in the exterior region. In particular, the wall-crossing is trivial if
-n_{2}<Q_{c}<n_{1}
. We will discuss wall-crossing further in section
6
.

5.4
Perturbative ground states in
\varGamma
-SQED with
n_{3}=0

We now consider adding the gauge-neutral fields
\varGamma^{j}_{i}
to the theory together with the
E
-term (
3.6
), while keeping
n_{3}=0
for simplicity. We turn on the same real mass
m
as in (
5.30
), which gives a common real mass
2m>0
to the
\varGamma
fields. Therefore, we can quantise these fermi multiplets in the usual way, which gives us:

\overline{\varGamma}^{i}_{j}\lvert\Psi\rangle=0\penalty\ ,\qquad\forall i,j\penalty\ ,

(5.57)

where
\Psi
denotes the free-field ground states (
5.33
) of the theory without
\varGamma
fields. Once we add in
\varGamma
, we must consider a larger set of free-field ground states obtained by tensoring the previous results with the full fermionic Fock space generated by the raising operators
\varGamma^{j}_{i}
. That is, we find:

\mathcal{F}^{0}=\left\{\begin{cases}\lvert s_{1},t_{2},\gamma\rangle_{+-}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{2}}(\varGamma_{i}^{j})^{\gamma_{i,j}}\lvert s_{1},t_{2}\rangle_{+-}\qquad&\text{if }\,\sigma>m\\
\lvert s_{1},s_{2},\gamma\rangle_{++}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{2}}(\varGamma_{i}^{j})^{\gamma_{i,j}}\lvert s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{++}\qquad&\text{if }\,|\sigma|<m\\
\lvert t_{1},s_{2},\gamma\rangle_{-+}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{2}}(\varGamma_{i}^{j})^{\gamma_{i,j}}\lvert t_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{-+}\quad\qquad&\text{if }\,\sigma<-m\end{cases}\right\}\penalty\ ,

(5.58)

with
\gamma_{i,j}\in\{0,1\}
. Note that we need to specify an ordering of the
\varGamma
fields, since they are fermionic; we choose to order them according to:

\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{2}}(\varGamma_{i}^{j})^{\gamma_{i,j}}\equiv(\varGamma_{1}^{1})^{\gamma_{1,1}}(\varGamma_{1}^{2})^{\gamma_{1,2}}\cdots(\varGamma_{1}^{n_{2}})^{\gamma_{1,n_{2}}}(\varGamma_{2}^{1})^{\gamma_{2,1}}\cdots(\varGamma_{n_{1}}^{n_{2}})^{\gamma_{n_{1},n_{2}}}\penalty\ .

(5.59)

Since the
\varGamma
’s are gauge-neutral, the Gauss law constraints remain the same as in (
5.34
).
The
E
-term interaction

E_{\varGamma^{j}_{i}}=\widetilde{\phi}^{j}\phi_{i}

(5.60)

will pair together many of the states in (
5.58
) and give them a positive energy. To see this explicitly, we treat this interaction in perturbation theory. Since we are only interested in the ground states, we can focus on the supercharges:

Ground-state perturbation theory.
We wish to find the exact ground states after deforming the supercharges as in (

Let us expand the new would-be ground states as

\lvert\Psi\rangle=\lvert\Psi_{0}\rangle+\varepsilon\lvert\Psi_{1}\rangle+\varepsilon^{2}\lvert\Psi_{2}\rangle+\cdots\penalty\ ,

\lvert\Psi_{0}\rangle

At first order in

\mathcal{Q}_{1}\lvert\Psi_{0}\rangle=-\mathcal{Q}_{0}\lvert\Psi_{1}\rangle\penalty\ ,\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}\lvert\psi_{0}\rangle=-\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}\lvert\Psi_{1}\rangle\penalty\ .

Recall that the ground states

\mathcal{Q}_{0}

Solving this cohomological problem determines
\lvert\Psi_{1}\rangle

More concretely, we can present this as a linear algebra problem. Consider an orthonormal basis of ground states
\{\lvert\Psi_{0};\alpha\rangle\}
of the undeformed theory, and define the matrices

(5.66)

where we chose a new normalisation of

\boldsymbol{Q}_{1}\equiv i\sqrt{2\omega\widetilde{\omega}}\,\mathcal{Q}_{1}\penalty\ ,\qquad\quad\overline{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{1}\equiv-i\sqrt{2\omega\widetilde{\omega}}\,\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}\penalty\ ,

(5.67)

Then, if follows from (

\lvert\Psi_{0}\rangle\equiv\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}\lvert\Psi_{0};\alpha\rangle

5.4.1
Deformed perturbative ground states in the exterior region

Let us first consider the exterior region
\sigma>m
, where the orthonormal basis for the free theory is:

\mathcal{F}^{0}_{\rm phys}=\big\{\lvert s_{1},t_{2},\gamma\rangle_{+-}\;\;\big|\;\;|s_{1}|+|t_{2}|=-n_{2}-Q_{c}\big\}\penalty\ .

(5.71)

Recall that these states only exist for
Q_{c}\leqslant-n_{2}
and with
\zeta<0

Up to terms that create excitations orthogonal to the zeroth-order ground states and drop out from (

), we have

{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{1}^{(+-)}=\sum_{i,j}\varGamma^{j}_{i}a_{1,i}b^{\dagger}_{2,j}\penalty\ ,

(5.72)

which is an operator that acts as

{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{1}^{(+-)}\lvert s_{1},t_{2},\gamma\rangle_{+-}=\sum_{i,j}\lunateepsilon_{i,j}^{\gamma}\lvert s_{1}-\delta_{i},t_{2}+\delta_{j},\gamma+\delta_{i,j}\rangle_{+-}\penalty\ .

(5.73)

Here we used the obvious notation that
s_{1}-\delta_{i}\equiv(s_{1,k}-\delta_{ki})
, and similarly for the shifts of
t_{2}
and
\gamma
. We also defined the important sign:

\lunateepsilon_{i,j}^{\gamma}=(-1)^{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\sum_{l=1}^{n_{2}}\gamma_{k,l}+\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\gamma_{i,l}\right)}\penalty\ ,

(5.74)

which corresponds to the ordering (
5.59

Note also that any would-be state on the right-hand-side with some
\gamma_{i,j}=2

so defined is a nilpotent operator. The complex-conjugate supercharge acts as:

\overline{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{1}^{(+-)}\lvert s_{1},t_{2},\gamma\rangle_{+-}=\sum_{i,j}\lunateepsilon_{i,j}^{\gamma}\lvert s_{1}+\delta_{i},t_{2}-\delta_{j},\gamma-\delta_{i,j}\rangle_{+-}\penalty\ ,

(5.75)

where any state with
\gamma_{i,j}=-1

Similarly, in the exterior region
\sigma<-m

(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})_{-+}^{\zeta>0}=\oint_{(x=\infty)}{dx\over 2\pi ix}Z(x,y)\penalty\ ,

5.4.2
Deformed perturbative ground states in the interior region

In the interior region
|\sigma|<m

\mathcal{F}^{0}_{\rm phys}=\big\{\lvert s_{1},s_{2},\gamma\rangle_{++}\;\;\big|\;\;|s_{1}|-|s_{2}|+Q_{c}=0\big\}\penalty\ ,

and:

{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{1}^{(++)}=\sum_{i,j}\varGamma^{j}_{i}a_{1,i}a_{2,j}\penalty\ ,\qquad\quad{\overline{\boldsymbol{Q}}}_{1}^{(++)}=\sum_{i,j}\overline{\varGamma}^{i}_{j}a^{\dagger}_{1,i}a^{\dagger}_{2,j}\penalty\ .

(5.83)

We thus find the action:

\displaystyle{{\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Q}}_{1}^{(++)}\lvert s_{1},s_{2},\gamma\rangle_{-+}=\sum_{i,j}\lunateepsilon_{i,j}^{\gamma}\lvert s_{1}-\delta_{i},s_{2}-\delta_{j},\gamma+\delta_{i,j}\rangle_{-+}\penalty\ ,}

(5.84)

\displaystyle{{\displaystyle\overline{\boldsymbol{Q}}}_{1}^{(++)}\lvert s_{1},s_{2},\gamma\rangle_{-+}=\sum_{i,j}\lunateepsilon_{i,j}^{\gamma}\lvert s_{1}+\delta_{i},s_{2}+\delta_{j},\gamma-\delta_{i,j}\rangle_{-+}\penalty\ .}

5.52

(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})_{++}=\begin{cases}-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\oint_{(x=y_{1,i})}{dx\over 2\pi ix}Z(x,y)\qquad&\text{if }\;Q_{c}<n_{1}\penalty\ ,\\
\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}\oint_{(x=y_{2,j})}{dx\over 2\pi ix}Z(x,y)\qquad&\text{if }\;Q_{c}>-n_{2}\penalty\ ,\\
\end{cases}

(5.85)

where we picked a contour that encloses all the poles from fundamental or antifundamental chiral multiplets, depending on the value of
Q_{c}
. As in the case without the
\varGamma

We also find, as before, that non-perturbative effects must lift certain fermion-boson pairs of ground states located in distinct regions of the Coulomb branch.

6
Wall-crossing, trialities and confinement

As we vary the FI parameters of a gauged
\mathcal{N}=2
SQM, the number of supersymmetric ground states can jump discontinuously, leading to discrete jumps in the Witten index. This is known as the wall-crossing phenomenon, which has been most studied in the case of 1d
\mathcal{N}=4
systems seen as the worldline theories of half-BPS particles on the Coulomb branch of 4d
\mathcal{N}=2
SQFTs. In this section, we explore aspects of wall-crossing for our 1d
\mathcal{N}=2
SQCD.

6.1
Trivial wall-crossing and trialities

For gauged
\mathcal{N}=2
SQM, a wall-crossing formula was first derived in
Hori:2014tda
together with the Coulomb-branch localisation result for the Witten index. Focussing on unitary SQCD, we are interested in the change in the Witten index as we go from positive to negative FI parameter:

\Delta\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}[N_{c},Q_{c},\boldsymbol{n}^{F}]\equiv\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}[N_{c},Q_{c},\boldsymbol{n}^{F}]^{\zeta>0}\,-\,\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}[N_{c},Q_{c},\boldsymbol{n}^{F}]^{\zeta<0}\penalty\ .

(6.1)

Of particular interest to us are the cases with trivial wall-crossing (TWC), in which case the supersymmetric ground states can be continuously transported through
\zeta=0
. We will show that trivial wall-crossing always occurs when
q_{c}
sits in a particular window determined by the parameter:

\mathcal{A}={n_{1}+n_{2}-n_{3}\over 2}-N_{c}\penalty\ ,

(6.2)

according to:

-\mathcal{A}-1<q_{c}<\mathcal{A}+1\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\Delta\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}[N_{c},Q_{c},\boldsymbol{n}^{F}]=0\quad\text{(TWC).}

(6.3)

Note that, since
q_{c}+\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{Z}
, the condition on
q_{c}
stated in (
6.3
) is equivalent to
|q_{c}|\leqslant\mathcal{A}
. We will shortly demonstrate that this condition is sufficient for having TWC, and we further conjecture that it is also a necessary condition, as claimed in (
6.3
), except in trivial cases where
\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}[N_{c},q_{c},\boldsymbol{n}^{F}]_{\pm}=0
. Note that the inequalities in (
6.3
) can be written as:

-n_{2}+N_{c}-1<Q_{c}<n_{1}-n_{3}-N_{c}+1\penalty\ .

(6.4)

For
N_{c}=1
, the condition for TWC is
-n_{2}<Q_{c}<n_{1}-n_{3}
, which we can rewrite as
-n_{2}<Q_{c}+p<n_{1}
for all
p=0,\cdots,n_{3}
— this condition therefore follows from the Coulomb-branch discussion of the previous section, as the condition that there are no perturbative ground states from the ‘exterior’ regions
|\sigma|>m
which could disappear to infinity as we cross
\zeta=0
.

Trialities from wall-crossing.
Consider the SQCD theory with
\sign(\zeta)=\pm
, which we denote here by
\mathcal{T}_{\pm}
, and let us denote by
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{-}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}_{+}
the theories obtained by right and left mutation, respectively. Assuming theory
\mathcal{T}
has trivial wall-crossing, which means that
\mathcal{A}\geqslant 0
and
|q_{c}|\leqslant\mathcal{A}
, we may view this as an ‘infrared’ duality:

\mathcal{T}_{-}\qquad\overset{\text{TWC}}{\longleftrightarrow}\qquad\mathcal{T}_{+}\penalty\ ,

(6.5)

since the supersymmetric ground states can be continuously deformed from positive to negative
\zeta
and so the two theories have isomorphic zero-energy ground states.
Combining TWC with mutations, we then obtain at least a triality of 1d gauge theories:

\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}_{+}\qquad\overset{\text{LM}}{\longleftarrow}\qquad\Big(\mathcal{T}_{-}\qquad\overset{\text{TWC}}{\longleftrightarrow}\qquad\mathcal{T}_{+}\Big)\qquad\overset{\text{RM}}{\longrightarrow}\qquad\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{-}\penalty\ ,

(6.6)

where LM and RM denote the left and right mutations, respectively.
Here the three gauge groups involved in the triality are:

U(n_{2}-N_{c})_{-q_{c}}^{(\zeta^{\prime\prime}>0)}\qquad\overset{\text{LM}}{\longleftarrow}\qquad U(N_{c})_{q_{c}}\qquad\overset{\text{RM}}{\longrightarrow}\qquad U(n_{1}-N_{c})_{-q_{c}}^{(\zeta^{\prime}<0)}\penalty\ .

(6.7)

The next natural question is whether we can go further in cases where the wall-crossing for the theory
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
or
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}
is trivial as well. Note that we have:

q_{c}^{\prime}=-q_{c}^{\prime}\penalty\ ,\quad\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=-\mathcal{A}\penalty\ ,\qquad\text{and}\qquad q_{c}^{\prime\prime}=-q_{c}\penalty\ ,\quad\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}=-\mathcal{A}

(6.8)

for the mutated theories, hence the absence of wall crossing in either theory is equivalent to
|q_{c}|\leqslant-\mathcal{A}
. This is in contradiction with the condition for TCW of theory
\mathcal{T}
except in the special case
q_{c}=\mathcal{A}=0
, which we shall discuss momentarily.
Hence, for
\mathcal{A}>0
, we have exactly the three distinct gauge theories (
6.7

The case

Consider the abelian case first. By deformation of the integration contours involved in the JK residue (see figure

The general case.
For the
U(N_{c})

\Delta\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}=\left[(-1)^{N_{c}}\oint_{(x=y_{1})}-\oint_{(x=y_{2})}\right]{d^{N_{c}}x\over(2\pi i)^{N_{c}}}{Z(x,y)\over\det(x)}\penalty\ ,

x_{\alpha}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle(A,...,\underbrace{\overset{0}{\infty}}_{=x_{\beta}},F,...,F)\equiv}

\Delta\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}=(-1)^{N_{c}}(F,F,...,F)-(A,A,...,A)\penalty\ .

and

7
More on 1d SQED: abelian examples

In this section, we present the ground states in a few examples of increasing complexity, in order to illustrate our general discussion above in a pedagogical manner. We focus on the case of an abelian gauge group, so that we can explicitly compare the Higgs-branch results of section
4
to the Coulomb-branch approach of section
5
.

7.1
Case
(n_{1}.n_{2},n_{3})=(1,1,0)
: an almost trivial model

Consider 1d
\varGamma
-SQED (
N_{c}=1
) with
n_{1}=n_{2}=1
and
n_{3}=0
, for any CS level
Q_{c}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
n_{1}-N_{c}=0
and
n_{2}-N_{c}=0
, we know from our dualities that this theory is essentially trivial, with a single ground state for either sign of
\zeta
. For
Q_{c}\neq 0
, the ground state actually depends on the sign of
\zeta
, as is apparent from the Witten index:

\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}^{\zeta>0}=y_{1,1}^{Q_{c}}\penalty\ ,\qquad\qquad\textbf{I}_{\text{W}}^{\zeta<0}=y_{2,1}^{Q_{c}}\penalty\ .

(7.1)

The two states are distinguished by their flavour charge, and therefore we have non-trivial wall-crossing for any
Q_{c}\neq 0
, while for
Q_{c}=0
we have a unique flavourless ground state with trivial wall-crossing.

From the Higgs-branch perspective, we have a single ground state because the Grassmannian geometry is a point in this case,
X_{\pm}=\mathbb{P}^{0}
, for either sign of
\zeta
, therefore the tautological bundle
\mathcal{S}\cong\mathcal{O}(-1)
is a trivial line and the quotient bundle
\mathcal{Q}
is non-existent. Nonetheless, the equivariant cohomology

H_{T}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{0},\mathcal{O}(-Q_{c}))=S_{[-Q_{c}]}\big(\mathbb{C}_{y_{1}^{-1}}\big)\penalty\ .

(7.2)

does distinguish between distinct values of
Q_{c}
. In the language of section
4
, we have the representation
\nu=[-Q_{c}]
of
G^{F}_{1}=U(1)
for
\zeta>0
, and similarly we have
\nu=[Q_{c}]
for the symmetry
G^{F}_{2}=U(1)
when
\zeta<0
. The corresponding Schur polynomials reproduce the flavoured index (
7.1
).

From the Coulomb branch perspective, there are several cases to consider separately. Consider first
Q_{c}=0
, where we have no wall-crossing. This means that the only states are the perturbative states (
5.46
) in the interior region
\sigma<|m|
together with the
\varGamma
oscillators. For any
Q_{c}
, we have an infinite tower of free-field states:

\lvert\Psi\rangle_{++}=\lvert s_{1},s_{2},\gamma\rangle_{++}=(\varGamma^{1}_{1})^{\gamma}{(a_{1,1}^{\dagger})^{s_{1}}\over\sqrt{s_{1}!}}{(a_{2,1}^{\dagger})^{s_{2}}\over\sqrt{s_{2}!}}\lvert 0\rangle\penalty\ ,\quad\qquad s_{1}-s_{2}=-Q_{c}\penalty\ .

(7.3)

Turning on the
E
-term interaction lifts all these states except for one. For
Q_{c}\geqslant 0
, we have the states
\lvert s_{1},s_{1}+Q_{c},\gamma\rangle
and the boson-fermion pairing:

\lvert s_{1},s_{1}+Q_{c},0\rangle_{++}\overset{\boldsymbol{Q}_{1}}{\longrightarrow}\lvert s_{1}-1,s_{1}-1+Q_{c},1\rangle_{++}\penalty\ ,

(7.4)

where all pairs are lifted to non-zero energy, and the only unpaired state is the one with
s_{1}=0
. Similarly, for
Q_{c}\leqslant 0
, we have the pairing

\lvert s_{2}-Q_{c},s_{2},0\rangle_{++}\overset{\boldsymbol{Q}_{1}}{\longrightarrow}\lvert s_{2}-1-Q_{c},s_{2}-1,1\rangle_{++}\penalty\ ,

(7.5)

with
s_{2}=0
being the unpaired state. Therefore, in the interior region we have the perturbative ground states:

|\sigma|<m\quad:\quad\begin{cases}\lvert 0,|Q_{c}|,0\rangle_{++}={(a_{2,1}^{\dagger})^{Q_{c}}\over\sqrt{Q_{c}!}}\lvert 0\rangle\qquad&\text{if }\,Q_{c}\geqslant 0\penalty\ ,\\
\lvert|Q_{c}|,0,0\rangle_{++}={(a_{1,1}^{\dagger})^{|Q_{c}|}\over\sqrt{|Q_{c}|!}}\lvert 0\rangle\qquad&\text{if }\,Q_{c}\leqslant 0\penalty\ .\\
\end{cases}

(7.6)

For
Q_{c}=0
, we have a unique ground state
\lvert 0\rangle
and trivial wall-crossing. For
Q_{c}\neq 0
, we also need to consider the exterior regions. Consider
Q_{c}>0
for definiteness. If furthermore
\zeta<0
, there is no perturbative ground state at
|\sigma|>m
and therefore we only need to consider the interior ground state
\lvert 0,Q_{c},0\rangle_{++}
, which indeed reproduces the Witten index (
7.1
). If
\zeta>0
instead, we have a finite number of perturbative ground states at
\sigma<-m
(before turning on the
E
-term), namely:

\lvert\Psi\rangle_{-+}=\overline{\lambda}\lvert t_{1},s_{2},\gamma\rangle_{-+}=\overline{\lambda}(\varGamma^{1}_{1})^{\gamma}{(b_{1,1}^{\dagger})^{t_{1}}\over\sqrt{t_{1}!}}{(a_{2,1}^{\dagger})^{s_{2}}\over\sqrt{s_{2}!}}\overline{\psi}_{1,1}\lvert 0\rangle\penalty\ ,\quad\qquad t_{1}+s_{2}=Q_{c}-1\penalty\ .

(7.7)

Turning on the
E
-term lifts most of the states due to the pairing:

\lvert t_{1},s_{2},\gamma\rangle_{-+}\overset{\boldsymbol{Q}_{1}}{\longrightarrow}\lvert t_{1}+1,s_{2}-1,\gamma+1\rangle_{-+}\penalty\ ,

(7.8)

but the following two states remain unpaired and survive as perturbative ground states:

\overline{\lambda}\lvert Q_{c}-1,0,0\rangle_{-+}=\overline{\lambda}{(b_{1,1}^{\dagger})^{Q_{c}-1}\over\sqrt{(Q_{c}-1)!}}\overline{\psi}_{1,1}\lvert 0\rangle\penalty\ ,\quad\overline{\lambda}\lvert 0,Q_{c}-1,1\rangle_{-+}=\overline{\lambda}\varGamma^{1}_{1}{(a_{2,1}^{\dagger})^{Q_{c}-1}\over\sqrt{(Q_{c}-1)!}}\overline{\psi}_{1,1}\lvert 0\rangle\penalty\ .

Thus, for
Q_{c}>0
and
\zeta>0
, we find three perturbative ground state — one in the interior region and two in the exterior region, with the respective contributions to the Witten index:

(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})_{++}^{\zeta>0}=y_{2,1}^{Q_{c}}\penalty\ ,\qquad\quad(\textbf{I}_{\text{W}})_{-+}^{\zeta>0}=y_{1,1}^{Q_{c}}-y_{2,1}^{Q_{c}}\penalty\ .

(7.9)

The cancellation in the Witten index suggests that the flavour-degenerate pair

\lvert\text{fer}\rangle=\overline{\lambda}\lvert 0,Q_{c}-1,1\rangle_{-+}\penalty\ ,\qquad\lvert\text{bos}\rangle=\lvert 0,Q_{c},0\rangle_{++}

(7.10)

must be lifted by non-perturbative effects, as they would be if the scalar potential were smoothened out around
\sigma=-m

Witten:1982im
. The case
Q_{c}<0
and
\zeta<0
is similar, with a non-perturbative cancellation between the state at
|\sigma|<m
and one of the two states at
\sigma>m
. Thus we see that this “almost trivial” example already captures most of the subtleties of our abelian Coulomb branch analysis, including both perturbative and non-perturbative effects.

Case
(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})=(2,1,0)

\varGamma
-SQED model with

A.1
The Witten index of gauged SQM

The 1d
\mathcal{N}=2

Hori:2014tda

Consider a generic 1d
\mathcal{N}=2

\Phi

\mathfrak{R}_{\Phi}

where

2.2

Hori:2014tda

and

To fully specify the 1d
\mathcal{N}=2

\mathfrak{R}_{0}

Equidistribution of realizable Steinitz classes for cyclic Kummer extensions
Brody Lynch

Abstract
Let ℓ \ell be prime, and K K be a number field containing the ℓ \ell -th roots of unity. We use techniques from classical algebraic number theory to prove that the Steinitz classes of ℤ / ℓ ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z} extensions of K K are equidistributed among realizable classes in the ideal class group of K K . For ℓ = 2 \ell=2 , this was proved by Kable and Wright using the deep theory of prehomogeneous vector spaces. Foster proved that Steinitz classes are uniformly distributed between realizable classes for tamely ramified elementary- m m extensions using the theory of Galois modules; our approach eliminates this tameness hypothesis.

Kable-Wright and Bhargava-Shankar-Wang use the deep theory of prehomogeneous vector spaces. Foster’s work was built on the theory of Galois modules, and the tamely ramified hypothesis was crucial to his argument. Agboola

∎

Remark 3.5
.

This corollary shows that an

1.

A unit

2.

An ideal class

\ell

3.2
Distribution of
\ell
-parts of the discriminant

Theorem

\ell

-Kummer extension of a number field

\ell

\ell

We will use

\ell
-Kummer extensions with

to count the number of

and

We saw in Theorem

that for a prime divisor

of

and an

\gamma

\mathfrak{q}
if

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{q}:\ell\mathcal{O}_{K}\mid\mathfrak{q}\text{ prime, }\nu_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathfrak{Q})=\ell-1+\ell\nu_{\mathfrak{q}}(\ell)\},

\displaystyle W^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{q}:\ell\mathcal{O}_{K}\mid\mathfrak{q}\text{ prime, }\nu_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathfrak{Q})\neq\ell-1+\ell\nu_{\mathfrak{q}}(\ell)\}.

Furthermore, let

G=(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{W})^{\times}
and

. Also, denote by

the set of elements of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
that are relatively prime to

and
\psi:\mathcal{O}_{K}(\mathfrak{W})\rightarrow H
be the composition of the quotient maps. This notation lets us state the main result of the section.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{Q}\in R
, and let
H
,
W
,
W^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{W}
be as defined above. Let

be the set of congruence classes in

\gamma\in H

\ell

By Theorem
3.1
, in order for

1.

For all
\mathfrak{q}\in W

2.

\mathfrak{W}

relatively prime to

This equidistrubtion statement is reminiscent of the standard result on the equidistribution of ideals in ideal classes, often proved using geometry of numbers

by counting principal ideals contained in a given ideal. We will adopt that argument with a couple of small changes to count

in a given congruence class modulo

\ell

\ell

\gamma\mathcal{O}_{K}

\log(x_1,\ldots, x_{r_1}, z_1, \ldots, z_{r_2})=(\log|x_{1}|,\ldots,\log|x_{r_{1}}|,\log|z_{1}|,\ldots\log|z_{r_{2}}|).

on

We note that

Let

. Letting

Now that we understand the fundamental domain, we can argue the equidistribution results that we need.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let all be as above and
a\in H
. Then

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{r\in D_{X}\cap M(\mathfrak{I}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}(\mathfrak{W})):\psi(M^{-1}(r))=a\}}{\#D_{X}\cap M(\mathfrak{I}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}(\mathfrak{W}))}=\frac{1}{\#H}.

Before we prove the proposition, we note that the numerator is independent of choice of coset representatives. This is because different coset representatives of

will have a ratio that is an
\ell
-power in

and thus a ratio that is an
\ell
-power modulo

.

Proof.

This result mostly follows from results in
(

since our fundamental domain is still

-Lipschitz parametrizable. The other difference is that we are looking at translations of the lattice

by elements of

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let all be as as above and
X\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. For notational simplicity, for
c\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
relatively prime to
\mathfrak{W}
, let

\displaystyle D(c)

\displaystyle=\{r\in D\cap M(\mathfrak{I}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}(\mathfrak{W})):\psi(M^{-1}(r))=c\},

\displaystyle D_{X}(c)

\displaystyle=\{r\in D_{X}\cap M(\mathfrak{I}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}(\mathfrak{W})):\psi(M^{-1}(r))=c\}.

Then for any
a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
relatively prime to
\mathfrak{W}
, we have

\#D_{X}(a)=\#D_{X}(b)+O(X^{n-1}).

Proof.

Let
F
denote the fundamental parallelepiped of the lattice corresponding to

under the logarithmic embedding. Then

\log(D(c))=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in F\\
\phi(d)=c\end{subarray}}(\log(D \cap M(\mf I \mf W))+d)

where sets in the union are translations of the lattice corresponding to

by

X
, the only possible difference in the number of points in

and

comes on the cells of the lattice that intersect the curve that gives the norm condition. As shown in the proof of
(

, the number of these cells grows at

.
∎

Looking ahead to our intended application in counting
\ell
-Kummer extensions, we would like to restrict our set of representatives to those that are
\ell
-power-free. We argue this by inclusion-exclusion.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let all notation be as above, and

be a positive integer and

\mathfrak{N}=\bigcup_{(\mathfrak{P},\mathfrak{I}\mathfrak{W})=\mathcal{O}_{K}}\mathfrak{P}^{n}.

Then

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{r\in D_{X}\cap M(\mathfrak{I}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}(\mathfrak{W}))\backslash M(\mathfrak{N}):\psi(M^{-1}(r))=a\}}{\#D_{X}\cap M(\mathfrak{I}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}(\mathfrak{W}))\backslash M(\mathfrak{N})}=\frac{1}{\#H}.

Proof.

For notational simplicity, let

\displaystyle D_{X}(\mathfrak{J},a)

\displaystyle=\#\{r\in D_{X}\cap M(\mathfrak{J}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}(\mathfrak{W})):\psi(M^{-1}(r))=a\},

\displaystyle D_{X}(\mathfrak{J})

\displaystyle=\#D_{X}\cap M(\mathfrak{J}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}(\mathfrak{W})).

These are the numerator and denominator in the Proposition
3.7

D^{\prime}_{X}(\mathfrak{I},a)
and

be the numerator and denominator in this proposition. Then

\displaystyle D^{\prime}_{X}(\mathfrak{I},a)

\displaystyle=D_{X}(\mathfrak{I},a)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\mathfrak{J},\mathfrak{I})=\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
(\mathfrak{J},\mathfrak{W})=\mathfrak{\mathcal{O}}_{K}\\
N(\mathfrak{I}\mathfrak{J}^{n})\leq X\end{subarray}}\mu(\mathfrak{J})D_{X}(\mathfrak{I}\mathfrak{J}^{n},a),

\displaystyle D_{X}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{I})

\displaystyle=D_{X}(\mathfrak{I})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\mathfrak{J},\mathfrak{I})=\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
(\mathfrak{J},\mathfrak{W})=\mathfrak{\mathcal{O}}_{K}\\
N(\mathfrak{I}\mathfrak{J}^{n})\leq X\end{subarray}}\mu(\mathfrak{J})D_{X}(\mathfrak{I}\mathfrak{J}^{n})

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{D_{X}(\mathfrak{I}\mathfrak{J}^{n},a)}{D_{X}(\mathfrak{I}\mathfrak{J}^{n})}=\frac{1}{\#H}.

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{D^{\prime}_{X}(\mathfrak{I},a)}{D_{X}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{I})}=\frac{1}{\#H}.

∎

Note that by design, our choice of

correspond to representatives
\gamma
of equivalence classes of
\ell
-Kummer extensions with a given
\ell
-part and
\ell
-power part (combined in the ideal
\mathfrak{I}

\ell

in such equivalence classes simply by computing the structure of the group

.

We should note that we also have to worry about the torsion subgroup of

in our definition of

\frac{\#U_{\tors}}{\ell}

\gamma\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
corresponding to each
\ell

\ell
-Kummer extensions.

Proposition

fully covers the congruence condition in calculating
\rho_{\mathfrak{Q}}
. Before we state and prove the main results of this subsection, we must also deal with the divisibility condition, which is to say we must determine the density of extensions corresponding to

for a given

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
K
be a number field of degree

and

be an ideal in

\#\{\mathfrak{I}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}:N(\mathfrak{I})\leq X,\ \gcd(\mathfrak{I},Q)=\mathcal{O}_{K},\ \mathfrak{I}\in\mathcal{C}\}=\frac{\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\zeta_{K}(s)}{h_{K}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{q}\mid Q\\
\mathfrak{q}\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{q})}\right)X+O(X^{1-\frac{1}{n}}).

Using the same inclusion-exclusion method with the lattice

in
(

Corollary 3.11

Let
K
be a number field of degree

\mathcal{C}\in\Cl(K)

Note that the constant

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{q}\mid Q\\

4
Equidistribution of quadratic extensions

Now that we have stated and proved our preliminary results, we can give the main argument. As can be seen in the formulas for the Steinitz class, the case of a general prime
\ell

\ell=2

Theorem 4.1

(Kable and Wright)
.

Let
K
be a number field with class number
h_{K}
and fix
\mathcal{C}\in\Cl(K)
. Then

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{L/K:[L:K]=2,N(\Delta_{L/K})\leq X,\St(\mathcal{O}_{L})=\mathcal{C}\}}{\#\{L/K:[L:K]=2,N(\Delta_{L/K})\leq X\}}=\frac{1}{h_{K}}.

Proof.

As mentioned previously, we will count
E^{\mathcal{C}}(X)
by summing
E_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\mathcal{C}}(X)
over all
\mathfrak{Q}\in R
. First, we will show that for any
\mathcal{C}\in\Cl(K)
,

\displaystyle\#E_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\mathcal{C}}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h_{K}}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\zeta_{K}(s)\left(\frac{2^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\rho_{\mathfrak{Q}}\#Z}{\zeta_{K}(2)}\right)\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{q}\mid 2\mathcal{O}_{K}\\

5.2
Equidistribution theorem

At last, we prove our equidistribution theorem.

Proof of Theorem

.

By Corollary

1.

By Dirichlet’s unit theorem and the fact that

2.

By Lemma

As before, we use

\ell

\ell\mathcal{O}_{K}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{L/K}=s(\mathfrak{A})^{\ell-1}

\ell

\ell

\displaystyle S(Y)

\displaystyle S_{Q,\mathcal{R}}(Y)

n\geq 2
,

\#\{(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n})\in G^{n}:g_{1}^{2}g_{2}^{3}\cdots g_{n}^{n+1}=g\}=\#G^{n-1}.

In other words, these products are equally distributed between elements of
G
.

Proof.

Let
f:G^{n}\rightarrow G
be the homomorphism
f(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})=g_{1}^{2}g_{2}^{3}\cdots g_{n}^{n+1}
. This homomorphism is surjective because for
g\in G
,
f(g^{-1},g,1,\ldots,1)=g
. The cosets of the kernel are the sets

\#\{(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n})\in G^{n}:g_{1}^{2}g_{2}^{3}\cdots g_{n}^{n+1}=g\}=\#G^{n-1}

for each
g\in G
. Each coset has size
\#G^{n-1}
.
∎

Applying this results to the case where
G
is the ideal class group of a number field
K
, we get the specific result that we needed in section
5
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
K
be a number field and
\mathcal{C}\in\Cl(K)
. Then for
n\geq 2
,

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{P=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{I}_{i}^{i+1}:\mathfrak{I}_{i}\text{ squarefree, }\ell\text{-free
ideal of }\mathcal{O}_{K},\ P\in\mathcal{C},\ N(P)\leq X\}}{\#\{P=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{I}_{i}^{i+1}:\mathfrak{I}_{i}\text{ squarefree, }\ell\text{-free,
ideal of }\mathcal{O}_{K},\ N(P)\leq X\}}=\frac{1}{h_{K}}.

Proof.

This follows from Corollary
3.11
and Lemma
6.1
.
∎

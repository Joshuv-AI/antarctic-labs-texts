Abstract
. In this paper we give an elementary proof that sets of zero p , 1 p,1 -Sobolev-Lorentz capacity are ℋ n − p \mathcal{H}^{n-p} -null sets independently of non-linear potential theory. We further show that there exists a set of Sobolev-Lorentz- ( p , 1 ) (p,1) capacity equal zero with Hausdorff dimension equal n − p n-p .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
31C40, 46E35, 46E30

1.
Introduction

The study of sets of zero capacity are key in understanding possible pathological behavior of Sobolev functions. It is well known that points have zero Sobolev
n
-capacity, a fact which is closely related to the existence of non-continuous functions in
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. One should compare this to the well-known Morrey estimates for
p>n
. The question arises naturally also in lower dimension, for example when we are interested in the set on which boundary values are satisfied in a pointwise sense (for example see
[
13
]
and
[
3
]
).

The case
p=n
is particularly interesting, since this is the threshold of regularity to obtain continuity. Although continuity fails for maps in
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it was proved in
[
16
]
that maps whose derivative belongs to the Lorentz space
L^{n,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(for the definition see the Preliminaries), are continuous and differentiable almost everywhere. This is closely connected to the fact that
n,1
-Sobolev-Lorentz null capacity sets are empty (see
[
4
]
). In
[
8
]
it was proven that the condition of having derivative in
L^{n,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is sharp and it also implies that a mapping satisfies the Lusin (N) condition. It is known that this condition fails for mappings only in
W^{1,n}
, we refer the reader to the example in
[
11
]
.

One is naturally led to ask whether there exists a better qualitative estimate for the size of sets of null
p,1
-Sobolev-Lorentz capacity than in the ordinary Sobolev case also for other
p
than
n
. The definition of Sobolev-Lorentz spaces can be found at Definition
2.3
and the (quasi)norm
\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\Omega)}
is defined in Definition
2.1
. We define Sobolev-Lorentz capacities as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1<p\leq n
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We define the variational
p,q
-Lorentz capacity of
E
as

\gamma_{p,q}(E):=\inf\Big\{\|Df\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p};f\in W^{1}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ s.t. }\exists G\supset E\text{ open and }f\geq 1\text{ on }G\Big\},

we define the relative
p,q
-variational-Lorentz capacity of
E
with respect to an open
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
as

\displaystyle\gamma_{p,q}(E,\Omega):=\inf\Big\{\|Df\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p};

\displaystyle f\in W^{1}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ s.t. }\exists G\supset E\text{ open and }f\geq 1\text{ on }G,

\displaystyle f=0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\Big\},

and the
p,q
-Sobolev-Lorentz capacity of
E
as

\gamma_{p,q}^{+}(E):=\inf\Big\{\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}^{p}+\|Df\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p};f\in W^{1}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ s.t. }\exists G\supset E\text{ open and }f\geq 1\text{ on }G\Big\}.

Finally we define
\gamma_{p,q}^{+}(E,\Omega)
as the obvious combination of the last two definitions.

In Lemma
3.1
we show that if a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
has zero capacity in any of these capacities then it has zero capacity in all of them.

It was proven in
[
4
]
that
n,1
-Lorentz-Sobolev null capacity sets are empty (equivalently have zero
\mathcal{H}^{0}
measure). An extension of this to higher derivative is the sharp Morse-Sard theorem in the Sobolev-Lorentz setting of
[
9
]
. A further result of
[
9
]
is a smallness result on the
n-p
Hausdorff content using the
p,1
-Sobolev-Lorentz capacity. We refer the author to
[
14
]
for further results and exposition. Let us also refer the reader to the paper in preparation
[
10
]
giving more detail on
\gamma_{p,1}
-null sets. The aim of this paper in Theorem
1.2
is to give an elementary alternative proof of the smallness result which avoids potential theory.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p\in(1,n]
. Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be such that
\gamma_{p,1}(E)=0
then
\mathcal{H}^{n-p}(E)=0
.

The key to our simplification is to take a detour through Orlicz-Sobolev spaces utilizing results of Malý, Swanson and Ziemer in
[
12
]
. We hope that this more elementary approach will help make the result more accessible and better known as well as showing other possible paths for further inquest. Further motivation for this paper is the upcoming paper in preparation about the size of graphs of maps in
W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m})
, see
[
2
]
.

Further, we show, by example, that such a claim does not hold for any
q>1
(see Theorem
4.1
) and that there exists a set
E
with
\gamma_{p,1}(E)=0
and
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)=n-p
(see Theorem
4.2
).

2.
Preliminaries

Let
f
be a measurable function. We define
\mu_{f},
the
distribution function

of
f
as follows (see
[
1
, Definition II.1.1]
):

\mu_{f}(t)=\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{x\in\Omega:|f(x)|>t\}),\qquad t\geq 0.

As can be found in
[
1
, Definition II.1.5]
, we define
f^{*},
the
decreasing rearrangement
of
f
by

f^{*}(t)=\inf\{v:\mu_{f}(v)\leq t\},\quad t\geq 0.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be measurable. By the
p,q
-Lorentz space on
\Omega
,
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
, we denote the collection of elements of
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
, such that

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\Omega)}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\left(\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{1/p}f^{*}(t))^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}}\right)^{1/q}&1\leq q<\infty\\
\\
\sup\limits_{t>0}t\mu_{[f]}(t)^{1/p}=\sup\limits_{s>0}s^{1/p}f^{*}(s)&q=\infty.\end{array}\right.

is finite. We often simplify the notation
\|f\|_{p,q}:=\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\Omega)}
, where there is no danger of misinterpretation.

Remark 2.2
.

In the case that
q>p
it is common to replace
f^{*}
with
f^{**}
in the above definition so that we have a norm, where

f^{**}(t)=\mu_{f^{*}}(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)ds,\quad t>0.

The two values are comparable and we do not need to do this in our paper. Moreover, we are especially interested in small values of
q
where
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,q}(\Omega)}
is a norm as the other cases can be retrieved by an embedding argument. For the details we refer the reader to
[
15
]
, especially to Section 8.2.

Further, it can be proven that

\|f\|_{L^{p,1}(\Omega)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\mu_{f}(s)\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,ds.

The proof is easy for simple functions and in general holds by approximation. We refer the reader to
[
7
]
for details.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. By the
p,q
-Sobolev-Lorentz space on
\Omega
,
W^{1}_{p,q}(\Omega)
, we denote the collection of elements of
W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
, such that

\|f\|_{1,p,q}:=\|f\|_{p,q}+\|Df\|_{p,q}<\infty.

We take the following definition from
[
15
]
(especially see Definition 4.2.1, Definition 4.8.1 and Theorem 4.8.5).

Definition 2.4
.

A function
\Phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is said to be a Young function if there exists a right-continuous, non-decreasing
\varphi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
satisfying

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\varphi(t)=0,\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\varphi(t)=\infty

such that

\Phi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(s)\,ds.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be measurable. The Orlicz space
L^{\Phi}(\Omega)
is defined as the space of all measurable functions
g

on
\Omega
for which there exists a
\lambda>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}\Phi\Big(\frac{|g|}{\lambda}\Big)\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\leq 1

and
\|g\|_{L^{\Phi}(\Omega)}
is this infimum of such of
\lambda
.

The following is due to
[
12
, Lemma 9.3]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Suppose that
f\in L^{p,1}(\Omega)
. Then there exists a Young function
\Phi
satisfying

\int_{0}^{\infty}(\Phi^{\prime}(t))^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,dt=1

and

\int_{\Omega}\Phi(|f|)\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\leq 1.

The following is
[
12
, Theorem 8.13]

Theorem 2.6
.

Suppose that
p>1
and
\Phi
is a Young function satisfying

\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\Phi^{\prime}(t)\big)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,dt\leq 1.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
f\in W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is precisely represented and
f\geq 1
on
E
, then

\mathcal{H}^{n-p}(E)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Phi(|f|)+\Phi(|Df|))\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Before we start the proof of Theorem
1.2
itself, let us prove the following preparatory lemma. The lemma also holds for the case
p=n
with a slightly altered proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1<p<n
, let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. The following are equivalent

\displaystyle\text{a) }

\displaystyle\gamma^{+}_{p,1}(E)=0

\displaystyle\text{b) }

\displaystyle\gamma^{+}_{p,1}(E,G)=0

\displaystyle\text{c) }

\displaystyle\gamma_{p,1}(E)=0

\displaystyle\text{d) }

\displaystyle\gamma_{p,1}(E,G)=0.

Proof.

It is obvious that

\gamma_{p,1}(E)\leq\gamma_{p,1}^{+}(E)\leq\gamma_{p,1}^{+}(E,G)

and

\gamma_{p,1}(E)\leq\gamma_{p,1}(E,G)\leq\gamma_{p,1}^{+}(E,G).

Let us assume that
\gamma_{p,1}(E)=0
. To begin with let us assume that
E\Subset G
. By the subadditivity of the capacity (for example see
[
4
, Theorem 5.1]
) we may assume that
E\Subset G\cap B(0,1)
and it suffices to show the claim for
G
of finite measure. We find an open set
V\Subset G
and for each
\varepsilon>0
, we find a
v\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
v\geq 1
on an open set
U_{\varepsilon}
,
E\Subset U_{\varepsilon}\subset V\Subset G
and

\|Dv\|_{L^{p,1}}^{p}\leq\varepsilon.

Further we find an
\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\eta=1
on
V
,
\eta(\mathbb{R}^{n})=[0,1]
,
\eta=0
outside
G
and
|\nabla\eta|\leq M
for some
M>1
. We define
\tilde{v}=\eta v
, then
\tilde{v}
is a contender in the infimum for
\gamma_{p,1}^{+}(E,G)
. Using the embedding of
L^{p^{*}}(G)
into
L^{p,1}(G)
and the Sobolev inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{p,1}^{+}(E,G)

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{v}\|^{p}_{p,1}+\|D\tilde{v}\|_{p,1}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq CM^{p}\|\chi_{G}v\|_{p,1}^{p}+C\|Dv\|_{p,1}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq CM^{p}\|\chi_{G}v\|_{p^{*}}^{p}+C\|Dv\|_{p,1}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq CM^{p}\|Dv\|_{p}^{p}+C\|Dv\|_{p,1}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq CM^{p}\|Dv\|_{p,1}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq CM^{p}\varepsilon

We send
\varepsilon
to zero prove that
\gamma_{p,1}^{+}(E,G)=0
.

To conclude the proof it suffices to write
E=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}
, where each
E_{i}\Subset G
and use the subadditivity of the capacity.
∎

Now we may proceed to the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The proof in the case
p=n
can be found in
[
4
]
, therefore we assume that
p<n
.

Let
E
be a set such that
\gamma_{p,1}(E)=0
. Then, by Lemma
3.1
, it holds that
\gamma_{p,1}^{+}(E,G)=0
for any arbitrary open
G
containing
E
. We define a sequence of functions
f_{k}\in W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
inductively. We find
f_{1}=1
on
G_{1}\supset E
and
\|f_{1}\|_{p,1}^{p}+\|Df_{1}\|_{p,1}^{p}\leq 2^{-1}
. Having found
f_{k-1}=1
on
G_{k-1}\supset E
with
\|f_{k-1}\|_{p,1}^{p}+\|Df_{k-1}\|^{p}_{p,1}\leq 2^{1-k}
we find an
f_{k}
and a
E\subset G_{k}\Subset G_{k-1}
and (since
\gamma_{p,1}(E,G_{k-1})=0
) we may assume that
\|f_{k}\|_{p,1}^{p}+\|Df_{k}\|_{p,1}^{p}\leq 2^{-k}
. By the absolute continuity of the norm on
L^{p,1}
we have the density of smooth functions thanks to the standard convolution approximation arguments. Therefore we may assume that
f_{k}
are smooth maps. We define
F:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|f_{k}|+|Df_{k}|
. It holds that
F\in L^{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

By Theorem
2.5
we find a Young function
\Phi
such that

(3.1)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[\Phi^{\prime}(t)\big]^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,dt=1

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi(F)\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}<\infty.

We define

g_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}f_{j}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E\\
\infty&x\in E.\end{cases}

Then each
g_{k}
is smooth on each of the sets
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus G_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Further, it can easily be observed that
\lim_{r\to 0}-\hskip-11.0pt\int_{B(x,r)}g_{k}=\infty
for all
x\in E
. Thus we see that
g_{k}
is its own precise representative. Since for almost every
x
there is at most one
k
such that
Df_{k}\neq 0
we have that

Dg_{k}(x)=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}Df_{j}(x).

It follows from Theorem
2.6
and the dominated convergence theorem that

\mathcal{H}^{n-p}(E)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi(|Dg_{k}|)+\Phi(g_{k})=0.

∎

4.
Examples showing the sharpness of the result

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1<p<n
and
1<q\leq\infty
. There exists a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathcal{H}^{n-p}(E)>0
and a sequence of continuous
f_{j}\in W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|Df_{j}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0

as
j\to\infty
and
f_{j}\geq 1
on open sets
G_{j}\supset E
.

Proof.

For a convenient shorthand we define

\mathbb{V}_{n}:=\{-1,1\}^{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta:=\frac{p}{n-p}.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
w\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{k}
, we define

(4.1)

c_{w}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}2^{\beta-i(\beta+1)}w_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad A_{w}:=Q\Big(c_{w},2^{\beta-k(\beta+1)}\Big)\setminus Q\Big(c_{w},2^{-k(\beta+1)}\Big).

The set

E:=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{\beta-i(\beta+1)}w_{i};w\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}\Big\}.

is a compact Cantor-type set of positive finite
\mathcal{H}^{n-p}
measure (for this standard computation see
[
5
]
).

It holds that

Q(0,1)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{w\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{k}}A_{w}\cup E

where the sets in the above union are all pairwise disjoint.

We define
|w|:=k
if
w\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{k}
, and
|w|=\infty
if
w\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
we find the smallest
J=J(j)
such that
C_{j}:=\sum_{k=j}^{J}k^{-1}\geq 1
. We define the function
f_{j}
as follows

f_{j}(x):=\begin{cases}0\quad&x\in A_{w}\text{ for }1\leq|w|\leq j-1\\
C_{j}^{-1}\sum_{k=j}^{|w-1|}\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{|w|}\Big(\frac{2^{-(\beta+1)|w|+\beta}-|x-c_{w}|_{\infty}}{2^{-(\beta+1)|w|+\beta}-2^{-(\beta+1)|w|}}\Big)\quad&x\in A_{w}\text{ for }j\leq|w|\leq J\\
1\quad&x\in\bigcup_{w\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{J}}Q\big(c_{w},2^{-J(\beta+1)}\big).\\
\end{cases}

It is easy to verify that
f_{j}
is Lipschitz continuous. It is an easy observation that for
k\geq j
and
2^{-(k+1)n\beta}<t<2^{-kn\beta}
we have
|Df|^{*}(t)\leq k^{-1}2^{k(\beta+1)}
and therefore

\|Df_{j}\|_{p,q}^{q}=\int_{0}^{\infty}[t^{\frac{1}{p}}|Df_{j}|^{*}(t)]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\leq C\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{q}}\xrightarrow{j\to\infty}0

for
1<q<\infty
. The calculation for
q=\infty
is even simpler, i.e.

\|Df_{j}\|_{L^{p,\infty}}\leq\frac{C}{j}.

Since
f_{j}
are uniformly bounded by 1 and supported on a set of measure at most
2^{-j\beta}
we easily observe that
\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
. It follows that
\gamma_{p,q}(E)=0
by definition.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1<p<n
. There exists a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)=n-p
(but
\mathcal{H}^{n-p}(E)=0
) and a sequence of continuous
f_{j}\in W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|Df_{j}\|_{L^{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0

as
j\to\infty
and
f_{j}\geq 1
on open sets
G_{j}\supset E
.

Proof.

The construction is somewhat similar to that in Theorem
4.1
. We use the similar notation

\mathbb{V}_{n}:=\{-1,1\}^{n},\quad\beta:=\frac{p}{n-p},

for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
w\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{k}
define

c_{w}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{2^{\beta-i(\beta+1)}}{i}w_{i},\quad A_{w}:=Q\Big(c_{w},\frac{2^{\beta-k(\beta+1)}}{(k-1)}\Big)\setminus Q\Big(c_{w},\frac{2^{-k(\beta+1)}}{k}\Big),

and

E:=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{\beta-i(\beta+1)}}{i}w_{i};w\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}\Big\}.

Again we denote by
J=J(j)
the smallest number such that
C_{j}:=\sum_{k=j}^{J}k^{-1}\geq 1
. We define the function
f_{j}
as follows

f_{j}(x):=\begin{cases}0\quad&x\in A_{w}\text{ for }1\leq|w|\leq j-1\\
C_{j}^{-1}\sum_{k=j}^{|w-1|}\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{|w|}\Big(\frac{|w|\cdot 2^{-(\beta+1)|w|+\beta}-(|w|-1)|x-c_{w}|_{\infty}}{|w|\cdot 2^{-(\beta+1)|w|+\beta}-(|w|-1)2^{-(\beta+1)|w|}}\Big)\quad&x\in A_{w}\text{ for }j\leq|w|\leq J\\
1\quad&x\in\bigcup_{w\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{J}}Q\Big(c_{w},\frac{2^{-J(\beta+1)}}{J}\Big).\\
\end{cases}

Similarly to before, we have that
f_{j}
are Lipschitz. It is a simple calculation (using the definition of
\beta=\frac{p}{n-p}
) to check that

|Df_{j}|^{*}(t)\approx 2^{k\frac{n}{n-p}}\quad\text{for }t\in\Big(\frac{2^{-(k+1)\frac{np}{n-p}}}{(k+1)^{n}},\frac{2^{-k\frac{np}{n-p}}}{k^{n}}\Big)

and therefore, for
p<n
,

\|Df_{j}\|_{p,1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{1}{p}}|Df|^{*}(t)\frac{dt}{t}\leq C\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{\frac{n}{p}}}\xrightarrow{j\to\infty}0.

By covering
E
with the sets
\bigcup_{w\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{k}}Q\Big(c_{w},\frac{2^{-k(\beta+1)}}{k}\Big)
, we estimate

\mathcal{H}^{n-p}(E)\leq 2^{nk}2^{-k(\beta+1)(n-p)}k^{p-n}=k^{p-n}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. For
d<n-p
we use the standard argument of the optimality of our covering (see
[
5
]
) and the same coverings give

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{2^{-k(\beta+1)}k^{-1}}(E)\geq C2^{nk}2^{-k(\beta+1)d}k^{-d}=C2^{kn\frac{n-p-d}{n-p}}k^{-d}\to\infty

as
k\to\infty
, proving that
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}E=n-p
.
∎

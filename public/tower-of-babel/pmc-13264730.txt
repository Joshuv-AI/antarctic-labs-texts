Mapping the research landscape of photoacoustic imaging in breast cancer: a text-based bibliometric analysis
Authors: Yang Pu, Rui-Zhi Wang, Lin Ma, Yan Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPhotoacoustic imaging (PAI) is a noninvasive functional imaging modality capable of assessing tissue oxygenation and other tumor-related biological characteristics, and it has been increasingly explored in both preclinical and clinical breast cancer research. However, bibliometric evidence regarding the developmental trajectory and knowledge structure of this field remains limited. To address this gap, this study reviews PAI literature for breast cancer assessment and summarizes the research status and emerging hotspots.MethodsPublications on PAI in breast cancer were retrieved from the Science Citation Index Expanded of the Web of Science Core Collection, with a data cutoff of December 31, 2025. After screening and exclusion of non-English publications, review articles, meeting abstracts, and other ineligible document types, eligible research articles were included for bibliometric analysis. VOSviewer and the bibliometrix R package were used to analyze publication trends, geographic and institutional distributions, author collaboration, journal characteristics, and keyword evolution.ResultsA total of 214 research articles were included. Annual publication output increased progressively between 2004 and 2025, reaching a peak of 27 articles in 2025. China and the United States were the dominant contributors, jointly accounting for 71% of all publications; China ranked first in publication volume (n=89), whereas the United States showed the highest total citations (n=4,053). Jinan University was the most productive institution (n=17). Journal of Biomedical Optics was the leading journal in publication output (n=18) and also had the highest total citations (n=879). Among authors, Manohar, Srirang was the leading contributor in both publication output (n=20) and total citations (n=1,229). Keyword analysis showed that, beyond core terms, “ultrasound”, “molecular imaging”, and “photothermal therapy” were major recurring themes, whereas “radiomics”, “prediction”, and “deep learning” emerged as recent research frontiers.ConclusionsResearch on PAI in breast cancer has expanded steadily over the past two decades, with the field evolving from technology-driven system development toward clinically oriented applications. Current hotspots indicate a growing emphasis on artificial intelligence-assisted diagnosis and predictive modeling. Future work should prioritize multicenter validation, cross-platform standardization, and deeper integration of PAI with multimodal imaging and intelligent analytical methods to facilitate broader clinical translation.

What is known and what is new?

• It is well recognized that PAI originated from biomedical optics and device innovation, as reflected by the prominence of technical journals and highly cited system-development studies.

• Our analysis newly shows that the field is now moving toward clinical adoption, with increasing output in journals such as Academic Radiology and Breast Cancer Research and growing emphasis on intelligent diagnostic modeling.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Although publication output is rising rapidly, much of the evidence still comes from single-center, device-specific studies. The next priority should be multicenter validation, external testing, and cross-platform standardization to improve generalizability and support broader clinical implementation of PAI in breast cancer care.

Introduction

Breast cancer remains a formidable challenge to global public health, with approximately 2.3 million new cases reported in 2022, accounting for 11.6% of all newly diagnosed malignancies in women. As the most prevalent cancer and the primary cause of cancer-related mortality among females worldwide (1), the disease necessitates a multi-faceted clinical approach. Current evidence consistently underscores that early detection, precise diagnostic stratification, and timely intervention are fundamental to enhancing patient quality of life (2,3) and substantially reducing mortality rates (4). Consequently, there is an urgent clinical demand for advanced imaging modalities that can provide comprehensive, multi-dimensional pathophysiological insights to optimize personalized management strategies.

Photoacoustic imaging (PAI) has emerged as a transformative functional imaging modality that synergizes the high contrast of optical spectroscopy with the deep tissue penetration and high spatial resolution of ultrasound (5-7). By utilizing the differential optical absorption properties of biological tissues, PAI operates predominantly within the near-infrared spectrum where hemoglobin—specifically oxyhemoglobin and deoxyhemoglobin—serves as the principal endogenous contrast agent (8,9). This allows PAI to move beyond the purely anatomical constraints of conventional ultrasound, enabling the precise quantification of tissue oxygen saturation and blood volume (10,11). Such capabilities provide a unique window into the tumor microenvironment, capturing critical metabolic signatures such as hypoxia and pathological angiogenesis, which are pivotal for differential diagnosis and the longitudinal monitoring of therapeutic efficacy (12).

Bibliometric analysis employs rigorous mathematical and statistical methodologies to quantitatively map the knowledge architecture and evolutionary trajectories of specific scientific domains (13-15). While the application of traditional ultrasound in breast cancer has been extensively evaluated through prior bibliometric lenses (16), a comprehensive, systematic assessment specifically dedicated to the landscape of PAI is currently absent. As the field undergoes an accelerating transition from benchtop technical prototyping to bedside clinical translation, the lack of a structured synthesis of existing literature hinders the identification of research gaps. Therefore, a comprehensive roadmap that distills past academic milestones and delineates future priorities is essential for the continued maturation of this field.

To address this gap, the present study systematically evaluates the academic literature concerning PAI for breast cancer evaluation up to December 31, 2025, offering a holistic overview of the contemporary research landscape and emerging hotspots. By delineating the evolutionary trajectory of the field and identifying nascent trends such as radiomics and deep learning integration, this analysis seeks to provide a strategic framework for researchers and clinicians alike. Ultimately, this work aims to catalyze the clinical implementation of PAI technology, ensuring its potential is fully realized in the fight against breast cancer. We present this article in accordance with the BIBLIO reporting checklist (available at https://gs.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/gs-2026-1-0106/rc).

Methods

Data source and retrieval strategy

Bibliographic data for this study were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), specifically utilizing the Science Citation Index Expanded (SCIE) edition, with a data cutoff date of December 31, 2025. To optimize both retrieval precision and comprehensiveness, a targeted search query combining Title and Author Keywords was implemented as illustrated in Figure 1, while the dataset was strictly limited to original research articles published in English to ensure high academic quality and linguistic consistency throughout the analysis.

Flowchart of the search strategy in the study. AK, author keywords; MSOT, multispectral optoacoustic tomography; PACT, photoacoustic computed tomography; WoSCC, Web of Science Core Collection; TI, title.

Eligibility criteria and screening process

A rigorous multi-stage manual screening process was enforced to ensure the relevance of the included literature, beginning with an initial review of titles and abstracts followed by a detailed full-text examination where necessary. Studies were eligible for inclusion if they met the following predefined criteria: a primary focus on the clinical or experimental application of PAI for breast cancer management—including detection, diagnosis, molecular subtyping, or therapeutic monitoring—the involvement of either human subjects or preclinical breast tumor models, and the utilization of PAI or its derived hybrid modalities such as multispectral optoacoustic tomography and photoacoustic computed tomography (PACT).

Bibliometric analysis and statistical analysis

Quantitative bibliometric analysis and network visualization were performed using VOSviewer and the Bibliometrix package in R, organized into a three-dimensional analytical framework. This framework encompassed the examination of annual publication trends and the distribution of contributions across countries, institutions, and journals; the mapping of co-authorship patterns to reveal the strength of geographic and institutional cooperative links; and the application of citation metrics alongside author keyword co-occurrence analysis to identify landmark works, evolving research hotspots, and emerging frontiers in the field. In the resulting visualization maps, the size of the nodes (circles) is proportional to the publication volume or citation count, while the thickness of the connecting lines reflects the intensity of collaborative or co-occurrence relationships between the analyzed entities.

Results

Annual publication output and trends

The initial search retrieved 444 records related to PAI in breast cancer evaluation. Following a rigorous screening process to exclude non-English manuscripts, review articles, meeting abstracts, and other document types, a total of 214 research articles were retained for the final bibliometric analysis. Between 2004 and 2025, 1,187 authors contributed to these 214 research articles across 96 journals. Discussion

This bibliometric study provides a systematic analysis of the research progress and evolving landscape of PAI in breast cancer evaluation through December 31, 2025. By examining critical indicators—including publication output, multi-scale collaboration networks, journal impact, and keyword trajectories—this analysis delineates the developmental trajectory of the field over the past two decades. These findings serve as a comprehensive overview and a strategic foundation, offering evidence-based insights to identify emerging research frontiers and accelerate the clinical translation of PAI technology in oncology.

General information

The global development of PAI has been led primarily by China and the United States, which collectively contribute more than 70% of the overall research output. An interesting pattern is that, unlike the Netherlands and Germany, where research has focused largely on system development and hardware optimization, China has shown a growing tendency to direct PAI research toward clinical translation. In this context, Jinan University has emerged as one of the most representative and productive institutions. Through collaborations with industry partners such as Mindray, this group has published a series of studies on the application of PAI in breast cancer, particularly in clinical diagnosis, treatment-related evaluation, and prognostic assessment. While these findings are promising, caution is warranted. Most of the available studies have been conducted within single-center, single-device settings, which may introduce patient cohort overlap and hardware-dependent bias. Thus, although China has contributed substantially to the expansion of this field, the current body of evidence should still be regarded as preliminary. To move PAI closer to routine clinical adoption, future research should prioritize multicenter validation, external testing, and technical standardization across different platforms.

Author-level analysis further suggests that highly productive researchers tend to achieve sustained output by continuously deepening their work along specific research trajectories within the field. A representative example is the group led by Manohar and colleagues, whose research evolved from early phantom construction and laboratory prototype development to initial human imaging, clinical feasibility assessment, characterization of lesion imaging patterns, and subsequent system optimization (21-24). In contrast, the Dong group has focused more strongly on the clinical application of PAI in breast disease, showing a clear progression from benign–malignant differentiation (25) to molecular pathological subtyping (26), lymph node metastasis assessment (27), and treatment-related response monitoring (28). Their studies have progressively incorporated oxygen saturation measurements, radiomics, deep learning, and multimodal fusion models into clinical decision-making scenarios, highlighting the considerable translational potential of PAI.

The journal distribution further reflects the developmental trajectory of PAI from a technology-driven field toward broader clinical translation. Although the discipline remains strongly rooted in engineering and applied physics, its presence in clinically oriented journals has increased steadily in recent years. Journal of Biomedical Optics remains the leading outlet in terms of productivity and citation influence, underscoring its central role in the technical advancement of the field. At the same time, the growing appearance of PAI-related studies in clinically influential journals, such as Academic Radiology and Breast Cancer Research, may represent an important sign of translational progress. This shift suggests that PAI is increasingly attracting attention from the broader medical community and is gradually being recognized by oncologists and radiologists as a promising functional complement to conventional diagnostic modalities (29).

Research hotspots

Tumor angiogenesis and subsequent intratumoral hypoxia are pivotal biological drivers of breast cancer progression, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance (30,31). PAI utilizes endogenous hemoglobin as a natural chromophore to non-invasively capture the functional state and oxygen metabolism dynamics of the tumor microcirculation with high spatial resolution (32,33). By quantifying parameters such as oxygen saturation and total hemoglobin concentration, PAI constructs three-dimensional metabolic maps of lesions, providing direct quantitative evidence for understanding aggressive biological behaviors that remain inaccessible to conventional anatomical imaging (34,35).

In the clinical management of breast nodules, PAI significantly enhances diagnostic specificity and risk stratification by supplementing morphological data with functional metabolic insights (12). Large-scale clinical evidence demonstrates that optoacoustic/ultrasound dual-modality imaging can downgrade the risk assessment of benign masses, effectively reducing unnecessary biopsies without compromising sensitivity (36). Beyond simple differentiation, PAI’s utility extends to molecular subtyping and preoperative staging, where primary tumor functional scores correlate with specific molecular subtypes and sentinel lymph node metastasis (26-28). To further facilitate clinical translation, future efforts must focus on establishing a standardized PAI-Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) lexicon and transitioning from operator-dependent handheld probes to automated three-dimensional whole-breast scanning systems to ensure diagnostic reproducibility.

The ability of PAI to monitor real-time shifts in oxygen saturation (SO2) and vascular density enables the early assessment of neoadjuvant chemotherapy response, capturing functional changes that often precede morphological alterations (37,38). Furthermore, PAI facilitates the non-invasive prediction of Ki-67 proliferation indices and HER2 status, while multi-spectral analysis provides novel insights into lipid and collagen distribution for surgical margin evaluation (26,28). In the realm of nanomedicine, the integration of PAI within photoacoustic-photothermal platforms allows for precise localization and real-time tracking of functional nanocarriers (39,40). Future research should prioritize the development of metabolizable photoacoustic contrast agents, similar to SonoVue, together with advanced spectral unmixing techniques to accurately distinguish residual tumor cells from inflammatory signals caused by treatment.

PAI-based radiomics and deep learning represent the vanguard of breast cancer evaluation, leveraging high-throughput feature extraction to achieve precise prediction beyond human visual perception (41-44). Current research highlights the potential of integrated models that combine deep learning, radiomics, and clinical factors to identify molecular subtypes and predict axillary lymph node metastasis (27,45). To further optimize these frameworks, future studies should move beyond arbitrary radial distances for peritumoral analysis. Instead, leveraging PAI-specific functional maps, such as SO2 gradients and total hemoglobin concentration (HbT) concentration heatmaps, through unsupervised clustering or gradient-based boundary detection allows for the construction of metabolic peritumoral zones. This biologically grounded approach accurately captures the metabolic inflection points from hyper-vascularized tumor regions to normal tissue, significantly enhancing the interpretability and predictive power of radiomic features regarding microenvironmental heterogeneity.

This study has several limitations. First, reliance on the WoSCC database may introduce selection bias toward English-language publications. Second, citation metrics often exhibit a time-lag bias, failing to reflect the immediate impact of the 2024–2025 clinical investigations which have not yet had sufficient time to accumulate citations commensurate with their value. Finally, as a bibliometric overview, this analysis provides a quantitative roadmap of research trajectories rather than a qualitative synthesis of clinical efficacy.

Conclusions

This bibliometric analysis delineates the transformative journey of PAI from a physics-based prototype to a burgeoning clinical diagnostic modality. The recent surge in productivity, particularly within China’s industrial-academic hubs, underscores the technology’s readiness for bedside translation. However, to overcome the current hurdles of single-center bias and lack of standardization, the field must now shift its focus toward large-scale, multi-center clinical trials. Ultimately, the integration of PAI with AI-driven radiomics holds the promise of a more precise, functional, and personalized approach to breast cancer management.

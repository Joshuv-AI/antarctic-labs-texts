Macdonald Index From Refined Kontsevich-Soibelman Operator
George Andrews, Anindya Banerjee, Ranveer Kumar Singh, Runkai Tao

Abstract
We propose a refinement of the Kontsevich-Soibelman operator for a class of “special” 4d 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 superconformal field theories characterized by the following conditions: (1) their Coulomb branch admits a source/sink chamber, i.e., a chamber in which the BPS quiver consists of only source and sink nodes, (2) The nodes with valency greater than 2 of the BPS quiver in a source/sink chamber are either all sources or all sinks. We present strong evidence that the trace of this refined operator is related to the Macdonald index of the theory. In particular, we conjecture new closed form expressions for the Macdonald indices of the ( A 1 , 𝔤 ) (A_{1},\mathfrak{g}) Argyres-Douglas theories for any simply-laced Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} .

II
Kontsevich-Soibelman Operator

Any 4d
{\cal N}=2
theory at a generic point on the Coulomb branch is IR free and is characterized by a
\mathrm{U(1)}^{r}
gauge theory. The massive spectrum of the theory consists of particles carrying electric and magnetic charges valued in the charge lattice
\Gamma
equipped with a Dirac pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. The spectrum of BPS states is particularly interesting because they behave nicely under certain supersymmetry-preserving RG flows. The spectrum of BPS states with a given charge
\gamma\in\Gamma
is captured by the protected spin character (PSC) defined as follows
17
: let
{\cal H}^{\gamma}_{\mathrm{int}}
be the internal Hilbert space of the BPS particle with charge
\gamma
. Then the PSC of
\gamma
is defined by

\begin{split}\Omega(\gamma,y):=\mathrm{Tr}_{{\cal H}_{\text{int}}^{\gamma}}(-1)^{R}y^{J+R}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{n}(\gamma)y^{n}~,\end{split}

(1)

where
J
is the generator of the Cartan subalgebra of the little group SU(2) of the Lorentz group SO(1,3) and
R
is the Cartan generator of the SU(2) R-symmetry group. As one moves in the moduli space, the BPS spectrum is piecewise constant but may jump discontinuously across certain codimension one subloci, called walls of marginal stability. To understand this jump, one needs to construct wall-crossing invariants. Define the quantum torus algebra to be the formal span of generators

\begin{split}{\cal A}:=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{X_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}~,\end{split}

(2)

with the multiplication on
{\cal A}
defined as

\begin{split}X_{\gamma}X_{\gamma^{\prime}}=q^{\frac{\left\langle\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle}{2}}X_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}=q^{\left\langle\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle}X_{\gamma^{\prime}}X_{\gamma}~,\end{split}

(3)

where
q
is a formal variable. For each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we construct the following element of
{\cal A}
:

\begin{split}U_{\gamma}=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{q}\left((-1)^{n}q^{n/2}X_{\gamma}\right)^{(-1)^{n}\Omega_{n}(\gamma)}~,\end{split}

(4)

where
E_{q}(X)
is the quantum dilogarithm

\begin{split}E_{q}(X):=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(1+q^{i+\frac{1}{2}}z\right)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-q^{\frac{1}{2}}z\right)^{n}}{(q)_{n}}~.\end{split}

(5)

The Kontsevich-Soibelman (KS) operator is defined as

\begin{split}{\cal O}(q):=\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}^{\curvearrowright}U_{\gamma}~,\end{split}

(6)

where the product is taken in the order of increasing
\text{Arg}(Z(\gamma))
with respect to an arbitrarily chosen phase
\theta
, where
Z(\gamma)
is the central charge corresponding to the charge
\gamma
. The celebrated result of Kontsevich and Soibelman
20
is that the operator
{\cal O}(q)
is a wall crossing invariant. Thus, if one can relate physically relevant quantities to this operator, then one can compute it at any point in the moduli space and glean information about the UV theory at the origin of the moduli space. This was the motivation behind the conjecture of Cordova-Shao
10
relating the Schur index of a 4d
{\cal N}=2
SCFT to the trace of the KS operator.

II.1
Trace Of KS Operator And Schur Index

Let us begin by quoting the conjecture from
10
: it states that the Schur index of a 4d
{\cal N}=2
SCFT is given by an appropriately defined trace:

\begin{split}{{\cal I}}_{\mathrm{S}}(q,z_{1},\dots,z_{N_{f}}):=(q)_{\infty}^{2r}\,\mathrm{Tr}\,{\cal O}(q)~,\end{split}

(7)

where
r
is the rank of the theory,
z_{1},\dots,z_{N_{f}}
are fugacities for flavor symmetries and

\begin{split}(a)_{\infty}:=(a;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{i})~.\end{split}

(8)

The factor
(q)_{\infty}^{2r}
is simply the Schur index of
r
free U(1) vector multiplets which is the contribution of the free
\text{U(1)}^{r}
vector multiplets at a generic point on the Coulomb branch.
Recall that a charge
\gamma\in\Gamma
is called a flavor charge if
\langle\gamma,\Gamma\rangle=0
. Let
\{\gamma_{f_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{N_{f}}
be an integral basis of the sublattice of flavor charges. For any charge
\gamma
, let

\begin{split}\gamma_{f}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}}f_{i}(\gamma)\gamma_{f_{i}}~,\end{split}

(9)

be the projection on to the subspace of flavor charges.
We define

\begin{split}\mathrm{Tr}[X_{\gamma}]=\begin{cases}\prod_{i}\operatorname{Tr}\left[X_{\gamma_{f_{i}}}\right]^{f_{i}(\gamma)}&\left\langle\gamma,\Gamma\right\rangle=0~,\\
0&\text{ else },\end{cases}~.\end{split}

(10)

Imposing the cyclicity of the trace, the trace of any element of
{\cal A}
can then be written in terms of
q
and
N_{f}
variables
\text{Tr}[X_{\gamma_{f_{i}}}]
. These variables are functions of the flavor fugacities
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
and the explicit functional relation can be determined as explained in
10
. In
10
, the trace of the KS operator was computed for a large class of examples and the conjecture (
7
) was verified.

III
The Refined KS Operator

In this section, we define a refined KS operator for a “special” class of 4d
{\cal N}=2
SCFTs.

III.1
BPS Quiver And Source/Sink Chamber

The BPS quiver of a 4d
{\cal N}=2
theory
12
captures the spectrum of BPS states of the theory at a given point the Coulomb branch. We will not go into relevance of quivers in 4d
\mathcal{N}=2
theories in this work, and instead refer readers to
12
;
11
;
21
;
22
for more details. The rules to draw the BPS quiver are as follows
8
;
2
:

1.

Draw a node for each basis element of the charge lattice.

2.

If for two nodes
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}
,
\langle\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}\rangle>0
, draw
\langle\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}\rangle
arrows from node
i
to node
j
and if
\langle\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}\rangle<0
then draw
|\langle\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}\rangle|
arrows from
j
to
i
.

Note that the quiver depends on a choice of basis and choosing a different basis gives a different quiver.
Some 4d
{\cal N}=2
theories admit a source/sink chamber, meaning that the charge lattice at a given point in this chamber admits a basis in which the BPS quiver has only source and sink nodes
2
2

2

A node with all arrows emanating from it is called a source node and a node with all arrows ending on it is called a sink node.
.
The (conjectured) crucial property
8
;
2
of the source/sink chamber is that the central charges corresponding to the nodes satisfy

\begin{split}\mathrm{Arg}(Z(\gamma))>\mathrm{Arg}(Z(\gamma^{\prime}))~,~~\text{for all}~\gamma\in\text{sink},~\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathrm{source}~.\end{split}

(11)

Moreover, it is clear that

\begin{split}[X_{\gamma},X_{\gamma}]=0~,\quad\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\text{sources, or},~~\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\text{sinks}~.\end{split}

(12)

Thus, one can compute the KS operator in the source/sink chamber:

\begin{split}{\cal O}(q)=\prod_{\gamma\in\text{source}}U_{-\gamma}\prod_{\gamma\in\text{sink}}U_{-\gamma}\prod_{\gamma\in\text{source}}U_{\gamma}\prod_{\gamma\in\text{sink}}U_{\gamma}~.\end{split}

(13)

III.2
Refined KS Operator

We consider 4d
{\cal N}=2
SCFTs which satisfy the following two properties:

1.

The theory admits a source/sink chamber.

2.

The nodes in the BPS quiver at a point in the source/sink chamber with valency greater than 2 are either all sources or all sinks.

The
(G,G^{\prime})
Argyres-Douglas theories introduced in
8
are prime examples of this special class of theories. Indeed, their moduli space admits a source/sink chamber. Each node in the BPS quiver in the source/sink chamber represents a single hypermultiplet with trivial internal degrees of freedom. Moreover, from the general rules laid out in
8
;
2
for the BPS quiver of
(G,G^{\prime})
theories, one can prove that the second condition is also satisfied.
Define the functions

\begin{split}E_{q,T}(X_{\gamma})&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-(qT)^{\frac{1}{2}}X_{\gamma})^{n}}{(q)_{n}},\\
\widetilde{E}_{q,T}(X_{\gamma})&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}}X_{\gamma})^{n}}{(qT)_{n}}~.\end{split}

(14)

Define the refined KS operator in the source/sink chamber by

\begin{split}{\cal O}(q,T):=&\prod_{\gamma\in\text{source}}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{q,T}((-1)^{n}q^{n/2}X_{-\gamma})^{(-1)^{n}\Omega_{n}(-\gamma)}\\
\times&\prod_{\gamma\in\text{sink}}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{E}_{q,T}((-1)^{n}q^{n/2}X_{-\gamma})^{(-1)^{n}\Omega_{n}(-\gamma)}\\
\times&\prod_{\gamma\in\text{source}}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{q,T}((-1)^{n}q^{n/2}X_{\gamma})^{(-1)^{n}\Omega_{n}(\gamma)}\\
\times&\prod_{\gamma\in\text{sink}}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{q}((-1)^{n}q^{n/2}X_{\gamma})^{(-1)^{n}\Omega_{n}(\gamma)}~.\end{split}

(15)

If the BPS quiver has a source node with valency greater than 2, then we define the refined KS operator as:

\begin{split}{\cal O}(q,T):=&\prod_{\gamma\in\text{source}}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{E}_{q,T}((-1)^{n}q^{n/2}X_{-\gamma})^{(-1)^{n}\Omega_{n}(-\gamma)}\\
\times&\prod_{\gamma\in\text{sink}}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{q,T}((-1)^{n}q^{n/2}X_{-\gamma})^{(-1)^{n}\Omega_{n}(-\gamma)}\\
\times&\prod_{\gamma\in\text{source}}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{q}((-1)^{n}q^{n/2}X_{\gamma})^{(-1)^{n}\Omega_{n}(\gamma)}\\
\times&\prod_{\gamma\in\text{sink}}\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{q,T}((-1)^{n}q^{n/2}X_{\gamma})^{(-1)^{n}\Omega_{n}(\gamma)}~.\end{split}

(16)

Note that, at the moment, we can define the refined KS operator only in the source/sink chamber.

IV
Macdonald Index From Modified KS Operator: A Conjecture

Recall that the Macdonald index of the free vectormultiplet is given by

\begin{split}{{\cal I}}_{\text{M}}^{\text{vec}}(q,T)=(q)_{\infty}(qT)_{\infty}~.\end{split}

(17)

We conjecture that the Macdonald index of the class of 4d
{\cal N}=2
SCFTs for which the modified KS operator is defined is given by

\begin{split}{{\cal I}}_{\text{M}}(q,T,z_{1},\dots,z_{N_{f}})=(q)^{r}_{\infty}(qT)^{r}_{\infty}\text{Tr}\,{\cal O}(q,T)~,\end{split}

(18)

where
r
is the rank of the theory.
Assuming the conjecture in
10
, this formula trivially satisfies the correct Schur limit
T\to 1
.

V
Future Directions

The intriguing relation we have found between Macdonald index of a 4d
{\cal N}=2
SCFT and the trace of the modified KS operator raises a lot of interesting questions. We list here some possible future directions. The first order problem is to perform more checks of our conjecture. Secondly, we would like to understand how the refined KS operator transforms under quiver mutations
8
;
2
. More generally, we would like to give a chamber independent prescription for the refined KS operator. Once we have a chamber independent prescription, our formulas for Argyres-Douglas theory gives strong evidence that the modified KS operator is a wall-crossing invariant.

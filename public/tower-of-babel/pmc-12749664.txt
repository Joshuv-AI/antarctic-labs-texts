Effect of novel irrigation solutions on the accuracy of Woodpex III and two integrated apex locators
Authors: Güliz Rana Tellioğlu Avcı, Merve Yeniçeri Özata
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This in vitro study aimed to evaluate the effect of four irrigation solutions [sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl), ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), Dual Rinse HEDP (DR-HEDP), and Triton] on the accuracy of measurements performed by three apex locators (Woodpex III Gold Plus, EndoRadar Pro, and X-Smart Pro +) using a hand file, and by the two integrated motors (EndoRadar Pro and X-Smart Pro +) using a glide path file with the ‘auto-stop (AS)’ mode. The actual working length (AWL) was determined for 240 Vertucci type 1 teeth. A total of 80 teeth were allocated for the hand file measurement groups, which were divided into four irrigation solution groups (n = 20 each). A sequential protocol was applied within each group. For instance, in the NaOCl group, the electronic working length (EWL) was measured sequentially using the Woodpex III Gold Plus, EndoRadar Pro, and X-Smart Pro + , with the solution being refreshed between each device measurement. This sequential process was repeated for the remaining three solution groups (EDTA, DR-HEDP, and Triton) (n = 20 for all 12 groups). Separately, 160 teeth were assigned for measurements in AS mode using the glide path files integrated into the two endomotors. These teeth were first divided into two main groups (n = 80 each) and subsequently subdivided into 4 groups based on the irrigation solution used, and the EWL measurements were completed (n = 20 for all 8 groups). AWL and EWL differences were evaluated within a ± 0.5 mm tolerance. The main effects of the solution and apex locator were not significant (p > 0.05). No significant difference was found between the hand file and AS mode (p > 0.05). The risk of measurement values falling outside the ± 0.5 mm range was 2.158 times higher in the NaOCl compared to the Triton (p = 0.036). Triton and DR-HEDP allowed for measurements to be within the ± 0.5 mm range in a greater number of samples.

Introduction

The apical constriction is the terminal anatomical reference point where root canal treatment should be terminated1. The conventional radiographic technique alone is often insufficient for the clinical determination of the apical constriction, due to the variable anatomy of this regioun and the non-coincidence of the radiographic and anatomical apices in at least 50% of cases2–4. The detection of apical constriction is possible with electronic apex locator devices, which offer an accuracy rate of over 90%5.

Woodpex III (Woodpecker Medical Instrument Co., Guilin, China) was introduced as a 5th-generation apex locator, boasting increased precision due to its multifrequency technology and a new algorithm6. While this apex locator, which has been the subject of limited studies in the literature, was reported to have sufficient accuracy in primary tooth pulpectomy7, it has been shown to determine canal length more accurately than Propex Pixi in permanent teeth8. Endodontic motors with integrated apex locators offer the opportunity to continuously monitor the apical region simultaneously with mechanical preparation, while controlling the torque and speed of the files9. EndoRadar Pro is a “two-in-one” wireless endo motor capable of simultaneous root canal length measurement. It can be used as an endo motor for root canal preparation, as an apex locator to measure canal length, or in a combined mode10. Similarly, the X-Smart Pro + , introduced by Dentsply Sirona in 2023, is also an integrated apex locator. The company has defined the accuracy of the device’s simultaneous canal length measurement as “dynamic accuracy.” Unlike EndoRadar Pro, this device can measure canal length in integrated mode using only the file, without a lip clip11. Both endo motors can be used in “auto stop (AS)” and “auto reverse (AR)” modes during integrated use10,11.

Determining root canal length in dry canals generally yields more accurate results compared to situations where the canals contain an irrigation solution1. However, during simultaneous measurements with integrated apex locators, the root canal must be filled with an irrigation agent, as root canal preparation is also initiated. Furthermore, numerous studies have demonstrated that the canal content affects the accuracy of apex locators due to its impact on electrical conductivity12.

In recent years, the concept of continuous chelation with ‘2-in-1’ or ‘all-in-one’ solutions has emerged as an alternative to the sequential chelation protocol of NaOCl and EDTA. Dual Rinse HEDP (DR-HEDP; Medcem, Weinfelden, Switzerland) is prepared by dissolving 0.9 g of etidronic acid (HEDP) powder from a capsule into 10 mL of 0.5% to 5.25% NaOCl. Triton (Brasseler, Savannah, United States), developed by Brasseler, contains approximately 8% NaOCl in one compartment of its two-compartment bottle and citric acid, surfactants, pH modifiers, and stabilizers in the other. Thanks to the bottle’s cap design, the solutions are mixed at a one-to-one ratio at the time of use, resulting in a solution containing 4% NaOCl13.

There are no studies in the literature evaluating the effect of DR-HEDP and Triton on the accuracy of apex locators. Furthermore, no studies have evaluated the accuracy of the X-Smart Pro + and EndoRadar Pro integrated apex locators. This in vitro study aimed to investigate the effect of NaOCl, EDTA, DR-HEDP, and Triton solutions on the accuracy of three apex locators, two of which are integrated, while also comparing the measurement methods (hand file and auto stop mode). The null hypotheses of this study can be listed as follows:H₀₁: The irrigation solutions have no significant effect on the accuracy of apex locators.H₀₂: There is no significant difference in the accuracy among the apex locators.H₀₃: The interaction between the solution and the apex locator has no significant effect on accuracy.H₀₄: There is no significant difference between the measurement methods performed with a hand file and in AS mode.

Methods

Ethical approval for this study was obtained from the Batman University Social and Human Sciences, Science-Engineering Sciences, and Health Sciences Non-Interventional Research Ethics Committee (protocol no: 2025/05–21). The authors have structured and written this laboratory study manuscript following the Preferred Reporting Items for Laboratory studies in Endodontology (PRILE) 2021 guidelines (Fig. 1)14.Fig. 1PRILE 2021 flowchart of this study.

PRILE 2021 flowchart of this study.

Sample selection and preparation

The teeth utilized in this study were obtained from patients who underwent tooth extraction due to periodontal or orthodontic indications at the oral and maxillofacial surgery department of our faculty, following the acquisition of their routine informed consent. Teeth with cracks, defects, caries, and restorations were excluded from the study. Subsequently, access cavity preparation was performed on the remaining teeth. The major apical foramina of the teeth were visualized under a dental microscope (Semorr 3000E-4 K, Bondent, China) at × 16 magnification, and teeth with an apical diameter larger than a size #20 K-file were excluded. Radiographs of the teeth were taken in both buccolingual and mesiodistal directions, and 240 teeth with a Vertucci type I configuration were included in the study. The teeth were numbered. The dental crowns were slightly flattened merely to provide a flat reference point. Preparation was performed in the coronal third using a ProTaper SX file (Dentsply Maillefer, Ballaigues, Switzerland). During coronal preparation, irrigation was performed using 5 mL of 5% NaOCl with a 30-gauge, side-vented irrigation needle. Afterward, the canals were rinsed with 10 ml of distilled water and dried with paper points.

Determination of actual working length (AWL)

Under a dental microscope at × 16 magnification, a size #10 or #15 K-file (Dentsply Maillefer) was advanced along the root canal until its tip was visible at the level of the apical foramen (AF) opening. After fixing a double stopper at the reference point, the distance between the file tip and the stopper was measured three times with a digital caliper (Mitutoyo CD15, Mitutoyo, Kawasaki, Japan) with a precision of 0.01 mm. The average of these three measurements was calculated. Thus, the AWL relative to the major apical foramen was determined.

Preparation of the alginate measurement setup

The sample teeth were embedded in an alginate-filled container up to 2 mm below the cementoenamel junction. Subsequently, leaving a space for the lip clip to be placed, the samples were fixed to the container with cold-cure acrylic resin (Imicryl SC; Imicryl, Türkiye). To ensure accurate measurements, the alginate was kept moist, and the setup for each group was used within 30 min of preparation.

Determination of electronic working length (EWL)

The entire procedure was performed by an endodontist (G.R.T.A.) with 4 years of experience.

EWL measurements with hand file

Eighty teeth were divided into four subgroups according to the solution: Group A, NaOCl (5%); Group B, EDTA (17%); Group C, DR-HEDP; Group D, Triton. The teeth in the groups were irrigated with 6 ml of the designated solutions. After aspirating the excess fluid from the pulp chamber, EWL measurements were initially performed on the same teeth in the alginate model using the Woodpex III Gold Plus with files (#10K, #15K) that achieved a snug fit in the canal. This completed the measurements for groups 1A (n = 20), 1B (n = 20), 1C (n = 20), and 1D (n = 20).

Subsequently, to conduct the hand file EWL measurements with the EndoRadar Pro, all teeth were re-irrigated with an additional 6 mL of their respective solutions (consistent with the previous application) to refresh the irrigants. The EWL measurements were then recorded, completing the data for groups 2A (n = 20), 2B (n = 20), 2C (n = 20), and 2D (n = 20).

Finally, following a similar irrigation protocol and measurement sequence, the EWL measurements were performed using the X-Smart Pro + , thus completing the measurements for groups 3A (n = 20), 3B (n = 20), 3C (n = 20), and 3D (n = 20).

Group 1. Woodpex III Gold Plus

After turning on the device, the file clip was attached to the lip clip, and the operator waited for the “-3” light to illuminate for 5 s to ensure device calibration. This was repeated for the measurement of all teeth. The clip was placed into the alginate. Excess irrigation solution in the coronal part of the tooth was absorbed with cotton pellets. An appropriately sized file was advanced into the canal, and when it reached the final “orange” bar, the position of the apical foramen was determined. The advancement was then stopped, and the double stopper on the file’s shaft was fixed at the reference point. After waiting for 5 s, if no change was observed, the EWL was measured three times using a digital caliper, and the average was then calculated.

Group 2. EndoRadar Pro

After pressing the En-Ap button on the device, the file clip was attached to the lip clip, and the operator waited for the “OVER” light to illuminate for 5 s to ensure calibration. When the advanced file reached the final “green” bar, the position of the apical foramen was determined. The same measurement protocol as in Group 1 was then applied.

Group 3. X-Smart Pro +

After turning on the device, the file clip was attached to the lip clip, and the operator waited for the “green confirmation light” to illuminate, ensuring calibration. Although the manufacturer recommends finalizing the measurement in the middle-apical region, the device indicates the apical foramen region with the final “orange” bar. The file was advanced until it reached the “orange bar” to determine the position of the apical foramen. The same measurement protocol as in Groups 1 and 2 was then applied.

EWL measurements in auto stop (AS) mode

One hundred sixty teeth were randomly divided into two groups. Eighty teeth for EndoRadar Pro and eighty teeth for X-Smart Pro + were subdivided according to the solutions to perform length measurements using the Auto Stop (AS) mode: Group A. NaOCl (5%), Group B. EDTA (17%), Group C. DR-HEDP, Group D. Triton. The teeth were irrigated with a total volume of 6 ml per tooth according to their assigned solution group. In the group where measurements were made using the EndoRadar Pro in AS mode, a preliminary EWL determination with a hand file and apex locator was not performed, as this mode conducts simultaneous root canal length determination. Following the device’s operational procedure (as described), the EWL measurement was performed simultaneously with the integrated glide path file. Thus, the measurements for groups 4A (n = 20), 4B (n = 20), 4C (n = 20), and 4D (n = 20) were completed.

Subsequently, a new set of teeth was introduced. These teeth were irrigated with 6 ml of the respective solutions, similar to the protocol of Group 4, and the canal length was measured with the glide path file using the X-Smart Pro + in AS mode. Thus, the measurements for groups 5A (n = 20), 5B (n = 20), 5C (n = 20), and 5D (n = 20) were completed. Measurements were performed using a ProGlider rotary file (#0.16, Dentsply Maillefer, Ballaigues, Switzerland) at 300 rpm and 4 Ncm torque.

Group 4. EndoRadar Pro (with AS mode)

After the device was turned on, the file clip was removed from the apex locator cable. A USB cable was then used to connect the motorized handpiece to the device. After pressing the En-Ap button, the file attached to the motorized handpiece was touched to the lip clip for 5 s to achieve calibration. Excess irrigation solution in the coronal part of the tooth was absorbed with cotton pellets. The program for the glide path file was then selected. The “AS” button on the device was pressed to activate the command. At the point where the advanced glide path file stopped, the double stopper was fixed, and the file length was measured.

Group 5. X-Smart Pro + (with AS mode)

After the device was turned on, the file attached to the motorized handpiece was touched to the lip clip, and the operator waited for the “green confirmation light” to illuminate, ensuring calibration. The AS mode was activated on the device. The program for the glide path file was then selected. At the point where the advanced glide path file stopped, the double stopper was fixed, and the file length was measured. Statistical analysis

The distance values to the apex were analyzed with SPSS Statistics (IBM SPSS Statistics version 23, IBM Inc., Armonk, NY, USA). The normality of the data was confirmed with the Shapiro–Wilk test. The effects of the irrigation solution and the apex locator were initially analyzed with a two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Main effects were compared using the Bonferroni test. To further investigate the significant interaction, a new combined variable (solution × apex locator) was created and analyzed using a one-way ANOVA. The homogeneity of variances was assessed using Levene’s test. Since homogeneity was achieved, Duncan’s test, which is known to be less conservative yet highly sensitive, was employed for post-hoc multiple comparisons. The analysis results were presented as mean ± standard deviation. The distributions of measurements within groups and tolerance ranges were evaluated using the Pearson chi-square test for apex locator groups and Fisher’s exact test for the apex locator-solution interaction. The results of these analyses are presented as frequencies and percentages. Whether the method (Hand file vs. AS mode) and irrigation solutions were risk factors was examined with Binary logistic regression analysis. The significance level was set at p < 0.05.

Discussion

Endodontic motors with integrated apex locators provide procedural convenience for clinicians by enabling simultaneous working length determination and real-time tracking of the file’s position within the canal during root canal preparation and disinfection. Considering the role of irrigation and canal disinfection in the success of endodontic treatment, the importance of the solution present in the canal during simultaneous measurements with these endomotors is evident. Although there are studies in the literature comparing integrated apex locators with each other15,16, and studies comparing their different apical functions (AS, AR, etc.)9,17, no studies are comparing the accuracy of these devices in the presence of different irrigation solutions using both hand files and simultaneous measurement.

Many studies in the literature determine EWL by subtracting 0.5 mm from measurements taken at the “apex” mark16,18,19. However, determining the location of the minor apical foramen by subtracting 0.5 mm is an estimation based on the literature20. Therefore, in methodologies similar to our study, determining the location of the major apical foramen can provide more precise results. Studies in the literature have adopted this method and validated it using micro-CT8,21,22.

This study aimed to investigate the accuracy of three apex locators, two of which were integrated, and to compare measurement methods (hand file and AS mode). It also evaluated the effect of NaOCl, EDTA, DR-HEDP, and Triton solutions on these measurements. According to the study’s results, the main effects of the solution and the apex locator did not create a significant difference in measurement accuracy, and thus, hypotheses H0₁ and H0₂ were accepted.

A meta-analysis including 15 studies evaluated the accuracy of different generations of apex locators and detected no significant difference among the 3rd, 4th, 5th, and 6th generations23. Similarly, we found no significant difference among the apex locators in the measurements performed using a hand file. Our study is consistent with the literature regarding the lack of a significant difference between the main effects.

Although not statistically significant, our study yielded a higher mean deviation (0.19 ± 0.39 mm) in the EndoRadar Pro (AS) group compared to the manually measured EndoRadar Pro group (0.17 ± 0.32 mm), regardless of the solution used. This was supported by the fact that this apex locator produced measurements outside the ± 0.5 mm range in 23 teeth (see Fig. 4). Similarly, the X-Smart Pro + (AS) group (0.09 ± 0.37 mm) had a higher mean deviation compared to the manually measured X-Smart Pro + group (0.05 ± 0.33 mm). This is further supported by the accuracy rates within the ± 0.5 mm range, which are 86.3% and 81.3%, respectively. The reason for the reduced accuracy in AS mode, though not statistically significant, may be that the inward-outward motion during the use of the glide path file makes it more difficult to detect the foramen’s location, in contrast to the smooth entry of a hand file.

The interaction between the irrigation solution and the apex locator had a significant impact on the measurements. In the presence of NaOCl, manual measurements with X-Smart Pro + were significantly closer to the AWL than those with EndoRadar Pro (AS). Therefore, the null hypothesis H0₃, stating that the interaction between the irrigation solution and apex locators would have no significant effect on accuracy, was rejected. Although the X-Smart Pro + (AS) group yielded results closer to the apical foramen than the Woodpex III Gold Plus group in the presence of DR-HEDP, it should also be noted that the mean measurement in the X-Smart Pro + (AS) group was beyond the AWL, even if within the acceptable range. This situation is concerning regarding the risk of over-preparation.

In an in-vivo study comparing the accuracy and sensitivity of Woodpex III, Propex Pixi, and Root ZX II using Micro-CT, it was stated that Woodpex III was more accurate than Propex Pixi in determining the position of the apical foramen and showed at least as much accuracy as Root ZX II8. That study reported an accuracy rate of 86.96% for Woodpex III. In our study, when the measurements made by Woodpex III Gold Plus were evaluated independently of the solution, results were within the ± 0.5 mm range in 64 out of 80 teeth (80%). It should be noted that in our methodology, the apex locator, which achieved an accuracy rate comparable to the previous study’s results, was tested with various solutions.

Due to changes in working length that occur during mechanical shaping, there is a possibility of over-instrumentation. New-generation endo motors with integrated apex locators have ‘auto stop’ and ‘auto apical reverse’ features, which are claimed to minimize the risk of file binding and fracture24,25. When evaluated separately for the four intracanal solutions, there was no significant difference between measurements made with EndoRadar Pro using a hand file (accuracy rate, 80%) and in AS mode (accuracy rate, 71.3%). Similarly, X-Smart Pro + showed no significant difference in measurements between the hand file and AS modes. Thus, hypothesis H0₄, based on the measurement method (hand file vs. AS mode), was accepted. Both integrated motors can provide clinically similar results for both pre-preparation length measurement and simultaneous length measurement. In one study, Tri Auto ZX2, Root ZX II, and VDW Gold were tested during glide path preparation, and no significant difference was found between the results for two separate apical functions (AS and optimum glide path). The authors stated that these functions could control the apical limit26. In our study, no significant difference was observed between X-Smart Pro + (accuracy rate 81.3%) and EndoRadar Pro (accuracy rate 71.3%) when used in AS mode.

Due to anatomical variations in teeth, measuring the minor apical foramen without damaging it is only possible with computed tomography27,28. In this study, considering the preparation that the glide path file would perform in the root canals, the aim was to minimize the effect of apical diameter changes by creating new tooth groups for the use of integrated endomotors.

Etidronic acid (HEDP) has emerged as a viable option as it can be mixed with sodium hypochlorite without altering its short-term antimicrobial or solvent activity29. Triton is a recently introduced “all-in-one” irrigation solution that provides continuous chelation30. Especially considering the goal of continuously irrigating and disinfecting canals with a single solution, the ability of integrated apex locators to perform simultaneous measurements and prepare canals filled with these solutions is important for clinicians. A systematic review indicated that EWL measurement after the application of 17% EDTA, followed by NaOCl, chlorhexidine, or 0.1% octenidine dihydrochloride, showed more accurate results compared to EWL measurement after the application of NaOCl alone. They attributed this to the fact that the high conductivity of sodium hypochlorite could cause inaccurate results in measurements performed with it alone1. There are no studies in the literature evaluating the effect of DR-HEDP and Triton on the accuracy of apex locators. However, in our study, when the main effect of NaOCl, EDTA, DR-HEDP, and Triton solutions on measurement accuracy was evaluated, although there was no significant difference between the groups, out of a total of 100 teeth for each solution, the measurements were within the ± 0.5 mm range in 74, 76, 83, and 86 teeth, respectively. Furthermore, when the risk of measurement values being inside or outside the ± 0.5 mm range was evaluated with Triton solution as the reference, the risk of being outside this range in the presence of NaOCl was found to be 2.158. In parallel with this systematic review, we also find that using NaOCl in a two-in-one (DR-HEDP) or all-in-one (Triton) concept, rather than alone, reduces the risk of obtaining measurements outside the ± 0.5 mm range.

This study has some limitations due to its in vitro nature. Although various studies have shown that the use of an alginate setup for apex locators corresponds with in vivo measurements9,12, this setup cannot mimic the conductivity of the periodontal ligament. Furthermore, the most accurate method for determining the actual working length is with micro-CT.

The ANOVA model indicated that the tested variables (solution and apex locator) accounted for only 4.8% of the total variance in measurement deviation. This low effect size, although statistically significant, suggests that the vast majority of the variance is likely attributable to other factors, such as the inherent anatomical variability of the apical foramen, the precision of file placement, or the intrinsic electronic ‘noise’ of the devices. Although the regression model identified a statistically significant risk (specifically, NaOCl vs. Triton), this finding highlights that these factors have a small, rather than a dominant, effect on accuracy compared to other anatomical variables. In light of this, these findings simultaneously highlight both a limitation and a methodological strength of our study. The limitation (and, concurrently, a natural consequence of an in vitro study) is that the vast majority of the variance (95.2%) in measurement deviation is attributable to factors beyond our control—particularly the inherent variability of apical anatomy—as confirmed by the low R2 value. However, the strength of our study emerges at this very point: our model proved sensitive enough to detect specific and statistically significant risks (interactions), such as that between NaOCl and Triton, despite this substantial anatomical ‘noise’ that accounts for almost all the variance. This demonstrates that the ‘small’ effect of the factors we tested does not imply they are clinically ‘irrelevant’; on the contrary, it shows that even these nuanced differences could be successfully identified.

It has been shown that these apex locators, with accuracy rates ranging from 71.3% to 86.3%, exhibit acceptable results both with hand file and integrated use. The probability of obtaining measurements closer to the apex increases in the presence of DR-HEDP and Triton compared to NaOCl. Further studies are needed to elucidate the accuracy of integrated motors in AS mode when used in conjunction with different solutions.

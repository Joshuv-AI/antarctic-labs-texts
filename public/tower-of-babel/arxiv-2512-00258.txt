Abstract
. We investigate the (ambient) bi-Lipschitz V V -equivalence of two-variable mixed polynomials satisfying the Newton inner non-degeneracy condition. Concerning triviality, we show that ambient bi-Lipschitz V V -triviality for families { f + ε ​ θ } ε ∈ ℝ \{f+\varepsilon\theta\}_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}} is guaranteed when f f is semi-radially weighted homogeneous and the weighted radial degree of every monomial in θ \theta is greater than the weighted radial degree associated with f f . However, in the general case, we prove that it is not guaranteed, even though ambient topological V V -triviality still holds. For the classification problem, we define two simple metric links and prove that they suffice to determine bi-Lipschitz V V -equivalence within the class of mixed polynomials that are Γ inn \Gamma_{\rm inn} -nice. A key outcome is that neither the Newton boundary Γ ⁡ ( f ) \Gamma(f) nor the C C -face diagram Γ inn ​ ( f ) \Gamma_{\mathrm{inn}}(f) constitutes an invariant of this equivalence for such mixed polynomials. To outcome this, we introduce new data extracted from the two face diagrams under consideration and prove that, under certain generic conditions, these data become fundamental invariants for the bi-Lipschitz equivalences. This provides a fundamental step toward a bi-Lipschitz classification of these mixed polynomials.

Introduction

Mixed polynomials in
n
variables are complex-valued functions defined as polynomials in
n
complex variables
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
and their complex conjugates
\bar{z}=(\bar{z}_{1},\ldots,\bar{z}_{n})
. This class of functions is equivalent to the class of real polynomial maps
f:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The seminal work
[
23
]
of Oka shows that this complex-variable approach has the advantage of bringing tools from the theory of holomorphic functions and complex algebraic geometry to investigate the topology of its zeros, such as Newton polyhedra, Newton and Khovanskii inner non-degeneracy, and resolution of singularities. The development of this methodology is today a very active research area, as shown by the recent works of Araújo dos Santos, Bode, Sanchez Quiceno, Espinel and Fukui in
[
1
,
2
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
17
]
.

Specifically, the recent works
[
1
]
and
[
13
]
develop a study of two-variable mixed polynomials under conditions imposed on their Newton boundary: the first one is Newton inner non-degeneracy, and the second condition is the so-called
\Gamma
-niceness. These conditions allow the authors to characterize the link
L_{f}:=\mathbb{S}^{3}_{\rho}\cap f^{-1}(0)
(in the sense of Milnor in
[
22
]
) of a mixed polynomial
f
in terms of the links associated with the regular part of the zeros of their face functions
f_{\Delta_{i}}
, where
\Delta_{i}
is a one-dimensional face of the Newton boundary. They conclude that adding monomials whose support lies strictly above the Newton boundary does not change the isotopy type of
L_{f}
or the topological type of
V(f):=f^{-1}(0)
. This result is improved in
[
15
]
, where the authors show that the link of
f
is determined solely by its restriction to a refined Newton
C
-diagram,
\Gamma_{\rm inn}(f)
.

Such results on link triviality shows that the topology of the zeros of mixed polynomials and holomorphic functions has many similarities. Motivated by a theorem of Pham and Teissier (see
[
24
]
), which states that exists a meromorphic bi-Lipschitz map between two germs of complex analytic plane curves
X
and
Y
if only if
X
and
Y
are topologically equivalent (it was extended to any bi-Lipschitz map by Fernandes in
[
16
]
, and to any ambient bi-Lipschitz map by Neumann and Pichon in
[
21
]
), it is natural to ask if the same bi-Lipschitz equivalence holds for mixed polynomials. Unfortunately, the Lipschitz geometry of real surface germs obtained from a real analytic map from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to
\mathbb{R}^{4}
can be very complicated, as it was shown by Birbrair, Mendes and Nuño-Ballesteros in
[
10
]
. When we deal with subanalytic surface germs in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, things can be very chaotic, and the extension of bi-Lipschitz maps between such germs to the ambient space is a very challenging problem. The work
[
4
]
of Birbrair, Brandenbusky and Gabrielov leads to a surprisingly infinite classification of surface germs up to ambient bi-Lipschitz equivalence, even when they are topologically equivalent and outer bi-Lipschitz equivalent (Birbrair, Denkowski, Medeiros and Sampaio showed in
[
5
]
that the same holds for germs whose outer and inner metrics are equivalent). Therefore, the relation between topology and Lipschitz geometry of mixed polynomials is not so obvious, and thus it deserves a deep investigation.

To achieve this goal, a good start is the special family of inner non-degenerate mixed polynomials introduced by Bode in
[
12
]
. He constructed, for any given braid
B
, an inner non-degenerate and
z_{1}
-semiholomorphic polynomial (a mixed polynomial
f
independent of
\bar{z}_{1}
) whose associated link is isotopic to the closure of the square of
B
(closure of
B\cdot B
). This result demonstrates that the class of inner non-degenerate mixed polynomials encompasses a significantly wider range of topological types. Further exploration of the diverse topological types realised by these mixed polynomials can be found in
[
13
]
. The family of mixed polynomials constructed by Bode is also radially weighted homogeneous mixed polynomials (radial mixed polynomials, for short) or, more generally, weighted homogeneous real polynomials. It follows from the paper
[
18
]
by Kerner and Mendes that, outside of a horn neighbourhood of the obstruction locus
\Sigma_{L}(f)
(
[
18
, Section 3.2]
) of a radial mixed polynomial
f
, the Lipschitz geometry of
V(f)
coincides with that of their higher degree deformations. More precisely, they studied the ambient bi-Lipschitz
V
-triviality for families of real map germs
\mathcal{F}=\{f+\varepsilon\theta\}_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}
, where

f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{m}):(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{m},0),\ n\geq m,

is weighted homogeneous of weight-type
(\bm{w};\bm{d})=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n};d_{1},\dots,d_{m})
, and
\theta=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{m})
is any real map germ. They defined an obstruction locus
\Sigma_{L}(f)
, and proved that under the conditions
\Sigma_{L}(f)=\{0\}
and
\operatorname{ord}_{\bm{w}}(\theta_{i})>\operatorname{ord}_{\bm{w}}(f_{i})=d_{i},\ i=1,\dots,m,
the family
\mathcal{F}
is ambient bi-Lipschitz
V
-trivial. In particular, this implies that the family is also bi-Lipschitz
V
-trivial. Another study concerning radial mixed polynomials appears in the work of Rabelo
[
25
]
, which focuses on the Lipschitz classification problem of mixed Pham-Brieskorn polynomials.

We approach the bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalence problem from the perspective of mixed polynomials in two variables that satisfy the inner non-degeneracy condition. We are motivated by the following two general questions:

\mathcal{Q}_{1}
.

Are families
\{f+\varepsilon\theta\}_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}}
of mixed polynomials (ambient) bi-Lipschitz
V
-trivial whenever
f
is inner non-degenerate and
\theta
lies above or on the radial Newton boundary of

\mathcal{Q}_{2}
.

Is the radial Newton boundary of an inner non-degenerate mixed polynomial an invariant of (ambient) bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalence?

The first question (
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
) has a negative answer in general. To reach this conclusion, we develop a study of contact order between the components of surfaces arising from inner non-degenerate mixed polynomials (see Proposition
3.12
). The main idea for this negative answer arises from two conditions, (
A
) and (
B

, which, although topologically

-trivial, is not bi-Lipschitz
V
-trivial. However, (

f
is semi-radially weighted homogeneous mixed polynomials (or semi-radial for short). These are those mixed polynomials that admit a decomposition into

satisfies

and it is radial with respect to a positive weight vector
P=(p_{1},p_{2})
and a fixed radial-degree

(referred to as radial-type
(P;d)

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a family of mixed polynomials satisfying:

(i)

f
is semi-radial of radial-type
(P;d)

d(P;\theta)\geq d

If the inequality in (ii) is strict, then the family

As a consequence of this theorem, in Corollary

is ambient bi-Lipschitz

-equivalent to its principal part

The answer to the second question (

) is also negative in general. We demonstrate this by studying two specific simple metric links: the 1-braid closure (Definition

) and the non-tangent Hopf-link (Definition

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

and

be inner non-degenerate mixed polynomials that are

-nice and such that their links

The question (

.

Let

Preliminaries

2.1.
Mixed polynomials and Newton boundaries

In this section, we review some background on mixed singularities and mixed hypersurfaces. A more detailed account of the concepts can be found in
[
1
]
and in a generalised way in
[
23
,
15
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
mixed polynomial in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a complex valued map
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
that can be represented as

f(\bm{z})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\bm{z}^{\nu_{j}}\bar{\bm{z}}^{\mu_{j}},

for
\ c_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
\nu_{j},\mu_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{2}
with
\nu_{j,i}+\mu_{j,i}\geq 1
, for each
j=1,\dots,n
and
i=1,2
. Here,
\bm{z}=(u,v)
,
\bar{\bm{z}}=(\bar{u},\bar{v})
,
\bm{z}^{\nu_{j}}={u}^{\nu_{j,1}}v^{\nu_{j,2}}
and
\bar{\bm{z}}^{\mu_{j}}=\bar{u}^{\mu_{j,1}}{\bar{v}}^{\mu_{j,2}}
(notice that
f(0)=0
).

Remark 2.2
.

Any mixed polynomial
f
can be seen as a real polynomial map
(\re(f),\im(f))
in four variables
\re(u),\im(u),\re(v),\im(v)
via the identifications
f(\bm{z})=\re(f(\bm{z}))+\mathrm{i}\im(f(\bm{z}))
and
z=(\re(u)+\mathrm{i}\im(u),\re(v)+\mathrm{i}\im(v))
. Moreover, we can transform any real polynomial map

F:(\mathbb{R}^{4},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)
into a mixed polynomial by defining it with

f(u,v)=F_{1}(\bm{x})+\mathrm{i}F_{2}(\bm{x}),\ \bm{x}=\left(\frac{u+\bar{u}}{2},\frac{u-\bar{u}}{2\mathrm{i}},\frac{v+\bar{v}}{2},\frac{v-\bar{v}}{2\mathrm{i}}\right).

Definition 2.3
.

We say that the mixed polynomial
f
is
radially weighted homogeneous
of radial-type
(P;d_{r})
, where
P=(p_{1},p_{2})\in{(\mathbb{Z}_{>0})}^{2}
and
d_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
(or radial mixed polynomial, for short), if, for all
j=1,\dots,n
, we have

(1)

p_{1}(\nu_{j,1}+\mu_{j,1})+p_{2}(\nu_{j,2}+\mu_{j,2})=d_{r}.

The weighted sum in Equation (
1
) is called the
radial degree
with respect to
P
of the monomial

c_{j}z^{\nu_{j}}\bar{z}^{\mu_{j}}
, and it is denoted by
\mathrm{rdeg}_{P}(c_{j}z^{\nu_{j}}\bar{z}^{\mu_{j}})
. Equivalently, the mixed polynomial
f
is radially weighted homogeneous of radial-type
(P;d_{r})
, if
f
satisfies

f(\lambda^{p_{1}}u,\lambda^{p_{2}}v)=\lambda^{d_{r}}f(u,v),\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

Remark 2.4
.

The radial mixed polynomials constitute a special class of weighted homogeneous polynomial map
F:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Here,
F
is homogeneous of weight-type
(\bm{w};\bm{d})=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4};d_{1},d_{2})
if, for every
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

F(\lambda^{w_{1}}x_{1},\lambda^{w_{2}}x_{2},\lambda^{w_{3}}x_{3},\lambda^{w_{4}}x_{4})=(\lambda^{d_{1}}F_{1}(\bm{x}),\lambda^{d_{2}}F_{2}(\bm{x})),

where
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
,
w_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0},d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
.
In this case, the real polynomial map
F
is called of
weighted homogeneous
of weight-type
(\bm{w};\bm{d})
. If
f
is a radially weighted homogeneous of radial-type
(p_{1},p_{2};d_{r})
, then

F=(\re(f),\im(f)):(\mathbb{R}^{4},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)

is weighted homogeneous of weight-type
(p_{1},p_{1},p_{2},p_{2};d_{r},d_{r})
.

Remark 2.5
.

While we can transform any real polynomial map

F:(\mathbb{R}^{4},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)
into a mixed polynomial (see Remark
2.2
), it is important to notice that not all weighted homogeneous real polynomial map
F
can be seen as a radial mixed polynomial. For example,

F(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=(x_{1},x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2})
is weighted homogeneous of weight-type
(1,1,1,1;1,2)
, but it does not yield a radial mixed polynomial, since

.

The homogeneity property of radial mixed polynomials allows us to derive several properties of
f

. However, radial mixed polynomials represent only a limited class in the category of mixed polynomials. To explore a broader and well-behaved class of mixed polynomials, we utilise the radial Newton polygon of

(introduced in

]
) and the inner non-degeneracy condition (introduced in

]
and amplified in

Definition 2.13

(Link-constancy)

Let

If

2.4.
Excerpts of Lipschitz geometry

Most of the preliminaries in Lipschitz geometry used in this paper are included with more detail in the survey paper
[
7
]
. We included the necessary ones to make the paper self-contained.

Definition 2.28
.

Given two metric spaces
(X_{1},d_{1})
and
(X_{2},d_{2})
, we say that a homeomorphism
\phi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
is
bi-Lipschitz for the metrics
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
(or bi-Lipschitz, for short) if there is
K\geq 1
such that

\frac{1}{K}\cdot d_{1}(p,q)\leq d_{2}(\phi(p),\phi(q))\leq K\cdot d_{1}(p,q),\quad\forall p,q\in X_{1}.

For each
K\geq 1
that satisfies such a condition, we say that
\phi
is
K
-bi-Lipschitz. Furthermore, we say that the metric spaces
(X_{1},d_{1})
and
(X_{2},d_{2})
are
bi-Lipschitz equivalent
if there is a bi-Lipschitz map
\phi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
.

Definition 2.29
.

Given
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the following two metrics in
X
:

•

outer metric of
X
: we define the outer metric of
X
as the distance
d:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, given by
d(x,y)=\|x-y\|
, for all
x,y\in X
;

•

inner metric of
X
: if
X
is path-connected, we define the inner metric of
X
as the distance
d_{X}:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, given by
d_{\rm inn}(x,y)=\inf{\{l(\alpha)\}}
, for all
x,y\in X
. The infimum is taken over all rectifiable paths
\alpha\subset X
from
x
to
y
, and
l(\alpha)
is the length of
\alpha
(if such a rectifiable path
\alpha
does not exist, we define
d_{\rm inn}(x,y)=\infty
).

Definition 2.30
.

Given two sets
X_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
X_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the following bi-Lipschitz maps from
X_{1}
to
X_{2}
:

•

outer bi-Lipschitz map
: an outer bi-Lipschitz map
\phi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
is a map that is bi-Lipschitz for the outer metrics of
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
;

•

inner bi-Lipschitz map
: an inner bi-Lipschitz map
\phi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
(assuming
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are path-connected) is a map that is bi-Lipschitz for the inner metrics of
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
.

•

ambient bi-Lipschitz map:
an ambient bi-Lipschitz map between
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
is a bi-Lipschitz map
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the outer metric, such that
\phi(X_{1})=X_{2}
. Notice that if such an ambient bi-Lipschitz map exists, then
m=n
.

Definition 2.31
.

Given two sets
X_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
X_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we say that
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are
outer bi-Lipschitz equivalent
(resp. inner, ambient) if there is an outer bi-Lipschitz (resp. inner, ambient) map between
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
. Given
p\in X_{1}
,
q\in X_{2}
, we say that two germs
(X_{1},p)
,
(X_{2},q)
are
outer bi-Lipschitz equivalent
(resp. inner, ambient) if there are neighbourhoods
U
of
p
and
V
of
q
and an outer bi-Lipschitz (resp. inner, ambient) map between
X_{1}\cap U
and
X_{2}\cap V
.

Remark 2.32
.

If
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are ambient bi-Lipschitz equivalent, then
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are outer bi-Lipschitz equivalent, and if
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are outer bi-Lipschitz equivalent, then
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are inner bi-Lipschitz equivalent. However, the converses are generally false (see
[
4
]
for counterexamples).

Remark 2.33
.

Throughout this paper, when we refer to a bi-Lipschitz map
\phi:X\to Y
(or a bi-Lipschitz map
\phi:(X,0)\to(Y,0)
), we mean that
\phi
is an outer bi-Lipschitz map. Moreover, when we say that
X
and
Y
(or
(X,0)
and
(Y,0)
) are bi-Lipschitz equivalent, this means that they are outer bi-Lipschitz equivalent.

Definition 2.34
.

We say that a path-connected set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
Lipschitz normally embedded
(or LNE, for short) if the outer metric and inner metric are bi-Lipschitz equivalent, i.e., there is a constant
K\geq 1
such that

d_{\rm inn}(x,y)\leq K\cdot d(x,y),\quad\forall\;x,y\in X.

In this case, we say that
X
is
K
-LNE. Given
p\in X
, we say that
X
is
Lipschitz normally embedded at
p
(or LNE at
p
) if there exists a neighbourhood
U
of
p
such that
X\cap U
is LNE, or equivalently, the germ
(X,p)
is LNE. If
X\cap U
is
K
-LNE for some
K\geq 1
, we say that
(X,p)
is
K
-LNE.

The following result, proved by Mendes and Sampaio in
[
20
]
, is a very useful criterion for detecting LNE set germs in terms of their links.

Theorem 2.35
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed subanalytic set, such that
0\in X
and the link of
X
is connected. Then,
X
is LNE at
0
if and only if there is a constant
K\geq 1
such that
X_{t}
is
K
-LNE for all
t>0
small enough.

Definition 2.36
.

An
arc
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(or
real half-branch
) is a germ at the origin of a map
\gamma\colon[0,\epsilon)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\gamma(0)=0
. Unless otherwise specified, we suppose that arcs are parametrized by the distance to the origin, i.e.,
||\gamma(t)||=t
. For a germ (at 0) of a set
X
, we define the set of all arcs
\gamma\subset X
as the
Valette link
of
X
(see
[
27
]
).

Definition 2.37
.

The
tangency order of two arcs

\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
in the Valette link of
X
is the exponent
q
where
||\gamma_{1}(t)-\gamma_{2}(t)||=ct^{q}+o(t^{q})
with
c>0
. We denote the tangency order as
\mathrm{tord}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
. By convention, for every arc
\gamma
, we set
\mathrm{tord}(\gamma,\gamma)=\infty
).

For an arc
\gamma
and a set of arcs
Z
in the Valette link of
X
,
the tangency order
of
\gamma
and
Z
is the supremum of
\mathrm{tord}(\gamma,\lambda)
over all arcs
\lambda
in the Valette link of
Z
. We denote this tangency order by either
\mathrm{tord}(\gamma,Z)
or
\mathrm{tord}(Z,\gamma)
.

Finally, the
contact order of two set germs

Z
and
Z^{\prime}
at
0
is the supremum of
\mathrm{tord}(\gamma,Z^{\prime})
over all arcs
\gamma
in the Valette link of
Z
. We denote the contact order as either
\mathrm{tord}(Z,Z^{\prime})
or
\operatorname{Cont}(Z,Z^{\prime})
.

Remark 2.38
.

It was shown in
[
6
]
that, for any outer bi-Lipschitz map
\phi:(X,0)\to(Y,0)
and for any arcs
\gamma
in the Valette link of
X
,
\lambda
in the Valette link of
Y
, we have
{\rm tord}(\gamma,\lambda)={\rm tord}(\phi(\gamma),\phi(\lambda))
. As a consequence, notice also that if
\phi:(X\cup Y,0)\to(X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime},0)
is an outer bi-Lipschitz map such that
\phi(X)=X^{\prime}
and
\phi(Y)=Y^{\prime}
, then
\operatorname{Cont}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Cont}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
. In particular, if
\operatorname{Cont}(X,Y)=1
, then
\mathrm{tord}(\gamma,\lambda)=1
, for all
\gamma
in the Valette link of
X
and
\lambda
in the Valette link of
Y
.

Remark 2.39
.

An interesting fact about the tangency order of arcs in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the so called “non-archimedean property” (it first appeared in
[
6
]
as “isosceles property”): given arcs
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

The next lemma allows us to extend radially a bi-Lipschitz map defined on the sphere.

Lemma 2.47
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
be a bi-Lipschitz map. Consider the map
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
given as

\Phi(\bm{z})=\begin{cases}\|z\|\phi\left(\dfrac{z}{\|z\|}\right),\ z\neq 0,\\
0,\ z=0.\end{cases}.

Then,
\Phi
is a bi-Lipschitz map.

Proof.

Notice first that
\Phi
is a well-defined, continuous map whose inverse
\Phi^{-1}:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is also well-defined, continuous and is given as

\Phi^{-1}(\bm{z})=\begin{cases}\|z\|\phi^{-1}\left(\dfrac{z}{\|z\|}\right),\ z\neq 0,\\
0,\ z=0.\end{cases}.

Therefore,
\Phi
is a homeomorphism. We will show that if
\phi
is
C
-bi-Lipschitz, then
\Phi
is
(C+1)
-bi-Lipschitz. Let
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and suppose, without loss of generality, that
\|p\|\geq\|q\|
. If
q=0
, then
\|\Phi(p)-\Phi(q)\|=\|p\|\leq(C+1)\|p-q\|
. If
q\neq 0
and
\|p\|=\|q\|
, then

\|\Phi(p)-\Phi(q)\|=\|p\|.\left\|\phi\left(\frac{p}{\|p\|}\right)-\phi\left(\frac{q}{\|q\|}\right)\right\|\leq C\|p\|.\left\|\frac{p}{\|p\|}-\frac{q}{\|q\|}\right\|=C.\|p-q\|\leq(C+1)\|p-q\|.

If
\|p\|>\|q\|>0
, then let
\tilde{p}=\frac{\|q\|}{\|p\|}.p
. Notice that
\|\tilde{p}\|=\|q\|
and
\angle p\tilde{p}q\geq 90^{\circ}
. Then, by the triangular inequality and by the previous case, we have

\|\Phi(p)-\Phi(q)\|\leq\|\Phi(p)-\Phi(\tilde{p})\|+\|\Phi(\tilde{p})-\Phi(q)\|\leq\|p-\tilde{p}\|+C\|\tilde{p}-q\|\leq(C+1).\max\{\|p-\tilde{p}\|,\|\tilde{p}-q\|\}.

On the other hand, since
\angle p\tilde{p}q\geq 90^{\circ}
, we have
\max\{\|p-p^{\prime}\|,\|p^{\prime}-q\|\}\leq\|p-q\|
, and hence

\|\Phi(p)-\Phi(q)\|\leq(C+1).\max\{\|p-\tilde{p}\|,\|\tilde{p}-q\|\}\leq(C+1)\|p-q\|.

Analogously, we have
\|\Phi^{-1}(p)-\Phi^{-1}(q)\|\leq(C+1)\|p-q\|
, and the lemma follows.
∎

is equivalent to

Remark 3.2

(

Throughout this subsection, notice that our strategy for investigating the Lipschitz geometry of the zeros of mixed polynomials involves splitting such a zero set into several pieces. We then prove that each of these pieces can be well approximated, in the bi-Lipschitz sense, by a specific set of weighted homogeneous zero sets coming from the face functions
f_{P}
with
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{inn}}(f)
. When extending this approach to families of polynomials
\{f+\varepsilon\theta\}_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}}
, we also need to investigate how deformations affect these approximations. Specifically, we want to ensure that small perturbations of
f_{P}
do not alter their bi-Lipschitz class. This means that for a given weight vector
P
within
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{inn}}(f)
, a perturbation of the form
f_{P}+\varepsilon\theta_{P}
(where
\theta_{P}
is not identically zero) should maintain the bi-Lipschitz equivalence for a sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, even though
\theta_{P}
has the same radial degree as
f_{P}
.

The following lemma addresses a particular case of the previous situation. It shows that we can deform certain radial mixed polynomials on the same radial degree and still preserve bi-Lipschitz equivalence, where the deformation parameter
\varepsilon
is not necessarily required to be sufficiently small.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
M(u,v):=A(u)v+B(u)\bar{v}
be a radial mixed polynomial of radial-type
(1,1;d)
such that
V(M)\cap\mathbb{C}^{\{1,2\}}=\emptyset
. If

is a radial mixed polynomial of radial-type
(1,1;d)
, which does not depend on

(i)

is a radial mixed polynomial of radial-type
(1,1;d)
, and

\Sigma_{L}(M+\delta M^{*})=Sing(V(M+\delta M^{*}))\subseteq\{u=0\},

(ii)

there is an ambient bi-Lipschitz map

satisfying

\Phi_{M}(V(L_{v};M))=V(L_{v};M+M^{*}).

Proof.

(i):
Let
P=(1,1)
. We have

, and Proposition
3.8
guarantees only a trivialisation of

for sufficiently small

. The condition

because, otherwise, if
(u,r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})\in V(M)
, with

, then

0=r\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t}(A(u)\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}t}+B(u))\Rightarrow A(u)=B(u)=0\text{ or }\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}t}=-\frac{B(u)}{A(u)}.

The first condition implies
u=0
, and the second condition is impossible since it must hold for all

. Therefore,
(u,0)
,

, is a regular point of
M
.
It is clear that
M+\delta M^{*}
is radial of radial-type
(P;d)
for any
\delta
. By
[
1

, the regularity of the points
(u,0)
,
u\neq 0
, and the independence of

from

implies that

Therefore, the result follows from Equation (
6

satisfies

.

(ii):
Consider the family
\{M+\delta M^{*}\}_{\delta\in[0,1]}
. The members of this family are radial of radial-type
(P;d)
. By (i),

, and thus, applying the implicit function theorem and the extension isotopy theorem in

, there is a smooth ambient isotopy

taking

to the link

, for each

. Since

is compact and

is smooth, we conclude that

is a bi-Lipschitz map. Now consider the map germ

given as

\Phi_{\delta}(\bm{z})=\begin{cases}|z|\phi_{\delta}\left(\dfrac{z}{|z|}\right),\ z\neq 0,\\
0,\ z=0.\end{cases}

By Lemma

, the maps

∎

3.3.
A Lipschitz contact criterion

The zero set
V(f)
of an IND mixed polynomial
f
that is
\Gamma_{\mathrm{inn}}
-nice can be approximated by the weighted homogeneous zero sets
V_{P_{i}},\ P_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{inn}}(f)
. Thus, if we want to study the contact at the origin of two components
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
of
V(f)
that are in the same horn neighbourhood
\mathcal{H}_{i}
, then we will consider the contact at the origin of their corresponding weighted homogeneous approximations (see Corollary
3.9
). However, if these components belong to different horn neighbourhoods, then their contact behaviour can be more complicated, since such zeros and their respective homogeneous parts can have different contact orders at the origin. In the following, we provide a broad criterion for understanding the contact at the origin of components of zeros of mixed polynomials when they are in different horn neighbourhoods. Motivated to preserve the property that the contact order at the origin of such zeros and their respective homogeneous parts are the same, we consider the following two conditions:

(A)

\displaystyle\text{For }i,j\in I_{f}^{(k>1)}\text{ with }i\neq j,\text{ and components }I\subset L_{i,f}\text{ and }J\subset L_{j,f}:

\displaystyle\mathrm{proj}_{2}(I)\cap\mathrm{proj}_{2}(J)\neq\emptyset\Rightarrow\ (\operatorname{Int}(\mathrm{proj}_{2}(I))\cap\mathrm{proj}_{2}(J)\neq\emptyset\text{ or }\mathrm{proj}_{2}(I)\cap\operatorname{Int}(\mathrm{proj}_{2}(J))\neq\emptyset).

(B)

\displaystyle\text{For }i,j\in I_{f}^{(k<1)}\text{ with }i\neq j,\text{ and components }I\subset L_{\underline{i},f}\text{ and }J\subset L_{\underline{j},f}:

\displaystyle\mathrm{proj}_{1}(I)\cap\mathrm{proj}_{1}(J)\neq\emptyset\Rightarrow(\operatorname{Int}(\mathrm{proj}_{1}(I))\cap\mathrm{proj}_{1}(J)\neq\emptyset\text{ or }\mathrm{proj}_{1}(I)\cap\operatorname{Int}(\mathrm{proj}_{1}(J))\neq\emptyset).

Here,
{\rm proj}_{1}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
{\rm proj}_{2}:\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
are projections.

Proposition 3.12

Let
f
be an IND mixed polynomial that is

5.
Ambient Lipschitz geometry of 1-braid closures and non-tangent Hopf-links

In this section, we aim to obtain ambient bi-Lipschitz triviality beyond semi-radial mixed polynomials. We begin our investigation with
\Gamma_{\mathrm{inn}}
-nice and IND mixed polynomials, whose link is ”quite simple” in both metric and topological senses. This motivates the definitions of metric 1-braid closure and non-tangent Hopf-link. Crucially, the characteristics of such links are sufficient for their classification and rigidity in the ambient Lipschitz category (see Theorem
5.5
), and this classification extends to several mixed polynomials whose link is isotopic to either a trivial knot (Proposition
5.8
) or a topological Hopf-link (Proposition
5.9
).

Definition 5.1
.

Let

be a mixed polynomial such that

associated with

or

(see Remark

).

(ii)

The tangent cone of

at the origin satisfies

\{v=0\},&\text{if $L_{u}$ is the braid axis of $L_{f}$.}\end{cases}

By definition, a metric 1-braid closure with braid axis
L_{u}
is isotopic to those with braid axis

and their surfaces have tangent cones at the origin that are ambient bi-Lipschitz equivalent.

.

be a mixed polynomial such that

associated with

admits a decomposition

and

.

\{(\phi(\bm{z}),z)\,;\,x\in\mathbb{C}\}
.

Now, for each
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, define
\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}:(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
by
\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1}-\varepsilon\phi(z_{2}),z_{2})
, for all
(z_{1},z_{2})\in(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
. It is easy to see that each
\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}
is a bijection.

Claim.
There is a uniform constant
K_{0}>1
such that each
\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}
is
K_{0}
-bi-Lipschitz. Moreover, such maps are subanalytic and vary smoothly with
\varepsilon
.

Since
\phi
is subanalytic (see Remark
2.22
) and
\varepsilon
is a linear parameter on
\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}
, it remains to prove the bi-Lipschitz property. To prove this, notice that if
K>1
is such that
\phi
is
K
-bi-Lipschitz, then for each
p=(u_{1},v_{1}),q=(u_{2},v_{2})\in(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, if
u=u_{1}-u_{2}
,
v=v_{1}-v_{2}
, and
w=\phi(v_{1})-\phi(v_{2})
, then
\frac{1}{K}|v|\leq|w|\leq K|v|
. We also have
\|\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}(p)-\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}(q)\|=\sqrt{|u-\varepsilon w|^{2}+|v|^{2}}
. Therefore, since
|u-\varepsilon w|^{2}\leq(|u|+|v|)^{2}=|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}+2|u||v|
and
2|u||v|\leq|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}(p)-\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}(q)\|\leq\sqrt{2|u|^{2}+2|w|^{2}+|v|^{2}}\leq\sqrt{(1+2K^{2})(|u|^{2}+|v|^{2})}=\sqrt{(1+2K^{2})}\|p-q\|.

If
|u-\varepsilon w|\geq\frac{1}{K}|u|
, then

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}(p)-\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}(q)\|=\sqrt{|u-\varepsilon w|^{2}+|v|^{2}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{1}{K^{2}}(|u|^{2}+|v|^{2})}=\frac{1}{K}\|p-q\|.

Now, if
|u-\varepsilon w|<\frac{1}{K}|u|
, then
|u|-|\varepsilon w|\leq|u-\varepsilon w|
implies
(1-\frac{1}{K})|u|<\varepsilon|w|\leq K\varepsilon|v|\leq K|v|
and thus
|v|>\frac{K-1}{K^{2}}|u|
. Let
K^{\prime}=1+\frac{K^{4}}{(K-1)^{2}}
. Then,
\frac{1}{\sqrt{K^{\prime}-1}}=\frac{K-1}{K^{2}}
and since
|v|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{K^{\prime}}(|u|^{2}+|v|^{2})
if, and only if,
|v|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{K^{\prime}-1}}|u|
, which is true, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}(p)-\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}(q)\|=\sqrt{|u-\varepsilon w|^{2}+|v|^{2}}\geq\sqrt{|v|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{K^{\prime}}}\sqrt{|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{K^{\prime}}}\|p-q\|.

Therefore,
\Phi_{i,1}
is an ambient bi-Lipschitz map such that
\Phi_{i,1}(V_{i})=\{u=0\}
, and the result follows. In particular,
(V_{i},0)
is LNE because
\{u=0\}
is obviously LNE.
∎

6.
Necessary conditions for bi-Lipschitz equivalence

In this section, we study IND mixed polynomials whose link is neither a 1-braid closure nor a non-tangent Hopf-link. We now focus on these cases because the associated surfaces allow us to obtain a meaningful comparison of the tangent orders of real half-branches and their contact orders at the origin, using the so-called test arc method in
[
16
]
. With this, we obtain several necessary conditions for outer bi-Lipschitz equivalence based on the Newton polygon associated with the mixed polynomials.

6.1.
The type of semi-radial mixed polynomial is a Lipschitz invariant

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
f
and
g
be semi-radial mixed polynomials. Assume that the links associated with both
f
and
g
are neither empty nor metric 1-braid closures nor non-tangent Hopf links, and that
f
is bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalent to
g
. Then,

(i)

If
f
is Type-II, then
g
must also be Type-II.

(ii)

If
f
is Type-III, then
g
must also be Type-III.

Proof.

Let
P_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{inn}}(f)
and
Q_{j}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{inn}}(g)
be the weight vectors associated with the radial-type of the semi-radial mixed polynomials
f
and
g
, respectively.

(i):
Since
f
is Type-II by hypothesis, Lemma
4.8
ensures that
C(V(f),0)
is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to
V(f_{P_{i}})
. In this case,
C(V(f),0)
consists of the cone over the components of
L_{f_{P_{i}}}
, meaning it comprises cones that share a common vertex at the origin. Given that

f
is bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalent to
g
, Theorem
2.46
implies that the tangent cone
C(V(f),0)
is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to
C(V(g),0)
via a bi-Lipschitz map
\phi
. We divide the proof into two claims:

Claim I.

g
is not Type-I.

We first assume for contradiction that
g
is Type-I. Without loss of generality, we can suppose that the radial mixed polynomial
g_{Q_{j}}
is
u
-convenient and satisfies
k_{g}=\frac{q_{j,1}}{q_{j,2}}>1
; an analogous argument applies for the other case. By Lemma
4.9
, the tangent cone
C(V(g),0)
is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to a subset of
\{u=0\}
. Since
\phi
is a bi-Lipschitz map satisfying
\phi(0)=0
, we have that
C(V(f),0)\setminus\{0\}
is homeomorphic to
C(V(g),0)\setminus\{0\}
. Therefore, by the topology of
C(V(f),0)
and the fact that
f
is Type II, the link

L_{f}
must be a knot and hence
C(V(g_{Q_{j}}))=\{u=0\}
. Moreover, as
f
is bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalent to
g
, the link
L_{g}
also has to be a knot.

Now, we apply the method of real half-branches used in
[
16
]
: Since, by hypothesis,
L_{g_{Q_{j}}}
is not a
metric 1-braid closure and
C(V(g_{Q_{j}}))=\{u=0\}
, there exist four distinct roots
(u_{l},\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{l}}),\ l=1,\dots,4,
of
(g_{Q_{j}})_{j}
such that
t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}<t_{4}
,
\frac{t_{3}-t_{1}}{2\pi}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{2\pi},\frac{t_{4}-t_{1}}{2\pi}\notin\mathbb{Z}
. Define the real arcs:

(15)

\gamma_{l}(\lambda)=(\lambda^{k_{g}}u_{l},\lambda\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{l}}),\ l=1,\dots,4,

which are contained in
V(g_{Q_{j}})
. Given that
t_{3}=t_{1}+2n\pi
,
\gamma_{1}
, and
\gamma_{3}
are tangent arcs where
\operatorname{tord}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{3})=k_{g}>1
.
By Proposition
4.2
, since
f
is ambient bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalent to
f_{P_{1}}
and
g
is ambient bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalent to
g_{Q_{j}}
, we can consider a bi-Lipschitz map
F:(V(f_{P_{1}}),0)\to(V(g_{Q_{j}}),0)
. Define:

\chi_{l}(\lambda)=(\lambda u(\tau_{l}(\lambda)),\lambda\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\tau_{l}(\lambda))})\in F^{-1}(\gamma_{i}),\ l=1,\dots,4.

Here,
(u(\tau),\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\tau)}),\ \tau\in[0,2\pi M]
, is a parametrization of the entire
V((f_{P_{i}})_{i})
, arising from the concatenation of the parametrizations of the curves (not necessarily closed) in Equation (
3
).
Since
\operatorname{tord}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{3})=k_{g}
and
F
is bi-Lipschitz, it follows that:

(16)

\operatorname{tord}(\chi_{1},\chi_{3})=k_{g}.

In this case, we have that

(17)

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}t(\tau_{1}(\lambda))-t(\tau_{3}(\lambda))=0,

otherwise,

||\chi_{1}(\lambda)-\chi_{3}(\lambda)||\geq||\lambda\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\tau_{1}(\lambda))}-\lambda\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\tau_{3}(\lambda))}||=\lambda||\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(t(\tau_{1}(\lambda))-t(\tau_{3}(\lambda)))}-1||,

which would imply that
\operatorname{tord}(\chi_{1},\chi_{3})=1
, contradicting Equation (
16
).
Therefore, Equation (
17
) holds, and it occurs in the following two cases:

(a)

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}(\tau_{1}(\lambda)-\tau_{3}(\lambda))=2\pi k,\ k=0\text{ or }M,

(b)

implying
\operatorname{tord}(\chi_{1},\chi_{3})=1
, contradicting (
16
).

Suppose
k=0
, that is,
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}(\tau_{1}(\lambda)-\tau_{3}(\lambda))=0;
the case

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}(\tau_{1}(\lambda)-\tau_{2}(\lambda))=0\text{ or }\lim_{\lambda\to 0}(\tau_{1}(\lambda)-\tau_{4}(\lambda))=0.

Suppose

In particular,

||\chi_{1}(\lambda)-\chi_{2}(\lambda)||\ll\lambda\therefore tord(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})>1.

Now, since
\gamma_{l}=F(\chi_{l})
for

, we have

||\gamma_{1}(\lambda)-\gamma_{2}(\lambda)||\gtrsim\lambda|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{1}}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{2}}|\approx\lambda\therefore tord(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=1,

which leads to a contradiction.

Claim II.

g
is not Type-III.

Suppose by contradiction that
g
is Type-III. Without loss of generality, we can suppose that

g_{Q_{j}}
is not
u
-convenient and
k_{g}>1
. By Lemma
4.10
, the tangent cone

is homeomorphic to the union of

with a subset of

. Therefore, since

is bi-Lipschitz

-equivalent to

, we get that

is homeomorphic to
C(V(g),0)
, and consequently by the topology of

and the fact that

is Type II,

has two connected components,

and

, each one of then with link homeomorphic to

and satisfying

By assumption,

has a link that is not a non-tangent Hopf-link, thus the link of

is not a metric 1-braid closure and it does not intersect

. Therefore, applying to

the same reasoning of the four real half-branches done in Claim I of this Proposition, we obtain a contradiction.

Therefore, since

is neither Type-I nor Type-III, then

is Type-II, as these three types describe the class of semi-radial mixed polynomials.

(ii):
Without loss of generality, we can suppose that
f_{P_{i}}
is not
u
-convenient and
k_{f}=\frac{p_{i,1}}{p_{i,2}}>1
. By (i) of this proposition, we know that
g
is not Type-II. We suppose by contradiction that

is Type-I. By Remark

, we know that the link
L_{f}
is isotopic to a nested braid
\mathbf{L}([L_{1}],L_{v})
. Notice that, since

is not a metric 1-braid closure,

is not empty. Thus,

has more than one component.

Let
V_{1}:=\{v=0\}
and

be the components of

. By Lemma

,

and

. Hence

, which implies
\operatorname{Cont}(F(V_{1}),F(V_{2}))=1
as

is a bi-Lipschitz map. Since

is Type-I, Lemma

implies

\displaystyle C(F(V_{1}),0)\subset\{u=0\},

\displaystyle C(F(V_{2}),0)\subset\{u=0\},

\displaystyle C(F(V_{1}),0)\cap C(F(V_{2}),0)=\{0\},

if

and

is

-convenient, or

\displaystyle C(F(V_{1}),0)\subset\{v=0\},

\displaystyle C(F(V_{2}),0)\subset\{v=0\},

\displaystyle C(F(V_{1}),0)\cap C(F(V_{2}),0)=\{0\},

, one of these cones has to be homeomorphic to

, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
f
and
g
be semi-radial mixed polynomials such that their links are neither empty nor metric 1-braid closures. If
f
is bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalent to
g
, and
f
is Type-I, then
g
must be Type-I and
k_{f}=k_{g}\text{ or }k_{f}=k_{g}^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
P_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{inn}}(f)
and
Q_{j}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{inn}}(g)
be the weight vectors associated with the radial-types of the semi-radial mixed polynomials
f
and
g
, respectively. First, notice that it is sufficient to prove the result for
f_{P_{i}}
being
u
-convenient and
k_{f}>1
, as those
v
-convenient with
k_{f}<1
are bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalent (via change of variables
u\Leftrightarrow v
) to a
u
-convenient satisfying
k_{f}>1
.
Notice that
L_{f}
cannot be a non-tangent Hopf-Link because its tangent cone is a subset of
\{u=0\}
by Lemma
4.9
, and thus only
L_{v}
can be the braid axis of the components of
L_{f}
. Therefore, by Proposition
6.1
and the fact that
f
is Type-I, it follows that
g
is neither Type-II nor Type-III.
Applying the same change of variables (
u\Leftrightarrow v
) if necessary, we assume
k_{g}>1
and that
g_{Q_{j}}
is
u
-convenient. Consequently, both
f
and
g
are Type-I semi-radial mixed polynomials, and we aim to show that
k_{f}=k_{g}
.

Since
f
and
g
are bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalent, by Corollary
4.7
, there is a bi-Lipschitz map
F:(V(f_{P_{1}}),0)\to(V(g_{Q_{1}}),0)
. We now divide the proof into two cases:

Case I
:
L_{f_{P_{1}}}
has a component
J
that is not a metric 1-braid closure.

If
C(V(J,f_{P_{1}}),0)
is a line, then
C(F(V(J,f_{P_{1}})),0)
is also a line, by Theorem
2.46
. By radial homogeneity,
V(J,f_{P_{1}})
and
F(V(J,f_{P_{1}}))
can be parametrized as
(r^{k_{f}}u(\tau),r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\tau)})
and
(r^{k_{g}}\tilde{u}(\tau),r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tilde{t}(\tau)})
, respectively, with
t(\tau)
,
\tilde{t}(\tau)
being constants because (see the proof of Lemma
4.9
)

C(V(J;f_{P_{1}}),0)=T_{2}(V(J;f_{P_{1}}))\text{ and }C(F(V(J;f_{P_{1}})),0)=T_{2}(F(V(J;f_{P_{1}}))).

Hence,
V(J;f_{P_{1}})
is a
k_{f}
-horn and
F(V(J;f_{P_{1}}))
is a
k_{g}
-horn, and therefore
k_{f}=k_{g}
.

If
C(V(J,f_{P_{1}}))
is not a line, then
C(F(V(J,f_{P_{1}})),0)
is not a line, by Theorem
2.46
. By radial homogeneity,
V(J,f_{P_{1}})
and
F(V(J,f_{P_{1}}))
can be parametrized as
(r^{k_{f}}u(\tau),r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\tau)})
and
(r^{k_{g}}\tilde{u}(\tau),r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tilde{t}(\tau)})
, respectively. As
C(V(J;f_{P_{1}}),0)=T_{2}(V(J;f_{P_{1}}))
and
V(J;f_{P_{1}})
is not a 1-braid closure, there are
\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\tau_{3}<\tau_{4}
such that
0<t(\tau_{1})<t(\tau_{2})=t(\tau_{4})<t(\tau_{3})<2\pi
and
u(\tau_{2})\neq u(\tau_{4})
. Therefore, for
i=1,2,3,4
, the arcs
\gamma_{i}(r)=(r^{k_{f}}u(\tau_{i}),r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(\tau_{i})})
satisfy
tord(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j})=1
for every
1\leq i<j\leq 4
, except for
i=2,j=4
, where

. Thus, if
k_{f}>k_{g}
, then there is a
k_{f}
-Hölder triangle

in
F(V(J;f_{P_{1}}))
whose boundary arcs are
F(\gamma_{2})
and
F(\gamma_{4})
. Since either
F(\gamma_{1})
or
F(\gamma_{3})
are in

, we have
tord(F(\gamma_{1}),F(\gamma_{2}))>1
or

, a contradiction. By reversing the roles of
f
and
g
, we also obtain a contradiction from
k_{g}>k_{f}
. Therefore,
k_{f}=k_{g}
.

Case II
:
L_{f_{P_{1}}}
and
L_{g_{Q_{1}}}
are unions of metric 1-braid closures with braid axis
L_{v}
.

Let

and

be components of
V(f_{P_{1}})
, where
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are components of
L_{f_{P_{1}}}
. By Proposition
3.12
, the contact order of these two components is given by

\operatorname{Cont}(V(I_{1};f_{P_{i}}),V(I_{2};f_{P_{i}}))=k_{f}.

Similarly, for two components of

, say,
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
,

\operatorname{Cont}(V(J_{1};g_{Q_{1}}),V(J_{2};g_{Q_{1}}))=k_{g}.

Therefore, since
f
is bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalent to
g
and the contact order is an invariant of bi-Lipschitz maps, we obtain
k_{f}=k_{g}
.
∎

Following the same idea as above, we obtain:

\bar{u}
). From this, we get that
i_{m}(f)=1
and
\operatorname{proj}_{2}(L_{1})=\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Furthermore, since
\deg_{|u|}f_{P_{1}}\geq 2
, it follows that
M\geq 2
. Since
k_{1}>1
, we conclude that
L_{P_{1}}
cannot be a metric 1-braid closure. Therefore,
f
satisfies Condition (
C
). Analogously, using vacuity or (ii), we obtain that
f
satisfies Condition (
D
).
∎

Example 6.10
.

Consider the IND mixed polynomials

and

Since both mixed polynomials are

-semiholomorphic (see Remark
2.26
), these are
\Gamma_{\mathrm{inn}}
-nice and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{inn}}
-true. Using Proposition
6.9
, we conclude that
f
and

satisfy conditions (
C
) and (
D
). The sets
\mathcal{NC}(f)
and

are:

\mathcal{NC}(f)=(5;(3,1),(2,1),(\tfrac{3}{2},2),(1,1))\text{ and }\mathcal{NC}(g)=(4;(3,1),(2,1),(\tfrac{3}{2},1),(1,1)).

Therefore, by Theorem

,

are not bi-Lipschitz

).

7.
Further examples and remarks about bi-Lipschitz V-triviality

We finish the paper by providing two interesting examples. The first one shows that bi-Lipschitz triviality is false in general, even when we have topological triviality. The second one shows that it is possible to obtain ambient bi-Lipschitz triviality in families that are not in the form
\{f+\varepsilon\theta\}_{\varepsilon\in I}
. Such examples show that although the topology of mixed polynomials shares some topological rigidity properties with holomorphic polynomials, their Lipschitz geometry behaves differently. That is why we had to develop proper methods to study their Lipschitz geometry, instead of using the techniques developed in
[
21
]
and
[
18
]
directly.

Example 7.1
.

By Theorem
2.16
and Remark
2.17
(b), any family
\{f+\varepsilon\theta\}_{\varepsilon\in I}
that is
\Gamma_{\rm{inn}}
-nice and
d(P_{i};\theta)\geq d(P_{i};f)
for
P_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{inn}}(f)
is ambient topologically
V
-trivial. Here, we present a family that proves that such conditions alone are insufficient to ensure bi-Lipschitz
V
-triviality. This highlights the importance of the semi-radial condition within the framework of bi-Lipschitz
V
-triviality. Consider the family

\{f+\varepsilon\theta\}_{\varepsilon\in I}=\{(u^{3}-(1+\varepsilon\mathrm{i})v\bar{v})(u^{5}-v\bar{v})\}_{\varepsilon\in I}.

Notice that, for
\varepsilon=0
, the mixed polynomial

f=f(u,v)=(u^{3}-v\bar{v})(u^{5}-v\bar{v})
is in the family. A direct calculation shows that
f
is IND. Furthermore, since
f
is
u
-semiholomorphic, it is also
\Gamma_{\rm{inn}}
-nice. We also have

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{inn}}(f)=\{P_{1},P_{2}\}\,;\,P_{1}=(2,3),P_{2}=(2,5).

The links
L_{\underline{1},f}
and
L_{\underline{2},f}
have 3 and 5 components, respectively. Let
L^{1}_{\underline{1},f}
and
L^{1}_{\underline{2},f}
denote the components of
L_{\underline{1},f}
and
L_{\underline{2},f}
, respectively, whose parametrizations are given by
(1,\mathrm{e}^{\tau\mathrm{i}}),\tau\in[0,2\pi]
for both. Notice that
\mathrm{proj}_{1}(L^{1}_{\underline{1},f})\cap\mathrm{proj}_{1}(L^{1}_{\underline{2},f})\neq\emptyset,
and both projections have empty interiors. Thus,
f
does not satisfy Condition (
B
). The corresponding surfaces
V(L^{1}_{\underline{1},f};f)
and
V(L^{1}_{\underline{2},f};f)
can be parametrized by
(R,R^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\tau\mathrm{i}})
and
(R,R^{\frac{5}{3}}\mathrm{e}^{\tau\mathrm{i}})
, respectively. By Proposition
3.12
(iv), we have

1\leq\operatorname{Cont}(V(L^{1}_{\underline{1},f};f),V(L^{1}_{\underline{2},f};f))\leq\frac{3}{2}.

Since the real half-branches
\gamma_{1}(\lambda)=(\lambda,\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}})
and
\gamma_{2}(\lambda)=(\lambda,\lambda^{\frac{5}{2}})
satisfy
\operatorname{tord}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\frac{3}{2}
, it follows that

\operatorname{Cont}(V(L^{1}_{\underline{1},f};f),V(L^{1}_{\underline{2},f};f))=\frac{3}{2}.

Similarly, using Proposition
3.12
(iv), we can show that the contact at the origin between pairwise distinct components of
V(f)
, except for the pair
(V(L^{1}_{\underline{1},f};f),V(L^{1}_{\underline{2},f};f))
, is equal to 1.

On the other hand, by Theorem
2.16
, the links
L_{f}
and
L_{f+\varepsilon\theta}
are isotopic. Denote by
L^{1}_{\underline{1},f+\varepsilon\theta}
and
L^{1}_{\underline{2},f+\varepsilon\theta}
the respective images of
L^{1}_{\underline{1},f}
and
L^{1}_{\underline{2},f}
under this isotopy. These components can be parametrized as

\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\arctan(\varepsilon)}{2}\mathrm{i}},\dfrac{1}{|1+\varepsilon\mathrm{i}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathrm{e}^{\tau\mathrm{i}}\right)\text{ and }(1,\mathrm{e}^{\tau\mathrm{i}}),

respectively. For
\varepsilon\neq 0
, we have

\mathrm{proj}_{1}(L^{1}_{\underline{1},f+\varepsilon\theta})\cap\mathrm{proj}_{1}(L^{1}_{\underline{2},f+\varepsilon\theta})=\emptyset.
Thus, by Proposition
3.12
(iv),

\operatorname{Cont}(V(L^{1}_{\underline{1},f+\varepsilon\theta};f+\varepsilon\theta),V(L^{1}_{\underline{2},f+\varepsilon\theta};f+\varepsilon\theta))=1.

The same contact at the origin holds for all other pairwise components of
V(f+\varepsilon\theta)
. Since the contact at the origin is an invariant of bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalence, and
V(f)
has pairwise components with contact at the origin equal to
\frac{3}{2}
,
V(f)
and
V(f+\varepsilon\theta)
cannot be bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalent for
\varepsilon\neq 0
. Consequently, the family is not bi-Lipschitz
V
-trivial.

The following example demonstrates that families, not necessarily of the form
\{f(u,v)+\varepsilon\theta(u,v)\}_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}}
, can still be ambient bi-Lipschitz
V
-trivial. Additionally, it shows that the parameter of ambient bi-Lipschitz
V
-trivial families can be a complex parameter and does not need to be sufficiently small, even when the deformation terms are on the Newton boundary of
f
.

Example 7.2
.

We present a specific example: a family of radial mixed polynomials,
\{f_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\mathbb{C}}
. This family is characterised by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{inn}}(f_{\omega})=\{P_{1}\}
. We explicitly construct the family of ambient bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms that trivialises this family. This construction is inspired by the works in
[
2
]
and
[
12
]
.

Consider
g(u,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})=u^{3}+\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}t}u
. The zero set of the function
g
forms the closure of a braid on
3
-strands. Consider the functions formed by the translations by
\omega\in\mathbb{C}
of the zero set of
g
, i.e.,

g_{\omega}(u,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})=(u+\omega)^{3}+\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}t}(u+\omega).

The isotopic class of the zero set of
g_{\omega}
does not depend on
\omega
.
For a positive integer number
\kappa
, define the semiholomorphic polynomial

f_{\kappa,\omega}(u,v)=|v|^{6\kappa}g_{\omega}(u/|v|^{2\kappa},v/|v|).

A direct calculation shows that

f_{\kappa,\omega}(u,v)=u^{3}+3\omega(v\bar{v})^{\kappa}u^{2}+(3\omega^{2}(v\bar{v})^{2\kappa}+(v\bar{v})^{2\kappa-1}v^{2})u+\omega^{3}(v\bar{v})^{3\kappa}+\omega(v\bar{v})^{3\kappa-1}v^{2}.

Then,
f_{\kappa,\omega}
is a radial mixed polynomial of radial-type
(P;d)=(2\kappa,1;6\kappa)
, and it is
u
-convenient. The mixed polynomials
f_{\kappa,\omega}
can be seen as a family of
f_{\kappa,0}
deformed by terms
\theta_{\kappa}(\omega,u,v,\bar{v})
with
d(P;\theta_{\kappa})=d(P;f_{\kappa,0})
. We prove that this family is ambient bi-Lipschitz
V
-trivial, and the trivialisation holds for any
\omega\in\mathbb{C}
. Indeed, the map
\phi_{\omega}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
defined by
\phi_{\omega}(u,v)=(u-|v|^{2\kappa}\omega,v)
satisfies
\phi_{\omega}(V(f_{\kappa,0}))=V(f_{\kappa,\omega})
, and

||\phi_{\omega}(u_{1},v_{1})-\phi_{\omega}(u_{2},v_{2})||=||(u_{1},v_{1})-(u_{2},v_{2})+((|v_{2}|^{2\kappa}-|v_{1}|^{2\kappa})\omega,0)||.

For
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
|v_{1}|,|v_{2}|<K
, we obtain

||v_{1}|^{2\kappa}-|v_{2}|^{2\kappa}|=||v_{1}|-|v_{2}||\cdot\left|\sum_{s=0}^{2\kappa-1}|v_{1}|^{s}|v_{2}|^{2\kappa-1-s}\right|\leq 2\kappa K^{2\kappa-1}|v_{1}-v_{2}|.

Then,

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\omega}(u_{1},v_{1})-\phi_{\omega}(u_{2},v_{2})\|\leq

\displaystyle\|(u_{1},v_{1})-(u_{2},v_{2})\|+\|((|v_{2}|^{2\kappa}-|v_{1}|^{2\kappa})\omega,0)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||(u_{1},v_{1})-(u_{2},v_{2})||+2\kappa|\omega|K^{2\kappa-1}|v_{1}-v_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+2\kappa|\omega|K^{2\kappa-1})||(u_{1},v_{1})-(u_{2},v_{2})||.

Analogously,
\phi_{\omega}^{-1}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
defined by
\phi_{\omega}^{-1}(u,v)=(u+|v|^{2\kappa}\omega,v)
is Lipschitz. Therefore,

\phi_{\omega}
is a ambient bi-Lipschitz map and
\phi_{\omega}(V(f_{\kappa,0}))=V(f_{\kappa,\omega})
for any
\omega\in\mathbb{C}
.

For any two complex numbers
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
, the two Type-I semi-radial mixed polynomials,
f_{\kappa,\omega_{1}}
and
f_{\kappa,\omega_{2}}
, are ambient bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalent because they belong to an ambient bi-Lipschitz trivial family. However, they are not equivalent to
f_{\kappa^{\prime},\omega_{1}}
and
f_{\kappa^{\prime},\omega_{2}}
for
\kappa^{\prime}\neq\kappa
(Proposition
6.3
). Our ongoing research focusses on studying the (ambient) bi-Lipschitz
V
-equivalence for pairs of IND mixed polynomials
f
and
g
that satisfy
\mathcal{NC}(f)=\mathcal{NC}(g)
and that are not necessarily part of a family of mixed polynomials. The goal is to obtain new, effective invariants derived from their
C
-diagrams that can fully classify these equivalence classes.

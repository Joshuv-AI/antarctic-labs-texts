254 | Tocolysis in Threatened Preterm Birth in Singletons: Pooled Analysis of APOSTEL 3 & 8 Trials

James A. L. Meliëzer1; L. van Gils1; L. I. Van der windt1; A. H. Van Kaam2; C. Roos1; Martijn A. Oudijk1

1Amsterdam UMC, University of Amsterdam, Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Amsterdam, Netherlands; 2Emma Children's Hospital, Amsterdam UMC, Department of Neonatology, Amsterdam, Netherlands

Objective: To evaluate the effects of the tocolytic drugs atosiban and nifedipine, compared to placebo and by direct comparison, on neonatal outcome in singleton pregnancies with a threatened preterm birth between 30 + 0–33 + 6 weeks of gestation.

Study Design: Pooled analysis of Individual participant data (IPD) of APOSTEL 3 and APOSTEL 8, two European multicenter randomized controlled trials. Analysis was performed on singleton pregnancies with threatened preterm birth between 30 + 0–33 + 6 weeks gestation, randomized to nifedipine, atosiban or placebo. Primary outcome was a composite of neonatal mortality and morbidity. Pairwise comparisons were reported as adjusted relative risks (aRR) with 95% confidence intervals, with adjustment for gestational age and rupture of membranes at inclusion.

Results: 844 women were included, of whom 109 (12.9%) were randomized to nifedipine, 416 (49.3%) to atosiban, and 319 (37.8%) to placebo. Composite adverse neonatal outcome occurred in 5 (4.6%) neonates in the nifedipine group, 30 (7.2%) neonates in the atosiban group, and 34 (10.7%) neonates in the placebo group. Pairwise comparisons did not show any differences for either nifedipine versus atosiban (aRR 0.66, 95% CI 0.26–1.67), nifedipine versus placebo (aRR 0.45, 95% CI 0.18–1.14), or atosiban versus placebo (aRR 0.69, 95% CI 0.44–1.10). Prolongation of pregnancy was similar (log‐rank p = 0.485).

Conclusion: In this pooled IPD analysis comparing nifedipine, atosiban and placebo, no difference was found in adverse neonatal outcome. These findings support not giving tocolytic therapy in singleton pregnancies with a threatened preterm birth after 30 weeks of gestation.

255 | Net Benefit and Cost Analysis of the QUiPP‐App for Threatened Preterm Birth Prediction

James A. L. Meliëzer; B. Y. Y. Gravesteijn; F. J. R. J. R. Hermans

Amsterdam UMC, University of Amsterdam, Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Amsterdam, Netherlands

Objective: Cervical length (CL) combined with biomarker testing such as fetal Fibronectin (fFN) may predict spontaneous preterm birth (PTB), but low positive predictive values (PPV) cause unnecessary admissions and treatments. The QUiPP app provides a PTB probability using various risk factors and available tests. Our aim was to investigate the clinical implications and economic consequences of using the QUiPP app in clinical practice compared to current guidelines.

Study Design: A net benefit and cost analysis of the QUiPP app using a European multicenter cohort (EUFiS trial), which analyzed CL and fFN in women with a threatened PTB at 24 to 34 weeks gestation. Main effect measure was PTB within 7 days of testing. PPV and negative predictive value (NPV) were calculated along with treatment costs for different strategies based on current treatment guidelines and on QUiPP thresholds of 5%, 10% and 15% risk of PTB. Net benefit decision curve analysis (DCA) compared clinical utility of the QUiPP app to current diagnostic guidelines. Costs were calculated from a healthcare perspective.

Results: QUiPP app risk assessment showed better PPV (minimum 25.9% with fFN and 25.6% without fFN) compared to current guidelines (19.3% with fFN and 23.3% without fFN). However, NPV was lower (maximum 96.1% with fFN and 92.6% without fFN) compared to current guidelines (97.3% with fFN and 94.3% without fFN). DCA with fFN showed comparable clinical utility up to 16% risk thresholds, with the QUiPP app providing additional net benefit only when patients are treated at 16% risk or higher (maximum of 6.3 overtreated patients per correctly treated patient). DCA without fFN showed clear benefit of current guidelines. Cost analysis showed lower costs for QUiPP app strategies compared to current guidelines (with fFN minimum EUR305.53 saved per patient and without fFN minimum EUR180.95 saved).

Conclusion: Without use of fFN, use of the QUiPP does not add clinical utility to current Dutch guidelines. Using fFN, clinical utility is similar, though the QUiPP app may save healthcare costs.

256 | Targeted Use of Aspirin to Prevent Recurrent Preterm Birth: Secondary Analysis of the APRIL Study

James A. L. Meliëzer1; J. Hanna1; Martijn A. Oudijk1; M. W. Heijmans2; Marjon A. de Boer3

1Amsterdam UMC, University of Amsterdam, Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Amsterdam, Netherlands; 2Department of Epidemiology and Data Science, Amsterdam UMC, Amsterdam, Netherlands; 3Amsterdam UMC, Amsterdam, Netherlands

Objective: To identify those women with a prior spontaneous preterm birth (sPTB) that benefit from aspirin in a next pregnancy to prevent recurrent sPTB.

Study Design: Secondary analysis of the multicenter APRIL study, which randomized 387 women with a singleton pregnancy and a prior sPTB between low dose aspirin or placebo. After screening of pre‐specified predictors, multivariable logistic regression model based on the Predictive Approaches to Treatment effect Heterogeneity (PATH) framework analyzed clinically relevant interactions. Internal validation with bootstrap resampling and adjustment for overfitting using a uniform shrinkage factor were applied. Absolute risks and absolute risk reductions (ARR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated with the Agresti–Caffo method.

Results: Cerclage in the study pregnancy best predicted treatment effect, along with uterus anomaly and onset of previous sPTB (contractions versus rupture of membranes) as risk predictors. Discrimination of the model was moderate (AUC 0.64; 95% CI 0.56–0.71). In women with a cerclage placement, aspirin reduced the risk of sPTB (14.8% vs 43.9%; ARR 28.4%, 95% CI 0.16–0.40). Addition of uterus anomaly and onset of labor with spontaneous contractions in the previous sPTB to the model increased the absolute risk of sPTB, but risk reduction of aspirin was similar (58.1% vs 86.2%; ARR 27.5%, 95% CI 0.16–0.39).

Conclusion: In women with a history of sPTB, low‐dose aspirin was associated with a lower risk of recurrent sPTB among those who also received a cerclage in the study pregnancy. These findings highlight a possible targeted use of aspirin in a clinically identifiable subgroup of women to prevent sPTB.

257 | Defining Fetal Growth Restriction: A Sensitivity Analysis to Predict Perinatal Mortality in Zambia

Austin Oberlin1; Madison Calvert2; Imanga Ikabongo3; Katelyn J. Rittenhouse4; M. Bridget Spelke5; Margaret P. Kasaro6; Bellington Vwalika7; Yuri Sebastião8; Jeffrey Stringer8; Elizabeth M. Stringer5

1Yale University, New Haven, CT; 2University of North Carolina School of Medicine, Chapel Hill, NC; 3University of Zambia, Lusaka, Zambia; 4University of North Carolina, Durham, NC; 5Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, University of North Carolina School of Medicine, Chapel Hill, NC; 6UNC Global Projects‐Zambia, LLC, Lusaka, Zambia; 7Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, University of Zambia School of Medicine, Lusaka, Zambia; 8Division of Global Women's Health, Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, University of North Carolina School of Medicine, Chapel Hill, NC

Objective: Ultrasound is widely used in high income settings to screen at‐risk pregnancies for fetal growth restriction (FGR). Whether FGR definitions meaningfully discriminate risk of perinatal mortality in low‐resource settings ‐ where implementation is costly, and access is limited ‐ remains unclear. We compared commonly used definitions of FGR for prediction of perinatal mortality in a Zambian cohort.

Study Design: We combined participants from one observational cohort and two RCTs conducted in Lusaka, Zambia between 2015–2022. Singleton pregnancies without fetal anomalies were included. All had at least one growth US after 20 wks, most frequently between 32–34 wks. Our primary outcome was perinatal mortality (stillbirth or neonatal death <28 days). We evaluated FGR definitions across biometric formulas (Hadlock, INTERGROWTH, Shepard) and growth standards (EFW < 10th percentile, EFW or AC < 10th percentile; Delphi in a subset). For each definition, we estimated area under the ROC curve and calculated sensitivity, specificity, PPV and NPV. Logistic regression models for each definition of FGR were fit for the odds of perinatal mortality and adjusted for maternal age, BMI, hypertensive disorders, diabetes, and HIV.

Results: Among 3239 pregnancies included, 180 (5.6%) experienced a perinatal death (Table 1). Across all FGR definitions, discrimination for perinatal mortality was poor (AUC range 0.52–0.61), with low sensitivity and moderate specificity (Table 2). The best performing model, EFW < 10th by INTERGROWTH, had an AOR of 2.6 (95% CI:1.7–3.9); sensitivity of 17.8% and specificity of 92.3%. A Delphi definition of FGR showed slightly improved performance (AUC 0.61) but was not associated with perinatal mortality in an adjusted model, possibly due to only a subset having Doppler available (n = 611/3239).

Conclusion: Common ultrasound‐based definitions of FGR demonstrate poor discrimination for perinatal mortality in a low‐resource setting, limiting their utility as standalone screening tools. Incorporation of Doppler shows promise but requires a larger sample to validate these findings in a similar setting.

258 | The Optimal Rate for Postpartum Prophylactic Oxytocin to Reduce Blood Loss After Vaginal Birth

Ethan Litman1; Kathleen Clarke1; Kimberley Campbell1; Yunping Li1; Eric Ganz1; Sarah E. Little2; Uma Deshmukh1

1Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center, Boston, MA; 2Maternal‐Fetal Medicine, Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center, Boston, MA

Objective: To determine the optimal rate of prophylactic oxytocin infusion following vaginal birth.

Study Design: We conducted an intention‐to‐treat block‐randomized quality improvement initiative at a high‐volume academic tertiary care center from June to October 2025. Pregnant patients who had a vaginal birth were included. In two‐week intervals, three prophylactic oxytocin regimens were implemented: low (83 mL/hr; 10 IU over 2 hours), intermediate (250 mL/hr; 30 IU over 2 hours), and high (500 mL/ hr; 30 IU over 1 hour). The primary outcome was median quantitative blood loss (QBL). Secondary outcomes related to postpartum hemorrhage and adverse outcomes were pre‐specified. Baseline demographic and delivery characteristics were abstracted from the electronic medical record. Outcomes were compared using Kruskal–Wallis testing with post hoc pairwise Wilcoxon rank‐sum tests with Bonferroni correction. Effect modification of body‐mass index and oxytocin exposure were evaluated in multivariable regression models.

Results: Among 1390 patients admitted for labor, 1094 (79.0%) had vaginal births, including 377, 349, and 368 patients in the ‘low’, ‘intermediate’, and ‘high’ rate groups, respectively. Most patients were multiparous, full term, and had singletons. The high‐rate group had the lowest QBL: 365 mL [IQR 244–631] vs. 430 [IQR 260 −735] (intermediate rate) and 465 [IQR 285–725] (low rate), (p = 0.014). Adverse events were rare and similar across groups.

Conclusion: Following vaginal birth, a higher prophylactic oxytocin infusion rate was associated with less blood loss compared to intermediate or low rates, without an increase in adverse events.

259 | Home Telemonitoring in High‐Risk Pregnancies: A Finnish Retrospective Cohort Study

Tiina Stark1; Heidi Kruit2; Leena Rahkonen3

1Helsinki University Hospital Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Helsinki, Finland; 2Helsinki University Hospital, Helsinki, Finland; 3Aalto University, Espoo, Finland

Objective: High‐risk pregnancies require intensive monitoring. Home telemonitoring may provide an alternative to improve access and patient satisfaction while reducing healthcare costs. This retrospective cohort study evaluated the clinical course of home telemonitoring in high‐risk pregnancies at an urban tertiary hospital.

Study Design: This retrospective three‐year cohort study included 117 singleton pregnancies enrolled in a home telemonitoring program at Helsinki University Hospital (January 2021–December 2023). Participants were stratified by primary indication: premature rupture of membranes, pre‐eclampsia and/or fetal growth restriction, or other high‐risk conditions. Outcomes included duration and course of telemonitoring, hospital visits and readmissions, onset of labor or emergency cesarean delivery, and severe complications during monitoring (e.g., maternal or fetal death, placental abruption, umbilical cord prolapse, bleeding placenta previa, chorioamnionitis, eclampsia, or worsening pre‐eclampsia).

Results: Median gestational age at telemonitoring initiation was 32.4 weeks, with a median duration of 7 days (IQR 2.5–13.5). Readmission occurred in 32.6% of cases. Median gestational age at delivery was 34.6 weeks, with 87.2% (102/117) delivering preterm. The cesarean rate was 47.0% (55/117). Spontaneous onset of labor during monitoring occurred in 9.4% (11/117), with no home deliveries. No maternal or intrauterine fetal deaths were observed. Complications occurred in 30.8% (36/117), most commonly chorioamnionitis (n = 21,17.9%) and worsening pre‐eclampsia (n = 11, 9.4%); rare events included placental abruption and bleeding placenta previa (n = 2, 1.7% each).

Conclusion: This study indicates that home telemonitoring may be a viable strategy for selected high‐risk pregnancies, contingent upon rigorous patient selection and strict adherence to clinical protocols.

260 | Characteristics and Trustworthiness of Randomised Controlled Trials on Preterm Birth Prevention

Wanlin Li1; Maya Reddy1; Qian Feng1; Janneke van't Hooft2; Wentao Li3; B. W. J. W. Mol1

1Monash University, Melbourne, Australia; 2University of Amsterdam, Amsterdam, Netherlands; 3The University of New South Wales, Sydney, Australia

Objective: Preterm birth (PTB) is one of the most pressing problems in modern obstetrics, with preventative strategies typically evaluated in Randomised Controlled Trials (RCTs). However, the characteristics and trustworthiness of these trials have not been systematically examined.

Study Design: We systematically included PTB prevention RCTs from a systematic review (CRD42019153728) and an updated search of MEDLINE, Embase, and CENTRAL (2013–2025) databases. Eligible RCTs on PTB prevention among asymptomatic women were assessed for trial characteristics, registration, reproducibility of univariable statistics, individual participant data (IPD) availability and trustworthiness using a checklist (TRACT), and excessive baseline characteristics similarity that is incompatible with chance.

Results: Of 153 eligible RCTs, five had been retracted. Among the remaining 148 RCTs, 52/148 (35%) were considered trustworthy based on shared valid IPD, 64/148 (43%) based on the checklist, while 32/148 (21%) did not meet trustworthiness criteria. As shown in part A of Table 1, progestogens were the most frequently studied intervention. Trustworthy trials, regardless of IPD sharing, were more frequently multicentre, government‐funded, and less often concluded evidence of benefit (all p < 0.001; Table 1, part A). Europe and Americas contributed most trustworthy trials, whereas trials not meeting trustworthiness criteria were most frequently observed in the Eastern Mediterranean (p < 0.001; Table 1, part A). Baseline characteristics in trials that met trustworthiness criteria showed consistent distribution expected under proper randomisation, while trials that did not meet the criteria were more likely to show deviation from this distribution (Figure 1). Reproducibility inconsistencies in univariable statistics were more frequent in trials not meeting trustworthiness criteria (Table 1, part B).

Conclusion: Among RCTs evaluating PTB prevention strategies, 20% raise trustworthiness concerns, potentially undermining the reliability of the evidence underlying practice.

261 | HIV and Pregnancy Outcomes: An Updated Retrospective Cohort Analysis

Sarena R. Hayer; Bharti R. Garg; Jamie O. Lo; Aaron B. Caughey; Irina R. Cassimatis

Oregon Health & Science University, Portland, OR

Objective: While human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) in the United States (US) has declined, HIV in pregnancy remains a relevant clinical concern. Historically, pregnancies complicated by HIV‐positive status have been associated with adverse perinatal outcomes. Despite improvements in screening and timely treatment, recent data on perinatal outcomes among pregnant individuals living with HIV remain limited. The objective of this study is to examine the association between HIV‐positive status in pregnancy and adverse outcomes in a contemporary cohort.

Study Design: This is a retrospective cohort study using linked vital statistics and hospital discharge data for births in California (2008–2020). We included HIV‐positive pregnant individuals with a singleton gestation who delivered between 23 and 42 weeks. HIV‐positive status was identified using ICD‐9 (0.42x) and ICD‐10 (B20) codes. Chi‐square tests and multivariable Poisson regression models were used to assess risk of outcomes.

Results: Compared to those without HIV, HIV‐positive pregnant individuals (n = 552) were more likely to be 35 years or older (29.5% vs. 20.5%; p < 0.001) and non‐Hispanic Black (27.7% vs. 4.9%; p < 0.001). They also had less prenatal care (5.0% vs. 2.4%; p < 0.001), attained high school or less education (64.9% vs. 42.8%; p < 0.001), and were more likely to be publicly insured (84.1% vs. 51.6%; p < 0.001). On multivariate regression, HIV‐positive status was associated with increased risk of placental abruption (aRR = 1.87, 95% CI 1.10, 3.18), postpartum hemorrhage (aRR = 2.19, 95% CI 1.61, 2.96), and severe maternal morbidity (aRR = 3.72, 95% CI 2.68, 5.17). HIV‐positive status was also associated with increased risk of preterm birth <37 weeks (aRR = 1.94, 95% CI 1.61, 2.34), neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) admission (aRR = 1.86, 95% CI 1.60, 2.16), and respiratory distress syndrome (aRR = 2.25, 95% CI 1.68, 3.01).

Conclusion: Despite changes to the HIV landscape in the US, HIV‐positive status remains associated with increased risk of perinatal outcomes.

1UTHealth Houson, Houston, TX; 2McGovern Medical School at UTHealth Houston, Houston, TX; 3McGovern Medical School at UTHealth, Houston, TX

Objective: The Society for Maternal‐Fetal Medicine (SMFM) currently does not recommend routine cervical length (CL) screening after cerclage placement. The objective of this study is to evaluate whether CL after cerclage placement correlates with risk of preterm birth in patients who underwent physical examination‐indicated cerclage (PEIC).

Study Design: This is retrospective cohort study of patients who underwent a PEIC placement at a tertiary center between 2021 and 2024. All patients received prophylactic antibiotics and indomethacin prior to the procedure. Post‐cerclage CL was measured via transvaginal ultrasound by trained sonographers. Patients were stratified into two groups: group 1 included patients with post‐cerclage CL: ≤25 mm and group 2 included patients with post‐cerclage CL >25 mm. The primary outcome was pretermbirth <34 weeks. Secondary outcomes included median latency period (time between cerclage placement and delivery), latency >28 days, preterm birth <34 weeks, preterm birth <28 weeks, and preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM).

Results: A total of 101 patients who underwent PEIC were included, 50 had a CL ≤25 mm (group 1) and 51 had a CL >25 mm (group 2). Baseline characteristics were similar between groups, except for higher obesity rates in the CL ≤25 mm group (77.6% vs. 54.9%, p = 0.017). Median latency was significantly shorter in group 1 (12.6 weeks [IQR 4.8–15.6]) compared to group 2 (15.3 weeks [IQR 9.21–16.5]) p = 0.049, with less pregnancies achieving latency >28 days (76.0% vs 92.2%, p = 0.026) (Table 1). While the rate of PTB <37 weeks was lower in group 2, the difference was not statistically significant.

Conclusion: In women undergoing emergent PEIC, those with a post‐procedure CL ≤25 mm have a 46% rate of PTB <34 weeks (Relative Risk 1.47). CL > 25 mm following PEIC is associated with significantly prolonged latency and a higher rate of achieving latency >28 days. Post‐procedure CL identifies patients at higher risk for earlier delivery, which allows for additional targeted interventions and patient counseling.

263 | Misoprostol Versus Oxytocin for Labor Induction in Women With Term PROM: Systematic Review and Meta‐Analysis

Aviya Hetzroni1; Bluma Yael Kisos1; Asnat Walfisch2; Hila Hochler1

1Laniado Medical Center, Ariel University, Natanya, Israel; 2Rabin Medical Center, Reichman University, Petah Tikva, Israel

Objective: To compare the efficacy and safety of misoprostol versus oxytocin for labor induction in women with term premature rupture of membranes (PROM) through an updated systematic review and meta‐analysis.

Study Design: This systematic review and meta‐analysis was conducted according to PRISMA guidelines. PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar were searched from 2005 through March 2026 for randomized controlled trials comparing misoprostol with oxytocin for labor induction in women with singleton pregnancies and term PROM (≥37 weeks). The primary outcome was induction‐to‐vaginal delivery interval. Secondary outcomes included induction‐to‐any‐delivery interval, cesarean delivery, NICU admission, and selected maternal and neonatal adverse outcomes. Risk of bias was assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool, and pooled estimates were calculated using fixed‐ or random‐effects models according to heterogeneity.

Results: A total of 3604 records were identified through the database search, of which 72 full‐text articles were assessed for eligibility. 14 randomized controlled trials including 1891 women were ultimately included in the meta‐analysis. The pooled analysis demonstrated a significantly shorter induction‐to‐vaginal delivery interval with misoprostol compared with oxytocin (mean difference −1.15 hours; 95% CI −1.95 to −0.35), although heterogeneity was considerable (I2 = 79.6%). Cesarean delivery rates showed a nonsignificant trend toward lower rates in the misoprostol group (RR 0.79; 95% CI 0.59–1.06; I2 = 47.7%). NICU admission did not differ significantly between groups (RR 0.77; 95% CI 0.49–1.20; I2 = 12.8%). Maternal and neonatal adverse outcomes including postpartum hemorrhage, chorioamnionitis, and low Apgar scores showed no consistent differences between induction methods.

Conclusion: Among women with term PROM undergoing labor induction, misoprostol is associated with a shorter induction‐to‐vaginal delivery interval and a trend toward lower cesarean delivery rates compared with oxytocin, suggesting it may be a preferred alternative for labor induction.

We aimed to identify baseline characteristics associated with inadequate response to IV iron to identify patients likely to require additional interventions beyond an initial treatment.

Participants had moderate iron deficiency anemia (Hb 7.0–9.9 g/dL and ferritin < 30 ng/mL or transferrin saturation (TSAT) <20%). For this analysis we included those randomized to IV iron. Objective: To evaluate maternal and neonatal outcomes associated with placement of a second sequential transcervical Foley balloon for cervical ripening when the cervix remained unfavorable after initial balloon ripening.

Study Design: Retrospective cohort study of 799 patients undergoing induction with transcervical Foley balloon at a single academic center from January 2020 to January 2025. Exposure was placement of a sequential Foley after removal or expulsion of the initial balloon. Primary outcomes were cesarean delivery and suspected intraamniotic infection per ACOG criteria. Secondary outcomes were time from initial balloon placement to delivery, umbilical venous cord pH, 1‐ and 5‐minute Apgar scores, and neonatal length of stay. Our data does not support routine use of a second, sequential balloon after ineffective cervical balloon ripening.

Study Design: We conducted a retrospective cohort study of singleton, vertex pregnancies undergoing medically indicated induction between 37.0–38.6 weeks gestation at a single academic center (2020–2025). Patients required a documented Bishop score and no history of prior cesarean. We excluded elective inductions, spontaneous labor, multifetal gestation, nonvertex presentation, fetal anomalies, and Bishop score >6.

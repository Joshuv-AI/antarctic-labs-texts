A variant of the Linnik-Sprindžuk theoremfor simple zeros of Dirichlet L L -functions Thanks: MSC Numbers: Primary: 11M06, 11M26; Secondary: 11M20. Thanks: Keywords: generalized Riemann hypothesis, Riemann zeta function, Lindelöf hypothesis, simple zeros.
William D. Banks

Abstract
. For a primitive Dirichlet character 𝔛 {\mathfrak{X}} , a new hypothesis 𝚁𝙷 𝚜𝚒𝚖 † ​ [ 𝔛 ] {\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}] is introduced, which asserts that (1) all simple zeros of L ⁡ ( s , 𝔛 ) L(s,{\mathfrak{X}}) in the critical strip are located on the critical line, and (2) these zeros satisfy some specific conditions on their vertical distribution. We show that 𝚁𝙷 𝚜𝚒𝚖 † ​ [ 𝔛 ] {\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}] (for any 𝔛 {\mathfrak{X}} ) is a consequence of the generalized Riemann hypothesis . Assuming only the generalized Lindelöf hypothesis , we show that if 𝚁𝙷 𝚜𝚒𝚖 † ​ [ 𝔛 ] {\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}] holds for one primitive character 𝔛 {\mathfrak{X}} , then it holds for every such 𝔛 {\mathfrak{X}} . If this occurs, then for every character χ \chi (primitive or not), all simple zeros of L ⁡ ( s , χ ) L(s,\chi) in the critical strip are located on the critical line. In particular, Siegel zeros cannot exist in this situation.

Introduction

An old result of Sprindžuk
[
13
,
14
]
, which he obtained
by developing ideas of Linnik
[
11
]
, asserts that the

generalized Riemann hypothesis
(GRH) holds for all
Dirichlet
L
-functions provided that the
Riemann hypothesis
(RH)
is true and that certain conditions on the vertical distribution of the zeros
of
\zeta(s)
are met. Specifically, Sprindžuk showed under RH that
every
L
-function
L(s,\chi)
satisfies GRH
provided that the asymptotic formula

\sum_{\gamma}|\gamma|^{i\gamma}\mathrm{e}^{-i\gamma-\pi|\gamma|/2}\Bigl(x+2\pi i\frac{h}{k}\Bigl)^{-1/2-i\gamma}=-\frac{\mu(k)}{x\sqrt{2\pi}\,\phi(k)}+O(x^{-1/2-\varepsilon})

holds as
x\to 0^{+}
for any coprime integers
h,k
with

0<|h|\leqslant k/2
, where the sum runs over the ordinates
\gamma

of the nontrivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
. This is known as the

Linnik–Sprindžuk theorem
.
Some similar results have
been obtained by Fujii
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
, Suzuki
[
15
]
,
Kaczorowski and Perelli
[
10
]
, and the author
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

In the present paper, we establish a variant
of the Linnik–Sprindžuk theorem focused on
the
simple
zeros of Dirichlet
L
-functions
.
Assuming the
generalized Lindelöf hypothesis
,
we show that the horizontal and vertical distribution of the simple zeros
of any single
L
-function

L(s,\chi)
can strongly influence
the horizontal and vertical
distribution of the simple zeros of any other Dirichlet
L
-function.
In particular, we give a new criterion for the
nonexistence of
Siegel zeros
.

Our results are formulated in §
2
after
the necessary notation has been introduced.
To prove the main theorem, we study the interplay
and consequences of various hypotheses on the
L
-functions attached
to Dirichlet characters
\chi
.

Hypothesis
{\tt RH}[\chi]
:

If
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
is a zero of
L(s,\chi)
with
\beta>0
,
then
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
.

In particular,
{\tt RH}[{\mathbbm{1}}]
is the Riemann hypothesis, where
{\mathbbm{1}}
is the trivial
character defined by
{\mathbbm{1}}(n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1
for all
n
. Also, GRH is equivalent to the
truth of
{\tt RH}[\chi]
for all characters
\chi
.

Hypothesis
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\chi]
:

If
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
is a
simple
zero of
L(s,\chi)
such that
\beta>0
,
then
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
.

Hypothesis
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\boldsymbol{\star}]
:

Hypothesis
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\chi]
is true for
every
character
\chi
.

The latter
two hypotheses lie at the heart of our work.
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\chi]
is
a weak form of
{\tt RH}[\chi]
which asserts that the
simple

L(s,\chi)
in the critical strip all lie on the critical line;
nothing is assumed about the

L(s,\chi)
(which are all simple)
or any zeros of multiplicity two or more

).

Hypothesis
{\tt LH}[\chi]
:

The function
L(s,\chi)
satisfies the
Lindelöf bound

(1.1)

L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\chi)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,\tau^{\varepsilon}\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R}),

where

is the modulus of

\tau=\tau(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|t|+10
.

In particular, note that
{\tt LH}[{\mathbbm{1}}]
is the classical
Lindelöf hypothesis

for
\zeta(s)
. More generally,
{\tt LH}[\chi]
is (a weak form of) the
generalized Lindelöf hypothesis
for
L(s,\chi)
.

Hypothesis
{\tt LH}[\boldsymbol{\star}]
:

The hypothesis
{\tt LH}[\chi]
holds for
every
character
\chi
.

Following Riemann, the letter

Any implied constants in the

any dependence on other parameters is indicated explicitly by the notation.
For example, (
1.1

\varepsilon>0

|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\chi)|\leqslant C\tau^{\varepsilon}
holds for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

with some constant

that depends only on

For an arbitrary character
\chi
, we make extensive use of the
function

D(s,\chi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi)^{2}}{L(s,\chi)}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\ell(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}

and extended analytically to the complex plane.
Here,

\ell(n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\Lambda*\log)(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in\mathbb{N}\\

Note that

The function

is meromorphic with

at every simple zero

On the other hand,

.

Recall the Laurent series expansion of
\zeta(s)
at
s=1

(see, e.g.,
[
5
, Prop. 10.3.19]

\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\,\gamma_{n}(s-1)^{n},

where
\{\gamma_{n}\}
are the
Stieltjes constants
given by

\gamma_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k\leqslant x}\frac{(\log k)^{n}}{k}-\frac{(\log x)^{n+1}}{n+1}\bigg)\qquad(n\geqslant 0).

In particular,
\gamma_{0}
is the
Euler-Mascheroni constant
. For any
q
, let

g_{q}(s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\prod_{p\,\mid\,q}(1-p^{-s}),

and denote

\displaystyle P_{q}(X)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\tfrac{1}{2}g_{q}(1)X(\log X)^{2}-(1+\gamma_{0})g_{q}(1)X\log X

\displaystyle+~\big((1+\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}+3\gamma_{1})g_{q}(1)-\gamma_{0}g_{q}^{\prime}(1)-\tfrac{1}{2}g_{q}^{\prime\prime}(1)\big)X.

This function is defined so that

(2.2)

\mathop{{\rm Res}}\limits_{s=1}D(s,\chi_{0,q})\frac{X^{s}}{s}=P_{q}(X)\qquad(X>0),

where
\chi_{0,q}
is the principal character mod

.

The following hypothesis on a primitive character
{\mathfrak{X}}

Hypothesis
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}]
:

The primitive character
{\mathfrak{X}}
mod
{\mathfrak{q}}

satisfies
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[{\mathfrak{X}}]
, and for any rational
number
\xi=h/k
with
h,k>0
and
(h,k)=1
,
any
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and
X\geqslant 10
, we have

(2.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma\\
\gamma>0\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}L^{\prime}(\rho,{\mathfrak{X}}\cdot\chi_{0,{\mathfrak{q}}k})\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-\rho)\mathcal{B}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi\xi X}\right)-C_{{\mathfrak{X}},\xi}\cdot F_{{\mathfrak{q}},k}(X)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{{\mathfrak{q}},\xi,\mathcal{B}}\,X^{1/2+\varepsilon},

where the sum runs over simple zeros
of
L(s,{\mathfrak{X}})
in the critical strip,

(2.4)

C_{{\mathfrak{X}},\xi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}(h){\mathfrak{X}}(k)\mu(k)}{\phi({\mathfrak{q}}k)}}&\quad\hbox{if $(h,{\mathfrak{q}}k)=1$},\\

\end{cases}

and

(2.5)

F_{{\mathfrak{q}},k}(X)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u/({\mathfrak{q}}X))P^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{q}}k}(u)\,du.

Observe that
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}]
is formulated
entirely in terms of the simple zeros of a single
L
-function

;
the hypothesis asserts nothing about any zeros of higher order
that might exist.
For characters
{\mathfrak{X}}_{1}\neq{\mathfrak{X}}_{2}
,
there is no reason

to expect that the hypotheses

{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}_{1}]

and
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}_{2}]
are related. However,
our principal theorem reveals a close connection.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume
{\tt LH}[\boldsymbol{\star}]
. For each primitive character
{\mathfrak{X}}
, the
hypotheses
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}]
and
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\boldsymbol{\star}]
are equivalent.

Thus, assuming
{\tt LH}[\boldsymbol{\star}]
, Theorem
2.3
implies that

{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}_{1}]\Longleftrightarrow{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}_{2}]

for all primitive characters
{\mathfrak{X}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{X}}_{2}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Under
GRH
,
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}]
holds for every primitive character
{\mathfrak{X}}
.

This is clear since GRH implies both
{\tt LH}[\boldsymbol{\star}]
and
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\boldsymbol{\star}]
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Assume
{\tt LH}[\boldsymbol{\star}]
, and suppose
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}]
is true for some
primitive character
{\mathfrak{X}}
. Then the simple zeros of any Dirichlet

L
-function
L(s,\chi)
lie on the critical line. In particular,
Siegel zeros do not exist.

The following statements are equivalent:

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\sigma_{0}>0
and
F\in\mathcal{V}(\sigma_{0})

F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}(\sigma_{0}+\frac{1}{25})

Proof.

Let

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{2}{25})

\mathcal{C}
be the circle in the complex plane
with center

and each

,
s\neq 1
. We consider three different cases.

First, suppose
s=\rho
. Then
f_{\rho}(s)=L^{\prime\prime}(\rho,\chi)
, and

\rho=s\in\mathcal{R}(\frac{4}{25})
, hence (
3.11
)
follows directly from (
3.10
).

Next, suppose
0<|s-\rho|\leqslant\frac{1}{50\log\tau}
, and write

f_{\rho}(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{f_{\rho}(z)}{(z-s)}\,dz=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{L^{\prime}(z,\chi)-L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}{(z-\rho)(z-s)}\,dz

where
\mathcal{C}
is the circle in the complex plane with center
s

and radius
\frac{1}{25\log\tau}
, oriented counterclockwise.
It is straightforward to check that

\min\{|z-s|,|z-\rho|\}\geqslant\tfrac{1}{50\log\tau}\qquad(z\in\mathcal{C}),

and therefore

f_{\rho}(s)\ll(\log\tau)\Big\{\max\limits_{z\in\mathcal{C}}\big|L^{\prime}(z,\chi)\big|+\big|L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)\big|\Big\}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,(\log\tau)\max\limits_{z\in\mathcal{C}}\big|L^{\prime}(z,\chi)\big|+\tau^{\varepsilon},

where we used (
3.12
) in the second step.
To prove (
3.11
) in this case, it is enough to show that

(3.13)

L^{\prime}(z,\chi)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,\tau^{\lambda(\sigma)+\varepsilon/2}\qquad(z\in\mathcal{C}).

Let
z=x+iy
be a number in
\mathcal{C}
.
Since
|x-\sigma|\leqslant|z-s|=\frac{1}{25\log\tau}
, we have

\tau^{\lambda(x)}\asymp\tau^{\lambda(\sigma)}
and also
z\in\mathcal{R}(\frac{1}{5})
;
hence (
3.13
) follows from (
3.8
).

Finally, suppose
|s-\rho|\geqslant\frac{1}{50\log\tau}
. In this case,

f_{\rho}(s)\ll(\log\tau)\Big\{\big|L^{\prime}(s,\chi)\big|+\big|L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)\big|\Big\}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,(\log\tau)\big|L^{\prime}(s,\chi)\big|+\tau^{\varepsilon}.

Since
s\in\mathcal{R}(\frac{4}{25})
, the required bound (
3.11
) follows from (
3.8
).
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Under
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\chi]
and
{\tt LH}[\chi]
, we have

(3.14)

D(s,\chi)+\delta_{\chi}\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi)}{s-1}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,\tau^{\lambda(\sigma)+\varepsilon}+\delta_{\chi}|s-1|^{-2}\tau^{\varepsilon}+|\sigma-\tfrac{1}{2}|^{-1}\tau^{\varepsilon}

uniformly for
s\in\mathcal{R}(\frac{1}{5})
.

Proof.

Suppose
\chi
is induced from the primitive character
{\mathfrak{X}}
.
Unconditionally, we have (see, e.g.,
[
12
, Lems. 12.1 and 12.6]
):

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,{\mathfrak{X}})=-\frac{\delta_{{\mathfrak{X}}}}{s-1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma-t|\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{s-\rho}+O_{{\mathfrak{q}}}(\log\tau)\qquad(\sigma\in[-1,2]),

where the sum runs over nontrivial zeros
\rho
of
L(s,{\mathfrak{X}})
.
Taking into account that

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)=\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,{\mathfrak{X}})+\sum_{p\,\mid\,q}\frac{{\mathfrak{X}}(p)\log p}{p^{s}-{\mathfrak{X}}(p)},

it follows that

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)=-\frac{\delta_{\chi}}{s-1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma-t|\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{s-\rho}+O_{q}(\log\tau)\qquad(\sigma\in[\tfrac{1}{5},2]).

Multiplying by
L^{\prime}(s,\chi)
, we get that

(3.15)

{\widetilde{D}}(s,\chi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma-t|\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi)}{s-\rho}+O_{q}(|L^{\prime}(s,\chi)|\log\tau)\qquad(\sigma\in[\tfrac{1}{5},2]),

where
{\widetilde{D}}(s,\chi)
is the function defined on the left side of (
3.14
).
The error term in (
3.15
) is acceptable in view of (
3.8
).
To bound the sum in (
3.15
), observe that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma-t|\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi)}{s-\rho}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho~\text{\tt non-simple}\\
|\gamma-t|\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}f_{\rho}(s)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho~\text{\tt simple}\\
|\gamma-t|\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}\frac{L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)}{s-\rho}.

As these sums all involve
\ll\log q\tau
zeros, we obtain (
3.14
) by
applying Lemma
3.4
together with (
3.12
), taking into
account that
|s-\rho|\geqslant|\sigma-\frac{1}{2}|
for any simple zero
\rho

(under
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\chi]
). This completes the proof.
∎

The next result, used in the proof of Theorem
5.1

below, is conditional on
{\tt LH}[\chi]
but not on
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\chi]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume
{\tt LH}[\chi]
.
For any
t\geqslant 2
, there is a real number
t_{*}\in[t,t+1]
such that

D(\sigma\pm it_{*},\chi)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,\tau^{\lambda(\sigma)+\varepsilon}\qquad(\sigma\in[\tfrac{2}{25},2]).

Proof.

By
[
12
, Lemmas 12.2 and 12.7]
, there is a number
t_{*}\in[t,t+1]
such that

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma\pm it_{*},\chi)\ll(\log qt)^{2}\qquad(\sigma\in[-1,2]).

Multiplying by
L^{\prime}(\sigma\pm it_{*},\chi)
and using (
3.8
), the result follows.
∎

4.
Criteria for
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\chi]

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\chi
be a character mod
q
that
satisfies
{\tt LH}[\chi]
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\chi]
is true;

(ii)

For any
X\geqslant 10
, we have

(4.1)

\sum_{n\leqslant X}\ell(n)\chi(n)=\delta_{\chi}P_{q}(X)+O_{q}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon});

(iii)

For any
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
X\geqslant 10
, we have

(4.2)

\sum_{n}\ell(n)\chi(n)\mathcal{B}(n/X)=\delta_{\chi}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u/X)P^{\prime}_{q}(u)\,du+O_{q,\mathcal{B}}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}).

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
.
Let
T\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sqrt{X}
,
\sigma_{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1+\frac{1}{\log X}
,
and
\sigma_{1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\log X}
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be the rectangular contour in
\mathbb{C}
consisting of the four
directed line segments:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}:

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}-iT\longrightarrow\sigma_{0}+iT,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}:

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}+iT\longrightarrow\sigma_{1}+iT,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{3}:

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}+iT\longrightarrow\sigma_{1}-iT,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{4}:

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}-iT\longrightarrow\sigma_{0}-iT.

Using Perron’s formula (see, e.g.,
[
12
, Thm. 5.2 and Cor. 5.3]
),
it follows that

\sum_{n\leqslant X}\ell(n)\chi(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}D(s,\chi)\,\frac{X^{s}}{s}\,ds+O(X^{1/2}(\log X)^{3}),

where the implied constant is absolute.
Under
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\chi]
, the function
D(s,\chi)
is analytic in
the half-plane
\{\sigma>\frac{1}{2}\}
unless
\chi=\mathbbm{1}
, in which case
there is a triple pole at
s=1
. Using Cauchy’s theorem and (
2.2
), we see that

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\bigg(\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}+\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}+\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{3}}+\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{4}}\bigg)D(s,\chi)\,\frac{X^{s}}{s}\,ds=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}D(s,\chi)\,\frac{X^{s}}{s}\,ds=\delta_{\chi}P_{q}(X);

consequently,

\sum_{n\leqslant X}\ell(n)\chi(n)=\delta_{\chi}P_{q}(X)-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\bigg(\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}+\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{3}}+\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{4}}\bigg)D(s,\chi)\,\frac{X^{s}}{s}\,ds+O_{\varepsilon}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}).

By Corollary
3.3
(bound (
3.8
)) and
Lemma
3.5
(bound (
3.14
)), the bound

D(s,\chi)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,\tau^{\varepsilon/2}\log X

holds uniformly for any
s
on the segments
\mathcal{C}_{2}
,
\mathcal{C}_{3}
, and
\mathcal{C}_{4}
; consequently,

\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}D(s,\chi)\,\frac{X^{s}}{s}\,ds\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,\frac{\log X}{T^{1-\varepsilon/2}}\intop\nolimits_{\sigma_{1}}^{\sigma_{0}}X^{\sigma}\,d\sigma\ll X^{1/2+\varepsilon}\qquad(j=2\text{~or~}4),

and

\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{3}}D(s,\chi)\,\frac{X^{s}}{s}\,ds\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,X^{1/2}\log X\intop\nolimits_{-T}^{T}\frac{\tau^{\varepsilon/2}}{1+|t|}\,dt\ll X^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

Putting everything together, we obtain (
4.1
).

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Let

(4.3)

S(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\leqslant u}\ell(n)\chi(n)=\delta_{\chi}P_{q}(u)+E(u),\qquad\text{where}\quad E(u)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,u^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

In the region
\{\sigma>1\}
we have

(4.4)

D(s,\chi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}=\delta_{\chi}\intop\nolimits_{1}^{\infty}u^{-s}\,P^{\prime}_{q}(u)\,du+\intop\nolimits_{1^{-}}^{\infty}u^{-s}\,dE(u).

The first integral in (
4.4
) evaluates to

(4.5)

D_{\infty}(s,\chi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{g_{q}(1)}{(s-1)^{3}}-\frac{\gamma_{0}g_{q}(1)}{(s-1)^{2}}+\frac{(\gamma_{0}^{2}+3\gamma_{1})g_{q}(1)-\gamma_{0}g_{q}^{\prime}(1)-\tfrac{1}{2}g_{q}^{\prime\prime}(1)}{s-1},

which is the singular part of
D(s,\chi)
at
s=1
in the case that
\chi=\chi_{0,q}
.
Integrating by parts, we see that the second integral in (
4.4
)
is equal to

\intop\nolimits_{1^{-}}^{\infty}u^{-s}\,dE(u)=-P_{q}(1)+s\intop\nolimits_{1}^{\infty}u^{-s-1}E(u)\,du.

Since
E(u)\ll_{q}u^{1/2+\varepsilon}
, the right side continues analytically
to the half-plane
\{\sigma>\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon\}
; consequently, the function

D(s,\chi)-\delta_{\chi}D_{\infty}(s,\chi)

continues analytically to the same half-plane. In particular,

L(s,\chi)
has no simple zeros in that region.
Taking
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, this verifies
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\chi]
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
. Using (
4.1
)
and (
4.3
), we have by partial summation:

\sum_{n}\ell(n)\chi(n)\mathcal{B}(n/X)=\delta_{\chi}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u/X)P^{\prime}_{q}(u)\,du+\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u/X)\,dE(u).

Using integration by parts and the bound
E(u)\ll_{q}u^{1/2+\varepsilon}
,
it is easily shown that the second integral is
\ll_{q,\mathcal{B}}X^{1/2+\varepsilon}
,
hence we have (
4.2
).

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
. For each
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
,
we define

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(s)=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u)u^{s-1}\,du\qquad(s\in\mathbb{C}),

and we fix a number
d_{\mathcal{B}}<1
such that
\mathcal{B}(u)=0
if
u\leqslant d_{\mathcal{B}}
or
u\geqslant d_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}
.
Let

f_{\mathcal{B}}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n}\ell(n)\chi(n)\mathcal{B}(n/x),\qquad g_{\mathcal{B}}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\delta_{\chi}\intop\nolimits_{xd_{\mathcal{B}}}^{x/d_{\mathcal{B}}}\mathcal{B}(u/x)P^{\prime}_{q}(u)\,du,

and observe that
f_{\mathcal{B}}(x)=0
for all
x\leqslant d_{\mathcal{B}}
. By (
4.2
), we have

(4.6)

f_{\mathcal{B}}(x)-g_{\mathcal{B}}(x)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\mathcal{B}}\,x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\qquad(x\geqslant 10).

In the half-plane
\{\sigma>1\}
, it is straightforward to show that

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{d_{\mathcal{B}}}^{\infty}f_{\mathcal{B}}(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(s)\cdot D(s,\chi),

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{d_{\mathcal{B}}}^{\infty}g_{\mathcal{B}}(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(s)\cdot\delta_{\chi}\intop\nolimits_{d_{\mathcal{B}}}^{\infty}u^{-s}P^{\prime}_{q}(u)\,du.

The latter integral can be split as
D_{0}(s,\chi)+D_{\infty}(s,\chi)
, where

D_{0}(s,\chi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\intop\nolimits_{d_{\mathcal{B}}}^{1}u^{-s}P^{\prime}_{q}(u)\,du\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad D_{\infty}(s,\chi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\intop\nolimits_{1}^{\infty}u^{-s}P^{\prime}_{q}(u)\,du.

The first integral defines
D_{0}(s,\chi)
as an entire function in the complex plane.
For
\sigma>1
,
D_{\infty}(s,\chi)
is explicitly given by (
4.5
). We see
that
D_{\infty}(s,\chi)
analytically continues to the complex plane except
for a possible pole at
s=1
. As mentioned above,
D_{\infty}(s,\chi)
is the singular part
of
D(s,\chi)
in
s=1
when
\chi=\chi_{0,q}
. As
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(s)

is entire, it follows that any pole of the function

I(s,\chi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\intop\nolimits_{d_{\mathcal{B}}}^{\infty}\big\{f_{\mathcal{B}}(x)-g_{\mathcal{B}}(x)\big\}x^{-s-1}\,dx=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(s)\cdot\big\{D(s,\chi)-D_{0}(s,\chi)-D_{\infty}(s,\chi)\big\}

with real part
\sigma>0
must occur at a
simple
zero
\rho
of
L(s,\chi)
.
Moreover, if
\rho
is such a zero, then choosing
\mathcal{B}
so that
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\rho)\neq 0
,
we see that
I(s,\chi)
does indeed have a pole at
s=\rho
.

On the other hand, in view of (
4.6
), the integral defining
I(s,\chi)

converges absolutely in the half-plane
\{\sigma>\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon\}
, so
I(s,\chi)

cannot have a pole there for any choice of
\mathcal{B}
. Taking
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we deduce
that
L(s,\chi)
has no simple zeros in
\{\sigma>\frac{1}{2}\}
, and
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\chi]

is verified.
∎

5.
Twisted sums with
\ell(n)

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\chi
be a character mod
q
induced from a primitive character
{\mathfrak{X}}

mod
{\mathfrak{q}}
that satisfies
{\tt LH}[{\mathfrak{X}}]
. For any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
T>0
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
\beta\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\\
0<\gamma\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-\rho)-\frac{\tau(\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}})}{{\mathfrak{q}}}\sum_{n\leqslant{\mathfrak{q}}T/(2\pi\xi)}\ell(n)\chi(n){\tt e}(-n\xi)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\xi}\,T^{1/2+\varepsilon}+1.

Proof.

The result is trivial for
T<100
, so we assume
T\geqslant 100
in what follows.
For any
u>0
, let

\Sigma_{1}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
\beta\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\\
0<\gamma\leqslant u\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-\rho),\qquad\Sigma_{2}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{\tau(\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}})}{{\mathfrak{q}}}\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant{\mathfrak{q}}u/(2\pi\xi)\end{subarray}}\hskip-5.0pt\ell(n)\chi(n){\tt e}(-n\xi).

In this notation, the theorem (for
T\geqslant 100
) asserts that

(5.1)

\Sigma_{1}(T)-\Sigma_{2}(T)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\xi}\,T^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

In fact, to prove (
5.1
) for any
T\geqslant 100
, it suffices to prove that

(5.2)

\Sigma_{1}(T_{*})-\Sigma_{2}(T_{*})\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\xi}\,T^{1/2+\varepsilon}

holds for
at least one
number
T_{*}\in[T,T+1]
. Indeed,
by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-\rho)\ll({\mathfrak{q}}\gamma)^{1/2}

uniformly for all nontrivial zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
L(s,\chi)

such that
\gamma\geqslant 10
(say). Consequently,

\big|\Sigma_{1}(T_{*})-\Sigma_{1}(T)\big|\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
\beta\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\\
T<\gamma\leqslant T_{*}\end{subarray}}\big|\xi^{-\rho}\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-\rho)\big|\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\xi}\,T^{1/2}\log T

since there at most
O(\log qT)
zeros with
T<\gamma\leqslant T_{*}
.
Furthermore,

\big|\Sigma_{2}(T_{*})-\Sigma_{2}(T)\big|\leqslant\frac{|\tau(\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}})|}{{\mathfrak{q}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{q}}T/(2\pi\xi)<n\leqslant{\mathfrak{q}}T_{*}/(2\pi\xi)\end{subarray}}\big|\ell(n)\chi(n){\tt e}(-n\xi/{\mathfrak{q}})\big|\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{{\mathfrak{q}},\xi}\,(\log T)^{2}

since
0\leqslant\ell(n)\leqslant(\log n)^{2}
.
These bounds make it clear that (
5.1
)
and (
5.2
) are equivalent, and the claim is proved.

By the preceding argument, and recalling Lemma
3.6
,
for the proof of (
5.1
) we can assume without loss of generality that

(5.3)

D(\sigma\pm iT,\chi)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,T^{\lambda(\sigma)+\varepsilon}\qquad(\sigma\in[\tfrac{2}{25},2]).

Moreover, by Lemma
3.6
, there is a number

t_{\circ}\in[2,3]
such that

D(\sigma\pm it_{\circ},\chi)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,1\qquad(\sigma\in[\tfrac{2}{25},2]).

For such
t_{\circ}
, it can be shown that

(5.4)

\Sigma_{1}(t_{\circ})\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\xi}\,1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\Sigma_{2}(t_{\circ})\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\xi}\,1;

see, e.g., the proof of
[
3
, Thm. 3.1]
.
We fix
t_{\circ}
and
T
with these properties.
Put
c\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1+\tfrac{1}{\log qT}
, and let
b
be any number
in the open interval
(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{\log qT},\frac{1}{2})

such that
L(s,\chi)\neq 0
for
\sigma\in[b,\frac{1}{2})
and
t\in[t_{\circ},T]
.
Finally, let
\mathcal{C}
be the rectangular contour consisting of the four
directed line segments:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}:

\displaystyle c+it_{\circ}\longrightarrow c+iT,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}:

\displaystyle c+iT\longrightarrow b+iT,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{3}:

\displaystyle b+iT\longrightarrow b+it_{\circ},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{4}:

\displaystyle b+it_{\circ}\longrightarrow c+it_{\circ}.

Our choices of
T
,
t_{\circ}
,
b
, and
c
guarantee that

D(s,\chi)
has no singularity on the contour
\mathcal{C}
.
By Cauchy’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}(T)-\Sigma_{1}(t_{\circ})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}D(s,\chi)\,\xi^{-s}\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\bigg(\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}+\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}+\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{3}}+\intop\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}_{4}}\bigg)D(s,\chi)\,\xi^{-s}\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-s)\,ds

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}\quad\text{(say)},

and thus by (
5.4
), we have

(5.5)

\Sigma_{1}(T)=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+O_{q}(1).

We estimate the four integrals
I_{j}
separately.

First, recalling the Dirichlet expansion

D(s,\chi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{L^{\prime}(s,\chi)^{2}}{L(s,\chi)}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\ell(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}\qquad(\sigma>1),

it is immediate that

I_{1}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\ell(n)\chi(n)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits_{c+it_{\circ}}^{c+iT}(n\xi)^{-s}\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-s)\,ds.

Applying Lemma
2.2
with both
T
and
t_{\circ}
, we derive the estimate

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau(\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}})}{{\mathfrak{q}}}\sum_{{\mathfrak{q}}t_{\circ}/(2\pi\xi)<n\leqslant{\mathfrak{q}}T/(2\pi\xi)}\ell(n)\chi(n){\tt e}(-n\xi/{\mathfrak{q}})+O\bigg(\sum_{n}\ell(n)\big|E({\mathfrak{q}},T,n\xi)\big|\bigg)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{2}(T)-\Sigma_{2}(t_{\circ})+O_{q,\xi}\big(T^{c-1/2}(E_{1}+E_{2})\big),

where

E_{1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n}\frac{\ell(n)}{n^{c}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad E_{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n}\frac{\ell(n)}{n^{c}}\frac{T}{|T-2\pi n\xi/{\mathfrak{q}}|+T^{1/2}}.

Clearly,

(5.6)

E_{1}=\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(c)^{2}}{\zeta(c)}\ll\frac{1}{(c-1)^{3}}=(\log T)^{3}.

Also, setting
T_{\circ}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=T/(2\pi\xi)
, we have

(5.7)

E_{2}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,T^{3/2}\sum_{n}\frac{\ell(n)}{n^{c}}\frac{1}{|n-T_{\circ}|+T^{1/2}}.

Modifying slightly the proof of
[
3
, Thm. 3.1]
, we find that
the sum in (
5.7
) is
\ll T^{-1}(\log T)^{3}
. Therefore,
using (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) along with (
5.4
),
we see that

I_{1}=\Sigma_{2}(T)+O_{q,\xi}(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}).

Next, by (
5.3
) and Lemma
2.1
, we have

D(\sigma+it,\chi)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q}\,\tau^{\varepsilon}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-\sigma-it)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{{\mathfrak{q}}}\,\tau^{\sigma-1/2}\qquad(\sigma\in[b,c],~t\geqslant 1),

from which we derive that

I_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits_{c+iT}^{b+iT}D(s,\chi)\,\xi^{-s}\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-s)\,ds\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\xi}\,T^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

Similarly, we have

I_{3}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\xi}\,T^{\varepsilon}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad I_{4}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\xi}\,1.

Combining (
5.5
) with the above estimates for the integrals
I_{j}
,
we obtain (
5.1
), finishing the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\chi
be a character mod
q
induced from a primitive character
{\mathfrak{X}}

mod
{\mathfrak{q}}
satisfying
{\tt LH}[{\mathfrak{X}}]
. For any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and

X\geqslant 10
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
\beta\geqslant\frac{1}{2},\,\gamma>0\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-\rho)\mathcal{B}\Big(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi\xi X}\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{\tau(\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}})}{{\mathfrak{q}}}\sum_{n}\ell(n)\chi(n){\tt e}(-n\xi/{\mathfrak{q}})\mathcal{B}(n/({\mathfrak{q}}X))\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\xi,\mathcal{B}}\,X^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
5.1
, we define for
u>0
:

\Sigma_{1}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
\beta\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\\
0<\gamma\leqslant u\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-\rho),\qquad\Sigma_{2}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{\tau(\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}})}{{\mathfrak{q}}}\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant{\mathfrak{q}}u/(2\pi\xi)\end{subarray}}\hskip-5.0pt\ell(n)\chi(n){\tt e}(-n\xi).

By Theorem
5.1

with
T\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=2\pi\xi Xu
, we have

(5.8)

\Sigma_{1}(2\pi\xi Xu)-\Sigma_{2}(2\pi\xi Xu)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\xi}\,(Xu)^{1/2+\varepsilon}+1\qquad(u>0).

Next, we denote

\displaystyle\Sigma_{3}(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Sigma_{2}(2\pi\xi u/{\mathfrak{q}})=\frac{\tau(\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}})}{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant u\end{subarray}}\ell(n)\chi(n){\tt e}(-n\xi),

\displaystyle\Sigma_{4}(X)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
\beta\geqslant\frac{1}{2},\,\gamma>0\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-\rho)\mathcal{B}\Big(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi\xi X}\Big),

\displaystyle\Sigma_{5}(X)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{\tau(\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}})}{{\mathfrak{q}}}\sum_{n}\ell(n)\chi(n){\tt e}(-n\xi/{\mathfrak{q}})\mathcal{B}(n/({\mathfrak{q}}X)).

Using Riemann-Stieltjes integration, we have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{4}(X)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}\Big(\frac{u}{2\pi\xi X}\Big)\,d\Sigma_{1}(u)=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u)\,d\Sigma_{1}(2\pi\xi Xu)

\displaystyle=-\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u)\Sigma_{1}(2\pi\xi Xu)\,du,

and

\displaystyle\Sigma_{5}(X)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u/({\mathfrak{q}}X))\,d\Sigma_{3}(u)=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u)\,d\Sigma_{3}({\mathfrak{q}}Xu)

\displaystyle=-\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u)\Sigma_{3}({\mathfrak{q}}Xu)\,du=-\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u)\Sigma_{2}(2\pi\xi Xu)\,du.

Hence, using (
5.8
), we get that

\Sigma_{4}(X)-\Sigma_{5}(X)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\xi}\,\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\big|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u)\big|\big((Xu)^{1/2+\varepsilon}+1\big)\,du\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\mathcal{B}}\,X^{1/2+\varepsilon},

which finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\boldsymbol{\star}]
and
{\tt LH}[\boldsymbol{\star}]
.
Let
{\mathfrak{X}}
be a primitive character mod
{\mathfrak{q}}
. Let
\xi=h/k

be a rational number with
h,k>0
and
(h,k)=1
. Put
q\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}={\mathfrak{q}}k
,
and let
\chi\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}={\mathfrak{X}}\cdot\chi_{0,q}
be the character mod
q

induced from
{\mathfrak{X}}
. For all
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and

(6.1)

\frac{\tau(\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}})}{{\mathfrak{q}}}\sum_{n}\ell(n)\chi(n){\tt e}(-n\xi/{\mathfrak{q}})\mathcal{B}(n/({\mathfrak{q}}X))=C_{{\mathfrak{X}},\xi}\cdot F_{{\mathfrak{X}},\xi}(X)+O_{q,\xi,\mathcal{B}}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}),

where

,
\chi
is the character mod

{\mathfrak{X}}
,

).

Proof.

The character
\chi
is supported on integers

) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\bmod q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}{\tt e}(-ah/q){\mathfrak{X}}(a)\sum_{n\equiv a\bmod q}\ell(n)\mathcal{B}(n/({\mathfrak{q}}X))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\bmod q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}{\tt e}(-ah/q){\mathfrak{X}}(a)\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\bmod q}\overline{\chi^{\prime}}(a)\sum_{n}\ell(n)\chi^{\prime}(n)\mathcal{B}(n/({\mathfrak{q}}X)),

where the middle sum runs over all characters
\chi^{\prime}
mod
q
.
By Theorem
4.1

(iii)

the total contribution from all nonprincipal characters
\chi^{\prime}

is
O_{\chi,\xi,\mathcal{B}}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon})

For the principal character

(6.2)

Using the well known relation

7.
Proof of Theorem
2.3

Throughout the proof,
{\tt LH}[\boldsymbol{\star}]
is assumed to hold.
Once and for all, let
{\mathfrak{X}}
be a fixed primitive character mod
{\mathfrak{q}}
.

In one direction, suppose that
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[\boldsymbol{\star}]
is true. In particular,
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}[{\mathfrak{X}}]
holds,
and the first condition of
{\tt RH}_{\tt sim}^{\dagger}[{\mathfrak{X}}]
is verified. Let

\xi\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=h/k
with
h,k>0
and
(h,k)=1
,

\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})

.
As

5.2
gives

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma\\
\gamma>0\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}L^{\prime}(\rho,\chi)\mathcal{M}_{\overline{{\mathfrak{X}}}}(1-\rho)\mathcal{B}\Big(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi\xi X}\Big)\\

Using estimate (

In the other direction, suppose that

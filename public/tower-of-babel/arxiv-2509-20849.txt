Classification of Lipschitz derivatives in terms of semicontinuity and the Baire limit functions Note: The research was supported by the University of Silesia in Katowice, Mathematics Department (Iterative Functional Equations and Real Analysis program) Journal: Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications
Oleksandr V. Maslyuchenko, Ziemowit M. Wójcicki, Oleksandr V. Maslyuchenko, Ziemowit M. Wójcicki

Abstract
We introduce the generalized notion of semicontinuity of a function defined on a topological space and derive the useful classification of the so-called Lipschitz derivatives of functions defined on a metric space. Secondly, we investigate some connections of the Lipschitz derivatives defined on normed spaces to the Fréchet derivative and relations between little, big and local Lipschitz derivatives (denoted by lip ⁡ f \operatorname{lip}f , Lip ⁡ f \operatorname{Lip}f and 𝕃 ​ ip ⁡ f \operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f respectively) in terms of Baire limit functions. In particular, we prove that lip ⁡ f \operatorname{lip}f is ℱ σ \mathcal{F}_{\sigma} -lower, Lip ⁡ f \operatorname{Lip}f is ℱ σ \mathcal{F}_{\sigma} -upper, 𝕃 ​ ip ⁡ f \operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f is upper semicontinuous. Moreover, for a function f f defined on an open or convex subset of a normed space, the upper Baire limit function of functions lip ⁡ f \operatorname{lip}f and Lip ⁡ f \operatorname{Lip}f are equal to 𝕃 ​ ip ⁡ f \operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f .

Introduction

The Lipschitz derivatives are useful tools for investigation of different notions of differentiability.
For example, the big Lipschitz derivative
\operatorname{Lip}f
of a given function

f
often occurs in theorems of Rademacher–Stepanov type (see for example
[
15
,
18
,
14
,
8
]
).
Statements of this type usually involves the set
L(f)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\colon\operatorname{Lip}f(x)<\infty\right\}
.
The local Lipschitz derivative
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f
together with
\operatorname{Lip}f
characterize the local and pointwise Lipschitzness
of functions defined on a metric space
[
10
]
.
The little Lipschitz derivative was introduced by Cheeger in
[
6
]
and together with

\operatorname{Lip}f
play important role in the research of the first order differential calculus in metric spaces.
The crucial fact is the purely metric character of the definitions of Lipschitz derivatives.
The above considerations lead to the natural question of characterizing the sets

\ell(f)
,
L(f)
and
\mathbb{L}(f)
for functions defined on metric spaces.
In recent years, this problem has been investigated in many articles, such as
[
16
,
5
,
9
]

In particular, in
[
5
]
it was shown that
\ell(f)
is a
G_{\delta\sigma}
-set
and
L(f)
is an
F_{\sigma}
-set for a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.
In our approach, we introduce generalized notions of semicontinuity of a function
and classify the Lipschitz derivatives with help of these properties. This allows
us to easily derive the Borel type of sets
\ell(f)
,
L(f)
,
\mathbb{L}(f)
of
a function
f
acting between arbitrary metric spaces.

Another interesting question to consider is whether for a given
triplet of functions
(u,v,w)
, there exists a continuous
function
f
such that
\operatorname{lip}f=u
,
\operatorname{Lip}f=v
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f=w
.
Some advances in this direction were made in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
.
Our characterization of Lipschitz derivatives in terms of generalized
semicontinuity constrains the possible choice of functions
(u,v,w)
.
Moreover, we obtain another necessary criterion for a triple
(u,v,w)

defined on locally convex subset of a normed space:
the upper Baire limits functions of
u
and
v
are equal to
w
.

2
Lipschitz derivatives

Let
X
be a metric space,
a\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
. We always denote the metric on
X
by
|\,\cdot\,-\,\cdot\,|_{X}
and

\displaystyle B(a,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=B_{X}(a,\varepsilon)=\big\{x\in X\colon|x-a|_{X}<\varepsilon\big\},

\displaystyle B[a,\varepsilon]

\displaystyle=B_{X}[a,\varepsilon]=\big\{x\in X\colon|x-a|_{X}\leq\varepsilon\big\}.

Definition 1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces,
f\colon X\to Y
be a function,
x\in X
. Denote

•

\|f\|_{\operatorname{lip}}=\sup\limits_{u\neq v\in X}\frac{1}{|u-v|_{X}}\big|f(u)-f(v)\big|_{Y}
,

•

\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x)=\limsup\limits_{(u,v)\to(x,x)}\frac{1}{|u-v|_{X}}\big|f(u)-f(v)\big|_{Y}
,

•

\operatorname{Lip}f(x)=\limsup\limits_{u\to x}\frac{1}{|u-x|_{X}}\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|_{Y}
,

•

\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\liminf\limits_{r\to 0^{+}}\sup\limits_{u\in B(x,r)}\frac{1}{r}\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|_{Y}
;

The number
\|f\|_{\operatorname{lip}}
is
Lipschitz constant of
f
. The functions
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f
,
\operatorname{Lip}f
and
\operatorname{lip}f
are called the
local, big and little Lipschitz derivative
respectively.

We denote by
X^{d}
the set of all non-isolated points of
X
.
Throughout the paper, we assume that
\sup\varnothing=0
.
As a consequence of this assumption we have

\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x)=\operatorname{Lip}f(x)=\operatorname{lip}f(x)=0
for any
x\in X\setminus X^{d}
.

Obviously, if
Y
is a normed space then

\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{lip}}
is an extended seminorm
on
Y^{X}
in the sense
[
17
]
. Moreover,

\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{lip}}
is a norm on the space
\operatorname{Lip}_{a}(X,Y)
of all
Lipschitz functions
f\colon{X\to Y}
vanishing at some fixed point
a\in X
.

We introduce some auxiliary notations:

•

\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}^{r}f(x)=\big\|f|_{B(x,r)}\big\|_{\operatorname{lip}}=\sup\limits_{u\neq v\in B(x,r)}\frac{1}{|u-v|_{X}}\big|f(u)-f(v)\big|_{Y}

•

\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)=\sup\limits_{u\in B(x,r)}\frac{1}{r}\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|_{Y},\operatorname{Lip}^{r}_{+}f(x)=\sup\limits_{u\in B[x,r]}\tfrac{1}{r}\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|_{Y}

•

\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x)=\sup\limits_{0<\varrho<r}\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho}f(x),\ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x)=\inf\limits_{0<\varrho<r}\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho}f(x)
;

Therefore, the definitions of the Lipschitz derivatives might be rewritten as follows.

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x)

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{r>0}\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}^{r}f(x)

(1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}f(x)

\displaystyle=\liminf\limits_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)

(2)

Some authors (see, for example,
[
9
,
4
,
5
]
)
define
\operatorname{Lip}f
and
\operatorname{lip}f
using the function

\operatorname{Lip}^{r}_{+}f
instead of
\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f
.
In the case where
X
is a normed space, we have
B[x,r]=\overline{B(x,r)}
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)=\operatorname{Lip}^{r}_{+}f(x)
for any continuous function
f
.
But the previous equality does not hold for the discrete metric on
X
,
nonconstant
f
and
r=1
. However, we have the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces and
f\colon X\to Y
be a function.
Then, for any non-isolated point
x\in X
, the following equalities hold

\operatorname{Lip}f(x)=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}_{+}^{r}f(x).

Proof.

Denote

\displaystyle\alpha(r)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\rho<r}\operatorname{Lip}^{\rho}f(x),

\displaystyle\beta(r)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\rho<r}\operatorname{Lip}_{+}^{\rho}f(x),

\displaystyle\gamma(r)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\frac{|f(u)-f(x)|_{Y}}{|u-x|_{X}}\colon u\in X,\;0<|u-x|_{X}<r\right\}.

Since
B(x,r)\subseteq B[x,r]
, we have
\alpha(r)\leq\beta(r)
. Next, we have

\displaystyle\beta(r)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\rho<r}\sup_{0<|u-x|_{X}\leq\rho}\tfrac{1}{\rho}|f(u)-f(x)|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0<\rho<r}\sup_{0<|u-x|_{X}\leq\rho}\tfrac{1}{|u-x|_{X}}|f(u)-f(x)|_{Y}=\gamma(r).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\gamma(r)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\rho<r}\sup_{|u-x|_{X}=\rho}\tfrac{1}{\rho}|f(u)-f(x)|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0<\rho<r}\sup_{0<|u-x|_{X}\leq\rho}\tfrac{1}{\rho}|f(u)-f(x)|_{Y}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\rho<r}\operatorname{Lip}_{+}^{\rho}f(x)=\beta(r).

We have shown, that
\alpha(r)\leq\beta(r)=\gamma(r)
for
r>0
.
It remains to show that
\alpha(r)=\beta(r)
,
r>0
.
Let us assume that there exists
r_{0}>0
, such that
\alpha(r_{0})<\beta(r_{0})
.
Denote
\varphi(r)=\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)
and
\psi(r)=\operatorname{Lip}_{+}^{r}f(x)
. Observe, that

\rho\varphi(\rho)\leq\rho\psi(\rho)\leq r\varphi(r),\text{ for }0<\rho<r.

(3)

Since
\beta(r_{0})=\sup\limits_{r<r_{0}}\psi(r)>\alpha(r_{0})
,
there exists
r_{1}<r_{0}
such that
\alpha(r_{0})<\psi(r_{1})
.
Let
\varepsilon=\psi(r_{1})-\alpha(r_{0})>0
. Then, for any
r<r_{0}
,

\varphi(r)+\varepsilon\leq\alpha(r_{0})+\varepsilon=\psi(r_{1})
, so

r_{1}\varphi(r)+r_{1}\varepsilon\leq r_{1}\psi(r_{1})\leq r\varphi(r),\text{ for }r_{1}<r<r_{0},

(4)

where the second inequality follows from (
3
).
Note that

\varphi(r)=\tfrac{1}{r}r\varphi(r)\leq\tfrac{1}{r_{1}}r_{0}\varphi(r_{0}),\text{ for }r_{1}<r<r_{0},

so, the function
\varphi
is bounded on the interval
(r_{1};r_{0})
.
However, by (
4
), we have

0=\lim_{r\to r_{1}^{+}}(r-r_{1})\varphi(r)\geq r_{1}\varepsilon>0,

which is impossible. We have
\alpha(r)=\beta(r)=\gamma(r)
for
r>0
.
But

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f(x)=\inf_{r>0}\alpha(r),

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}_{+}^{r}f(x)=\inf_{r>0}\beta(r)

and

\operatorname{Lip}f(x)=\inf\limits_{r>0}\gamma(r)

and the proof is finished.
∎

Note, that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}_{r^{\prime}}f(x)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x)\geq\operatorname{lip}_{r^{\prime}}f(x)\text{ if }0<r<r^{\prime},

(5)

So, the definitions and the previous proposition yield

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}f(x)

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{r>0}\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x)=\lim\limits_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x),

(6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{r>0}\operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x)=\lim\limits_{r\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x).

(7)

Therefore, it is easy to see that the following inequalities hold.

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x)\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}^{r}f(x)\text{ for any }r>0,

(8)

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}f(x)\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x).

(9)

Definition 2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces and
\gamma\geq 0
. A function
{f\colon X\to Y}
is called

•

\gamma
-Lipschitz
if
\|f\|_{\operatorname{lip}}\leq\gamma
;

•

Lipschitz
if
\|f\|_{\operatorname{lip}}<\infty
;

•

locally Lipschitz
if
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f<\infty
.

•

pointwise Lipschitz
if
\operatorname{Lip}f<\infty
;

•

weakly pointwise Lipschitz
if
\operatorname{lip}f<\infty
.

Inequalities (
9
) yield the next assertion.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces, and
f\colon X\to Y
be a function. Then
\mathbb{L}(f)\subseteq L(f)\subseteq\ell(f)
and
\ell^{\infty}(f)\subseteq L^{\infty}(f)\subseteq\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(f)
.

3
Connections of Lipschitz derivatives to classical notion of a derivative

One might ask under what conditions the Lipschitz derivative (of any given type)
coincides with one of the “traditional” notions of the derivative
of a given function, provided that an appropriate derivative exists.
It is obvious that for a real differentiable function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
we have
\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\operatorname{Lip}f(x)=\left|f(x)\right|
at any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

In
[
10
]
, the following theorem was proved.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be normed spaces,
G
be an open subset of
X
,
and
f\colon G\to Y
have a locally bounded Gateaux derivative
f^{\prime}
.
Then,
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x)=\limsup\limits_{u\to x}\left\|f^{\prime}(x)\right\|
for
x\in X

and so,
f
is locally Lipschitz. Moreover, if
f
is
C^{1}
function,
then
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x)=\left\|f^{\prime}(x)\right\|
,
x\in X
.

The following result was also stated in
[
10
]
, but the proof
contains a small blunder. Here, we provide the correct proof.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
, where
X
and
Y
are normed space
and assume that there exists the Fréchet derivative

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}f\left(x_{0}\right)
of
f
at point
x_{0}\in X
.
Then,
\operatorname{Lip}f(x_{0})=\left\|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}f\left(x_{0}\right)\right\|
.

Proof.

It is enough to consider the case
X\neq\{0\}
.
Denote by
A=\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{0}\left(f\right)
the Fréchet derivative
of
f
at a point
x_{0}
. We have

f(x)-f(x_{0})=A(x-x_{0})+\alpha(x),\text{ for }x\in X,

(10)

where
\alpha
is a function, such that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to x_{0}}\frac{\alpha(x)}{\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|}=0
.
By (
10
) we have

\left\|f(x)-f(x_{0})\right\|\leq\left\|A\right\|\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|+\left\|\alpha(x)\right\|,

hence

\frac{\left\|f(x)-f(x_{0})\right\|}{\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|}\leq\left\|A\right\|+\left\|\frac{\alpha(x)}{\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|}\right\|.

Thus,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}f(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\limsup_{x\to x_{0}}\frac{\left\|f(x)-f(x_{0})\right\|}{\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{x\to x_{0}}\left(\left\|A\right\|+\left\|\frac{\alpha(x)}{\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|}\right\|\right)=\left\|A\right\|.

We want to prove the reverse inequality.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there exists
e\in X
with
\left\|e\right\|=1
and
such that

\left\|Ae\right\|\geq\left\|A\right\|-\varepsilon.

For any
t>0
, denote
x_{t}=x_{0}+te
.
Note that
t=\left\|x_{t}-x_{0}\right\|
, and
x_{t}\to x_{0}
when
t\to 0
.
Therefore, by (
10
)

f(x_{t})-f(x_{0})=A(x_{t}-x_{0})+\alpha(x_{t})=tAe+\alpha(x_{t}),

so

\frac{f(x_{t})-f(x_{0})}{t}=Ae+\frac{\alpha(x_{t})}{t},

and

\frac{f(x_{t})-f(x_{0})}{\left\|x_{t}-x_{0}\right\|}=Ae+\frac{\alpha(x_{t})}{\left\|x_{t}-x_{0}\right\|}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|f(x_{t})-f(x_{0})\right\|}{\left\|x_{t}-x_{0}\right\|}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|Ae\right\|-\left\|\frac{\alpha(x_{t})}{\left\|x_{t}-x_{0}\right\|}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\left\|A\right\|-\varepsilon-\left\|\frac{\alpha(x_{t})}{\left\|x_{t}-x_{0}\right\|}\right\|\xrightarrow{t\to 0}\left\|A\right\|-\varepsilon.

Thus,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}f(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\limsup_{x\to x_{0}}\frac{\left\|f(x)-f(x_{0})\right\|}{\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|}=\inf_{r>0}\sup_{\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|<r}\frac{\left\|f(x)-f(x_{0})\right\|}{\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{r>0}\sup_{\left\|x_{t}-x_{0}\right\|<r}\frac{\left\|f(x_{t})-f(x_{0})\right\|}{\left\|x_{t}-x_{0}\right\|}\geq\left\|A\right\|-\varepsilon-\lim_{t\to 0}\left\|\frac{\alpha(x_{t})}{\left\|x_{t}-x_{0}\right\|}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|A\right\|-\varepsilon.

Since the
\varepsilon
was chosen arbitrarily, the proof is finished.
∎

4
Semicontinuity with respect to a family of sets

In this section we introduce some modification of semicontinuity,
which will help us to classify the Lipschitz derivatives.

Let
X
be a topological space and
\mathcal{A}
be a family of subset of
X
. We denote

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{c}&=\Big\{X\setminus A\colon A\in\mathcal{A}\Big\},\\
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}&=\Big\{\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\colon A_{n}\in\mathcal{A}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\},\\
\mathcal{A}_{\delta}&=\Big\{\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\colon A_{n}\in\mathcal{A}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\},\end{split}

We will also combine this symbols.
It is easy to check, for example, that
\mathcal{A}_{c\delta c}=\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
,
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma c}=\mathcal{A}_{c\delta}
,
\mathcal{A}_{\delta c}=\mathcal{A}_{c\sigma}
and so on.
If
\mathcal{T}
denotes the topology of
X
,
then applying above notation to the family
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{T}
,
the
\mathcal{T}_{\delta}
is the familiar Borel class
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}

of
G_{\delta}
-subsets of
X
. Complementary, the family
\mathcal{T}_{c\sigma}

is the Borel class
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
of
F_{\sigma}
-subsets of
X
.

We say that
f\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is an
\mathcal{A}
-upper
(
\mathcal{A}
-lower) semicontinuous function
if

f^{-1}\big([-\infty;\gamma)\big)\in\mathcal{A}

(resp.
f^{-1}\big((\gamma,+\infty]\big)\in\mathcal{A}
) for any

\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. If
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{T}
is the topology of
X
,
then we omit the symbol
\mathcal{A}
in the previous definitions.
For our purposes, the
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
-upper and lower
semicontinuous functions are particularly important.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space,
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{X}
and

f\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an
\mathcal{A}
-upper
semicontinuous function. Then

(i)

f^{-1}\big[[\gamma,+\infty]\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{c}
for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
;

(ii)

f^{-1}\big[[-\infty,+\infty)\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}

and, so,
f^{-1}\big[\{+\infty\}\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{\sigma c}
;

(iii)

f^{-1}\big[\{-\infty\}\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{\delta}
and, so,
f^{-1}\big((-\infty,+\infty]\big)\in\mathcal{A}_{\delta c}
;

(iv)

f
is
\mathcal{A}_{c\sigma}
-lower semicontinuous

Proof.

(i)
For any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
we have that
f^{-1}\big([-\infty,\gamma)\big)\in\mathcal{A}
and then

f^{-1}\big([\gamma,+\infty]\big)=X\setminus f^{-1}\big([-\infty,\gamma)\big)\in\mathcal{A}_{c}.

(ii)
Since
f^{-1}\big[[-\infty,n)\big]\in\mathcal{A}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
we conclude that

f^{-1}\big[\left[-\infty,+\infty\right)\big]=\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}f^{-1}\big[\left[-\infty,n\right)\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{\sigma},

and so,

f^{-1}\big[\{+\infty\}\big]=X\setminus f^{-1}\big[\left[-\infty,+\infty\right)\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{\sigma c}
.

(iii)
Since
f^{-1}\big[\left[-\infty,-n\right)\big]\in\mathcal{A}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

f^{-1}\big[\{-\infty\}\big]=\bigcap\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}f^{-1}\big[\left[-\infty,-n\right)\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{\delta},

and so,

f^{-1}\big[\left(-\infty,+\infty\right]\big]=X\setminus f^{-1}\big[\{-\infty\}\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{\delta c}

(iv)
Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma_{n}\downarrow\gamma
.
Since
f^{-1}\big[[\gamma_{n};+\infty]\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{c}
by
(i)
,
we conclude that

f^{-1}\big[(\gamma;+\infty]\big]=\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}f^{-1}\big[[\gamma_{n};+\infty]\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{c\sigma}

i.e.
f
is
\mathcal{A}_{c\sigma}
-lower semicontinuous.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
X
be a topological space,
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{X}
,

f_{n}\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an
\mathcal{A}
-upper
semicontinuous function for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

f\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a function such that

f(x)=\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}(x)
for any
x\in X
.
Then
f
is an
\mathcal{A}_{c\sigma}
-lower semicontinuous function.

Proof.

Consider
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.
By Proposition
4.1
(iv)
the functions
f_{n}
are

\mathcal{A}_{c\sigma}
-lower semicontinuous.
So,
f_{n}^{-1}\big[\left(\gamma,+\infty\right]\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{c\sigma}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Consequently,

f^{-1}\big[\left(\gamma,+\infty\right]\big]=\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}^{-1}\big[\left(\gamma,+\infty\right]\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{c\sigma}.

Thus,
f
is an
\mathcal{A}_{c\sigma}
-lower semicontinuous function.
∎

Observe that
f
is an
\mathcal{A}
-upper semicontinuous function
if and only if
-f
is
\mathcal{A}
-lower semicontinuous. Therefore,
using Proposition
4.1
and
4.2

with
g=-f
we obtain the following two propositions.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
X
be a topological space,
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{X}
and
f\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be an
\mathcal{A}
-lower semicontinuous function. Then

(i)

f^{-1}\big[\left[-\infty,\gamma\right]\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{c}
for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
;

(ii)

f^{-1}\big[\left(-\infty,+\infty\right]\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}

and, so,
f^{-1}\big[\{-\infty\}\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{\sigma c}
;

(iii)

f^{-1}\big[\{+\infty\}\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{\delta}
and, so,

f^{-1}\big[\left[-\infty,+\infty\right)\big]\in\mathcal{A}_{\delta c}
;

(iv)

f
is
\mathcal{A}_{c\sigma}
-upper semicontinuous.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
X
be a topological space,

\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{X}
,
f_{n}\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be
an
\mathcal{A}
-lower semicontinuous function for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

and
f\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a function such that

f(x)=\inf\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}(x)
for any

.
Then

is an
\mathcal{A}_{c\sigma}
-upper semicontinuous function.

5
Classification of the Lipschitz derivatives

Now we pass to the investigation of the type of semicontinuity of Lipschitz derivatives of continuous functions.
In
[
5
]
semicontinuity of Lipschitz derivatives of a continuous function

f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
was obtained from the continuity of
\operatorname{Lip}^{r}f
.
But in the general situation, this function need not be continuous.
Therefore, we prove semicontinuity of Lipschitz derivatives directly by the definitions.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces,
f\colon X\to Y
be a continuous function and
r>0
.
Then
\operatorname{lip}_{r}f\colon X\to\left[0,+\infty\right]
is an upper semicontinuous function.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in X
and
\gamma>\operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x_{0})
. Then

\inf_{\varrho<r}\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho}f(x_{0})=\operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x_{0})<\gamma.

So, there is positive
\varrho<r
such that
\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho}f(x_{0})<\gamma
. Pick
\gamma_{1}
such that
\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho}f(x_{0})<\gamma_{1}<\gamma
. Then we choose
\varrho_{1}
such that
\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma}\varrho<\varrho_{1}<\varrho
. So,
\gamma\varrho_{1}>\gamma_{1}\varrho
.
Therefore,

\sup\limits_{u\in B(x_{0},\varrho)}\big|f(u)-f(x_{0})\big|_{Y}=\varrho\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho}f(x_{0})<\gamma_{1}\varrho.

Then

\big|f(u)-f(x_{0})\big|_{Y}<\gamma_{1}\varrho\ \ \ \text{ for any }\ \ \ u\in B(x_{0},\varrho).

By the continuity of
f
at
x_{0}
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\varrho_{1}+\delta<\varrho
and

\big|f(x)-f(x_{0})\big|_{X}<\gamma\varrho_{1}-\gamma_{1}\varrho\ \ \ \text{ for any }\ \ \ x\in U=B(x_{0},\delta).

Consider
x\in U
and
u\in B(x,\varrho_{1})
.
Then

|u-x_{0}|_{X}\leq|u-x|_{X}+|x-x_{0}|_{X}<\varrho_{1}+\delta<\varrho,

and so,
u\in B(x_{0},\varrho)
. Therefore,

\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|_{Y}\leq\big|f(u)-f(x_{0})\big|_{Y}+\big|f(x_{0})-f(x)\big|_{Y}<\gamma_{1}\varrho+(\gamma\varrho_{1}-\gamma_{1}\varrho)=\gamma\varrho_{1}.

Thus,
\frac{1}{\varrho_{1}}\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|_{Y}\leq\gamma
for any
u\in B(x,\varrho_{1})
.
Hence,
\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho_{1}}f(x)\leq\gamma
. But
0<\varrho_{1}<r
. Therefore,
\operatorname{lip}_{r}f(x)\leq\gamma
for any
x\in U
. Thus,
\operatorname{lip}_{r}f
is upper semicontinuous at
x_{0}
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric space,
f\colon X\to Y
be a continuous function.

\operatorname{lip}f\colon X\to\left[0,+\infty\right]
is a
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
-lower semicontinuous function.

Proof.

By (
5
) and (
7
) we conclude that

\operatorname{lip}f(x)=\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{lip}_{\frac{1}{n}}f(x)
for any

.
By Lemma

, the functions
\operatorname{lip}_{\frac{1}{n}}f
are

\mathcal{T}
-upper semicontinuous, where
\mathcal{T}
is the topology of
X
.
Therefore, by Proposition
4.2

\operatorname{lip}f
is

\mathcal{T}_{c\sigma}
-lower semicontinuous.
This means that
\operatorname{lip}f
is
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
-lower semicontinuous.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces,
f\colon X\to Y
be a continuous function and
r>0
.
Then
\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f\colon X\to\left[0,+\infty\right]
is a lower semicontinuous function.

Proof.

Fix
r>0
. Let
x_{0}\in X
and
\gamma<\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x_{0})
. Then

\sup\limits_{\varrho<r}\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho}f(x_{0})=\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x_{0})>\gamma.

So, there is
\varrho\in(0;r)
such that
\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho}f(x_{0})>\gamma
.
Pick
\gamma_{1}
such that
{\gamma<\gamma_{1}<\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho}f(x_{0})}
.
Therefore,

\sup\limits_{u\in B(x_{0},\varrho)}\big|f(u)-f(x_{0})\big|_{Y}=\varrho\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho}f(x_{0})>\gamma_{1}\varrho.

Thus, there is
u\in B(x_{0},\varrho)
with

\big|f(u)-f(x_{0})\big|_{Y}>\gamma_{1}\varrho.

Then we choose
\varrho_{1}
such that
\varrho<\varrho_{1}<\min\big\{r,\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma}\varrho\big\}
. Consequently,

\gamma\varrho_{1}<\gamma_{1}\varrho
.
By the continuity of
f
at
x_{0}
there exists
\delta>0
such
that
\varrho+\delta<\varrho_{1}
and

\big|f(x)-f(x_{0})\big|_{Y}<\gamma_{1}\varrho-\gamma\varrho_{1}\ \ \ \text{ for any }\ \ \ x\in U:=B(x_{0},\delta).

Consider
x\in U
. Then

|u-x|_{X}\leq|u-x_{0}|_{X}+|x_{0}-x|_{X}<\varrho+\delta<\varrho_{1},

and, so,

. Consequently,

\big|f(u)-f(x)\big|_{Y}\geq\big|f(u)-f(x_{0})\big|_{Y}-\big|f(x)-f(x_{0})\big|_{Y}>\gamma_{1}\varrho-(\gamma_{1}\varrho-\gamma\varrho_{1})=\gamma\varrho_{1}.

Hence,
\operatorname{Lip}^{\varrho_{1}}f(x)>\gamma
. But
0<\varrho_{1}<r
. Therefore,
\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f(x)>\gamma
for any
x\in U
. Thus,
\operatorname{Lip}_{r}f
is lower semicontinuous at
x_{0}
.
∎

.

6
Characterization of Lipschitz functions on a convex subset of a normed space

The following lemma was applied by Buczolich, Hanson, Maga and Vertesy
in certain investigations of Lipschitz derivatives of the real functions
of real variable.

Lemma 6.1

(
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

If
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\operatorname{lip}f\leq\chi_{E}
then

\left|f(a)-f(b)\right|\leq\mu(\left[a,b\right]\cap E)
for every

a,b\in\mathbb{R}
(where
a<b
) so
f
is Lipschitz and hence
absolutely continuous.

In the above,
\mu
denotes the Lebesgue measure.
We will state the following

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
f\colon\left[0,1\right]\to\mathbb{R}

be a function such that
\operatorname{lip}f(x)\leq\gamma
for any

x\in\left[0,1\right]
. Then
f
is
\gamma
-Lipschitz.

Proof.

Extend
f
to
\tilde{f}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\tilde{f}(x)=f(0)
if
x<0
and
(x)=f(1)
if
x>1
.
Let
g=\frac{1}{\gamma}\tilde{f}
and
E=\left[0,1\right]
. Then by
Lemma
6.1
we conclude that

\frac{1}{\gamma}\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|=\left|g(x)-g(y)\right|\leq\mu\big(\left[x,y\right]\cap E\big)=\left|x-y\right|

for any
x,y\in\left[0,1\right]
.
∎

Next result will allow us to apply
Lemma
6.1
and Corollary
6.2

for functions defined on normed spaces.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
A
be a convex subset of the normed space
X
,

f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be a function, and
a,b\in A
.
Moreover, let
T\colon\left[0,1\right]\to A
be an
affine function given by

T(u)=a+t(b-a)
for
0\leq u\leq 1

and
g=f\circ T\colon\left[0,1\right]\to\mathbb{R}
.
Then,

\operatorname{lip}g\leq\left\|b-a\right\|\big((\operatorname{lip}f)\circ T\big).

(11)

As
\big((\operatorname{lip}f)\circ T\big)\left\|b-a\right\|=\big((\operatorname{lip}f)\circ T)\cdot\left\|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}\right\|
,
the right side of the inequality
(
11
)
is reminiscent of the “chain rule”
for the usual derivative.
Nevertheless, the inequality can be strict.
To see that, take a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

defined as
f(x,y)=y
,
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}
and consider
the usual distance on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof of the Lemma
6.3
.

Fix
u_{0}\in\left[0,1\right]
and
observe, that

\left\|T(u)-T(u_{0})\right\|=\left\|(u-u_{0})(b-a)\right\|=\left|u-u_{0}\right|\left\|b-a\right\|,\;0\leq u\leq 1.

(12)

Put
x_{0}=T(u_{0})
.
We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}g(u_{0})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\sup_{\left|u-u_{0}\right|<r}\frac{\left|g(u)-g(u_{0})\right|}{r}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\sup_{0<\left|u-u_{0}\right|<r}\frac{\left|f(T(u))-f(T(u_{0}))\right|}{r}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\sup_{0<\left|u-u_{0}\right|<r}\frac{\left\|T(u)-T(u_{0})\right\|}{r}\cdot\frac{\left|f(T(u))-f(T(u_{0}))\right|}{\left\|T(u)-T(u_{0})\right\|}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\sup_{0<\left|u-u_{0}\right|<r}\frac{\left\|b-a\right\|\left|u-u_{0}\right|}{r}\frac{\left|f(T(u))-f(T(u_{0}))\right|}{\left\|T(u)-T(u_{0})\right\|},

(13)

where the last equality follows from (
12
).
Note, that

\sup_{\left|u-u_{0}\right|<r}\frac{\left\|b-a\right\|\left|u-u_{0}\right|}{r}=\left\|b-a\right\|.

(14)

Thus, by (
13
) and (
14
)

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}g(u_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\sup_{0<\left|t-u_{0}\right|<r}\frac{\left\|b-a\right\|\left|t-u_{0}\right|}{r}\sup_{0<\left|u-u_{0}\right|<r}\frac{\left|f(T(u))-f(T(u_{0}))\right|}{\left\|T(u)-T(u_{0})\right\|}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|b-a\right\|\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\sup_{0<\left|u-u_{0}\right|<r}\frac{\left|f(T(u))-f(T(u_{0}))\right|}{\left\|T(u)-T(u_{0})\right\|}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|b-a\right\|\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\sup_{\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|<r}\frac{\left|f(x)-f(x_{0})\right|}{\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|}=\left\|b-a\right\|\operatorname{lip}f(x_{0}).\qed

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
D
be a convex subset of a normed space
X
,

Y
be a metric space,
f\colon D\to Y
be a function and
\gamma\geq 0
.
Then
f
is
\gamma
-Lipschitz if and only if
\operatorname{lip}f(x)\leq\gamma
for any
x\in D
.

Proof.

Fix
a,b\in D
. Define

T\colon\left[0,1\right]\to D
as

T(u)=a+u(b-a)\;\text{ for }u\in\left[0,1\right]

and put
g=f\circ T\colon\left[0,1\right]\to\mathbb{R}
.
Applying Lemma
6.3

we get

\operatorname{lip}g(u)\leq\left\|b-a\right\|\operatorname{lip}f\big(T(u)\big)\leq\left\|b-a\right\|\gamma.

for any
u\in\left[0,1\right]
. Therefore, Corollary
6.2
implies that

g
is Lipschitz with the constant
\gamma_{1}=\left\|b-a\right\|\gamma
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\left|f(a)-f(b)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|g(0)-g(1)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{1}\left|0-1\right|

\displaystyle=\gamma\left\|a-b\right\|.

So,
f
is
\gamma
-Lipschitz on
D
.
∎

By
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}
we denote standard norm on
space of bounded functions
B(D,Y)
acting
from a set
D
into a normed space
Y
, i.e. for any
h\colon D\to Y

we have

\left\|h\right\|_{\infty}=\sup\limits_{x\in D}\left\|h(x)\right\|
.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
f\colon D\to Y
be a continuous function, where

D
is a convex subset of some normed space
X
and
Y
be also a
normed space. Then,

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{lip}}=\left\|\operatorname{lip}f\right\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

We simply check, that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{lip}}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\gamma>0\colon f\text{ is }\gamma\text{-Lipschitz}\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\gamma>0\colon\operatorname{lip}f(x)\leq\gamma,\,x\in D\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\operatorname{lip}f(x)\colon x\in D\right\}=\sup_{x\in D}\left|\operatorname{lip}f(x)\right|=\left\|\operatorname{lip}f\right\|_{\infty},

where the second equality follows from Theorem
6.4

∎

Therefore,

\operatorname{lip}
is an isometric injection
of the normed space
\operatorname{Lip}_{a}(D,Y)

with some
a\in X
,
into the space
B(D,\mathbb{R})
.

7
Baire limit functions of Lipschitz derivatives

For a given function
f\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, defined on a metric
space
X
, their
upper Baire function

f^{\vee}

is defined by

f^{\vee}(x)=\inf_{U\in\mathcal{U}\left(x\right)}\sup_{u\in U}f(u),\;x\in X,

and their
lower Baire function

f^{\wedge}
is defined by

f^{\wedge}(x)=\sup_{U\in\mathcal{U}\left(x\right)}\inf_{u\in U}f(u),\;x\in X,

where
\mathcal{U}\left(x\right)
is the family of all the neighborhoods of
x
in
X
.
(See, for example,
[
13
]
.)
The upper Baire function
f^{\vee}
is upper semicontinuous and
the lower Baire function
f^{\wedge}
is lower semicontinuous.

A subset
D
of a normed space
X
is called
locally convex
if for any point
x\in D
and
their neighborhood
U
of
x
in
D
there is a convex neighborhood
V
of
x
in
D
such that

V\subseteq U
. For example, every convex set and every open set in
X
is locally convex.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
D
be a locally convex subset of a normed space
X

and let
{f\colon D\to\mathbb{R}}
be a function. Then

(\operatorname{lip}f)^{\vee}=(\operatorname{Lip}f)^{\vee}=\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{lip}f\leq\operatorname{Lip}f\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f
is upper semicontinuous by Theorem
5.5
, we have

(\operatorname{lip}f)^{\vee}\leq(\operatorname{Lip}f)^{\vee}\leq(\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f)^{\vee}=\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f.

Therefore, it is enough to prove that
\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f\leq(\operatorname{lip}f)^{\vee}
.
Fix
x_{0}\in D
. The case where
(\operatorname{lip}f)^{\vee}(x_{0})=\infty
is obvious. So, we suppose that
(\operatorname{lip}f)^{\vee}(x_{0})<\infty
. Let
\gamma>(\operatorname{lip}f)^{\vee}(x_{0})
.
Then,
there exists a convex neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
, such that

\operatorname{lip}f(x)<\gamma\,\text{ for any }x\in U.

By Theorem
6.4
, the function
f
is
\gamma
-Lipschitz on
U
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{L}ip}f(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\inf_{r>0}\left\|f\big|_{B\left(x_{0},r\right)}\right\|_{\mathrm{lip}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\big|_{U}\right\|_{\mathrm{lip}}\leq\gamma,

where
B\left(x_{0},r\right)
means the ball in the metric subspace
D
.
Passing to the limit with

\gamma\to(\operatorname{lip}f)^{\vee}(x_{0})

we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

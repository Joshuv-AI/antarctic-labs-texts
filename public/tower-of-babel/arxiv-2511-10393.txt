Backbone three-point correlation function in the two-dimensional Potts model
Ming Li, Youjin Deng, Jesper Lykke Jacobsen, Jesús Salas

Abstract
We study the three-point correlation function of the backbone in the two-dimensional Q Q -state Potts model using the Fortuin-Kasteleyn (FK) representation. The backbone is defined as the bi-connected skeleton of an FK cluster after removing all dangling ends and bridges. To circumvent the severe critical slowing down in direct Potts simulations for large Q Q , we employ large-scale Monte Carlo simulations of the O ( n ) (n) loop model on the hexagonal lattice, which is regarded to correspond to the Potts model with Q = n 2 Q=n^{2} . Using a highly efficient cluster algorithm, we compute the universal three-point amplitude ratios for the backbone ( R BB R_{\text{BB}} ) and FK clusters ( R FK R_{\text{FK}} ). Our computed R FK R_{\text{FK}} exhibits excellent agreement with exact conformal field theory predictions, validating the reliability of our numerical approach. In the critical regime, we find that R BB R_{\text{BB}} is systematically larger than R FK R_{\text{FK}} . Conversely, along the tricritical branch, R BB R_{\text{BB}} and R FK R_{\text{FK}} coincide within numerical accuracy, strongly suggesting that R BB = R FK R_{\text{BB}}=R_{\text{FK}} holds throughout this regime. This finding mirrors the known equality of the backbone and FK cluster fractal dimensions at tricriticality, jointly indicating that both structures share the same geometric universality.

Introduction

The
Q
-state Potts model is one of the most extensively studied models in statistical physics, providing a unifying framework for understanding both continuous and discontinuous phase transitions
Potts 1952
;
Wu 1982
;
Wu 1983
;
Wu 1984
;
Baxter 1985
. In this model, spins interact via a ferromagnetic nearest-neighbor coupling, and the Hamiltonian (reduced by the Boltzmann constant
k_{\rm B}
and temperature
T
) is given by

\mathcal{H}/k_{\rm B}T=-K\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\delta_{\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j}}\,,

(1)

where the spin variable
\sigma_{i}
takes one of
Q
possible states, and
\delta_{a,b}
is the Kronecker delta function.

Through the Fortuin-Kasteleyn (FK) representation
Kasteleyn and Fortuin 1969
;
Fortuin and Kasteleyn 1972
, the partition function of Potts model can be expressed in terms of random clusters as

\mathcal{Z}_{\text{FK}}=\sum_{\{\mathcal{E}\}}v^{b(\mathcal{E})}Q^{c(\mathcal{E})}\,,\qquad v=e^{K}-1\,,

(2)

where the sum runs over all possible subsets
\mathcal{E}
of occupied bonds,
b(\mathcal{E})
is the number of occupied bonds, and
c(\mathcal{E})
is the number of connected components (FK clusters). This formulation provides a geometrical interpretation of the Potts model in terms of clusters (for
Q,v>0
), allowing its critical properties to be studied in close analogy with percolation theory
Stauffer and Aharony 1994
;
Grimmett 1999
;
Bollobás and Riordan 2006
. Indeed, in the limit
Q\to 1
, the partition function (
2
) reduces to that of standard bond percolation. More generally, for arbitrary values of
Q
and
v
, Eq. (
2
) defines the random-cluster model
Grimmett 2006
. The FK representation has an important role in conformal field theory (CFT)
Di Francesco et al. 1997
and in stochastic Loewner evolution
Kagery and Nienhuis 2004
;
Cardy 2005
, leading to major theoretical advances for the Potts model.

In the two-dimensional (2D) Potts model, the Coulomb-gas (CG) theory
Nienhuis 1982
;
Nienhuis 1984
;
Nienhuis 1987
provides a unified theoretical framework. Within this theory, the critical exponents for different
Q
can be consistently expressed as functions of the CG coupling
g
, which is related to
Q
by

\sqrt{Q}=-2\cos(\pi g)\,.

(3)

The range
Q\in[0,4]
corresponds to
g\in[1/2,1]
.
In this framework, the thermal and magnetic renormalization exponents for the leading fields are given by

\displaystyle y_{t1}

\displaystyle=3-\frac{3}{2g}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle y_{h1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2g+3)(2g+1)}{8g}\,,

(5)

where the leading thermal exponent
y_{t1}
is the inverse of the correlation-length exponent,
y_{t1}=1/\nu
, and the leading magnetic exponent
y_{h1}
corresponds to the fractal dimension
d_{f}
of the FK clusters,
y_{h1}=d_{f}
.
The corresponding exponents for the subleading fields are given by

\displaystyle y_{t2}

\displaystyle=4-\frac{4}{g}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle y_{h2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2g+5)(2g-1)}{8g}\,.

(7)

These subleading fields govern the convergence of corrections to scaling.

An important extension of the Potts model is the

Nienhuis et al. 1980

In the limit

as

(K_{c},D_{c})

(K_{t},D_{t})

4

7

\mathcal{Z}_{\text{loop}}=\sum_{\{\mathcal{C}\}}x^{\mathcal{L(C)}}n^{\mathcal{N(C)}}\,,

(10)

\mathcal{L(C)}

The RG analysis of this model shows that the RG trajectories correspond to constant values of

. Therefore, for a fixed value of

, the interval

The O
(n)
loop model is believed to be equivalent to the
Q
-state Potts model with
Q=n^{2}
, sharing the same CG coupling
g
defined in Eq. (
3
) and thus belonging to the same universality class. This conjecture is supported by the fact that both models have the same central charge in the CG theory and share many critical exponents. However, subtle differences exist. For instance, the leading thermal eigenvalue
y_{t1}
is not identical to the leading thermal eigenvalue of the Potts model, but rather to its subleading one
y_{t2}

Nienhuis 1982
. The proposed equivalence specifically implies that the FK clusters of the critical and tricritical Potts models have the same critical behavior as the domains enclosed by the loops in the O
(n)
model. This hypothesis has been supported by both analytical
Nienhuis 1987
and numerical
Fang et al. 2022
;
Xu et al. 2025a
;
Xu et al. 2025b
studies. In particular, Ref.
Fang et al. 2022
provided numerical evidence that the backbone and shortest-path exponents coincide in both models.

A central aspect in the study of these models is the analysis of their geometric structures and the statistical correlations they induce, which reveal the underlying critical behavior. A fundamental example is the two-point connectivity, measuring the probability that two distant sites, located at positions
\bm{x}_{1}
and
\bm{x}_{2}
, belong to the same FK cluster. At criticality, it decays algebraically with distance as

P_{2}^{\text{FK}}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})\sim\frac{A_{\text{FK}}}{|\bm{x}_{1}-\bm{x}_{2}|^{2\Delta_{\text{FK}}}}\,,

(12)

where
\Delta_{\text{FK}}
is the scaling dimension associated with the FK clusters, and
A_{\rm FK}
is the corresponding amplitude. Within the CG theory
Nienhuis 1982
;
Nienhuis 1984
;
Nienhuis 1987
,
\Delta_{\text{FK}}
can be exactly determined in 2D and is related to the corresponding fractal dimension via
d_{f}=y_{h1}=2-\Delta_{\text{FK}}
, where
y_{h1}
is given by Eq. (
5
).

Beyond the FK clusters themselves, the geometric representation also enables one to investigate more intricate substructures within clusters, such as the so-called
backbone
, which captures the bi-connected skeleton after removing all dangling ends and bridges
Fang et al. 2022
;
Deng et al. 2004
. Analogous to the FK clusters, the two-point connectivity function of the backbone at large distances behaves as

P_{2}^{\text{BB}}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})\;\sim\;\frac{A_{\text{BB}}}{|\bm{x}_{1}-\bm{x}_{2}|^{2\Delta_{\text{BB}}}}\,,

(13)

where
\Delta_{\text{BB}}
is the scaling dimension associated with the backbone, and
A_{\text{BB}}
is the corresponding amplitude. The exactly value of
\Delta_{\text{BB}}
was recently determined for the
Q
-state Potts model using the conformal loop ensemble
Nolin et al. 2023
;
Nolin et al. 2025
. Unlike previously known exactly solved critical exponents, the backbone exponent is a transcendental number rather than a rational number.
For 2D percolation, it is
\Delta_{\text{BB}}=0.3566668\ldots
.
This value is well consistent with the previously reported Monte Carlo estimates of
d_{\rm B}=1.64339(5)

Fang et al. 2022
via the relation
\Delta_{\text{BB}}=2-d_{\rm B}
.

A natural extension is to consider the corresponding three-point functions,
P_{3}^{\text{FK}}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})
and
P_{3}^{\text{BB}}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})
, which represent the probability that the three sites belong to the same FK or backbone cluster, respectively. CFT predicts that
Di Francesco et al. 1997
, when the three points are far apart, these functions decay as

\displaystyle P_{3}^{\text{FK}}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{B_{\text{FK}}}{r_{12}^{\Delta_{\text{FK}}}\,r_{13}^{\Delta_{\text{FK}}}\,r_{23}^{\Delta_{\text{FK}}}}\,,

(14)

\displaystyle P_{3}^{\text{BB}}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{B_{\text{BB}}}{r_{12}^{\Delta_{\text{BB}}}\,r_{13}^{\Delta_{\text{BB}}}\,r_{23}^{\Delta_{\text{BB}}}}

(15)

where
r_{ij}=|\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{x}_{j}|
, and
B_{\text{FK}}
(
B_{\text{BB}}
) is the amplitude for FK (backbone) clusters.

In CFT, the two- and three-point correlation functions (
12
)–(
15
) are often expressed as expectation values of certain fields:
P_{2}^{j}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})=\langle\Phi_{j}(\bm{x}_{1})\Phi_{j}(\bm{x}_{2})\rangle
and
P_{3}^{j}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})=\langle\Phi_{j}(\bm{x}_{1})\Phi_{j}(\bm{x}_{2})\Phi_{j}(\bm{x}_{3})\rangle
, with
j\in\{\text{FK},\text{BB}\}
. By choosing a suitable normalization of the field
\Phi_{j}
, the constants in the two-point functions can be set to
A_{j}=1
, and the constants
B_{j}
in the three-point functions then define the so-called three-point structure constants
R_{\text{FK}}
and
R_{\text{BB}}

On the lattice, the normalization of the fields used in CFT is not directly possible. To extract the universal three-point structure constants
R_{j}
, one considers the ratio

R_{j}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})\;=\;\frac{P_{3}^{j}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})}{\sqrt{P^{j}_{2}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})P^{j}_{2}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{3})P^{j}_{2}(\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})}}\,,

(16)

where the points
\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3}
are chosen to be mutually far apart. This ratio eliminates nonuniversal metric factors, so that
R_{j}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})
converges to the universal constant
R_{j}
in the thermodynamic limit. For finite lattices, however, some residual dependence on the specific choice of points is expected.

The universal three-point amplitude ratio

for the FK clusters in the 2D

In this work, we perform large-scale Monte Carlo simulations to determine the universal three-point amplitude ratio

II
Monte Carlo simulations

In this section, we describe the Monte Carlo simulations used to generate FK and backbone clusters in both the critical and tricritical Potts models. To this end, we employ the O
(n)
loop model on the hexagonal lattice, which is equivalent to a generalized Ising model on its dual triangular lattice. Section
II.1
outlines the simulation algorithm, while Sec.
II.2
details the measurement of the two- and three-point correlation functions.

II.1
Monte Carlo algorithm

As explained in the Introduction, we adopt the O
(n)
loop model on the hexagonal lattice rather than directly simulating the corresponding
Q
-state Potts models at their critical or tricritical points. The loops on the hexagonal lattice correspond to the boundaries of Ising domains on the dual triangular lattice, establishing a one-to-two correspondence between loop configurations and dual Ising-spin configurations. The dual coupling strength satisfies
2K^{*}=-\ln(x)
, being ferromagnetic for
x<1
and antiferromagnetic for
x>1
, while
n
serves as the fugacity associated with each Ising domain. The partition function of this generalized Ising model on the triangular lattice can be written as

\mathcal{Z}_{\rm GIsing}=\sum_{\{\sigma\}}n^{N_{d}}\prod_{\langle i,j\rangle}e^{K^{*}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}},

(17)

where
N_{d}
is the number of dual Ising domains.

Following the algorithm in Ref.
Fang et al. 2022
, the update procedure of this generalized Ising model on the triangular lattice proceeds iteratively as follows:

1.

For each Ising domain, all sites are independently set to be “active” with probability
1/n
, and “inactive” otherwise. We restrict to
n\in[1,2]
.

2.

Bonds between neighboring sites are then occupied according to:

(a)

If both sites are active and have the same spin value, occupy the bond with probability
p=1-x_{\pm}
, where
x_{\pm}
is given by Eq. (
11
).

(b)

If both sites are active but have opposite spins, leave the bond empty.

(c)

If at least one of the adjacent sites is inactive, occupy the bond with probability one.

3.

New Ising domains are obtained by flipping the spins of all sites in each connected component of occupied bonds, with probability
1/2
. Note that sites within a connected component may carry different spin values [because of point 2(c) above].

The resulting Ising domains correspond to FK clusters of the Potts model, with
x_{-}
and
x_{+}
representing the critical and tricritical regimes, respectively. The associated backbone structure is extracted by scanning each FK cluster using a depth-first search. The two- and three-point correlation functions are then sampled as described in Sec.
II.2
.

It is worth noting that the FK clusters generated by this algorithm correspond to site configurations (Ising domains), whereas those obtained by the Swendsen-Wang cluster algorithm
Swendsen and Wang 1987
correspond to bond configurations. A special case occurs at
n=\sqrt{Q}=1
, where the Swendsen-Wang cluster algorithm reduces to standard bond percolation, while our algorithm yields site percolation. Although both types of clusters share identical large-scale geometric properties at criticality, they differ in how correlation functions are computed. For bond percolation, the correlation function is obtained by scanning all clusters, whereas for site percolation, only clusters formed by occupied sites are considered.

Because of spin symmetry in this generalized Ising model, FK clusters corresponding to the two spin values exhibit identical statistics. However, similar to site percolation, when computing correlation functions for
Q>1
, only clusters of one spin species are considered. Including both spin species would make the ratios
R_{j}
defined in Eq. (
16
) smaller by a factor of
1/\sqrt{2}
than those defined for standard (bond) FK clusters, due to double counting. This subtlety was also discussed in the Supplementary Material of Ref.
Ikhlef et al. 2016
. In our simulations, observables are measured separately for both spin species, and the results are averaged.

The equivalence between the O
(n)
loop model on the hexagonal lattice and the generalized Ising model on its dual triangular lattice holds exactly for planar lattices. In practice, however, we simulate finite systems with periodic boundary conditions, which break planarity. Such boundary effects are expected to have negligible influence on bulk critical behavior, and indeed no observable deviation has been reported in previous studies
Fang et al. 2022
;
Xu et al. 2025a
;
Xu et al. 2025b
.

We performed simulations of the O
(n=\sqrt{Q})
loop model for
Q=1,1.5,2,2.5,3,3.5,4
, on both branches
x_{-}
and
x_{+}
. For each
n
, we used triangular lattices with linear sizes
L=32,64,128,256,512,1024,2048,4096,8192
. The number of statistically independent samples ranged from
10^{8}
for the smallest systems down to
10^{3}
for the largest ones.

II.2
Observables

As discussed in the Introduction, the primary quantity of interest is the ratio
R_{j}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})
defined in Eq. (
16
). To compute this ratio efficiently, we select the triplet
(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})
such that they form an equilateral triangle with side length
r\equiv|\bm{x}_{1}-\bm{x}_{2}|=|\bm{x}_{1}-\bm{x}_{3}|=|\bm{x}_{2}-\bm{x}_{3}|
(recall that the model is defined on a triangular lattice; see Sec.
II.1
). For each site
\bm{x}_{1}
, several pairs
(\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})
satisfy this condition. In our simulations, we select one equilateral triangle such that
\bm{x}_{2}
shares the same horizontal coordinate as
\bm{x}_{1}
, while
\bm{x}_{3}
lies above them with a larger vertical coordinate.

Traversing
\bm{x}_{1}
over all lattice sites in a given configuration, we record the number of occurrences
N^{j}_{12}
that sites
\bm{x}_{1}
and
\bm{x}_{2}
belong to the same cluster of type
j
(the other pairs are treated analogously). When checking whether two sites are connected, the connectivity of the third site is not considered. Similarly,
N^{j}_{123}
counts the occurrences where all three sites belong to the same cluster.

For
M
independent realizations, the two-point (e.g.,
\bm{x}_{1}
and
\bm{x}_{2}
) and three-point correlation functions are estimated as

\displaystyle P^{j}_{2}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{MV}\sum_{i=1}^{M}N^{j}_{1,2}(i)\,,

(18)

\displaystyle P^{j}_{3}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{MV}\sum_{i=1}^{M}N^{j}_{1,2,3}(i)\,,

(19)

where
V
is the total number of lattice sites, and the sum runs over all realizations. Finally, substituting these correlation functions into Eq. (
16
), the ratio
R_{j}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{3})
can be obtained as a function of
r
.

(
=1.5,2.5,3.5

For equilateral triangles of side length
r
, we denote the three-point ratios as
R_{j}(r)
with
j\in\{\text{FK},\text{BB}\}
. The raw data show a weak, yet noticeable, dependence on the triangle size
r
(see Figs.
4
and
5
). For the
x_{-}
branch,
R_{\rm FK}(r)
exhibits an inflection point and
R_{\rm BB}(r)
a local maximum near
r\simeq\sqrt{L/2}
, while for the
x_{+}
branch a minimum occurs for both quantities at the same value. The case
Q=4
is less clear, especially for
R_{\text{BB}}
, indicating slower convergence.

To extract the values in the thermodynamic limit, we focus on
r=\sqrt{L/2}
and fit the corresponding data to the ansatz

R_{j}(\sqrt{L/2})=R_{j}+L^{-y}\left(a_{0}+a_{1}L^{-y^{\prime}}\right)\,,

(20)

where
R_{j}
is the value in the infinite-volume limit and
y,y^{\prime}>0
are correction-to-scaling exponents. For system sizes where
r=\sqrt{L/2}
is not integer,
R_{j}(r)
is estimated via linear interpolation between the two nearest integer
r
values. To check the robustness of this procedure, we tried a polynomial interpolation based on the four nearest integer values; the differences were negligible.

It is worth noting that fitting all five free parameters in the ansatz (
20
) (i.e.,
R_{j}
,
y
,
y^{\prime}
,
a_{0}
, and
a_{1}
) simultaneously often leads to unstable results. In practice, we therefore fixed the correction-to-scaling exponent
y^{\prime}
to a reasonable value (e.g.,
y^{\prime}=0.25,0.5,1,2
) and performed the fit for the remaining four parameters
\{R_{j},y,a_{0},a_{1}\}
.

To assess possible corrections to scaling not captured by the ansatz (
20
), we repeated the fits using only data with
L\geq L_{\text{min}}
and examined how the estimated parameters vary with
L_{\text{min}}
. In general, our preferred fit corresponds to the smallest
L_{\text{min}}
for which the goodness of fit is reasonable, and for which further increases of
L_{\text{min}}
do not reduce
\chi^{2}
by significantly more than one unit per degree of freedom. If increasing
L_{\text{min}}
produces a monotonic trend, we consider the fit has not converged, even if the
\chi^{2}
reduction is moderate. For each fit, we report
\chi^{2}
and the number of degrees of freedom (DF). Since
y^{\prime}
is fixed and additional correction terms may be present, the statistical error from a single fit may be underestimated. Therefore, the final error quoted in Table
1
also accounts for the observed variation of
R_{j}
when
L_{\text{min}}
and/or
y^{\prime}
are varied, providing a conservative (though subjective) estimate of uncertainty.

R_{\text{FK}}=1.1891(2)
. In this case, the statistical uncertainty is comparable to the variability of the estimates when
L_{\text{min}}
and/or
y^{\prime}
are varied.

By contrast,
R_{\text{BB}}(\sqrt{L/2})
converges much more slowly, as seen in panel (d1) of Fig.
7
. To address this, we modified our usual fitting strategy. We first applied the ansatz (
20
) with
a_{1}=0
. The corresponding results are listed in the upper part of Table
7
. As
L_{\text{min}}
increases, both
R_{\text{BB}}
and
y
decrease. Our preferred fit corresponds to
L_{\text{min}}=512
, yielding
R_{\text{BB}}=1.210(9)
,
y=0.17(5)
,
\chi^{2}=0.08
, and
\text{DF}=2
. These error bars should be interpreted cautiously, as
R_{\text{BB}}(\sqrt{L/2})
may still be far from its asymptotic regime even for the largest sizes considered (
R_{\text{BB}}=1.2351(3)
for
L=4096
and
R_{\text{BB}}=1.2322(4)
for
L=8182
).

We next fixed
y^{\prime}=1
, finding a similar but slightly weaker decreasing trend for both
R_{\text{BB}}
and
y
, with differences within the error bars. The preferred fit in this case corresponds to
L_{\text{min}}=64
, as shown in Table

. From these two fits, we estimate
R_{\text{BB}}\approx 1.21(1)
and
y=0.18(5)
.

Given the slow convergence of the data in Fig.
7
(d), it is natural to ask whether smaller values of the correction-to-scaling exponent

could yield better fits. To explore this, we performed additional fits with fixed

and free

L_{\text{min}}=32
were satisfactory. For larger

,

a_{1}=0.08(1)

In Ref.
Fang et al. 2022
, a logarithmic ansatz to fit the backbone exponent
d_{\rm B}
is proposed, which slightly improved their results. We repeated the analysis of

using the form

R_{\text{BB}}(\sqrt{L/2})=R_{\text{BB}}+a_{0}\left(\ln L+c\right)^{y}\,,

(21)

and also tested more complex versions of this ansatz. However, adding the leading correction-to-scaling term

In conclusion, our numerical results do not allow us to determine unambiguously whether logarithmic corrections to scaling exist at
Q=4
. Larger system sizes would be required to resolve this question, which is beyond our current computational capacity.

IV
Discussion

We performed extensive Monte Carlo simulations of the O(
n
) loop model to measure the universal three-point structure constants
R_{\text{FK}}
(FK cluster) and
R_{\text{BB}}
(backbone) for both the critical and tricritical
Q
-state Potts models with
Q=1,1.5,2,2.5,3,3.5
, and
4
. The results for
R_{\text{FK}}
(see Table
1
and Fig.
1
) show excellent agreement with the exact theoretical predictions (see Appendix
A
for a brief introduction for the computational process used in Refs.
Delfino and Viti 2011
;
Picco et al. 2013
), confirming the reliability of our simulations and finite-size scaling analysis over a wide range of
Q
and Coulomb-gas coupling
g
.

In the critical regime, we find that
R_{\text{BB}}
is systematically larger than
R_{\text{FK}}
, indicating that the backbone is geometrically denser and exhibits stronger three-point connectivity correlations than the FK clusters. A strikingly different behavior is observed in the tricritical regime: as the data shown in Table
1
,
R_{\text{BB}}
and
R_{\text{FK}}
become indistinguishable within numerical uncertainty. This leads to the conjecture

R_{\text{BB}}(g)\;=\;R_{\text{FK}}(g)\,.

(22)

We have tested this conjecture by directly comparing our estimates of
R_{\text{BB}}
with the exact
R_{\text{FK}}
values for some typical values of
g
from
1
to
4/3
(excluding
g=1
due to its larger statistical uncertainty). The agreement is again excellent, with
\chi^{2}=4.41
for
\text{DF}=6
. These observations suggest that Eq. (
22
) likely holds throughout the entire tricritical branch
g\in[1,3/2]
.

This coincidence between the FK and backbone structure constants is not entirely unexpected. Previous work
Deng et al. 2004
;
Fang et al. 2022
reported that, along the tricritical line, the backbone fractal dimension
d_{\rm B}
coincides with that of the FK cluster
d_{f}
, indicating that both structures share the same universality class at tricriticality. Our results extend this correspondence to three-point correlations, suggesting that the geometric and connectivity aspects of FK and backbone clusters become indistinguishable at the tricritical point. A full theoretical understanding of why such an equivalence arises, and how it connects to the underlying CFT, remains to be elucidated.

We conclude with some remarks about the perspectives of finding an analytical expression for
R_{\rm BB}(g)
in the critical regime.
The Coulomb gas approach has been very powerful in the 1980s for obtaining the critical exponents of loop models
Di Francesco et al. 1987

(see also
Jacobsen and Saleur 2019
). However, the three-point structure constants of operators that modify the weights of loops
Ikhlef et al. 2016
—and
of which
R_{\rm FK}(g)
is a special case
Delfino and Viti 2011
;
Picco et al. 2013
—challenge this framework, since they do not respect the charge conservation
(up to discrete shifts by screening charges) that is built into the Coulomb gas. Such structure constants require the technology of imaginary Liouville
field theory
Zamolodchikov 2005
(or the conformal loop ensemble
Ang et al. 2024
in probability theory).
But also imaginary Liouville field theory is challenged
by other three-point structure constants of operators that insert open loop segments, since their expression
Jacobsen et al. 2025
,
found from conformal bootstrap considerations, is more general than the DOZZ formula (see Appendix
A
). Therefore it is presently unclear by which means
a formula for
R_{\rm BB}(g)
could possibly be derived, in particular since the backbone
exponent has features that are foreign to all the above-mentioned field-theoretical methods.
In particular, the operator inserting a backbone does not have fixed
Kac indices as
g
is varied (see Appendix
A
).

Appendix A
The three-point structure constant

The three-point structure constant associated with the FK clusters in 2D, denoted as
R_{\rm FK}
, has been derived analytically within the framework of CFT in Refs.
Delfino and Viti 2011
;
Picco et al. 2013
. In the following, we first review the basic CFT concepts and notations associated with the Potts model, and subsequently present how to calculate
R_{\rm FK}
in the manner of Refs.
Delfino and Viti 2011
;
Picco et al. 2013
.

A.1
The critical Potts model

In 2D, the fixed point of the critical
Q
-state Potts model is described by a CFT with central charge

c(g)\;=\;1-6\frac{(1-g)^{2}}{g}\,,

(23)

where
g
is the coupling constant, and relates to
Q
via Eq. (
3
) within the range
g\in[1/2,1]
. For convenience, one may parametrize
g
as

g\;=\;\frac{p}{p+1},

(24)

so that Eq. (
23
) becomes

c(p)\;=\;1-\frac{6}{p(p+1)}\,.

(25)

The conformal weights of primary fields are then given by the Kac formula

\Delta_{m,n}(p)\;=\;\frac{[(p+1)m-pn]^{2}-1}{4p(p+1)}\,,

(26)

which agrees with the convention of Ref.
Picco et al. 2013
, but differs by a factor of
2
from Refs.
Delfino and Viti 2011
;
Delfino et al. 2013

which use the notation
\Delta_{m,n}(p)
for the full scaling dimension (the sum of holomorphic and antiholomorphic conformal weights).

The scaling dimension associated with the FK cluster spin operator
\Phi_{\frac{1}{2},0}
is

\Delta_{\text{FK}}\;=\;2\Delta_{\frac{1}{2},0}\;=\;\frac{(p+3)(p-1)}{8p(p+1)}\,.

(27)

Rewriting Eq. (
27
) in terms of
g
yields

\Delta_{\text{FK}}(g)\;=\;1-\frac{3}{8g}-\frac{g}{2}\,.

(28)

This result is consistent with
y_{h1}
given by Eq. (
5
) via the relation
\Delta_{\text{FK}}(g)=2-y_{h1}(g)
.
In the same way that
y_{h1}(g)
is related to the Kac weight
\Delta_{\frac{1}{2},0}
, it is found that
y_{h2}(g)
is related to

\Delta_{\frac{3}{2},0}
, and
y_{t1}(g)
to
\Delta_{2,1}
, and
y_{t2}(g)
to
\Delta_{3,1}
. More generally, the full set of order-parameter
fields have weights
\Delta_{\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{N},0}
, and the full set of thermal fields have weights
\Delta_{2+\mathbb{N},1}

Di Francesco et al. 1987
;
Jacobsen and Saleur 2019
(here
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of
nonnegative

integers).

In CFT, each spin operator
\Phi_{m,n}
is also associated with a charge
\alpha_{m,n}
, related to its scaling dimension
\Delta_{m,n}
through

\Delta_{m,n}\;=\;\alpha_{m,n}(\alpha_{m,n}-2\alpha_{0})\,,

(29)

where
2\alpha_{0}
denotes the background charge,

2\alpha_{0}(g)\;=\;\sqrt{g}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\,.

(30)

Accordingly, the charge
\alpha_{\text{FK}}
associated with the operator
\Phi_{\frac{1}{2},0}
is obtained as the positive root of

\alpha(\alpha-2\alpha_{0})=\Delta_{\frac{1}{2},0}
, namely

\alpha_{\text{FK}}\;=\;\frac{\sqrt{g}}{2}-\frac{1}{4\sqrt{g}}\,.

(31)

A.2
The tricritical Potts model

The tricritical
Q
-state Potts model also admits a CG with coupling constant
g
related to
Q
by Eq. (
3
), but within the range
g\in[1,3/2]
. In this sense, the tricritical model can be viewed as the analytic continuation of the critical Potts model, and vice versa.

Consequently, all eigenvalue expressions for the tricritical Potts model can be obtained as analytic continuations in
g
of those for the critical case. Specifically, the associated charge
\alpha_{\text{FK}}
can likewise be determined from Eq. (
31
). However, the identification of operators differs from that of the critical Potts model.

First, the parametrization of the coupling strength
g
in terms of
p
is no longer given by Eq. (
24
); instead, it becomes

g\;=\;\frac{p+1}{p}\,.

(32)

It is worth noting that, under this parametrization, the central charge
c(p)
remains expressed by Eq. (
25
).

In the tricritical Potts model, the role of the spin operator corresponding to FK cluster is now played by
\Phi_{0,\frac{1}{2}}
(rather than
\Phi_{\frac{1}{2},0}
in the critical case), leading to

\Delta_{\text{FK}}\;=\;2\Delta_{0,\frac{1}{2}}\;=\;\frac{(p-2)(p+2)}{8p(1+p)}\,.

(33)

By expressing this in terms of
g
through Eq. (
32
), one recovers the same form as Eq. (
28
) for the critical case. This consistency confirms that the tricritical and critical Potts models are indeed related by analytic continuation in
g
.

A.3
Numerical evaluation of
R_{\rm FK}

As derived analytically in Refs.
Delfino and Viti 2011
;
Picco et al. 2013
, the three-point structure constant associated with FK clusters can be expressed as

R_{\rm FK}(g)\;=\;C_{\rm FK}\,C(g,\alpha_{\rm FK})\,,

(34)

where
C_{\rm FK}=\sqrt{2}
is a
g
-independent prefactor, and
\alpha_{\rm FK}
is the charge corresponding to the FK spin operator introduced above.

The function
C(g,\alpha)
appearing in Eq. (
34
) is given by the so-called imaginary DOZZ formula

C(g,\alpha)\;=\;\frac{W(g,\alpha)}{W(g,0)}\,,

(35)

where
Zamolodchikov 2005

W(g,\alpha)\;=\;\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{[\Upsilon_{\beta}(\beta-\alpha)]^{3}\,\Upsilon_{\beta}(2\beta-\beta^{-1}-3\alpha)}{\big[\Upsilon_{\beta}(\beta-2\alpha)\,\Upsilon_{\beta}(2\beta-\beta^{-1}-2\alpha)\big]^{3/2}}\,,

(36)

with
\beta\equiv\sqrt{g}
. The normalization factor is given by

W(g,0)\;=\;\frac{[\Upsilon_{\beta}(\beta)]^{3/2}}{[\Upsilon_{\beta}(2\beta-\beta^{-1})]^{1/2}}\,.

(37)

Here
\Upsilon_{\beta}(x)
is Zamolodchikov’s special function
Zamolodchikov and Zamolodchikov 1996
, defined for
0<\Re(x)<\widetilde{Q}
by

\log\Upsilon_{\beta}(x)\;=\;\\
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\left[\frac{\big(\frac{\widetilde{Q}}{2}-x\big)^{2}}{e^{t}}-\frac{\sinh^{2}\!\left[\frac{t}{2}\!\left(\frac{\widetilde{Q}}{2}-x\right)\!\right]}{\sinh\frac{\beta t}{2}\,\sinh\frac{t}{2\beta}}\right]\,,

(38)

where
\widetilde{Q}=\beta+\beta^{-1}
, and outside the strip of convergence for the above integral by the shift equations

\frac{\Upsilon_{\beta}(x+\beta)}{\Upsilon_{\beta}(x)}\;=\;\gamma(\beta x)\,\beta^{1-2\beta x}\,.

(39)

In this equation, the function
\gamma
is defined as

\gamma(x)\;=\;\frac{\Gamma(x)}{\Gamma(x+1)}\,,

(40)

where
\Gamma(x)
is the Euler gamma function. Notice also the relation

\Upsilon_{\beta}(x)\;=\;\Gamma_{\beta}^{-1}(x)\Gamma_{\beta}^{-1}(\widetilde{Q}-x)\,,

(41)

where
\Gamma_{\beta}(x)
is the Barnes double gamma function
(
Eberhardt 2023
, Appendix B.1)
.

In summary, for a given value of
g
, the numerical evaluation of
R_{\rm FK}(g)
proceeds as follows:

1.

Determine the value of
\alpha_{\rm FK}
via Eq. (
31
);

2.

Evaluate
W(g,\alpha_{\rm FK})
using the definitions above and a numerical implementation of
\Upsilon_{\beta}(x)
;

3.

Compute the normalization factor
W(g,0)
;

4.

Finally, evaluate
R_{\rm FK}(g)=\sqrt{2}W(g,\alpha_{\rm FK})/W(g,0)
.

This procedure allows for the numerical determination of
R_{\rm FK}(g)
for any desired value of
g
within the critical or tricritical regimes. The results for several selected values of
g
are listed in Table
1
, while the continuous curve of
R_{\rm FK}(g)
for successive
g\in[0.6,1.4]
is shown as the line in Fig.
3
.

Moreover, the above procedure can also be directly applied to compute the three-point structure constant
R_{\text{spin}}
associated with the spin clusters
Cai et al. 2025
. The calculation follows exactly the same formalism and functional relations as for the FK clusters, with only a few modifications. Firstly, the dual spin operator
\Phi_{0,\frac{1}{2}}
should be used in Eqs. (
26
) and (
29
) to obtain the corresponding scaling dimension
\Delta_{\text{spin}}
and CG charge
\alpha_{\text{spin}}
. Secondly, for
R_{\text{spin}}(g)=C_{\text{spin}}(g)C(g,\alpha_{\text{spin}})
, in analogy with Eq. (
34
) for FK clusters, the prefactor
C_{\text{spin}}(g)
becomes
g
-dependent and takes the form
Cai et al. 2025

C_{\text{spin}}\;=\;\sqrt{\frac{2g}{Q}}\,\frac{\sin(2\pi g)}{\sin(\pi/g)}\,.

(42)

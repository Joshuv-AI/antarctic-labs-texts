ALMA [CI] Image of the Circumnuclear Disk of the Milky Way: Inflowing Low-density Molecular Gas Journal: ApJ
Kunihiko Tanaka, Makoto Nagai, Kazuhisa Kamegai

Abstract
We present ALMA [C I] P 1 3 {}^{3}P_{1} – P 0 3 {}^{3}P_{0} imaging of the central 6.6 × 4.2 ​ pc 2 6.6\times 4.2~\mathrm{pc}^{2} region of the Galaxy encompassing the circumnuclear disk (CND). The data reveal low-density ( n H 2 ∼ 10 3 n_{\mathrm{H_{2}}}\sim 10^{3}~ cm -3 ) molecular gas with inward motion, widespread both inside and outside the CND. The normalized [C I] to CS 7–6 intensity difference decreases inwardly from R = 4 R=4 pc to 1.7 pc and azimuthally along the CND’s rotation, likely tracing paths of low-density gas inflow. By projecting spaxels into orbital coordinates assuming a velocity field model, we identify four kinematic features: a pair of spiral outer streamers toward the CND, inner streamers extending to 0.5 pc from Sgr A ∗ , an outer disk at R ∼ 3 R\sim 3 –6 pc, and the rotating ring at R = 2 R=2 pc. P P – P P – V V correlation between the inner streamers and H42 α \alpha indicates gas supply to the mini-spiral through the western arc (WA) and northern arm (NA). The total inflowing mass is 1.5 × 10 4 ​ M ⊙ 1.5\times 10^{4}~M_{\odot} , 1.7 times the mass of the rotating ring. The identified flows can be organized into two main pathways connecting the CND exterior and interior: “WA flow” feeding the mini-spiral WA via the CND, and “NA flow” bypassing the purely rotating orbit. The inflow rate along the former is approximately constant (0.1–0.16 M ⊙ ​ yr − 1 M_{\odot}~\mathrm{yr}^{-1} ), implying a CND dwelling time comparable to its orbital period and supporting the CND’s transient nature. We also identify two [C I]-bright clumps (CBCs) lacking dense-gas counterparts near the contact point between the northern outer streamer and the CND. Apparently intact against tidal disruption despite subcritical densities, the CBCs may represent a chemically young phase shortly after formation in colliding flows.



I
Introduction

The Milky Way’s circumnuclear disk (CND) is a dense molecular ring rotating at a radius of
\sim
2 pc from the nuclear supermassive black hole
\mathrm{SgrA^{*}}
.
The CND is regarded as a Galactic analog of circumnuclear dense-gas condensations commonly found in galactic nuclei, which are considered reservoirs of material that fuel nuclear activity and circumnuclear star formation.
Although the present Galactic center does not exhibit quasar activity or ongoing massive star formation, observations suggest a past active phase of
\mathrm{SgrA^{*}}
and a nuclear starburst event that formed the central stellar cluster
(
Paumard et al. 2006
;
Zubovas et al. 2011
;
Heywood et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
.
As the closest dense-gas condensation to
\mathrm{SgrA^{*}}
, the CND is crucial for understanding the relationship between these events and the properties of the circumnuclear dense gas, providing the nearest template for quasar and nuclear starburst systems.

Although the CND appears as an isolated structure in dense-gas tracer images, recent observations increasingly suggest that it is physically connected to ionized and molecular gas both inside and outside the CND ring.
Molecular cloud features, referred to as streamers and anomalies, are distributed from just outside the CND ring to the 20-
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}
and 50-
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}
clouds
(
Coil & Ho 1999
;
Christopher et al. 2005
;
Montero-Castaño et al. 2009
;
Liu et al. 2012
;
Takekawa et al. 2017
;
Hsieh et al. 2017
;
Tsuboi et al. 2018
)
.
Their line-of-sight velocities often deviate from the purely rotational motion exhibited by the CND ring, indicating the presence of radial motion.
At the inner edge of the CND ring lies the mini-spiral, a system of dense ionized gas structures that follow high-eccentricity Keplerian orbits or spiral streams
(
Zhao et al. 2009
;
Irons et al. 2012
;
Tsuboi et al. 2017
)
.
The consistent magnetic field structure across the CND and the central cavity inside the CND
(
Hsieh et al. 2018
)
, along with the spatial and velocity continuity of molecular and hydrogen recombination lines at their interfaces
(
Lo & Claussen 1983
;
Genzel et al. 1985
;
Serabyn & Lacy 1985
;
Guesten et al. 1987
;
Christopher et al. 2005
;
Tsuboi et al. 2018
)
, suggest a physical continuity between the ionized inner surface of the CND and the mini-spiral.
Recent observations have also revealed cloudlets and filaments inside the CND
(
Yusef-Zadeh et al. 2013
;
Moser et al. 2017
;
Goicoechea et al. 2018
;
Hsieh et al. 2019
)
, which were previously thought to be dominated by ionized gas, though their kinematic relationship to the CND is not fully established.

These molecular and ionized features represent molecular inflow toward the Galactic center.
The CND and its associated streamers or anomalies, as well as the mini-spiral, are thought to be immediate products of one or multiple GMC capture events
(
Sanders 1998
;
Vollmer & Duschl 2001a
;
Vollmer & Duschl 2001b
;
Vollmer & Duschl 2002
;
Bonnell & Rice 2008
;
Wardle & Yusef-Zadeh 2008
;
Namekata et al. 2009
;
Hobbs & Nayakshin 2009
;
Alig et al. 2013
;
Mapelli & Trani 2016
;
Ballone et al. 2019
;
Tress et al. 2020
)
, which are also considered to have formed the young stellar disks within the central 0.5 pc (the ‘
in-situ
’ formation scenario;
Paumard et al. 2006
;
Bonnell & Rice 2008
;
Hobbs & Nayakshin 2009
;
Alig et al. 2013
;
Mapelli & Trani 2016
).
The approximate alignment of the CND’s orbital plane with that of the central molecular zone (CMZ)
(
Jackson et al. 1993
;
Sofue et al. 2025
)
supports the idea that the CND originated from CMZ clouds, whose infall could be facilitated by dynamical friction, SN feedback, the self-gravity of the CMZ ring, or interaction with the nested bar potential
(
Stark et al. 1991
;
Sanders 1998
;
Namekata et al. 2009
;
Tress et al. 2020
)
.
The average dense gas in the CND is tidally subcritical
(
Guesten et al. 1987
;
Requena-Torres et al. 2012
)
, and the present ring structure is considered a transient feature, although opposing arguments also exist
(
Vollmer & Duschl 2001a
;
Vollmer & Duschl 2001b
;
Blank et al. 2016
)
.
The CND transfers mass to the central cavity inside it through cloud-cloud collisions within the CND and between the CND and streamers, viscous processes, and mixing with ejecta from stellar winds and supernovae
(
Blank et al. 2016
;
Solanki et al. 2023
;
Barna et al. 2025
)
.

The CND and its associated molecular cloud features, namely, the streamers and anomalies, have been primarily observed using high-critical density (
n_{\rm crit}
) molecular lines such as
\rm NH_{3}
, HCN, CS, and high-
J
CO
(
Guesten et al. 1987
;
Jackson et al. 1993
;
Marr et al. 1993
;
Coil & Ho 1999
;
Coil & Ho 2000
;
Christopher et al. 2005
;
Herrnstein & Ho 2005
;
Montero-Castaño et al. 2009
;
Liu et al. 2012
;
Martín et al. 2012
;
Liu et al. 2013
;
Tsuboi et al. 2018
;
Hsieh et al. 2019
;
Hsieh et al. 2021
)
, which target molecular cloud components with high density and high temperature (
{T_{\rm kin}}\gtrsim 200~\mathrm{K}
,
{n_{\rm H_{2}}}\gtrsim 10^{4}~\mathrm{cm^{-3}}
;
Requena-Torres et al. 2012
).
However, these observations were not sufficiently sensitive to the low-excitation molecular gas (
{T_{\rm kin}}\sim 20\mbox{--}30~\mathrm{K}
,
{n_{\rm H_{2}}}\sim 10^{3}~\mathrm{cm^{-3}}
;

Such low-excitation gas in the central
\sim 10
pc region has been observed to extend into areas devoid of high-density tracer emissions, including the apparent gap between the 50-
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}
cloud and the CND

A cold dust component (
{{T_{\mathrm{dust}}}}\sim 23.5

{{T_{\mathrm{dust}}}}\sim~44.5

Despite its significance in mass and kinematics, the low-excitation gas remains less understood than the high-excitation component, primarily due to the difficulty of low-

Liu et al. 2012

III.2
CS 10–9

The overall CS 10–9 distribution is consistent with the CS 7–6 image, with bright emission predominantly associated with the southern rim of the CND ring.
The intensity contrast between the northern and southern portions of the ring is more pronounced in the CS 10–9 image compared to CS 7–6.
This suggests a concentration of the highest-density gas toward the southern portion of the ring, which roughly corresponds to the southern extension and southwestern (SW) lobe
(
Christopher et al. 2005
)
.
However, since this paper focuses on low-density molecular gas, we do not investigate the details of the density structure of the dense-gas component further.

IV
Discussion

IV.1
Inner Streamer and the mini-spiral

The CND and its associated features are considered the primary source of mass supply to the ionized gas comprising the mini-spiral.
Several feeding channels have been proposed based on dense-gas observations.
One such channel is the interface between the southwestern rim of the CND and the mini-spiral WA, where molecular and ionized emissions show strong
P
–
P
–
V

(
Guesten et al. 1987
;
Christopher et al. 2005
;
Tsuboi et al. 2018

.
Another is anomaly-B
(
Tsuboi et al. 2018
)
, a dense-gas filament spiraling from outside the CND to the eastern arm (EA) of the mini-spiral.
Additionally,
Tsuboi et al. 2020
detected molecular emission along the mini-spiral NA, suggesting a physical connection with molecular gas outside the central cavity.

In our data, the inflow toward the mini-spiral is traced by CND-IS, the [C I] component located inside the CND with large
V_{\mathrm{rad}}
.

IV.2
Molecular Gas Flows To and From the CND

Our ALMA data reveal a large amount of low-density (
{n_{\rm H_{2}}}\sim 10^{3}

\mathrm{cm^{-3}}
) molecular gas both inside and outside the CND ring.
Most of the low-density features, except for CND-OD, exhibit fast inward radial velocities of
\sim
40–70
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}
, forming a global inflow that connects the CND exterior to the mini-spiral. The masses of the inflowing components outside and inside the CND are
0.91\times 10^{3}

{M_{\odot}}
and
0.62\times 10^{3}

{M_{\odot}}
, respectively, each comparable to the CND-IR mass of
0.88\times 10^{3}

{M_{\odot}}
. The total inflowing mass is
1.5\times 10^{4}

{M_{\odot}}
, approximately 100 times the previous estimates based on dense-gas tracers
(
Hsieh et al. 2017
)
. Given the limited FoV of our observations, the actual inflowing mass could be even larger. In the wider-field [C I] image
(
Tanaka et al. 2021
)
, the emission within the ALMA FoV accounts for only 30 % of the total luminosity in the contamination-free
{v_{\rm LSR}}
ranges (i.e., [
-150

-40
]
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}
and [
90

150
]
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}
; see §
III.6.1
). Including this out-of-FoV [C I] emission would increase the inflowing mass by a factor of 2–3, even conservatively omitting the mass of the 0-KM and 80-KM anomalies (
0.59\times 10^{3}

{M_{\odot}}
). Hence, the overall structure of the low- and high-excitation molecular gas associated with the CND is better described as a globally infalling system toward the mini-spiral, with the CND ring comprising only a relatively small fraction in mass.

As we have seen in §
III.5
and §
III.6
,
D_{\mathrm{CI}}
(or the [C I] to CS 7–6 intensity ratio) primarily depends on the mass fraction of the low-excitation to high-excitation components, rather than on local environmental parameters such as UV, X-ray, or CR fluxes.
Assuming that the radial and azimuthal gradients of
D_{\mathrm{CI}}
arise from the supply of high-
D_{\mathrm{CI}}
gas from outer radii, we identify the major paths of molecular gas streams associated with the CND, which we schematically show in Figure
15
.

The flows represented by the CND-OS, IR, and IS can be sorted into two main inflowing paths.
The first path consists of spiral flows originating from outer radii (
R\gtrsim 5~\mathrm{pc}
) that merge with CND-IR and eventually reach the mini-spiral WA. We refer to this path, which transits the CND ring, as the “WA flow.”
CND-OS(N) and (S), CND-IS(S), and CND-IR are included in the WA flow.
As their emission extends beyond the FoV of our observations, the starting points of these outer flows are not identifiable in our data.
Previous wider-field observations suggest that the upstreams of these CND-associated streamers connect to the 50-
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}
and 20-
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}
clouds
(
Hsieh et al. 2017
;
Takekawa et al. 2017
)
.
The second path is the “NA flow,” which directly feeds the mini-spiral without merging with the flow along CND-IR.
CND-IS(N) represents this path, which originates near the location where CND-OS first contacts CND-IR and immediately flows toward the interior of CND-IR.
In addition, we may identify the third pathway, the “EA flow” connecting the Anomaly-B and the mini-spiral EA, which was discovered by
Tsuboi et al. 2018
with dense-gas observation, although we failed to clearly detect this channel in the [C I] data.

Figure
15
shows the mass inflow rate (
\dot{M}
) of the individual streams estimated in two ways.
The first method uses the mass flux in the azimuthal direction:

\displaystyle\dot{M}_{\mathrm{az}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta M}{\Delta\phi}\Omega_{2\mathrm{pc}},

(7)

where
\Omega_{2\mathrm{pc}}=2\pi\times 10^{-5}~\mathrm{rad}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}
is the circular frequency at
R=2
pc,
\Delta\phi
is the azimuthal span of the overlapping region with CND-IR, and
\Delta M
is the mass associated with the overlapping region.
Equation
7
applies to streams that have a significant circular portion overlapping CND-IR.
The second method is based on the mass flux in the radial direction:

\displaystyle\dot{M}_{\mathrm{rad}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{M}{\Delta R}\left|{V_{\mathrm{rad}}}\right|,

(8)

where
M
and
\Delta R
are the total mass and radial extent of the infalling component, respectively.
This estimate is applicable for streams with a spiral or radially linear shape, whose
\Delta R
is sufficiently larger than their intrinsic transverse widths.
We apply Equation
7
to CND-IS(S), Equation
8
to CND-IS(N), and both Equations
7
and
8
to CND-OS(N) and CND-OS(S).

\dot{M}_{\mathrm{az}}
and
\dot{M}_{\mathrm{rad}}
are approximately consistent for CND-OS, although the latter tends to give slightly higher inflow rates.

The estimated inflow rates indicate that CND-IR is rapidly gaining low-density molecular gas through the WA flow.
With a total inflow rate of
\sim 0.1~{M_{\odot}}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}
from CND-OS(N) and (S), CND-IR will accumulate molecular gas comparable to its current mass (
0.88\times 10^{4}

{M_{\odot}}
) within one orbital period at
R
=2 pc (
\sim 10^{5}

\mathrm{yr}
).
The mass loss rate through CND-IS(S) is
0.1~{M_{\odot}}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}
, which approximately balances the inflow rate.

This implies that the dwelling time of gas in CND-IR is comparable to the CND’s orbital period, supporting the CND’s transient nature.

The inflow rate of the NA flow through CND-OS(N) is estimated to be
0.08~{M_{\odot}}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}
, comparable to the inflow rates of the WA flow.
When combined with the EA flow, which was not identified with our analysis, the flows bypassing the CND form a major channel of molecular gas transport within the CND system, alongside the WA flow transiting the CND.

CND-OD does not exhibit significant inflow motion, but its high
D_{\mathrm{CI}}
value and spatial proximity to CND-OS suggest a physical association with the WA flow.
CND-OD may be interpreted as a low-density gas disk, supplied by CND-OS and settled into rotating orbits at
R>2
pc before reaching the CND orbit.
Whether the rotating disk of CND-OD is a stable structure remains uncertain.
However, its morphology deviates significantly from an axisymmetric disk, with a large fraction of the [C I] emission concentrated near the junctions of CND-OS and CND-IR (
\phi\sim$$
and
[formula]
).
This non-uniform azimuthal distribution suggests that the disk may be short-lived.

The high mass flux of
\sim 0.1~{M_{\odot}}\mathrm{yr}^{-1}
is approximately a factor of a few to an order of magnitude higher than the theoretically calculated rate of steady mass loss of the CND
(
Vollmer & Duschl 2002
;
Tress et al. 2020
)
.
This implies that the CND system has not yet reached a dynamically settled state after the most recent GMC capture event, where fast inward motion could be facilitated by collisions between different streams.
Two scenarios can be considered for the origin of this situation.
One possibility is that the CND-IR and the streamers were formed in a single GMC capture event. The observed spiral pattern along the WA flow resembles that found in numerical models of single GMC capture
(
Sanders 1998
;
Bonnell & Rice 2008
;
Mapelli & Trani 2016
)
.
Our results show that the CND-IR and CND-OS share a common orbital plane, supporting the idea that they inherited the angular momentum of the same progenitor GMC.
In this scenario, the CND-IR can be regarded as a transient structure emerging within the WA flow, which eventually connects to the mini-spiral.
This scenario may also explain the NW gap of the CND-IR, whose endpoints approximately coincide with the contact point between CND-OS(N) and CND-IR (i.e., where gas inflow into the CND-IR begins) and the downstream end of CND-IS(S) (i.e., where gas outflow toward the mini-spiral WA ends).
An alternative scenario is that the WA flow consists of a combination of a pre-existing dense-gas ring and a newly infalling low-density stream, similar to the configuration assumed in the model by
Alig et al. 2013

This multi-infall scenario naturally explains the coexistence of two types of features with contrasting physical and kinematical properties, namely, a steadily rotating dense-gas ring and low-density infalling streamers, by assuming two progenitor GMCs.
The observed

gradient along the WA flow can then be understood as gradual mixing of the pre-existing CS-dominant gas with newly infalling [C I]-dominant gas.

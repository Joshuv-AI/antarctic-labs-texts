Factors associated with the uptake of intermittent preventive treatment of malaria in pregnancy in Nambale Sub-County Hospital, Kenya
Authors: Collins Omondi Angute, Stephen Joram Olubulyera, Maurice Owiny, Elvis Oyugi, Diana Menya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMalaria remains a significant public health problem globally, with highest morbidity and mortality reported in sub Saharan Africa. In 2022, 12.7 million (36%) Malaria in Pregnancy (MiP) cases in Sub Saharan Africa and 27% were reported from East Africa. In Kenya, 4,080,441 malaria cases and 5% MiP cases reported. Busia County, Kenya, reported 341,886 malaria cases and 22% MiP cases. WHO recommends administering intermittent preventive treatment of malaria in pregnancy using sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine (IPTp-SP) to prevent MIP in malaria-endemic zones. The objective of the study was to determine proportion of pregnant women utilizing IPTp-SP 3 and describe factors influencing utilization of IPTp-SP 3.MethodsA cross-sectional study was employed, pregnant women of nine months, aged 14–49 years were interviewed, using questionnaires on Kobo-collect. The dependent variable was the uptake of three doses of IPTp-SP, with sociodemographic, health facility and individual factors as the independent variables. Chi square test was used in bivariate analysis to determine association between independent variables and dependable variables, variable with p value of ≤ 0.2, were subjected to multivariable logistic regression analysis to identify significant factors (variables with p value of ≤ 0.05)Results384 pregnant women were interviewed, 90% resided in rural areas; 93% aware of IPTp use, 68% were married, 67% unemployed, 60% utilized IPTp-SP3 and 47% had secondary education. In the bivariate analysis, participant age 21–30 years {cOR = 2.34, 95% CI = 1.4–3.7}, belief in > 3 doses of IPTp preventing MiP {cOR = 3.09, 95% CI = 1.5–6.2}and having tertiary education {cOR = 2.71, 95% CI = 1.4–5.1} were associated with uptake of ≥3doses of IPTp. On multivariable logistics regression analysis, attendance of ANC at least 4 times {aOR = 8.42, 95% CI = 4.4–16.0} and uptake of IPTp-SP at 14–17 gestation weeks {aOR = 7.79, 95% CI = 3.2–18.7} independently associated with optimal utilization of IPTp (IPTp-SP3).Conclusion and recommendationA sub-optimal IPTp-SP 3 utilization (60%) way below WHO target of 80%. ≥4ANC attendance and first IPTp-SP uptake at 14–17 gestation weeks were independently associated with optimal utilization of IPTp. Pregnant women attending ANC at the facility should ensure that they attend ANC at least four times and take at least three doses of IPTp for maximum protection against malaria in pregnancy.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

Background

Despite WHO recommendations that all pregnant women in malaria-endemic areas receive at least three doses of intermittent preventive treatment with Sulfadoxine–Pyrimethamine (IPTp-SP) to reduce the risk of malaria in pregnancy, coverage in many parts of Kenya—including Busia County—remains suboptimal.

The specific determinants of IPTp utilization in this setting have not been well documented. This study therefore seeks to assess the level of IPTp-SP utilization and identify the factors associated with uptake of the recommended three or more IPTp doses among pregnant women attending ANC at Nambale Sub-County Hospital.

Malaria in pregnancy (MIP) is a major public health problem, with substantial risks for the mother, her foetus and the new-born. MIP adverse effects includes but not limited to: maternal anaemia, low birth weight, retarded child growth and death depending on the malaria patterns in an area. In 2022, 36% of all pregnancies in WHO African region were exposed to MIP (World Malaria Report [7]). East Africa had MIP prevalence (27%), West Africa (39.3%) and Central Africa (40.1%). In Kenya, there was total of 4,080,441 malaria cases and 5% MIP cases. Busia county reported 341,886 malaria cases and 22% MIP cases.

The WHO recommends the administration of intermittent preventive treatment with sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine (IPTp-SP) to pregnant women living in malaria endemic zones. The IPTp-SP should be given at the onset of second trimester (gestational weeks > 13). IPTp-SP averts the MIP and its adverse effects. WHO recommends a minimum of at least 3 doses of IPTp-SP, with each dose given one month apart during the ANC visits.

Despite increased IPTp utilization in the WHO Africa region in the last two years, optimal use still remains a mirage due to late initiation of ANC visits by pregnant women, inadequate number of ANC contacts i.e. less than 8 visits, frequents SP stock-outs and supply chain gaps, geographical and transport barriers hindering access to IPTp, and also some socio-cultural influences ANC attendance. Optimal IPTp utilization is set at a target of 80% for the IPTp3 as per the WHO recommendations. Methodology

This study was carried out between 1st July to 30th September 2023. The study was carried out in Busia county, Kenya, in East Africa (Fig. 2). Busia county was identified due to high prevalence of malaria in the country and thus also being county with highest consumption of IPTp-SP. Nambale sub-county, one of the 7 sub-counties in Busia county was identified to have the highest decline rate of IPTp3 uptake despite having a higher IPTp-SP 1 uptake in the year proceeding 2023, thus was chosen to be the study region. Within the government health facilities in Nambale sub-county, Nambale sub-county hospital was identified as the facility with highest decline in IPTp3 use. A cross-sectional study was employed with a consecutive sampling technique used to sample study participants whereby, pregnant women of nine months, aged between 14 and 49 years were identified and interviewed, using the interviewer- administered questionnaires on Kobo-collect. The dependent variable was the uptake of three doses of IPTp-SP, with sociodemographic, health facility and individual factors as the independent variables. A Descriptive of factors was done, Chi square test was used in bivariate analysis to determine association between independent variables and dependable variables, variable with p value of ≤ 0.2, were subjected to multivariable logistic regression analysis to identify variables with p value of ≤ 0.05 associated with utilization of IPTp-SP among pregnant women.Fig. 2A Kenya map showing Busia county, its sub-counties, and the location of Nambale Sub-county hospital in Nambale. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria

Pregnant women of 36 weeks’ gestation or more attending Antenatal care (ANC) at the Nambale sub-county hospital.

Pregnant women on another preventive treatment like cotrimoxazole prophylaxis (CTXp)

Exclusion criteria

Pregnant women with contraindications to sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine

Sample size determination

Cochrane formula was used for sample size calculations/determination

The following assumptions were made:

Z: The score at 95% confidence interval (CI) = 1.96.

P: Expected proportion of pregnant women taking three or more doses of IPTp-SP = 49% based on the prevalence obtained during Kenya Malaria indicator survey KMIS of 2020.

d: Degree of precision = 0.05

Sampling procedure

Counties offering IPTp in Kenya listed and utilization of IPTp 3 noted. County with the lowest percentage uptake of IPTp3 identified- Busia county. Then utilization of IPTp 3 within the Sub-counties of Busia county listed. Nambale sub-county was identified, and facilities utilization of IPTp3 listed. Nambale sub-county Hospital (NSCH) was identified as facility with the lowest IPTp 3 utilization. Eligible study participants attending ANC at NSCH were identified and consent sought from them and a consecutive sampling method used. A semi-structured questionnaire on IPTp 3 utilization was administered to consenting participants.

Data management and analysis

Data cleaning done in Ms Excel and analysis in Stata v.15.1 software whereby, frequencies and proportions were calculated for categorical variables and measures for central tendency and dispersion was calculated to summarize continuous variables.

Bivariate Analysis (Logistic Regression) was used to calculate; Crude Odds Ratio (cOR), 95% C.I.s and p-value) and those variables with p-value ≤ 0.2 at bivariate analysis were taken to Multivariate analysis.

Multivariate Analysis was also done, whereby, a backward stepwise Logistic Regression analysis technique used to calculate, Adjusted Odds Ratio (aOR), 95% C.I.s and p-values. Those Variables with p-value ≤ 0.05 were considered statistically significant

Individual and cultural characteristics of the respondents (N = 384)

The majority of respondents (n=356, 93%) acknowledged being aware of IPTp-SP. A huge number of respondents believed in IPTp-SP protection against MIP (n=229, 60%), with only a few dissenting (n=38, 10%) (Table 2).

Only one study participant out of the 384 responded “Yes” to having cultural belief against the IPTp uptake, the rest dissenting (n=383, 99%). A large number of the participants reported on making sole decision on matters pertaining to health in their homes (n=172, 45%), whereas a small number of respondents reported that their parents contributed towards their decision making in the house (n=9, 2%) (Table 2). A large number of participants reported on being accompanied during ANC visits (n=301, 78%).

Discussion

In this study, 60% of eligible pregnant women received the recommended three or more doses of IPTp-SP, indicating moderate progress but still falling short of the WHO target of 80%. This coverage reflects improving ANC service delivery in Nambale Sub-County Hospital but also highlights persistent gaps, particularly late ANC initiation, which limits the time required for proper dose spacing, and missed opportunities during ANC visits due to provider oversight, stock-outs, or inconsistent DOT. Sociocultural and behavioural factors including misconceptions about SP and limited partner support—may also have contributed to the 40% who did not complete IPTp3. These findings are consistent with national and regional trends, where IPTp uptake has improved yet remains below optimal levels. Overall, the results underscore the need to strengthen early ANC attendance, ensure consistent SP availability, and enhance provider adherence to IPTp guidelines to increase IPTp3 coverage and reduce the risk of malaria in pregnancy.

In this study, only 60% of pregnant women eligible for the IPTp uptake, received the recommended three doses of IPTp-SP as per the WHO requirements. The factors that were statically significant with the uptake of more than three doses were the attendance of more than four (4) ANC visits and commencement of IPTp utilization earlier in the second trimester. Attendance of ≥ 4 ANC visits was significantly associated with uptake of ≥ 3 IPTp doses for the following reasons; it increased opportunities to receive SP doses and with each multiple visits pregnant women got repeated messages on malaria prevention, benefits of IPTp-SP and the risks of maternal malaria. The early commencement of IPTp-SP utilization in the second trimester allows enough time for spacing doses and reduces missed opportunities, this thus ensures women do not miss the widow for administering the required 3 doses. This study finding revealed a sub-optimal IPTp3 uptake way below the WHO targets of 80%. Similar findings, were found too in a study done of 12 countries of Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) which also showed a sub-optimal IPT3 uptake, they used a multilevel analysis data analysis and still found similar results, i.e. they found out that uptake of more than 3 dose of IPTp was 59.64% and 57.46% for Ghana and Burkina respectively [2].

However, this study results differ from a number of previous studies. One such study was that done in Sabatia in Western Kenya, a cross sectional study, that reported an optimal utilization of more than three doses of IPTp-SP at 79.6% [6]. Another cross sectional study, on the determination of IPTp-SP utilization in Sub-Saharan Africa, found out a suboptimal utilization of IPTp-SP of 29.5% [8]. Also a sub optimal uptake of IPTp-3, i.e. 8% was noted in a study of a cross sectional done in Tanzania in 2021 [5], 53% in a cross sectional study done in western Kenya [3], 46.6% in a cross sectional study in Tema Metropolis in Ghana [1], 22.3% in a study done in East-Central Uganda [4].

In this study finding, IPTp3 utilization by pregnant women at Nambale Sub-county hospital in Busia County was 60%, which is a sub-optimal utilization. Therefore, this means that at the time of the study, of all pregnant women commencing their ANC attendance at the facility only 60% end up taking the recommended doses of IPTp-SP of three or more, with the rest missing out, either due one or more reasons. This therefore means that the population that misses on IPTp-SP, the 40%, are more likely to contract malaria in pregnancy (MIP) and suffer its adverse effects i.e. abortions, still births, low birth weights among others; if these occurs, then mortality and morbidity related to MIP would be experienced, thus affecting the socio-economic status of the region and county at large. Therefore, our study settings should put in place accelerated interventions for IPTp-SP utilization in order to reach the recommended targets by World Health Organization (WHO).

Limitations

Recall bias on the exact gestation week of pregnancy among pregnant women at the ANC clinic in Nambale Sub-County Hospital (NSCH). However, this was solved by checking on the individual ANC booklets and Hospital register, MOH 405.The study was conducted only at Nambale Sub-County Hospital, which may not represent pregnant women in other facilities in Nambale Sub-County, or private clinics or the general community, thus limiting the generalizability of the findings.

In addition, the study only explored client level perspectives leaving out health system variables (Key informant interviews –KII) that significantly contribute to deficiencies in health service delivery. Also pregnant women who were in the 1st and 2nd trimester, allergic to sulphonamides were excluded yet these groups may have different characteristics affecting representativeness.

As a cross-sectional study, our factors identified may not necessarily represent causal factors as may better be identified through a cohort study following clients’/study participants over time, however, the factors we identified are in line with other studies on IPTp utilization. However, we do believe that our findings generated evidence on the optimal IPTp-service utilization and will therefore guide future policy direction not only in Busia County, but also nationally and regionally.

Conclusion and recommendation

This cross-sectional study conducted at Nambale Sub-County Hospital demonstrated that although IPTp uptake has improved, only 60% of eligible pregnant women received the recommended three doses of IPTp-SP, which remains below the WHO target of universal coverage of 80%. Several maternal, socio-demographic, and health-system factors influenced uptake, i.e, the attendance of ANC at least four times during the pregnancy period and the uptake of IPTp-SP as early as gestation weeks of 14–17 weeks was associated with utilization of more than 3 doses of SP same as having a tertiary education level

Key barriers included late ANC initiation, suboptimal frequency of ANC visits, inconsistent availability of clean water for DOT, knowledge gaps among mothers, and occasional facility-level constraints. Despite high general attendance at ANC, missed opportunities were evident, particularly among women who presented late or made inadequate ANC contacts. The findings highlight the need for strengthened maternal education, improved facility readiness, and enhanced provider practices to ensure optimal IPTp-SP delivery. Overall, the study underscores persistent gaps in malaria prevention in pregnancy and the importance of targeted interventions to improve uptake in similar rural settings.

Recommendations

This study highlighted the various factors that impede utilization of IPTp-SP in the study area.

To improve uptake of IPTp-SP3 at Nambale Sub-County Hospital, a coordinated multi-level approach should be implemented. The Sub-County Health Management Team (SCHMT) and facility in-charge should ensure early ANC initiation through community sensitization led by Community Health Volunteers (CHVs), with emphasis on booking before 12 weeks of gestation and completing at least four ANC visits. The ANC clinic staff and nurses should strengthen routine, client-centred counselling on IPTp-SP at every ANC contact, address myths and misconceptions, actively involve male partners, and ensure consistent implementation of DOT by maintaining a functional DOT corner. The hospital management and county malaria coordinator should provide regular refresher training and supportive supervision to improve staff attitude and adherence to national IPTp guidelines, while ensuring uninterrupted availability of SP and basic DOT supplies. Additionally, the county health department should support enrolment of pregnant women into health insurance schemes and facilitate reminder and default-tracing mechanisms through CHVs to enhance continuity of ANC attendance and completion of recommended IPTp doses.

Pregnant women are encouraged to begin antenatal care (ANC) as early as possible in the second trimester and to attend all scheduled ANC visits to ensure they receive the full protection offered by intermittent preventive treatment with Sulfadoxine–pyrimethamine (IPTp-SP). Starting ANC early provides enough time for proper spacing of the IPTp doses, while consistent attendance allows health providers to monitor pregnancy, administer SP under directly observed therapy (DOT), and address any concerns or misconceptions about the medication.

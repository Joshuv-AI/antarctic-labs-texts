Knots that share four surgeries
Marc Kegel, Lisa Piccirillo

Abstract
. Distinct knots K , K ′ K,K^{\prime} can sometimes share a common p / q p/q -framed Dehn surgery. A folk conjecture held that for a fixed pair of knots, this can occur for at most one value of p / q p/q . We disprove this conjecture by constructing pairs of distinct knots K , K ′ K,K^{\prime} that have common Dehn surgeries for four distinct slopes. We also construct non-isotopic Legendrian knots K , K ′ K,K^{\prime} that have contactomorphic contact ( + 1 ) (+1) - and ( − 1 ) (-1) -surgeries, disproving an analogous conjecture in contact geometry.

Dehn surgery for a fixed rational slope
p/q
can be thought of as a function from the set of knots in
S^{3}
to the set of closed, oriented 3-manifolds. This foundational connection between knot theory and 3-manifold topology underlies many significant results in both areas, and motivates the study of these Dehn surgery functions themselves. This paper is concerned with the injectivity of these functions.

Generically, the topology of any
p/q
-Dehn surgery closely resembles the topology of the knot complement, and the more slopes one considers, the more tightly the knot complement is constrained. It is surprising for distinct knots
K,K^{\prime}
to have even a single common
p/q
-Dehn surgery. However, such
K,K^{\prime}
and
p/q
do exist; this was originally proven in
[
Lic77
]
.

This contrasts to a folk theorem
1

Outline

In Section
1
we will give a method of constructing knots that share four surgeries using RBG links. The casual reader need not read past this section.
In Section
2
, we start the discussion of distinguishing such pairs of knots. We will discuss a long list of invariants (double branched covers, knot Floer and Khovanov homology groups, and hyperbolic invariants such as volume, cusp shape, and systole) that do not appear to distinguish our knots. We will also discuss some invariants which, via computer experimentation, apparently do (HOMFLYPT polynomials, the canonical triangulations, or the number of covers of a fixed degree of the knot exterior).
Theorem
0.2
is proven in Section
3
.
In Section
4
we give some open problems.
In Appendix
A

Conventions

We work in the smooth category. All manifolds, maps, and ancillary objects are assumed to be smooth. When we say manifolds are ‘the same’ we mean orientation preserving diffeomorphic. Surgery coefficients of smooth links in
S^{3}
are measured with respect to the Seifert longitude. Surgery coefficients of Legendrian knots are with respect to the contact longitude.

1.
Constructing knots that share 4-surgeries

In this section, we use RBG links to construct pairs of knots that share four integral surgeries. RBG links were introduced in
[
Pic19
]
and are usually used to produce a pair of knots with a single common surgery.

Definition 1.1

A
3
-component link
L
in
S^{3}
with components
R
,
B
, and
G
, is called
Hopf RBG link
if the following hold:

(1)

R\cup B
,
R\cup G
, and
B\cup G
are Hopf links.

(2)

The Seifert disks
D_{R}
,
D_{B}
, and
D_{G}
of
R
,
B
, and
G
can be isotoped such that the following three conditions hold simultaneously:

(a)

D_{B}
and
D_{G}
intersect in a single clasp singularity,

(b)

D_{B}
intersects
D_{R}
in a single clasp singularity and finitely many ribbon singularities, and

(c)

D_{G}
intersects
D_{R}
in a single clasp singularity and finitely many ribbon singularities.

Figure 2.
Two schematic views on a Hopf RBG link.

A schematic picture of a Hopf RBG link is shown in Figure
2
. For a concrete simple example of a (non-trivial) Hopf RBG link we refer to Figure
6
. After an isotopy, we can always assume that
D_{B}
intersects
D_{R}
in a single clasp singularity or that
D_{G}
intersects
D_{R}
in a single clasp singularity. But in general, both are not possible simultaneously, see for example Figure
6
.

We will show that every Hopf RBG link induces pairs of knots that share four surgeries. For that, we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 1.2

Surgery on a Hopf link with surgery coefficients
p/q
and
r/s
yields
S^{3}
if and only if
pr-qs=\pm 1
.

Proof.

Any Dehn surgery on the Hopf link yields a lens space. Among lens spaces, we can recognize
S^{3}
by the vanishing of its first homology group. Since
|pr-qs|
yields the order of the first homology of a
(p/q,r/s)
-surgery on a
2
-component link, the claim follows.
∎

Corollary 1.3

To any Hopf RBG link
L
, pair of rationals
p/q
and
r/s
with
pr-qs=\pm 1
, and
C\in\{R,B,G\}
, we can naturally associate a knot
K_{C}^{p/q,r/s}
by considering the image of
C
in the
S^{3}
obtained by performing
p/q
and
r/s
surgery on
L\setminus C
. ∎

In this paper, we will mainly be concerned with the knots
K_{G}^{0,n}
and
K_{B}^{0,n}
.

By convention, we always choose an orientation of the Hopf RBG link such that

\displaystyle\lk(R,B)=1=\lk(R,G).

Thus the linking number
\lk(B,G)
is either
+1
or
-1
. Then the precise version of our main theorem reads as follows.

Theorem 1.4

For any Hopf RBG link L and integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the knots
K_{G}^{0,n}
and
K_{B}^{0,n}
have orientation-preserving diffeomorphic surgeries for the following set of surgery slopes.

•

(n+1,n+2,n+3,2n+2)
, if
\lk(B,G)=-1
, and

•

(n-3,n-2,n-1,2n-2)
, if
\lk(B,G)=1
.

To prove Theorem
1.4
, we first observe that there are distinct fillings of a Hopf RBG link which produce the same manifold. We use the notation
L(r,b,g)
to denote filling
R,B,G
with slopes
r,b,g
, respectively. We use
\ast
to denote no filling.

Lemma 1.5

Let
L
be a Hopf RBG link. Then for any
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s\in\{-1,0,1\}
we have that
L(n,m,s)
is orientation-preserving diffeomorphic to
L(n,s,m)
.

Proof.

For each
s
, these diffeomorphisms are induced by the sequences of handle slides and slam dunks shown in Figures
3
and
4
.
∎

is diffeomorphic to

Figure 4.

With this lemma, we are ready to give the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For knots
K_{G}^{0,n}
and
K_{B}^{0,n}
constructed from a Hopf RBG link
L
as in Corollary

, the diffeomorphisms from Lemma

readily show that
K_{G}^{0,n}
and
K_{B}^{0,n}
share four integer surgeries as follows.

\displaystyle K_{G}^{0,n}(s_{n})

\displaystyle=L(0,n,n)=K_{B}^{0,n}(s_{n})

\displaystyle K_{G}^{0,n}(s_{0})

\displaystyle=L(0,n,0)=L(0,0,n)=K_{B}^{0,n}(s_{0})

\displaystyle K_{G}^{0,n}(s_{1})

\displaystyle=L(0,n,+1)=L(0,+1,n)=K_{B}^{0,n}(s_{1})

\displaystyle K_{G}^{0,n}(s_{-1})

\displaystyle=L(0,n,-1)=L(0,-1,n)=K_{B}^{0,n}(s_{-1})

Here we are using

to stand in for a not-yet-computed surgery coefficient. To compute the coefficients

∎

Remark 1.6

A few remarks are in order.

(1)

If the Hopf RBG links are overly simple or symmetric then it might happen that the knots
K_{G}^{0,n}
and
K_{G}^{0,n}
are isotopic. But in general, there is no reason why these knots should be isotopic, and we will prove in Section
2
that often they are not.

(2)

On the other hand for
n=-1,0,1
, the knots
K_{G}^{0,n}
and
K_{B}^{0,n}
are always isotopic. Indeed, Lemma
1.5
implies that

\displaystyle K_{G}^{0,0}

\displaystyle=L(0,0,*)=L(0,*,0)=K_{B}^{0,0},

\displaystyle K_{G}^{0,+1}

\displaystyle=L(0,+1,*)=L(0,*,+1)=K_{B}^{0,+1},

\displaystyle K_{G}^{0,-1}

\displaystyle=L(0,-1,*)=L(0,*,-1)=K_{B}^{0,-1}.

(3)

Similarly for all integers
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the knots
K_{G}^{n,0}
and
K_{B}^{n,0}
are isotopic.

(4)

It follows from Figure
5
that the knots
K_{B}^{0,n}
and
K_{G}^{0,n}
are related by taking the tangle shown in the lower row of Figure
5
and rotating it by
\pi
. This can be thought of as a generalized mutation.

In Theorem
1.4
we established that the knots
K_{G}^{0,n}
and
K_{B}^{0,n}
have four common surgeries. Recall that for any
s\in\mathbb{Z}
we can associate a 4-manifold to a knot
K
by attaching a
2
-handle with framing
s
along a knot
K
in
\partial D^{4}
. This
4
-manifold is called the
s
-trace,
X_{s}(K)
. The boundary of
X_{s}(K)
is diffeomorphic to the
s
-surgery along
K
. Since the RBG link which induced
K_{G}^{0,n}
and
K_{B}^{0,n}
had
R=U
and
r=0
, our Theorem
1.4
immediately generalizes to traces by taking the RBG link
L
to be a handle diagram of a 4-manifold where
R
is a
1
-handle in dotted circle notation.

Corollary 1.7

Let
L
be a Hopf RBG link. For every integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
the knots
K_{G}^{0,n}
and
K_{B}^{0,n}

•

(n+1,n+2,n+3,2n+2)

\lk(B,G)=-1

•

(n-3,n-2,n-1,2n-2)

\lk(B,G)=1

Remark 2.2

We make a few remarks about other ways to distinguish or not distinguish knots that share four surgeries.

(1)

In all examples of Hopf RBG links that we have inspected, the knots
K_{B}^{0,n}
and
K_{G}^{0,n}
share many knot invariants. For example, they have isomorphic knot Floer and Khovanov homologies and thus also the same Alexander and Jones polynomial and the same
3
-genus. Moreover, verified computations with SnapPy suggest that such knots always have the same hyperbolic invariants (volume, cusp shape, symmetry group, systole, and short slopes). This is probably explained by the fact that the knots coming from our construction are always related by a generalized mutation, as explained in Remark
1.6
.

(2)

Knots coming from our construction have the same double branched cover; see Theorem
2.3
below.

(3)

We can also distinguish
K_{B}^{0,n}
and
K_{G}^{0,n}
using the fundamental groups of their complements by looking at the number of conjugacy classes of index
k
subgroups. Indeed, for the Hopf RBG link
L
from Figure
6
we have used SnapPy to compute (for small values of
n
) the number of covers of
K_{B}^{0,n}
and
K_{G}^{0,n}
to be different. For example it turns out that
K_{B}^{0,2}
has
32
covers of degree
8
while
K_{G}^{0,2}
has
33
covers of degree
8
.

(4)

Another successful and computationally fast way to distinguish such knots is by using canonical triangulations. Here we have used the verified methods in SnapPy
[
CDGW
]
to construct the canonical triangulations of
K_{B}^{0,n}
and
K_{G}^{0,n}
for small values of
n
and show that they are not combinatorial equivalent, cf.
[
SW95
]
. With the results and methods from
[
GS10
]
it should also be possible to distinguish infinitely many
K_{B}^{0,n}
and
K_{G}^{0,n}
using their canonical triangulations.

(5)

There exist also examples of Hopf RBG links where
D_{R}\cap(D_{B}\cup D_{G})
consists of a single ribbon and two clasp singularities such that
K_{B}^{0,n}
and
K_{G}^{0,n}
are non-isotopic, see
[

]
for a concrete example. However, then the inductive formula for the HOMFLYPT polynomial yields that for all
n
, the knots
K_{B}^{0,n}
and
K_{G}^{0,n}
have the same HOMFLYPT polynomial. On the other hand, the canonical triangulations can still distinguish
K_{B}^{0,n}
and
K_{G}^{0,n}
(for
n\neq-1,0,1
).

Lemma 3.2

If

\displaystyle K_{G}=

\displaystyle\,L(+1,-1,*)\textrm{ and }K_{B}=\,L(+1,*,-1)

Proof.

The cancellation lemma implies that contact

Let

4.
Some open questions

We end this article with some open questions. In Remark
2.2
we observed that many invariants agree on our knots that share four surgeries. We don’t know how general this is.

Question 4.1

Let
K
and
K^{\prime}
be two knots that share more than one surgery. Do
K
and
K^{\prime}
have the same knot Floer and Khovanov homology? Do they have the same hyperbolic invariants, such as volume or cusp shape?

Recall that in Remark
1.6
(5) we showed that our pairs of knots with four common surgeries are related by a generalized mutation operation. This might explain why our knots share many knot invariants; it might not be inherent to having many common surgeries. The effect of a different generalization of mutation on various invariants was studied in
[
APR89
,
DIPY04
]
, but our examples are not covered by these results.

We also conjecture that in fact there is no bound on the number of slopes for which some pair of knots can have common surgeries.

Conjecture 4.2

For any natural number
N
there exist pairwise-different integers
n_{1},\ldots n_{N}
and non-isotopic knots
K
and
K^{\prime}
such that for
i=1,\ldots N
,
K(n_{i})
is orientation-preserving diffeomorphic to
K^{\prime}(n_{i})
.

One can also ask more specific versions of these questions, such as whether, for any finite set of rationals, one can find pairs of distinct knots which share those surgeries, or whether one can find infinite families of distinct knots with several common surgeries.

A.1.
The degree 0 part of the HOMFLYPT polynomial

The HOMFLYPT polynomial
H_{L}(v,z)
of an oriented link
L
is defined via
H_{\textrm{unknot}}(v,z)=1
and the following skein relation

\displaystyle v^{-1}H_{L+}(v,z)-vH_{L_{-}}(v,z)=zH_{L_{0}}(v,z).

Here
L_{+}
,
L_{-}
, and
L_{0}
are links that agree outside a
3
-ball
B
such that
L_{\pm}
admits a single positive/negative crossing inside
B
and this crossing is resolved (following the orientation) in
L_{0}
.
It is known that the HOMFLYPT polynomial can be written as

H_{L}(v,z)=(v^{-1}z)^{1-\#L}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}H^{i}_{L}(v)z^{2i},

where
\#L
denotes the number of components of the link
L
and
H^{i}_{L}(v)
is a Laurent polynomial in
v^{2},v^{-2}
. We write
F_{L}(v)
for
H^{0}_{L}(v)
; this

is characterized by
F_{\text{unknot}}(v)=1
and the following simple skein relation:

v^{-2}F_{L_{+}}(v)-F_{L_{-}}(v)=\begin{cases}F_{L_{0}}(v)\,\,&\text{ if }\delta=0,\\
0\,\,&\text{ if }\delta=1,\end{cases}

where
\delta=0
if the two strands at the resolved crossing belong to the same component and
\delta=1
otherwise. In particular, this implies that
F_{L}(v)
of a link

with components

only depends on the linking numbers
\lk(K_{i},K_{j})
and the polynomials

of the components. More precisely, we have for an oriented link
L=K_{1}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n}
with components

the following linking number formula

\displaystyle F_{L}(v)=(v^{-2}-1)^{n-1}v^{2\sum_{i<j}\lk(K_{i},K_{j})}F_{K_{1}}(v)\cdots F_{K_{n}}(v).

We refer to
[

Abstract
. Edoukou, Ling and Xing in 2010, conjectured that in ℙ n ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) {\mathbb{P}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}) , n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , the maximum number of common points of a non-degenerate Hermitian variety 𝒰 n \mathcal{U}_{n} and a hypersurface of degree d d is achieved only when the hypersurface is union of d d distinct hyperplanes meeting in a common linear space Π n − 2 \Pi_{n-2} of codimension 2 2 such that Π n − 2 ∩ 𝒰 n \Pi_{n-2}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n} is a non-degenerate Hermitian variety. Furthermore, these d d hyperplanes are tangent to 𝒰 n \mathcal{U}_{n} if n n is odd and non-tangent if n n is even. In this paper, we show that the conjecture is true for d = 3 d=3 and q ≥ 7 q\geq 7 .

Introduction

Hermitian varieties over finite fields are among the most widely studied varieties in algebraic geometry due to their alluring geometric structure, high number of rational points, and applications to coding theory. These varieties became more prominent among coding theorists and combinatorists after G. Lachaud introduced functional codes in
[
14

[
8
]
, Bose and Chakravarti were the first to study these varieties with a strong emphasis on geometry in
[

]
. In order to determine the minimum distance and the minimum weight codewords of the functional code defined over a non-degenerate Hermitian variety as well as from a combinatorial perspective, the following question has been studied in
[

,
3
,
4
,
7
,
10
,
12
]

Question 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{n}
be a non-degenerate Hermitian variety in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational points common to a hypersurface

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and

Bose and Chakravarti answered the above question for

in
[

,
6

. When

many

-rational points. Although it is easy to set an example of a curve of degree

For the case when

2.
Preliminaries

We fix an integer
q
, a prime power. As customary,
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
denote the finite fields with
q
and
q^{2}
elements, respectively. For
n\geq 0
, we denote by
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, the projective space of dimension
n
over the algebraic closure
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
, while
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
will denote the set of all
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational points on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.
Likewise,
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
and
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
will denote the affine space of dimension
n
over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
and the set of all
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational points of
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
respectively.
Furthermore, for a homogeneous polynomial
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
, we denote by
V(F)
, the set of zeroes of
F
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and by
V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
the set of all
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational points of
V(F)
. By an algebraic variety, we mean a set of zeroes of a certain set of polynomials in the affine space or projective space, depending on the context. In particular, an algebraic variety need not be irreducible. However, when we say that a hypersurface
V(F)
is irreducible, we will mean that the polynomial
F
is irreducible in the field of its definition.

This section is organized into three subsections. The first subsection reviews several well-known facts about Hermitian varieties over finite fields. The second subsection focuses on the intersection of a non-degenerate Hermitian variety with a quadric. The third subsection revisits some fundamental concepts from basic algebraic geometry. All the results presented in this section are already established, and their proofs can be found in the cited references.

2.1.
Geometry of Hermitian varieties over a finite field

First, we recall the definition and key properties of Hermitian varieties (cf.
[
2
,
6
]
), which will be essential in the later sections of this paper.

Definition 2.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
H=(h_{ij})
(
0\leq i,j\leq n
) be an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. We denote by
H^{(q)}
the matrix whose
(i,j)
-th entry is given by
h_{ij}^{q}
. The matrix
H
is said to be a
Hermitian matrix
if
H\neq 0
and
H^{T}=H^{(q)}
. A
Hermitian variety
of dimension
n-1
, denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{n}
, is the zero set of the polynomial
x^{T}Hx^{(q)}
inside
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, where
H
is an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
Hermitian matrix and
x=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})^{T}
. The Hermitian variety is classified as
non-degenerate
if
\mathrm{rank}\ H=n+1
and
degenerate
otherwise.

Bose and Chakravarti in
[
2
, Corollary of Theorem 4.1]
proved that if the rank of a Hermitian matrix is
r
, then after a suitable projective change of coordinates over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, we can recognize the corresponding Hermitian variety by the zero set of the polynomial

(1)

x_{0}^{q+1}+x_{1}^{q+1}+\dots+x_{r-1}^{q+1}=0.

Here we note that the polynomial
x_{0}^{q+1}+x_{1}^{q+1}+\dots+x_{r-1}^{q+1}
is irreducible over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
only when
r\geq 3
. Thus, it follows that a Hermitian variety of rank at least three is always absolutely irreducible. Moreover, the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational points of a non-degenerate Hermitian variety
\mathcal{U}_{n}
is also well known and well established in the literature.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
2
, Theorem 8.1]
Let
\mathcal{U}_{n}
be a non-degenerate Hermitian variety in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. Then

|\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|=\frac{\big(q^{n}-(-1)^{n}\big)\left(q^{n+1}-(-1)^{n+1}\right)}{q^{2}-1}.

As mentioned in the introduction, the hyperplane sections of a non-degenerate Hermitian variety are well-studied, thanks to
[
2
,
6
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{n}
be a non-degenerate Hermitian variety in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and
\Sigma
be a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

(a)

[
2
, Theorem 7.4]
if
\Sigma
is tangent to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
at a point
P\in\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
, then
\Sigma\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}
is a degenerate Hermitian variety of rank
n-1
contained in
\Sigma
. That is,

\Sigma\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}
is a cone over a non-degenerate Hermitian variety
\mathcal{U}_{n-2}
contained in a hyperplane of
\Sigma
with center at
P
.

(b)

[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
if
\Sigma
is not a tangent to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
, then
\Sigma\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}
is a non-degenerate Hermitian variety
\mathcal{U}_{n-1}
in
\Sigma
.

Remark 2.4
.

Chakravarti proved
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
for a polar hyperplane at an external point of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
with respect to
\mathcal{U}_{n}=V\left(x^{T}Hx^{(q)}\right)
, where a point

with row vector

is called an external point with respect to

if

and the polar hyperplane

of

at

is the set of

-rational solutions of

is a hyperplane in

is the polar hyperplane at

.

Let

and Theorem

Remark 2.8
.

Geometrically,
\Pi_{0}\mathcal{U}_{n-3}
is a cone over the non-degenerate Hermitian variety

with vertex at

. Thus it follows from Theorem

|\Pi_{0}\mathcal{U}_{n-3}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|=1+q^{2}\frac{\big(q^{n-3}-(-1)^{n-3}\big)\left(q^{n-2}-(-1)^{n-2}\right)}{q^{2}-1}.

And since

is a cone with vertex as the line

and base as

For ease of computation, let us list the cardinalities of three types of sections of

Case 1:

is even.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{U}_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2n-3}-q^{n-1}+q^{n-2}-1}{q^{2}-1}

\displaystyle|\Pi_{0}\mathcal{U}_{n-3}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2n-3}+q^{n-1}-q^{n-2}-1}{q^{2}-1}

Definition 2.9

[

Let

A_{4}:=q^{5}+q^{4}+4q^{3}-3q+1

For

2.3.
Preliminaries from algebraic geometry

Here, we recall several fundamental results from algebraic geometry that will play a crucial role in subsequent sections. We will rely on concepts such as the dimension and degree of variety, as discussed in standard algebraic geometry textbooks, including Harris
[
11
]
. We begin with the following definitions. A variety is said to have
pure dimension
or be
equidimensional
if all its irreducible components share the same dimension. Additionally, two equidimensional varieties
X,Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
are said to
intersect properly
if
\mathrm{codim}(X\cap Y)=\mathrm{codim}X+\mathrm{codim}Y
.
Notably, obtaining effective upper bounds for the number of rational points on varieties defined over a finite field depends on the degree and dimension of the variety. For this reason, the following Proposition from
[
11
]
will be indispensable.

Proposition 2.12
.

[
11
, Cor. 18.5]

Let
X
and
Y
be equidimensional varieties that intersect properly in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. Then

\deg(X\cap Y)\leq\deg X\deg Y
.

In particular, we have the following Proposition relating to the intersection of two hypersurfaces.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
F,G\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be non-constant homogeneous polynomials having no common factors. Then

(a)

V(F)
and
V(G)
intersect properly.

(b)

V(F)\cap V(G)
is equidimensional of dimension
n-2
.

(c)

\deg(V(F)\cap V(G))\leq\deg F\deg G
.

Proof.

Part (a) is a well-known result in algebraic geometry and is typically established using Krull’s principal ideal theorem. Part (b) is derived from Macaulay’s unmixedness theorem (see
[
17
, Chapter 7, Theorem 26]
). Part (c) follows directly from Proposition
2.12
.
∎

We conclude this subsection by stating a result of Lachaud and Rolland on the number of rational points of a variety.

Proposition 2.14
.

[
15
, Prop. 2.3]

Let
X
be an equidimensional projective (resp. affine) variety defined over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Furthermore, assume that
\dim X=\delta
and
\deg X=d
. Then

|X({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|\leq dp_{\delta}\ \ \ \ \ \ (\mathrm{resp.}\ \ |X({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|\leq dq^{\delta}),

where
p_{\delta}=1+q+\dots+q^{\delta}
.

3.
Cubic section of a non-degenerate Hermitian variety

In this section, we introduce a sequence
\{B_{n}\}_{n\geq 4}

that will play an indispensable role in our analysis of intersections of Hermitian varieties and cubic hypersurfaces. This sequence is defined recursively, capturing structural properties that will be essential in the subsequent lemmas and proofs. The definition is as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. We define
B_{4}:=3(q^{5}+1)
and for
n\geq 5

B_{n}:=\begin{cases}q^{2}B_{n-1}-q^{n-2}\ \ \text{if}\ n\ \text{is even}\\
q^{2}B_{n-1}+3q^{n-2}+q^{n-3}\ \ \text{if}\ n\ \text{is odd.}\end{cases}

Lemma 3.2
.

For
n\geq 4
,

B_{n}=\begin{cases}3q^{2n-8}(q^{5}+1)+3\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-4}{2}}q^{2i+n-3}\right)\ \ \text{if}\ n\ \text{is even}\\
3q^{2n-8}(q^{5}+1)+3\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-3}{2}}q^{2i+n-4}\right)+q^{n-3}\ \ \text{if}\ n\ \text{is odd.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Clearly, the assertion is true for
n=4,5
. Suppose the assertion is true for
n-1
. We distinguish two cases:

Case 1:

n is even
. It follows from Definition
3.1
, that
B_{n}=q^{2}B_{n-1}-q^{n-2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle=q^{2}\left[3q^{2n-10}(q^{5}+1)+3\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-4}{2}}q^{2i+n-5}+q^{n-4}\right]-q^{n-2}\ \ \text{(by induction hypothesis)}

\displaystyle=3q^{2n-8}(q^{5}+1)+3\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-4}{2}}q^{2i+n-3}\right)+q^{n-2}-q^{n-2}

\displaystyle=3q^{2n-8}(q^{5}+1)+3\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-4}{2}}q^{2i+n-3}\right).

Case 2:

n
is odd.
Then by Definition
3.1
,
B_{n}=q^{2}B_{n-1}+3q^{n-2}+q^{n-3}
. Hence,

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle=q^{2}\left[3q^{2n-10}(q^{5}+1)+3\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-5}{2}}q^{2i+n-4}\right]+3q^{n-2}+q^{n-3}\ \ \text{(using induction hypothesis)}

\displaystyle=3q^{2n-8}(q^{5}+1)+3\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-5}{2}}q^{2i+n-2}\right)+3q^{n-2}+q^{n-3}

\displaystyle=3q^{2n-8}(q^{5}+1)+3\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-3}{2}}q^{2i+n-4}\right)+q^{n-3}.

The assertion now follows from induction.
∎

The following corollary is a consequence of Lemma
3.2
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 5
. Then

B_{n-1}\begin{cases}>q^{2n-5}+q^{2n-6}\ \ \text{if $n$ is even}\\
<3q^{2n-5}+q^{2n-6}\ \ \text{if $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof is divided into two cases:

Case 1:

n
is even
. From Lemma
3.2
it follows that

\displaystyle B_{n-1}

\displaystyle=q^{2n-10}B_{4}+3\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-4}{2}}q^{2i+n-5}\right)+q^{n-4}

\displaystyle=3q^{2n-5}+3q^{2n-10}+3\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-4}{2}}q^{2i+n-5}\right)+q^{n-4}

\displaystyle>q^{2n-5}+q^{2n-6}.

The last inequality follows since
3q^{2n-5}>q^{2n-5}+q^{2n-6}
.

Case 2:

n
is odd
. It follows from Lemma
3.2
that

\displaystyle(3q^{2n-5}+q^{2n-6})-B_{n-1}

\displaystyle=(3q^{2n-5}+q^{2n-6})-q^{2n-10}B_{4}-3\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-5}{2}}q^{2i+n-4}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{2n-6}-3q^{2n-10}-3\left(\frac{q^{2n-7}-q^{n-2}}{q^{2}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}\Bigg[q^{2n-10}(q^{6}-q^{4}-3q^{3}-3q^{2}+3)+3q^{n-2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle>0.

The last inequality follows as
q^{6}-q^{4}-3q^{3}-3q^{2}+3>0
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Sigma
be a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, where
n\geq 4
. Then

|\Sigma(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|+A_{n}<B_{n},

for
q\geq 3
.

Proof.

We consider two cases:

Case 1:

n
is even
. In this case Lemma
2.5
entails that
|\Sigma(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|\leq|\mathcal{U}_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|=\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}(q^{2n-1}+q^{n}-q^{n-1}-1)

A_{n}
and
B_{n}
from Lemma
2.10
and from Lemma
3.2
respectively, we conclude

\displaystyle B_{n}-A_{n}-|\Sigma(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-\frac{q^{2n-1}+q^{n}-q^{n-1}-1}{q^{2}-1}

\displaystyle B_{n}-A_{n}-|\Sigma(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-2q^{n-3}-\frac{q^{2n-1}+q^{n+1}-q^{n}-1}{q^{2}-1}

\displaystyle=q^{2n-8}(2q^{5}-q^{4}-4q^{3}+3q+2)+3\left(\frac{q^{2n-5}-q^{n-2}}{q^{2}-1}\right)-q^{n-3}

\displaystyle=q^{7}-q^{6}-6q^{5}+10q^{3}+q^{2}-5q-1

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
q\geq 7
and
\mathcal{C}_{4}
be a cubic threefold in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. If

|\mathcal{C}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{4}|>B_{4},

then
\mathcal{C}_{4}
contains a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

Proof.

Let us suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{4}
does not contain any hyperplane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. If
\mathcal{C}_{4}
contains no generator of
\mathcal{U}_{4}
or, if
\mathcal{C}_{4}
contains a plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, then by
[
7
, Proposition 4.1 and Proposition 4.2]
we conclude that
|\mathcal{C}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{4}|\leq 3(q^{5}+1)
. Thus, we may assume that
\mathcal{C}_{4}
contains a generator of
\mathcal{U}_{4}
, but no plane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
, defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. If
\mathcal{C}_{4}
contains a tangent line to
\mathcal{U}_{4}
then
[
7
, Proposition 4.8]
entails that
|\mathcal{C}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{4}|\leq 2q^{5}+2q^{4}+3q^{3}+2q^{2}+1\leq 3(q^{5}+1)
, as
q\geq 7
. Therefore for any plane
\Pi
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, the cubic plane curve
\mathcal{C}_{4}\cap\Pi
contains at most three generators of
\mathcal{U}_{4}
. If there exists a plane
\Pi
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{4}\cap\Pi
contains exactly three generators, then
[
7
, Proposition 4.3]
implies that
|\mathcal{C}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{4}|\leq 3q^{5}-q^{4}+3q^{3}+3q^{2}+1\leq 3(q^{5}+1)
, since

. Furthermore, for
q\geq 7
, if for any plane
\Pi
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}

contains at most two generators of
\mathcal{U}_{4}
, then from the proof of
[
7
, Proposition 5.6]

|\mathcal{C}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{4}|\leq 2q^{5}+5q^{4}-q^{3}+q^{2}-3q-4\leq 3(q^{5}+1)
. Thus if
\mathcal{C}_{4}
contains no hyperplane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}

.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
P
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational point of
\mathcal{U}_{n}
. The number of tangent hyperplanes to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
passing through
P
and defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is
q^{2}|\mathcal{U}_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|+1.

For any point
Q\in\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
, let

denote the tangent hyperplane to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
at
Q
. Now consider the set

\Gamma:=\{Q\in\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}):\ P\in T_{Q}(\mathcal{U}_{n})\}.

\Gamma=T_{P}(\mathcal{U}_{n})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
.

Proof of claim:
For any point
Q\in\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
, it follows from Theorem
2.3
(a) that

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
\mathcal{C}_{n}
be a cubic hypersurface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. If
|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|>B_{n}
, then there exists a hyperplane
\Sigma_{0}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that
\Sigma_{0}
is non-tangent to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
and
|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\Sigma_{0}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|>B_{n-1}
.

Proof.

Suppose that for every non-tangent hyperplane
\Sigma
to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
the following inequality is satisfied

|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\Sigma\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|\leq B_{n-1}.

Let us consider the incidence set:

\mathcal{I}=\{(P,\Sigma):P\in\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n},\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\ \text{is a $\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}$-hyperplane non-tangent to $\mathcal{U}_{n}$},P\in\Sigma\}.

We will count
|\mathcal{I}|
in two ways. First, by Lemma
3.6
, the number of tangent hyperplanes to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
passing through an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational point of
\mathcal{U}_{n}
is independent of the chosen point, so we see that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}}\#\{\Sigma:P\in\Sigma\ \ \text{and $\Sigma$ is non-tangent to $\mathcal{U}_{n}$ }\}

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|\bigg[\#\{\Sigma:P\in\Sigma\ ,\Sigma\ \text{is a hyperplane}\}-

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\#\{\Sigma:P\in\Sigma\ ,\Sigma\ \text{is a tangent hyperplane to $\mathcal{U}_{n}$}\}\bigg]

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|\left[\frac{q^{2n}-1}{q^{2}-1}-q^{2}|\mathcal{U}_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|-1\right]\ \ (\text{using Lemma \ref{numberoftangent}})

\displaystyle>B_{n}\frac{q^{2n}-q^{2}-q^{2}(q^{n-2}-(-1)^{n-2})(q^{n-1}-(-1)^{n-1})}{q^{2}-1}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\Sigma,\\
\text{$\Sigma$ is non-tangent}\\
\text{to $\mathcal{U}_{n}$}\end{subarray}}}|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\Sigma\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(|{\mathbb{P}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|-|\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|\right)B_{n-1}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{q^{2n+2}-1-(q^{n}-(-1)^{n})(q^{n+1}-(-1)^{n+1})}{q^{2}-1}\right]B_{n-1}.

Thus, comparing the two bounds on
\mathcal{I}
, we get

(2)

B_{n}<\left[\frac{q^{2n+2}-1-(q^{n}-(-1)^{n})(q^{n+1}-(-1)^{n+1})}{q^{2n}-q^{2}-q^{2}(q^{n-2}-(-1)^{n-2})(q^{n-1}-(-1)^{n-1})}\right]B_{n-1}.

We now distinguish two cases:

Case 1:

n
is even
. From Equation (
2
) we obtain

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle<\left[\frac{q^{2n+2}-1-(q^{n}-1)(q^{n+1}+1)}{q^{2n}-q^{2}-q^{2}(q^{n-2}-1)(q^{n-1}+1)}\right]B_{n-1}

\displaystyle=B_{n-1}q^{2}-B_{n-1}\frac{q^{n+3}-q^{n+2}-q^{n+1}+q^{n}}{q^{2n}-q^{2n-1}+q^{n+1}-q^{n}}

\displaystyle<B_{n-1}q^{2}-(q^{2n-5}+q^{2n-6})\frac{q^{n+3}-q^{n+2}-q^{n+1}+q^{n}}{q^{2n}-q^{2n-1}+q^{n+1}-q^{n}}\ \ (\text{using Corollary \ref{inq}})

\displaystyle=B_{n-1}q^{2}-\frac{q^{3n-2}-2q^{3n-4}+q^{3n-6}}{q^{2n}-q^{2n-1}+q^{n+1}-q^{n}}

\displaystyle=B_{n-1}q^{2}-q^{n-2}-\frac{q^{3n-3}-2q^{3n-4}+q^{3n-6}-q^{2n-1}+q^{2n-2}}{q^{2n}-q^{2n-1}+q^{n+1}-q^{n}}

\displaystyle<B_{n-1}q^{2}-q^{n-2}=B_{n},

which leads us to a contradiction.

Case 2:

n
is odd
. Equation (
2
) entails that

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle<\left[\frac{q^{2n+2}-1-(q^{n}+1)(q^{n+1}-1)}{q^{2n}-q^{2}-q^{2}(q^{n-2}+1)(q^{n-1}-1)}\right]B_{n-1}

\displaystyle=q^{2}B_{n-1}+B_{n-1}\frac{q^{n+3}-q^{n+2}-q^{n+1}+q^{n}}{q^{2n}-q^{2n-1}-q^{n+1}+q^{n}}

\displaystyle<q^{2}B_{n-1}+(3q^{2n-5}+q^{2n-6})\frac{q^{n+3}-q^{n+2}-q^{n+1}+q^{n}}{q^{2n}-q^{2n-1}-q^{n+1}+q^{n}}\ \ (\text{using Corollary \ref{inq}})

\displaystyle=q^{2}B_{n-1}+3\left[\frac{q^{3n-2}-q^{3n-3}-q^{3n-4}+q^{3n-5}}{q^{2n}-q^{2n-1}-q^{n+1}+q^{n}}\right]+\frac{q^{3n-3}-q^{3n-4}-q^{3n-5}+q^{3n-6}}{q^{2n}-q^{2n-1}-q^{n+1}+q^{n}}

\displaystyle=q^{2}B_{n-1}+3q^{n-2}+q^{n-3}-\frac{3q^{3n-4}-2q^{3n-5}-q^{3n-6}-3q^{2n-1}+2q^{2n-2}+q^{2n-3}}{q^{2n}-q^{2n-1}-q^{n+1}+q^{n}}

\displaystyle=q^{2}B_{n-1}+3q^{n-2}+q^{n-3}-\frac{(3q^{2}-2q-1)(q^{3n-6}-q^{2n-3})}{q^{2n}-q^{2n-1}-q^{n+1}+q^{n}}

\displaystyle<q^{2}B_{n-1}+3q^{n-2}+q^{n-3}=B_{n},

which is a contradiction to the definition of
B_{n}
. Thus, in both cases, we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{n}

{\mathbb{P}}^{n}

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}

. It follows from Theorem
3.5
that the Proposition is true for
n=4
. Suppose that the Proposition is true for
n-1
. It follows from the Proposition
3.7

\Sigma_{0}
to
\mathcal{U}_{n}

\Sigma_{0}

\mathcal{U}_{n}

{\mathbb{P}}^{n}

(3)

\displaystyle\leq|\Pi(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|+2(q^{2}+1)(q+1)q^{2n-6}.

n
is even.

3
) entails that

Case 2:

n
is odd.
In this case
|\Pi(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|\leq|\Pi_{1}\mathcal{U}_{n-4}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|=\frac{q^{2n-3}+q^{n+1}-q^{n}-1}{q^{2}-1}
. Thus, from Equation (
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{2n-3}+q^{n+1}-q^{n}-1}{q^{2}-1}+2(q^{2}+1)(q+1)q^{2n-6}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}\left[q^{2n-6}(2q^{5}+2q^{4}+q^{3}-2q-2)+q^{n}(q-1)-1\right].

Hence, we have

\displaystyle B_{n}-|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|

\displaystyle\geq q^{2n-8}(3q^{5}+3)+3\frac{q^{2n-5}-q^{n-2}}{q^{2}-1}+q^{n-3}

\displaystyle\ \ -\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}\left[q^{2n-6}(2q^{5}+2q^{4}+q^{3}-2q-2)+q^{n}(q-1)-1\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}\big[q^{2n-8}(q^{7}-2q^{6}-4q^{5}+5q^{3}+5q^{2}-3)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-q^{n-3}(q^{4}-q^{3}-q^{2}+3q+1)+1\big]

\displaystyle>0.

The last inequality follows since
(q^{7}-2q^{6}-4q^{5}+5q^{3}+5q^{2}-3)-(q^{4}-q^{3}-q^{2}+3q+1)>0
.
Therefore
B_{n}>|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|
, a contradiction to the hypothesis. This completes the proof.

∎

We now present the main theorem of this section, which characterizes cubic hypersurfaces in projective space under a given cardinality condition. Building on Proposition
3.9
, this result establishes that such hypersurfaces must decompose into a union of three hyperplanes when the specified bound is exceeded.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{n}
be a cubic hypersurface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with
n\geq 4
and
q\geq 7
. If
|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|>B_{n}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is the union of three hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, each defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
3.9
that
\mathcal{C}_{n}
contains a hyperplane
\Sigma
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Let us suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{n}=\Sigma\cup\mathcal{Q}_{n}
, where
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
is a quadric hypersurface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. If
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
is an irreducible quadric, then
[
1
, Theorem 3.3]
entails that
|\mathcal{Q}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|\leq A_{n}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle B_{n}<|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq|\Sigma(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|+|\mathcal{Q}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq|\Sigma(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|+A_{n},

which is a contradiction to Lemma
3.4
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Structure of cubic hypersurfaces

The main goal of this section is to determine the structure of a cubic hypersurface sharing the maximum number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational points with
\mathcal{U}_{n}
. To this end, let us first prove the following two lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\Sigma_{0}
is a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
that is not tangent to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
, then for any hyperplane
\Sigma\neq\Sigma_{0}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, the intersection
\Sigma\cap\Sigma_{0}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}
is either
\mathcal{U}_{n-2}
, a non-degenerate Hermitian variety of rank
n-1
, or
\Pi_{0}\mathcal{U}_{n-3}
, a degenerate Hermitian variety of rank
n-2
.

Proof.

As
\Sigma_{0}\neq\Sigma
, so
\Sigma_{0}\cap\Sigma
is a hyperplane in the projective space
\Sigma_{0}\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
. Moreover, since
\Sigma_{0}
is non-tangent to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
, it follows from Theorem
2.3
(b) that
\Sigma_{0}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}
is a non-degenerate Hermitian variety contained in
\Sigma_{0}
. Thus,
\Sigma\cap\Sigma_{0}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}=(\Sigma\cap\Sigma_{0})\cap(\Sigma_{0}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n})
is a Hermitian variety either of rank
n-1
, or of rank
n-2
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
n\geq 4
and
T_{P}(\mathcal{U}_{n})
,
T_{Q}(\mathcal{U}_{n})
be two tangent hyperplanes to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
at two distinct
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational points
P
and
Q
respectively. Then the intersection
T_{P}(\mathcal{U}_{n})\cap T_{Q}(\mathcal{U}_{n})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}
is either a non-degenerate Hermitian variety
\mathcal{U}_{n-2}
of rank
n-1
, or a degenerate Hermitian variety
\Pi_{1}\mathcal{U}_{n-4}
of rank
n-3
.

Proof.

The intersection
T_{P}(\mathcal{U}_{n})\cap T_{Q}(\mathcal{U}_{n})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}
can never be a cone
\Pi_{0}\mathcal{U}_{n-3}
. Indeed, if it were a cone with vertex at some point, say
R
, take two lines
\ell
and
m
within the cone. Since
\ell,m\subseteq T_{P}(\mathcal{U}_{n})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}
, it follows that
P\in\ell\cap m
. Likewise,
Q\in\ell\cap m
. This implies
R=P=Q
, leading to a contradiction. Now, the lemma follows from Corollary
2.7
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
n\geq 4
be an even integer and
\mathcal{C}_{n}
be a cubic hypersurface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with
q\geq 7
. Then
|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|\leq 3|\mathcal{U}_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|-2|\mathcal{U}_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|
. Moreover, this bound is attained by a cubic hypersurface
\mathcal{C}_{n}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is a union of three distinct non-tangent hyperplanes
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
and
\Sigma_{3}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, such that each of the hyperplanes contains a common
(n-2)
-space
\Pi_{n-2}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and
\Pi_{n-2}
intersects
\mathcal{U}_{n}
at a non-degenerate Hermitian variety.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
3.10
, it is enough to consider the cubic hypersurfaces, which are the union of three distinct hyperplanes defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{n}=\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}\cup\Sigma_{3}
be such a cubic hypersurface. We now distinguish two cases:

Case 1:

\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
and
\Sigma_{3}
intersect in a subspace
\Pi_{n-2}
having codimension two.
In this case,

(4)

|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\Sigma_{i}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|\right)-2|\Pi_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|.

If
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
and
\Sigma_{3}
are all tangents to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
, then from Remark
2.8
and Equation (
4
) it follows

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq 3\left(q^{2}|\mathcal{U}_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|+1\right)-2|\Pi_{1}\mathcal{U}_{n-4}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|

(5)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2n-3}(3q^{2}-2)-q^{n}(q-1)-1}{q^{2}-1}.

Now suppose that at least one hyperplane is non-tangent to
\mathcal{U}_{n}

2.8

|\Pi_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|\geq|\mathcal{U}_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|

4

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq 3|\mathcal{U}_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|-2|\mathcal{U}_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|

(6)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2n-3}(3q^{2}-2)+q^{n-2}(3q^{2}-q-2)-1}{q^{2}-1}.

Comparing Equations (
5

6

|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|\leq 3|\mathcal{U}_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|-2|\mathcal{U}_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|,

and the bound is attained in this case only if all the hyperplanes are non-tangent to

and their intersection meets the Hermitian variety
\mathcal{U}_{n}
at a non-degenerate Hermitian variety.

Case 2:

\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
and
\Sigma_{3}
intersect in a subspace
\Pi_{n-3}
having codimension three.
In this case,

(7)

|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|=\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\Sigma_{i}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}|\Sigma_{i}\cap\Sigma_{j}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|+|\Pi_{n-3}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|.

If
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
and
\Sigma_{3}
are all tangents to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
, then from Equation (
7

2.8

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\Sigma_{i}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|-|\Sigma_{1}\cap\Sigma_{2}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|-|\Sigma_{1}\cap\Sigma_{3}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|

\displaystyle\leq 3\left(q^{2}|\mathcal{U}_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|+1\right)-2|\Pi_{1}\mathcal{U}_{n-4}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|

\displaystyle<3|\mathcal{U}_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|-2|\mathcal{U}_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|.

The last inequality has already been seen in Case 1. Now suppose that at least one hyperplane, say

. It follows from Lemma

that

\displaystyle<3|\mathcal{U}_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|-2|\mathcal{U}_{n-2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|\ \ (\text{using Remark \ref{cardinality of codim two}}).

Theorem 4.4

\mathcal{C}_{n}
is a union of three distinct tangent hyperplanes
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
and

, such that each of the hyperplanes contains a common
(n-2)
-space
\Pi_{n-2}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and

Let
\mathcal{C}_{n}=\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}\cup\Sigma_{3}

\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
and

intersect in a subspace

having codimension two.

|\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}|=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\Sigma_{i}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|\right)-2|\Pi_{n-2}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|.

Suppose

and

are all tangents to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
. It follows from Lemma

and Remark

that

Let at least one of the hyperplanes, say
\Sigma_{1}
, be non-tangent to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
. Therefore Equation (

and

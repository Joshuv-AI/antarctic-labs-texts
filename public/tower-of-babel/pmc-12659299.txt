High serum isthmin-1 as a biomarker for the progression of renal function decline in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus
Authors: Chuan Wang, Yifei Cheng, Mingyue Xu, Li Chen, Jinbo Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo determine the relationship between serum Ism-1 levels and the progression of renal function decline in patients with T2DM.MethodsThis prospective longitudinal study included 223 patients with T2DM. Patients were divided into quartiles according to serum Ism-1 levels. A Cox proportional hazards ratio model was applied to analyze the relationship between CKD progression and serum Ism-1 levels.ResultsA total of 197 patients were finally analyzed, and the mean length of follow-up was 40.86 months. The serum Ism-1 levels were associated with eGFR decline and an increased risk of composite renal outcomes after adjusting for age, gender, body mass index, diabetes duration, history of hypertension, fasting blood glucose, fasting C-peptide, total cholesterol, triglycerides, drinking, smoking and follow-up period (all p < 0.05). However, the predictive ability of Ism-1 for composite renal outcomes disappeared after adjusting for baseline eGFR in Cox regression analysis and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.ConclusionsHigh serum Ism-1 may be a biomarker for the progression of renal function decline in patients with T2DM.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Keywords: Isthmin-1, Biomarker, Chronic kidney disease, Type 2 diabetes mellitus

Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is diagnosed by the persistent elevation of albuminuria, low estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), or other manifestations of kidney damage. It affects approximately 20–40% of patients with diabetes [1]. CKD progresses gradually and may ultimately lead to kidney failure, requiring dialysis or kidney transplantation, which significantly increases healthcare costs [2]. Moreover, individuals with type 1 or type 2 diabetes who develop CKD face a higher risk of end-stage renal disease (ESRD), atherosclerotic cardiovascular disorders, heart failure,and premature mortality [3, 4].

The imperative for early screening and intervention in DKD is underscored by its treatment dynamics. Early and timely management of glycemia and hypertension can markedly slow,even prevent renal deterioration, reducing cardiovascular and ESRD risks. However, once manifested by overt albuminuria or a progressive eGFR decline, the disease reflects extensive structural injury. At this late stage, management can only aim to delay ESRD, as cardiovascular complications and mortality rates rise sharply [5]. This critical window for effective intervention highlights the vital importance of early detection.Given the limitations of serum creatinine and UACR in clinical practice [6], significant research interest has grown in recent years regarding novel biomarkers for predicting the progression of renal function,such as neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL) [7], kidney injury molecule (KIM)-1 [8]. Recently, the effects of adipokines on CKD have also been proposed, such as adiponectin [9]. Despite these advances, a pressing need remains for the discovery of novel biomarkers to improve early clinical diagnosis of DKD.

Isthmin-1 (Ism-1) is a novel adipokine that was originally identified in the Xenopus midbrain- hindbrain organizer, termed the isthmus [10]. Previous studies have demonstrated the roles of Ism-1 in angiogenesis, aging, cancer, insulin resistance, inflammation and glucose-lipid metabolism [11–14]. Under normal conditions, Ism-1 is specifically expressed in podocytes, and further upregulated during the progression of nephrotic syndrome. It has been proved to play certain roles in early renal development by promoting cell-cell adhesion via interaction with Integrin α8β1 [15]. In renal pathophysiology, Ism-1 interacts with two glomerular receptors, GRP78 and integrin αvβ5, triggering mitochondrial membrane depolarization in podocytes through both caspase-dependent and -independent pathways. This mechanism accelerates the progression of focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) [16].

Our previous studies have shown that serum Ism-1levels were associated with CKD in patients with T2DM [17]. However, these prior studies were limited by cross-sectional design. To further elucidate the relationship between serum Ism-1 and renal outcomes, we conducted a follow-up study of the original cohort. The present study aimed to evaluate whether high Ism-1 levels could serve as a predictive biomarker for kidney function impairment in patients with T2DM.

Methods

Study design and subjects

This is a prospective cohort study initiated with baseline data derived from a cross-sectional investigation. A total of 223 patients were enrolled between October 2020 and January 2022 during the baseline phase, with specific inclusion/exclusion criteria and recruitment protocols detailed in our prior publication [17, 18]. All baseline participants underwent scheduled follow-up assessments between August 2024 and March 2025, with a standardized protocol for clinical and laboratory evaluations. Patients with baseline eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73 m2 (n = 5) or mortality (n = 12) or loss of follow-up (n = 9) were excluded, resulting in 197 participants included in the final analytical cohort. This study was approved by the ethics committee of the Qilu Hospital of Shandong University (2021–289). All procedures were performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and informed consent was obtained from all subjects.

Clinical data collection

Follow-up time was calculated from the date of completing the baseline serum Ism-1 levels measurement to the end of follow-up, the date of the initiation of renal replacement therapy (RRT), or lost to follow up, whichever was earliest. Demographics, clinical parameters, and medical history were systematically documented during the baseline investigation. Fasting blood samples were collected during follow-up visits for the measurement of serum creatinine levels using standardized enzymatic assays. eGFR was calculated using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD- EPI) equation [19].

Composite renal outcome

Composite renal outcome was defined as a > 30% decline in eGFR, ESRD, or RRT during the follow-up period. [20–22].

Statistical analyses

All patients were divided into quartiles according to their serum Ism-1 levels. Patient characteristics are presented as mean±SD or median (IQR) for continuous variables and as percentages for categorical variables. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was conducted to determine whether the variables were normally distributed. Comparisons between groups were evaluated using one- way analysis of variance and most significant difference post hoc test for continuous variables with normal distribution, Kruskal-Wallis test for continuous data with skewed distribution and Pearson χ2 test for categorical variables. Linear regression analyses were performed to explore the relationship between the Ism- 1 levels and eGFR decline. Cox regression analysis was used to investigate the associations of serum Ism-1 or Ism-1 quartiles with composite renal outcome.During model construction, multicollinearity was assessed to evaluate dependencies among the independent variables. Diagnostic metrics, including tolerance and the variance inflation factor (VIF), indicated no significant multicollinearity among the included factors. Kaplan-Meier curve was constructed to identify serum isthmin-1 as a predictor for composite renal outcome. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses were used to evaluate the predictive efficacy of Ism-1 for composite renal outcome All analyses were conducted using SPSS V.28.0 and R language (version 3.4.4; R Foundation for Statistical Computing). Graphs were constructed using the GraphPad Prism software. p value < 0.05 was considered as significant.

The association between serum Ism-1 levels and composite renal outcome

During follow-up, a total of 34 (17.3%) patients experienced a composite renal outcome. The Kaplan-Meier curves (Fig. 1) revealed that the higher quartile of serum Ism-1 was associated with poor composite renal outcome (p < 0.001). A Log-rank test for trend was performed and revealed a statistically significant trend across the groups (χ2 = 26.22, df = 1, p < 0.0001).Then Cox regression analysis was used to investigate the associations of serum Ism-1 or Ism-1 quartiles with composite renal outcome. Interestingly, before adjusting for baseline eGFR, both serum Ism-1 and highest Ism-1 quartiles showed significantly increased HRs, even after incorporating age, gender, BMI, diabetes duration, history of hypertension, FBG, FCP, TC, TG, drinking, smoking and follow-up period into the model (Tables 3, 4). However, after adjusting for baseline eGFR, the power of Ism-1 as a predictor of composite renal outcome disappeared.

Discussion

In this study, we demonstrated that the high serum Ism-1 levels were significantly associated with eGFR decline. The highest serum Ism-1 quartile was also associated with an increased risk of composite renal outcome on multivariable analysis in the cohort. However, after adjusting for the baseline eGFR, the power of Ism-1 as a predictor of renal outcomes disappeared.

In our previous study, we found that serum Ism-1 levels were high in diabetic patients with albuminuria and were positively associated with UACR in patients with T2DM [17], and were inversely associated with eGFR [18], which suggested that Ism-1 might play a certain role in the occurrence and development of CKD. However, the mechanisms underlying the association between high serum Ism-1 and renal function decline are not fully understood.

Ism-1 was first identified in Xenopus laevis gastrula embryos by an unbiased secretion cloning screen and was described as a member of the FGF8 synexpression group because of its prominent expression location in the isthmus region of the brain [23, 24]. As a secreted protein, Ism-1 is characterized by two functional domains: one is a central thrombospondin type I repeats (TSR) [25] domain, which is implicated in substantial fundamental physiological processes including cell-cell/cell-ECM interactions, tumor metastasis, angiogenesis, TGF-β activation; the other is a C-terminal adhesion-associated domain in MUC4 and other proteins (AMOP) [26] domain, which is associated with cell adhesion and tumor invasion [24]. In recent years, Ism-1 has garnered significant scientific interest owing to its pivotal roles in modulating anti-inflammatory responses and metabolic homeostasis. Mechanically, Ism-1 can improve glucose tolerance and inhibit lipid synthesis [12], and clinical research found that the increase of serum Ism-1 was associated with a decreased risk of DM, and it did not reduce the risk of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease in diabetic patients [27]. It has also been demonstrated that Ism-1, as an endogenous anti-inflammation factor, could inhibit nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) activation and proinflammatory cytokine/chemokine production in acute lung injury [28] while inducing apoptosis of alveolar macrophage apoptosis [29]. Although Ism-1 is capable of modulating vascular permeability [30, 31], experimental studies have shown that mice under different dose injections of Ism-1 showed significantly increased lung vascular permeability in a dose-dependent manner, while no such alterations were observed in the kidney [32]. Comprehensive ligand-receptor interactome mapping through single-cell RNA sequencing of murine embryonic kidneys (E10.5-E11.5) revealed spatiotemporal co-expression patterns between Ism-1 and glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (Gdnf), with both molecules modulating kidney branching morphogenesis. Through horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-induced proximity labelling, integrin α8β1 was further identified as a receptor of Ism-1 in the E11.5 kidney, and it was demonstrated that cell-cell adhesion was promoted by Ism-1 through its interaction with Integrin α8β1,which emphasized the important role of Ism-1 in early kidney development [15]. While the functional implications of Ism-1 in renal physiology remain poorly characterized, current evidence is insufficient to elucidate its potential involvement in the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying CKD.

In this study, higher serum Ism-1 levels were associated with an increased risk of composite renal outcome in T2DM patients after adjusting for accepted risk factors including age, gender, BMI, diabetes duration, history of hypertension, fasting blood glucose, fasting C-peptide, total cholesterol, triglycerides, drinking, smoking and follow-up period. However, after adjusting for the baseline eGFR, the power of Ism-1 as a predictor of composite renal outcomes disappeared. We speculate that serum Ism-1 is a biomarker of renal dysfunction rather than a true risk factor, intimately involved in the progression of renal function decline. We hypothesize that Ism-1 may undergo renal excretion, and its systemic clearance could diminish with reduced eGFR. We also attempted to measure urinary Ism-1 levels, however, the concentrations were below the assay’s lower detection limit, hindering the confirmation of this hypothesis. Currently, no studies have directly quantified urinary Ism-1 concentrations or excretion rates, and its precise excretion pathways and in vivo clearance mechanisms remain uncharacterized. Analogously, high serum adiponectin is noted in several conditions of CKD and is a predictor for end stage renal disease, while the prediction capability of adiponectin would disappear once eGFR is considered [33]. Notably, even if Ism-1 is indeed renally excreted, its potential physiological role in renal pathologies cannot be definitively excluded (as noted above). Whether Ism-1 contributes to the pathophysiology of renal injury in patients with T2DM remains unresolved. Further investigation is needed to identify the critical position of Ism-1 in the pathogenesis of CKD.

Compared to other recently highlighted biomarkers such as KIM-1 and NGAL, Ism-1 possesses distinct advantages. Both KIM-1 and NGAL are primarily secreted by renal tubular epithelial cells and are recognized as promising biomarkers for tubular injury [34–36]. Both are sensitive indicators of acute kidney injury (AKI).Their long-term expression patterns, significance, and potential implications in the context of chronic kidney injury remain to be fully elucidated [36]. Under normal conditions, Ism-1 is specifically expressed in podocytes and interacts with two glomerular receptors [16], offering a particular advantage in the detection of glomerular injury.The pathological progression of DKD initiates with glomerular hyperfiltration and hyperperfusion, advancing to glomerular injury and subsequently to tubular damage [37]. Given this sequence, Ism-1 holds significant promise for detecting functional alterations in the early phases of DKD, offering considerable potential for early diagnosis and timely intervention.

Our study has some limitations, including a relatively small sample size, short-term follow-up with regard to disease outcomes. The small sample size constrains the statistical power and external validity of our results, elevates the risk of Type II error, and prevented meaningful subgroup analyses to investigate outcome variations across patient demographics. Additionally, the follow-up duration was likely insufficient, which may have compromised the accuracy of our survival analyses. Despite these limitations, our findings highlight the potential of Ism-1 as a predictor of renal function. Crucially,confirming its role necessitates validation through larger, prospective, multi-center cohorts with longer-term follow-up. Further studies are needed to investigate the underlying mechanisms of Ism-1 in the decline of kidney function. Furthermore, as no renal biopsy was done during recruitment, patients with T2DM and non-diabetic kidney disease may be involved, so the relationship between serum Ism-1 levels and eGFR in this study cannot rule out the impact of possible existing non-diabetic nephropathy. Moreover, all subjects were of Chinese ethnicity. Whether these results are generalizable to other populations requires further investigation.

In conclusion, high serum Ism-1 levels are associated with composite renal outcome in T2DM patients. These findings suggest that serum Ism-1 may be a biomarker rather than a true risk factor in CKD progression.

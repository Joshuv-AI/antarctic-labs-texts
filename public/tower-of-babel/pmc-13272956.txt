Dynamical analysis and exact solitary wave solutions of -dimensional integro-differential Jaulent-Miodek equation using two analytical schemes
Authors: Bakhtawar Nasir, Muhammad Abbas, Muhammad Zain Yousaf, Tahir Nazir, Magda Abd El-Rahman, Asnake Birhanu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research explores wave phenomena in the -dimensional integro-differential Jaulent–Miodek equation associated with dispersive nonlinear systems. The primary objective of this study is to derive and analyze exact wave solutions of the governing model using the generalized Arnous approach and the modified simple equation method. By applying a suitable travelling-wave transformation, the nonlinear partial differential equation is reduced to an ordinary differential equation, which is then solved analytically using the proposed techniques. The results reveal a rich variety of nonlinear wave structures such as kink, anti-kink, bright, dark, bell-shaped, anti-bell, periodic, singular periodic, V-shaped, W-shaped, and singular wave solutions. The physical characteristics of the obtained solutions are illustrated through two-dimensional, three-dimensional, and contour plots generated using Mathematica. Furthermore, the dynamical behavior of the system is investigated through bifurcation analysis using two-dimensional phase portraits, while chaotic dynamics are examined via corresponding three-dimensional phase portraits together with time-series plots to identify transitions from periodic to quasi-periodic and chaotic behavior. In addition, sensitivity analysis is carried out using two-dimensional phase portraits to examine the dependence of the system response on initial conditions. The findings confirm the effectiveness and applicability of the proposed analytical methods for constructing exact solutions of higher-dimensional nonlinear evolution equations and provide deeper insight into nonlinear wave propagation phenomena arising in optics, plasma physics, and fluid flows.Keywords: -dimensional integro-differential Jaulent–Miodek equation, Generalized Arnous approach, Modified simple equation method, Solitary waves, Bifurcation and sensitivity analyses, Chaotic dynamics, Phase portraitsSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Partial differential equations (PDEs) describe various complex physical phenomena encompassing fluid dynamics, light propagation, plasma behavior, and ecological interactions. Such phenomena have been modeled using nonlinear governing equations that capture intricate interdependence and interactions within the systems. In particular, nonlinear partial differential equations (NPDEs) serve as powerful mathematical tools for modeling a wide range of physical processes including ion-acoustic wave propagation in plasmas with quasi-electrons, superfluid dynamics, irregular wave behavior in paramagnetic media, and shallow-water wave simulations. Accordingly, the family of nonlinear evolution equations (NEEs) has formed a fundamental subclass describing the temporal evolution of dynamical structures. This class of equations has recently become one of the most important objects of mathematical physics and applied mathematics. The NEEs frequently arise in higher-dimensional wave models as well as in wave-supporting media. Owing to this broad range of applications, the described class plays a central role in nonlinear science research and enables the modeling of phenomena that cannot be adequately approximated by linear models particularly when interactions, feedback mechanisms, and other nonlinear effects are substantial.

Most PDEs are not easily solvable analytically, which has motivated the development of several approaches to obtain numerical simulations1,2. However, some classical and fractional PDEs have been solved exactly by analytical methods such as the Lie symmetry method3,4, the tanh method5, the Kudryashov method6,7, and several other related techniques8. Numerical approaches for solving these models may include finite difference, finite element, spectral methods, and various others. These approaches primarily involve discretizing the governing differential equation and approximating the derivatives through either approximation or functional expansions. Both closed-form and computational solutions of PDE-based systems have significantly enhanced developments across science and engineering. In addition to the development of analytical and numerical tools, understanding the qualitative nature of solutions to PDEs is equally important, since their behavior strongly depends on the structure of the framework and the imposed initial or boundary constraints. The NPDEs support diverse wave phenomena including solitons that propagate without dispersion while preserving their shape and velocity, as well as propagating, traveling, standing, and periodic waves. Due to their robustness against perturbations and interactions, periodic coherent structures are widely used in the study of spatiotemporal evolution and signal propagation in photonic systems, extended-interaction materials, Bose–Einstein condensates, and negative-index media.

Recent studies in nonlinear science have significantly advanced the analysis of NPDEs through the combination of analytical solution techniques and dynamical system investigations. For instance, Riaz et al.9 studied nonlinear vibrations in embedded single-walled carbon nanotubes by employing the Khater method and obtained various wave structures including bright, dark, and kink solitons. Their work further incorporated bifurcation and chaotic analyses through Poincaré maps and Lyapunov exponents to examine the influence of physical parameters on system dynamics. Similarly, Tariq et al.10 analyzed a coupled nonlinear Schrödinger equation using the modified Khater and Sardar subequation methods and derived various analytical solutions including trigonometric, exponential, and hyperbolic wave structures. In addition, the authors examined phase portraits, sensitivity analysis, and chaotic behavior to better understand nonlinear wave propagation dynamics. Furthermore, Tariq et al.11 investigated the nonlinear third-order Klein–Fock–Gordon equation and constructed dark, bright, kink, and periodic optical solitons using the modified Khater and improved modified Sardar subequation methods. Their findings demonstrated that the integration of analytical techniques with dynamical tools, including phase portraits, sensitivity analysis, and chaotic investigations, provides deeper insight into nonlinear wave interactions and stability behavior.

The integro-differential Jaulent–Miodek equation (IDJME) is one of the important NPDEs that has been widely employed to describe many phenomena occurring in optics, condensed matter physics, and fluid mechanics. In 1976, M. Jaulent and I. Miodek first derived this equation as a coupled system using the inverse scattering transform with energy-dependent Schrödinger potentials. JM-type equations are physically significant and have therefore attracted considerable research attention. The JM hierarchy consists of four well-known models, as follows12:

These include wave propagation in nonlinear optical media, internal waves in stratified fluids, shallow water dynamics, and plasma oscillations. The balance between nonlinearity and dispersion in the JM system leads to the formation of stable, localized soliton structures that behave as particle-like wave packets and preserve their shape during propagation and interaction. Due to these properties, the JM hierarchy plays a significant role in studying energy transmission and stability in nonlinear optical fibers as well as conservation laws in fluid dynamics. Moreover, its integrable nature provides a powerful framework for analyzing nonlinear wave behavior in two-dimensional systems, making it an important model in nonlinear science.

A review of the existing literature reveals numerous investigations on the JM equation and its hierarchy, emphasizing their significance in various studies. For instance, Ige and Oderinu13 applied the Elzaki transform along with Adomian polynomials, and validated the results against those derived from Adomian’s decomposition method to compute numerical solutions of the coupled JM equation. Akkilic et al.14 investigated the -dimensional JM model using the -expansion method combined with the extended rational sine–cosine approach. Zayed and Rahman15 solved the -dimensional JM equations using the extended tanh method. In addition, Shen et al.16 obtained new solitary solutions of the time-fractional coupled JM equation through the and modified -expansion techniques. Raza et al.17 employed the modified sub-equation and modified auxiliary equation techniques to derive various wave structures including periodic waves, bright solitons, kink solitons, and singular solitons for the -dimensional JM equation. Later, Liu and Wang18 studied exotic wave structures arising in the Riemann problem for the higher-order JM equation. Several other analytical and computational techniques for the JM equation have also been reported in the literature19,20.

In the present study, the -dimensional JM equation is investigated via the generalized Arnous (GA) approach and the modified simple equation (MSE) method. Applying a travelling-wave transformation converts the PDE into an ordinary differential equation (ODE), enabling the construction of various closed-form solutions. The obtained analytical results reveal diverse nonlinear wave structures including kink and anti-kink profiles, bright–dark solitons, bell-shaped and anti-bell configurations, V- and W-shaped forms, periodic waves, as well as singular periodic solitons and singular wave solutions. These solutions are further investigated through qualitative dynamical analyses including bifurcation, sensitivity, and chaotic behavior to examine the influence of the governing parameters on the wave profiles, stability, and interaction characteristics. The combined analytical and graphical results demonstrate that the employed techniques provide an efficient and reliable framework for investigating the complex dynamics of the JM system.

Such variations may result in the emergence of new stable equilibrium states, periodic orbits, or chaotic dynamics. Bifurcation theory provides a systematic framework for analyzing these abrupt transitions and for predicting how system dynamics evolve under different parameter conditions.

The corresponding reduced system admits a variety of trajectory configurations in phase space including fixed points, closed orbits, and deformed closed curves. Physical interpretation and applications

The JM equation has its origins in the early development of nonlinear wave theory and integrable systems studied during the 1970 s and 1980s. The pioneering investigations on NEEs and soliton theory provided a theoretical foundation for describing complex nonlinear phenomena in multidimensional dispersive media. Such equations have been widely used as mathematical models for understanding wave propagation mechanisms in various physical environments.

The generalized -dimensional IDJME considered in this work incorporates nonlinear interactions together with dispersive and transverse effects, making it capable of describing more realistic wave processes occurring in nature. These processes occur in various physical contexts including nonlinear fluid dynamics, plasma physics, nonlinear optics, and other complex dispersive media, where multidimensional wave interactions play an important role. The -dimensional form of the equation is obtained through scaling and non-dimensionalization procedures, which allow the governing dynamics to be expressed in a mathematically tractable and physically meaningful manner.

The exact analytical solutions obtained in this work, including kink, anti-kink, bright, dark, V-shaped, W-shaped, periodic, and singular wave structures, are physically significant in describing nonlinear wave propagation phenomena in real systems. In fluid dynamics, such solutions model the evolution of long internal waves and surface waves in stratified fluids, where the balance between nonlinearity and dispersion leads to stable or steep wave formations. In plasma physics, these solutions represent ion-acoustic and electrostatic wave structures in magnetized plasma, where different soliton profiles correspond to localized disturbances in plasma density and electric potential. In nonlinear optics, bright and dark solitons describe pulse propagation in optical fibers, while kink-type solutions model switching waves in optical communication systems. They provide useful insight into wave propagation mechanisms in nonlinear dispersive media and may help in understanding phenomena arising in fluid dynamics, plasma physics, and optical communication systems. Limitations of the present work

Although the GA and MSE methods provide an effective analytical framework for constructing exact solutions of the -dimensional JM equation, the present study has certain limitations.

Both methods are applicable to reducible forms of NEEs, which limits their applicability to strongly non-integrable or highly complex nonlinear systems. In addition, the GA method relies on a predefined auxiliary function, which reduces the diversity of admissible solution structures. Although the MSE method is more flexible, it often leads to highly nonlinear algebraic systems that are computationally demanding.

Conclusion

In order to solve the -dimensional JM equation, the GA approach and the MSE method are applied in this work. Using these methods, various analytical solutions of the given NPDE are obtained. Various types of nonlinear wave profiles including kink waves, anti-kink waves, bright, dark, bell, and anti-bell solitons, V-shaped, W-shaped, periodic waves, singular periodic solitons, and singular waves, are derived and exhibited through graphical representations, demonstrating their unique features according to specific parameters of the model under consideration. The obtained nonlinear wave structures are illustrated through two-dimensional, three-dimensional, and contour plots presented in Figures 1–12, which demonstrate the influence of the governing parameters on the qualitative nature of the solutions. The proposed approaches are capable of describing various types of nonlinear behavior of waves in higher dimensions. The obtained results are also relevant to physical applications in fluid dynamics, plasma physics, and nonlinear wave propagation, where such nonlinear structures are commonly observed. In addition to the development of analytical solutions of the system by using GA and MSE techniques, the dynamical behavior of the system has been examined. In bifurcation analysis, specific values of parameters are determined at which qualitative changes in the system dynamics occur, i.e., at which equilibrium points appear or disappear. Chaotic behavior of the system is also observed for specific values of parameters, indicating the complex and random nature of the system. Sensitivity analysis indicates that small variations in initial conditions result in large differences in the system response, revealing the complex dependence on initial conditions. Furthermore, the bifurcation analysis shown in Figure 13 reveals the existence and transition of equilibrium states under different parameter choices. The chaotic and sensitivity analyses illustrated in Figures 14–20 confirm the complex dynamical characteristics and strong dependence of the system on initial conditions. In future work, the applied approaches can be extended to more complex NEEs, including variable-coefficient models and perturbed systems arising in real physical applications. Moreover, the incorporation of fractional-order derivatives and stochastic effects can provide deeper insight into the underlying dynamics. The present analytical results can also be complemented with numerical and stability analyses to further investigate their physical applicability and robustness.

Long time evolution of a pair of 2D viscous point vortices
Ping Zhang, Yibin Zhang

Abstract
. This paper studies the long-time evolution of two point vortices under the 2D Navier–Stokes equations. Starting from initial data given by a pair of Dirac measures, we derive an asymptotic expansion for the vorticity over time scales significantly longer than the advection time, yet shorter than the diffusion time. Building on previous works [ 16 , 7 ] , we construct suitable approximate solutions Ω a \Omega_{a} and employ Arnold’s method to define a nonlinear energy functional E ε ​ [ ω ] E_{\varepsilon}[\omega] , with respect to which the linearized operator Λ E , ⋆ \Lambda^{E,\star} around Ω a \Omega_{a} is nearly skew-adjoint. A key innovation in this work is the introduction of “pseudo-momenta”: ϱ a e , ϱ a o , ϱ a t ​ e , ϱ a t ​ o \varrho^{e}_{a},\varrho^{o}_{a},\varrho^{te}_{a},\varrho^{to}_{a} , which correspond to eigenfunctions or other nontrivial elements in invariant subspaces of Λ E \Lambda^{E} , derived from the Lie structure of the 2D Euler equations. In particular, by transitioning to a co-moving frame that tracks the trajectories of the vortex centers and introducing the “frame stream function” X ^ \hat{X} and its associated “frame derivative” X = { X ^ , ⋅ } V X=\{\hat{X},\cdot\}_{V} , we obtain the relation { ϱ a e , Ω a E } V ≈ X ​ Ω a E \{\varrho^{e}_{a},\Omega^{E}_{a}\}_{V}\approx X\Omega^{E}_{a} , which is shown to be an important eigenfunction of Λ a E \Lambda^{E}_{a} of eigenvalue zero. Imposing orthogonality of the perturbation ω \omega with respect to these carefully chosen “pseudo-momenta” enables us to prove the coercivity of E ε ​ [ ω ] E_{\varepsilon}[\omega] . Finally, we estimate the error between Ω a \Omega_{a} and the true solution by projecting it onto the directions spanned by these “pseudo-momenta” and their orthogonal complement, and then analyzing the evolution of each projection. Our analysis reveals how the vortex centers evolve and how the solution deviates from a simple superposition of Oseen vortices, generalizing previous result [ 7 ] and providing a refined description of metastable vortex dynamics in the small viscosity regime.



1.
introduction

The study of coherent vortex structures in two-dimensional incompressible flows, which refers to organized, swirling patterns of fluid motion that persist in time and space, has long been a central topic in fluid dynamics, both from a theoretical and applied perspective. At high Reynolds numbers, such structures often persist over long time intervals and dominate the transport and mixing properties of the flow.
Far from being mere theoretical concepts, these structures are ubiquitous in nature and technology. They are the invisible architects behind the familiar: from the swirling eddies that shed off a bridge pier in a river, influencing erosion and sediment transport, to the powerful wingtip vortices generated by aircraft, which dictate safe separation distances during takeoff and landing. In the atmosphere, large-scale coherent vortices define weather systems, including hurricanes and tornadoes, while in engineering, controlling them is key to enhancing the efficiency of turbines and reducing drag on vehicles. Essentially, these coherent structures are the fundamental building blocks of fluid flow, connecting abstract theory to the dynamic world we live in.

A natural entry into this field is the study of vortex interactions, which are mathematically more tractable yet more singular than vortex patches or sheets. Indeed, according to numerical studies
[
6
]
and nonrigorous asymptotic analysis
[
29
,
30
]
, the interaction of vortices commences with a rapid relaxation phase. In this stage, each vortex undergoes shape adaptation in response to the velocity field produced by surrounding vortices. This initial phase is sensitive to the detailed initial conditions and features oscillatory behavior in the vortex cores that diminish within an inviscid time scale. Following this, the vortices gradually approach Gaussian-type profiles at a diffusion-controlled rate. The system eventually attains a “metastable regime” that no longer reflects the initial data and remains stable until vortices come into close proximity, triggering a merging process.
During this “metastable regime”, the dynamic of the vortex centers is characterized by the following “point vortex system”, which originally derived by Helmholtz
[
19
]
and Kirchhoff
[
20
]
:

\dot{z}_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{j\neq i}\Gamma_{j}\frac{(z_{i}(t)-z_{j}(t))^{\perp}}{|z_{i}(t)-z_{j}(t)|^{2}}.

(1.1)

Here
z_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\Gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
designate the positions and circulations of the
i
-th vortice. This ODE system describes the motion of idealized singular vortices in an inviscid fluid, and arises naturally as a formal limit of the 2D Euler or Navier–Stokes equations when the vorticity is highly concentrated.

To render this approximation mathematically rigorous, we start from the 2D Navier-Stokes equations in vorticity formulation:

\partial_{t}w+u\cdot\nabla_{x}w-\nu\Delta_{x}w=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},

(1.2)

where
\nu\ll 1
represents the viscous coefficient,
\omega
designates the vorticity of the fluid and the corresponding velocity
u
can be expressed by the Biot-Savart formula

u\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\nabla_{x}^{\perp}\varphi,\quad\text{where}\ \varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\omega=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log|x-y|\omega(y)\,dy.

To investigate the long time evolution of a pair of point vortices, we implement (
1.2
) with

w(t=0)=\Gamma_{1}\delta_{P_{1}}+\Gamma_{2}\delta_{P_{2}}.

(1.3)

Here
\Gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
P_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}(i=1,2)
, are the strength and initial position of the
i
-th Dirac measure, respectively. Provided the total circulation
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2}\neq 0
, we may apply a rotation and translation to assume, without loss of generality:

\Gamma_{1}\geq|\Gamma_{2}|>0,\quad P_{i}=(\ell_{i},0),\quad\Gamma_{1}\ell_{1}+\Gamma_{2}\ell_{2}=0\quad\hbox{and}\quad d\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}>0,

(1.4)

which together with the conservation laws implies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}w(t)dx=\Gamma\quad\hbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}x_{j}w(t)dx\equiv 0,\quad\text{for}\ j=1,2.

(1.5)

Among the numerous parameters governing this complex system, three dimensionless quantities are paramount: the Reynolds number, the advection time, and the diffusion time, defined respectively by

{\rm Re}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\Gamma_{1}/\nu\gg 1,\quad T_{{\rm adv}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}d^{2}/\Gamma_{1},\quad T_{{\rm dif}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}d^{2}/\nu=T_{{\rm adv}}\times{\rm Re}.

(1.6)

Introducing the dimensionless variables

x=d\tilde{x},\quad\ell_{i}=d\tilde{\ell}_{i},\quad t=T_{{\rm adv}}\tilde{t},\quad w(t,x)=T_{{\rm adv}}^{-1}\tilde{w}(\tilde{t},\tilde{x}),\quad\Gamma_{2}=\Gamma_{1}\tilde{\Gamma}_{2}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\nu=\Gamma_{1}\tilde{\nu},

(1.7)

we obtain the rescaled system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{\tilde{t}}\tilde{w}+\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{x}}\tilde{w}-\tilde{\nu}\Delta_{\tilde{x}}\tilde{w}=0,\quad\tilde{u}=\nabla^{\perp}_{\tilde{x}}\Delta_{\tilde{x}}^{-1}\tilde{w},\quad(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
&\tilde{w}(\tilde{t}=0)=\delta_{(\tilde{\ell}_{1},0)}+\tilde{\Gamma}_{2}\delta_{(\tilde{\ell}_{2},0)}.\end{aligned}\right.

From (
1.4
) and (
1.7
), we deduce:

|\tilde{\Gamma}_{2}|\leq 1,\quad\tilde{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\tilde{\ell}_{1}-\tilde{\ell}_{2}=1.

Hence, unless stated otherwise, we assume the normalized parameter regime:

\Gamma_{1}=1,\quad|\Gamma_{2}|\leq 1,\quad d=1.

(1.8)

1.2
) with measure data.

1.2.
Point vortex system

Setting
\nu=0

\partial_{t}w+u\cdot\nabla_{x}w=0,\quad u=\nabla^{\perp}_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}w,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

A natural conjecture is that for small

However, unlike the case of 2D Naveier-Stokes equations, the measure initial data

w_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Gamma_{i}\delta(\cdot-p_{i})

[

To study such evolutions rigorously, three approximation approaches are commonly employed (see
[
23
]
for instance):

•

using vortex blob method to construct the approximate solutions
\{w^{\epsilon}\}_{0<\epsilon<1}

•

and solving (
1.14

\{w^{\epsilon}\}_{0<\epsilon<1}

•

solving (
1.2

Along these lines, the inviscid motion of point vortices (

We list below some known results:

•

•

\Gamma_{2}\rightarrow-1
, the key point is that the case
\Gamma=0
is entirely different. In this scenario, one cannot use a rigid transformation to set the initial momentum to zero, i.e., to achieve
\Gamma_{1}\ell_{1}+\Gamma_{2}\ell_{2}=0
. Instead, the two point vortices undergo translational motion while maintaining an approximately fixed separation. Consequently, the rotational motion described in (
1.20
) fails to accurately represent the long-time dynamics, leading to the blow-up of
C_{s}(\Gamma_{2})
as
\Gamma_{2}\rightarrow-1
, i.e.,
\Gamma=0
. In our proof, the condition
\Gamma\neq 0
is used to ensure that the four “pseudo-momenta” constructed in Section
5
are linearly independent at leading order.

Despite the difference between the physical scenarios of cases
\Gamma=0
and
\Gamma\neq 0
, they still share some similarity, since the motion of the case
\Gamma=0
can be viewed as a rotation around infinity. In particular, the leading-order linear velocities of the two point vortices are given by

v_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\ell_{1}\times\dot{\theta}(t)=\frac{\Gamma_{2}}{2\pi d}+{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{2})\quad\hbox{and}\quad v_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\ell_{2}\times\dot{\theta}(t)=-\frac{\Gamma_{1}}{2\pi d}+{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{2}),

(1.26)

which resembles the result for the case
\Gamma=0
. For higher-order corrections, we refer to Remark
3.4
.

1.5.
Sketch of the proof and new ingredients.

The proof of Theorem
1.7
proceeds in several steps:

Step 1. The derivation of the resacled equations.

By virtue of the fundamental solution of transport-diffusion equations, we decompose the solution of (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) into two parts
w=w_{1}+w_{2}
(see (
2.2
)), which interplay with each other and have fast decay away from two moving centroids. Furthermore, taking advantage of the self-similar structure, we introduce co-moving frame (
2.3
) and self-similar coordinates with different centroid (
2.5
), to get a vector-valued equation (see (
2.8
), (
2.9
)):

(t\partial_{t}-{\mathcal{L}})\Omega+\frac{1}{\nu}\Bigl\{{\mathcal{B}}\Omega+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\dot{\theta}|\xi|^{2}Y_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}(\dot{\theta}\alpha\xi_{1}+\dot{\alpha}\xi_{2})Y_{2},\Omega\Bigr\}_{V}=0,

(1.27)

where we use
\dot{f}
to denote
\partial_{t}f
and
(\Omega,\theta,\alpha)
are unknowns.

Step 2. Construction of approximate solutions.

Using finite power series expansion in small parameters
\varepsilon\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\sqrt{\nu t}
and
\nu
, we then construct approximate solutions of (
1.27
) by

\Omega_{a}=\sum_{k=0}^{M}\varepsilon^{k}\bigl(\Omega^{E}_{k}+\nu\Omega^{NS}_{k}\bigr),\quad\dot{\theta}_{a}=\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\varepsilon^{k}\bigl(\dot{\theta}^{E}_{k}+\nu\dot{\theta}^{NS}_{k}\bigr),\quad\dot{\alpha}_{a}=\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\nu\varepsilon^{k}\dot{\alpha}^{NS}_{k},

so that the residual

\begin{split}{\rm R}_{a}&\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}(t\partial_{t}-{\mathcal{L}})\Omega_{a}+\frac{1}{\nu}\Bigl\{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\Omega_{a}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\dot{\theta}_{a}|\xi|^{2}Y_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}(\dot{\theta}_{a}\alpha_{a}\xi_{1}+\dot{\alpha}_{a}\xi_{2})Y_{2},\Omega_{a}\Bigr\}_{V}\\
&={\mathcal{O}}(\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}+\nu\varepsilon^{2}).\end{split}

With these approximate solutions, we set

\Omega=\Omega_{a}+\omega,\quad\dot{\theta}=\dot{\theta}_{a}+\dot{\theta}_{p}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\dot{\alpha}=\dot{\alpha}_{a}

and derive the linearized equation

(t\partial_{t}-{\mathcal{L}})\omega+\frac{1}{\nu}\Lambda^{E}\omega+\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}\alpha_{a}{\dot{\theta}_{p}}\{\hat{X},\Omega^{E}_{a}\}_{V}+\text{l.s.t.}=-{\rm R}_{a}+\text{n.l.t.},

(1.28)

where
\hat{X}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\xi_{1}Y_{2}+\frac{\varepsilon\Gamma}{2{\alpha_{a}}}|\xi|^{2}Y_{1}
is called “frame stream function”, and “l.s.t.” and “n.l.t.” designate “lower size term” and “nonlinear term”, respectively.
For detailed definitions, one may check (
4.5
)-(
4.6
) below. Here we do not use the modulation parameter
\dot{\alpha}
, which might appear to reduce the available freedom. But
Step 5
will show that this freedom is in fact unnecessary.

Step 3. Functional relationship and weighted spaces.

Following
[
16
,
7
]
, we denote

\Psi^{E}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\Omega^{E}_{a}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\dot{\theta}^{E}_{a}|\xi|^{2}Y_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}\dot{\theta}^{E}_{a}\alpha_{a}\xi_{1}Y_{2}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|\varepsilon/\alpha_{a}|(\Gamma_{2},\Gamma_{1})^{T}\quad\text{with}\quad\Omega^{E}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\sum_{k=0}^{M}\varepsilon^{k}\Omega^{E}_{k},

and then recover the functional relationship between
\Psi^{E}_{a}
and
\Omega^{E}_{a}
, i.e., there exist
F_{i}
so that

\Psi^{E}_{i,a}\approx F_{i}(\Omega^{E}_{i,a})
. Inspired by Arnold’s Method
[
1
]
and works in
[
15
]
, we define

W_{i,\varepsilon}(\xi)\approx F^{\prime}_{i}\left(\Omega^{E}_{i,a}(\xi)\right),\quad\|f\|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{i,\varepsilon}}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(\xi)|^{2}W_{i,\varepsilon}(\xi)d\xi,\quad E_{\varepsilon}[\omega]\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\omega,\omega\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega\right\rangle_{V}.

Here for the detailed definitions, see (
4.5
)-(
4.7
), (
4.11
)-(
4.14
). With this energy functional,

\Lambda^{E}\omega=\left\{\Psi^{E}_{a},\omega\right\}_{V}+\left\{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega,\Omega^{E}_{a}\right\}_{V}\approx\left\{\omega\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega,\ \Omega^{E}_{a}\right\}_{V},

which leads to

\left\langle\Lambda^{E}\omega,\ \omega\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega\right\rangle_{V}\approx 0,\quad\text{i.e., $\Lambda^{E}$ is almost skew-symmetric with respect to $E_{\varepsilon}$. }

Step 4. Coercivity of
E_{\varepsilon}[\omega]
and “pseudo-momenta”.

This step is crucial and differs from previous approaches. To restore the coercivity of
E_{\varepsilon}[\omega]
, earlier works exploited the freedom in modulation parameters (here,
\dot{\theta}
and
\dot{\alpha}
) to enforce the momentum orthogonality condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\xi_{j}\omega d\xi=0,\quad j=1,2,

as observed in
[
15
]
.
However, in this paper we demonstrate that this condition is incompatible with the operator
\Lambda^{E}
and allows small perturbations. Instead, it can be replaced by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\xi_{1}+\varepsilon P_{1})\omega\,d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\xi_{2}+\varepsilon P_{2})\omega\,d\xi=0,\quad\text{for some $P_{1},P_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}$},

while still preserving the coercivity of
E_{\varepsilon}[\omega]
. The key innovation of this paper lies in constructing suitable functions and-referred to as “pseudo-momenta”-that are compatible with the linearized equation. This construction is detailed in Subsection
4.3
.

To better illustrate the concept of “pseudo-momenta”, we consider a toy model:

(t\partial_{t}-{\mathcal{L}})f+\nu^{-1}\Lambda f={\rm R},\quad\text{with}\quad\Lambda f\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\{\Delta^{-1}_{\xi}G,f\}+\{\Delta_{\xi}^{-1}f,G\}.

We denote the adjoint of
\Lambda
with respect to standard
L^{2}_{\xi}
inner product by
\Lambda^{\star}
. Then it’s easy to check that

\Lambda[\partial_{j}G]=0,\quad\Lambda^{\star}[\xi_{j}]=0,\quad\{\xi_{1},G\}=\partial_{2}G,\quad\{\xi_{2},G\}=-\partial_{1}G.

(1.29)

Motivated by (
1.29
), “pseudo-momenta” should be the eigenfunctions, or some elements in invariant spaces of
\Lambda^{E,\star}
, up to an error
{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{M+1})
, whose leading order is
S\xi
for some non-constant matrix
S
. Similar idea-modulating the kernel of the linearized operator to restore coercivity-has been used in constructing blow-up solutions and stability analysis.

Let
\xi=\rho(\cos\vartheta,\sin\vartheta)
, we recall the “frame stream function”
\hat{X}
and define the “frame derivative”
X
by

\hat{X}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\xi_{1}Y_{2}+\frac{\varepsilon\Gamma}{2{\alpha_{a}}}|\xi|^{2}Y_{1}\quad\hbox{and}\quad X\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\{\hat{X},\Omega\}_{V}=\Bigl(Y_{2}\partial_{2}+\frac{\varepsilon\Gamma}{{\alpha_{a}}}Y_{1}\partial_{\vartheta}\Bigr)\Omega.

A key observation is that, up to an small error,
X\Omega^{E}_{a}
is an eigenfunction of
\Lambda^{E}
with eigenvalue zero. More precisely, there exist four “pseudo-momenta”:

\displaystyle\Lambda^{E,\star}[\xi_{1}Y_{1}]=\varepsilon^{2}\dot{\theta}^{E}_{a}\xi_{2}Y_{1},\quad\Lambda^{E,\star}[\xi_{2}Y_{1}]=-\varepsilon^{2}\dot{\theta}^{E}_{a}\xi_{1}Y_{1}-\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}\dot{\theta}^{E}_{a}{\alpha_{a}}Y_{2},

\displaystyle\Lambda^{E,\star}[\varrho^{e}_{a}]\approx 0,\quad\Lambda^{E,\star}\varrho^{o}_{a}\approx\lambda^{e}_{a}\varrho^{e}_{a},

\displaystyle\text{with}\quad\lambda^{e}_{a}={\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{2}),\quad\varrho^{o}_{a}=\xi_{2}Y_{2}+{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon),\quad\varrho^{e}_{a}=\xi_{1}Y_{2}+{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon).

And enlighten by (
1.29
), we use the Lie structure of 2D Euler equations to introduce

\displaystyle-\partial_{1}\Omega^{E}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\{\xi_{2}Y_{1},\Omega^{E}_{a}\}_{V},\quad\partial_{2}\Omega^{E}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\{\xi_{1}Y_{1},\Omega^{E}_{a}\}_{V},

\displaystyle f^{o}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\{\varrho^{o}_{a},\Omega^{E}_{a}\}_{V},\quad f^{e}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\{\varrho^{e}_{a},\Omega^{E}_{a}\}_{V}\approx\{\hat{X},\Omega^{E}_{a}\}_{V},

which also turn out to be the eigenfunctions, or some elements in invariant spaces of
\Lambda^{E}
.
Moreover,
\varrho^{o}_{a},f^{e}_{a}
are
\xi_{2}
-odd,
\varrho^{e}_{a},f^{o}_{a}
are
\xi_{2}
-even and
\partial_{1}\Omega^{E}_{a},\partial_{2}\Omega^{E}_{a},f^{e}_{a},f^{o}_{a}
enjoy certain specific inner product structures (see (
5.23
)).

Step 5. Choice of modulation parameter
\dot{\theta}_{p}
.

Thanks to (
1.5
),
\omega
is always orthogonal to
\xi_{1}Y_{1}
and
\xi_{2}Y_{1}
. Thus it’s natural to decompose
\omega
into

\begin{split}\omega\approx\mu^{e}f^{e}_{a}+\mu^{o}f^{o}_{a}+\omega^{R},\quad\text{with}\quad\left\langle\omega^{R},Z\right\rangle_{V}=0\quad\text{for}\ Z=\xi_{1}Y_{1},\xi_{2}Y_{1},\varrho^{o}_{a},\varrho^{e}_{a}.\end{split}

Inserting the above decomposition into (
1.28
), we find

\displaystyle(t\partial_{t}+1/2)\mu^{o}\times f^{o}_{a}+(t\partial_{t}+1/2)\mu^{e}\times f^{e}_{a}+(t\partial_{t}-{\mathcal{L}}+\nu^{-1}\Lambda^{E})\omega^{R}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu^{-1}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}{\alpha_{a}}\dot{\theta}_{p}-\lambda^{e}_{a}\mu^{o}\right)f^{e}_{a}=-{\rm R}_{a}+\textrm{l.s.t. + n.l.t.}.

Thus it suffices to choose
\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}{\alpha_{a}}\dot{\theta}_{p}-\lambda^{e}_{a}\mu^{o}=0
, to eliminate the growth of solutions induced by the large factor
\nu^{-1}
, which completely stems from the structure of the equations and the linearized operator
\Lambda^{E}
, as well as a simple and universal perspective of linear algebra.

Finally by testing above equality by
\varrho^{o}_{a},\varrho^{e}_{a},\omega^{R}\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}
to get the estimate of
\mu^{e},\mu^{o},\omega^{R}
, respectively, we can bound
\omega
by
{\rm R}_{a}
and thus complete the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

2.3.

We consider the following operators on

{\mathcal{L}}f=\Delta_{\xi}f+\frac{\xi}{2}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f+f\quad\text{with}\ {\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{L}})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\bigl\{f\in{\mathcal{Y}}\ :\ \Delta_{\xi}f\in{\mathcal{Y}},\,\xi\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f\in{\mathcal{Y}}\bigr\},

\Lambda f\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\{\Upsilon,f\}+\{\Delta_{\xi}^{-1}f,G\}\quad\text{with}\ {\mathcal{D}}(\Lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\bigl\{f\in{\mathcal{Y}}\ :\ \{\Upsilon,f\}\in{\mathcal{Y}}\bigr\}.

where we denote

\begin{split}\Upsilon(\xi)&\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\Delta_{\xi}^{-1}G=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log|\xi-\eta|G(\eta)d\eta=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\textrm{Ein}(|\xi|^{2}/4)-\gamma_{E}\right),\end{split}

with
\textrm{Ein}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\int_{0}^{x}(1-e^{-t})/tdt
and
\gamma_{E}
is the Euler constant.
We denote the adjoint of
{\mathcal{L}}
,
\Lambda
in
L^{2}_{\xi}
by
{\mathcal{L}}^{\star},\Lambda^{\star}
, respectively, i.e.,

\left\langle f,{\mathcal{L}}^{\star}g\right\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\left\langle{\mathcal{L}}f,g\right\rangle\quad\hbox{and}\quad\left\langle f,\Lambda^{\star}g\right\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\left\langle\Lambda f,g\right\rangle,

from which it follows directly that

{\mathcal{L}}^{\star}f=\Delta_{\xi}f-\frac{\xi}{2}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f\quad\text{with}\ {\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{L}}^{\star})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\bigl\{f\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}\ :\ \Delta_{\xi}f,\ \xi\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}\bigr\},

and

\Lambda^{\star}f=-\{\Upsilon,f\}-\Delta_{\xi}^{-1}\{f,G\}\quad\text{with}\ {\mathcal{D}}(\Lambda^{\star})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\bigl\{f\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}\ :\ \{\Upsilon,f\}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\star}\bigr\}.

Below we list some fundamental properties of
{\mathcal{L}}
and
\Lambda
.

Proposition 2.2
.

There hold

(1)

{\mathcal{L}}
is self-adjoint in
{\mathcal{Y}}
with compact resolvent and purely discrete spectrum:

\sigma({\mathcal{L}})=\left\{-\frac{n}{2}\ :\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Moreover,
\ker{\mathcal{L}}=\text{span}\{G\}
,
\ker(\frac{1}{2}+{\mathcal{L}})=\text{span}\{\partial_{1}G,\partial_{2}G\}
. And the eigenvalue
-\frac{n}{2}
has multiplicity
n+1
, with eigenfunctions being Hermite functions of degree
n
.

(2)

For any
\kappa>0
and
f\in{\mathcal{Z}}
, one has
(\kappa-{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}f\in{\mathcal{Z}}
.

(3)

\Lambda
is skew-adjoint in
{\mathcal{Y}}
with

\ker\Lambda={\mathcal{Y}}_{0}\oplus\text{span}\bigl\{\partial_{1}G,\partial_{2}G\bigr\}.

(4)

Let

{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}\cap(\ker{\Lambda})^{\perp}=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}\ :\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\xi_{j}f(\xi)\,d\xi=0,\ j=1,2\right\}.

Then for
f\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{n}\cap{\mathcal{Z}}
if
n\geq 2
, or
f\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}_{1}\cap{\mathcal{Z}}
if
n=1
, there exists a unique
\Omega
in the same space so that
\Lambda\Omega=f
.
Furthermore, if
f=b(\rho)\sin(n\vartheta)
(respectively,
f=b(\rho)\cos(n\vartheta)
), then
\Omega=w(\rho)\cos(n\vartheta)
(respectively,
\Omega=-w(\rho)\sin(n\vartheta)
), where

w(\rho)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}-\frac{\rho^{2}/4}{e^{\rho^{2}/4}-1}\varphi(\rho)-\frac{2\pi\rho^{2}}{n(1-e^{-\rho^{2}/4})}b(\rho),

(2.10)

with
\varphi
solving

-\varphi^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{\rho}\varphi^{\prime}+\left(\frac{n^{2}}{\rho^{2}}-\frac{\rho^{2}/4}{e^{\rho^{2}/4}-1}\right)\varphi=\frac{2\pi\rho^{2}}{n(1-e^{-\rho^{2}/4})}b(\rho),\quad\rho>0.

(2.11)

In particular, if
f
is
\xi_{2}
-even (odd), then
\Omega
is
\xi_{2}
-odd (even).

(5)

For any
h\in G^{-1}{\mathcal{Y}}_{n}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}
if
n\geq 2
, or
h\in G^{-1}{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}_{1}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}
if
n=1
, there exists a unique
\varrho
in the same space so that
\Lambda^{\star}\varrho=h
. In particular, if
h
is
\xi_{2}
-even (odd), then
\varrho
is
\xi_{2}
-odd (even).

Proof.

For the assertions (1)–(4), see for instance
[
17
,
7
,
16
]
. Using (3), we compute for any
f,g\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
:

\left\langle f,\Lambda^{\star}g\right\rangle=\left\langle\Lambda f,g\right\rangle=\left\langle\Lambda f,Gg\right\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}=-\left\langle f,\Lambda[Gg]\right\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}=-\left\langle f,G^{-1}\Lambda[Gg]\right\rangle,

which gives rise to

\Lambda^{\star}g=-G^{-1}\Lambda[Gg].

In particular, we arrive at

\Lambda^{\star}\varrho=h\iff\Lambda[G\varrho]=-Gh,

from which and (4), we deduce (5) and thus complete the proof of Proposition
2.2
.
∎

2.4.
Expansion of the interacting velocity

Let
\xi=(\rho\cos\vartheta,\rho\sin\vartheta)
, we recall the following n-homogeneous polynomials in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
from
[
7
]
:

Q^{c}_{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}{\rm Re}(\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2})^{n}=\rho^{n}\cos(n\vartheta)\quad\hbox{and}\quad Q^{s}_{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}{\rm Im}(\xi_{1}+i\xi_{2})^{n}=\rho^{n}\sin(n\vartheta).

Lemma 2.3
.

There hold

\partial_{1}Q^{c}_{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=nQ^{c}_{n-1}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\quad\hbox{and}\quad\partial_{2}Q^{c}_{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=-nQ^{s}_{n-1}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}).

(2.12)

Proof.

Notice that

\partial_{1}=\cos\vartheta\partial_{\rho}-\sin\vartheta\frac{\partial_{\vartheta}}{\rho},\quad\partial_{2}=\sin\vartheta\partial_{\rho}+\cos\vartheta\frac{\partial_{\vartheta}}{\rho},

we get

\begin{split}\partial_{1}Q^{c}_{n}&=\Bigl(\cos\vartheta\partial_{\rho}-\sin\vartheta\frac{\partial_{\vartheta}}{\rho}\Bigr)(\rho^{n}\cos(n\vartheta))=n\rho^{n-1}\left(\cos\vartheta\cos(n\vartheta)+\sin\vartheta\sin(n\vartheta)\right)\\
&=n\rho^{n-1}\cos((n-1)\vartheta)=nQ^{c}_{n-1},\\
\partial_{2}Q^{c}_{n}&=\Bigl(\sin\vartheta\partial_{\rho}+\cos\vartheta\frac{\partial_{\vartheta}}{\rho}\Bigr)(\rho^{n}\cos(n\vartheta))=n\rho^{n-1}\left(\sin\vartheta\cos(n\vartheta)-\cos\vartheta\sin(n\vartheta)\right)\\
&=-n\rho^{n-1}\sin((n-1)\vartheta)=-nQ^{s}_{n-1},\end{split}

which completes the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<|\lambda|\ll 1
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Omega={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{Z}}(1)
and
\Phi=\Delta_{\xi}^{-1}\Omega
. There hold

\begin{split}&\Phi(\xi+\lambda^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|\lambda|^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Omega(\eta)d\eta\\
&\quad+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{2\pi n}\lambda^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Q^{c}_{n}\big(\xi-\eta\big)\Omega(\eta)d\eta+{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}}(\lambda^{N+1}).\end{split}

(2.13)

Proof.

First of all, in view of

\Phi(\xi)=(\Delta_{\xi}^{-1}\Omega)(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log|\xi-\eta|\Omega(\eta)d\eta,

we have
\Phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}
and thus
\Phi(\xi+\lambda^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}
. Notice that any
f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}
supported on
|\xi|\geq\frac{1}{8|\lambda|}
belongs to
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}}(\lambda^{M})
for any
M
, it suffices to consider the expansion of
\Phi(\xi+\lambda^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})
for
|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{8|\lambda|}
. We write

\begin{split}\Phi(\xi+\lambda^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})&=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\bigl|\xi+\lambda^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}}-\eta\bigr|^{2}\Omega(\eta)d\eta\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|\lambda|^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Omega(\eta)d\eta+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\left|{\mathbf{e}_{1}}+\lambda(\xi-\eta)\right|^{2}\Omega(\eta)d\eta.\end{split}

Thanks to

\begin{split}&\Bigl|\frac{1}{2}\log|{\mathbf{e}_{1}}+x|^{2}-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}Q^{c}_{n}(x)\Bigr|\\
&\lesssim|x|^{N+1}\mathbbm{1}_{|x|\leq\frac{1}{4}}+\left|\log|x+{\mathbf{e}_{1}}|\right|\mathbbm{1}_{|x+{\mathbf{e}_{1}}|\leq\frac{1}{4}}+|x|^{N}\mathbbm{1}_{|x|\geq\frac{1}{4}},\end{split}

we have

\begin{split}&\Bigl|\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\left|{\mathbf{e}_{1}}+\lambda(\xi-\eta)\right|^{2}\Omega(\eta)d\eta-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{2\pi n}\lambda^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Q^{c}_{n}\big(\xi-\eta\big)\Omega(\eta)d\eta\Bigr|\\
&\lesssim\int_{\{|\xi-\eta|\leq\frac{1}{4|\lambda|}\}}\lambda^{N+1}|\xi-\eta|^{N+1}|\Omega(\eta)|d\eta+\int_{\{|\xi-\eta|\geq\frac{1}{4|\lambda|}\}}\lambda^{N}|\xi-\eta|^{N}|\Omega(\eta)|d\eta\\
&\quad+\int_{\{|{\mathbf{e}_{1}}+\lambda(\xi-\eta)|\leq\frac{1}{4}\}}\Big|\log|{\mathbf{e}_{1}}+\lambda(\xi-\eta)|\Big||\Omega(\eta)|d\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}{\mathcal{J}}_{1}+{\mathcal{J}}_{2}+{\mathcal{J}}_{3}.\end{split}

We can easily bound
{\mathcal{J}}_{1}
by

|{\mathcal{J}}_{1}|\lesssim\lambda^{N+1}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{N+1}.

(2.14)

Now for
{\mathcal{J}}_{2}
, we get by
|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{8|\lambda|}
and
\Omega={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{Z}}(1)
that

\begin{split}&|{\mathcal{J}}_{2}|\lesssim\int_{\{|\eta|\geq\frac{1}{8|\lambda|}\}}\lambda^{N}|\xi-\eta|^{N}|\Omega(\eta)|d\eta\lesssim\int_{\{|\eta|\geq\frac{1}{8|\lambda|}\}}\lambda^{N}|\eta|^{N}|\Omega(\eta)|d\eta\lesssim\lambda^{N}e^{-c|\lambda|^{-2}}.\end{split}

(2.15)

And similarly, we have

\begin{split}&|{\mathcal{J}}_{3}|\lesssim\int_{\{|\eta|\geq\frac{1}{8|\lambda|}\}\cap\{|{\mathbf{e}_{1}}+\lambda(\xi-\eta)|\leq\frac{1}{4}\}}\big|\log|\lambda(\xi-\eta)+{\mathbf{e}_{1}}|\big||\Omega(\eta)|d\eta\lesssim|\lambda|^{-2}e^{-c|\lambda|^{-2}}.\end{split}

(2.16)

Finally by summarizing (
2.14
)-(
2.16
), we obtained the desired estimate for
\Phi(\xi+\lambda^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})
, and the estimates of
\nabla_{\xi}^{\beta}\Phi(\xi+\lambda^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})
follow along the same line. This completes the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

3.
The construction of approximate solutions

In this section, we shall construct approximate solutions of (
2.6
-
2.7
).
To measure the error of the approximation solutions and its true solution, we introduce

\begin{split}&{\rm R}_{i}[\Omega,\dot{\alpha},\dot{\theta}]\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}t\partial_{t}\Omega_{i}-{\mathcal{L}}\Omega_{i}+\frac{1}{\nu}\Big\{\Phi_{i}(\xi)+\Phi_{i^{\star}}\left(\xi+\kappa_{i}\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\dot{\theta}|\xi|^{2}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\quad\qquad+\frac{\varepsilon\kappa_{i}\Gamma_{i^{\star}}}{\Gamma}(\dot{\theta}\alpha\xi_{1}+\dot{\alpha}\xi_{2}),\ \Omega_{i}\Big\},\quad i=1,2.\end{split}

(3.1)

For any fixed
M\in\mathbb{N}
, we will seek approximate solutions
(\Omega_{i,a},\dot{\alpha}_{a},\dot{\theta}_{a})
, so that

m[\Omega_{i,a}]\equiv\Gamma_{i},\quad M[\Omega_{i,a}]\equiv 0\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\rm R}_{i}[\Omega_{a},\dot{\alpha}_{a},\dot{\theta}_{a}]={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}+\nu\varepsilon^{2}),\quad i=1,2.

3.1.
Analysis of the momentum.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
m[\Omega_{i}]=\Gamma_{i}
and
M[\Omega_{i}]=0
for
i=1,2
. Then we have

m[{\rm R}_{i}[\Omega,\dot{\alpha},\dot{\theta}]]=0,

(3.2)

and

\begin{split}\nu M[{\rm R}_{1}[\Omega,\dot{\alpha},\dot{\theta}]]&=-\nu M[{\rm R}_{2}[\Omega,\dot{\alpha},\dot{\theta}]]\\
&=\frac{\varepsilon\Gamma_{1}\Gamma_{2}}{\Gamma}\begin{pmatrix}\dot{\alpha}\\
-\dot{\theta}\alpha\end{pmatrix}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla_{\xi}^{\perp}\Phi_{2}\left(\xi+\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\right)\Omega_{1}(\xi)d\xi.\end{split}

(3.3)

Proof.

In view of (
3.1
), we get, by
using integration by parts and Proposition
2.1
, that

\begin{split}m[{\rm R}_{i}[\Omega,\dot{\alpha},\dot{\theta}]]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bigl(t\partial_{t}-\Delta_{\xi}-\frac{\xi}{2}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}-1\bigr)\Omega_{i}\,d\xi=t\partial_{t}m[\Omega_{i}]=0,\end{split}

which gives (
3.2
). Similarly, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(t\partial_{t}\Omega_{i}-{\mathcal{L}}\Omega_{i})\xi_{j}d\xi=t\partial_{t}M_{j}[\Omega_{i}]+\frac{1}{2}M_{j}[\Omega_{i}]=0,

from which, Proposition
2.1
, and

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big\{\Delta_{\xi}^{-1}f,f\big\}\xi d\xi&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big\{\Delta_{\xi}^{-1}f,\xi\big\}fd\xi=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f\nabla_{\xi}^{\perp}\Delta_{\xi}^{-1}fd\xi\\
&=\frac{-1}{2\pi}\iint\frac{(\xi-\eta)^{\perp}}{|\xi-\eta|^{2}}f(\eta)f(\xi)\,d\xi d\eta=0,\end{split}

we infer

\begin{split}&\nu M[{\rm R}_{i}[\Omega,\dot{\alpha},\dot{\theta}]]\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big\{\Phi_{i}(\xi)+\Phi_{i^{\star}}\left(\xi+\kappa_{i}\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\dot{\theta}|\xi|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon\kappa_{i}\Gamma_{i^{\star}}}{\Gamma}(\dot{\theta}\alpha\xi_{1}+\dot{\alpha}\xi_{2}),\ \Omega_{i}\Big\}\xi d\xi\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big\{\Phi_{i^{\star}}\left(\xi+\kappa_{i}\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\dot{\theta}|\xi|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon\kappa_{i}\Gamma_{i^{\star}}}{\Gamma}(\dot{\theta}\alpha\xi_{1}+\dot{\alpha}\xi_{2}),\ \Omega_{i}\Big\}\xi d\xi\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big\{\Phi_{i^{\star}}\left(\xi+\kappa_{i}\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\dot{\theta}|\xi|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon\kappa_{i}\Gamma_{i^{\star}}}{\Gamma}(\dot{\theta}\alpha\xi_{1}+\dot{\alpha}\xi_{2}),\ \xi\Big\}\Omega_{i}d\xi\\
&=-\varepsilon^{2}\dot{\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Omega_{i}\xi^{\perp}d\xi-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\nabla_{\xi}^{\perp}\Phi_{i^{\star}}\left(\xi+\kappa_{i}\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon\kappa_{i}\Gamma_{i^{\star}}}{\Gamma}\begin{pmatrix}-\dot{\alpha}\\
\dot{\theta}\alpha\end{pmatrix}\right)\Omega_{i}(\xi)d\xi\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla_{\xi}^{\perp}\Phi_{i^{\star}}\left(\xi+\kappa_{i}\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\right)\Omega_{i}(\xi)d\xi-\frac{\varepsilon\kappa_{i}\Gamma_{i^{\star}}}{\Gamma}m[\Omega_{i}]\begin{pmatrix}-\dot{\alpha}\\
\dot{\theta}\alpha\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\kappa_{i}\frac{\varepsilon\Gamma_{1}\Gamma_{2}}{\Gamma}\begin{pmatrix}\dot{\alpha}\\
-\dot{\theta}\alpha\end{pmatrix}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla_{\xi}^{\perp}\Phi_{i^{\star}}\left(\xi+\kappa_{i}\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\right)\Omega_{i}(\xi)d\xi.\end{split}

This together with

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla_{\xi}^{\perp}\Phi_{1}\big(\xi-\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\big)\Omega_{2}(\xi)d\xi=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla_{\xi}^{\perp}\Phi_{2}\big(\xi+\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\big)\Omega_{1}(\xi)d\xi,

implies (
3.3
). We thus completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

∎

4.
Analysis of the linearized equation

4.1.
derivation of the perturbed equation

Given
(\Omega_{a}^{(M)},\dot{\alpha}_{a}^{(M)},\dot{\theta}^{(M)}_{a})
constructed in the previous section with
{\rm R}_{i}[\Omega_{a}^{(M)},\dot{\alpha}_{a}^{(M)},\dot{\theta}_{a}^{(M)}]={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}+\nu\varepsilon^{2})
, we set

\begin{split}\Omega_{i}=\Omega_{a}^{(M)}+\omega_{i},\quad\Phi_{i}=\Phi_{i,a}^{(M)}+\phi_{i},\quad\dot{\theta}=\dot{\theta}_{a}^{(M)}+{\dot{\theta}_{p}},\quad\dot{\alpha}=\dot{\alpha}_{a}^{(M)}+\dot{\alpha}_{p},\end{split}

(4.1)

where the modulation parameters
\dot{\theta}_{p}
and
\dot{\alpha}_{p}
remain to be determined.
Then by virtue of Proposition
2.1
and the properties of
\Omega_{i,a}
, one has

m[\omega_{i}]=0\quad\hbox{and}\quad M[\omega_{1}]+M[\omega_{2}]=0\quad\text{for}\ i=1,2.

(4.2)

For simplicity, we neglect the superscript

(M)
below.
Motivated by
[
16
,
7
]
, a primary consideration is to choose
\dot{\theta}_{p},\dot{\alpha}_{p}
so that
M[\omega_{i}]\equiv 0
for
t>0
, thereby recovering the coercivity of the energy functional
E_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
4.14
). However, as already emphasized in the introduction, the orthogonal condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\xi_{1}\omega_{i}\,d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\xi_{2}\omega_{i}\,d\xi=0

(4.3)

exhibits incompatibility with the linearized equation around
\Omega_{a}
and allows an “
{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon)
perturbation”(see subsection
4.3
). More precisely, (
4.3
) can be replaced by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\xi_{1}+\varepsilon P_{1})\omega_{i}\,d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\xi_{2}+\varepsilon P_{2})\omega_{i}\,d\xi=0,\quad\text{for some $P_{1},P_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}$},

which still keeps the coercivity of
E_{\varepsilon}
. Seeking suitable
P_{1},P_{2}
compatibility with the linearized equation is the key imgredient in this paper, and this will be carried out in subsection
4.3
.

For a concise presentation, we bypass this step for the time being and set
\alpha_{p}=0
, then we get, by inserting (
4.1
) into (
2.9
), that

\begin{split}&(t\partial_{t}-{\mathcal{L}})\omega+\frac{1}{\nu}\Lambda^{E}\omega+\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}\alpha_{a}{\dot{\theta}_{p}}\{\hat{X},\Omega^{E}_{a}\}_{V}+\Lambda^{NS}\omega+\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}\alpha_{a}{\dot{\theta}_{p}}\{\hat{X},\Omega^{NS}_{a}\}_{V}\\
&=-{\rm R}_{a}-\frac{1}{\nu}\{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega,\omega\}_{V}-\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}\alpha_{a}{\dot{\theta}_{p}}\{\hat{X},\omega\}_{V}.\end{split}

(4.4)

where we define

\begin{split}&{\rm R}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}({\rm R}_{1}[\Omega_{a},\dot{\alpha}_{a},\dot{\theta}_{a}],\ {\rm R}_{2}[\Omega_{a},\dot{\alpha}_{a},\dot{\theta}_{a}])^{T}={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}+\nu\varepsilon^{2}),\\
&\Lambda^{E}\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\{\Psi^{E}_{a},\omega\}_{V}+\{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega,\Omega^{E}_{a}\}_{V},\quad\Lambda^{NS}\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\{\Psi^{NS}_{a},\omega\}_{V}+\{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega,\Omega^{NS}_{a}\}_{V},\end{split}

(4.5)

with

\begin{split}&\Omega_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\Omega^{E}_{a}+\nu\Omega^{NS}_{a},\quad\hat{X}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\xi_{1}Y_{2}+\frac{\varepsilon\Gamma}{2\alpha_{a}}|\xi|^{2}Y_{1}\\
&{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\Omega(\xi)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta^{-1}_{\xi}\Omega_{1}(\xi)+\Delta^{-1}_{\xi}\Omega_{2}(\xi+\varepsilon^{-1}{\alpha_{a}}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})\\
\Delta^{-1}_{\xi}\Omega_{2}(\xi)+\Delta^{-1}_{\xi}\Omega_{1}(\xi-\varepsilon^{-1}{\alpha_{a}}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})\end{pmatrix},\\
&\Psi^{E}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}(\Psi^{E}_{1,a},\Psi^{E}_{2,a})^{T}={\mathcal{B}}_{a}\Omega^{E}_{a}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\dot{\theta}^{E}_{a}|\xi|^{2}Y_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}\dot{\theta}^{E}_{a}\alpha_{a}\xi_{1}Y_{2}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\big|\varepsilon/{\alpha_{a}}\big|(\Gamma_{2},\Gamma_{1})^{T}\\
&\qquad={\mathcal{B}}_{a}\Omega^{E}_{a}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}\dot{\theta}^{E}_{a}\alpha_{a}\hat{X}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\big|\varepsilon/{\alpha_{a}}\big|(\Gamma_{2},\Gamma_{1})^{T},\\
&\Psi^{NS}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\Omega^{NS}_{a}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\dot{\theta}^{NS}_{a}|\xi|^{2}Y_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}(\dot{\theta}^{NS}_{a}\alpha_{a}\xi_{1}+\dot{\alpha}^{NS}_{a}\xi_{2})Y_{2}.\end{split}

(4.6)

Momentarily disregarding the precise topology and derivative structures,
and assuming
|\dot{\theta}_{p}|\thicksim|\omega|
, we observe from

\Omega_{a}^{NS}={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(\varepsilon^{2})
and
\Psi_{a}^{NS}={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\star}}(\varepsilon^{2})

(t\partial_{t}-{\mathcal{L}})\omega+\frac{1}{\nu}\Lambda^{E}\omega+\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}\alpha_{a}{\dot{\theta}_{p}}\{\hat{X},\Omega^{E}_{a}\}_{V}+\text{l.s.t.}=-{\rm R}_{a}+\text{n.l.t.}.

Consequently, studying the linearized operator
\Lambda^{E}
is the most crucial task.

4.2.
Weight function and energy functional

Heuristically, in view of Propositions

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\varepsilon\ll 1

\begin{split}&\varepsilon\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}}}(1+|\cdot|)^{-3}\phi(\xi+\varepsilon^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})\right\|_{L^{q}_{\xi}}+\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}}}(1+|\cdot|)^{-3}\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi+\varepsilon^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})\right\|_{L^{q}_{\xi}}\\

(4.16)

Proof.

We decompose

(4.17)

Therefore by denoting
\phi^{R}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\Delta_{\xi}^{-1}f^{R}

\zeta(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}f^{R}(-\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

\begin{split}&\varepsilon\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}}}(1+|\cdot|)^{-3}\partial_{j}\Upsilon(\xi+\varepsilon^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})\right\|_{L^{q}_{\xi}}+\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}}}(1+|\cdot|)^{-3}\nabla_{\xi}\partial_{j}\Upsilon(\xi+\varepsilon^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})\right\|_{L^{q}_{\xi}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2},\end{split}

\begin{split}&\varepsilon\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}}}(1+|\cdot|)^{-3}\phi(\xi+\varepsilon^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})\right\|_{L^{q}_{\xi}}+\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}}}(1+|\cdot|)^{-3}\nabla_{\xi}\phi(\xi+\varepsilon^{-1}{\mathbf{e}_{1}})\right\|_{L^{q}_{\xi}}\\

4.4.
Toy model

In this subsection, we investigate a toy model to illuminate both the structure of
\Lambda^{E}

Specifically, we examine the scalar equation obtained by linearizing (

G

(t\partial_{t}-{\mathcal{L}})f+\nu^{-1}\Lambda f={\rm R},

(4.22)

where
\Lambda f=\left\{\Upsilon,f\right\}+\{\Delta_{\xi}^{-1}f,G\}=\{fW_{0}+\Delta_{\xi}^{-1}f,G\}
.
It is straightforward to verify the relations

\Lambda[\partial_{j}G]=0,\quad{\mathcal{L}}\partial_{j}G=-1/2\partial_{j}G\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Lambda^{\star}[\xi_{j}]=0,\quad{\mathcal{L}}^{\star}\xi_{j}=-1/2\xi_{j}.

(4.23)

In particular,
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
serve as eigenfunctions of
\Lambda^{\star}
, and are related to
\partial_{j}G
by

\{\xi_{1},G\}=\partial_{2}G\quad\hbox{and}\quad\{\xi_{2},G\}=-\partial_{1}G.

(4.24)

By decomposing
f=\mu_{1}\partial_{1}G+\mu_{2}\partial_{2}G+f^{R}
with
M[f^{R}]=0
, we find

(t\partial_{t}+1/2)\mu_{1}\times\partial_{1}G+(t\partial_{t}+1/2)\mu_{2}\times\partial_{2}G+(t\partial_{t}-{\mathcal{L}}+\nu^{-1}\Lambda)f^{R}={\rm R}.

(4.25)

&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\big|\big\langle\big\{\left({\mathcal{B}}_{a}\Omega^{E}_{a}\right)_{i}+\varepsilon\kappa_{i}\Gamma_{i}\dot{\theta}^{E}_{a}{\alpha_{a}}\xi_{1}/\Gamma,\ \exp(|\xi|^{2\gamma}/4)\big\},\mathbbm{1}_{|\xi|\geq\varepsilon^{-\sigma_{2}}}|\omega^{R}_{i}|^{2}\big\rangle\big|\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon^{M+1-N\sigma_{1}}\left\|\omega^{R}\right\|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{\sigma_{2}}\big(\varepsilon+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\|\nabla_{\xi}{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\Omega^{E}_{a}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}\big)\left\|\omega^{R}\right\|_{{\mathcal{D}}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\left(\varepsilon^{M+1-N\sigma_{1}}+\varepsilon^{\sigma_{2}}\right){\mathcal{D}}.\end{split}

(6.25)

While we can similarly bound
A_{2}
by

\begin{split}|A_{2}|&\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left|\left\langle\left\{\left({\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right)_{i},\Omega^{E}_{i,a}\right\}F^{\prime}_{i}(\Omega^{E}_{i,a})+\left\{\left({\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right)_{i},\Psi^{E}_{i,a}\right\},\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}}}\omega^{R}_{i}\right\rangle\right|\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\big|\big\langle\left\{\left({\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right)_{i},\Omega^{E}_{i,a}\right\}W_{i,\varepsilon}+\left\{\left({\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right)_{i},\Psi^{E}_{i,a}\right\},\mathbbm{1}_{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}^{c}}\omega^{R}_{i}\big\rangle\big|\\
&\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left|\left\langle\left\{\left({\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right)_{i},\Theta_{i}\right\},\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}}}\omega^{R}_{i}\right\rangle\right|\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\big|\big\langle\left\{\left({\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right)_{i},\Omega^{E}_{i,a}\right\}W_{i,\varepsilon}+\left\{\left({\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right)_{i},\Psi^{E}_{i,a}\right\},\mathbbm{1}_{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}^{c}}\omega^{R}_{i}\big\rangle\big|\\
&\lesssim\left(\varepsilon^{M+1}+\exp\left(-c\varepsilon^{-2\sigma_{1}}\right)\right)\left\|\nabla_{\xi}{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}\left\|\omega^{R}\right\|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\\
&\lesssim\left(\varepsilon^{M+1}+\exp\left(-c\varepsilon^{-2\sigma_{1}}\right)\right){\mathcal{D}}.\end{split}

(6.26)

By inserting (
6.25
), (
6.26
) into (
6.24
), and then letting
\varepsilon,\sigma_{1}\ll 1
, we achieve (
6.22d
).

Now we turn to the proof of (
6.22e
). We decompose
\Psi^{NS}_{a}
into
\Psi^{NS}_{a}=\Psi^{NS,\mathrm{I}}_{a}+\Psi^{NS,\mathrm{II}}_{a}
with

\begin{split}&\Psi^{NS,\mathrm{I}}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\Omega^{NS}_{a}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma}(\dot{\theta}^{NS}_{a}\alpha_{a}\xi_{1}+\dot{\alpha}^{NS}_{a}\xi_{2})Y_{2},\quad\Psi^{NS,\mathrm{II}}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\dot{\theta}^{NS}_{a}|\xi|^{2}Y_{1},\end{split}

and then split

\begin{split}&\left\langle\Lambda^{NS}\omega^{R},\omega^{R}\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}\\
&=\left\langle\left\{\Psi^{NS,\mathrm{I}}_{a},\omega^{R}\right\}_{V},\omega^{R}\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}+\left\langle\left\{\Psi^{NS,\mathrm{II}}_{a},\omega^{R}\right\}_{V},\omega^{R}\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}\\
&\quad+\left\langle\left\{{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R},\Omega^{NS}_{a}\right\}_{V},\omega^{R}\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}A_{3}+A_{4}+A_{5}.\end{split}

(6.27)

Using integration by parts,
\dot{\theta}^{NS}_{0}=\dot{\alpha}^{NS}_{0}=0
and
\Omega^{NS}_{a}={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\varepsilon^{2})
, we get

\begin{split}|A_{3}|&\leq\big|\big\langle\big\{\Psi^{NS,\mathrm{I}}_{a},\omega^{R}\big\}_{V},\omega^{R}\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle_{V}\big|+\big|\big\langle\big\{\Psi^{NS,\mathrm{I}}_{a},{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\big\}_{V},\omega^{R}\big\rangle_{V}\big|\\
&\lesssim\left\|\nabla_{\xi}\Psi^{NS,\mathrm{I}}_{a}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}\big(\left\|\nabla_{\xi}\omega^{R}\right\|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}+\left\|\nabla_{\xi}{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}\big)\left\|\omega^{R}\right\|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}{\mathcal{D}},\end{split}

(6.28)

and similarly

\begin{split}|A_{5}|\lesssim\left\|\nabla_{\xi}{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}\left\|\nabla_{\xi}\Omega^{NS}_{a}\right\|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\left\|\omega^{R}\right\|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}{\mathcal{D}}.\end{split}

(6.29)

Finally for
A_{4}
, one obtains, by using Proposition
4.1
and taking
\sigma_{1}
to be sufficiently small, that

\begin{split}|A_{4}|&\leq 1/2\times\big|\left\langle\left\{\Psi^{NS,\mathrm{II}}_{a},W_{\varepsilon}\right\}_{V},\omega^{R}\dot{\otimes}\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}\big|+\big|\left\langle\left\{\Psi^{NS,\mathrm{II}}_{a},{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right\}_{V},\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}\big|\\
&\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{2}\big|\big\langle\big\{\Psi^{NS,\mathrm{II}}_{i,a},W_{i,\varepsilon}\big\},\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}}}|\omega^{R}_{i}|^{2}\big\rangle_{V}\big|+\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\nabla_{\xi}{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}\left\|\omega^{R}\right\|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left|\left\langle\xi^{\perp}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}(W_{i,\varepsilon}-W_{0}),\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}}}|\omega^{R}_{i}|^{2}\right\rangle_{V}\right|+\varepsilon^{2}{\mathcal{D}}\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon^{2.5-\sigma_{1}}{\mathcal{E}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{\mathcal{D}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}{\mathcal{D}}.\end{split}

(6.30)

By inserting (
6.28
)-(
6.30
) into (
6.27
), we achieve (
6.22e
). This completes the proof of Proposition
6.7
.

∎

6.5.
Estimation of
\mu^{o}
and
\mu^{e}
.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
\varepsilon,\sigma_{1}\ll 1
and

{\mathcal{F}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}(t\partial_{t}+1/2)\mu^{o}\times\big(f^{o}_{a}-\frac{\bar{a}}{\Gamma}f^{to}_{a}\big)+(t\partial_{t}+1/2)\mu^{e}\times\big(f^{e}_{a}+\frac{\bar{c}}{\Gamma}f^{te}_{a}\big).

There hold

\displaystyle\left\langle{\mathcal{F}},\ -\varrho^{o}_{a}\right\rangle_{V}=\left(\bar{b}+\frac{\bar{a}\bar{c}}{\Gamma}\right)(t\partial_{t}+1/2)\mu^{e},\quad\left\langle{\mathcal{F}},\ \varrho^{e}_{a}\right\rangle_{V}=\left(\bar{b}+\frac{\bar{a}\bar{c}}{\Gamma}\right)(t\partial_{t}+1/2)\mu^{o},

(6.32a)

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle{\mathcal{F}},\ \omega^{R}\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}\right|\lesssim\varepsilon^{M+1}\left(|t\partial_{t}\mu^{o}|+|\mu^{o}|+|t\partial_{t}\mu^{e}|+|\mu^{e}|\right){\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(6.32b)

Proof.

(
6.32a
) simply follows (
5.23
). Now for (
6.32b
), notice that

\begin{split}&\left\langle f^{o}_{a},\omega^{R}\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}=\left\langle\left\{\varrho^{o}_{a},\Omega^{E}_{a}\right\}_{V},\omega^{R}\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}\\
&=\left\langle\left\{\varrho^{o}_{a},\Omega^{E}_{a}\right\}_{V}\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}+{\mathcal{B}}_{a}\left\{\varrho^{o}_{a},\Omega^{E}_{a}\right\}_{V},\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}\\
&=-\left\langle\Lambda^{E,\star}\varrho^{o}_{a},\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}+\left\langle\left\{\varrho^{o}_{a},\Omega^{E}_{a}\right\}_{V}\dot{\otimes}W_{\varepsilon}-\left\{\Psi^{E}_{a},\varrho^{o}_{a}\right\}_{V},\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}\\
&=-\left\langle{\rm R}_{a}^{o,s},\omega^{R}\right\rangle_{V}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\langle\left\{\varrho^{o}_{i,a},\Omega^{E}_{i,a}\right\}F^{\prime}_{i}(\Omega^{E}_{i,a})-\left\{\Psi^{E}_{i,a},\varrho^{o}_{i,a}\right\},\omega^{R}_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}}}\right\rangle\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\big\langle\left\{\varrho^{o}_{i,a},\Omega^{E}_{i,a}\right\}W_{i,\varepsilon}-\left\{\Psi^{E}_{i,a},\varrho^{o}_{i,a}\right\},\omega^{R}_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}^{c}}\big\rangle\\

&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\big\langle\left\{\varrho^{o}_{i,a},\Omega^{E}_{i,a}\right\}W_{i,\varepsilon}-\left\{\Psi^{E}_{i,a},\varrho^{o}_{i,a}\right\},\omega^{R}_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathrm{I}_{i,\varepsilon}^{c}}\big\rangle,\end{split}

we can bound

by using Proposition

6.6.
proof of Theorem
1.7

We are now in a position to present the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Proof.

By taking
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{V}
inner product of (
6.5

\begin{split}\left|\bar{b}+\frac{\bar{a}\bar{c}}{\Gamma}\right|\left|(t\partial_{t}+1/2)\mu^{o}\right|\lesssim\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}+\nu\varepsilon^{2}+\left(\varepsilon+\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}\right){\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\nu^{-1}{\mathcal{D}},\end{split}

(6.33)

which implies that

\begin{split}t\partial_{t}|\mu^{o}|^{2}+|\mu^{o}|^{2}\lesssim\left(\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}+\nu\varepsilon^{2}\right){\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\varepsilon+\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}\right){\mathcal{E}}+\nu^{-1}{\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{D}}.\end{split}

Similarly, we have

\begin{split}\left|\bar{b}+\frac{\bar{a}\bar{c}}{\Gamma}\right|\left|(t\partial_{t}+1/2)\mu^{e}\right|\lesssim\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}+\nu\varepsilon^{2}+\left(\varepsilon+\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}\right){\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\nu^{-1}{\mathcal{D}},\end{split}

(6.35)

and

\begin{split}t\partial_{t}|\mu^{e}|^{2}+|\mu^{e}|^{2}\lesssim\left(\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}+\nu\varepsilon^{2}\right){\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\varepsilon+\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}\right){\mathcal{E}}+\nu^{-1}{\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{D}}.\end{split}

(6.36)

While by taking
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{V}
inner product of (
6.5
) with

, we get, by

6.5
-

, that

\begin{split}&t\partial_{t}E_{\varepsilon}[\omega^{R}]+D_{\varepsilon}[\omega^{R}]-C\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\omega^{R}\right\|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\left(\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}+\nu\varepsilon^{2}\right){\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\varepsilon+\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}\right){\mathcal{E}}+\nu^{-1}{\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{D}}\\
&\quad+\nu^{-1}(\varepsilon^{M}+\varepsilon^{\sigma_{2}}){\mathcal{D}}+\varepsilon^{2}{\mathcal{D}}+\varepsilon^{M+1}(|t\partial_{t}\mu^{o}|+|t\partial_{t}\mu^{e}|+{\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}){\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

which together with (

) and (

) implies

\begin{split}&t\partial_{t}E_{\varepsilon}[\omega^{R}]+D_{\varepsilon}[\omega^{R}]\\
&\lesssim\left(\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}+\nu\varepsilon^{2}\right){\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\varepsilon+\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}\right){\mathcal{E}}+\nu^{-1}{\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{D}}+\nu^{-1}\left(\varepsilon^{M}+\varepsilon^{\sigma_{2}}\right){\mathcal{D}}+\varepsilon^{2}{\mathcal{D}}\\
&\quad+\varepsilon^{M+1}\left(\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}+\nu\varepsilon^{2}+\left(\varepsilon+\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}\right){\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\nu^{-1}{\mathcal{D}}+{\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right){\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

(6.37)

By summarizing (

6.36
) and (

), we find

\begin{split}&t\partial_{t}{\mathcal{E}}+{\mathcal{D}}\lesssim\left(\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M+1}+\nu\varepsilon^{2}\right){\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\varepsilon+\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{M}+\nu^{-1}\varepsilon^{\sigma_{2}}\right){\mathcal{D}}+\nu^{-1}{\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{D}}.\end{split}

(6.38)

For any given

, we take
\sigma_{2}=M>\max(\frac{5-s}{1-s},\frac{2}{1-s})=\frac{5-s}{1-s}
, which ensures that

Then by virtue of (

(6.39)

Now recalling (

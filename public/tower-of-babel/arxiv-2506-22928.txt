Douglas–Rachford for multioperator comonotone inclusions with applications to multiblock optimization
Jan Harold Alcantara Minh N. Dao Akiko Takeda

Abstract
We study the convergence of the adaptive Douglas–Rachford (aDR) algorithm for solving a multioperator inclusion problem involving the sum of maximally comonotone operators. To address such problems, we adopt a product space reformulation that accommodates nonconvex-valued operators, which is essential when dealing with comonotone mappings. We establish convergence of the aDR method under comonotonicity assumptions, subject to suitable conditions on the algorithm parameters and comonotonicity moduli of the operators. Our analysis leverages the Attouch–Théra duality framework, which allows us to study the convergence of the aDR algorithm via its application to the dual inclusion problem. As an application, we derive a multiblock ADMM-type algorithm for structured convex and nonconvex optimization problems by applying the aDR algorithm to the operator inclusion formulation of the KKT system. The resulting method extends to multiblock and nonconvex settings the classical duality between the Douglas–Rachford algorithm and the alternating direction method of multipliers in the convex two-block case. Moreover, we establish convergence guarantees for both the fully convex and strongly convex-weakly convex regimes. Keywords. adaptive Douglas–Rachford; multioperator inclusion; comonotone operator; product space reformulation; Attouch–Théra dual; multiblock optimization; ADMM

1.1
Motivation

The Douglas–Rachford algorithm for comonotone operators is of particular interest because such operators naturally arise in the convergence analysis of the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) for linearly constrained multiblock problems of the form

\min_{u_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}}\,f_{1}(u_{1})+\cdots+f_{m}(u_{m})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{m}L_{i}u_{i}=b,

(1.2)

where
b\in\mathcal{H}
, each
f_{i}:\mathcal{H}_{i}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is a proper closed function, and
L_{i}:\mathcal{H}_{i}\to\mathcal{H}
is a bounded linear operator. This connection becomes especially relevant when the functions
f_{i}
exhibit a mix of strong and weak convexity, as will be elaborated in detail in
Section

5
.

This correspondence was observed by Bartz, Campoy, and Phan
[
4
]
in the two-block case:

\min_{u_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}}\,f_{1}(u_{1})+f_{2}(u_{2})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad L_{1}u_{1}-u_{2}=0,

(1.3)

where
f_{1}
is strongly convex and
f_{2}
is weakly convex. Under suitable assumptions, critical points of the Lagrangian associated with (
1.3
) are indeed solutions of the original problem and correspond to solutions of the inclusion problem
[
4
, Lemma 4.3 and Proposition 4.1]

\text{Find }x\in\mathcal{H}\text{ such that }0\in A_{1}(x)+A_{2}(x),

(1.4)

where the operators
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are defined by

A_{1}\coloneqq-L_{1}\circ(\partial f_{1})^{-1}\circ(-L_{1}^{\top}),\quad A_{2}\coloneqq(\widehat{\partial}f_{2})^{-1},

(1.5)

with the subdifferential and inverse mappings defined as in
Section

2.1
. It is shown in
[
4

that

and
A_{2}
are strongly and weakly comonotone, respectively.
Moreover, the Douglas–Rachford (DR) algorithm applied to the inclusion (
1.4
) with operators
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
defined in (
1.5
) is equivalent to the ADMM iteration for (
1.3
) under suitable conditions
[
4
, Lemmas 4.6 and 4.8]
. Leveraging this equivalence, the convergence of ADMM for (
1.3
) with strongly convex

and weakly convex

was established via the DR framework, using the properties of strongly and weakly comonotone operators
A_{1}
and
A_{2}

[
15
]
to broader problems involving both strongly and weakly convex functions.

However, extending this framework to the multiblock setting remains an open question, as the Douglas–Rachford algorithm for multioperator inclusion problems involving comonotone operators is itself not yet explored.

\mathbf{F}_{\rm Pierra}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq A_{1}(x_{1})\times\cdots\times A_{m}(x_{m})

1.3
Our approach and contributions

In this work, we use the product space reformulation proposed in
[
1
]
, which introduces a slight but crucial modification of Campoy’s formulation (
1.7
) to accommodate nonconvex-valued operators, such as comonotone ones. In particular, the flexible reformulation is given by

\text{Find}~\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{H}^{m-1}~\text{such that }0\in\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x}),

(1.8)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbf{F}_{\rm Campoy}(\mathbf{x})=A_{1}(x_{1})\times\cdots\times A_{m-1}(x_{m-1}),

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{x})+N_{\mathbf{D}_{m-1}}(\mathbf{x}),

(1.10)

with

\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\{\frac{1}{m-1}(v,\ldots,v):v\in A_{m}(x_{1})\}&\text{if}~\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m-1})\in\mathbf{D}_{m-1}\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Indeed,

0\in\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists x\in\mathcal{H}~\text{s.t.}~\mathbf{x}=(x,\dots,x)~\text{and}~0\in A_{1}(x)+\cdots+A_{m}(x).

(1.11)

With this product space reformulation, we are able to apply the adaptive Douglas–Rachford (aDR) algorithm for two-operator inclusion problems proposed in
[
13
]
to the reformulated problem (
1.8
). The resulting algorithm is detailed in
Algorithm

1
in
Section

4
. Our main contributions are twofold:

1.

We establish the convergence of the adaptive Douglas–Rachford algorithm for multioperator inclusion problems where each
A_{i}
is maximally
\sigma_{i}
-comonotone, with
\sigma_{i}\geq 0
for
i=1,\dots,m{-}1
and
\sigma_{m}\leq 0
. Convergence is guaranteed under suitable assumptions on the monotonicity moduli and algorithm parameters; see
Theorem

4.12
. Our analysis relies on the Attouch–Théra duality framework
[
2
]
, which facilitates the analysis of the aDR algorithm on the dual inclusion problem. As a by-product, our results resolve (in the negative) an open question posed in
[
3
, Remark 4.2]
regarding the strong convergence of the shadow sequence in the case

and
\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}>0
. We also improve the result in

by establishing the weak convergence of the shadow sequence under less restrictive assumptions.

2.

We derive a multiblock ADMM algorithm with convergence guarantees for solving problem (
1.2
).

(a)

This result generalizes to the multiblock case the well-known duality between the Douglas–Rachford and ADMM algorithms for two-block problems in the convex setting, as well as its extension to the strongly convex-weakly convex setting in
[
4
]
. Notably, the derived multiblock ADMM algorithm preserves this duality structure even when the involved functions are not convex. In particular, the algorithm corresponds to the Douglas–Rachford method applied to the multioperator inclusion associated with the (nonsmooth) KKT system of (

(b)

), the proposed multiblock ADMM algorithm enjoys theoretical convergence guarantees in both the fully convex case (i.e., each
f_{i}
is convex) and the strongly convex-weakly convex setting (i.e.,
f_{i}
is strongly convex for
i=1,\dots,m{-}1
, and

is weakly convex).

1.4
Organization of the paper

Section

2
reviews key definitions and results on generalized monotone and comonotone operators, as well as existing convergence results for the adaptive Douglas–Rachford (aDR) algorithm in the two-operator setting. In
Section

3
, we revisit the aDR algorithm through the lens of the Attouch–Théra duality framework. This perspective enables us to leverage convergence results established for generalized monotone operators to obtain

Using the refined convergence theory from

, we establish convergence results for the multioperator inclusion problem reformulated as the two-operator problem (

. A more delicate analysis is presented in

In

Throughout this paper,
\mathcal{H}
denotes a real Hilbert space endowed with the inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
and induced norm
\|\cdot\|
. For any real numbers
\alpha,\beta\in{\rm I}\!{\rm R}

{\left\|{\alpha x+\beta y}\right\|}^{2}=\alpha(\alpha+\beta){\left\|{x}\right\|}^{2}+\beta(\alpha+\beta){\left\|{y}\right\|}^{2}-\alpha\beta{\left\|{x-y}\right\|}^{2}.

(2.1)

When
\alpha+\beta\neq 0
, (

) is equivalent to

\alpha{\left\|{x}\right\|}^{2}+\beta{\left\|{y}\right\|}^{2}=\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}{\left\|{x-y}\right\|}^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha+\beta}{\left\|{\alpha x+\beta y}\right\|}^{2}.

(2.2)

The following identity is an important tool in our convergence analysis.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any nonzero real numbers
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\theta_{i}}=1
and for any
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
, it holds that

{\left\|{\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}}\right\|}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}{\left\|{u_{i}}\right\|}^{2}-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{1}{\theta_{i}\theta_{j}}{\left\|{\theta_{i}u_{i}-\theta_{j}u_{j}}\right\|}^{2}.

(2.3)

Proof.

From
[
6
, Lemma 2.14]
, we have

{\left\|{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\theta_{i}}x_{i}}\right\|}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2\theta_{i}\theta_{j}}{\left\|{x_{i}-x_{j}}\right\|}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\theta_{i}}{\left\|{x_{i}}\right\|}^{2},

for any
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
. Letting
x_{i}=\theta_{i}u_{i}
gives the desired result.
∎

Set-valued operators

A set-valued operator

2.3
Subdifferentials

A function
f:\mathcal{H}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is said to be
proper
if
\dom(f)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{H}:f(x)<\infty\}
is nonempty, and it is
closed
if it is lower semicontinuous. Given a proper and closed function
f
and a point
x\in\dom(f)
, the
regular subdifferential
of
f
is defined

\widehat{\partial}f(x)\coloneqq\left\{z\in\mathcal{H}:\liminf_{y\to x}\frac{f(y)-f(x)-\langle z,y-x\rangle}{\|y-x\|}\geq 0\right\}.

The
limiting subdifferential
of
f
at
x
, is given by

\partial f(x)\coloneqq\left\{z\in\mathcal{H}:\exists x^{k}\to x~\text{and}~z^{k}\in\widehat{\partial}f(x^{k})~\text{with}~f(x^{k})\to f(x)~\text{and}~z^{k}\to z\right\}.

If
x\notin\operatorname{dom}f
, both
\widehat{\partial}f(x)
and
\partial f(x)
are defined to be empty. When
f
is convex, the regular and limiting subdifferentials coincide and reduce to the classical subdifferential from convex analysis. In this case,

\widehat{\partial}f(x)=\partial f(x)=\left\{x^{*}\in\mathcal{H}:\forall y\in\mathcal{H},\;f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle\right\}.

2.4
The Adaptive Douglas–Rachford Algorithm for Two-Operator Inclusion

Let
(\kappa,\gamma,\delta,\lambda,\mu)\in{\rm I}\!{\rm R}^{5}_{++}

Numerical example

In this subsection, our aim is to provide a numerical illustration of the multiblock ADMM method
Algorithm

3
. To this end, we adopt the denoising problem from
[
4

, which includes both strongly and weakly convex components. The observed signal
\hat{\phi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is modeled as
\hat{\phi}=\phi+\xi
, with
\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the true signal and
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I)
Gaussian noise.:

\hat{\phi}=\phi+\xi.

(5.17)

The goal is to accurately recover
u\approx\phi
from the noisy data
\hat{\phi}
. A widely used approach is to solve a total variation regularized problem of the form:

\min_{u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})\in{\rm I}\!{\rm R}^{n_{1}}\times{\rm I}\!{\rm R}^{n_{N}}}\ \frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\left\|{u_{i}-\hat{\phi}_{i}}\right\|}^{2}+\omega\,P(Du),

(5.18)

where
\hat{\phi}=(\hat{\phi}_{1},\dots\hat{\phi}_{N})\in{\rm I}\!{\rm R}^{n_{1}}\times{\rm I}\!{\rm R}^{n_{N}}
,
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{N}=n-1
,
\omega>0
is a regularization parameter,
P:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a sparsity-promoting penalty, and
D\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-1)\times n}
is the first-order difference matrix, i.e.,

D_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }j=i,\\
-1,&\text{if }j=i+1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The single block (
N=1
) model was considered in
[
4
]
. As in
[
4
]
, we use the minimax concave penalty function given by

P(w)\coloneqq P_{\tau}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}p_{\tau}(w_{i}),\quad\text{with}\quad p_{\tau}(t)=\begin{cases}|t|-\frac{t^{2}}{2\tau}&\text{if}~|t|\leq\tau,\\
\frac{\tau}{2}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Letting
u_{N+1}=Du
, (
5.18
) can be written in the form (
5.1
) with

,

f_{i}(u_{i})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}{\left\|{u_{i}-\hat{\phi}}\right\|}^{2}&\text{if }1\leq i\leq N,\\
\omega P_{\tau}(u_{N+1})&\text{if}~i=N+1,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{i}\coloneqq\begin{cases}D_{i}&\text{if }1\leq i\leq N,\\
-I_{n-1}&\text{if}~i=N+1,\end{cases}

where
D_{i}\in{\rm I}\!{\rm R}^{(n-1)\times n_{i}}
such that

. Note that

\rho_{i}
-convex with

for all

Problem data.

We generate a piecewise constant signal

using the following MATLAB code:

We set

6
Conclusion

We established the convergence of the adaptive Douglas–Rachford (aDR) algorithm applied to a two-operator reformulation of the multioperator inclusion problem, in the case where all but one of the operators are strongly comonotone and the remaining operator is weakly comonotone. Previously, our work
[
1
]
investigated the Douglas–Rachford algorithm for multioperator inclusions where the operators involved are weakly and strongly monotone. Hence, the present work contributes to the broader understanding of the Douglas–Rachford method in nonmonotone settings involving more than two operators, extending the theory to a wider class of comonotone mappings. An interesting direction for future research is to explore whether the convergence result can be extended to settings where more than one operator is weakly comonotone. In addition, our convergence analysis relies on the Attouch–Théra duality framework. It would be worthwhile to investigate whether the analysis can be carried out directly in the primal formulation, and whether the techniques developed here can be generalized to other multioperator splitting schemes, such as the generalized framework recently proposed in
[
14
]
, which does not rely on the product space reformulation technique.

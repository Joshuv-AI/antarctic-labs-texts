On Some Hypergeometric Modularity Conjectures of Dawsey and McCarthy
Brian Grove

Abstract
. In recent work, the author, in collaboration with Allen, Long, and Tu, developed the Explicit Hypergeometric Modularity Method (EHMM), which establishes the modularity of a large class of hypergeometric Galois representations in dimensions two and three. One important application of the EHMM is the construction of an explicit family of eta-quotients, which we call the 𝕂 2 \mathbb{K}_{2} functions, from the hypergeometric background. In this article, we introduce an analogous family of eta-quotients, which we call the 𝕂 3 \mathbb{K}_{3} functions. These 𝕂 3 \mathbb{K}_{3} functions are constructed using the theory of weight one cubic theta functions originally developed by Jonathan and Peter Borwein. We then use the 𝕂 3 \mathbb{K}_{3} functions in the EHMM to resolve several hypergeometric modularity conjectures of Dawsey and McCarthy. Further, we provide applications to special L L -values of the 𝕂 3 \mathbb{K}_{3} functions and to the study of generalized Paley graphs.

Introduction

A central goal in number theory is to establish the modularity of motivic Galois representations, predicted by the Langlands program. For example, the modularity of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}

[
17
,
55
,
56
]
has led to many important advances in mathematics, such as the resolution of the Sato–Tate conjecture for non-CM elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}

[
7
,
18
,
26
,
54
]
. One important class of motivic Galois representations is hypergeometric Galois representations introduced by Katz
[
28
,
29
]
. The traces of these hypergeometric Galois representations involve certain character sums, such as the
H_{p}
function defined in (
1.1
). These hypergeometric character sums naturally arise when counting points on many algebraic varieties over finite fields, such as the Legendre family of elliptic curves
[
22
,
30
,
46
]

E_{\lambda}:y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\lambda)

and the Dwork family of hypersurfaces
[
6

,
41
,
53
,
57

X_{\lambda}^{d}:x_{1}^{d}+x_{2}^{d}+\cdots+x_{d}^{d}=d\lambda x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{d}.

Further, the explicit nature of these hypergeometric representations has led to an abundance of new identities and transformations for special
L
-values of many holomorphic modular forms
[

,

,
51

, such as the
\mathbb{K}_{2}
functions in (

).

Another important application of hypergeometric Galois representations lies in graph theory. For example, take a fixed finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
a prime power, as the vertex set of a graph. Then draw an edge between two vertices
a,b
if and only if

is a nonzero square in

. Graphs constructed in this way are called Paley graphs
[

,
35
,
48

[

,

,

,

,
19
,
21
,
36
,
43
,
44
]
using hypergeometric character sums. The desire to match these hypergeometric character sums with coefficients of modular forms has motivated the papers
[
3
,
4
,
20
]
and this article. The necessary hypergeometric background is as follows.

Fix a positive integer
n
and consider the multisets
\alpha=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
and
\beta=\{b_{1}=1,b_{2},\ldots,b_{n}\}
of rational numbers with
b_{2},\ldots,b_{n}\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
. The collection
\textrm{HD}=\{\alpha,\beta\}
is called a

and we also assume that

is
primitive
so

for
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Further, the least positive common denominator of

is defined as

. Now let

be a prime,
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
the multiplicative group for the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}=\langle\omega\rangle
the associated character group, and

(1.1)

H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&\ldots&a_{n}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&b_{2}&\ldots&b_{n}\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\;;\,\omega\right]:=\frac{1}{1-p}\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{g(\omega^{k+(p-1)a_{i}})g(\omega^{-k-(p-1)b_{i}})}{g(\omega^{(p-1)a_{i}})g(\omega^{-(p-1)b_{i}})}\omega^{k}((-1)^{n}\lambda),

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and

g(\chi)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\chi(x)\zeta_{p}^{x}

is the Gauss sum associated to the multiplicative character
\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}
. The
H_{p}
function was originally introduced by McCarthy
[
40
]
and further developed by Beukers, Cohen, Mellit
[

]
, and others. Another important component of the traces of hypergeometric Galois representations are character twists that arise when relating the
H_{p}
function to the

coefficients of modular forms. The relevant characters are defined as follows. Take
M
as defined earlier and to each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
coprime to

we can associate to the residue field

of size

(1.2)

\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(\frac{i}{M}\right)(x):=\left(\frac{x}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)_{M}^{i}\equiv x^{(q-1)\frac{i}{M}}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}

for all

. For example, if
\mathfrak{p}
is coprime to

then
\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}(1/2)
is the quadratic character for the residue field. Further, we denote the Legendre symbol as

throughout.

2.
The Explicit Hypergeometric Modularity Method (EHMM)

We now recall the EHMM, as developed in
[
3
,
4
]
, in preparation for the proof of
Theorem

1
. The EHMM includes two main parts, computing the target modular form using hypergeometric information and establishing several congruences between various types of hypergeometric functions.

An illustrative example of the EHMM involves a family of modular forms called the
\mathbb{K}_{2}
functions, as defined in
[
4
]
and further studied in
[
3
,
51
]
. We will now recall the EHMM for this particular family of modular forms.

Part I: Computing the Modular Form

The first part is an algorithm to go from a hypergeometric datum
HD
to a corresponding modular form
f_{\textrm{HD}}
. Computing the modular form
f_{\textrm{HD}}
involves combining the Euler integral formula for certain classical
{}_{3}F_{2}
and
{}_{4}F_{3}
hypergeometric functions
[
22
, Equation (3.2)]
with the theory of arithmetic triangle groups, in particular the Schwarz map
[
22
, Section 3.2.2]
. This approach to computing modular forms using hypergeometric information and triangle groups was motivated by earlier work of Li, Long, and Tu
[
33
,
34
]
.

Consider hypergeometric data of the form

(2.1)

\textrm{HD}_{\mathbb{K}_{2}}(r,s)=\{\{1/2,1/2,r\},\{1,1,s\}\},

for
r,s\in\mathbb{Q}
. We now restrict the
(r,s)
pairs to a particular subset of
\mathbb{Q}^{2}
in order to ensure convergence in the Euler integral formula, exclude degenerate cases, and to guarantee holomorphic congruence modular forms. The chosen subset is defined as

(2.2)

\mathbb{S}_{2}=\{(r,s)\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}\,|\,0<r<s<3/2,r\neq 1,s\neq 1/2,24s\in\mathbb{Z},8(r+s)\in\mathbb{Z}\},

as in
[
3
,
4
]
. The cardinality of
\mathbb{S}_{2}
is 167. Now if
(r,s)\in\mathbb{S}_{2}
the well-known Euler integral formula
[
22
, Equation (3.2)]
applied to the
{}_{3}F_{2}(\textrm{HD}_{\mathbb{K}_{2}}(r,s);1)
functions gives

(2.3)

_{3}F_{2}(\textrm{HD}_{\mathbb{K}_{2}}(r,s);1)=\frac{1}{B(r,s-r)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{r}(1-t)^{s-r-1}\cdot\,_{2}F_{1}(\{1/2,1/2\},\{1,1\};t)\,\frac{dt}{t},

where
B(x,y)
is the Beta function and
{}_{2}F_{1}(\{1/2,1/2\},\{1,1\};t)
is a
{}_{2}F_{1}
hypergeometric function over the complex numbers with parameter
t

[
22
, Equation (3.1)]
. The idea now is to evaluate the differential of (
2.3
) at an appropriate Hauptmodul. In particular, the Schwarz map
[
22
, Section 3.2.2]
, gives an association between the
{}_{2}F_{1}(\{1/2,1/2\},\{1,1\};t)
function and the congruence subgroup
\Gamma(2)
. Therefore, we are led to the modular lambda function

\lambda(\tau)=16\frac{\eta(\tau/2)^{8}\eta(2\tau)^{16}}{\eta(\tau)^{24}},

It is well-known that
\lambda(\tau)
is a Hauptmodul for
\Gamma(2)
. Now evaluating the differential of (
2.3
) at
t=\lambda(\tau)
leads to

(2.4)

Part II: Several congruences modulo
p^{2}

Now the other main part of the EHMM consists of combining various congruences modulo
p^{2}
, for primes
p\equiv 1\pmod{M}
. Assume that
p
is an ordinary prime. The first key observation is that proving an equality between the
H_{p}(\textrm{HD};1;\mathfrak{p})
function and the Fourier coefficients
a_{p}(f_{\textrm{HD}}^{\sharp})
, up to an appropriate character twist, is equivalent to proving a congruence modulo
p^{2}
under the chosen list of assumptions. This reduction is possible due to the Deligne bound on
a_{p}(f_{\textrm{HD}}^{\sharp})
and a Weil-type bound on the
H_{p}(\textrm{HD};1;\mathfrak{p})
function due to Katz
[
28
]
.

For example, if
\textrm{HD}=\textrm{HD}_{\mathbb{K}_{2}}(1/2,1)=\{\{1/2,1/2,1/2\},\{1,1,1\}\}
then the zig-zag procedure, as discussed in
[
50
]
, gives that the (hypergeometric) weight

of
HD
is 3. Then
Theorem

4
combined with (
4.1
) implies that
H_{p}(\textrm{HD};1;\mathfrak{p})=O(p^{\frac{w-1}{2}})=O(p)
in this case. Further, the
a_{p}(f_{\textrm{HD}}^{\sharp})
values must have the same order of magnitude if the
H_{p}(\textrm{HD};1;\mathfrak{p})
and
a_{p}(f_{\textrm{HD}}^{\sharp})
values agree up to an appropriate character twist.

Now the EHMM relies on three main congruences. The first two congruences are bridged by the truncated hypergeometric series

F(\textrm{HD};1)_{p-1}=\,_{n}F_{n-1}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{n}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&b_{2}&\cdots&b_{n}\end{matrix}\;;\;1\right]_{p-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(a_{j})_{k}}{k!\prod_{j=2}^{n}(b_{j})_{k}}

when
n=3,4
, where
(a)_{0}=1
and
(a)_{k}:=a(a+1)\cdots(a+k-1)
for
k>0
. In particular, the first congruence relates the
H_{p}(\textrm{HD};1;\mathfrak{p})
function to the
F(\textrm{HD};1)_{p-1}
function by the Gross-Koblitz formula and various tools from
p
-adic analysis mainly coming from
[
37
]
. Then the second congruence relates the
F(\textrm{HD};1)_{p-1}
function to a
p
-adic unit root
\mu_{p}(\textrm{HD};1)
by the residue sum technique, as used in
[
2
,
38
]
. Now the final congruence relates the
p
-adic unit root
\mu_{p}(\textrm{HD};1)
to the coefficients
a_{p}(f_{\textrm{HD}}^{\sharp})
, up to an appropriate character, modulo
p^{2}
using the modularity of hypergeometric Galois representations and the explicit computation of
f_{\textrm{HD}}^{\sharp}
from Part I of the EHMM. See
[
4
]
for more details on the EHMM.

We now recall a special case of the EHMM. See Theorem 2.1 of
[
4
]
for the EHMM in full generality. In particular, we restrict to the case of

with
\textrm{HD}=\{\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\},\{1,1,b_{3}\}\}
such that

\gamma(\textrm{HD}):=1+b_{3}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}\neq 1.

2
include the cases with hypergeometric data
\textrm{HD}_{\textrm{DM}}(u,v)
. Now the EHMM has two main assumptions. The first assumption involves the existence of a holomorphic cusp form

computed from the Euler integral formula, such as the

) or the

4.
Hypergeometric Character Sums and Extendability

The traces of hypergeometric Galois representations involve the
H_{p}
function, as mentioned earlier. However, it is sometimes more convenient to use the following normalization of the
H_{p}
function

(4.1)

\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&\ldots&a_{n}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&b_{2}&\ldots&b_{n}\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\;;\,\omega\right]:=\prod_{i=2}^{n}J(\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}(a_{i}),\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}(b_{i}-a_{i}))\cdot\,H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&\ldots&a_{n}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&b_{2}&\ldots&b_{n}\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\;;\,\omega\right].

The
\mathbb{P}
function in (
4.1
) was originally defined as the
{}_{n}\mathbb{P}_{n-1}
function in
[
22
]
. Further, the shorthand notation
\mathbb{P}(\textrm{HD};1;\mathfrak{p})
will be used, in analogy with the
H_{p}
case. The traces of hypergeometric Galois representations involve a particular character twist of the
\mathbb{P}(\textrm{HD};1;\mathfrak{p})
function. In particular, the precise foundational results of Katz on hypergeometric Galois representations are recalled below.

Theorem 4

(Katz
[
28
,
29
]
)
.

Let
\ell
be a prime. Given a primitive hypergeometric datum
\textnormal{HD}=\{\{r_{1},\cdots,r_{n}\},\{q_{1}=1,q_{2},\cdots,q_{n}\}\}
with
M=\textnormal{lcd}(\textnormal{HD})
and

\gamma(\textnormal{HD})=-1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(q_{i}-r_{i}),

for
any
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{M},1/M]\smallsetminus\{0\}
the following hold.

i).

There exists an
\ell
-adic Galois representation
\rho_{\{\textnormal{HD};\lambda\}}:G(M)\rightarrow GL(W_{\lambda})
unramified almost everywhere such that at each nonzero
prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{M},1/(M\ell\lambda)]
of norm
N(\mathfrak{p})=|\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{M})]/\mathfrak{p}|

{\rm Tr}\rho_{\{\textnormal{HD};\lambda\}}(\textnormal{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}})=(-1)^{n-1}\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}(r_{1})(-1)\cdot\mathbb{P}(\textnormal{HD};1/\lambda;\mathfrak{p}),

where
\textnormal{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
denotes the geometric Frobenius conjugacy class
of
G(M)
at
\mathfrak{p}
.

ii).

When
\lambda\neq 1
, the dimension
d:=dim_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}W_{\lambda}
equals
n
and all roots of the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{\{\textnormal{HD};\lambda\}}(\textnormal{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}})
are algebraic numbers and have the same absolute value
N(\mathfrak{p})^{(n-1)/2}
under all archimedean embeddings. If
\rho_{\{\textnormal{HD};\lambda\}}
is self-dual, namely isomorphic to any of its complex conjugates, then
W_{\lambda}
admits a non-degenerate alternating (resp. symmetric) bilinear pairing if

n
is even and
\gamma(\textnormal{HD})\in\mathbb{Z}
(resp. otherwise).

iii).

When
\lambda=1
, in the self-dual case the dimension is
n-1
. All roots of the Frobenius eigenvalues at
\wp
have absolute value less or equal to
N(\mathfrak{p})^{(n-1)/2}
. If
\rho_{\{\textnormal{HD};\lambda\}}
is self-dual, it contains a subrepresentation that admits a non-degenerate alternating (resp. symmetric) bilinear pairing if

n
is even and
\gamma(\textnormal{HD})\in\mathbb{Z}
(resp. otherwise). For this subrepresentation, the roots of the characteristic polynomial of
\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
have absolute value exactly
N(\mathfrak{p})^{(n-1)/2}
.

7.
An Application to Graph Theory

In this section, we give an overview of the connections between hypergeometric character sums and counting certain statistics of graphs with vertex set
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We then resolve a question of Dawsey and McCarthy posed in
[
19
, Section 8]
by using the modularity result in the
(u,v)=(3,3)
case of
Theorem

1
. Several authors, such as
[
10
,
11

12

13

19
,
21
,
35
,
43
,
44
,
48
]
, have studied the connection between graph theory and hypergeometric character sums. The philosophy in this area is to count a statistic of a certain family of graphs
G
with vertex set
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, such as the number of complete subgraphs or cliques of a fixed order
m\geq 2
in
G
, by counting with Jacobi sums. In certain cases, these combinations of Jacobi sums agree with special values of hypergeometric character sums, such as the
H_{p}
function defined in (

[
19
,
44
]
, these counts involving hypergeometric character sums have been used to recover or improve the sharpest known bounds on various types of Ramsey numbers.

One such choice for
G
are Paley graphs, as discussed briefly in the introduction. Let
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
be a prime power, and define
S_{2}
to be the subgroup of squares in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. The Paley graph of order
q
, denoted by
G_{2}(q)
, is the graph with vertex set
\mathbb{F}_{q}
where
ab
is an edge with
a-b\in S_{2}
. It is clear that
-1\in S_{2}
when
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, so the Paley graphs are undirected. Furthermore, Paley graphs are known to be connected and self-complementary
[
35
]
.

One important application of Paley graphs and their generalizations involves determining lower bounds for multicolored diagonal Ramsey numbers. Let
\mathcal{K}_{m}(G)
denote the number of order
m
complete subgraphs contained in a family of graphs
G
, such as the Paley graphs
G_{2}(q)
. In the work of Dawsey and McCarthy
[
19

it is observed that the largest value of
q
such that
\mathcal{K}_{m}(G_{2}(q))=0

q+1\leq R(m,m)
, where
R(m,m)
is the two-color diagonal Ramsey number associated with
m

m=4

R(4,4)=18
, by work of Greenwood and Gleason
[
25
]
. The lower bound of 18 is equivalent to the assertions that
\mathcal{K}_{4}(G_{2}(17))=0
and
\mathcal{K}_{4}(G_{2}(q))>0
for
q>17
. In the
q=p
case the following closed formula for
\mathcal{K}_{4}(G_{2}(p))
was established by Evans, Pulham, and Sheehan
[
21
]
.

Theorem 5

(
[
21
]
)

Suppose that
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Write
p=x^{2}+y^{2}
for integers
x
and
y

y
even. Then

\mathcal{K}_{4}(G_{2}(p))=\frac{p(p-1)\left((p-9)^{2}-4y^{2}\right)}{2^{9}\cdot 3}.

A natural modification of a Paley graph is to replace quadratic residues with
k

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}

k

\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where

a-b\in S_{k}

\mathcal{K}_{m}(G_{k}(q))

It is then natural to wonder if Theorem

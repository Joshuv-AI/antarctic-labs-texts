A regularized theta lift on the symmetric space of SL N \mathrm{SL}_{N}
Romain Branchereau

Abstract
We define a regularized lift from harmonic weak Maass forms of weight 2 − N 2-N to differential forms of degree N − 1 N-1 on the symmetric space SL N ​ ( ℝ ) / SO ​ ( N ) \mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(N) , that are smooth outside of certain modular symbols. We show that this lift is adjoint to the derivative of a theta lift. We compute the periods of the regularized lift over tori and relate them to Fourier coefficients of Hilbert-Eisenstein series.



1.  Introduction

Regularized lifts are theta lifts from harmonic weak Maass forms to functions on orthogonal Grassmannians
O(p,q)/O(p)\times O(q)
with singularities along smaller sub-Grassmannians. The regularization is done by taking the limit of an integral over a truncated fundamental domain. First introduced by Borcherds in his seminal work
[
4
,
5
]
, many variations of these regularized lifts (now often called Borcherds lifts) have been considered since then, for example in
[
16
,
20
,
1
,
19
,
17
]
. Most notably, Bruinier-Funke
[
11
]
constructed a lift from weak harmonic Maass forms to differential forms with singularities along special cycles, and which is adjoint to the Kudla-Millson lift. Since the work of Bruinier
[
9
,
15
]
and Bruinier-Funke
[
11
]
, it is known that these regularized lifts satisfy certain current equations and play an important role in arithmetic geometry. When the signature is
(p,2)
and the Grassmannian is a hermitian domain, these regularized lifts are Green currents for special cycles. For general signature
(p,q)
, the regularized lift of Bruinier-Funke defines a differential character.

When the regularized lift is a function on the Grassmannian, it can be evaluated at special points associated with CM extensions. There is an extensive literature on these special values of regularized lifts, mainly in the case
O(p,2)
– see for example
[
35
,
36
,
12
,
14
,
10
,
18
,
30
,
31
,
7
]
. This was greatly motivated by the work of Gross-Zagier
[
23
]
and Gross-Kohnen-Zagier
[
21
]
. In particular, in signature
(2,2)
these values were conjectured by Gross-Zagier to be logarithms of algebraic numbers, which has been recently proved by Li
[
31
]
and Bruinier–Li–Yang
[
7
]
. An important aspect of these special values is that they are related to Fourier coefficients of derivatives of Eisenstein series; see
[
12
,
14
,
10
]
. This plays an important role in the proof of Li
[
31
]
of the conjecture of Gross-Zagier. There have been a few results about special values of regularized lifts in signature
(n,1)
. First, by Bruinier-Schwagenscheidt
[
13
]
, and more recently by Herrero-Imamoğlu-Pippich-Schwagenscheidt
[
24
]
in signature
(3,1)
.

In this paper, we consider a regularized lift similar to the construction of Bruinier-Funke
[
11
]
, lifting weak harmonic Maass forms to singular differential forms on a symmetric space. Instead of an orthogonal symmetric space, we consider the symmetric space
S=\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(N)
. We show that this lift is adjoint to the derivative of a cohomological lift on
S
. In particular, we replace the evaluation at a special point by the integral over a cycle attached to an algebraic torus, and relate it to the derivative of a Hilbert-Eisenstein series.

2.2. Differential forms

Over

\displaystyle E=G\times_{K}V_{\mathbb{R}}

[g,v]\equiv[gk,k^{-1}v]

(2.19)

Lemma 3.3

Lemma 3.4

Let

1.

\displaystyle R(z_{1},\mathbf{v})+Q(\mathbf{v})=\lVert g_{1}^{-1}v\rVert\lVert g_{1}^{t}w\rVert\geq\sqrt{2}DC_{U}\lVert v\rVert\lVert w\rVert

3.3. Local integrability

Let
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
be an
(N-1)
-simplex. A chain
c\colon\Delta\longrightarrow S_{\Gamma}
is smooth if there exists an open neighbourhood
\Delta\subset U\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
of the simplex such that the map extends to a smooth immersion
c\colon U\longrightarrow S_{\Gamma}
.

When the cycle
c
intersects
S_{\mathbf{v}}
transversally in
S
, then the preimage
\bar{c}
of
c
in
X
intersect
X_{\mathbf{v}}
transversally. In a neighbourhood of an intersection point
X_{\mathbf{v}}\cap\bar{c}
, the restriction of the singular form looks like a differential form on a ball
B_{\delta}(0)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with a singularity at most
1/\lVert x\rVert^{N-1}
at the origin. This explains that the form is integrable over transverse cycles, which is proved in the following proposition.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
c\colon\Delta\longrightarrow S_{\Gamma}
be a smooth
N-1
chain that intersects
S_{f}(\phi)
transversally. Then the integral of
\Phi(z_{1},f,\phi)
over
c
converges, and we have

\displaystyle\int_{c}\Phi(z_{1},f,\phi)

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(\int_{F_{T}}\int_{c}E_{\psi}(z_{1},\tau,\phi)f(\tau)\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}-\log(T)\int_{c}Q_{\psi}(z_{1},f,\phi)\bigg).

(3.69)

Proof.

Let
\pazocal{F}
be a fundamental domain for
\Gamma
is
S
. Let
\pi_{\Gamma}\colon S\rightarrow S_{\Gamma}
be the projection. By subdividing
\Delta
if necessary, we can write
c
as a finite sum of cycles
c_{i}\colon\Delta\longrightarrow S_{\Gamma}
such that each
c_{i}
is a smooth embedding and the image of
c_{i}(\Delta)
is contained in an open subset
U_{i}\subset S_{\Gamma}
that is small enough to be diffeomorphic to
\pi_{\Gamma}^{-1}(U_{i})\subset\pazocal{F}
in the fundamental domain. The integral over
c
is then

\displaystyle\int_{c}\Phi(z_{1},f,\phi)=\sum_{i}\int_{c_{i}}\Phi(z_{1},f,\phi),

(3.70)

where we view
c_{i}
as a chain in
\pazocal{F}\subset S
, that intersects each submanifold
S_{\mathbf{v}}\subset\widetilde{S}_{f}(\phi)=\pi^{-1}(S_{f}(\phi))
transversally. Let us show that these integrals converge (we will write
c
instead of

).

We proceed as in Theorem
3.1
and write

\displaystyle\Phi(z_{1},f,\phi)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\widetilde{\Phi}_{\gamma}(z_{1},f,\phi)+\int_{F_{1}}E_{\psi}(z_{1},\tau,\phi)f(\tau)\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}

(3.71)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}_{\gamma}(z_{1},f,\phi)\coloneqq\mathrm{CT}_{\rho=0}\left\{\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\int_{U_{1}\smallsetminus U_{T}}E_{\psi}(z_{1},\tau,\phi)|_{\gamma}\left(f|_{\gamma}\right)(\tau)y^{-\rho}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\right\}.

(3.72)

The integral over
F_{1}
defines a smooth form, so integrable over

and we can interchange the integrals:

\displaystyle\int_{c}\int_{F_{1}}E_{\psi}(z_{1},\tau,\phi)f(\tau)\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}=\int_{F_{1}}\int_{c}E_{\psi}(z_{1},\tau,\phi)f(\tau)\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}.

(3.73)

Hence, it is enough to show that
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\gamma}(z_{1},f,\phi)
is integrable. We can take
\gamma=1
without loss of generality. As in Proposition
3.2

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}_{1}(z_{1},f,\phi)

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(\int_{U_{1}\smallsetminus U_{T}}E_{\psi}(z_{1},\tau,\phi)f(\tau)\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}-a^{+}(f,0)e_{\psi}(z_{1},\phi)\log(T)\bigg).

(3.74)

The integral against
f^{-}
is also a smooth form since
f^{-}
is rapidly decreasing and
E_{\psi}
moderately growing as

. Thus is enough to show that the integral

\displaystyle\int_{c}\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(\int_{1}^{T}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}E_{\psi}(z_{1},\tau,\phi)f^{+}(\tau)\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}-a^{+}(f,0)e_{\psi}(z_{1},\phi)\log(T)\bigg)

(3.75)

against
f^{+}
converges and we can exchange the two integrals. By plugging-in the Fourier expansion and recalling that
ye_{\psi}(z_{1},\phi)
is the constant term of

(see (

\displaystyle\int_{c}\int_{1}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in V_{\mathrm{reg}}}\phi(\mathbf{v})b_{\psi}(z_{1},\sqrt{y}\mathbf{v})a^{+}(f,-Q(\mathbf{v}))\frac{dy}{y}.

(3.76)

By Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{c}\int_{1}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in V_{\mathrm{reg}}}\phi(\mathbf{v})b_{\psi}(z_{1},\sqrt{y}\mathbf{v})a^{+}(f,-Q(\mathbf{v}))\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{c}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in V_{\mathrm{reg}}}\phi(\mathbf{v})b_{\psi}(z_{1},\sqrt{y}\mathbf{v})a^{+}(f,-Q(\mathbf{v}))\frac{dy}{y}

(3.77)

where the integral on the left converges absolutely if and only if the integral on the right converges absolutely. Assuming the convergence, it follows from the Fourier expansion
of

that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{c}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in V_{\mathrm{reg}}}\phi(\mathbf{v})b_{\psi}(z_{1},\sqrt{y}\mathbf{v})a^{+}(f,-Q(\mathbf{v}))\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\left(\int_{U_{1}\smallsetminus U_{T}}\int_{c}E_{\psi}(z_{1},\tau,\phi)f^{+}(\tau)\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}-a^{+}(f,0)\int_{c}e_{\psi}(z_{1},\phi)\log(T)\right).

(3.79)

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function such that
f(x)=O(|x|^{-N-\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon>0
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\mathfrak{d}^{-1}\\
\mathrm{Tr}(\nu)=n\\
\nu_{l}<0\\
\nu_{k}>0,\ k\neq l\end{subarray}}f(|\nu_{l}|)

(4.71)

converges absolutely.

Proof.

Assume without of loss of generality that
l=N
. Let
L=\{\sigma(\nu)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\ |\ \nu\in\mathfrak{d}^{-1}\}
be the lattice as above. The subset

\displaystyle L_{<0}\coloneqq\{w=\sigma(\nu)\in L\ |\ w_{1},\dots,w_{N-1}>0,\ w_{N}<0,\ \mathrm{tr}(\nu)=n\}

(4.72)

is the intersection of the hyperplane
\mathrm{tr}(\nu)=n
with the quadrant spanned by the vector
e_{1},\dots,e_{N-1}
and
-e_{N}
(instead of
e_{N}
). Since the first

\displaystyle n=\mathrm{tr}(\nu)=|\nu_{1}|+\cdots+|\nu_{N-1}|-|\nu_{N}|=\lVert w_{\nu}\rVert_{1}-2|\nu_{N}|,

where

let

be the number of vectors in

that satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{d>n/m}\frac{r(dm)}{(dm-n)^{N+\epsilon}}.

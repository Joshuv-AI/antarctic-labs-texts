Sharp propagation of chaos in Rényi divergence
Matthew S. Zhang

Abstract
We establish sharp rates for propagation of chaos in Rényi divergences for interacting diffusion systems at stationarity. Building upon the entropic hierarchy established in 18 , we show that under strong isoperimetry and weak interaction conditions, one can achieve 𝖱 q ​ ( μ 1 ∥ π ) = O ~ ​ ( d ​ q 2 N 2 ) \mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu^{1}\mathbin{\|}\pi)=\widetilde{O}(\frac{dq^{2}}{N^{2}}) bounds on the q q -Rényi divergence.

Introduction

In this work, we study propagation of chaos in divergences stronger than entropy. Consider minimization of energy functionals of the following type,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\int V\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\frac{1}{2}\iint W(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\otimes 2}(x,y)+\int\log\mu\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,.

(1.1)

Write
\pi
for the minimizer of this energy functional, which exists and is unique under some convexity conditions of
\mathcal{E}
over the space of measures. If such a minimizer does exist, then it satisfies the following implicit equation:

\displaystyle\pi\propto\exp\Bigl(-V-\int W(\cdot-z)\,\mathrm{d}\pi(z)\Bigr)\,.

(1.2)

An alternate viewpoint describes
\pi
as the stationary law of a particle following the McKean–Vlasov equation, which describes the Wasserstein gradient flow of
\mathcal{E}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}Y_{t}=\Bigl(-\nabla V(Y_{t})-\int\nabla W(Y_{t}-\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}\operatorname{law}(Y_{t})(\cdot)\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\,.

(1.3)

The presence of an integral against
\operatorname{law}(Y_{t})
in (
1.3
) makes it difficult to simulate. To correct this, one considers a system of evolving particles,
(X_{t}^{i})_{t\geq 0,i\in[N]}
, with each
X_{t}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{i}=\Bigl(-\nabla V(X_{t}^{i})-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[N]\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}\nabla W(X_{t}^{i}-X_{t}^{j})\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\,.

(1.4)

Again, under mild conditions, this system admits a stationary measure

\displaystyle\mu^{1:N}(X^{1:N})\propto\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(X^{i})-\frac{1}{2(N-1)}\sum_{i\in[N],j\in[N]\backslash i}W(X^{i}-X^{j})\Bigr)\,.

(1.5)

One is naturally led to inquire how close
\mu^{1:N}
is to
\pi^{\otimes N}
under appropriate assumptions on
V,W
. This general question is known as (stationary)
propagation of chaos

More canonically, propagation of chaos is typically used to show evolving bounds along the dynamic systems given by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), which can be used to demonstrate properties at stationarity. However, the two phenomena are related and the techniques used are not particularly different.
.

Early results by
23
;
19
have led to quantitative bounds on the quality of this approximation, both in Wasserstein and relative entropy senses, which follow from a subadditivity type result
\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{2}^{2}(\mu^{i},\pi)\leq W_{2}^{2}(\mu^{1:N},\pi^{\otimes N})
. However, the result of
18
and subsequent work
(
17
;
22
)
has established that, under certain convexity conditions, we have bounds
\KL(\mu^{1}\mathbin{\|}\pi)\lesssim 1/N^{2}
, which is much smaller than the
1/N
typically obtained via central limit theorems or subadditivity.

Rényi divergences.

In this work, we are interested in stronger notions of convergence: namely, the
q
-Rényi divergences. For two measures
\mu,\nu
with
\mu\ll\nu
, we define for
q>1

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu\mathbin{\|}\nu)\coloneqq\frac{1}{q-1}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\bigl(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\nu}\bigr)^{q}\,,

(1.6)

with
\lim_{q\searrow 1}\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu\mathbin{\|}\nu)=\KL(\mu\mathbin{\|}\nu)
.
These divergences are a natural strengthening of the relative entropy, with guarantees in
q
-Rényi immediately implying closeness in relative entropy and total variation distance (and the Wasserstein distance under a transport inequality). Furthermore, they allow for fine-grained control of tail probabilities, which has implications for statistical procedures which use (
1.5
) as an approximation for (
1.2
) (or vice versa). See Corollary
2
and the following remark for some further discussions.

Establishing propagation of chaos in Rényi divergence is not simple. Firstly, the classical argument for both relative entropy and Wasserstein distances bounds the relative entropy
\KL(\mu^{1:N}\mathbin{\|}\pi^{\otimes N})\lesssim O(1)
, and then uses subadditivity to conclude. This, however, does not work in Rényi divergence, as the analogous subadditivity property does not hold in general, even when the second argument is a product measure.

The recent work of
12
studies propagation of chaos in
\chi^{2}
-divergence for interactions in the dynamical setting (i.e., for the solutions evolving along (
1.4
) and (
1.3
) outside of stationarity), which they then apply towards deriving measures which better approximate (compared to the mean-field dynamics) the finite-particle dynamics. This also relies on a hierarchical analysis, but one which is highly dependent on the boundedness of the interaction kernel. This is not easily extended to more general cases, and does not seem to generalize well beyond
q=2
.

Instead, we aim to directly apply the hierarchical approach of
18
. However, compared to the hierarchy in
18
, we cannot apply transport and log-Sobolev inequalities immediately to obtain a recursive system. This is because the Rényi analogue of the Fisher information involves the expectation of the square norm of the score
\nabla\log\frac{\mu^{1}}{\pi}
under a reweighted measure, which does not easily compare to a Wasserstein distance or another Rényi divergence.

Through some analytic innovations, we show that it suffices to demonstrate that the relative score
\nabla\log\frac{\mu^{1}}{\pi}
is sub-Gaussian under
\mu^{1}
with variance proxy which diminishes as
1/N^{2}
, for
N\gg 1
. To study this, we provide bounds on the Lipschitz constant of the relative score. This relies on a hierarchical argument which has a similar flavour as previous work
(
18
)
, but which aims to bound the Wasserstein distance between two conditional measures
\mu^{k+1\mid[k]}(\cdot\mid x^{[k]})
,
\mu^{k+1\mid[k]}(\cdot\mid\bar{x}^{[k]})
in terms of the distance of their conditioning points,
\lVert x^{[k]}-\bar{x}^{[k]}\rVert^{2}
.

This allows us to successfully study the propagation of chaos in Rényi divergences, showing the following:

If
\mu
,
\pi
satisfy sufficiently strong isoperimetric and smoothness conditions, then
\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu^{1}\mathbin{\|}\pi)\lesssim\widetilde{O}(\frac{dq^{2}}{N^{2}})
, for
N
sufficiently large.

This answers a conjecture posed in
16
, and also allows one to quantify the complexity of obtaining samples close to
\pi
in
q
-Rényi divergence. Furthermore, it serves as a natural extension of the work in
17
, translating to bounds on
\lVert\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{1}}{\mathrm{d}\pi}\rVert_{L^{q}(\pi)}
for
q\geq 1
.

1.1
Related work

The study of
propagation of chaos
has a venerable history which is not easily summarized; we touch only upon the most relevant works below. Beginning with the seminal work of
23
and
20
;
19
, quantitative chaos bounds on the order of
1/N
in
W_{2}^{2}
or
\KL
divergence have been shown by first bounding the divergence between
\mu^{1:N}
and
\pi^{\otimes N}
by something that is independent of
N
, and then applying subadditivity. This line of thought has been successfully generalized to the case of singular interactions of
1/\lVert x^{i}-x^{j}\rVert^{\alpha}
type
(
13
;
14
;
3
)
,
\alpha>0
, which permits the study of Coulomb and Riesz gases, as well as other physical systems. See
4
for a more complete survey.

A surprising result from
18
showed that this rate could be improved to
\widetilde{O}(k^{2}/N^{2})
under a “weak interaction” assumption (requiring a trade-off between the isoperimetric constant of
\pi
, and the smoothness of
W
) which is sharp for e.g., quadratic interactions and potentials. This was further developed in
17
;
10
to handle more general settings, and in
11
for the Fisher information. The work of
22
also significantly expanded the scope, removing the “weak interaction” assumption by relying on a “non-linear” log-Sobolev inequality.

We also mention that a similar “propagation of chaos” for minimizers of functionals of a more general form has been proposed and studied in works such as
6
;
5
;
25
. This covers some applications such as training dynamics for two-layer neural networks which do not fall under (
1.1
). However, this setting has not proven amenable to analysis using hierarchical techniques, and the
1/N^{2}
rate remains elusive in this setting.

The most relevant work to ours is
12
, which also provides a means of establishing higher order propagation of chaos in the dynamical setting. When the interaction is a.s. bounded, they show the sharp rate of
1/N^{2}
via hierarchical techniques reminiscent of
18
in the
\chi^{2}
-divergence (equivalent to
2
-Rényi), and which do not contain a “weak interaction” assumption. However, their analysis cannot be easily adapted to the case of unbounded-but-smooth interactions considered in this work. Finally,
2
offer a different proof for dynamical propagation of chaos in some
\chi^{p}
-norms, but which only works for short times and without quantitative
N
-dependence.

2.2
Background

Divergences/distances between probability measures

For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with finite second moments, we define

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}\mathbb{E}_{(X,Y)\sim\gamma}[\lVert X-Y\rVert^{2}]\,,

where
\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
is the set of measures on the product space
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with marginals
\mu,\nu
respectively. We define the relative entropy and Fisher information for
\mu\ll\nu
as, respectively

\displaystyle\KL(\mu\mathbin{\|}\nu)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\nu}\,,\qquad\mathsf{FI}(\mu\mathbin{\|}\nu)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\Bigl[\Bigl\lVert\,\nabla\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\nu}\,\Bigr\rVert^{2}\Bigr]\,.

Recalling the definition of the Rényi divergence in (
1.6
), we define the Rényi Fisher information as, with
\rho\coloneqq\mu/\nu
as usual,

\displaystyle\mathsf{RFI}_{q}(\mu\mathbin{\|}\nu)\coloneqq\frac{4}{q}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\lVert\nabla(\rho^{q/2})\rVert^{2}]}{\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\rho^{q}]}=q\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\psi^{q}\lVert\nabla\log\rho\rVert^{2}]\,,\qquad\psi^{q}\coloneqq\frac{\rho^{q-1}}{\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\rho^{q}]}\,.

Isoperimetric inequalities

We say a measure
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies a

inequality which holds with constant

if for all compactly supported and smooth

,

\displaystyle\operatorname{ent}_{\pi}f\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\Bigl[f\log\frac{f}{\mathbb{E}_{\pi}f}\Bigr]\leq\frac{C_{\LSI}}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\lVert\nabla f\rVert^{2}]\,.

(
\mathsf{LSI}
)

Additionally, we say that a measure
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\operatorname{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies Talagrand’s inequality with constant
C_{\mathsf{T}_{2}}
, which is implied by (

LSI

) with the same constant, if for all measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2,\operatorname{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\pi)\leq 2C_{\mathsf{T}_{2}}\KL(\mu\mathbin{\|}\pi)\,.

(
\mathsf{T}_{2}

Main results

We require a smoothness assumption on the potential

Assumption 1
.

The potential

is
\beta_{W}
-smooth, implying that for all

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla W(x)-\nabla W(y)\rVert\leq\beta_{W}\lVert x-y\rVert\,.

We also assume a notion of strong isoperimetry, given below.

Assumption 2
.

Assume that
\mu^{k+1:k+\ell\mid[k]}(\cdot\mid x^{[k]})
satisfies (

LSI

) with constant
\bar{C}_{\LSI}
uniformly in
x^{[k]}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
for all
k\in[N-1]
,
\ell\in[N-k]
, and that a minimizer
\pi
exists for (
1.1
) and satisfies (

LSI

) with the same constant. Furthermore, suppose that
\mu^{[k]}
also satisfies (

LSI

) with the same constant.

Remark.

This requires that the finite-particle measure
\mu^{[k]}
satisfy (

LSI

) with a constant independent of
N
, for which it suffices that
\mu^{[N]}
satisfy (

LSI

) with the same constant. See §
3
for some discussion of when this holds.

We now introduce the following notion of very weak interaction, which is a strengthening of the weak interaction condition found in the original sharp propagation of chaos result
(
18
)
. It does not reflect the subsequent improvement in
22
, which is able to dispense with this assumption using a non-linear variant of the log-Sobolev inequality. As far as we are aware, the techniques of the latter paper cannot be adapted for our argument.

Assumption 3

(Very weak interaction)
.

Let
\beta_{W}\bar{C}_{\LSI}\lesssim 1
for a small enough implied constant.

Broadly speaking, this assumption permits some degree of non-convexity in the problem, although this amount needs to be on a smaller scale than the smoothness parameter of
W
.

Theorem 1

(Sharp propagation of chaos under very weak interaction)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
hold, and
N=\widetilde{\Omega}(1\vee\sqrt{d}k^{3/2}q)
for a sufficiently large implied constant. Then,

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu^{[k]}\mathbin{\|}\pi^{\otimes k})\lesssim\widetilde{O}\Bigl(\frac{dk^{3}q^{2}}{N^{2}}\Bigr)\,.

In particular, for
k=O(1)
, this implies sharp Rényi propagation of chaos rates with order
O(N^{-2})
.

Remark.

The scaling
k^{3}
is suboptimal and in particular does not recover a sensible result when
k/N\asymp 1
. There are also additional
\log k
factors hidden by the
\widetilde{O}
-notation. However, the current proof does not provide an easy avenue to recover the optimal
k^{2}
scaling.

Remark.

The above also suffices to obtain bounds on
\lVert\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{[k]}}{\mathrm{d}\pi^{\otimes k}}\rVert_{L^{q}(\pi^{\otimes k})}-1
when the
q
-Rényi is sufficiently small, via a Taylor expansion of the logarithm.

Remark.

As a corollary, since it is possible to obtain sampling guarantees in Rényi divergence from
\mu^{1:N}
via many of the results in
16
, we can also obtain samples
x^{1}
which are close in
q

\pi
with complexities similar to those found in
16
.

We recall a standard change of measure lemma: for any event
A
and two measures
\mu,\nu
, with
q>1
,

\displaystyle\mu(A)\leq\nu(A)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\cdot\exp\Bigl(\frac{q-1}{q}\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu\mathbin{\|}\nu)\Bigr)\,.

This is a consequence of Hölder’s inequality applied to the product of
\one_{A}
and
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\nu}
under the measure
\nu
. Using this, we can deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 2

(Change of measure)
.

If
N=\widetilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{d}k^{3/2})
and Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
hold, then for any event
A
,

\displaystyle\mu^{[k]}(A)\leq 2\bigl[\pi^{\otimes k}(A)\bigr]^{1/2}\,.

Remark.

As a consequence, if there is a family of events
\{A_{z}\}_{z>0}
and
\phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
for which
\pi^{\otimes k}(A_{z})\leq\exp(-C\phi(z))
, then
\mu^{[k]}(A_{z})\leq 2\exp(-\frac{C}{2}\phi(z))
, so that similar exponential behaviour is observed as
\phi(z)\to\infty
. The constant
C/2
in the exponent can be improved to
C(1-\zeta)
for
\zeta>0
arbitrarily small by increasing the Rényi order

. In the work
15
, it was shown that, under different assumptions on the potential and interaction kernel, the empirical measure obtained with particles drawn from
\mu^{[N]}
is
\varepsilon

\pi
in Wasserstein-
p
distances with high probability.

We can recover this result by first applying exponential concentration results for the empirical measure under
\pi^{\otimes k}
, using techniques from

, and then using Corollary

. The final result is, up to the constant prefactor and the constant in the exponent, the same behaviour as verified in the setting of

(Explicit computations for Gaussians)

\displaystyle\bar{C}_{\LSI}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{[k]}}[\lVert\mathsf{A}_{i,k}\rVert^{2}]+\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{[k]}}[\lVert\mathsf{B}_{i,k}\rVert^{2}]\Bigr)=\widetilde{O}\Bigl(\frac{dk^{3}}{N^{2}}\Bigr)\qquad\bar{C}_{\LSI}\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{[k]}}[\lVert\nabla\log\rho_{k}\rVert^{2}]\Bigr)=\widetilde{O}\Bigl(\frac{dk^{3}}{N^{2}}\Bigr)\,.

This immediately gives a bound on the
\mathsf{T}
-term. Putting all this together concludes the proof.
∎

In the proof above, we made use of some standard sub-Gaussian concentration properties. See e.g.,

4.1
Lipschitz recursion

Lemma 12
.

Under Assumption
3
,
N\geq 100
,
k\ll N
, it holds for any
x^{[k]},\bar{x}^{[k]}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
that

\displaystyle\lvert W_{2}(\mu^{k+1\mid[k]}(\cdot\mid x^{[k]}),\pi)-W_{2}(\mu^{k+1\mid[k]}(\cdot\mid\bar{x}^{[k]}),\pi)\rvert\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{k}\lVert x^{[k]}-\bar{x}^{[k]}\rVert}{N}\,.

Proof.

Applying the triangle inequality then (

T
2

) and then (

LSI

), we find that, letting
\upmu^{\ell},\bar{\upmu}^{\ell}
denote the measures
\mu^{k+1:k+\ell\mid[k]}(\cdot\mid x^{[k]}),\mu^{k+1:k+\ell\mid[k]}(\cdot\mid\bar{x}^{[k]})
respectively for
\ell\in[N-k]
,

\displaystyle\lvert W_{2}(\upmu^{\ell},\pi)-W_{2}(\bar{\upmu}^{\ell}(\cdot),\pi)\rvert\leq\lvert W_{2}(\upmu^{\ell},\bar{\upmu}^{\ell})\rvert\leq\sqrt{2\bar{C}_{\LSI}}\sqrt{\KL\bigl(\upmu^{\ell}\mid\bar{\upmu}^{\ell}\bigr)}\leq\bar{C}_{\LSI}\sqrt{\mathsf{FI}\bigl(\upmu^{\ell}\mid\bar{\upmu}^{\ell}\bigr)}\,.

Now, we have for
i\in[\ell]
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{z^{i}}\log\upmu^{\ell}(z^{1:\ell})

\displaystyle=-\nabla V(z^{i})-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\nabla W(z^{i}-x^{j})-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}=1\\
j\neq j^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\nabla W(z^{i}-z^{j^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{N-k-\ell}{N-1}\int\nabla W(z^{i}-\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{k+\ell+1\mid[k]+\ell}(\cdot\mid x^{[k]},z^{1:\ell})\,.

As a result, we can proceed with, defining
\uprho^{\ell}(z^{1:\ell})=\frac{\upmu^{\ell}}{\bar{\upmu}^{\ell}}(z^{1:\ell})
, using exchangeability of
\upmu^{\ell}
in its
\ell
coordinates,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\upmu^{\ell}}

\displaystyle[\lVert\nabla\log\uprho^{\ell}\rVert^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\ell}{(N-1)^{2}}\,\underset{\mathsf{C}_{\ell}}{\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\upmu^{\ell}}\Bigl[\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{k}(\nabla W(z^{1}-x^{j})-\nabla W(z^{1}-\bar{x}^{j}))\rVert^{2}\Bigr]}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2\ell(N-k-\ell)^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}}\,\underset{\mathsf{D}_{\ell}}{\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{z^{[\ell]}\sim\upmu^{\ell}}\Bigl[\lVert\int\nabla W(z^{1}-\cdot)\{\mathrm{d}\mu^{k+\ell+1\mid[k+\ell]}-\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}^{k+\ell+1\mid[k+\ell]}\}\rVert^{2}\Bigr]}}\,,

where we defined
\mu^{k+\ell+1\mid[k+\ell]}=\mu^{k+\ell+1\mid[k+\ell]}(\cdot\mid x^{[k]},z^{[\ell]})
and
\bar{\mu}^{k+\ell+1\mid[k+\ell]}=\mu^{k+\ell+1\mid[k+\ell]}(\cdot\mid\bar{x}^{[k]},z^{[\ell]})
as shorthands.
For the first term, simply applying Lipschitzness of
\nabla W
and Cauchy–Schwarz permits us to bound it by

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{\ell}\leq\beta_{W}^{2}k\lVert x^{[k]}-\bar{x}^{[k]}\rVert^{2}\,.

On the other hand, the chain-rule for
\KL
and Assumption
2
yield

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{W}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{z^{[\ell]}\sim\upmu^{\ell}}W_{2}^{2}(\mu^{k+\ell+1\mid[k+\ell]}(\cdot\mid x^{[k]},z^{[\ell]}),\mu^{k+\ell+1\mid[k+\ell]}(\cdot\mid\bar{x}^{[k]},z^{[\ell]}))

\displaystyle\leq 2\beta_{W}^{2}\bar{C}_{\LSI}\mathbb{E}_{z^{[\ell]}\sim\upmu^{\ell}}\KL(\mu^{k+\ell+1\mid[k+\ell]}(\cdot\mid x^{[k]},z^{[\ell]})\mathbin{\|}\mu^{k+\ell+1\mid[k+\ell]}(\cdot\mid\bar{x}^{[k]},z^{[\ell]}))

\displaystyle=2\beta_{W}^{2}\bar{C}_{\LSI}\Bigl\{\KL(\upmu^{\ell+1}\mathbin{\|}\bar{\upmu}^{\ell+1})-\KL(\upmu^{\ell}\mathbin{\|}\bar{\upmu}^{\ell})\Bigr\}\,.

We can now rearrange this inequality. Letting
\mathsf{K}_{\ell}=\KL(\upmu^{\ell}\mid\bar{\upmu}^{\ell})
, we have shown the following hierarchy,

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{\ell}\leq\underset{\mathcal{C}_{\ell}}{\underbrace{\frac{2\ell\beta_{W}^{2}\bar{C}_{\LSI}^{2}}{1+2\ell\beta_{W}^{2}\bar{C}_{\LSI}^{2}}}}\,\Bigl(\frac{k}{2\bar{C}_{\LSI}N^{2}}\lVert x^{[k]}-\bar{x}^{[k]}\rVert^{2}+\mathsf{K}_{\ell+1}\Bigr)\,.

Finally, at
\mathsf{K}_{N-k}
, we bound (as the term
\mathsf{D}
is not present in this case)

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{N-k}\leq\frac{\beta_{W}^{2}k\bar{C}_{\LSI}}{2N}\lVert x^{[k]}-\bar{x}^{[k]}\rVert^{2}\,.

Solving this recurrence relation, we obtain, defining the ratio
\xi\coloneqq(2\beta_{W}^{2}\bar{C}_{\LSI}^{2})^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl\{\Bigl(\prod_{\ell=1}^{N-k-1}C_{\ell}\Bigr)\frac{\beta_{W}^{2}k\bar{C}_{\LSI}}{2N}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-k-1}\Bigl(\prod_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell}C_{\ell^{\prime}}\Bigr)\frac{k}{2\bar{C}_{\LSI}N^{2}}\,\biggr\}\lVert x^{[k]}-\bar{x}^{[k]}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{c_{N}}{\underbrace{\biggl\{\Bigl(\prod_{\ell=1}^{N-k-1}C_{\ell}\Bigr)\frac{1}{4\xi N}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-k-1}\Bigl(\prod_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell}C_{\ell^{\prime}}\Bigr)\frac{1}{N^{2}}\,\biggr\}}}\frac{k\lVert x^{[k]}-\bar{x}^{[k]}\rVert^{2}}{2\bar{C}_{\LSI}}\,.

Now, we use the generic formula in Lemma
13
for
k\ll N
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{1}\leq(1+\xi)^{\xi}\Bigl(\frac{N^{-1-\xi}}{\xi}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N-k-1}\frac{1}{i^{\xi}}\Bigr)\frac{k\lVert x^{[k]}-\bar{x}^{[k]}\rVert^{2}}{2\bar{C}_{\LSI}}\,.

Of course, under our assumptions, we already know that
\xi\geq 2
, and we can always bound it above by an absolute constant by taking a worse bound on the
\beta_{W}
. As a result, we can bound
\sum_{i\leq N-k-1}i^{-\xi}\lesssim 1
, which suffices to bound this overall by

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{1}\lesssim\frac{k\lVert x^{[k]}-\bar{x}^{[k]}\rVert^{2}}{\bar{C}_{\LSI}N^{2}}\,.

∎

5
Discussion

We briefly outline some directions for future work. Firstly, the argument above has not been made dynamic, i.e., for the simultaneous evolutions of a measure along (
1.3
) and (
1.4
). This is due to difficulties in propagating Assumption
2
along the dynamics, as the stability of the log-Sobolev constant of the conditional measures along the dynamics remains difficult to check outside of the strongly convex case. Another interesting and complementary direction would be to remove the strong log-Sobolev assumptions, in particular those on the conditional measures
\mu^{k+1\mid[k]}(\cdot\mid x^{[k]})
, by appealing to similar arguments as
22
. It would be interesting to consider more general settings, such as the minimizer of general functionals
\mu\mapsto\mathcal{F}(\mu)
considered in
5
;
6
; this problem would need significantly new techniques, as the hierarchical argument is not easily adapted to that setting. Finally, the scaling
k^{3}
(and the additional polylogarithmic factors) is likely sub-optimal, and reducing these to the standard
k^{2}
would be preferred. A similar remark holds for the quadratic scaling
q^{2}
compared to the expected
q
observed in the Gaussian example.

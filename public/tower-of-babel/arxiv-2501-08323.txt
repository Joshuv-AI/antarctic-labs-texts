Maximal estimates and pointwise convergence for solutions of certain dispersive equations with radial initial data on Damek-Ricci spaces
Utsav Dewan

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

One of the most celebrated problems in Euclidean Harmonic analysis is the Carleson’s problem: determining the optimal regularity of the initial condition
f
of the
Schrödinger equation given by

\begin{cases}i\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u=0\>,\>\>\>(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\\
u(0,\cdot)=f\>,\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\>,\end{cases}

in terms of the index
\beta
such that
f
belongs to the inhomogeneous Sobolev space
H^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, so that the solution of the Schrödinger operator
u
converges pointwise to
f
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0+}u(x,t)=f(x)\>,\>\>\text{ almost everywhere }.

Such a problem was first studied by Carleson
[
Ca80
]
and Dahlberg-Kenig
[
DK82
]
in dimension
1
, followed by several other experts in the field for arbitrary dimension (see
[
Co83
,
Sj87
,
Ve88
,
Bo16
,
DGL17
,
DZ19
]
and references therein).

Recently, the investigation on Carleson’s problem for the Schrödinger equation in the non-Euclidean setting of rank one Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type or (more generally) Damek-Ricci spaces has received considerable interest
[
WZ19
,
De1
,
DR24
,
KS24
,
De2
]
. Damek-Ricci spaces are also known as Harmonic
NA
groups. These spaces
S
are non-unimodular, solvable extensions of Heisenberg type groups
N
, obtained by letting
A=\mathbb{R}^{+}
act on
N
by homogeneous dilations. The rank one Riemannian Symmetric spaces of noncompact type are the prototypical examples of (and in fact accounts for a very small subclass of the more general class of) Damek-Ricci spaces
[
ADY96
, p. 643]
. For unexplained notations and terminologies, the reader is referred to section
2
.

Let
\Delta
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator
on
S
corresponding to the left-invariant Riemannian metric. Its
L^{2}
-spectrum is the half line
(-\infty,-Q^{2}/4]
, where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of
N
. The Schrödinger equation on
S
is given by

(1.1)

\begin{cases}i\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\Delta u=0\>,\>\>\>(x,t)\in S\times\mathbb{R}\>,\\
u(0,\cdot)=f\>,\>\text{ on }S\>.\end{cases}

To quantify the Carleson’s problem on
S
, for
\beta\geq 0
, we recall the fractional
L^{2}
-Sobolev spaces on
S
for the special case of radial functions
[
APV15
]
:

(1.2)

H^{\beta}(S):=\left\{f\in L^{2}(S):{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}:={\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{\beta}{|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|}^{2}{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}<\infty\right\}.

One can also talk about the fractional
L^{2}
-Sobolev spaces for non-radial functions, by asking suitable fractional powers of (distributional)
\Delta
of
f
to be in
L^{2}
. We note that in the case
\beta=0
,
H^{0}(S)
is simply
L^{2}(S)
.

In
[
WZ19
]
, Wang and Zhang showed that on Real Hyperbolic spaces,
\beta>1/2
is a sufficient condition for the (almost everywhere) pointwise convergence of the solution of the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
). In
[
De1
]
, the author improved the above regularity condition from
\beta>1/2
to
\beta\geq 1/4
, while specializing to radial initial data, on the vastly general setting of Damek-Ricci spaces. Along with Ray, the author then established the sharpness of the endpoint
\beta=1/4
in
[
DR24
]
. Now unlike in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the spectral gap of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on non-flat manifolds such as Damek-Ricci spaces, creates difficulty in extending the arguments employed for the Schrödinger equation
[
De1
,
DR24
]
to the fractional setting (for more details see
[
De1
, point (1), subsection 6.1]
). In this regard, Kumar and Sajjan then considered the Carleson’s problem for the Fractional Schrödinger equation with
a>1
, on rank one Riemannian symmetric spaces of non-compact type
G/K
, with radial initial data:

(1.3)

\begin{cases}i\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+{(-\Delta)}^{a/2}u=0\>,\>\>\>(x,t)\in G/K\times\mathbb{R}\\
u(0,\cdot)=f\>,\>\text{ on }G/K\>,\end{cases}

and proved that
\beta>1/2
is a sufficient condition
[
KS24
]
.

This gives rise to a natural question on whether
\beta>1/2
is also necessary, in order to guarantee the pointwise convergence of the solution for the Fractional Schrödinger equation with radial initial data, on rank one Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type or (more generally) Damek-Ricci spaces. In this article, we answer the above question in the negative and improve their bound
\beta>1/2
down to the sharp bound
\beta\geq 1/4
. In fact, we do much more: we obtain a complete description of the local (in space) mapping properties for the local (in time) maximal function corresponding to more general dispersive equations with radial initial data on Damek-Ricci spaces.

The Fractional Schrödinger equation is a special example of dispersive equations of the form,

(1.4)

\begin{cases}i\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\Psi(\sqrt{-\Delta})u=0\>,\>(x,t)\in S\times\mathbb{R}\\
u(0,\cdot)=f\>,\>\text{ on }S\>,\end{cases}

where
\Psi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies suitable conditions. We note that for
\Psi(r):=r^{a}
, with
a>1
, we recover the Fractional Schrödinger equation (
1.3
). Some other examples are given by
\Psi(r):=r\sqrt{1+r^{2}}
and
\Psi(r):=\sqrt{1+r^{4}}
corresponding to the Boussinesq equation and the Beam equation (for more details see
[
FX24
,
GPW08
]
), which often appear in Mathematical Physics. Then for a radial function
f
belonging to the
L^{2}
-Schwartz class
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
(for the definition see (
2.6
)), the solution of (
1.4
) is given by,

(1.5)

S_{\psi,t}f(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\>e^{it\psi(\lambda)}\>\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>,

where
\varphi_{\lambda}
are the spherical functions,
\psi(\lambda):=\Psi\left(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}}\right)
, is the phase function of the corresponding multiplier,
\widehat{f}
is the Spherical Fourier transform of
f
and
{\bf c}(\cdot)
denotes the Harish-Chandra’s
{\bf c}
-function. By replacing
\Delta
with the shifted Laplace-Beltrami operator
\tilde{\Delta}:=\Delta+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}
, in (
1.4
), we would also consider the shifted variants of the Fractional Schrödinger equation, the Boussinesq equation and the Beam equation. We note that in this case, due to the absence of the spectral gap, one simply has
\psi(\lambda)=\Psi(\lambda)
, reminiscent of the classical Euclidean situation.

Let
B_{R}
denote the geodesic ball on
S
, centered at the identity
e
and radius
R>0
. As in the literature, to address the problem of pointwise convergence, the key is to consider the corresponding maximal function,

(1.6)

S^{*}_{\psi}f(x):=\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<1}\left|S_{\psi,t}f(x)\right|\>,

and then obtain its
L^{p}_{loc}
boundedness on balls, for some
p\in[1,\infty]
,

(1.7)

{\|S^{*}_{\psi}f\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}\>,

for all
R>0
. As mentioned before, the main result of this article is the following complete description of the pairs
(p,\beta)\in[1,\infty]\times[0,\infty)
, for which one has the maximal estimates (
1.7
) for any
R>0
, and every radial
L^{2}
-Schwartz class function
f
on
S
:

Theorem 1.1
.

For the maximal functions corresponding to the Fractional Schrödinger equation (with
a>1
), the Boussinesq equation and the Beam equation with respect to both
\Delta
and
\tilde{\Delta}
, we have:

(i)

If
\beta<1/4
, then (
1.7
) holds for no
p
.

(ii)

If
1/4\leq\beta<n/2
, then (
1.7
) holds if and only if
p\leq 2n/(n-2\beta)
.

(iii)

If
\beta=n/2
, then (
1.7
) holds if and only if
p<\infty
.

(iv)

If
\beta>n/2
, then (
1.7
) holds for all
p
.

Then by standard arguments in the literature (for instance see the proof of Theorem 5 of
[
Sj87
]
), one gets the desired result on pointwise convergence:

Corollary 1.2
.

For the Fractional Schrödinger equation (with
a>1
), the Boussinesq equation and the Beam equation corresponding to both
\Delta
and
\tilde{\Delta}
, the pointwise convergence

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0+}S_{\psi,t}f(x)=f(x)\>,

holds for almost every
x
in
S
with respect to the left Haar measure on
S
, whenever the radial initial data
f\in H^{\beta}(S)
with
\beta\geq 1/4
.

Remark 1.3
.

(i)

In the special case of rank one Riemannian symmetric spaces of non-compact type, Kumar and Sajjan obtained
\beta>1/2
to be a sufficient condition for the pointwise convergence to hold true in the context of the Fractional Schrödinger equation (
a>1
) with radial initial data
[
KS24
, Theorem A]
. Our corollary
1.2
improves the regularity threshold down to
\beta\geq 1/4
. Theorem
1.1
also establishes the sharpness of the endpoint
\beta=1/4
.

(ii)

Theorem
1.1
generalizes the local mapping properties for the local maximal function corresponding to the Schrödinger equation
[
DR24
, Theorem 1.1]
to more general class of dispersive equations with radial initial data (see subsection
6.1
).

The Carleson’s problem for the fractional Schrödinger equation (
a>1
) with radial initial data on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
was first studied by Prestini in
[
Pr90
]
, where she proved the sharp upto the endpoint bound
\beta\geq 1/4
. Afterwards, the complete mapping properties of the corresponding maximal function were obtained by Sjölin in
[
Sj97
]
, which were partially generalized to more general dispersive equations by Ding and Niu
[
DN17
]
. Their arguments hinge heavily on the small and large argument asymptotics of the Bessel functions and the oscillation afforded by them and the corresponding mutiplier. Unlike in the Euclidean case, in the general setting of Damek-Ricci spaces however, no explicit expression of the spherical function is known. In fact, it only admits certain series expansions (the Bessel series expansion and a series expansion similar to the classical Harish-Chandra series expansion) depending on the geodesic distance from the identity of the group.

We commence the proof of the sufficient conditions in Theorem
1.1
by introducing two cut-offs near
0
and at
\infty
and then decomposing the linearized maximal function into two pieces corresponding to small and large frequency. The former is easy to handle. Estimating the latter one however is more involved, as it is the high frequency part that essentially dictates maximal estimates. In order to do so, we invoke the various series expansions of
\varphi_{\lambda}
. This prompts us to decompose the ball
B_{R}
into a closed ball
\overline{B_{R_{0}}}
and an open annulus
A(R_{0},R)
,

B_{R}=\overline{B_{R_{0}}}\sqcup A(R_{0},R)\>.

On the ball
\overline{B_{R_{0}}}
, we expand the spherical function into the Bessel series. Then by building up on ideas employed by the author and Ray in
[
DR24
]
, we estimate the linearized maximal function on
\overline{B_{R_{0}}}
by forming a connection with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and lifting Euclidean results to
S
.

On the annulus
A(R_{0},R)
, the above mentioned Bessel series expansion is no longer valid and thus this is the genuine non-Euclidean situation. We first consider the high regularity scenario, that is
\beta>1/2
. In this case, the pointwise decay of
\varphi_{\lambda}
given by (
2.9
) suffices to estimate the linearized maximal function. In the lower regularity regime
1/4\leq\beta\leq 1/2
however, it is much more delicate. Invoking a variation of the Harish-Chandra series expansion of
\varphi_{\lambda}
, recently obtained by Anker et. al.
[
APV15
]
,
we decompose the linearized maximal function into three parts: two of them are oscillatory in nature, while the third one corresponds to certain error terms. To estimate the oscillatory parts, we first employ an abstract
TT^{*}
argument. Then we expand the cut-off near
\infty
into infinitely many small pieces. On each such small window of frequency, we estimate the joint oscillation afforded by the spherical functions and the multipliers in such a way that the estimates turn out to be summable. In this oscillatory integral estimate, we dispense of the explicitness of the phase function
\psi
and only use large frequency asymptotics of
\psi^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime\prime}
. This step is somewhat technical. Summing up the above estimates, we appeal to the one dimensional Pitt’s inequality to obtain the desired estimate. The third part corresponding to the error term is taken care of by certain pointwise estimates. Here one crucially uses the extra decay (compared to the classical case of the Harish-Chandra series expansion) of the
\Gamma
coefficients in
\lambda
, given by (
2.8
).

For the proof of sharpness of the end-point
\beta=1/4
, we first consider the case of the fractional Schrödinger equation corresponding to
\Delta
. In this case, we work in a small annulus and hence naturally expand the spherical function in the Bessel series. The oscillation of the spherical function is realized in terms of the leading Bessel term in the series. Then invoking the oscillatory asymptotic expansion for that Bessel function, we eventually decompose the linearized maximal function into three pieces. Controlling the joint oscillation afforded by the spherical function and the multiplier, we realize that one of the above pieces exhibits divergence while the other two decay. Our computations involve derivative estimates of the Harish-Chandra’s
{\bf c}
-function and again, the spectral gap of
\Delta
makes the computations more difficult (compared to Sjölin’s arguments in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
). The fractional Schrödinger equation corresponding to
\tilde{\Delta}
follows exactly the same line of arguments and is perhaps even a bit simpler due to the absence of the spectral gap. The other equations are then taken care of by applying an abstract transference principle (Theorem
1.6
), which we explain next.

Definition 1.4
.

Let us consider two dispersive equations of the form (
1.4
) corresponding to
\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}
(recall that
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
are the phase functions of the corresponding multipliers).

(i)

They are called
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
-
locally transferrable
if given that for all
R>0
, some
\beta_{0}>0
and some
p\in[1,\infty]
, the maximal estimate

{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{1}}f\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}\>,

holds for all
\beta>\beta_{0}
and all
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
, it follows that the maximal estimate

{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{2}}f\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}\>,

also holds for all
R>0
, all
\beta>\beta_{0}
and all
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
.

(ii)

If the two equations are both
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
-locally transferrable as well as
(\psi_{2},\psi_{1})
-locally transferrable, then they are simply called
locally transferrable
.

(iii)

If there exist
\Lambda>0
and
C>0
, such that the phase functions
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
satisfy

\left|\psi_{1}(\lambda)-\psi_{2}(\lambda)\right|\leq C\>,\text{ for all }\lambda>\Lambda\>,

then the equations are said to be
of comparable oscillation
.

Remark 1.5
.

Point
(iii)
of definition
1.4
means that in high frequency, both the phase functions are within bounded error. Let us now consider the equations mentioned before.

(i)

For
a>1
, the phase function of the multiplier of the Fractional Schrödinger equation corresponding to
\Delta
and the shifted operator
\tilde{\Delta}
are given by
\psi_{1}(\lambda)={\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{a/2}
and
\psi_{2}(\lambda)=\lambda^{a}
respectively. Now for
1<a\leq 2
and
\lambda
large,

{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{a/2}=\lambda^{a}+\mathcal{O}(1)\>,

and thus for
1<a\leq 2
, both the Fractional Schrödinger equations are of comparable oscillation. This is clearly not the case when
a>2
.

(ii)

The phase functions of the multiplier of the Boussinesq operator and its shifted counterpart are given respectively as,

\psi_{3}(\lambda)=\sqrt{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}\sqrt{1+\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}\>,\text{ and }\psi_{4}(\lambda)=\lambda\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}\>.

For
\lambda
large,

\displaystyle\psi_{3}(\lambda)=\left(\lambda+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1})\right)\left(\lambda+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1})\right)=\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{O}(1)\>,

\displaystyle\psi_{4}(\lambda)=\lambda\left(\lambda+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1})\right)=\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{O}(1)\>,

and thus both are of comparable oscillation to the classical Schrödinger equation (that is,
a=2
). The same is true for both the Beam operators and can be verified similarly.

The notion of
comparable oscillation
defines an equivalence relation. Intuitively, all the equations in the same equivalence class should ‘behave similarly’ as essentially, it is the oscillation of the multiplier in the high frequency regime that dictates maximal estimates. Our next result confirms this.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
be continuous real-valued functions on
[0,\infty)
such that they are
C^{\infty}
away from the origin. If the dispersive equations corresponding to
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
are of comparable oscillation, then they are also locally transferrable.

This article is organized as follows. In section
2
, we fix our notations, recall certain aspects of Euclidean Fourier Analysis, first principles of oscillatory integral estimates in dimension one and the essential preliminaries about Damek-Ricci spaces and Spherical Fourier Analysis thereon. The sufficient conditions of Theorem
1.1
are proved in section
3
. In section
4
, the transference principle (Theorem
1.6
) is proved. The necessary conditions of Theorem
1.1
are proved in section
5
. Finally, we conclude in section
6
, by making some remarks and posing some new problems.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some preliminaries and fix our notations.

2.1.
Some notations

Throughout, the symbols ‘c’ and ‘C’ will denote positive constants whose values may change on each occurrence. The enumerated constants
C_{1},C_{2},\dots
will however be fixed throughout.
\mathbb{N}
will denote the set of positive integers. For
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lceil x\rceil
will denote the smallest integer
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
x\leq m
. For non-negative functions
f_{1},\>f_{2},\>f_{3}
we write,

•

f_{1}\lesssim f_{2}
if there exists a positive constant
C
, so that

f_{1}\leq Cf_{2}\>\>.

•

f_{1}\asymp f_{2}
if there exist constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
, so that

Cf_{1}\leq f_{2}\leq C^{\prime}f_{1}\>.

•

f_{1}=f_{2}+\mathcal{O}(f_{3})
if

|f_{1}-f_{2}|\lesssim f_{3}\>.

2.2.
Fourier Analysis on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

In this subsection, we recall some Euclidean Fourier Analysis, most of which can be found in
[
SW90
]
. On
\mathbb{R}
, for “nice” functions
f
, the Fourier transform
\tilde{f}
is defined as

\tilde{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\>e^{-ix\xi}\>dx\>.

We next state the Pitt’s inequality for one-dimensional Fourier transforms:

Lemma 2.1
.

[
St56
, p. 489]

One has the inequality

{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\left|\tilde{f}(\xi)\right|}^{2}\>{|\xi|}^{-2\beta}d\xi\right)}^{1/2}\lesssim{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\left|f(x)\right|}^{p}\>{|x|}^{\beta_{1}p}dx\right)}^{1/p}\>,

where
\beta_{1}=\beta+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
and the following two conditions are satisfied:

0\leq\beta_{1}<1-\frac{1}{p}\>,\>\text{ and }0\leq\beta<\frac{1}{2}\>.

A
C^{\infty}
function
f
on
\mathbb{R}
is called a Schwartz class function if

\left|{\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)}^{M}f(x)\right|\lesssim{(1+|x|)}^{-N}\>,\text{ for any }M,N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\>.

We denote by
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
the class of all such functions and
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})_{e}
will denote the collection of all even Schwartz class functions on
\mathbb{R}
. Similarly,
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})_{e}
denotes the collection of all even, compactly supported smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
Re(\beta)>0
. The Riesz potential of order
\beta
is the operator

I_{\beta}={(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}})}^{-\beta/2}\>,

which can also be written as,

I_{\beta}(f)(x)=C\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\>{|x-y|}^{\beta-1}\>dy\>,

for some
C>0
(depending on
\beta
), whenever
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
. For
\beta>0
, the Fourier transform of the Riesz potential of
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies the following identity:

(2.1)

(I_{\beta}(f))^{\sim}(\xi)=C_{\beta}{|\xi|}^{-\beta}\tilde{f}(\xi)\>.

For a “nice” radial function
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, the Euclidean spherical Fourier transform is defined as

\mathscr{F}f(\lambda):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r)\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\lambda r)\>r^{n-1}\>dr\>,\>\>\>\>\>\lambda\in[0,\infty),

where for all
\mu\geq 0\>,\mathscr{J}_{\mu}
denotes the modified Bessel function:

\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(z)=2^{\mu}\>\pi^{1/2}\>\Gamma\left(\mu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{J_{\mu}(z)}{z^{\mu}}\>.

Here
J_{\mu}
are the Bessel functions
[
SW90
, p. 154]
. The asymptotic oscillation of Bessel functions is well-studied:

Lemma 2.2
.

[
Pr90
, lemma 1]

Let
\mu\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
. Then
there exists a positive constant
A_{\mu}
such that

J_{\mu}(s)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi s}}\cos\left(s-\frac{\pi}{2}\mu-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+\tilde{E}_{\mu}(s)\>,

where

|\tilde{E}_{\mu}(s)|\lesssim s^{-3/2}\>,\>\text{ for }s\geq A_{\mu}\>.

2.3.
Preliminaries on oscillatory integral estimates:

In this subsection, we recall some preliminary results that are useful in estimating oscillatory integrals. The first one is the standard van der Corput’s lemma:

Lemma 2.3
.

[
St86
, p. 309]

Let
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and a real-valued function
\theta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

|\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|>\lambda>0\>,\text{ for all }\xi\in Supp(\zeta)\>.

Then we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\theta(\xi)}\>\zeta(\xi)\>d\xi\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-1/2}\>\left\{\|\zeta\|_{\infty}\>+\>\|\zeta^{\prime}\|_{1}\right\}\>.

We will also require the following lemma which is essentially integration by parts:

Lemma 2.4
.

[
Sj81
]

Let
I
denote an open interval in
\mathbb{R}
. Assume
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(I)
and a real-valued function
\theta\in C^{\infty}(I)
with
\theta^{\prime}\neq 0
. If
l
is a positive integer, then

\int_{I}e^{i\theta(\xi)}\>\zeta(\xi)\>d\xi=\int_{I}e^{i\theta(\xi)}\>\kappa_{l}(\xi)\>d\xi\>,

where
\kappa_{l}
is a linear combination of functions of the form,

\zeta^{(m)}{\left(\theta^{\prime}\right)}^{-l-r}\displaystyle\prod_{q=1}^{r}\theta^{(j_{q})}\>,\>\text{ for }0\leq m,r\leq l\>,\>2\leq j_{q}\leq l+1\>.

2.4.
Damek-Ricci spaces and spherical Fourier Analysis thereon:

In this subsection, we will explain the notations and state relevant results on Damek-Ricci spaces. Most of these results can be found in
[
ADY96
,
APV15
,
As95
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be a two-step real nilpotent Lie algebra equipped with an inner product
\langle,\rangle
. Let
\mathfrak{z}
be the center of
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{v}
its orthogonal complement. We say that
\mathfrak{n}
is an
H
-type algebra if for every
Z\in\mathfrak{z}
the map
J_{Z}:\mathfrak{v}\to\mathfrak{v}
defined by

\langle J_{z}X,Y\rangle=\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle,\>\>\>\>X,Y\in\mathfrak{v}

satisfies the condition
J_{Z}^{2}=-|Z|^{2}I_{\mathfrak{v}}
,
I_{\mathfrak{v}}
being the identity operator on
\mathfrak{v}
. A connected and simply connected Lie group
N
is called an
H
-type group if its Lie algebra is
H
-type. Since
\mathfrak{n}
is nilpotent, the exponential map is a diffeomorphism
and hence we can parametrize the elements in
N=\exp\mathfrak{n}
by
(X,Z)
, for
X\in\mathfrak{v},Z\in\mathfrak{z}
. It follows from the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula that the group law in
N
is given by

\left(X,Z\right)\left(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime}\right)=\left(X+X^{\prime},Z+Z^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}[X,X^{\prime}]\right),\>\>\>\>X,X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{v};~Z,Z^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{z}.

The group
A=\mathbb{R}^{+}
acts on an
H
-type group
N
by nonisotropic dilation:
(X,Z)\mapsto(\sqrt{a}X,aZ)
. Let
S=NA
be the semidirect product of
N
and
A
under the above action. Thus the multiplication in
S
is given by

\left(X,Z,a\right)\left(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime},a^{\prime}\right)=\left(X+\sqrt{a}X^{\prime},Z+aZ^{\prime}+\frac{\sqrt{a}}{2}[X,X^{\prime}],aa^{\prime}\right),

for
X,X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{v};~Z,Z^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{z};a,a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
Then
S
is a solvable, connected and simply connected Lie group having Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathbb{R}
with Lie bracket

\left[\left(X,Z,l\right),\left(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime},l^{\prime}\right)\right]=\left(\frac{1}{2}lX^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}l^{\prime}X,lZ^{\prime}-lZ+[X,X^{\prime}],0\right).

We write
na=(X,Z,a)
for the element
\exp(X+Z)a,X\in\mathfrak{v},Z\in\mathfrak{z},a\in A
. We note
that for any
Z\in\mathfrak{z}
with
|Z|=1
,
J_{Z}^{2}=-I_{\mathfrak{v}}
; that is,
J_{Z}
defines a complex structure
on
\mathfrak{v}
and hence
\mathfrak{v}
is even dimensional.
m_{\mathfrak{v}}
and
m_{z}
will denote the dimension of
\mathfrak{v}
and
\mathfrak{z}
respectively. Let
n
and
Q
denote dimension and the homogenous dimension of
S
respectively:

n=m_{\mathfrak{v}}+m_{\mathfrak{z}}+1\>\>\textit{ and }\>\>Q=\frac{m_{\mathfrak{v}}}{2}+m_{\mathfrak{z}}.

The group
S
is equipped with the left-invariant Riemannian metric induced by

\langle(X,Z,l),(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime},l^{\prime})\rangle=\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle+\langle Z,Z^{\prime}\rangle+ll^{\prime}

on
\mathfrak{s}
. For
x\in S
, we denote by
s=d(e,x)
, that is, the geodesic distance of
x
from the identity
e
. Then the left Haar measure
dx
of the group
S
may be normalized so that

dx=A(s)\>ds\>d\sigma(\omega)\>,

where
A
is the density function given by,

A(s)=2^{m_{\mathfrak{v}}+m_{\mathfrak{z}}}\>{\left(\sinh(s/2)\right)}^{m_{\mathfrak{v}}+m_{\mathfrak{z}}}\>{\left(\cosh(s/2)\right)}^{m_{\mathfrak{z}}}\>,

and
d\sigma
is the surface measure of the unit sphere. As we will be dealing with finite balls centered at the identity, the following local growth asymptotics of the density function will be useful:

(2.2)

A(s)\asymp s^{n-1}\>.

A function
f:S\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be radial if, for all
x
in
S
,
f(x)
depends only on the geodesic distance of
x
from the identity
e
. If
f
is radial, then

\int_{S}f(x)~dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(s)~A(s)~ds\>.

We now recall the spherical functions on Damek-Ricci spaces. The spherical functions
\varphi_{\lambda}
on
S
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
are the radial eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
, satisfying the following normalization criterion

\begin{cases}&\Delta\varphi_{\lambda}=-\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)\varphi_{\lambda}\\
&\varphi_{\lambda}(e)=1\>.\end{cases}

For all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in S
, the spherical functions satisfy

\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=\varphi_{-\lambda}(s)\>.

It also satisfies for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and all
s\geq 0
:

(2.3)

\left|\varphi_{\lambda}(s)\right|\leq 1\>.

The spherical functions are crucial as they help us define the Spherical Fourier transform of a “nice” radial function
f
(on
S
) in the following way:

\widehat{f}(\lambda):=\int_{S}f(x)\varphi_{\lambda}(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(s)\varphi_{\lambda}(s)A(s)ds\>.

The Harish-Chandra
{\bf c}
-function is defined as

{\bf c}(\lambda)=\frac{2^{(Q-2i\lambda)}\Gamma(2i\lambda)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{Q+2i\lambda}{2}\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{m_{\mathfrak{v}}+4i\lambda+2}{4}\right)}\>,

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. We will need the following pointwise estimates (see
[
RS09
, Lemma 4.8]
):

(2.4)

{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\asymp\>{|\lambda|}^{2}{\left(1+|\lambda|\right)}^{n-3}\>.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, we also have the
derivative estimates (
[
As95
, Lemma 4.2]
):

(2.5)

\left|\frac{d^{j}}{d\lambda^{j}}{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\right|\lesssim_{j}{(1+|\lambda|)}^{n-1-j}\>,\>\>\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

One has the following inversion formula (when valid) for radial functions:

f(x)=C\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\lambda)\varphi_{\lambda}(x){|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\>,

where
C
depends only on
m_{\mathfrak{v}}
and
m_{\mathfrak{z}}
. Moreover, the Spherical Fourier transform extends to an isometry from the space of radial
L^{2}
functions on
S
onto
L^{2}\left((0,\infty),C{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\right)
.

The class of radial
L^{2}
-Schwartz class functions on
S
, denoted by
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
, is defined to be the collection of
f\in C^{\infty}(S)_{o}
such that

(2.6)

\left|{\left(\frac{d}{ds}\right)}^{M}f(s)\right|\lesssim{(1+s)}^{-N}e^{-\frac{Q}{2}s}\>,\text{ for any }M,N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\>,

(see
[
ADY96
, p. 652]
). The spherical Fourier transform defines a topological isomorphism from
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
to
{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})}_{e}
. We also recall
[
DR24
, p. 14]

\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})^{\infty}_{e}:=\{g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})_{e}\mid 0\notin Supp({g})\}.

So,
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})^{\infty}_{e}
is the collection of all even Schwartz class functions on
\mathbb{R}
which are supported outside an interval containing
0
. Consequently, we also look at

\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\infty}_{o}:=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})^{\infty}_{e}\right)\>,\>\>\text{ and }\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)^{\infty}_{o}:=\wedge^{-1}\left(\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})^{\infty}_{e}\right)\>.

Then by repeated applications of the Abel transform and the Schwartz multiplier corresponding to the ratio of the weights of the Plancherel measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
S
(for details see
[
DR24
, Eqn. (4.7), pp. 14-15]
), we have the following one-one correspondence:

Lemma 2.5
.

For each
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)^{\infty}_{o}
, there exists a unique
g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\infty}_{o}
(and conversely) such that

\lambda^{n-1}\mathscr{F}g(\lambda)={|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>,

for all
\lambda
.

Depending on the distance from the identity of the group, one has different series expansions of the spherical functions. We first see an expansion of
\varphi_{\lambda}
in terms of Bessel functions for points near the identity. But before that, let us define the following normalizing constant in terms of the Gamma functions,

c_{0}=2^{m_{\mathfrak{z}}}\>\pi^{-1/2}\>\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\Gamma((n-1)/2)}\>,

Lemma 2.6
.

[
As95
, Theorem 3.1]

There exist
R_{0},2<R_{0}<2R_{1}
, such that for any
0\leq s\leq R_{0}
, and any integer
M\geq 0
, and all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=c_{0}{\left(\frac{s^{n-1}}{A(s)}\right)}^{1/2}\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{M}a_{l}(s)\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}+l}(\lambda s)s^{2l}+E_{M+1}(\lambda,s)\>,

where

a_{0}\equiv 1\>,\>|a_{l}(s)|\leq C{(4R_{1})}^{-l}\>,

and the error term has the following behaviour

\left|E_{M+1}(\lambda,s)\right|\leq C_{M}\begin{cases}s^{2(M+1)}&\text{ if }\>|\lambda s|\leq 1\\
s^{2(M+1)}{|\lambda s|}^{-\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+M+1\right)}&\text{ if }\>|\lambda s|>1\>.\end{cases}

Moreover, for every
0\leq s<2
, the series

\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=c_{0}{\left(\frac{s^{n-1}}{A(s)}\right)}^{1/2}\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}a_{l}(s)\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}+l}(\lambda s)s^{2l}\>,

is absolutely convergent.

For the asymptotic behaviour of the spherical functions away from the identity, we look at the following series expansion
[
APV15
, pp. 735-736]
:

(2.7)

\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=2^{-m_{\mathfrak{z}}/2}{A(s)}^{-1/2}\left\{{\bf c}(\lambda)\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{\mu}(\lambda)e^{(i\lambda-\mu)s}+{\bf c}(-\lambda)\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{\mu}(-\lambda)e^{-(i\lambda+\mu)s}\right\}\>.

The above series converges for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, uniformly on compacts not containing the group identity, where
\Gamma_{0}\equiv 1
and for
\mu\in\mathbb{N}
, one has the recursion formula,

(\mu^{2}-2i\mu\lambda)\Gamma_{\mu}(\lambda)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\mu-1}\omega_{\mu-j}\Gamma_{j}(\lambda)\>.

Then one has the following estimate on the coefficients
[
APV15
, Lemma 1]
, for constants
C>0,d\geq 0
:

(2.8)

\left|\Gamma_{\mu}(\lambda)\right|\leq C\mu^{d}{\left(1+|\lambda|\right)}^{-1}\>,

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\mu\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, for any positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
, one has the following pointwise decay of
\varphi_{\lambda}
(
[
BD23
, Proposition 3.1]
):

(2.9)

|\varphi_{\lambda}(s)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{-\frac{Q}{2}s},\>\>\>\>\>\>\lambda>c_{1},\>\>s>c_{2}.

The relevant preliminaries on series expansions of
\varphi_{\lambda}
, for the degenerate case of the Real hyperbolic spaces can be found in
[
ST78
,
AP14
]
.

3.
Proof of the sufficient conditions of Theorem
1.1

To study the boundedness results for the maximal function (
1.6
), it suffices to consider its linearization,

T_{\psi}f(s):=S_{\psi,t(s)}f(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(s)\>e^{it(s)\psi(\lambda)}\>\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>,

where
t(\cdot):S\to(0,1)
is a radial measurable function and obtain inequalities of the form,

{\|T_{\psi}f\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}\>,

for all
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
.

We commence the proof by first decomposing
T_{\psi}
in terms of small and large frequency. More precisely, we consider a non-negative, even function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
such that
Supp(\eta)\subset\left\{\xi:1/2<|\xi|<2\right\}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\eta\left(2^{-k}\xi\right)=1\>,\>\text{ for }\xi\neq 0\>.

Let us now define,

\eta_{1}(\xi):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{0}\eta(2^{-k}\xi)\>,\text{ and }\eta_{2}(\xi):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\eta(2^{-k}\xi)\>.

We note that both
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are even non-negative smooth functions with
Supp(\eta_{1})\subset(-2,2)
,
Supp(\eta_{2})\subset\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)
and
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}\equiv 1
. Accordingly, we decompose
T_{\psi}
as,

(3.1)

T_{\psi}f(s):=T_{\psi,1}f(s)+T_{\psi,2}f(s)\>,

where,

\displaystyle T_{\psi,1}f(s):=\int_{0}^{2}\varphi_{\lambda}(s)\>e^{it(s)\psi(\lambda)}\>\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>\eta_{1}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>,

\displaystyle T_{\psi,2}f(s):=\int_{1}^{\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(s)\>e^{it(s)\psi(\lambda)}\>\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>\eta_{2}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>.

Note that
T_{\psi,1}
and
T_{\psi,2}
correspond to the small and large frequency respectively. We now obtain boundedness results for them seprately.

3.1.
Estimating
T_{\psi,1}
:

From the viewpoint of boundedness,
T_{\psi,1}
is extremely well-behaved. In fact, we have:

Lemma 3.1
.

The inequality,

{\|T_{\psi,1}f\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}\>,

holds for all
\beta\in[0,\infty)
and all
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Proof.

We note that it suffices to prove the estimate at the end-points,
p=\infty
and
\beta=0
. In this proof, we only use the boundedness of the spherical functions and the multiplier, that is, oscillation plays no role here. Indeed, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the small frequency asymptotics of the Plancherel measure (
2.4
), for
0\leq s<R
,

\displaystyle|T_{\psi,1}f(s)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\left|\widehat{f}(\lambda)\right|\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|}^{2}\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}{\left(\int_{0}^{2}{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{\|f\|}_{H^{0}(S)}\>.

This proves the desired
H^{0}(S)\to L^{\infty}(B_{R})
estimate.
∎

3.2.
Estimating
T_{\psi,2}
:

In this subsection, we are interested in the inequalities of the form,

(3.2)

{\|T_{\psi,2}f\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}\>.

The boundedness properties of
T_{\psi,2}
are summarized as follows:

Lemma 3.2
.

(i)

If
1/4\leq\beta<n/2
, then (
3.2
) holds if
1\leq p\leq 2n/(n-2\beta)
.

(ii)

If
\beta=n/2
, then (
3.2
) holds if
p\in[1,\infty)
.

(iii)

If
\beta>n/2
, then (
3.2
) holds for all
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Remark 3.3
.

In view of the decomposition given in (
3.1
) and the boundedness properties of
T_{\psi,1}
(Lemma
3.1
), to complete the proof of the sufficient conditions of Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to prove Lemma
3.2

The key strategy in the proof of Lemma
3.2
is to invoke the various series expansions of
\varphi_{\lambda}
. Now as the validity of these series expansions depend on the geodesic distance from the group identity, it prompts us to decompose the ball

d2^{k\delta_{2}}\leq C_{5}\>2^{k}|s-s^{\prime}|\>.

Hence, by (
3.28
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{4}}I_{k}(s,s^{\prime})\lesssim{|s-s^{\prime}|}^{-1}\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{4}}2^{-k/2}\lesssim\frac{1}{{|s-s^{\prime}|}^{1/2}}\>.

Finally, we consider

\mathscr{K}_{5}:=\left\{k\geq 1\mid 2^{k}>C_{6}\>{\left(\frac{|s-s^{\prime}|}{d}\right)}^{1/(\delta_{2}-1)}\right\}\>.

By (
3.27
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{5}}I_{k}(s,s^{\prime})\lesssim d^{-1/2}\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{5}}2^{k\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}\right)}\lesssim d^{-1/2}{\left(\frac{|s-s^{\prime}|}{d}\right)}^{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}\right)/(\delta_{2}-1)}=\frac{1}{{|s-s^{\prime}|}^{1/2}}\>.

Thus summing up the above three subcases, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}I_{k}(s,s^{\prime})=\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{3}}I_{k}(s,s^{\prime})+\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{4}}I_{k}(s,s^{\prime})+\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{5}}I_{k}(s,s^{\prime})\lesssim\frac{1}{{|s-s^{\prime}|}^{1/2}}\>.

Case 3:

|s-s^{\prime}|\geq 1
.

We first consider the collection,

\mathscr{K}_{6}:=\left\{k\geq 1\mid 2^{k}\leq C_{6}\>{\left(\frac{|s-s^{\prime}|}{d}\right)}^{1/(\delta_{2}-1)}\right\}\>.

As
\delta_{2}>1
, we note that for
k\in\mathscr{K}_{6}
, we also have

d2^{k\delta_{2}}\leq C_{5}\>2^{k}|s-s^{\prime}|\>.

Hence, by (
3.28
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{6}}I_{k}(s,s^{\prime})\lesssim{|s-s^{\prime}|}^{-1}\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{6}}2^{-k/2}\lesssim\frac{1}{{|s-s^{\prime}|}^{1/2}}\>.

So the only case left is when
k\in\mathscr{K}_{5}
, defined above. By (
3.27
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{5}}I_{k}(s,s^{\prime})\lesssim d^{-1/2}\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{5}}2^{k\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}\right)}\lesssim d^{-1/2}{\left(\frac{|s-s^{\prime}|}{d}\right)}^{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}\right)/(\delta_{2}-1)}=\frac{1}{{|s-s^{\prime}|}^{1/2}}\>.

Again summing up, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}I_{k}(s,s^{\prime})=\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{5}}I_{k}(s,s^{\prime})+\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathscr{K}_{6}}I_{k}(s,s^{\prime})\lesssim\frac{1}{{|s-s^{\prime}|}^{1/2}}\>.

This completes the proof of the claim (
3.22
).

Then by (
3.21
) and (
3.22
), it follows that

\int_{1}^{\infty}{\left|P^{*}h(\lambda)\right|}^{2}\>d\lambda\lesssim\int_{R_{0}}^{R}\int_{R_{0}}^{R}\frac{|h(s)|\>|h(s^{\prime})|}{{|s-s^{\prime}|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\>s^{-\left(\frac{n-1}{4}\right)}\>{(s^{\prime})}^{-\left(\frac{n-1}{4}\right)}\>ds\>ds^{\prime}\>.

) to the function

for

\displaystyle c{\left(\int_{R_{0}}^{R}{|h(s)|}^{\frac{4}{3}}\>s^{-\left(\frac{n-1}{3}\right)}\>ds\right)}^{3/2}

\displaystyle c\>R^{-\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}_{0}\>{\|h\|}^{2}_{L^{4/3}(R_{0},R)}\>.

This completes the proof of part

.

The proof of part

4.
The transference principle

In this section, we prove the transference principle (Theorem
1.6
), but first we look at the following lemma which compares different Sobolev norms of radial functions with Spherical Fourier transform supported away from the origin:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
with
Supp(\widehat{f})\subset(c,\infty)
for some
c>0
. Then for
0\leq\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}<\infty
, we have

\|f\|_{H^{\beta_{1}}(S)}\leq c^{-(\beta_{2}-\beta_{1})}\>\|f\|_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}\>.

Proof.

The Lemma follows from a straightforward computation. Indeed,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{\beta_{1}}(S)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left(\int_{c}^{\infty}{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{\beta_{1}}\>{|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|}^{2}\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left(\int_{c}^{\infty}{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{-(\beta_{2}-\beta_{1})}\>{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{\beta_{2}}\>{|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|}^{2}\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\left(\int_{c}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2(\beta_{2}-\beta_{1})}\>{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{\beta_{2}}\>{|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|}^{2}\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c^{-(\beta_{2}-\beta_{1})}\>\|f\|_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}\>.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let us assume that for all
R>0
, some
\beta_{0}>0
and some
p\in[1,\infty]
, the maximal estimate

(4.1)

{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{1}}f\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}\>,

holds for all
\beta>\beta_{0}
and all
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
. We have to show that for all
R>0
, the maximal estimate

(4.2)

{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{2}}f\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}\>,

holds for all
\beta>\beta_{0}
and all
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
.

By the hypothesis, there exist positive constants
\Lambda
and
C_{7}
such that
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
, the phase functions of the corresponding multipliers satisfy

(4.3)

|\psi_{1}(\lambda)-\psi_{2}(\lambda)|\leq C_{7}\>,\>\text{ for }\lambda\in(\Lambda,\infty)\>,

and
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\Lambda,\infty)
. Now let us choose and fix
R>0
and
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
. We next consider an auxiliary non-negative, even function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
such that
Supp(\eta)\subset\left\{\xi:1/2<|\xi|<2\right\}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\eta\left(2^{-k}\xi\right)=1\>,\>\text{ for }\xi\neq 0\>.

Let
k_{0}
be the largest
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
k\leq\log_{2}\lceil\Lambda\rceil+1
. Then setting

\eta_{k_{0}}(\xi):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}\eta\left(2^{-k}\xi\right)\>,

and for each
k>k_{0}
, setting

\eta_{k}(\xi):=\eta\left(2^{-k}\xi\right)\>,

we get,

\widehat{f}\equiv\widehat{f}\cdot\eta_{k_{0}}+\displaystyle\sum_{k>k_{0}}\widehat{f}\cdot\eta_{k}\>.

By the Schwartz isomorphism theorem, for each
k\geq k_{0}
, there exists
f_{k}\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
such that
\widehat{f_{k}}=\widehat{f}\cdot\eta_{k}
and hence,

(4.4)

\widehat{f}\equiv\widehat{f_{k_{0}}}+\displaystyle\sum_{k>k_{0}}\widehat{f_{k}}\>,

with
Supp\left(\widehat{f_{k_{0}}}\right)\subset\left[0,2^{k_{0}+1}\right)
and for
k>k_{0}
,
Supp\left(\widehat{f_{k}}\right)\subset\left(2^{k-1},2^{k+1}\right)
. Then (
4.4
) yields,

(4.5)

{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{2}}f\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{2}}f_{k_{0}}\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}+\displaystyle\sum_{k>k_{0}}{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{2}}f_{k}\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\>.

Now proceeding as in the proof of the small frequency case (Lemma
3.1
), we have

(4.6)

{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{2}}f_{k_{0}}\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim{\|f_{k_{0}}\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}\leq{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}\>,\text{ for all }\beta>\beta_{0}\>.

Next for each
k>k_{0}
, by using the formula (
1.5
) and Taylor’s formula, we have for
s\in[0,R]
and
t\in(0,1)
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left|S_{\psi_{1},t}f_{k}(s)-S_{\psi_{2},t}f_{k}(s)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(s)\>\left(e^{it\psi_{1}(\lambda)}-e^{it\psi_{2}(\lambda)}\right)\>\widehat{f_{k}}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(s)\>e^{it\psi_{1}(\lambda)}\>\left(1-e^{it(\psi_{2}(\lambda)-\psi_{1}(\lambda))}\right)\>\widehat{f_{k}}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(s)\>e^{it\psi_{1}(\lambda)}\>\left[1-\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{{\left\{it(\psi_{2}(\lambda)-\psi_{1}(\lambda))\right\}}^{j}}{j!}\right]\>\widehat{f_{k}}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(s)\>e^{it\psi_{1}(\lambda)}\>\left[\psi_{2}(\lambda)-\psi_{1}(\lambda)\right]^{j}\>\widehat{f_{k}}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>\right|\>.

Now again by the Schwartz isomorphism theorem, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
g_{j,k}\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
such that

\widehat{g_{j,k}}=\left[\psi_{2}-\psi_{1}\right]^{j}\>\widehat{f_{k}}\>.

Then again using the formula (
1.5
), we obtain from (
4.7
),

(4.8)

\left\|\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<1}\left|S_{\psi_{1},t}f_{k}-S_{\psi_{2},t}f_{k}\right|\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}{\left\|S^{*}_{\psi_{1}}g_{j,k}\right\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\>.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we set
\beta_{1}=\beta_{0}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and
\beta_{2}=\beta_{0}+\varepsilon
. Then noting that
Supp(\widehat{g_{j,k}})\subset\left(2^{k-1},2^{k+1}\right)
and applying (
4.1
) and Lemma
4.1
, we get that

{\left\|S^{*}_{\psi_{1}}g_{j,k}\right\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim{\|g_{j,k}\|}_{H^{\beta_{1}}(S)}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{k\varepsilon}{2}}\>{\|g_{j,k}\|}_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}\>,

where the resultant implicit constant is independent of
k
. Plugging the above in (
4.8
),

(4.9)

\left\|\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<1}\left|S_{\psi_{1},t}f_{k}-S_{\psi_{2},t}f_{k}\right|\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{k\varepsilon}{2}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}{\|g_{j,k}\|}_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}\>.

Next using the definition of
g_{j,k}
and (
4.3
), it follows that

{\|g_{j,k}\|}_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}\leq C_{7}^{j}\>{\|f_{k}\|}_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}\>.

Plugging the above in (
4.9
), we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle\left\|\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<1}\left|S_{\psi_{1},t}f_{k}-S_{\psi_{2},t}f_{k}\right|\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{k\varepsilon}{2}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{7}^{j}}{j!}{\|f_{k}\|}_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{k\varepsilon}{2}}{\|f_{k}\|}_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}\>.

Hence for each
k>k_{0}
using (
4.1
), (
4.10
) and Lemma
4.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{2}}f_{k}\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<1}\left|S_{\psi_{1},t}f_{k}-S_{\psi_{2},t}f_{k}\right|\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}+{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{1}}f_{k}\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{k\varepsilon}{2}}{\|f_{k}\|}_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}\>+\>{\|f_{k}\|}_{H^{\beta_{1}}(S)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{k\varepsilon}{2}}{\|f_{k}\|}_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{k\varepsilon}{2}}{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}\>.

Then plugging the above and (
4.6
) in (
4.5
), we get that

\displaystyle{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{2}}f\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{2}}f_{k_{0}}\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}+\displaystyle\sum_{k>k_{0}}{\|S^{*}_{\psi_{2}}f_{k}\|}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(1+\displaystyle\sum_{k>k_{0}}2^{-\frac{k\varepsilon}{2}}\right){\|f\|}_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta_{2}}(S)}\>.

Now as
R>0,\>\varepsilon>0
and
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
were arbitrarily chosen, (
4.2
) follows. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
∎

We present the proof of necessity of the conditions by dividing it into three different cases depending on the value/range of values of the Sobolev exponent

and

\displaystyle U_{1}f_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptN-N^{1/2}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle N+N^{1/2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left(\frac{s^{n-1}}{A(s)}\right)}^{1/2}\frac{1}{{(\lambda s)}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\>e^{i\left(\lambda s-\frac{\pi}{4}(n-1)\right)}\>e^{it(s){\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{a/2}}\>\widehat{f_{N}}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>,

\displaystyle U_{2}f_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptN-N^{1/2}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle N+N^{1/2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left(\frac{s^{n-1}}{A(s)}\right)}^{1/2}\frac{1}{{(\lambda s)}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\>e^{-i\left(\lambda s-\frac{\pi}{4}(n-1)\right)}\>e^{it(s){\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{a/2}}\>\widehat{f_{N}}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>,

\displaystyle U_{3}f_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptN-N^{1/2}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle N+N^{1/2}$}}$}}\nolimits\mathscr{E}_{1}(\lambda,s)\>e^{it(s){\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{a/2}}\>\widehat{f_{N}}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>.

By plugging in the definition of
\widehat{f_{N}}
in terms of
\eta
and then performing the change of variable
\xi=-N^{-1/2}\lambda+N^{1/2}
, we get

\displaystyle U_{2}f_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ce^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(n-1)}}{{A(s)}^{1/2}}\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptN-N^{1/2}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle N+N^{1/2}$}}$}}\nolimits e^{-i\left\{\lambda s-t(s){\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{a/2}\right\}}\>\frac{N^{-1/2}\>\eta\left(-N^{-1/2}\lambda+N^{1/2}\right)}{\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-1}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ce^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(n-1)}}{{A(s)}^{1/2}}\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt-1$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 1$}}$}}\nolimits e^{i\theta(\xi)}\>\zeta(\xi)\>d\xi\>,

where

\displaystyle\theta(\xi)=\left(N^{1/2}\xi-N\right)s+t(s)\left[(N-N^{1/2}\xi)^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right]^{a/2}\>,

\displaystyle\zeta(\xi)=\eta(\xi)\>\frac{{\left|{\bf c}\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)\right|}^{-1}}{\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\>.

Now we note that

\theta(\xi)=N^{1/2}\xi s-Ns+t(s)N^{a}\left[1-2N^{-1/2}\xi+N^{-1}\xi^{2}+\frac{N^{-2}Q^{2}}{4}\right]^{a/2}\>.

As
|\xi|\leq 1
, there exists
N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N>N_{2}
,

\left|-2N^{-1/2}\xi+N^{-1}\xi^{2}+\frac{N^{-2}Q^{2}}{4}\right|\leq 2N^{-1/2}+N^{-1}+\frac{N^{-2}Q^{2}}{4}<1\>,

and thus applying the generalized Binomial expansion along with
|\xi|\leq 1
, we get that

\displaystyle\theta(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N^{1/2}\xi s-Ns+t(s)N^{a}\left[1-aN^{-1/2}\xi+\frac{a(a-1)}{2}N^{-1}\xi^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(N^{-3/2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t(s)N^{a}-Ns+N^{1/2}\xi s-t(s)aN^{a-\frac{1}{2}}\xi+\frac{a(a-1)}{2}t(s)N^{a-1}\xi^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(t(s)N^{a-\frac{3}{2}}\right)\>.

Now choosing,

t(s)=\frac{s}{aN^{a-1}}\>,

we get rid of the linear terms in
\xi
to obtain,

\theta(\xi)=\frac{a-1}{2}s\xi^{2}+C(a,N,s)\>.

Thus for
s\in[\varepsilon\>,\>2\varepsilon]
, using the growth asymptotic of the density function (
2.2
), it follows that

\left|U_{2}f_{N}(s)\right|\geq c\>\varepsilon^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\left|\int_{-1}^{1}e^{i\frac{(a-1)}{2}s\xi^{2}}\>\zeta(\xi)\>d\xi\right|\>.

Now recalling the definition of
\zeta
, we note that
\eta
is non-negative and as
\xi\in[-1,1]
,

N-N^{1/2}\leq N-N^{1/2}\xi\leq N+N^{1/2}\>,

and thus

\frac{{\left|{\bf c}\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)\right|}^{-1}}{\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\asymp 1\>.

Then using the above information, expanding the last integral in terms of sine and cosine and using their properties along with the fact that
s\in[\varepsilon,2\varepsilon]
for
\varepsilon>0
small, it easily follows that for
N>\max\{N_{1},N_{2}\}
, there exists a positive constant
C_{8}
, independent of
N
such that

(5.10)

\left|U_{2}f_{N}(s)\right|>C_{8}\>,\>\>\>\>\text{for }s\in[\varepsilon,2\varepsilon]\>.

We next show that for large
N
, the growths of both
U_{1}f_{N}
and
U_{3}f_{N}
are subordinated by
U_{2}f_{N}
. We first focus on
U_{3}f_{N}
. For
s\in[\varepsilon,2\varepsilon]
, using the error term estimates (
5.8
), we get that there exists
N_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N\geq N_{3}
,

(5.11)

\displaystyle\left|U_{3}f_{N}(s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c}{N^{1/2}}\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptN-N^{1/2}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle N+N^{1/2}$}}$}}\nolimits(\lambda s)^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}\>\eta\left(-N^{-1/2}\lambda+N^{1/2}\right)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-1}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c\>\varepsilon^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}}{N^{1/2}}\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptN-N^{1/2}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle N+N^{1/2}$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c\>\varepsilon^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}}{N}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{C_{8}}{4}\>.

The inequality in the third line follows from the elementary estimate

\log\left(\frac{x+1}{x-1}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{x}\>,

which is valid for all large
x
.

We now shift our focus to
U_{1}f_{N}
. Again proceeding as in the case of
U_{2}f_{N}
, we get

U_{1}f_{N}(s)=\frac{ce^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}(n-1)}}{{A(s)}^{1/2}}\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt-1$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 1$}}$}}\nolimits e^{i\theta(\xi)}\>\zeta(\xi)\>d\xi\>,

where

\displaystyle\theta(\xi)=-N^{1/2}s\xi+Ns+t(s)\left[(N-N^{1/2}\xi)^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right]^{a/2}\>,

\displaystyle\zeta(\xi)=\eta(\xi)\>\frac{{\left|{\bf c}\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)\right|}^{-1}}{\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\>.

Now by Lemma
2.4
(for
l=1
in the statement),

(5.12)

\int_{-1}^{1}e^{i\theta(\xi)}\>\zeta(\xi)\>d\xi=i\int_{-1}^{1}e^{i\theta(\xi)}\>\left[\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\theta^{\prime}(\xi)}-\frac{\zeta(\xi)\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{{\left(\theta^{\prime}(\xi)\right)}^{2}}\right]\>d\xi\>.

We note that

\theta^{\prime}(\xi)=-N^{1/2}\left[s+t(s)\>a\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)\left\{\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right\}^{\left(\frac{a}{2}-1\right)}\right]\>.

Now as
\xi\in[-1,1]
, we have that

N-N^{1/2}\xi\geq 0\>,

and thus for
s\in[\varepsilon,2\varepsilon]
,

s+t(s)\>a\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)\left\{\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right\}^{\left(\frac{a}{2}-1\right)}\geq\varepsilon\>.

This implies that

(5.13)

|\theta^{\prime}(\xi)|\geq\varepsilon N^{1/2}\>.

We next note that

\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=t(s)aN\left\{\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right\}^{\left(\frac{a}{2}-2\right)}\left\{(a-1)\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right\}\>.

Recalling that
\xi\in[-1,1]
,
t(s)=s/(aN^{a-1})
and
s\in[\varepsilon,2\varepsilon]
, we get that there exists
N_{4}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N>N_{4}
,

(5.14)

|\theta^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|\leq CN^{1-a}NN^{a-4}N^{2}=C\>.

Now recalling the definition of
\zeta
, we note that as
\xi\in[-1,1]
,

N-N^{1/2}\leq N-N^{1/2}\xi\leq N+N^{1/2}\>,

and thus

\frac{{\left|{\bf c}\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)\right|}^{-1}}{\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\asymp 1\>.

It follows that

(5.15)

|\zeta(\xi)|\leq C\>.

We next note that

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}(\xi)\>\frac{{\left|{\bf c}\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)\right|}^{-1}}{\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\>+\>\eta(\xi)\>\frac{\frac{d}{d\xi}\left({\left|{\bf c}\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)\right|}^{-1}\right)}{\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(n-1)N^{1/2}\eta(\xi)\>{\left|{\bf c}\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)\right|}^{-1}}{2\left(N-N^{1/2}\xi\right)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}

Then using the pointwise and derivative estimates of
{\left|\bf c(\cdot)\right|}^{-2}
given in (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we get that there exists
N_{5}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N>N_{5}
,

(5.16)

|\zeta^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq C\left(1+N^{-1/2}+N^{-1/2}\right)\leq C\>.

Thus for
s\in[\varepsilon,2\varepsilon]
, using (
5.12
)-(
5.16
), there exists
N_{6}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N>N_{6}
,

(5.17)

\displaystyle\left|U_{1}f_{N}(s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\>\varepsilon^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\left|\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt-1$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 1$}}$}}\nolimits e^{i\theta(\xi)}\>\zeta(\xi)\>d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\>\varepsilon^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\>N^{-1/2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{C_{8}}{4}\>.

Hence for
s\in[\varepsilon,2\varepsilon]
, setting
N_{0}=\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq 6}N_{j}
, for all
N>N_{0}
, in view of the decomposition (
5.9
) we get from (
5.10
), (
5.11
) and (
5.17
) that

\left|T_{\psi}f_{N}(s)\right|\geq\left|U_{2}f_{N}(s)\right|-\left|U_{1}f_{N}(s)\right|-\left|U_{3}f_{N}(s)\right|>\frac{C_{8}}{2}\>.

This yields (
5.3
) and hence the proof of case 1 for the Fractional Schrödinger equation (for
a>1
) corresponding to
\Delta
, is completed.

The above arguments can be carried out verbatim for the Fractional Schrödinger equation (for
a>1
) corresponding to the shifted operator
\tilde{\Delta}
(perhaps even a bit simpler due to no spectral gap).

The case of the Boussinesq equation and the Beam equation (both corresponding to
\Delta
as well as
\tilde{\Delta}
) follow from the result for the Schrödinger equation which we just proved and the Transference principle (Theorem
1.6
). Indeed, in their case, if the estimate (
1.7
) is true for some
\beta_{0}<1/4
, then it would also be true for any
\beta>\beta_{0}
. Then by point
(ii)
of Remark
1.5
and the transference principle (Theorem
1.6
), (
1.7
) is also true for the classical Schrödinger equation (
a=2
) for any
\beta>\beta_{0}
and hence in particular for the choice

\beta=\frac{1}{2}\left(\beta_{0}+\frac{1}{4}\right)<\frac{1}{4}\>.

But that is a contradiction. This completes the proof of case 1.

Case 2
:
If
1/4\leq\beta<n/2
, then the condition
p\leq 2n/(n-2\beta)
is necessary for validity of the inequality (
1.7
).

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})_{e}
be (non-zero) non-negative with
Supp(\eta)\subset(1,2)
. Then for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
g_{N}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
such that

\mathscr{F}g_{N}(\lambda)=\eta(\lambda/N)\>,\>\>\>\>\>\>\lambda\in[0,\infty)\>.

We note that

(5.18)

g_{1}(0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(\lambda)\lambda^{n-1}d\lambda=C_{9}>0\>,

and for
s\in[0,\infty)
, by the Euclidean Fourier inversion applied to radial functions

\displaystyle g_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta\left(\frac{\lambda}{N}\right)\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\lambda s)\lambda^{n-1}d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(\lambda)\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\lambda Ns)\lambda^{n-1}d\lambda=N^{n}g_{1}(Ns)\>.

Therefore, by (
5.18
) there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
such that

(5.19)

|g_{N}(s)|>\frac{C_{9}}{2}N^{n}\>,\>\>\>\>\>\>\text{for all, $0\leq s<\frac{\varepsilon}{N}$.}

Now from the definition of
g_{N}
, it follows that
Supp\>(\>\mathscr{F}g_{N}\>)\subset(N,2N)
. Then by Lemma
2.5
, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
f_{N}\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
such that

(5.20)

\lambda^{n-1}\mathscr{F}g_{N}(\lambda)={|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>\widehat{f_{N}}(\lambda)\>,

for all
\lambda
. By (
5.20
), it follows that

(5.21)

\displaystyle{\|f_{N}\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{\beta}\eta\left(\lambda/N\right)^{2}\>\frac{\lambda^{2(n-1)}}{{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-4}}\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{\beta+\frac{n}{2}}{\left(\int_{1}^{2}\eta(\lambda)^{2}\>\lambda^{2\beta+n-1}\>d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{\beta+\frac{n}{2}}\>.

Next, for
s\in[0,\varepsilon/N)
, using the spherical Fourier inversion along with the decomposition (
3.4
), we write

\displaystyle S_{\psi,0}f_{N}(s)=f_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f_{N}}(\lambda)\varphi_{\lambda}(s)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}\left(\frac{s^{n-1}}{A(s)}\right)^{1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f_{N}}(\lambda)\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\lambda s)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda

\displaystyle\>\>\>\>+\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f_{N}}(\lambda)E_{1}(\lambda,s)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda=f_{N,1}(s)+f_{N,2}(s)\>.

Then using (
5.20
) and proceeding as in (

), it follows that

|f_{N,1}(s)|\asymp|g_{N}(s)|\;,\>\>\>\>\>\text{for all, $s\in[0,\varepsilon/N)$,}

where the implicit constant is independent of
N
. Consequently, it follows from (
5.19
) that there exists a positive number
c
(independent of
N
) such that

(5.22)

|f_{N,1}(s)|\geq cN^{n}\>,\>\>\>\>\>\text{for all, $s\in[0,\varepsilon/N)$.}

We next show that the growth of
f_{N,2}
is subordinated by
f_{N,1}
. As
s\in[0,\varepsilon/N)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
it follows that for
\lambda\in(1,2)
the quantity
\lambda Ns
is smaller than
1
. Hence using the estimate (
3.5
) of the error term
E_{1}
, we get that for
s\in[0,\varepsilon/N)
,

\displaystyle|f_{N,2}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta\left(\frac{\lambda}{N}\right)\>|E_{1}(\lambda,s)|\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\int_{1}^{2}\eta(\lambda)\>|E_{1}(\lambda N,s)|\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda N)|}^{-2}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N\|\eta\|_{\infty}\int_{1}^{2}s^{2}\>(\lambda N)^{n-1}d\lambda

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle c^{\prime}\varepsilon^{2}N^{n-2}\>.

Therefore, using triangle inequality and (
5.22
), it follows that for all
s\in[0,\varepsilon/N)
and

sufficiently large,

\displaystyle|S_{\psi,0}f_{N}(s)|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|f_{N,1}(s)|-|f_{N,2}(s)|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle cN^{n}-c^{\prime}\varepsilon^{2}N^{n-2}\geq c^{\prime\prime}N^{n}\>.

This implies that for all
x\in B_{\varepsilon/N}
,

(5.23)

S^{*}_{\psi}f_{N}(x)\geq c\>N^{n}\>,

with the constant
c
being independent of
N
. Now, if the estimate (
1.7
) holds, then by (
5.21
) and (
5.23
), it follows that

{\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon/N}N^{np}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/p}\lesssim N^{\beta+\frac{n}{2}}\>,

which implies that

N^{n\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\lesssim N^{\beta+\frac{n}{2}}\>.

Then letting

, it follows that

that is,

.

Case 3
:
If
\beta=n/2
then (
1.7
) does not hold for

.

In this case we show the failure of the inequality

(5.24)

{\|S^{*}f\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq C_{B}\>{\|f\|}_{H^{n/2}(S)}\>,

for every ball centered at the identity
e
. We first note by the sufficient conditions of Theorem
1.1
(and hence by Corollary

) that for all radial

, one has

(5.25)

\lim_{t\to 0}S_{\psi,t}f(x)=f(x)\>,\text{ for almost every }x\in S\>.

Now if we assume that the inequality (

) holds, combining it with (

), it would follow that

{\|f\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}={\|\lim_{t\to 0}S_{\psi,t}f\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq{\|S^{*}f\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq C_{B}\>{\|f\|}_{H^{n/2}(S)}\>,

for every ball centered at the identity

. But this is not true
[
DR24
, Remark 5.1]
. Hence we get the desired failure of (

.

Remark 5.1
.

It is curious to note that the oscillatory nature of the multiplier is crucially used in the proof of case 1, that is, to show that no maximal estimate (
1.7
) is possible if
\beta<1/4
. In the other two cases however, the crux of the arguments are weaved only in terms of the initial data and hence the multiplier plays no role there.

6.
Concluding remarks

In this section, we make some remarks and pose some new problems:

6.1.
Results for general dispersive equations:

Our proof of the sufficient conditions of Theorem
1.1
, essentially works for the general class of dispersive equations whose corresponding multipliers have phase functions
\psi
satisfying the following asymptotic properties for some real numbers
\delta_{1}>0
and
\delta_{2}>1
:

\begin{cases}|\psi^{\prime}(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda^{\delta_{1}-1}\>,\text{ for }\lambda\in(0,1)\>,\\
|\psi^{\prime}(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda^{\delta_{2}-1}\>,\text{ for }\lambda\geq 1\>,\\
|\psi^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)|\asymp\lambda^{\delta_{2}-2}\>,\text{ for }\lambda\geq 1\>.\end{cases}

Interestingly, in the proof of the necessity of the conditions of Theorem
1.1
(along with the asymptotics of the first two derivatives), as clarified by the Transference principle (Theorem
1.6
), it is the high frequency asymptotics of
\psi
that turns out to be the heart of the matter. Thus dispensing of the explicit expressions of
\psi
, our arguments can be employed to obtain mapping properties (and consequently results on pointwise convergence) for general dispersive equations, even if one only has suitable asymptotic control on
\psi
and its first and second derivatives.

6.2.
Riemannian symmetric spaces of higher rank:

A special case of the class of Damek-Ricci spaces, is the class of rank one Riemannian symmetric spaces of non-compact type, that is, homogeneous spaces
G/K
, where
G
is a connected, non-compact, semi-simple Lie Group with finite center and real rank one and
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. In the case of rank one, the geometric notion of radiality coincides with the algebraic notion of
K
-biinvariance. It then becomes natural to pose the Carleson’s problem for the Schrödinger equation (or more general dispersive equations) with
K
-biinvariant initial data on Riemannian symmetric spaces of non-compact type of arbitrary rank.

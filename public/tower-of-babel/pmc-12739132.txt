The quality and reliability of short videos about uraemia on BiliBili and TikTok: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Hui Zeng, Jiaqin Yuan, Bo Zhang, Jia Tang, Deqiong Xie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to assess the quality and reliability of short videos about uraemia on BiliBili and TikTok. On June 12, 2025, we searched the top 100 videos arranged by default using the keyword “uraemia” on both platforms. We collected and analysed their characteristics, content, and uploaders. To evaluate the quality, the Global Quality Score (GQS) and the modified DISCERN tool (mDISCERN) were used. This study included 153 videos in total, with 81 from TikTok and 72 from Bilibili. Overall, the audience engagement on TikTok was significantly greater than that on Bilibili. Although the median mDISCERN and GQS scores were identical across platforms (2 [2–2] and 2 [2–3], respectively), their score distributions differed significantly (p < 0.05). Interestingly, we found that videos from self-media achieved higher GQS scores than those from doctors or official media. A weak positive correlation was found between video quality and collections and shares on Bilibili alone (p < 0.05). In conclusion, the quality and reliability of health information from uraemia videos on TikTok and Bilibili were found to be suboptimal. Concerted efforts from the public, uploaders, and social media platforms are needed to foster the dissemination of high-quality health information.Keywords: Bilibili, Reliability, Social media, TikTok, Uraemia, Video qualitySubject terms: Diseases, Health care, Medical research, Nephrology, Urology

Introduction

Uraemia, the end-stage manifestation of chronic kidney disease (CKD), is diagnosed when the glomerular filtration rate (GFR) declines below 15 mL/min/1.73 m². This condition has emerged as a critical global public health challenge1. Epidemiological studies have revealed that CKD affects 11–14% of the worldwide population2, with more than 2 million patients progressing annually to uraemia requiring kidney replacement therapy (KRT) for survival3, and CKD is expected to become the fifth leading cause of global mortality within two decades4. Studies5,6 have demonstrated substantial economic escalation during CKD progression, with average annual costs increasing fourfold from $2,214 at the G3a stage to $8,227 at the non-KRT G5 stage. The financial burden becomes particularly severe at the uraemic stage, where annual haemodialysis costs reach $57,334 per patient.

It is well established that patients who understand the etiology, pathophysiology, treatment, and prevention of their condition can more effectively participate in and adhere to disease prevention or treatment regimens7. However, owing to previous limitations in information technology, the incidence of CKD was restricted. A cross-sectional study8 (2018–2019) revealed that while approximately 82 million Chinese adults suffer from CKD, the disease awareness rate is merely 10%. Moreover, due to its insidious onset, CKD is easily overlooked in the early stages, and many patients have already progressed to the uraemic stage before seeking medical attention. Studies have confirmed that health information distribution can enhance the early diagnosis and prevention of diseases by improving health behaviour9. Therefore, there is an urgent need to increase awareness of uraemia through the communication of effective health information.

With the advent of digital health care, most people turn to the internet to look up health-related information10. Social media (such as TikTok, Bilibili, Kuaishou, etc.) have become primary sources of health information by overcoming traditional physical barriers to medical support and resources11,12. Compared with conventional text-based formats (e.g., medical journals and health care websites), short videos have gained widespread popularity because of their intuitive and vivid content, easy access, and fast dissemination. These characteristics have established short videos as crucial channels for public health communication. According to the 56th Statistical Report on China’s Internet Development, China’s short video user base has reached 1.068 billion, accounting for 95.1% of the total internet population13. As China’s most dominant short-video platforms, TikTok and Bilibili, have become critical channels for the public to access health information14.

It is worth noting that short videos are not merely audiovisual media but often contain substantial textual information, placing demands on the public’s health literacy. Given the varying levels of public health literacy, the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, and the American Medical Association (AMA) all recommend that patient education materials be written at a sixth-grade reading level or below to ensure comprehensibility15. However, health videos on social media are diverse and lack regulation, and the readability of their textual information often falls short of this standard. Furthermore, while health professionals and the public can utilize social media for health information sharing, concerns emerge when nonexperts (including patients) engage in medical information exchange16. This practice may facilitate the spread of misinformation, potentially compromising public health and health behaviours.

Previous studies have been conducted to analyse short videos covering various health topics, including gastric cancer17, gallstone disease18, and cervical cancer19. These investigations have consistently revealed deficiencies in their quality and reliability. Whereas studies assessing the quality of videos about uraemia are still limited, this study aimed to systematically assess the information quality and dependability of uraemia-related videos on TikTok and Bilibili, with the goal of providing a scientific basis for the distribution of superior health information.

Materials and methods

Ethical considerations

All the data are derived from publicly accessible TikTok and Bilibili videos, and none of them are subject to personal privacy issues. After review by the Medical Ethics Committee of The Second People’s Hospital of Yibin, this study was found to meet the criteria for exemption from ethical review.

Study design

This study employed a cross-sectional design to evaluate uraemia-related videos on TikTok and Bilibili.

Data collection

On June 12, 2025, we conducted a systematic search for related videos on two social media platforms (TikTok and Bilibili) using the Chinese keyword “uraemia”. To ensure data stability and minimize potential bias from short-term fluctuations in viewer engagement, we excluded videos published within seven days of data collection. The exclusion criteria were videos unrelated to the topic, duplicate videos and adverts. Default sorting was used to filter the top 100 videos. For each video, we recorded and analysed the following characteristics: title, release date, video duration, video length, the quantity of likes, comments, collections and shares, video content, and quality score. Similarly, details about the uploader, including the ID, number of followers, authentication status and type, were collected.

Video classification

The uploaders were categorized as follows: (1) doctors, (2) self-media (i.e., nonmedical professionals, individual accounts), and (3) official media (e.g., network media, TV stations, and hospitals). Doctor certification types were categorized as follows: nephrologists, other internal medicine physicians (including pulmonologists, endocrinologists, etc.), surgeons, other specialists (including psychiatrists, reproductive medicine specialists, etc.), and traditional Chinese medicine practitioners. The topics of the videos were categorized as anatomy, physiology, risk factors, aetiology, epidemiology, symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, complication, prevention, and prognosis. Some videos covered multiple topics.

Quality assessment

The information quality of the selected videos was assessed using the modified DISCERN tool (mDISCERN) and global quality score (GQS). The DISCERN tool was modified to create mDISCERN, which is more suited for evaluating the dependability of video content20. It includes five parameters regarding the video’s reliability: clarity, relevance, traceability, robustness, and fairness. Every question has a “yes” score of one point and a “no” score of zero. Higher scores indicate more dependability. Another popular tool for assessing the health information in videos is the GQS21, which evaluates video quality based on comprehensiveness, usability, and overall presentation quality- including dimensions such as logical structure, visual presentation, and audience comprehension. It rates the overall quality of videos on a 5-point scale that ranges from bad (1) to outstanding (5). From June 13 to 20, 2025, two authors (H.Z. and B.Z.) separately evaluated the quality of the remaining videos. If the two authors did not agree, a third arbiter (JQ.Y.) assigned the final score.

Statistical analysis

All the statistical analyses were performed using R4.5.0. The Shapiro‒Wilk test was used to determine if continuous variables were normally distributed. For normally distributed data, the means ± standard deviations (SDs) were used, and for nonnormally distributed data, the medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs) were used. Intergroup comparisons were performed using the Mann‒Whitney U test, and comparisons involving three or more independent groups were performed using the Kruskal‒Wallis test. To evaluate the relationship between audience engagement and video quality scores, Spearman’s test was performed. In addition, we employed Cohen’s kappa (κ) to measure the two authors’ agreement, interpreted as follows: < 0.20 (poor), 0.21–0.40 (fair), 0.41–0.60 (moderate), 0.61–0.80 (good), and 0.81–1.00 (excellent). A p value < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

Video characteristics

In accordance with the inclusion and exclusion rules, similar, irrelevant and advertising videos were removed. Our study included a total of 153 videos (Fig. 1), of which 72 were from Bilibili and 81 from TikTok. The comprehensive characteristics of these videos are presented in Table 1. The overall video duration was 114 (57–205) seconds, and they were all available online for an extended period, with a median duration of 190 (82–688) days. In terms of audience interaction, the overall video received 3196 (634–12,000) likes, 223 (57–833) comments, 936 (229–4081) collections, and 592 (74–4524) shares. In terms of video quality, the mDISCERN score of all the videos was 2 (2–2) points, and the GQS was 2 (2–3) points. Overall, the κ value of the video quality score was 0.808, indicating excellent consistency.

Uploader characteristics

In this study, the majority of the video uploaders on both platforms were doctors, with doctors accounting for as many as 84.0% of TikTok accounts (Table 3). Self-media accounts were the second most common uploaders, with 30.5% on Bilibili and 11.1% on TikTok, while official media accounts were the least common on both platforms (Fig. 3a). With respect to platform verification, both platforms had high certification rates for doctors, exceeding 95%. However, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) practitioners were underrepresented. Among verified doctor accounts on TikTok, nephrology specialists were the most prevalent (56.7%), followed by other internal medicine physicians (17.9%) and surgeons (16.4%). In contrast, Bilibili was dominated by surgeons (34.8%), with nephrology specialists ranking second (30.4%) (Fig. 3b).

Discussion

In the contemporary era, social media has become a powerful tool for controlling information dissemination and shaping public understanding and behaviour. The COVID-19 pandemic, in particular, has further highlighted the role of social media in the spread of health information22,23. While both public and health care professionals can utilize social media to disseminate information, its misuse can transform it into a source of misleading information, potentially leading to significant negative consequences due to its vast reach and influence24. To accurately reflect the health information environment faced by the Chinese public, this study focuses on the most popular and representative short-video platforms in China—TikTok and Bilibili—to evaluate the quality and reliability of videos related to uraemia.

Principal findings

To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first cross-sectional analysis to examine the dependability and quality of short videos related to uraemia on TikTok and Bilibili. This study revealed that uraemia-related videos attracted considerable attention on both platforms, with four of them receiving more than 100,000 likes. However, the median mDISCERN score of the overall videos was 2 (2–2), and the median GQS was 2 (2–3), indicating that the video quality was unsatisfactory. This aligns with prior studies on other health topics (such as cervical cancer19, COVID-1922, and ADPKD25). Due to the limited ability of the public to discern health information, only accurate popular science information can enhance the public’s understanding of diseases, prevent them from being misled by inaccurate information, and promote informed decision-making.

Video characteristics

In comparison to Bilibili, TikTok videos exhibited significantly greater audience engagement (likes, comments, collections, and shares), which is likely attributable to TikTok’s larger user base. This is in line with the observation that TikTok creators generally possess more followers. Moreover, TikTok mainly features short videos, characterized by rapid updates and short durations, resulting in relatively stronger interactivity. In fact, we found that video quality does not consistently match the effectiveness of its dissemination. In this study, video quality on the Bilibili platform was weakly correlated with the number of collections and shares. Conversely, on TikTok, video quality was not significantly correlated with audience interaction. This discrepancy may be related to the nature of entertainment and the platform’s recommendation algorithm. The current recommendation algorithm aims to maximize user engagement by prioritizing highly interactive content, which may lead to the neglect of high-quality content. This does not necessarily correlate positively with the quality and reliability of health information. Our findings are consistent with this phenomenon. For instance, some cautionary videos, despite being short and lacking health information, resonate more strongly with the public and achieve greater engagement. This might be because they emphasize the harm of diseases, which can attract more attention. In contrast, high-quality health videos often hold less appeal due to their longer duration and serious tone, resulting in lower traffic and reduced visibility. This observation is consistent with previous studies26,27 and indicates that the public struggles to identify the quality of health videos. As a result, platforms should implement professional vetting specifically for health videos and incorporate information quality and reliability metrics into their recommendation algorithms, promoting the dissemination of high-quality science communication videos. Simultaneously, video creators should consider how to create more vivid yet professional science communication videos, balancing content quality with interactivity. This approach can secure long-term advantages in algorithmic visibility, enabling them to attract public attention and disseminate accurate medical information.

Video quality

In this study, the uploaders of the included videos from both platforms were predominantly doctors. However, the mDISCERN scores and GQS scores for these videos were generally low, indicating unsatisfactory quality and reliability. This may be related to the assessment tools used. For instance, within the mDISCERN criteria, sources of information were frequently missing (Question 2) and did not include additional supplementary information (Question 4). Moreover, the GQS rules involve comprehensiveness, usability, and overall presentation quality. In addition, the internet is rife with science misinformation. Some doctors treat medical science communication merely as a profit-making tool, engaging in “fake popularization and real product promotion”. Others severely impede the spread of health knowledge by exaggerating effects or fabricating medical cases. Consequently, enhancing the overall quality of science popularization videos is vital.

In contrast to the findings of previous studies19,28, this study revealed that videos from self-media accounts achieved higher GQS scores than those from doctors or official media, but there was no significant difference in audience interaction. This discrepancy may be attributed to several factors. Firstly, the self-media accounts in this study were mainly operated by professional video creators, whose videos are typically longer in duration, contain more comprehensive health information, have stronger visual aids, and employ more attractive narrative techniques. Secondly, videos from doctors, although medically accurate, were typically shorter and more concise, often omitting detailed explanations, citations, or supporting evidence, which may have limited their scores. Thirdly, the higher production quality and more deliberate communication strategies employed by self-media creators likely resulted in more polished and viewer-friendly presentations, thereby improving perceived quality in GQS assessments. Finally, it is possible that doctors, due to the unique demands of their profession, may experience occupational burnout, which could potentially impact video quality29. Furthermore, there was no significant difference in the mDISCERN score among the uploaders. This might be due to distinct evaluative dimensions compared to the GQS: the mDISCERN focuses on the reliability of information (including source citations, evidence support, etc.). Our analysis revealed that all types of uploaders demonstrated consistent deficiencies in information reliability, particularly in providing source references, resulting in generally low mDISCERN scores with no inter-group differences.

There has been little previous research on the quality of health videos with regard to kidney disease. The study by YAO27 involved only 65 users from TikTok and revealed that the quality of health information regarding CKD videos was inconsistent. The dissemination of high-quality videos improves public health education30, while the spread of misinformation can amplify its negative effects exponentially because of the reach of the internet24. Therefore, social media platforms should strengthen the oversight of uploaders and video content. Clear release requirements for science videos should be established (e.g., mandatory citation of information sources and professional medical credential verification for self-media accounts) to promote the dissemination of high-quality health content.

With respect to doctor verification, we observed a relatively high verification rate on both platforms, which may be related to platform bias in algorithmic recommendations that favour verified accounts. Additionally, in this study, surgeons (34.8%) on Bilibili accounted for a greater proportion of uploaders than nephrologists did (30.4%). Although most doctors possess foundational knowledge of uraemia, the depth of knowledge can vary by specialty. Therefore, encouraging more nephrologists to share health information is recommended.

Video content

Previous studies17,26 have indicated that the aetiology, symptoms, and treatment of diseases are often topics of greater public interest. Our survey results also reveal similar topic distributions across both platforms, primarily revolving around these areas. However, we observed relatively limited coverage of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) content on both platforms, which may be associated with the following factors: the potential for certain Chinese herbal medicines to exacerbate kidney damage if misused and the fact that uraemia primarily relies on renal replacement therapy, with TCM often serving as an auxiliary therapy. The most popular video was uploaded by a self-media account specializing in medical science communication videos. This video comprehensively covered the epidemiology, risk factors, aetiology, symptoms, and prevention related to uraemia, thereby addressing the public’s fundamental need to understand the condition, which likely contributed to its high engagement rate. For the general healthy public, health information is often acquired from platform recommendation pages, and limited disease knowledge content may suffice for their needs. However, for uraemia patients or their families, there is a demand for higher-quality, more professional video content (such as understanding disease pathogenesis, how to choose treatment options, and how to improve prognosis). Therefore, platforms should consider the diverse needs of different user groups and leverage big data to provide targeted recommendations, thereby promoting the effective dissemination of health videos.

Limitations

This study has the following limitations. First, the assessment was confined to short videos in Chinese on only two platforms (TikTok and Bilibili), which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Other artificial intelligence tools (such as ChatGPT, Gemini, etc.), YouTube, and specialized health websites are all important channels for disseminating health information. Therefore, future analyses should incorporate a more diverse range of platforms to provide a more comprehensive digital health information landscape. Second, this study employs a cross-sectional design. Given the rapid pace of short video content updates, it cannot capture dynamic changes in content. Consequently, the findings reflect only the content characteristics observed during the specific study period. Future longitudinal research could be conducted to track evolving content trends. Third, selecting the top 100 videos based on default platform sorting may introduce selection bias due to algorithmic preferences, potentially overrepresenting highly interactive or hot-topic related content, while underrepresenting high-quality educational videos with lower visibility. Future research will employ more systematic sampling strategies, such as stratified sampling, to reduce bias. Fourth, the potential deletion or hiding of videos by uploaders could affect the representativeness of search results. Finally, this study did not conduct subgroup analysis based on video style or format (such as animation, live-action), and these factors may influence user engagement and perception of quality.

Strengths

Our study presents the first systematic assessment of the quality of uraemia-related information on leading Chinese video platforms, TikTok and Bilibili. By employing both the GQS and mDISCERN tool, we achieved a multidimensional analysis of both content quality and information reliability. Furthermore, we revealed a core contradiction in health information dissemination driven by platform algorithms: there exists no inherent correlation between viewer engagement and the actual quality of health information. This provides crucial empirical evidence for optimizing health communication strategies, refining platform algorithms, and enhancing public health literacy.

Conclusion

In summary, this investigation provides a basis for understanding the current landscape of uraemia-related videos on social media platforms. The results indicate that videos on TikTok and Bilibili attract significant attention. However, the quality and dependability of associated health information were suboptimal. Specifically, the GQS scores reflect generally poor overall video quality, with self-media videos outperforming other sources. Meanwhile, the mDISCERN scores reveal significant deficiencies in information reliability, particularly in evidence citation. Furthermore, we observed no significant correlation between audience engagement and video quality, suggesting limited public ability to discern content quality. Therefore, creators should enhance video quality, prioritize the standardization of information citations and the integrity of evidence, while also considering video interactivity. Social media platforms should strengthen oversight and increase requirements for the release of science communication videos. They should incorporate information quality and reliability metrics into their recommendation algorithms to promote more high-quality health information.

SWIPT in Cell-Free Massive MIMO Using Stacked Intelligent Metasurfaces
Thien Duc Hua, Mohammadali Mohammadi, Hien Quoc Ngo, Michail Matthaiou

Abstract
We investigate the integration of stacked intelligent metasurfaces (SIMs) into cell-free massive multiple input multiple output (CF-mMIMO) system to enhance the simultaneous wireless information and power transfer (SWIPT) performance. Closed-form expressions for the spectral efficiency (SE) of the information-decoding receivers (IRs) and the average sum of harvested energy (sum-HE) at the energy-harvesting receivers (ERs) in the novel system model are derived to subsequently formulate a maximum total average sum-HE problem under a minimum SE threshold per each IR. This problem jointly optimizes the SIM phase-shift (PS) configuration and access points’ (APs) power allocation, relying on long-term statistical channel state information (CSI). This non-convex problem is then transformed into more tractable forms. Then, efficient algorithms are proposed, including a layer-by-layer heuristic method for SIMs PS configuration that prioritizes sum-HE for the ERs and a successive convex approximation (SCA)-based power allocation scheme to improve the achievable SE for the IRs. Numerical results show that our proposed algorithms achieve an almost 7-fold sum-HE gain as we increase the number of SIM layers, while the proposed power allocation (PPA) scheme often gains up to 40% in terms of the achievable minimum SE, compared to the equal power allocation. † † footnotetext: This work was supported by the U.K. Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council (EPSRC) (grants No. EP/X04047X/1 and EP/X040569/1). The work of T. D. Hua and M. Matthaiou was supported by the European Research Council (ERC) under the European Union’s Horizon 2020 research and innovation programme (grant agreement No. 101001331). I
Introduction

The growing demand for instantaneous and reliable wireless connectivity, combined with the need for ubiquitous energy harvesting in Internet-of-Everything (IoE) devices, has positioned SWIPT as a key area of interest in both academic and industrial research. Over the past decade, extensive studies have highlighted the effectiveness of integrating SWIPT with CF-mMIMO systems, demonstrating notable improvements in both HE and SE metrics
[
1
]
.
However, a significant limitation of current CF-mMIMO SWIPT systems lies in the reliance on conventional transmit antennas at the APs. On one hand, an insufficient number of antennas can increase the probability of losing line-of-sight (LoS) propagation, which would adversely affect the SWIPT performance. On the other hand, deploying an excessive number of antennas at APs leads to prohibitive power consumption and increased inter-user interference due to high spatial correlation and pilot contamination (PC)
[
2
]
. To mitigate interference, fully digital beamforming techniques, like zero-forcing (ZF), are commonly used. However, these methods come with high hardware cost and computational complexity
[
3
]
. As a result, there is an urgent need for a CF-mMIMO SWIPT system that leverages innovative technologies to enable efficient and power-saving operation.

Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs) are promising architectures for enhancing the wireless networks’ performance. Specifically, SIMs, consisting of multiple layers of reconfigurable surface arrays, have gained significant attention compared to conventional single-layer RISs due to their superior signal processing capabilities. Leveraging SIMs in the vicinity of APs (with a small number of antennas and radio frequency (RF) chains) enables the formation of strong LoS connections while ensuring acceptable energy consumption
[
4
]
. Additionally, SIMs facilitate the development of practical, low-cost prototypes, offering a scalable and efficient solution
[
5
]
. Previous studies have investigated the integration of SIMs in various scenarios. An
et al.

[
6
]
designed a pair of SIM-attached transceivers to achieve interference-free transmission. Perovic
et al.

[
7
]
investigated the channel cutoff rate and the mutual information problem under MIMO systems with SIM-attached APs. Li
et al.

[
8
]
and Shi
et al.

[
9
]
respectively developed a gradient descent algorithm and a cyclic search with bisection scheme to maximize the achievable SE with SIM-attached APs in a CF-mMIMO system. To our knowledge, none of the studies mentioned above have investigated SWIPT CFmMIMO systems with novel SIM-attached APs design and long-term statistical CSI, PC, and pratical non-linear energy harvesting (NL-EH) model. To address this gap, we propose a SIMs-assisted SWIPT CFmMIMO system including:

•

We introduce a SIM-assisted CF-mMIMO SWIPT system for a Ricean channel model and provide the second- and fourth-order moments of the actual channels and estimated channels under the PC effect.

•

Closed-from expressions for the achievable SE of the IRs and the average sum HE of the ERs with a NL-EH model are derived.

•

We propose a layer-by-layer heuristic PS search and power allocation scheme to maximize the total average sum-HE, while satisfying a minimum SE per IR. Our numerical results demonstrate that the ER benefits significantly from increasing the SIM layers, while the achievable minimum SE increases about
40\%
by applying the PPA scheme.

II-A

Channel Model

We assume a block fading channel that remains invariant and frequency-flat during each coherence interval
\tau_{c}

\tau

In each coherence interval, the instantaneous channel from AP
m
to receiver

{\textbf{g}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}\triangleq{\bf F}_{m}^{{\dagger}}{\textbf{z}}_{mk}
, where
{\bf F}_{m}\in\C^{S\times N}

m
, and channel from the last SIM layer of AP

to receiver

In particular, the SIM-effected aggregated channel is formulated as

~{\bf F}_{m}\triangleq\bm{\Phi}^{mL}{\textbf{H}}^{mL}\cdots\bm{\Phi}^{m\ell}{\textbf{H}}^{m\ell}\cdots\bm{\PHI}^{m1}{\textbf{H}}^{m1},

(1)

where

6
]
, the coefficient from the
\breve{s}
-th element of the
(\ell-1)
-th layer to the
s
-th element of the
(\ell-1)
-th layer is formulated as

~[{\textbf{H}}^{m\ell}]_{s,\breve{s}}\!=\!\frac{\lambda^{2}\cos(\chi^{m\ell}_{s,\breve{s}})}{4d^{\ell}_{s,\breve{s}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi d^{\ell}_{s,\breve{s}}}\!-\!\frac{j}{\lambda}\bigg)\exp\Bigg(\frac{j2\pi d^{\ell}_{s,\breve{s}}}{\lambda}\Bigg),

(2)

where
\lambda
is the wavelength,
\chi^{m\ell}_{s,\breve{s}}
is the angle between the propagation and normal directions of the
(\ell-1)
-th layer, while
d^{\ell}_{s,\breve{s}}
is the geometric distance from the
\breve{s}
-th element of the
(\ell-1)
-th layer to the
s
-th element of the
\ell
-th layer, i.e.,

~d^{\ell}_{s,\breve{s}}=\sqrt{\Big(d_{\mathtt{PS}}\sqrt{(s_{z}-\breve{s}_{z})^{2}+(s_{y}-\breve{s}_{y})^{2}}\Big)^{2}+d_{\mathtt{SIM}}^{2}},

(3)

where
d_{\mathtt{PS}}
and
d_{\mathtt{SIM}}
are the spacings between two adjacent elements and metasurfaces, respectively; the indices of the elements
s_{z}
and
s_{y}
along the
z
- and
y
-axes can be computed as
s_{z}\triangleq\lceil s/\sqrt{S}\rceil
and
s_{y}=\mod(s-1,\sqrt{S})+1
, respectively.

We note that the propagation coefficient from the
n
-th transmit antenna to the
s
-th element of the first SIM layer
[{\textbf{H}}^{m,1}]_{s,n}
is formulated by (
2
). Subsequently, we adopt the Ricean channel model to characterize the instantaneous channel from the last SIM layer of AP
m
to receiver
k
as
{\textbf{z}}_{mk}\triangleq\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk}\big(\sqrt{\kappa}\bar{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}+\tilde{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}\big)
,
where
\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk}\triangleq\beta_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}/(1+\kappa)
, with
\beta_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}
representing the large-scale fading coefficient,
\kappa
is the Ricean factor,
\tilde{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}
is the NLoS component, whose
s
-th element is distributed as
[\tilde{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}]_{s}\sim\CN(0,1),\forall m\in\M\text{, }\forall k\in\K
. In addition,
\bar{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}
is the LoS component whose
s
-th element is given by

\displaystyle\big[\bar{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}\big]_{\!s}

\displaystyle\!=\!\exp\!\Big(\!\zeta s_{z}\sin{\big(\chi^{L}_{s,k}\big)}\!+\!\zeta s_{y}\sin\big(\varepsilon^{L}_{s,k}\big)\cos\big(\chi^{L}_{s,k}\big)\Big),

(4)

where
\zeta\triangleq j2\pi d_{\mathtt{PS}}/\lambda
,
\sin\big(\varepsilon^{L}_{s,k}\big)\cos\big(\chi^{L}_{s,k}\big)=(n_{z}\!-\!k_{z})/d^{L}_{n,k}
,
\sin{\big(\chi^{L}_{s,k}\big)}=(|n_{z}-k_{z}|)/\big(d^{L}_{n,k}\big)
,
where
d^{L}_{n,k}
is the geometric distance from receiver
k
to element
n
of the last SIM layer, while
\chi^{L}_{n,k}
and
\varepsilon^{L}_{n,k}
are the elevation and azimuth angle between the propagation direction and the
Oxy
and
Oyz
surfaces, respectively. To facilitate subsequent derivations, we present the channel statistics as
\mathbb{E}\{\|{\textbf{g}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}\|^{2}\}\!=\!\kappa\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk}\mathrm{tr}\big({\bf F}_{m}{\bf F}_{m}^{{\dagger}}\bar{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}^{{\dagger}}\bar{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}\big)\!\!+\!\!\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk}\mathrm{tr}\big({\bf F}_{m}{\bf F}_{m}^{{\dagger}}\big)
, where
{\textbf{g}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}\sim\CN\Big(\bar{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}},\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk}{\bf F}_{m}^{\dagger}{\bf F}_{m}\Big)
with
\bar{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}\triangleq\sqrt{\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk}\kappa}{\bf F}_{m}^{{\dagger}}\bar{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}
.

II-B

Uplink Training and Channel Estimation

In the training phase, all IRs and ERs transmit their pilot sequences of length
\tau
symbols to the APs.
We consider the general case where shared pilot sequences are used and denote
\mathcal{P}_{k}\subset\K
as the set of receivers
k^{\prime}
, including
k
, that are assigned with the same pilot sequence as the receiver
k
.
In this regard,
i_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}
denotes the index of the pilot sequence used by receiver
k
. The pilot sequences
\VARPHI_{i_{k}}\in\C^{\tau\times 1}
, with
\|\VARPHI_{i_{k}}\|^{2}=1
, are mutually orthogonal so that
\VARPHI_{i_{k^{\prime}}}^{H}\VARPHI_{i_{k}}=1\text{ if }i_{k^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
and
\VARPHI_{i_{k^{\prime}}}^{H}\VARPHI_{i_{k}}=0,\text{ otherwise}
.
Then, the AP
m
receives

~{\bf Y}_{p,m}^{\mathtt{i}}\!=\!\sqrt{\tau\rho_{u}}\big({\textbf{g}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}\VARPHI_{i_{k}}^{\dagger}\!+\!\sum\nolimits^{K}_{k^{\prime}=1\setminus k}\!{\textbf{g}}_{mk^{\prime}}\VARPHI_{i_{k^{\prime}}}^{\dagger}\big)\!+\!{\bf N}_{p,m},

(5)

where
\rho_{u}
is the normalized UL signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), while
{\bf N}_{p,m}\in\C^{N\times\tau}
is the receiver noise matrix containing independent
and identically distributed (i.i.d.)
\CN(0,\sigma_{n}^{2})
random variables (RVs). By invoking
[
10
]
,
{\textbf{g}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}
is estimated using the linear minimum mean-squared error (MMSE) technique as

\displaystyle\hat{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}

\displaystyle\!=\!\sqrt{\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk}\kappa}{\bf F}_{m}^{{\dagger}}\bar{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}+{\bf A}_{mk}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\times\Big(\!\sqrt{\tau\rho_{u}}\sum\nolimits_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\!\!{\sqrt{\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk^{\prime}}}{\bf F}_{m}^{{\dagger}}\tilde{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk^{\prime}}+\tilde{{\bf n}}_{p,mk}}\!\Big),

(6)

where
\tilde{{\bf n}}_{p,mk}\triangleq{\bf N}_{p,m}\VARPHI_{i_{k}}\sim\CN(\bm{0},\sigma_{n}^{2}{\textbf{I}}_{N})
, and

{\bf A}_{mk}\!\triangleq\!\!\big(\sqrt{\tau\rho_{u}}\!\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk}{\bf F}_{m}^{{\dagger}}{\bf F}_{m}\big)\big(\tau\rho_{u}\!\sum\nolimits_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}{\!\!\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk^{\prime}}}{\bf F}_{m}^{{\dagger}}{\bf F}_{m}\!+\!\sigma_{n}^{2}{\textbf{I}}_{N}\big)^{-1}.

Thus, the statistical distribution of
{\textbf{g}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}
is the following
\hat{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}\sim\CN\big(\bar{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}},\bm{\Sigma}_{\hat{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}}\big)
, where
\bm{\Sigma}_{\hat{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}}\triangleq\sqrt{\tau\rho_{u}}\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk}{\bf F}_{m}^{{\dagger}}{\bf F}_{m}{\bf A}_{mk}
. In addition, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\{\|\hat{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}\|^{2}\}\triangleq\kappa\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk}\mathrm{tr}({\bf F}_{m}{\bf F}_{m}^{\dagger}\bar{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}\bar{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}^{\dagger})+\gamma^{i}_{mk},

(7)

where

\gamma^{i}_{mk}=\frac{\tau\rho_{u}(\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk})^{2}\big(\mathrm{tr}({\bf F}_{m}{\bf F}_{m}^{{\dagger}})\big)^{2}}{\tau\rho_{u}\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}{\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk^{\prime}}}\mathrm{tr}({\bf F}_{m}{\bf F}_{m}^{{\dagger}})+N\sigma_{n}^{2}}.

(8)

The expectation of the norm square of the estimate error
\tilde{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}\triangleq{\textbf{g}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}-\hat{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}
is
\mathbb{E}\{\|\tilde{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}\|^{2}\}=\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk}\mathrm{tr}({\bf F}_{m}{\bf F}_{m}^{{\dagger}})-\gamma^{i}_{mk}
.

II-C

Downlink Wireless Information and Power Transmission

Define
\mathcal{M}{\mathtt{I}}\subset\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}{\mathtt{E}}\subset\mathcal{M}
as the sets of APs providing DL service for the IRs and ERs, respectively, with
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}{\mathtt{I}}\cup\mathcal{M}{\mathtt{E}}

k_{i}
(ER

\mathbb{E}\{|x_{\mathtt{I},k_{i}}|^{2}\}=\mathbb{E}\{|x_{\mathtt{E},k_{e}}|^{2}\}=1
. The IR and ER receive

\displaystyle y_{\mathtt{I},k_{i}}

\displaystyle\!=\!n_{d,k_{i}}\!\!+\!\!\sum\nolimits_{k^{\prime}_{i}\in\K_{\mathtt{I}}}\sum\nolimits_{m\in\M_{\mathtt{I}}}\sqrt{\rho_{d}\eta_{mk_{i}^{\prime}}^{\mathtt{I}}}({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}})^{\dagger}{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}^{\prime}}x_{\mathtt{I},k^{\prime}_{i}}

\displaystyle y_{\mathtt{E},k_{e}}

\displaystyle\!=\!n_{d,k_{e}}\!\!+\!\!\sum\nolimits_{k^{\prime}_{e}\in\K_{\mathtt{E}}}\!\sum\nolimits_{m\in\M_{\mathtt{E}}}\!\!\sqrt{\rho_{d}\eta_{mk_{e}^{\prime}}^{\mathtt{E}}}({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{e}}^{\mathtt{E}})^{\dagger}{\bf w}_{\mathtt{E},mk^{\prime}_{e}}x_{\mathtt{E},k^{\prime}_{e}}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt+\!\sum\nolimits_{k_{i}\in\K_{\mathtt{I}}}\!\sum\nolimits_{m\in\M_{\mathtt{I}}}\!\!\sqrt{\rho_{d}\eta_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}}}({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{e}}^{\mathtt{E}})^{\dagger}{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}}x_{\mathtt{I},k_{i}}\!,

(9b)

respectively, where

are the additive noise terms,
\rho_{d}=\tilde{\rho}_{d}/\sigma_{n}^{2}
is the normalized DL SNR;
{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}}\in\C^{N\times 1}
(

) represents the precoding vector for IR
k_{i}
(ER

) with

.

To this end, we apply the normalized maximum ratio transmission (MRT) precoder since power transfer with MRT is shown to be optimal for WPT systems, especially with a large number of antennas

11
]
. Mathematically speaking:
{\bf w}_{\mathtt{i},mk}^{\mathsf{MRT}}=\Big(\!\sqrt{\alpha^{\mathsf{MRT}}_{mk}}\Big)^{-1}\hat{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}
, where

\alpha^{\mathsf{MRT}}_{mk}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\{\|\hat{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}\|^{2}\}=\kappa\bar{\beta}^{\mathtt{i}}_{mk}\mathrm{tr}({\bf F}_{m}{\bf F}_{m}^{\dagger}\bar{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}\bar{{\textbf{z}}}_{mk}^{\dagger})+\gamma^{i}_{mk}.

Applying the use-and-then-forget technique
[
12
]
, the signal received at the
k_{i}
-th IR can be expressed as

\displaystyle~y_{\mathtt{I},k_{i}}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{DS}_{k_{i}}x_{\mathtt{I},k_{i}}+\mathsf{BU}_{k_{i}}x_{\mathtt{I},k_{i}}+\sum\nolimits_{k_{i}^{\prime}\in\K_{\mathtt{I}}\setminus k_{i}}\!\!\mathsf{IUI}_{k_{i}{k_{i}}^{\prime}}x_{\mathtt{I},k^{\prime}_{i}}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\sum\nolimits_{k_{e}\in\K_{\mathtt{E}}}\mathsf{EUI}_{k_{i}k_{e}}x_{\mathtt{E},k_{e}}+n_{k_{i}},~\forall k_{i}\in\K_{\mathtt{I}},

(10)

where
\mathsf{DS}_{k_{i}}
,
\mathsf{BU}_{k_{i}}
,

, and

-th IR, and the interference caused by the

-th ER, respectively, given by

\displaystyle~\mathsf{DS}_{k_{i}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum\nolimits_{m\in\M_{\mathtt{I}}}\sqrt{\rho_{d}\eta_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}}}\mathbb{E}\big\{({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}})^{\dagger}{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}}^{\mathsf{MRT}}\big\},

(11a)

\displaystyle\mathsf{BU}_{k_{i}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum\nolimits_{m\in\M_{\mathtt{I}}}\sqrt{\rho_{d}\eta_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}}}\Bigl(({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}})^{\dagger}{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}}^{\mathsf{MRT}}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt-\mathbb{E}\big\{({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}})^{\dagger}{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}}^{\mathsf{MRT}}\big\}\Bigl)~,

(11b)

\displaystyle\mathsf{IUI}_{k_{i}{k_{i}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum\nolimits_{m\in\M_{\mathtt{I}}}\sqrt{\rho_{d}\eta_{mk_{i}^{\prime}}^{\mathtt{I}}}({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}})^{\dagger}{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}^{\prime}}^{\mathsf{MRT}},~

\displaystyle~\mathrm{SE}_{k}

\displaystyle=\Big(1\!-\!\frac{\tau}{\tau_{c}}\Big)\log_{2}\Big(1\!+\mathrm{SINR}_{k_{i}}^{\mathsf{MRT}}\big(\bm{\Theta},\ETAI,\ETAE\big)\Big),

\Lambda(\mathcal{E}_{k_{e}})=\phi\big(1+\exp\big(\!-\xi\big(\mathcal{E}_{k_{e}}\!-\!\chi\big)\!\big)\big)^{-1}
.

k_{e}
as the performance metric of the WPT operation, which is

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}\big\{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{NL}}_{k_{e}}\big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\big\{\Lambda\big(\mathcal{E}_{k_{e}}\big)\big\}-\phi\Omega}{1-\Omega}\approx\frac{\Lambda\big(\mathcal{Q}_{k_{e}}\big)-\phi\Omega}{1-\Omega},

(14)

\mathbb{E}\left\{\Lambda\left(\mathrm{E}_{k_{e}}\right)\right\}\approx\Lambda\left(Q_{k_{e}}\big\}\right)

13
]

k_{e}

V
Conclusion

We analyzed the SE and average HE performance in a SIM-assisted CF-mMIMO SWIPT setup, where the APs are equipped with a SIM and simultaneously perform two distinct WIT and WPT operations. By leveraging closed-form expressions for the average sum-HE of ERs and the achievable SE of IRs, we formulated and solved a non-convex optimization problem to maximize sum-HE. Our simulation results showed an up to a
7
-fold gain in the sum-HE with the proposed layer-by-layer heuristic SIM’s PS design over the random PS design. Moreover, a
44\%
improvement in the achievable minimum SE can be achieved with the
PPA

: Given that
\hat{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}
and

are independent due to the properties of MMSE estimation, we obtain

\displaystyle~\mathsf{DS}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{m\in\M_{\mathtt{I}}}\sqrt{\rho_{d}\eta_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}}}\mathbb{E}\big\{(\hat{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}}+\tilde{{\textbf{g}}}_{mk}^{\mathtt{i}})^{H}{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}}^{\mathsf{MRT}}\big\}

(27)

2)
Compute
\mathsf{BU}_{k}
: Invoking that the variance of a sum of independent RVs is equal to the sum of the variances, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{m\in\M_{\mathtt{I}}}\!\rho_{d}\eta_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}}\!\mathbb{E}\Big\{\Big|({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}})^{{\dagger}}{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}}^{\mathsf{MRT}}\!-\!\mathbb{E}\big\{({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}})^{{\dagger}}{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}}^{\mathsf{MRT}}\big\}\Big|^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt=\!\!\!\sum\nolimits_{m\in\M_{\mathtt{I}}}\!\!\!\rho_{d}\eta_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}}\!\Big(\!\mathbb{E}\big\{\!\big|\!({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}})^{\!{\dagger}}\!{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}}^{\mathsf{MRT}}\big|^{2}\!\big\}\!-\!\!\Big|\mathbb{E}\big\{({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}})^{\!{\dagger}}\!{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}}^{\mathsf{MRT}}\big\}\!\Big|^{2}\!\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt=\!\!\!\sum\nolimits_{m\in\M_{\mathtt{I}}}\!\rho_{d}\eta_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big\{\!\big|\!({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}})^{{\dagger}}\!{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}}^{\mathsf{MRT}}\big|^{2}\big\}-\alpha^{\mathsf{MRT}}_{mk_{i}}\Big).

(28)

28

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}\big\{\big|({\textbf{g}}_{mk_{i}}^{\mathtt{I}})^{{\dagger}}{\bf w}_{\mathtt{I},mk_{i}}^{\mathsf{MRT}}\big|^{2}\big\}

where we have exploited Lemma

and the independence property between the estimation error and the precoding vector. Thus, by substituting (

in (

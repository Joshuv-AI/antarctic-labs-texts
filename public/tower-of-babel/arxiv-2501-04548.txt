Optimal Control of the Navier-Stokes equations via Pressure Boundary Conditions
Boris Vexler, Jakob Wagner

Abstract
. In this work we study an optimal control problem subject to the instationary Navier-Stokes equations, where the control enters via an inhomogeneous Neumann/Do-Nothing boundary condition. Despite the Navier-Stokes equations with these boundary conditions not being well-posed for large times and/or data, we obtain wellposedness of the optimal control problem by choosing a proper tracking type term. Moreover, we derive first and second order optimality conditions. In order to discuss the regularity of the optimal control, state and adjoint state, we present new results on L 2 ​ ( I , H 2 ​ ( Ω ) ) L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega)) regularity of solutions to a Stokes problem with mixed inhomogeneous boundary conditions.

1.
Introduction

In this work, we aim to consider an optimal control problem for the Navier-Stokes equations of the form

(
\mathcal{P}
)

\displaystyle\min_{(\mathbf{u},p,q)}

\displaystyle\qquad J(\mathbf{u},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{u}_{d}},p,q)

s.t.

\displaystyle\qquad\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\mathbf{u}-\Delta\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla p&=\mathbf{0}&&\text{in }I\times\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}&=0&&\text{in }I\times\Omega,\\
\mathbf{u}&=\mathbf{0}&&\text{on }I\times\Gamma_{D},\\
g(\mathbf{u},p)&=q_{i}(t)\mathbf{n}&&\text{on }I\times\Gamma_{N,i},\quad i=1,...,L,\\
\mathbf{u}(0)&=\mathbf{u}_{0}&&\text{in }\Omega.\end{aligned}

Our focus lies on finding formulations of the objective function
J
and
boundary conditions
g
such that this becomes a well defined and meaningful problem.
In the argument of
J
, the term
\mathbf{u}_{d}
denotes a target velocity field, obtained, e.g.,
from measurements.
In the above formulation, we consider
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
some given domain, where its boundary

\partial\Omega=:\Gamma=\Gamma_{D}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{L}\Gamma_{N,i}
is partitioned into a wall part of the boundary

\Gamma_{D}
, where we prescribe no-slip (Dirichlet) boundary conditions,
and
L

disjoint boundary segments
\Gamma_{N,i}
,
on which
we prescribe some boundary conditions

involving
the velocity
\mathbf{u}
and the pressure
p
,
and the outer normal vector
\mathbf{n}
.
As fluid may enter or leave
\Omega
through the boundary segments
\Gamma_{N,i}
, they are often
referred to as
open boundaries
.

The equations are considered on the time interval
I
and the initial condition

\mathbf{u}_{0}
is supposed to be given.

We
wish to
control the Navier-Stokes equations by the right hand sides
q_{i}
of these
boundary conditions.
It is quite common, that the boundary segments
\Gamma_{N,i}
stem from truncating a larger
domain, in order to reduce the total number of degrees of freedom of a full model, and
to facilitate numerical computations.
One important application, in which such domains arise, are cardiovascular CFD simulations,
where
\Omega
contains one or more bifurcations of a bloodvessel.
These simulated data can be used by clinicians to diagnose the severity of
cardiovascular diseases, which makes this field of research highly impactful. We refer the reader to
the survey
[
70
]
, where a vast collection of relevant problems and solution
approaches in this context is presented. In order to obtain CFD data of the bloodflow in a truncated domain,
proper values for the boundary conditions at the open boundaries have to
be specified. As these are generally unknown, optimal control problems like (

P

)
facilitate detailled CFD simulations, provided that a patient specific scan of the bloodvessel
geometry
\Omega
and space time velocity data
\mathbf{u}_{d}
are available.
Both can be obtained non-invasively by the use of 4D MRI imaging,
see, e.g.,
[
81
]
for an introduction to this method.
There, the authors give references on how other flow quantities like pressure and wall sheer stress
can be indirectly deduced from the measured data.
Solving an optimal control formulation like (

P

) makes all these quantities available
immediately without further need of postprocessing the MRI data.
Such time dependent boundary control problems are among the
methods
presented
in
[
70
]

and in Section 6.5 therein, the authors remark, that a wellposedness analysis of such formulations
is an open question. The techniques and results presented in this work contribute
towards answering this question.

Throughout this work, we shall assume, that the controls
q_{i}
are
only time dependent and constant in space.
This structure is partially motivated by the Poiseuille flow in a rectangular channel,
where the inhomogeneous Do-Nothing boundary data are indeed constant in space.
At the same time, we will see, that this allows us to circumvent some tedious technicalities
in the analysis of the problem under consideration, while still keeping the main difficulties
to solve. Lastly, from a numerical point of view, the pure time dependence greatly reduces the
number of degrees of freedom of the discretized control. Let us also specifically mention

[
70
, Section 2]
and
[
71
]
, where
the time dependent Navier-Stokes equations are bi-directionally coupled to an ODE model on each
open boundary segment. These ODE models, also called Windkessel models, describe the lumped
impact, e.g., resistance and capacity, of the truncated smaller bloodvessels, on the main flow
in
\Omega
. As each ODE component only carries time-dependent information, it is necessary to
prescribe a spatial profile, e.g., constant, to couple the models. In turn, only the parameters
of the Windkessel models have to be optimized, even further reducing the number of degrees of
freedom. We hope that the analysis of (

P

) with purely time dependent controls,
may in the future contribute towards the analysis of optimal control problems subject to such
coupled systems.

Not only the question of specifying the correct data at the open boundaries, but also the choice
of boundary condition itself is a very active reasearch topic,
and a large number of possible boundary conditions is being discussed.
At the present point in time, the following conditions seem to be the most used in the literature

(DN)

Do-Nothing Boundary Condition:

\displaystyle p\mathbf{n}-\nabla\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=q_{i}\mathbf{n}

(TP)

Total Pressure Boundary Condition:

\displaystyle p\mathbf{n}-\nabla\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=q_{i}\mathbf{n}

(AD)

Advective Boundary Condition:

\displaystyle p\mathbf{n}-\nabla\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n})\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=q_{i}\mathbf{n}

(DDN)

Directional Do-Nothing Boundary Condition:

\displaystyle p\mathbf{n}-\nabla\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\frac{1}{2}[\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}]^{-}\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=q_{i}\mathbf{n}

where in the last condition,
[\,\cdot\,]^{-}=\min\{\cdot,0\}
denotes the negative part.

The (
DN
) and (
TP
) conditions have been discussed in
[
42
]
,
the (
DDN
) conditions were
introduced in
[
15
]
, and for the (
AD
) boundary conditions,
we refer the reader to

[
79
]
.
It is well known, that while the instationary Stokes equations with the (
DN
)
boundary condition are well posed,
the same is not true anymore for the Navier-Stokes equations. Only for short times or small data, the existence
of a weak solution is known. The reason for this is the fact, that the term
\left(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}\right)_{\Omega}

in the tested weak formulation collapses to a boundary integral term, which a priori cannot be bounded.
Thus, it is possible that an infinite amount of inflow through
\Gamma_{N}
occurs, making the total energy
of the system unbounded.
The modifications present in the (
TP
), (
AD
) and (
DDN
) boundary conditions aim to cancel out this boundary term,
in order to retrieve existence of weak solutions globally in time for data of any size.
In the literature, there are also other strategies to overcome the difficulty in applying the (
DN
) boundary conditions.
In
[
53
]
the authors search for solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations with
(
DN
) boundary conditions in a subset of the solution space, such that the inflow remains bounded.
This allows them to consider the equations in form of a variational inequality.
In
[
69
]
the authors explore regularized (
DDN
) conditions for the stationary
Navier-Stokes equations.
While none of the above boundary conditions carry a physical meaning at parts of the boundary, that are
introduced by truncating a larger domain, the (
DN
) boundary conditions are at least valid
for Poiseuille flow in a rectangular channel.
Moreover, while the additional terms in the (
TP
), (
AD
) or (
DDN
) boundary condition allow for a canceling of terms
in the state equation, the same is not true anymore in the adjoint equation that arises as part of the
discussion of the optimal control problem.
Lastly, choosing the same type of boundary condition at all
open boundaries
eliminates the need
to distinguish inflow and outflow boundaries a priori, as all parts of the boundary in question
are treated the same.
Thus, our aim is to work with the (
DN
) boundary conditions and to devise a strategy to overcome the
difficulties associated with that choice mentioned above.
Since the Navier-Stokes equations are embedded into the optimal control problem, we do not
require solvability of the equations for arbitrary data, but get existence of solutions along
a minimizing sequence.
Additionally, we have the added freedom of choosing the norm in the tracking type term in the cost functional,
and thus the a priori known
smoothness of the solutions in the minimizing sequence.
This strategy of choosing a stronger norm in the tracking term was recently employed in

[
21
]
, in order to tackle a distributed control
problem of the 3D Navier-Stokes equations,
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
.
There the stronger norm guaranteed uniqueness
of solutions, whereas in our case, we will use it to obtain existence of solutions.
The origin of such approaches can be traced back to as early as
[
39
]
,
where a Dirichlet control problem of the stationary Navier-Stokes was considered.

While there is a lot of literature on distributed control
(e.g.,
[
20
,
22
]
)
or boundary control (e.g.,
[
45
]
) of the Navier-Stokes equations with pure
Dirichlet boundary conditions, much less is available for mixed boundary conditions.

The authors of
[
37
,
83
,
77
]

use the standard
L^{2}
tracking norm in a Dirichlet control problem for the stationary Navier-Stokes
equations, but additionally admit a homogeneous (
DN
) condition on a subset of the boundary.
This forces them to impose a constraint on the size of the controls,
in order to get solvability of the state equation.
In
[
38
,
36
]
the authors have extended these approaches to the
instationary Navier-Stokes equations.

In the very recent work
[
72
]
, the authors study optimal control problems
which are very similar to ours, but consider the 3D instationary case and admit fully space-time dependent
controls on the right hand side of the (
DN
) conditions. While they do not present theoretical
results concerning the optimal control problems, they implement model order reduction techniques
to overcome the large computational effort necessary to solve such problems.

The mixed boundary conditions in the state equation of (

P

) require careful analysis
of the available regularity of the primal and adjoint states.

The work
[
74
]
highlights, that even for pure Dirichlet conditions,
the adjoint equation needs special attention.

It is known that the available regularity on piecewise smooth domains depends crucially on the angle
between boundary segments. Moreover, in certain special cases additional compatibility conditions
between the Neumann and Dirichlet data need to be imposed.
While for the Poisson problem results for mixed inhomogeneous boundary conditions and a piecewise smooth
boundary can be found in
[
5

, for the parabolic case there seems to be
much less known.
In
[

In

This line of research is carried on in

The authors of

Section

Following up, in

We carefully examine the existence

Our main result is

A
.
With these spaces, we can introduce the mixed boundary condition Stokes operator by

A\colon\mathcal{D}(A)\to\mathbf{H}

(8)

\left(A\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}.

Its domain is given by
\mathcal{D}(A)=\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{V}:\exists\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{H}\text{ s.t. }A\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{f}\}
.
We introduce the following two spaces of space and time dependent functions,

which we will repeatedly use in our regularity discussions.

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}

\displaystyle:=L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})\cap H^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})

\displaystyle\hskip-102.43008pt\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};\mathbf{H}),

(10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}

\displaystyle:=L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega)^{2}\cap\mathbf{V})\cap H^{1}(I;\mathbf{H})

\displaystyle\hskip-102.43008pt\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};\mathbf{V}).

From
[
78
, Corollary 8]
we obtain the following compact imbedding:

(11)

\mathbb{W}\overset{c}{\hookrightarrow}L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{W}\overset{c}{\hookrightarrow}C(\bar{I};\mathbf{H}).

The main difficulty in discussing the Navier-Stokes equations with (
DN
) boundary conditions,
is the fact, that boundary terms arise when testing the equations with its solution.
These do not vanish, contrary to the pure Dirichlet case.
In order to discuss them in the following, we shall recall some important trace results.
For a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega

and

1<p<\infty
it holds,
see
[
63
, Thm. 3]
:
There exists a linear, bounded trace operator

(12)

\tr\colon W^{s,p}(\Omega)\to W^{s-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\Omega),\qquad\text{whenever }\frac{1}{p}<s<1+\frac{1}{p}.

The norm of fractional Sobolev spaces can be estimated according to the following theorem,
compare
[
17
, Theorem 1]
:

Theorem 2.3

(Gagliardo Nirenberg)
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain and let
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}s_{2}\geq s_{1}\geq 0}
,
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
and
\theta\in(0,1)
.
Define
s=\theta s_{1}+(1-\theta)s_{2}
and
1/p=\theta/p_{1}+(1-\theta)/p_{2}
. If the
condition

s_{2}\text{ is integer }\geq 1,p_{2}=1\text{ and }s_{2}-s_{1}\leq 1-\frac{1}{p_{1}}

is violated, then the following inequality holds

\|v\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\|v\|_{W^{s_{2},p_{2}}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}\quad\text{for all }v\in W^{s_{1},p_{1}}(\Omega)\cap W^{s_{2},p_{2}}(\Omega).

As the boundary terms arising in our discussion of the problem do not involve any derivatives,
we are interested in precise estimates for
L^{p}
norms on the boundary.

From
[
16
, Thm. 1.6.6]
there holds for
1\leq p\leq\infty

(13)

\|v\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\|v\|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all }v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

The result there is stated without proof, a proof can be found, e.g., in
[
34
]
.
Intuitively, the scaling in this theorem means, that if
v
possesses increasing regularity in
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
less additional regularity of its first derivatives is needed, in order to properly define a trace.
If
v
does not possess any
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
regularity for
p>2
, but is only in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, this tradeoff
can be switched in the sense that a higher contribution of the
H^{1}(\Omega)
norm allows for a bound of
the trace in
L^{p}
for larger values of
p
. There holds the following result, in which we want to emphasize
the switch in the exponents compared to (
13
).

Theorem 2.4

(Trace
L^{p}
estimate)
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
and let
2\leq p<\infty
.
Then there is a constant
C{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=C(p)}
, such that

\|v\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|v\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all }v\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Proof.

Note, that the case
p=2
is a special case of (
13
), which is why
we only focus on the case
p>2
.
Since
p>2
, it holds
1/p<1/2
, which allows us to define
s:=1-1/p
and we have
1/2<s<1
.
By the Sobolev imbedding theorem, since the boundary
\partial\Omega

of the domain is one-dimensional, there holds the embedding

H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\partial\Omega)
, for

q\leq\frac{1\cdot 2}{1-(s-1/2)\cdot 2}=\frac{2}{2-2s}=\frac{2}{2-2+2/p}=p
.
Thus, together with (
12
) and
Theorem

2.3
there holds

\|v\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{s}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1-s}=C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

∎

Let us lastly summarize some identities and estimates concerning the nonlinear term in the
Navier-Stokes equations.
It is straightforward to verify with integration by parts, that for
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{V}
, it holds

\left((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\right)_{\Omega}=-\left((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n},\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{v}\right)_{\partial\Omega}.

Choosing
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{v}
and using the homogeneous Dirichlet data on
\Gamma_{D}
, there thus holds

(14)

\left((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n},|\mathbf{v}|^{2}\right)_{\partial\Omega}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n},|\mathbf{v}|^{2}\right)_{\Gamma_{N}}.

From
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
, we obtain that for any
2\leq p<\infty
, there exists
C={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C(p)}>0
,
such that

(15)

\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{p}}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

for all
\varphi\in\{\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega):\psi|_{\Gamma_{D}}=0\}
.
We shall mostly use this estimate for
p=4
, as together with Hölder’s inequality, it allows us to estimate

(16)

\left((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\right)_{\Omega}\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, for
p=4
this estimate yields the imbeddings

(17)

\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow L^{4}(I\times\Omega)^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{W}\hookrightarrow W^{1,4}(I;L^{4}(\Omega)^{2}).

3.
Discussion of State Equation

With the auxiliary results of the previous section established, we can now turn towards the discussion of
the state equation.
In its general form for initial data
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}
, right hand side
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})
and
boundary data
q_{i}\in L^{2}(I)
,
i=1,...,L
, it has the form

(18)

\left\{\begin{aligned} {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left\langle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{\Omega}}+\left(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}&=\left(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}-\sum_{i=1}^{L}q_{i}(t)\left(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}\right)_{\Gamma_{N,i}}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V},\text{ a.e. }\ t\in I,\\
\mathbf{u}(0)&=\mathbf{u}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

To analyze this problem, let us recall some results on related and auxiliary problems.
We first discuss the stationary Stokes equations with mixed homogeneous boundary data, i.e.,
for
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
we search
(\mathbf{w},p_{\mathbf{w}})\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{2}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

(19)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta\mathbf{w}+\nabla p_{\mathbf{w}}&=\mathbf{f}&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}&=0&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\mathbf{w}&=\mathbf{0}&&\text{on }\Gamma_{D},\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{w}-p_{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{n}&=\mathbf{0}&&\text{on }\Gamma_{N}.\end{aligned}\right.

Its weak formulation in terms of divergence free spaces reads: Find
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{V}
such that

\left(\nabla\mathbf{w},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}.

Solvability of this problem in terms of
\mathbf{w}
can be argued by the Lax-Milgram theorem, while the existence
of a unique associated pressure
p_{\mathbf{w}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
can be argued as in

[
64
, Lemma 5.1, Theorem 5.2]
.

The argument presented there for the 3D case, directly translates to 2D.
Note that due to the presence of Do-Nothing boundary conditions, the pressure is unique in the whole

L^{2}(\Omega)
and not only up to constants, as it would be the case for pure Dirichlet conditions.

Moreover, due to
Assumption

2.1
, there holds the following
H^{2}
regularity result,
see
[
10
, Theorem A.1]
:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\mathbf{w},p_{\mathbf{w}})\in\mathbf{V}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
be weak solutions to (
19
)
for
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
. Then
(\mathbf{w},p_{\mathbf{w}})\in[\mathbf{V}\cap H^{2}(\Omega)^{2}]\times H^{1}(\Omega)
and there holds the
bound

\|\mathbf{w}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|p_{\mathbf{w}}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

A similar result also holds for the instationary Stokes equations with mixed homogeneous boundary data

(20)

\left\{\begin{aligned} {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left\langle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{\Omega}}+\left(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}&=\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V},\text{ a.e. }t\in I,\\
\mathbf{u}(0)&=\mathbf{u}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

For this, we have the following, see
[
10
, Theorem 3.4]
,
also
[
30
, Proposition 72.2]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{H}
.
Then

(
20
) possesses a unique solution
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{V}
with a bound

\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})}\leq C{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})}\right)}.

Further, if
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}
, then

\mathbf{u}\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{W}}

and there exists a unique pressure
p_{\mathbf{u}}\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))
, such that

\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}-\Delta\mathbf{u}+\nabla{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}p_{\mathbf{u}}}=\mathbf{f}

holds in the
L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})
sense, and there holds a bound

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}p_{\mathbf{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C(\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}).

Here, the statements for
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}p_{\mathbf{u}}}
were obtained by bootstrapping and applying the ones
of
Theorem

3.1
for almost every
t\in I
.
With this, we can consider the instationary Navier-Stokes equations,
first with homogeneous (
DN
) boundary conditions.
We consider the problem: Find
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{V}
such that

(21)

\left\{\begin{aligned} {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left\langle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{\Omega}}+\left(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}&=\left(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V},\text{ a.e. }t\in I,\\
\mathbf{u}(0)&=\mathbf{u}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Due to the choice of boundary conditions, existence of weak solutions to the
Navier-Stokes problem is not a priori given.
However, if a solution exists,
we are able to show uniqueness and, if permitted by the data, improved regularity. Let us first discuss regularity and defer the discussion of uniqueness to
Theorem

3.8
. If
the data are sufficiently regular, the solution has the
same regularity as the one for the corresponding Stokes problem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{u}\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{V}}
be a weak solution to (
21
) for
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})
and

\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}
. Then
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{W}
and there holds a bound

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{H})})\cdot(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{V}}).

Moreover, there exists a unique pressure
p_{\mathbf{u}}\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))
such that

\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}-\Delta\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla p_{\mathbf{u}}=\mathbf{f}

holds in the
L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})
sense, together with a bound

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|p_{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\right).}

Proof.

The result for
\mathbf{u}
can be shown following precisely the same steps as

[
82
, Ch.3, Theorem 3.10]
, using the
H^{2}
-regularity result of

Theorem

3.1
.

The regularity of
p_{\mathbf{u}}
can be obtained by a bootstrapping argument similar to the one
in
Proposition

3.2
, where the term
\|(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}
can be estimated using
[
86
, Theorem 3.8]
.

∎

Note that the possible non-existence of solutions is due to the fact, that we do not have a bound
of
\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})}
and
\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{H})}
purely depending on the data.
As these terms arise on the right hand side of the estimate shown in
Theorem

3.3
,
in order to apply this result
to obtain boundedness of a minimizing sequence,

we need not only assume
the existence of solutions, but also provide a bound for the norms in question.

While existence of weak solutions is in general only shown for small times and/or data,

[
10
, Theorem 4.1]
show that if a solution exists for some data,
then for small perturbations of the data, the equations remain solvable.
Note that, while the authors of
[
10
]
assume the existence
of a solution in
\mathbb{W}
,

Theorem

3.3
shows that it suffices to
assume existence of a weak solution in
\mathbb{V}
,
as the
H^{2}
regularity can then be deduced from the data.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}
such that a weak solution

\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{V}
to (
21
)
exists. Then there exist
\varepsilon,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\delta}>0
, such that for all

\tilde{\mathbf{f}}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})
and
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}
, satisfying

\|\tilde{\mathbf{f}}-\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{0}-\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\varepsilon,

there exists a solution
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{W}

to (
21
) with data
\tilde{\mathbf{f}}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{0}
,
satisfying

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|\tilde{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\delta.}

Proof.

The result for
\mathbf{f},\tilde{\mathbf{f}}\in L^{2}(I;\mathbf{H})
is presented in
[
10
, Theorem 4.1]
.
With the characterization of
L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}=\mathbf{H}\oplus\mathbf{H}^{\perp}
presented in
(
7
),
and the orthogonal projection
\mathbb{P}\colon L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}\to\mathbf{H}
, we further know that
a right hand side
\mathbb{P}\mathbf{f}
yields the same solution
\mathbf{u}
as
\mathbf{f}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

This shows, that the set of data, for which
a solution in
\mathbb{W}
exists,
is an open subset of
L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})\times\mathbf{V}
.
Note however, that again the above result does not contain a bound for
\|\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})}
and

\|\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H^{1}(I;\mathbf{H})}
in terms of norms of the data.

Up to now, we have considered only problems with homogeneous boundary data.
To conclude this section, we show that all results derived previously, can be also extended to the
type of inhomogeneous boundary data under consideration in this work.
We achieve this, by transforming (
18
) into a formulation where
no boundary data terms arise.
Here we will
use the fact,
that the controls of (

P

) are purely time dependent.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}
,
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})
and
q_{i}\in L^{2}(I)
,
i=1,...,L
. Then
there exist functions
\zeta_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

i=1,...,L
, such that
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{W}
solves
(
18
) if and only if
\mathbf{u}
solves the following

(22)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\partial_{t}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}&=\left(\mathbf{f}-\sum_{i=1}^{L}q_{i}\nabla\zeta_{i},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V},\text{ a.e. }\ t\in I,\\
\mathbf{u}(0)&=\mathbf{u}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

Due to
Assumption

2.1
, for every
i=1,...,L
, there exist two nested neighborhoods,

\mathcal{U}_{i},\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{i}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}}
such that

\Gamma_{N,i}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{i}\subset\overline{\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}}_{i}\subset\mathcal{U}_{i}

and

\Gamma_{N}\cap\mathcal{U}_{i}=\Gamma_{N,i}
. Define smooth functions
\chi_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}[0,1]}
such that

\chi_{i}(\mathbf{x})=0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\mathcal{U}_{i}
and

\chi_{i}(\mathbf{x})=1
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{i}
.
Note that this implies
\nabla\chi_{i}=0
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{i}
.
Denote the constant normal vector of
\Gamma_{N,i}
by
\mathbf{n}_{i}
and let
\beta_{i}:=\mathbf{n}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{0,i}
for
some arbitrary
\mathbf{x}_{0,i}\in\Gamma_{N,i}
.

Then
\mathbf{n}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{x}=\beta_{i}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\Gamma_{N,i}
.

Consider the function

{\zeta}_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R},\ {\zeta}_{i}(\mathbf{x}):=\chi_{i}(\mathbf{x})\cdot((\mathbf{n}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{x})-\beta_{i}+1).

For all
j=1,...,L
and
\mathbf{x}\in\Gamma_{N,j}
, it holds

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\zeta_{i}(\mathbf{x})|_{\Gamma_{N,j}}}=\delta_{i,j}
and

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nabla\zeta_{i}(\mathbf{x})|_{\Gamma_{N,j}}}=\delta_{i,j}\mathbf{n}_{i}
,
where by
\delta_{i,j}
we denote the Kronecker delta.
With this, using
\mathbf{v}|_{\Gamma_{D}}=\mathbf{0}
for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}
, we can write

\left(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}\right)_{\Gamma_{N,i}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=(\mathbf{v},\zeta_{i}\mathbf{n})_{\Gamma_{N}}}=(\mathbf{v},\zeta_{i}\mathbf{n})_{\partial\Omega}=\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v},\zeta_{i}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\mathbf{v},\nabla\zeta_{i}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\mathbf{v},\nabla\zeta_{i}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Note that, despite the right hand side terms
q_{i}\nabla\zeta_{i}
being gradients of smooth functions,
as
\zeta_{i}|_{\Gamma_{N,i}}=1
, it holds
q_{i}\nabla\zeta_{i}\not\in\mathbf{H}^{\perp}
, due to
the characterization of the latter space derived in (
7
).

Remark 3.7
.

While

3.5
shows, that the two formulations
(

) and (

) yield the same velocity solution
\mathbf{u}
, they lead to

Testing (

) especially with any

,

\mathring{\mathbf{V}}

),

As functions from
\mathring{\mathbf{V}}
vanish on the whole
\partial\Omega
, by

[

, Chapter I, Proposition 1.2]
,
there exists
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{u}}}\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))
such that

\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}-\Delta\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u})+\nabla{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{u}}}=\mathbf{f}-\sum_{i=1}^{L}q_{i}\nabla\zeta_{i},

.
Subtracting (

) from this equation tested with any

gives for a.e.
t\in I

0=\left(-\Delta\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{u}}},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{u}}}\mathbf{n}-\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\partial\Omega}-\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{u}}},\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{u}}}\mathbf{n}-\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Gamma_{N}}.

Following the same approach, we obtain from (
18
), that
there exists
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}p_{\mathbf{u}}}\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))
such that

\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}-\Delta\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u})+\nabla{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}p_{\mathbf{u}}}=\mathbf{f}.

Subtracting (
18
) from this equation tested with
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}
yields for a.e.
t\in I

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{L}q_{i}\mathbf{n},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Gamma_{N}}=\left(-\Delta\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}p_{\mathbf{u}}},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}p_{\mathbf{u}}}\mathbf{n}-\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\partial\Omega}-\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}p_{\mathbf{u}}},\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}p_{\mathbf{u}}}\mathbf{n}-\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Gamma_{N}}.

As the solution
\mathbf{u}
is the same for both formulations, this shows that only the associated pressures
differ by
\sum_{i=1}^{L}q_{i}\zeta_{i}
.
In fact, equation (
22
) is the weak formulation to

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\mathbf{u}-\Delta\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{u}}}&=\mathbf{f}&&\text{in }I\times\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}&=0&&\text{in }I\times\Omega,\\
\mathbf{u}(0)&=\mathbf{u}_{0}&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\mathbf{u}&=\mathbf{0}&&\text{on }I\times\Gamma_{D},\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{u}}}\mathbf{n}-\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{u}&=\mathbf{0}&&\text{on }I\times\Gamma_{N},\end{aligned}\right.

where we have defined the modified pressure
\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{u}}=p_{\mathbf{u}}-\sum_{i=1}^{L}q_{i}\zeta_{i}
.

As
\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{u}}
is the associated pressure to (
22
), which is formulated
with homogeneous boundary conditions, the result of
Theorem

3.3
is applicable, and it holds

\|\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})}+\|q\|_{L^{2}(I)}\right).

Due to the relationship between
p_{\mathbf{u}}
and
\tilde{p}_{\mathbf{u}}
, a similar bound also holds for
p_{\mathbf{u}}
.
Note that this estimate cannot be stated purely in terms of data, as the norms of
\mathbf{u}
cannot a priori be
bounded in terms of the data
q,\mathbf{f}
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}
.

Concluding the considerations on existence
and regularity
of solutions, let us now turn toward the question of uniqueness.
We shall see that this question is much easier to discuss, as there holds the following.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{V}
solve (
18
)
for given
q\in L^{2}(I)^{L},\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{H}
.

Then
\mathbf{u}
is the unique solution of (
18
) in the space
\mathbb{V}
.

Proof.

Assume there are
\mathbf{u}_{1}=\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{u}_{2}
two solutions in
\mathbb{V}

to (
18
).
For almost every
t\in I
,
we then test both equations with
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{u}_{1}-\mathbf{u}_{2}
and arrive by
standard
calculations at

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left((\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{1}-(\mathbf{u}_{2}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{e}\right)_{\Omega}=0.

By inserting an artificial zero and using (
14
),
the trilinear term can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left((\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{1}-(\mathbf{u}_{2}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{e}\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left((\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{1}-(\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{2}+(\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{2}-(\mathbf{u}_{2}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{e}\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left((\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{e},\mathbf{e}\right)_{\Omega}+\left((\mathbf{e}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{e}\right)_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{n},|\mathbf{e}|^{2})_{\partial\Omega}+\left((\mathbf{e}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{e}\right)_{\Omega}.

Using (
16
) to estimate the domain integral term,
and the trace
L^{p}
estimate from
Theorem

2.4
, together with
the extended Poincaré-Steklov inequality, see
[
29
, Lemma 3.30]
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\left((\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{1}-(\mathbf{u}_{2}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{e}\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathbf{u}_{1}\|_{L^{3}(\partial\Omega)}\|\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{3}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathbf{u}_{1}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\mathbf{u}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\left(\|\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}_{1}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

In the last step, we have used Young’s inequality twice,
with
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=1
and
\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3}=1
.
Absorbing the term
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
to the left,
yields that for almost every
t\in I
, there holds

\partial_{t}\|\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\left(\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}_{1}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\|\mathbf{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

As
\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2}\in\mathbb{V}
, the term

\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}_{1}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

is integrable in time.

An application of Gronwall’s Lemma,
together with the fact, that
\mathbf{e}(0)=\mathbf{0}
,

then yields
\mathbf{e}\equiv{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{0}}
.
∎

As already discussed, the possible non existence of solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations
is due to the fact, that the
nonlinear
term

\left((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}\right)_{\Omega}
may be unbounded for the unknown quantity
\mathbf{u}
.

In
Proposition

3.2
, we observed that this difficulty is not present in the
linear Stokes equations.
Moreover, in
Theorem

3.3
we have seen that for the Navier-Stokes equations we can obtain improved
regularity, assuming that we already have some information on the solution.
These considerations motivate the definition of the following linearized problem,
where we assume some given regularity of the linearization point.

For given
\mathbf{u}\in L^{4}(I;L^{4}(\Omega)^{2})
,
\mathbf{w}_{0}\in\mathbf{H}
and
q_{i}\in L^{2}(I)
,
i=1,...,L
,
we search for

\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{V}
solving

(23)

\left\{\begin{aligned} {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left\langle\partial_{t}\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{\Omega}}+\left(\nabla\mathbf{w},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}&=-\sum_{i=1}^{L}q_{i}\left(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}\right)_{\Gamma_{N,i}}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V},\text{ a.e. }t\in I,\\
\mathbf{w}(0)&=\mathbf{w}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathbf{u}\in L^{4}(I;L^{4}(\Omega)^{2})
,
\mathbf{w}_{0}\in\mathbf{H}
and
q_{i}\in L^{2}(I)
,
i=1,...,L
.
Then problem (
23
)
possesses a unique solution
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{V}

with an estimate

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})}+}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})}+\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{H})}\leq C(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}})\left[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|q\|_{L^{2}(I)}\right],

where the constant
C
depends nonlinearly on
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}}
.

Proof.

The proof uses standard arguments based on the Gronwall Lemma.
For the solution
\mathbf{w}
, one has to bound the term

\displaystyle((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

where we have used (
15
) and Young’s inequality.
By assumption
\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}
is integrable in time.
The bound then follows by a standard Gronwall argument. Existence is obtained by a Galerkin procedure.
Uniqueness is obtained from the stability estimate by replacing
\mathbf{w}
with
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{2}
,
the difference between two solutions.

∎

4.
Optimal Control Problems

With the considerations from the previous sections, we can now formulate our optimal control problem.
To overcome the problem of
non-existence
of solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations subject to the
(
DN
) boundary conditions for general data, we restrict the admissible set of controls.
We
will
search only in the set of controls that lead to a weak solution of the Navier-Stokes equations,
which by
Proposition

3.4
is an open set
of the space of controls.
In the following, we denote the control space by
Q:=L^{2}(I)^{L}
.

While this remedies the
problem of solvability of the state equation, there remains
the lack of an energy estimate of the state in terms of the controls.
For the optimal control problem, this means that for a minimizing sequence
(q_{n},\mathbf{u}_{n})

with
q_{n}
bounded in
Q
, the sequence of states
\mathbf{u}_{n}
could be unbounded in
\mathbb{V}
.
However, as we are able to obtain boundedness of
\mathbf{u}_{n}
in the norm of the tracking type term,
and we have seen in
Theorem

3.9
, that a linearized equation is stable if the
linearization point is in
L^{4}(I\times\Omega)^{2}
, this lead us to the choice of a
non-standard tracking functional in our objective function, using the
L^{4}(I\times\Omega)^{2}
norm,
instead of the
L^{2}(I\times\Omega)^{2}
norm.

All in all, we consider the problem

(
P
)

\displaystyle\min_{q,\mathbf{u}}\quad

\displaystyle J({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}q,\mathbf{u}}):=\frac{1}{4}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{d}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}^{4}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|q\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}

s.t.

\displaystyle q\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}Q},\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{V},

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left\langle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{\Omega}}+\left(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}&=-\sum_{i=1}^{L}q_{i}\left(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Gamma_{N,i}}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V},\text{ a.e. }t\in I,\\
\mathbf{u}(0)&=\mathbf{u}_{0},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}q_{a,i}\leq q_{i}\leq q_{b,i}\qquad\text{a.e. in }I,\qquad i=1,...,L.}

where
\mathbf{u}_{d}\in L^{4}(I\times\Omega)^{2}
is some desired data,

q_{a},q_{b}\in[\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}]^{L}

represent the control constraints

with
q_{a}<q_{b}
componentwise,
\alpha>0
is a regularization parameter,

and
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{H}
are fixed initial data.

Using the construction presented in
Lemma

3.5
,
we obtain an equivalent optimization problem

(
\widehat{P}
)

\displaystyle\min_{q,\mathbf{u}}\quad

\displaystyle J({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}q,\mathbf{u}}):=\frac{1}{4}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{d}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}^{4}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|q\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}

(24)

s.t.

\displaystyle q\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}Q},\ \mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{V},

(25)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left\langle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{\Omega}}+\left(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}&=-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{L}q_{i}\nabla\zeta_{i},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V},\text{ a.e. }t\in I,\\
\mathbf{u}(0)&=\mathbf{u}_{0},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(26)

\displaystyle q_{a,i}\leq q_{i}\leq q_{b,i}\qquad\text{a.e. in }I,\qquad i=1,...,L.

4.1.
Wellposedness

We first show, that this is a wellposed problem.
To deduce convergence of minimizing sequences, we need to recall the following,
see
[
82
, Ch. 3, Theorem 2.3]

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X_{0}\overset{c}{\hookrightarrow}X\hookrightarrow X_{1}
be three Hilbert spaces with
X_{0},\ X_{1}
being
reflexive, then the following embedding is compact:

\left[L^{2}(I;X_{0})\cap W^{1,1}(I;X_{1})\right]\overset{c}{\hookrightarrow}L^{2}(I;X).

In order to ensure, that the feasible set of the above problem is nonempty, we shall work under the
following assumption

Assumption 4.2
.

There exists
\accentset{\circ}{q}\in Q
satisfying
q_{a,i}\leq\accentset{\circ}{q}\leq q_{b,i}
a.e. in
I
,

i=1,...,L
, for which (
25
) possesses a solution
\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{V}
.

Remark 4.3
.

If
it holds
\mathbf{u}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
, and

q_{a},q_{b}
admit the control
q=0
, then it is easy to check, that in the above assumption, we can use

\accentset{\circ}{q}=0
and
\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}}=\mathbf{0}
. Moreover, if there exists a constant
d
, such that
q_{a,i}\leq d\leq q_{b,i}

for all
i=1,...,L
, setting
\accentset{\circ}{q}_{i}=d
, we observe

\sum_{i=1}^{L}\left(\accentset{\circ}{q}_{i}\nabla\zeta_{i},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=d\sum_{i=1}^{L}\left(\nabla\zeta_{i},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=d\sum_{i=1}^{L}\left(\zeta_{i}\mathbf{n},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\partial\Omega}-d\sum_{i=1}^{L}\left(\zeta_{i},\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=d\left(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\partial\Omega}=d\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},1}\right)_{\Omega}=0,

for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}
, where we have used that
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0
,
\zeta_{i}(x)=\delta_{i,j}
for

x\in\Gamma_{N,j}
and
\mathbf{v}=0
on
\Gamma_{D}
. Thus also in this case, it is straightforward to check,
that
\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}}=\mathbf{0}
solves (
25
).

If in the above settings
\mathbf{u}_{0}\neq\mathbf{0}
, then a smallness condition on
\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{H}}

still guarantees existence of
\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(\accentset{\circ}{q},\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}})
satisfy
Assumption

4.2
,
and let
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{H}
.
Then problem (

^
P

) possesses a
global solution
(\bar{q},\bar{\mathbf{u}})\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}Q\times\mathbb{V}}
, which satisfies the bounds

\|\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\leq(2\alpha)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{u}_{d}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}^{2}+\|\accentset{\circ}{q}\|_{L^{2}(I)},\qquad\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}\leq\|\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{u}_{d}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}+(2\alpha)^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\accentset{\circ}{q}\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\mathbf{u}_{d}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)},

as well as

\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})}+\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})}\leq C_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{d},\alpha,\accentset{\circ}{q},\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{u}_{0},\Omega).

Moreover,
if
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}
, then
\bar{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{W}
and
there exists a bound

\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{d},\alpha,\accentset{\circ}{q},\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{u}_{0},\Omega).}

Proof.

Let us first introduce the set of feasible control-state pairs

M:=\{(q,\mathbf{u})\in Q\times\mathbb{V}:(q,\mathbf{u})\text{ satisfy }\eqref{eq:stateequation},\eqref{eq:control_constraint}\}.

By
Assumption

4.2
, it holds
(\accentset{\circ}{q},\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}})\in M
and thus
M\neq\emptyset
.

Since the objective functional is bounded from below by
0
, there exists a minimizing sequence

\{(q_{n},\mathbf{u}_{n})\}\subset{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}M}
such that

J({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}q_{n},\mathbf{u}_{n}})\rightarrow{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{J}}:={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\inf\{J({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}q,\mathbf{u}}):(q,\mathbf{u})\in M\}.}

Especially, due to the equivalence of (

P

) and (

^
P

),
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(q_{n},\mathbf{u}_{n})
satisfy the initial condition

\mathbf{u}_{n}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}
and the state equation

(27)

\left\langle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{n},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{n},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left((\mathbf{u}_{n}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}_{n}),\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=-\sum_{i=1}^{L}q_{n,i}\left(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}\right)_{\Gamma_{N,i}}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V},\text{ a.e. }t\in I,

where we have written the right hand side as in the original problem formulation
(

P

).

As
(\accentset{\circ}{q},\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}})
are feasible,
it holds
J(\accentset{\circ}{q},\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}})\geq\bar{J}
.
In the case of equality, we immediately obtain, that
(\bar{q},\bar{\mathbf{u}})=(\accentset{\circ}{q},\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}})
is a global
solution. Hence we can w.l.o.g. assume that
J(\accentset{\circ}{q},\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}})>\bar{J}
.
In this case we obtain,
that for
n
large enough, there holds

\frac{\alpha}{2}\|q_{n}\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}\leq J(q_{n},\mathbf{u}_{n})\leq J(\accentset{\circ}{q},\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}})=\frac{1}{4}\|\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{u}_{d}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}^{4}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\accentset{\circ}{q}\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}.

Dividing by
\frac{\alpha}{2}
and taking the square root, yields

(28)

\|q_{n}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\leq(2\alpha)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{u}_{d}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}^{2}+\|\accentset{\circ}{q}\|_{L^{2}(I)}=\text{ const.}

Similarly, using the same arguments plus the triangle inequality,
from
\frac{1}{4}\|\mathbf{u}_{n}-\mathbf{u}_{d}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}^{4}\leq J(\accentset{\circ}{q},\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}})
, we obtain

(29)

\|\mathbf{u}_{n}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}\leq\|\accentset{\circ}{\mathbf{u}}-\mathbf{u}_{d}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}+(2\alpha)^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\accentset{\circ}{q}\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\mathbf{u}_{d}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}=\text{const.}

As we have just shown a uniform bound for
\|\mathbf{u}_{n}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

the corresponding
\mathbf{u}_{n}
may be used as a linearization point in (
23
).
We thus define
\mathbf{w}_{n}\in\mathbb{V}
to be the unique solution to

(30)

\left\langle\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}_{n},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\mathbf{w}_{n},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left((\mathbf{u}_{n}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{w}_{n}),\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=-\sum_{i=1}^{L}q_{n,i}\left(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{n}\right)_{\Gamma_{N,i}}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V},\text{ a.e. }t\in I,

with initial condition
\mathbf{w}_{n}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}
.
By
Theorem

3.9
there holds

(31)

\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})}+\|\mathbf{w}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})}+\|\mathbf{w}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{H})}\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{u}_{n}\|_{L^{4}(I\times\Omega)}\right)\left(\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{H}}+\|q_{n}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\right).

Since for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds
\mathbf{u}_{n}\in\mathbb{V}
, we may also insert
\mathbf{u}_{n}

in place of
\mathbf{w}_{n}
into (
30
). In this case we have on the left hand
side exactly the left hand side of (
27
).
As the right hand sides of (
27
) and (
30
)
coincide, the uniqueness of solutions to (
30
) implies
\mathbf{w}_{n}=\mathbf{u}_{n}
.
Hence the estimate (
31
) translates to

(32)

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})}+}\|\mathbf{u}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})}+\|\mathbf{u}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;\mathbf{H})}\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{u}_{n}\|_{L^{4}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}I\times\Omega})}\right)\left(\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{H}}+\|q_{n}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\right)\leq C,

which due to (
28
) and (
29
) holds uniformly for all
n
.

After
we have shown these boundedness statements,
we can extract subsequences, which converge in some topologies.
Due to (
28
), for a subsequence of controls, again denoted by
\{q_{n}\}
, it holds

\left(\nabla\mathbf{w},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v},p_{\mathbf{w}}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w},p_{\mathbf{v}}\right)_{\Omega}=\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{\Omega}+\left(\rho,p_{\mathbf{v}}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }(\mathbf{v},p_{\mathbf{v}})\in\widehat{\mathbf{V}}\times L^{2}(\Omega).

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathbf{f}\in\widehat{\mathbf{V}}^{*}
and
\rho\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Then (
45
) has

a unique solution
(\mathbf{w},p_{\mathbf{w}})\in\widehat{V}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, and there exists a constant

C>0
such that

\|\mathbf{w}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|p_{\mathbf{w}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\widehat{\mathbf{V}}^{*}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

If moreover,
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
and
\rho\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, it holds

(\mathbf{w},p_{\mathbf{w}})\in\left[{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\widehat{\mathbf{V}}\cap H^{2}(\Omega)^{2}}\right]\times H^{1}(\Omega)
and
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|\mathbf{w}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla p_{\mathbf{w}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\rho\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right).

Proof.

The unique solvability is a standard result, and can be found in, e.g.,

[
12
, Theorem 4.2.3]
. The higher regularity result can be shown by a localization
argument, which is carried out in
[
59
, Appendix A.3]
for a rectangular domain, but
due to
Assumption

2.1
the result holds in our setting as well.
∎

In the case, that the right hand side
\rho
of the divergence condition is already the divergence of
some known vector field, by a duality argument, we obtain a bound for the
L^{2}
norm of the velocity field
of the stationary problem.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\mathbf{E}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}
and
\mathbf{f}\in\widehat{\mathbf{V}}^{*}
.

Then the velocity component
\mathbf{w}\in\widehat{\mathbf{V}}
of the weak solution to

(
45
)
with
\rho=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}
satisfies

\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{E}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\widehat{\mathbf{V}}^{-2}}}\right),

where we use the notation
\widehat{\mathbf{V}}^{-2}:=(\widehat{\mathbf{V}}\cap H^{2}(\Omega)^{2})^{*}
.

Proof.

We use the above lemma in a dual formulation: given
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
,
let
(\mathbf{z}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}},p_{\mathbf{z}}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}})\in\widehat{\mathbf{V}}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfy

\left(\nabla\mathbf{v},\nabla\mathbf{z}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{z}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}},p_{\mathbf{v}}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v},p_{\mathbf{z}}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{\Omega}=\left({\boldsymbol{\psi}},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }(\mathbf{v},p_{\mathbf{v}})\in\widehat{\mathbf{V}}\times L^{2}(\Omega).

Then it holds

\|\mathbf{z}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla p_{\mathbf{z}}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}\|{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Thus
choosing
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}=\mathbf{w}
, using the dual and primal equations,
integration by parts,
\mathbf{E}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

and the
above estimate of
\mathbf{z}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}
and
\nabla p_{\mathbf{z}}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}
in terms of

the
L^{2}
norm of
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}
,
yields

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{w},{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\nabla\mathbf{w},\nabla\mathbf{z}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{z}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}},p_{\mathbf{w}}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w},p_{\mathbf{z}}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{\Omega}=\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{z}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rangle_{\Omega}+\left(\rho,p_{\mathbf{z}}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{z}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rangle_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E},p_{\mathbf{z}}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{\Omega}=\left\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{z}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rangle_{\Omega}-\left(\mathbf{E},\nabla p_{\mathbf{z}}^{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{\Omega}\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{E}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\widehat{\mathbf{V}}^{-2}}\right)\|{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Dividing by
\|{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
concludes the proof.

∎

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}\ni\mathbf{E}\mapsto\mathbf{w}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}

As for

the solution operator of (

\|R(\mathbf{E})\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\mathbf{E}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\text{ for }\mathbf{E}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}}\ \text{ and }\ \|R(\mathbf{E})\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\mathbf{E}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\text{ for }\mathbf{E}\in(H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{2}}.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let

\mathbf{w}\in L^{s}(I;H^{2}(\Omega)^{2})

.

The

.

∎

Since the controls in our setting are purely time dependent, in

To this end, recall the

to an interval in

By

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\mathbf{g}_{i}\in H^{\frac{1}{4}}(I;L^{2}(\Gamma_{N,i})^{2})\cap L^{2}(I;H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00}(\Gamma_{N,i})^{2})

\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{L}\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{4}}(I;L^{2}(\Gamma_{N,i})^{2})\cap L^{2}(I;H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00}(\Gamma_{N,i})^{2})}.

for

Proof.

Away from the corners of
\Omega

[

Close to a corner,

[
61

62
]

The following scalar considerations can be applied to the individual components of

Consider the sector

From

[H^{2}_{y}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\cap H^{1}_{0,y}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),L^{2}_{y}(\mathbb{R}^{+})]_{\frac{3}{4}}=H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00,y}(\mathbb{R}^{+})

For related results on interpolation spaces with homogeneous boundary conditions, see also

[

,
[

[

]

We introduce the spaces

Thus,

[

, Chapter 1, Equation (9.24)]

\left[X,Y\right]_{\frac{3}{4}}=H^{\frac{1}{4}}_{t}(I;L^{2}_{y}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))\cap L^{2}_{t}(I;H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00,y}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))

, for every

(48)

Due to the intermediate derivative theorem (

Thus, by reordering the derivatives, we have just proven

∎

Note that the above result provides an extension of the mixed Dirichlet-Neumann data, but in general

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})
and

\mathbf{g}_{i}\in H^{\frac{1}{4}}(I;L^{2}(\Gamma_{N,i})^{2})\cap L^{2}(I;H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00}(\Gamma_{N,i})^{2})

for
i=1,...,L
.
Then there exists a

(\mathbf{w},p_{\mathbf{w}})\in\mathbb{W}\times L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))

\left(\partial_{t}\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\mathbf{w},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(p_{\mathbf{w}},\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w},p_{\mathbf{v}}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\sum_{i=1}^{L}\left(\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Gamma_{N,i}}

for all
(\mathbf{v},p_{\mathbf{v}})\in\widehat{\mathbf{V}}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, a.e.
t\in I
, and
\mathbf{w}(0)=\mathbf{0}
,

satisfying

\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+\|p_{\mathbf{w}}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))}}\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\sum_{i=1}^{L}\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{4}}(I;L^{2}(\Gamma_{N,i}))\cap L^{2}(I;H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00}(\Gamma_{N,i}))}\right).

Proof.

Unique solvability for this problem was already shown in

Proposition

3.2
. For low regularity of the data, existence
of a distributional pressure can be shown with a similar argument as in the proof of

Proposition

3.2

By
Theorem

4.12
, there exists

\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}}\in L^{2}\left(I;{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{2}}\right)\cap H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})

\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}}|_{\Gamma_{N,i}}=\mathbf{g}_{i}

\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{L}\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{4}}(I;L^{2}(\Gamma_{N,i}))\cap L^{2}(I;H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00}(\Gamma_{N,i}))}\right).

Furthermore,
using integration by parts
, we obtain

(50)

\left(\nabla\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(-\Delta\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\sum_{i=1}^{L}\left(\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Gamma_{N,i}}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\widehat{\mathbf{V}},\text{ a.e. }t\in I}.

Since at this point,
\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}}
might not have zero divergence,
let us introduce for a.e.
t\in I
the function
\mathbf{w}_{1}(t)=R(\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}}(t))
with

defined as in
(
46
).
As
\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}}\in L^{2}\left(I;{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{2}}\right)
,
due to (
47

\|\mathbf{w}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))},

and
\mathbf{w}_{1}(t){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)^{2}\cap\widehat{\mathbf{V}}}
,

, satisfy for a.e.

(51)

\left(\nabla\mathbf{w}_{1}(t),\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}_{1}(t),p_{\mathbf{v}}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v},p_{\mathbf{w}_{1}}(t)\right)_{\Omega}=\left(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{g}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(t)},p_{\mathbf{v}}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }(\mathbf{v},p_{\mathbf{v}})\in\widehat{\mathbf{V}}\times L^{2}(\Omega).

t\in I

Putting (

\displaystyle\left[L^{2}(I;(H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00}(\Gamma_{N,i}))^{*}),H^{\frac{1}{4}}(I;L^{2}(\Gamma_{N,i}))\cap L^{2}(I;H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00}(\Gamma_{N,i}))\right]_{\theta}\hskip 142.26378pt

\displaystyle=H^{\frac{\theta}{4}}\left(I;[(H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00}(\Gamma_{N,i}))^{*},L^{2}(\Gamma_{N,i})]_{1-\theta}\right)\cap L^{2}\left(I;[{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00}(\Gamma_{N,i}))^{*}},H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00}(\Gamma_{N,i})]_{\theta}\right).

Similarly using several times the reiteration theorem (

\displaystyle\left[(H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00}(\Gamma_{N,i}))^{*},H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{00}(\Gamma_{N,i})\right]_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\left[[H^{-1}(\Gamma_{N,i}),L^{2}(\Gamma_{N,i})]_{\frac{1}{2}},[H^{1}_{0}(\Gamma_{N,i}),L^{2}(\Gamma_{N,i})]_{\frac{1}{2}}\right]_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\left[\left[H^{-1}(\Gamma_{N,i}),\left[H^{-1}(\Gamma_{N,i}),H^{1}_{0}(\Gamma_{N,i})\right]_{\frac{1}{2}}\right]_{\frac{1}{2}},\left[H^{1}_{0}(\Gamma_{N,i}),\left[H^{-1}(\Gamma_{N,i}),H^{1}_{0}(\Gamma_{N,i})\right]_{\frac{1}{2}}\right]_{\frac{1}{2}}\right]_{\theta}

The next two technical lemmas gives us regularity results for multiplication of two functions in some
Bochner spaces, as well as instationary trace results.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}
be open and let
p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty]
satisfy
1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}=1/2
and let

s_{1},s_{2},s\in[0,1]
satisfy
s\leq\min\{s_{1},s_{2}\}
and
s_{1}+s_{2}>s+1/2
.
Then for any
u\in H^{s_{1}}(I;L^{p_{1}}(\omega))
and
w\in H^{s_{2}}(I;L^{p_{2}}(\omega))
, there holds

u\cdot w\in H^{s}(I;L^{2}(\omega))
with a bound

\|u\cdot w\|_{H^{s}(I;L^{2}(\omega))}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s_{1}}(I;L^{p_{1}}(\omega))}\|w\|_{H^{s_{2}}(I;L^{p_{2}}(\omega))}.

Moreover, for any

u\in L^{p_{1}}(I;H^{s_{1}}(\omega))
and
w\in L^{p_{2}}(I;H^{s_{2}}(\omega))
, there holds

u\cdot w\in L^{2}(I;H^{s}(\omega))
with a bound

\|u\cdot w\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{s}(\omega))}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}}(I;H^{s_{1}}(\omega))}\|w\|_{L^{p_{2}}(I;H^{s_{2}}(\omega))}.

Proof.

We only show one of the claims, the other follows by switching the roles of
I
and
\omega
.
By Hölder’s inequality and the assumptions on
p_{1},p_{2}
there holds

\|u\cdot v\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\omega)}\|v\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\omega)}.

By assumptions on the regularities of
u,v
, the norms on the right hand sides are functions in

H^{s_{i}}(I)
,
i=1,2
.
The result then is a direct consequence of the multiplication theorem in Sobolev spaces

[
6
, Theorem A.1]
.
Note that the second result can be obtained with the same argument and the same conditions on
s_{i}
,
as
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}
was assumed to be one-dimensional.
∎

Lemma 4.16
.

The spacial trace operator

is a well defined, continuous, linear operator between
the following spaces

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}

\displaystyle\to H^{\gamma}(I;H^{\frac{1}{2}-2\gamma}(\partial\Omega)^{2}),

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}

\displaystyle\to H^{\gamma}(I;H^{\frac{3}{2}-2\gamma}(\partial\Omega)^{2}),

\displaystyle\text{if }\gamma\in\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\right),

\displaystyle L^{2}(I;H^{1+\theta}(\Omega)^{2})\cap H^{\theta}(I;H^{1-\theta}(\Omega)^{2})

\displaystyle\to H^{\gamma}(I;H^{\frac{1}{2}+\theta-2\gamma}(\partial\Omega)^{2}),

\displaystyle\text{if }\gamma\in[0,\theta)\cap\left(\frac{\theta}{2}-\frac{1}{4},\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right),

where in the last statement,

is some fixed parameter, satisfying
\theta\in(0,1)

Proof.

Let first
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{V}=L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})\cap H^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})

Note that for
\gamma=0
, this is just a direct application
of the trace theorem to
\mathbf{w}\in L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})

the intermediate derivative theorem (
2
) gives

Applying the intermediate derivative theorem (
2

\mathbf{w}\in H^{\frac{\beta}{2}}(I;H^{1-\beta}(\Omega)^{2})

\beta\in(0,1)

If we introduce
\gamma:=\frac{\beta}{2}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})

the trace theorem (

\tr(\mathbf{w})\in H^{\gamma}(I;H^{1/2-2\gamma}(\partial\Omega)^{2})

Thus the result holds for all

Let now

\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{W}=L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}\cap H^{2}(\Omega)^{2})\cap H^{1}(I;\mathbf{H})\subset L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega)^{2})\cap H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})

the intermediate derivative theorem (
2

gives
\mathbf{w}\in H^{\gamma}(I;H^{2-2\gamma}(\Omega)^{2})
for any

\gamma\in(0,1)

\tr(\mathbf{w})\in H^{\gamma}(I;H^{\frac{3}{2}-2\gamma}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\partial\Omega})^{2})

.

Lastly, let

\mathbf{w}\in L^{2}(I;H^{1+\theta}(\Omega)^{2})\cap H^{\theta}(I;H^{1-\theta}(\Omega))
,
for some fixed
\theta\in(0,1)
.
We can define
\sigma:=\frac{\gamma}{\theta}

iff
\gamma\in(0,\theta)

The intermediate derivative theorem (

\mathbf{w}\in H^{\sigma\theta}(I;H^{\sigma(1-\theta)+(1-\sigma)(1+\theta)}(\Omega)^{2})

Thus with the definition of

Whenever

\gamma\in(\frac{\theta}{2}-\frac{1}{4},\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{1}{4})

\tr(\mathbf{w})\in H^{\gamma}(I;H^{\frac{1}{2}+\theta-2\gamma}(\partial\Omega)^{2})

Combining this condition with

concludes the proof.
∎

Note that while this improved regularity of the adjoint state is an interesting result
in itself for the continuous optimal control problem,
it is essential in deriving error estimates for the discretized optimal control problem.
While a derivation of error estimates is beyond the scope of this work, the improved
regularity of the adjoint state, can be viewed as one important step towards this goal.
The second order conditions shown in the next section are another main ingredient.

4.4.

Following

for the mixed boundary condition setting, to the one for pure Dirichlet boundary conditions.
This requires a careful extension of the domain, i.e. definition of a domain

\overline{\Omega^{E}}=\overline{\Omega}\cup\overline{\Omega^{C}}
.
A sketch of such a domain can be seen in

. Note that in order to
be able to extend any solenoidal function in
\mathbf{V}
to a solenoidal function on the larger domain,
all Neumann boundaries have to be connected to each other.
By
Assumption

2.1
, each connected component of
\Gamma_{D,i}
has a small neighborhood

\mathcal{U}_{i}
, such that
\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{D,j}=\emptyset
for
j\neq i
.
Each connected component of
\Gamma_{D,i}
can be augmented to a closed, not-self intersecting
curve
\gamma_{i}
, such that
\gamma_{i}\subset\mathcal{U}_{i}
and
\gamma_{i}\cup\bar{\Omega}=\Gamma_{D,i}
.
Let us denote the domain enclosed by
\gamma_{i}
by
\omega_{i}
.
Define
R
large enough, such that
\Omega\cup\bigcup_{i}\gamma_{i}\subset B_{R}(0)
.
Then a possible extension is given by
\Omega^{E}:=B_{R}(0)\backslash\bigcup_{i}\bar{\omega}_{i}
,
and we define
\Omega^{C}:=\interior(\Omega^{E}\backslash\Omega)
.
We can split the boundary of
\Omega^{C}
into three parts

\partial\Omega^{C}=\Gamma_{N}\cup\Gamma_{C}\cup\Gamma_{B}
, where
\Gamma_{N}
is the original Neumann
boundary of
\Omega
,
\Gamma^{C}=\bigcup(\gamma_{i}\backslash\Gamma_{D,i})
and

\Gamma_{B}=\partial B_{R}(0)
.
On
\Omega^{C}
, we solve the following Stokes problem subject to pure Dirichlet conditions,
see
[
31
, Theorem IV.1.1]

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta\mathbf{w}+\nabla p_{\mathbf{w}}&=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{in }\Omega^{C},\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}&=0\quad\text{in }\Omega^{C},\\
\mathbf{w}&=\mathbf{v}\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{N},\\
\mathbf{w}&=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{C}\cup\Gamma_{B}.\end{aligned}\right.

Using integration by parts, and using that the traces of
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{v}
coincide on
\Gamma_{N}
,
it is straightforward to show, that the composite function

\mathbf{u}=\begin{cases}{}\mathbf{v}&\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\\
\mathbf{w}&\mathbf{x}\in\Omega^{C}\end{cases}

is in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{E})^{2}
and
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0
.
As due to
[
82
, Chapter I, Theorem 1.6]
, there exists a sequence of functions

\{\mathbf{u}_{n}\}\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega^{E})
, such that
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\|\nabla(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{E})}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
.
This shows that
\|\nabla(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u}_{n})|_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
,
concluding the proof.

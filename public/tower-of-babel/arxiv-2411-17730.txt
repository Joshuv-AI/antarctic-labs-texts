Almost sure well-posedness and orbital stability for Schrödinger equation with potential
Jun Wang, Zhaoyang Yin

Abstract
In this paper, we study the almost sure well-posedness theory and orbital stability for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with potential { i ∂ t u + Δ u − V ( x ) u + | u | 2 u = 0 , ( x , t ) ∈ ℝ 4 × ℝ , u | t = 0 = f ∈ H s ​ ( ℝ 4 ) , \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u-V(x)u+|u|^{2}u=0,\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R},\\ \left.u\right|_{t=0}=f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),\end{array}\right. where 1 3 < s < 1 \frac{1}{3}<s<1 and V ⁡ ( x ) : ℝ 4 → ℝ V(x):\mathbb{R}^{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} satisfies appropriate conditions. The main idea in the proofs is based on Strichartz spaces as well as variants of local smoothing, inhomogeneous local smoothing and maximal function spaces. To our best knowledge, this is the first orbital stability result for this model.

Introduction and main results

This paper studies the almost sure well-posedness theory and orbital stability for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with potential

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u-V(x)u+|u|^{2}u=0,\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\left.u\right|_{t=0}=f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
V(x):\mathbb{R}^{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies appropriate conditions.

Consider the following equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\pm|u|^{2}u,\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\left.u\right|_{t=0}=f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{4}).\end{array}\right.

For this model under the probabilistic setting, the local well-posedness, small data scattering, and conditional global well-posedness results have been established before. This approach was initiated by Bourgain
[
2
,
3
]
for the periodic nonlinear Schrödinger equation in one and two space dimensions, building
upon the constructions of invariant measures by Burq-Tzvetkov
[
8
,
6
]
in the context of the cubic nonlinear wave
equation on a three-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold. There has since been a
vast and fascinating body of research, using probabilistic tools to study many nonlinear
dispersive or hyperbolic equations in scaling super-critical regimes, see for example
[
4
,
7
,
9
,
10
]
and references therein. Previously for the random data local result, Pocovnicu and
Wang’s argument
[
21
]
is based on the dispersive inequality; Dodson et. al
[
12
]
used the high dimensional version of smoothing effect and maximal function estimates. Recently,
[

]
give some simple new approaches combining the atom space method by Koch-Tataru
[
17
]
and the variants of bilinear Strichartz estimate.

In this paper, we are interested in the almost sure well-posedness of the Schrödinger equation with potential and the orbital stability of the ground state. The potential
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is assumed to satisfy the following assumptions

V\in\mathcal{K}\cap L^{\frac{d}{2}}

(1.2)

and

\left\|V_{-}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}}<d(d-2)\alpha(d),

(1.3)

\mathbb{R}^{d}(d\geq 3)

Preliminary

In this section, we provide some notations and some important lemma in the proof of main theorems. Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
be an even, non-negative bump function with
\operatorname{supp}(\psi)\subseteq B(0,1)
and such that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}}\psi(\xi-k)=1\quad\text{ for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{4}.

Let

We let

(2.2)

This quantity is understood as a Cauchy limit in

for every
\omega\in\Sigma

[

Functional framework

(q,r)
is called admissible if
2\leq q,r\leq\infty
and the following scaling condition is satisfied

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{4}{r}=2.

\left\|e^{itH}P_{N}f\right\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{2,\infty}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\lesssim N^{\frac{3}{2}}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}.

On the other hand, by using Fubini’s theorem, Bernstein estimates and (

\displaystyle\left\|e^{itH}P_{N}f\right\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{4,4}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|e^{itH}P_{N}f\right\|_{L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{4}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|e^{itH}P_{N}f\right\|_{L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{\frac{8}{3}}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}

\displaystyle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}

Hence, the estimate (

asserts that

\left\|e^{itH}P_{N}P_{N,\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}f\right\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\infty,2}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}

and interpolation.
∎

Note that the lateral space norms
\|h\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{p,q}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}
are continuous as functions of the endpoints of the time interval
I
and for
p,q<\infty
have the following time-divisibility property

\left\|\left\{\|h\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{p,q}\left(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\right\}_{j=1}^{J}\right\|_{\ell_{j}^{\max\{p,q\}}}\leq\|h\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{p,q}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}

(3.8)

for any partition of a time interval
I
into consecutive intervals

, with disjoint interiors. The estimate (
3.8
) is a consequence of Minkowski’s inequality and the embedding properties of the sequence spaces
\ell^{r}
. For
p,q<\infty
, (
3.8
) allows to partition the time interval
I
into a controlled number of subintervals on each of which the restricted norm is arbitrarily small.

Finally, we are ready to give the precise definition of the space
X(I)
to hold the solutions to the forced cubic NLS on a given time interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
. It is built from dyadic pieces in the sense that

\|v\|_{X(I)}:=\left(\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|P_{N}v\right\|_{X_{N}(I)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The dyadic subspace
X_{N}(I)
scales at
\dot{H}_{x}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)

0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. Throughout this
work it will always be implicitly understood that

\frac{1}{3}+3\varepsilon\leq s
, where

refers to the Sobolev regularity assumption for the random data in the statement of Theorem

. For every dyadic integer

we then set

\displaystyle\left\|P_{N}v\right\|_{X_{N}(I)}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle N\left\|P_{N}v\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}+N\left\|P_{N}v\right\|_{L_{t}^{3}L_{x}^{3}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}+N\left\|P_{N}v\right\|_{L_{t}^{6}L_{x}^{\frac{12}{5}}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\left\|P_{N}v\right\|_{W_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\frac{4}{2-\varepsilon},\frac{4}{\varepsilon}}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}.

We will estimate the forced cubic nonlinearity in the space

which is also built from dyadic pieces

\|h\|_{G(I)}:=\left(\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|P_{N}h\right\|_{G_{N}(I)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\left\|P_{N}h\right\|_{G_{N}(I)}:=\inf_{P_{N}h=h_{N}^{(1)}+h_{N}^{(2)}}\left\{N\left\|h_{N}^{(1)}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}N^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\left\|h_{N}^{(2)}\right\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\frac{4}{4-\varepsilon},\frac{4}{2+\varepsilon}}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\right\}.

It will be convenient to introduce a space
Y(I)
, in which we will place the forcing term

\displaystyle\left\|P_{N}F\right\|_{Y_{N}(I)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}\langle N\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}+3\varepsilon}N^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\left\|P_{N,\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}P_{N}F\right\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\frac{4}{\varepsilon},\frac{4}{2-\varepsilon}}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}.

) for the lateral spaces, respectively. Relying on the identity (

\displaystyle P_{N}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{N,\mathbf{e}_{1}}P_{N}u+P_{N,\mathbf{e}_{2}}\left(1-P_{N,\mathbf{e}_{1}}\right)P_{N}u+P_{N,\mathbf{e}_{3}}\left(1-P_{N,\mathbf{e}_{2}}\right)\left(1-P_{N,\mathbf{e}_{1}}\right)P_{N}u

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a time interval with
0=\inf I
and let
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
be any dyadic integer. Then we have for any admissible Strichartz pair

N\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)H}P_{N}u(s)ds\right\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}N^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\frac{4}{4-\varepsilon},\frac{4}{2+\varepsilon}}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}.

(3.12)

\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}N^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)H}P_{N}u(s)ds\right\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\frac{4}{2-\varepsilon},\frac{4}{\varepsilon}}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\lesssim\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}N^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\frac{4}{4-\varepsilon},\frac{4}{2+\varepsilon}}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}.

(3.13)

Proof.

We only prove (

3.9
) of the lateral spaces, we can proceed inductively to construct for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
a partition

\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}N^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\frac{4}{4-\varepsilon},\frac{4}{2+\varepsilon}}\left(I_{j}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\leq 2^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4})n}.

We then perform a Whitney type decomposition of the interval
I

and

and where

I_{j+1}^{n}

(p,q)=\left(\frac{4}{2-\varepsilon},\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\right)
. Note that by Lemma
3.2

and

\displaystyle N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{I_{j}^{n}}e^{i(t-s)H}P_{N}h(s)ds\right\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}^{p,q}\left(I_{j+1}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\lesssim\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}N^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\|P_{N}u\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\frac{4}{4-\varepsilon},\frac{4}{2+\varepsilon}}\left(I_{j}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\leq 2^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4})n}.

\frac{p}{q}\leq 1
, we compute that

\displaystyle N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)H}P_{N}h(s)ds\right\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}^{p,q}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{2^{n}}\chi_{I_{j+1}^{n}}\int_{I_{j}^{n}}e^{i(t-s)H}P_{N}h(s)ds\right\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}^{p,q}\left(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}

\displaystyle 1,

and every

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{C})

component. Here we first apply the local smoothing type estimate (

) for the lateral spaces and then use the large deviation estimate from Lemma
5.3
to obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}\langle N\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}+3\varepsilon}N^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\|e^{itH}P_{N,\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}P_{N}f^{\omega}\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\frac{4}{\varepsilon},\frac{4}{2-\varepsilon}}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\right\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\langle N\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}+3\varepsilon}\|P_{N}f^{\omega}\|_{L_{x}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\right\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{p}\langle N\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}+3\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{|k|\sim N}\left\|P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{p}\langle N\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}+3\varepsilon}\left\|P_{N}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}.

Finally, we turn to the maximal function type component of the
Y_{N}(\mathbb{R})
norm, where we distinguish the large frequency regime
N\gtrsim 1
and the small frequency regime
N\lesssim 1
. For large frequencies
N\gtrsim 1
we first use the large deviation estimate from Lemma
5.1
and then interpolate between the improved maximal function estimate (
5.1
) for unit-scale frequency localized data and an estimate of the
L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{4}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)
norm of the free evolution of unit-scale frequency localized data (based on the unit-scale Bernstein estimate and Strichartz estimates) to conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}\langle N\rangle^{-\frac{1}{6}}\|e^{itH}P_{N}f^{\omega}\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\frac{4}{2-\varepsilon},\frac{4}{\varepsilon}}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\right\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{p}\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}\langle N\rangle^{-\frac{1}{6}}\left(\sum_{|k|\sim N}\left\|e^{itH}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\frac{4}{2-\varepsilon},\frac{4}{\varepsilon}}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{p}N^{-\frac{1}{6}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{|k|\sim N}\left\|P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{p}\langle N\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}}\left\|P_{N}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}.

For small frequencies
N\lesssim 1
, we directly apply (
3.4
), trivially bounding the resulting frequency factors, and then use the large deviation estimate to infer that in this case

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}\langle N\rangle^{-\frac{1}{6}}\|e^{itH}P_{N}f^{\omega}\|_{L_{\mathbf{e}_{\ell}}^{\frac{4}{2-\varepsilon},\frac{4}{\varepsilon}}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\right\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{-\frac{1}{6}}N^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}\left\|\|P_{N}f^{\omega}\|_{L_{x}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\right\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{p}\left\|P_{N}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}.

Putting all of the above estimates together, we find that

\left\|\left\|e^{itH}f^{\omega}\right\|_{Y(\mathbb{R})}\right\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}}\lesssim\sqrt{p}\left(\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\langle N\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}+3\varepsilon}\left\|P_{N}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\sqrt{p}\|f\|_{H_{x}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)},

from which the assertion follows by Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Perturbation theory

In this subsection, we establish perturbation theory to compare solutions to the forced nonlinear Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(i\partial_{t}-H)v+|F+v|^{2}(F+v)=0,\\
&v(t_{0})=v_{0}\in H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\end{aligned}\right.

(7.1)

with solutions to the “usual” nonlinear Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(i\partial_{t}-H)u+|u|^{2}u=0,\\

(7.2)

Theorem 7.1
.

(Short-time perturbations)
Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a time interval with
t_{0}\in I
and let
v_{0},u_{0}\in H_{x}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)

8
Orbital stability

In this section, we consider the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation with mixed power nonlinearities

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u-V(x)u+|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u=0,\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R},

(8.1)

where
d\geq 4,u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\mu>0,2<q<2+\frac{4}{d}
and
2^{*}=\frac{2d}{d-2}
. Obviously, we can obtain global well-posedness results for (
8.1
), see
[
24
]
. Now, we are interested in almost sure orbital stability of ground state standing waves of prescribed mass for (
8.1
).

We recall that standing waves to (
8.1
) are solutions of the form
v(x,t)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x),\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the function
u(x)
satisfies the equation

-\Delta u+V(x)u-\lambda u=|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(8.2)

One can search for solutions to (
8.2
) having a prescribed
L^{2}
-norm. Defining on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
the energy functional

I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)u^{2}dx-\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\|u\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}},

it is standard to check that
I(u)
is of class
C^{1}
and that a critical point of
I_{\mu}
restricted to the (mass) constraint

S(a):=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}):\|u\|_{2}^{2}=a\}

gives rise to a solution to (
8.2
), satisfying
\|u\|_{2}^{2}=a
.

8.1
Ground state solution

We shall make use of the following classical inequalities:

Sobolev inequality
(see
[
5
]
) For any
d\geq 4
there exists an optimal constant
\mathcal{S}>0
depending only on
d
, such that

\mathcal{S}\|u\|_{2^{*}}^{2}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2},\ \forall u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(8.3)

Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
(see
[
19
]
) If
d\geq 2,p\in[2,\frac{2d}{d-2})
and
\beta=d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
, then

\|u\|_{p}\leq C_{d,p}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\beta}\|u\|_{2}^{1-\beta},

(8.4)

for all
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Now, it follows from (
8.4
) that

\displaystyle I(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)u^{2}dx-\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\|u\|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\left\|V_{-}\right\|_{\frac{d}{2}}\mathcal{S}^{-1}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{C_{d,q}^{q}a^{\frac{2q-d(q-2)}{4}}}{q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{d(q-2)}{4}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2^{*}\cdot\mathcal{S}^{\frac{2^{*}}{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{2^{*}}{2}},

we consider the function
f(a,\rho)
defined on

m(a)\geq m\left(a-a_{1}\right)+I\left(u_{a}\right)=m\left(a-a_{1}\right)+m\left(a_{1}\right)>m\left(a-a_{1}+a_{1}\right)=m(a),

which is impossible. Thus, the claim follows and from (
8.14
) we obtain that
\left\|u_{a}\right\|_{2}^{2}=a
.

Now, in order to complete the proof, we need to show that
\left\|\nabla w_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}\rightarrow 0
. Note that, by using (
8.15
) and
u_{a}\neq 0
, we have
\left\|\nabla w_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}<\rho_{0}
for
n
large enough, so
\left(w_{n}\right)\subset B_{\rho_{0}}
and it is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then by using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and recalling
\left\|w_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}\rightarrow 0
, we also have
\left\|w_{n}\right\|_{q}^{q}\rightarrow 0
. Thus, it follows from Lemma
8.6
that

I\left(w_{n}\right)\geq\beta_{0}\left\|\nabla w_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}+o_{n}(1)\text{ where }\beta_{0}>0.

(8.18)

Now we remember that

I\left(u_{n}\right)=I\left(u_{a}\right)+I\left(w_{n}\right)+o_{n}(1)\rightarrow m(a).

Since
u_{a}\in\mathbf{V}(a)
by weak limit, we have that
I\left(u_{a}\right)\geq m(a)
and hence
I\left(w_{n}\right)\leq o_{n}(1)
. Then we get that
\left\|\nabla w_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}\rightarrow 0
by using (
8.18
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The fact that if
\left(u_{n}\right)\subset V(a)
is such that
I\left(u_{n}\right)\rightarrow m(a)
then, up to translation,
u_{n}\rightarrow u\in M_{a}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
follows from Lemma
8.8
. In particular, it insures the existence of a minimizer for
I(u)
on
\mathbf{V}(a)
and this minimizer is a ground state.
∎

8.2
Orbital stability

In this subsection, we prove that the set
\mathcal{M}_{a}
is orbitally stable.

Lemma 8.9
.

Let
v\in\mathcal{M}_{a}
. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that:

\forall\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ s.t. }\|\varphi-v\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\delta\Longrightarrow\sup_{t\in\left[0,+\infty\right)}\mathop{\operatorname{dist}}\limits_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(u_{\varphi}(t),\mathcal{M}_{a}\right)<\varepsilon,

where
u_{\varphi}(t)
is the global solution of (
8.1
) corresponding to the initial data in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

Suppose the theorem is false, there exists a decreasing sequence
\left(\delta_{n}\right)\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
converging to 0 and
\left(\varphi_{n}\right)\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

\left\|\varphi_{n}-v\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\delta_{n},

but

\sup_{t\in\left[0,+\infty\right)}\mathop{\operatorname{dist}}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(u_{\varphi_{n}}(t),\mathcal{M}_{a}\right)>\varepsilon_{0}

for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. We observe that
\left\|\varphi_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}\rightarrow a
and
I\left(\varphi_{n}\right)\rightarrow m(a)
by continuity of
I(u)
. By conservation laws, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough,
u_{\varphi_{n}}
will remains inside of
\mathbf{B}_{\rho_{0}}
for all
t\in\left[0,+\infty\right)
. Indeed, if for some time
\bar{t}>0,\ \left\|\nabla u_{\varphi_{n}}(\bar{t})\right\|_{2}^{2}=\rho_{0}
, then in view of Lemma
8.4
we have that
I\left(u_{\varphi_{n}}(\bar{t})\right)\geq 0
in contradiction with
m(a)<0
. Now let
t_{n}>0
be the first time such that
\mathop{\operatorname{dist}}\limits_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(u_{\varphi_{n}}\left(t_{n}\right),\mathcal{M}_{a}\right)=\varepsilon_{0}
and set
u_{n}:=u_{\varphi_{n}}\left(t_{n}\right)
. By conservation laws,
\left(u_{n}\right)\subset\mathbf{B}_{\rho_{0}}
satisfies
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}\rightarrow a
and
I\left(u_{n}\right)\rightarrow m(a)
and thus, in view of Lemma
8.8
, it converges, up to translation, to an element of
\mathcal{M}_{a}
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{a}
is invariant under translation this contradicts the equality
\mathop{\operatorname{dist}}\limits_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(u_{n},\mathcal{M}_{a}\right)=\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
∎

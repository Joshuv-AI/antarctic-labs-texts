Exact results and the structure of extremal families for the Duke–Erdős forbidden sunflower problem
Andrey Kupavskii, Fedor Noskov

Abstract
In 1977, Duke and Erdős asked the following general question: What is the largest size of a family ℱ ⊂ ( [ n ] k ) \mathcal{F}\subset\binom{[n]}{k} that does not contain a sunflower with s s petals and core of size exactly t − 1 t-1 ? This problem is closely related to the famous Erdős–Rado sunflower problem of determining the size ϕ ⁡ ( s , t ) \phi(s,t) of the largest t t -uniform family with no s s -sunflower. In this paper, we answer this question exactly for t = 2 t=2 , odd s s and k ⩾ 5 k\geqslant 5 , provided n n is large enough. Previously, the only know exact extremal result on this problem was due to Chung and Frankl from 1987. One of the important ingredients for the proof that we obtained is a stability result for the Duke–Erdős problem, which was previously not known, mostly due to our lack of understanding of the behaviour of ϕ ⁡ ( s , t ) \phi(s,t) . For large k k and n n we in fact manage to reduce the Duke–Erdős problem to an Erdős–Rado-like problem which depends on t t and s s only. In particular, we get a good understanding of the structure of extremal families for the Duke–Erdős problem in terms of the Erdős–Rado problem. Previously, a much looser variant of this connection (only in terms of the sizes, rather than the structure, of respective extremal families) was established in a seminal work of Frankl and Füredi from 1987.

Although Frankl and Füredi managed to obtain the asymptotic solution to the Duke–Erdős problem in the regime
n>n_{0}(s,k),
they did not prove any results concerning the structure of extremal results. A big obstacle in that respect is that a stability result was missing. Here, the situation is in contrast with the aforementioned Erdős–Sós problem. Let us formulate the corresponding extremal question: what is the largest size of the family
\mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose k}
that has no two sets

(i.e., a family that avoids sunflowers with

)? There, we should mention a result of Keevash, Mubayi, and Wilson
[
37

, who proved a stability result for the Erdős-Sós problem for

[

]
, where they used the delta-system method to find an exact solution to the problem for

[

of the first author.

The difficulty of the Duke–Erdős problem is again in our poor understanding of
\phi(s,t)
and the corresponding extremal families. In this paper, we fill this gap and prove a stability result. The following result can be thought of as a ‘first-order’ stability for this problem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k\geqslant 2t+1
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family that does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size
t-1
. Suppose that
|\mathcal{F}|\geqslant(1-\delta)\phi(s,t)\binom{n}{k-t}
for some

\mathcal{T}\subset\binom{[n]}{t}
that does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and a constant

depending on

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{T}]|\leqslant\left(3k\phi^{2}(s,t)\cdot\delta+\frac{C(s,k)}{n}\right)\cdot\phi(s,t)\binom{n-t}{k-t},

provided
n\geqslant n_{0}(s,k)
for some function
n_{0}(s,k)
.

The idea behind the proof of Theorem
1.3
is based on expansion of the Johnson graph on
\binom{[n]}{k-t}
, which can be thought as an action of a certain random walk on the shadow of
\mathcal{F}
. Related techniques were used previously in Extremal Set Theory, cf. the seminal proof of the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem by Lovász
[
44

, application of Along and Chung bound
[

]
to the analysis of the Erdős Matching Conjecture
[
24
]
, hypercontracitivty approach of Keevash, Lifshitz, Keller and coathors
[
35
,
36
]
based on the action of a certain semigroups or proofs of the spread lemma
[
45
,
51
,
50
]
based on certain Markov chains. However, as our paper shows, the connection between the hypergraph Turan-type problems and the semigroup/Markov chains theory is still under development. Because of the lack of our knowledge on Problem
1
, the techniques that we use are not that specific for Duke–Erdős Problem
2
. In our further research, we plan to apply it to other Turan-type problems, combining with other available techniques.

Theorem
1.3
is the starting point for iteratively getting higher-order stability for extremal
|\mathcal{F}|
that gradually reveals the structure of extremal families. Building on this theorem, we prove the following theorem, which guarantees in some regimes of the parameters that any extremal family

must in fact contain the family from Example

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
k\geqslant\max\{2t+1,2t(t-1)\}
and
n\geqslant n_{0}(s,k)
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\binom{[n]}{k}
be an extremal family that does not contain a sunflower with
s

. Then, there exists a family
\mathcal{T}\subset\binom{[n]}{t}
that does not contain a sunflower with

\displaystyle\left\{F\in\binom{[n]}{k}\,:\,F\cap\support(\mathcal{T})\in\mathcal{T}\right\}\subset\mathcal{F}.

In particular, for each

, we have either

for some

or

.

The appearance of

For a family

If

, we omit braces and use

. We define the support of a family

as

. For families

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\vee\mathcal{B}=\{F\cup B\,:\,F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ and }B\in\mathcal{B}\}.

\leqslant k

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(h)}=\{F\in\mathcal{F}\,:\,|F|=h\}.

The

\displaystyle\partial_{h}\mathcal{F}=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\binom{F}{h}.

2
Extremal families

In this section, we state a result that shows that the Duke–Erdős problem for large
k=k(s,t)
and
n=n(k,s,t)
can be reduced to another extremal problem that is independent of
n,k
. First, we describe the corresponding extremal problem, together with an extremal family.

Denote
T=t\cdot\phi(s,t)
. Throughout this paper, we use
T
as the universal upper bound of
\support\mathcal{T}
for any
t
-uniform family
\mathcal{T}
without a sunflower of size
s
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{*}
be a family with the following properties:

1.

\mathcal{S}_{*}
does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
;

2.

\mathcal{S}_{*}\subset\bigcup_{q=t}^{T}\binom{\support\mathcal{S}^{(t)}_{*}}{q}
;

3.

a vector-valued function
\widetilde{\phi}(\mathcal{S}_{*})=(\widetilde{\phi}_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{*}),\ldots,\widetilde{\phi}_{T-t}(\mathcal{S}_{*}))
which components are defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}_{0}(\mathcal{S})

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{S}^{(t)}|,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S})

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{S}^{(t+i)}|+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}|\mathcal{S}^{(t+j)}|\binom{T-|\support\mathcal{S}^{(t)}|}{i-j};

is maximal in the lexicographical order over all choices of
\mathcal{S}_{*}
satisfying properties
1
-
2
. Here we assume that
\binom{m}{a}=0
if
m<a
.

Then, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{*}=\left\{F\in\binom{[n]}{k}\,:\,F\cap\support\mathcal{S}^{(t)}_{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{*}\right\}.

(1)

Let us show that
\mathcal{F}_{*}
does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size
t-1
. Suppose that such a sunflower
F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}\in\mathcal{F}
exists. Then,
F\cap\support\mathcal{S}^{(t)}_{*},\ldots,F_{s}\cap\support\mathcal{S}^{(t)}_{*}
form a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
. But
F_{i}\cap\support\mathcal{S}^{(t)}_{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{*}
, contradicting property
1
.

Naturally, we know very little of the possible structure of
\mathcal{S}^{*}
, except in some special cases. It is well possible that the family
\mathcal{S}_{*}
is not unique. However, we are still able to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
k\geqslant(t+T)(t-1)
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\binom{[n]}{k}
be an extremal family that does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size
t-1
. Then,
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{*}
for some
\mathcal{S}_{*}
satisfying properties
1
-
3
, provided
n
is large enough as a function of
s,k
.

The proof of the theorem is presented in Section
7
.

Let us comment on the property
3
. As a part of our analysis, we will prove that for any extremal family
\mathcal{F}
there is a family
\mathcal{S}
without a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
such that
\mathcal{F}
is almost fully contained in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{S}}
, see Lemma
5.3
. The representation

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{S}}|=\sum_{i=0}^{T-t}|\mathcal{S}^{(t+i)}|\binom{n-|\support\mathcal{S}^{(t)}|}{k-t-i},

is not convenient for describing extremal families as
|\support\mathcal{S}^{(t)}|
may vary for different
\mathcal{S}
. To overcome this obstacle, WLOG assume for a while that
\support\mathcal{S}^{(t)}\subset[T]
and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{i}_{\mathcal{S}}=\left\{G\in\binom{[T]}{t+i}\,:\,G\cap\support\mathcal{S}^{(t)}\in\mathcal{S}\right\}.

Then, we clearly have
|\mathcal{G}^{i}_{\mathcal{S}}|=\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S})
and

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{S}}|=\sum_{i=0}^{\min\{T-t,k-t\}}|\mathcal{G}^{i}_{\mathcal{S}}|\binom{n-T}{k-i-t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\min\{T-t,k-t\}}\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S})\binom{n-T}{k-i-t}.

This yields two simple, but important corollaries. First, it implies that for any
\mathcal{S}_{*}
satisfying
1
-
3
,
\mathcal{F}_{*}
defined by (
1
) is an extremal family, provided
k\geqslant(t+T)(t-1)
. Second, we have the following asymptotic expansion, which will be used in our analysis.

Proposition 2.2
.

Fix some
q
,
t\leqslant q\leqslant\min\{T,k\}
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a family satisfying
1
-
2
with
\mathcal{S}
in place of
\mathcal{S}_{*}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{S}}|=\sum_{i=0}^{q-t}\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S})\binom{n-T}{k-t-i}+O_{s,k}(n^{-1})\binom{n}{k-q}.

We would like to mention that the reduction to the problem of maximizing
\widetilde{\phi}(\cdot)
subject to a property such as avoiding a sunflower is a general phenomenon. In our upcoming paper
[
41
]
, we will show that the same reduction can be obtained for a large class of Turan-type problems.

We start this section by describing the isoperimetric inequality for expander graphs. Given a

-regular graph
G=(V,E)
and a subset of its vertices

, we denote by
\partial S
the set of edges

such that

, i.e., the edges

that connect vertices of

with vertices of

. Define the Laplacian

of the graph

as
L_{G}=d\cdot I_{|V|}-A_{G}
, where

stands for the adjacency matrix of

Lemma 3.1

(Theorem 20.1.1 from
[

]
)
.

Let
\lambda_{2}(L_{G})
be the second smallest eigenvalue of

. Then, for any

of size at most

, we have

\displaystyle|\partial S|\geqslant\lambda_{2}(L_{G})|S|/2.

To prove Theorem
1.1

]
. Although they did not explicitly state the uniqueness of extremal examples, it can be easily deduced from their proof.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

Finally, we need the Kruskal–Katona theorem in the Lovász form.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\geqslant(1-\delta)\phi(s,t)\binom{n}{k-t}.

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}}|\mathcal{F}_{D}|+\sum_{D\in\binom{[n]}{k-t}\setminus\mathcal{D}}|\mathcal{F}_{D}|\leqslant\phi(s,t)|\mathcal{D}|+(\phi(s,t)-1)\left(\binom{n}{k-t}-|\mathcal{D}|\right).

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(1-\delta-O_{s,k}(1/n)\right)\phi(s,t)\binom{n}{k-t}-(\phi(s,t)-1)\binom{n}{k-t}

\displaystyle|D_{1}\cap D_{2}|=k-t-1\geqslant t-1.

\displaystyle=\{F\in\mathcal{F}\,:\,|F\cap\mathbf{S}|=t+i\},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\left\{S\in\binom{|\mathbf{S}|}{t+i}\,:\,|\mathcal{F}_{i}(S)|\geqslant sk\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-t-i-1}\right\}

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i,S}^{*}

\displaystyle=\bigg\{D\in\binom{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S}}{k-t-i}\,:\,\forall p\in[t-1]\;\forall X\in\binom{D}{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad|\mathcal{F}_{i}(S\sqcup X)|\geqslant sk\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|-|X|}{k-t-i-|X|-1}\bigg\},\quad S\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i},

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{S\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}}\left(\{S\}\vee\{\cap_{S\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{i,S}\}\right).

(10)

Informally, we include in
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}
only those sets
F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
for which any subset of
F
containing their intersection with
\mathbf{S}
and having at most
t-1
elements outside
\mathbf{S}
has high degree in
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. This yields the following partition of
\mathcal{F}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{S}_{i-1}}\sqcup\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}\sqcup(\mathcal{F}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i})\sqcup\mathcal{O}_{i}\sqcup\mathcal{R}_{i-1},

where
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{i}=\{F\in\mathcal{F}\,:\,|F\cap\mathbf{S}|\geqslant t+i+1\}.

Clearly, we have
|\mathcal{O}_{i}|\leqslant 2^{|\mathbf{S}|}\binom{n}{k-t-i-1}\leqslant 2^{t\phi(s,t)}\binom{n}{k-t-i-1}=O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n-t}{k-t}
.
Let us bound
|\mathcal{F}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}|
. For each set
F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}
, there are two options:

1.

F\cap\mathbf{S}\not\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
;

2.

F\cap\mathbf{S}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
and
F\setminus\mathbf{S}\not\in\mathcal{D}^{*}_{i,S}
for some
S\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
, i.e., there exist a number
p\in[t-1]
and a set
X\subset F\setminus\mathbf{S}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{i}(S\sqcup X)\leqslant sk\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|-|X|}{k-t-i-|X|-1}
.

For each
S\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{i,p,S}=\bigg\{X\in\binom{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S}}{p}\,:\,\mathcal{F}_{i}(S\sqcup X)<sk\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|-|X|}{k-t-i-|X|-1}\bigg\}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{S\in\binom{\mathbf{S}}{t+i}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}}|\mathcal{F}_{i}[S]|+\sum_{S\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}}\sum_{p=1}^{t-1}|\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{E}_{i,p,S}]|

\displaystyle\leqslant sk\binom{|\mathbf{S}|}{t+i}\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-t-i-1}+sk\sum_{S\in\binom{\mathbf{S}}{t+i}}\sum_{p=1}^{t-1}|\mathcal{E}_{i,p,S}|\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|-p}{k-t-i-p-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n-t}{k-t}+sk\binom{|\mathbf{S}|}{t+i}\sum_{p=1}^{t-1}\binom{n}{p}\binom{n}{k-t-i-p-1}

\displaystyle=O_{s,k}\left(n^{-i-1}\right)\binom{n-t}{k-t}.

This yields the following bound on
|\mathcal{F}\setminus(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{S}_{i-1}}\sqcup\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i})|
:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}\setminus(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{S}_{i-1}}\sqcup\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i})|

\displaystyle\leqslant|\mathcal{F}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}|+|\mathcal{O}_{i}|+|\mathcal{R}_{i-1}|

\displaystyle=O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n-t}{k-t}+O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n-t}{k-t}+O_{s,k}\left(n^{-\frac{k-t(t-1)}{t-1}}\right)\binom{n-t}{k-t}

\displaystyle=O_{s,k}\left(n^{-i-1}\right)\binom{n-t}{k-t},

(11)

where we used
k\geqslant(q+1)(t-1)
in the third equality.

Step 5. Upper bound on
|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}|
.
In order to analyze the structure in
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}
, we use the decomposition

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}|=\sum_{D\in\binom{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S}}{k-t-i}}|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)|.

Our ultimate goal in that respect is to show that almost all
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}(D)
are, in fact, the same. This common family will then be added to
\mathcal{S}_{i-1}
in order to form
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(t+i)}
.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i-1}=

\displaystyle\bigg\{D\in\binom{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S}}{k-t-i}\,:\,\forall S\in\mathcal{S}_{i-1}\;\forall p\in[t-1]\;\forall X\in\binom{D}{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad|\mathcal{F}(S\sqcup X,\mathbf{S}\sqcup X)|\geqslant sk\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|-p}{k-p-|S|-1}\bigg\}.

(12)

By property
2
we have
|\mathcal{F}(S)|=(1-O_{s,k}(1/n))\binom{n}{k-|S|}
for each
S\in\mathcal{S}_{i-1}
. This allows us to bound
|\mathcal{D}_{i-1}|
from below. We state it as a separate proposition. Its proof is postponed to Section
5.2
.

Proposition 5.5
.

We have

\displaystyle\left|\binom{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S}}{k-t-i}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{i-1}\right|=O_{s,k}(1/n)\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-t-i}

The proposition implies

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{D\in\binom{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S}}{k-t-i}}|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{i-1}}|\mathcal{F}^{*}(D)|+\binom{|\mathbf{S}|}{t+i}\cdot\left|\binom{[n]\setminus|\mathbf{S}|}{k-t-i}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{i-1}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{i-1}}|\mathcal{F}^{*}(D)|+O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n-t}{k-t}.

(13)

To bound
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)
for each
D\in\mathcal{D}_{i-1}
, we introduce the following quantity:

\displaystyle\phi_{i}=\max\bigg\{|\mathcal{Q}|\,:\,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}\subset\binom{\mathbf{S}}{t+i}\text{ and }

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i-1}\cup\mathcal{Q}\text{ does not contain a sunflower with $s$ petals}

\displaystyle\text{and the core of size at most $t-1$}\bigg\}.

We claim that
|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)|\leqslant\phi_{i}
. It is enough to show that
\mathcal{S}_{i-1}\sqcup\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)
does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
. Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{s}
be such a sunflower. Define
\ell=t-1-|\cap_{i}T_{i}|
. WLOG, we assume that
T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}\in\mathcal{S}_{i-1}
and
T_{p+1},\ldots,T_{s}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)
. Note that
T_{j}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
for
j>p
by the definition of
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}
, so
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(T_{p})\geqslant sk\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-t-i-1}
by the definition (
8
) of
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
. Consider two cases.

Case 1. We have
\ell=0
.
We rely on the following standard proposition, which proof is postponed to Section
5.3
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Consider the families
\mathcal{A}_{1}\subset\binom{U}{k_{i}},\mathcal{A}_{2}\subset\binom{U}{k_{2}},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{s}\subset\binom{U}{k_{s}}
such that
|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\geqslant\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}\right)\binom{|U|}{k_{i}-1}
. Then, there exist disjoint
F_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,F_{s}\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
.

The constructed family
\mathcal{S}_{i-1}
satisfies property
2
, so
|\mathcal{F}(T_{j},\mathbf{S})|\geqslant sk\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-|T_{p}|-1}
for
j\in[p]
, provided
n
is large enough. Applying Proposition
5.6
with
U=[n]\setminus\mathbf{S}
and
k_{i}=k-|T_{i}|
, we deduce that there are disjoint sets
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}(T_{1},\mathbf{S}),\ldots,F_{s}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(T_{s})
. Note that
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(T_{s})=\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(T_{s},\mathbf{S})
by the definition (
8
) of
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}\ni T_{s}
and the construction (
10
) of
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}
. Hence,
\mathcal{F}
contains a sunflower
T_{1}\sqcup F_{1},\ldots,T_{s}\sqcup F_{s}
with
s
petals and the core of size
t-1
.

Case 2. We have
\ell>0
.
Then, choose any
X\in\binom{D}{\ell}
. Note that for any
S\in\mathcal{F}^{*}(D)
, we have
|\mathcal{F}^{*}(S\sqcup X)|\geqslant\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|-|X|}{k-t-i-|X|-1}
by the definition (
9
) of
\mathcal{D}_{i,S}^{*}
. In particular,
|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(T_{j}\sqcup X)|\geqslant sk\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|-|X|}{k-t-i-|X|-1}
for
j=p+1,\ldots,s
. Since
D\in\mathcal{D}_{i-1}
, we have
|\mathcal{F}(T_{j}\sqcup X,\mathbf{S}\sqcup X)|\geqslant sk\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|-|X|}{k-t-i-|X|-1}
for all
j\in[p]
. Then, we apply Proposition
5.6
again, and find disjoint
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}(T_{1}\sqcup X,\mathbf{S}\sqcup X),\ldots,F_{s}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(T_{s}\sqcup X)
. Hence,
\mathcal{F}
contains a sunflower
X\sqcup T_{1}\sqcup F_{1},\ldots,X\sqcup T_{s}\sqcup F_{s}
with
s
petals and the core of size
t-1
, a contradiction.

Thus,
\mathcal{S}_{i-1}\sqcup\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)
does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
. Therefore,
|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)|\leqslant\phi_{i}
, and

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}|\leqslant\phi_{i}\cdot\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-t-i}+O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n-t}{k-t}.

(15)

Step 6. Deriving the value of
\phi_{i}
.
We claim that

\displaystyle\phi_{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}|\mathcal{S}^{(t+j)}_{i-1}|\binom{T-|\mathbf{S}|}{i-j}=\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{*}),

(16)

where
\widetilde{\phi}_{j},\mathcal{S}_{*}
were defined in Section
2
. First, we show that

\displaystyle\phi_{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}|\mathcal{S}^{(t+j)}_{i-1}|\binom{T-|\mathbf{S}|}{i-j}\leqslant\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{*}).

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be any family for which the maximum in the definition (
14
) of
\phi_{i}
is attained. Then, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{i-1}\sqcup\mathcal{Q})=\phi_{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}|\mathcal{S}^{(t+j)}|\binom{T-|\mathbf{S}|}{i-j}.

(17)

By induction, the family
\mathcal{S}_{i-1}
satisfies property
4
, and so we have
\widetilde{\phi}_{j}(\mathcal{S}_{i-1}\sqcup\mathcal{Q})=\widetilde{\phi}_{j}(\mathcal{S}_{i-1})=\widetilde{\phi}_{j}(\mathcal{S}_{*})
for all
j\in[i-1]
. By the lexicographic maximality of
\widetilde{\phi}(\mathcal{S}_{*})
, we have
\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{i-1}\sqcup\mathcal{Q})\leqslant\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{*})
, so (
17
) follows. It remains to prove that

\displaystyle\phi_{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}|\mathcal{S}^{(t+j)}_{i-1}|\binom{T-|\mathbf{S}|}{i-j}\geqslant\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{*}).

Recall the definition (
1
) of
\mathcal{F}_{*}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is extremal, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\geqslant|\mathcal{F}_{*}|=\sum_{r=0}^{i}\widetilde{\phi}_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{*})\binom{n-T}{k-t-r}+O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n}{k-t},

(18)

where we used Proposition
2.2
for the equality. Using Proposition
2.2
for
\mathcal{S}_{i-1}
with
q=t+i
,
we also have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{S}_{i-1}}|=\sum_{r=0}^{i}\widetilde{\phi}_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{i-1})\binom{n-T}{k-t-r}+O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n}{k-t}

(19)

Next, we combine the above.

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{i}\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{*})\binom{n-T}{k-t-r}+O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n}{k-t}\overset{\eqref{eq: extremality condition}}{\leqslant}|\mathcal{F}|\overset{\eqref{eq: removing raminder on i-th layer}}{\leqslant}|\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{S}_{i-1}}|+|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}|+O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n}{k-t}

(20)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\overset{\eqref{eq: upper bound on Fi star},\eqref{eqsi-1}}{\leqslant}\sum_{r=0}^{i}\widetilde{\phi}_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{i-1})\binom{n-T}{k-t-r}+\phi_{i}\binom{n}{k-t-i}+O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n}{k-t}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt=\sum_{r=0}^{i-1}\widetilde{\phi}_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{i-1})\binom{n-T}{k-t-r}+\left(\phi_{i}+\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{i-1})\right)\binom{n}{k-t-i}+O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n}{k-t},

Using again that
\widetilde{\phi}_{j}(\mathcal{S}_{i-1})=\widetilde{\phi}_{j}(\mathcal{S}_{*})
for
j\in[i-1]
due to property
4
and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{i-1})=\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}|\mathcal{S}^{(t+j)}_{i-1}|\binom{T-|\mathbf{S}|}{i-j},

(21)

we deduce (
16
), provided
n
is large enough.

Finally, we can lower bound
|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}|.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: lower bound on F-S and F-i star},\eqref{eqphi}}{\geqslant}\left(\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{*})-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}|\mathcal{S}_{i-1}^{(t+j)}|\binom{T-|\mathbf{S}|}{i-j}\right)\binom{n-T}{k-t-i}-O_{s,k}\left(n^{-i-1}\right)\binom{n}{k-t}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: phi-i equality}}{=}\phi_{i}\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-t-i}-O_{s,k}(n^{-i-1})\binom{n}{k-t}.

(22)

Step 7. Applying isoperimetry.
This part follows the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}^{*}=\bigcap_{S\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{i,S}.

Note that
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}(D)
is non-empty iff
D\in\mathcal{D}^{*}_{i}
by (
10
). We may refine the bound (
13
):

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{i-1}}|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)|+O_{s,k}(1/n)\binom{n}{k-t-i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{i-1}\cap\mathcal{D}^{*}_{i}}|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)|+O_{s,k}(1/n)\binom{n}{k-t-i}.

Recall that
|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)|\leqslant\phi_{i}
. Let

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}=\{D\in\mathcal{D}_{i}^{*}\cap\mathcal{D}_{i-1}\,:\,|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)|=\phi_{i}\}.

From the lower bound (
22
), we derive

\displaystyle\phi_{i}\cdot|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}|+(\phi_{i}-1)\cdot\left|\binom{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S}}{k-t-i}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}\right|\geqslant\phi_{i}\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-t-i}-O_{s,k}(1/n)\cdot\binom{n}{k-t-i},

and so

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}|\geqslant\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-t-i}-O_{s,k}(1/n)\binom{n}{k-t-i}.

(23)

Consider the Jonson graph
J_{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S},k-t-i}
with the vertex set
\binom{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S}}{k-t-i}
and two sets
D_{1},D_{2}
being adjacent iff
|D_{1}\cap D_{2}|=k-t-i-1
. As in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we claim that if
D_{1},D_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
are adjacent, then
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D_{1})=\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D_{2})
. Otherwise
|\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D_{1})\cup\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D_{2})|\geqslant\phi_{i}+1
, so by the definition (
14
) of
\phi_{i}
, there exists a sunflower
T_{1},\ldots,T_{s}\in\mathcal{S}_{i-1}\cup\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D_{1})\cup\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D_{2})
with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
. WLOG, we assume that
T_{1},\ldots,T_{p}\in\mathcal{S}_{i-1}
and
T_{p+1},\ldots,T_{s}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D_{1})\cup\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D_{2})
. Let
\ell=t-1-|\cap_{i}T_{i}|
and consider two cases.

Case 1. We have
\ell=0
.
Then, we have
|\mathcal{F}(T_{j},\mathbf{S})|\geqslant(1-O_{s,k}(1/n))\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-|T_{j}|}
for

by property
2
of the lemma and

. Hence, by Proposition
5.6
, there exist disjoint sets

, so

contains a sunflower

, a contradiction.

Case 2. We have
\ell>0
.
Since
k\geqslant(q+t+1)(t-1)\geqslant t+i+t-1
, there exists
X\subset D_{1}\cap D_{2}
of size
\ell
. Since
D_{1},D_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}\subset\mathcal{D}_{i}^{*}\cap\mathcal{D}_{i-1}
, we have
|\mathcal{F}(T_{j}\sqcup X,\mathbf{S}\sqcup X)|\geqslant sk\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-|T_{j}|-|X|-1}
for

by the definition of (

) of
\mathcal{D}_{i-1}
and
|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}(T_{j}\sqcup X,\mathbf{S}\sqcup X)|\geqslant sk\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|-|X|}{k-t-i-|X|-1}
for

(cf. (

. Hence,
\mathcal{F}
contains a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size

, a contradiction.

The above implies
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}(D_{1})=\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}(D_{2})
, so for each connected component
\mathcal{C}
of the subgraph
J_{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S},k-t-i}[\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}]
there exists a family
\mathcal{Q}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}(D)=\mathcal{Q}
for any

, and
\mathcal{S}_{i-1}\sqcup\mathcal{Q}
does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
. Let
\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}]
be the set of connected components of

and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
be the largest connected component of
J_{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S},k-t-i}[\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}]
. For each
\mathcal{C}\in\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}]\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
, edges of

connect vertices of
\mathcal{C}
and
\binom{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S}}{k-t-i}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
. Also, we have
|\mathcal{C}|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-t-i}
. Recall that the spectral gap of

is
(n-|\mathbf{S}|)
(see (
4
)), so Lemma
3.1
yields:

\displaystyle\frac{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{2}|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}|

\displaystyle=\frac{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{2}\sum_{\mathcal{C}\in\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}]\setminus\{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\}}|\mathcal{C}|\leqslant\sum_{\mathcal{C}\in\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}]\setminus\{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\}}|\partial\mathcal{C}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\Delta(J_{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S},k-t-i})\cdot\left|\binom{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S}}{k-t-i}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}\right|,

where
\Delta(J_{[n]\setminus\mathbf{S},k-t-i})=(n-|\mathbf{S}|)(k-t-i)
is the largest degree of

. Using (

), we derive

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}|=O_{s,k}(1/n)\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-t-i},

(24)

so
|\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}|\geqslant(1-O_{s,k}(1/n))\binom{n-|\mathbf{S}|}{k-t-i}
. Since
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}(D)
is the same family for all

, set
\mathcal{T}_{i}=\mathcal{F}^{*}_{i}(D)
for any
D\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
. Then, set
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\mathcal{S}_{i-1}\sqcup\mathcal{T}_{i}
. Note that by construction,
\mathcal{S}_{i}
does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
, so property
1
holds; moreover,
\mathcal{S}_{i}\subset 2^{\mathbf{S}}
, so property
3
follows. Since
\mathcal{C}_{i}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
, we have
|\mathcal{T}_{i}|=\phi_{i}
, so

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{i})

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{T}_{i}|+\sum_{r=0}^{i-1}|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(t+j)}|\binom{T-|\mathbf{S}|}{i-r}

\displaystyle=\phi_{i}+\sum_{r=0}^{i-1}|\mathcal{S}_{i-1}^{(t+j)}|\binom{T-|\mathbf{S}|}{i-r}=\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(\mathcal{S}_{*})

by (
16
). Since
\widetilde{\phi}_{j}(\mathcal{S}_{i})=\widetilde{\phi}_{j}(\mathcal{S}_{i-1})=\widetilde{\phi}_{j}(\mathcal{S}_{*}),j<i,
by the lexicographic maximality of
(\widetilde{\phi}_{j}(\mathcal{S}_{i-1}))_{j=0}^{i-1}
from property
4
, we derive that the vector

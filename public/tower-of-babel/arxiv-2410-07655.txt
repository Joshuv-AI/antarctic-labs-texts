Sobolev and Hölder estimates for the ∂ ¯ \overline{\partial} equation on pseudoconvex domains of finite type in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2}
Ziming Shi

Abstract
. We prove a homotopy formula which yields almost sharp estimates in all (positive-indexed) Sobolev and Hölder-Zygmund spaces for the ∂ ¯ \overline{\partial} equation on pseudoconvex domains of finite type in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} , extending the earlier results of Fefferman-Kohn (1988), Range (1990), and Chang-Nagel-Stein (1992). The main novelty of our proof is the construction of holomorphic support functions that admit precise estimates when the parameter variable lies in a thin shell outside the domain.

Introduction

The goal of the present paper is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

The study of global existence and regularity of the
\overline{\partial}
-equation on pseudoconvex domains is a fundamental problem in several complex variables. Since the early 1960s, two parallel schools of research have developed to solve the
\overline{\partial}
equation. The first one is by solving the
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann problem. On any pseudoconvex domain of
C^{\infty}
boundary, one can define the
L^{2}
canonical solution
\overline{\partial}^{\ast}\mathcal{N}\varphi
, where
\mathcal{N}
is the operator that solves the
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann boundary value problem. The solution
\overline{\partial}^{\ast}\mathcal{N}\varphi
is called the canonical solution since it is the unique solution which is orthogonal to
\operatorname{Ker}(\overline{\partial})
under the
L^{2}
inner product. The first global regularity result was obtained by Kohn
[
Koh64
]
, who showed that on a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
C^{\infty}
boundary,
\overline{\partial}^{\ast}\mathcal{N}
is a bounded operator from
H^{s,2}(\Omega)
to
H^{s+\frac{1}{2},2}(\Omega)
for
s\geq 0
. Later on in their monograph
[
GS77
]
, Greiner and Stein proves the boundedness
\overline{\partial}^{\ast}\mathcal{N}:H^{s,p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\to H^{s+\frac{1}{2},p}(\Omega)
for
s\geq 0
, and
\overline{\partial}^{\ast}\mathcal{N}:\Lambda^{r}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\to\Lambda^{r+\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)
for
r>0
. Chang
[
Cha89
]
extends the
(Sobolev space) result of Greiner-Stein to any
(p,q)
forms.

The second approach for solving the
\overline{\partial}
equation – the one which we will follow in this paper – is to look for solution operators in the form of integral formula. This can be viewed as the higher dimensional generalizations of the Cauchy-Green operator which solves the
\overline{\partial}
equation in
\mathbb{C}
. The method of integral formula has several advantages. It is more geometric in nature; requires less boundary regularity; and gives
L^{\infty}
estimate that is not accessible by the
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann method. The theory was
pioneered by Grauert and Lieb, and independently, by Henkin, and has been developed extensively on many classes of pseudoconvex domains. We mention a few notable works in this direction.

On a strictly pseudoconvex domain with
C^{2}
boundary, Henkin and Ramirez
[
RH71
]
constructed a solution operator that is bounded from
C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
to
C^{\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{\Omega})
. Assuming the boundary is
C^{k+2}
for positive integers
k
, Siu
[
Siu74
]
and Lieb-Range
[
LR80
]
constructed solution operators that is bounded from
C^{k}(\overline{\Omega})
to
C^{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{\Omega})
. More recently, Gong
[
Gon19
]
showed that under the minimal smoothness of
C^{2}
boundary, there exists a solution operator that is bounded from
\Lambda^{r}(\overline{\Omega})
to
\Lambda^{r+\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{\Omega})
, for any
r>1
. In
[
SY24b
]
, the authors constructed a solution operator which is bounded from
H^{s,p}(\overline{\Omega})\to H^{s+\frac{1}{2},p}(\overline{\Omega})
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
, under the assumption that
b\Omega\in C^{\infty}
. For convex domains of finite type, sharp
estimates have been obtained by
[
DFFs99
]
,
[
Ale06
]
and
[
Yao24
]
.

Pseudoconvex domains of finite type in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
was introduced by Kohn
[
Koh72
]
, as a natural generalization of strict pseudoconvexity. Kohn showed that if the domain has type

(which must be even due to pseudoconvexity), then the
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann problem is subelliptic of order

\overline{\partial}^{\ast}\mathcal{N}:H^{s,2}\to H^{s+\frac{1}{m},2}
follows. Using microlocal analysis, Fefferman and Kohn
[

proved the boundedness
\overline{\partial}^{\ast}\mathcal{N}:C^{s}(\overline{\Omega})\to C^{s+1/m}(\overline{\Omega})
for any

is not an integer, and they also proved the sup norm estimate
\overline{\partial}^{\ast}\mathcal{N}:L^{\infty}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)

[

]
used method similar to the ones in
[

to show that
\overline{\partial}^{\ast}\mathcal{N}
is bounded from

to

the boundedness of the operator

for any

The method of integral formula also plays an important role in the study of
\overline{\partial}
equation on
pseudoconvex domains of finite type in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. As in the case of strict pseudoconvexity, the success of this method depends largely on solving a holomorphic division problem

h_{1}(z,\zeta)(z_{1}-\zeta_{1})+h_{2}(z,\zeta)(z_{2}-\zeta_{2})=\Phi(z,\zeta).

Here the functions
h_{i}
are called
Leray maps
and has the important property that they are holomorphic in
z\in\Omega
. The function
\Phi
– called the
holomorphic support function
– is non-vanishing for
z\in\Omega
and
\zeta\in b\Omega

[
KN73
]
constructed an example – a finite type pseudoconvex domain on which such function
\Phi
does not exist while vanishing at
z=\zeta\in b\Omega
. Thus in general one cannot expect to obtain
h_{1},h_{2}
that are bounded uniformly for
(z,\zeta)\in(\Omega,b\Omega)
. Nevertheless, on a class of domains in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
which include the Kohn-Nirenberg example, Fornaess
[
Fs86
]
proved the existence of
h_{1},h_{2}
with
\Phi\equiv 1
and
h_{i}
,
i=1,2
satisfying a certain weighted integral estimate, which then allows him to prove the sup-norm estimate for the
\overline{\partial}
equation on such domains. Belanger
[
Bel93
]
then refined Fornaess’ method and obtained some (far from optimal) Hölder estimates for
\overline{\partial}
on the same class of domains.

In
[
Ran90
]
, Range constructed for each
\eta>0
an integral solution operator

that is bounded from
L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
to

[

]
and
[
CNS92
]
, but Range’s proof is much simpler and geometric; it is based on the earlier work of Catlin

\int_{D_{p}}\frac{|h^{\eta}_{i}(z,p)|^{2}}{|z-p|^{2}}\operatorname{dist}(z,D_{p})^{2\eta}\,dV(z)<C_{D,\eta},\quad\eta>0.

The domain

is pseudoconvex and touches

.
In Range’s proof, the functions

is defined only for

.
For our proof of Theorem
1.1
, we construct the functions

which are defined for

More specifically, we show that for each

, there is a pseudoconvex domain

with

,

, and whose boundary is as far away from

as possible, as measured by certain non-isotropic polydisks with centers in

. The polydisks we use are from
[

]
with some slight modification. Roughly speaking, the size of these polydisks are determined by the distance of its center and that of
q
to
bD
. Once we have the

which are holomorphic in

z

for

and

There is an additional problem of how

depends on

. This problem arises since we need to integrate

in the second variable in our solution operator. Here as in Range, we replace

with a smooth function

, by restricting

in an interior domain
D^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\Subset D
,
D^{\prime}_{\epsilon}
are all relatively compact in
D
and approximate
D
from inside as
\varepsilon\to 0
. We can then modify the function
h_{i}(z,\zeta)
to a smooth function in
\zeta
by restricting
\zeta
to a small ball centered at
q
and whose radius shrinks to
0
as
\epsilon
. In our case we need to control this radius, which can be estimated using the sup norm of

on
\overline{D}_{\epsilon}
. We show that the radius is small compared to
r(q)+\epsilon
. This allows us to estimate the error arising from the switching of
q
to
\zeta
, and in the end we obtain a modified

which is holomorphic in

and smooth in
\zeta
in
\mathcal{U}\setminus D

We can now solve the
\overline{\partial}
equation on each approximating domain

, using the homotopy operator constructed in
[
Gon19
]
and
[
SY25
]
. The resulting estimate does not depend on

as

by taking limits in suitable function spaces. We mention two auxiliary results which play crucial roles in our proof: the Hardy-Littlewood lemma for Sobolev and Hölder-Zygmund spaces (Proposition

) which reduces the problem to estimating a weighted integral norm; and the commutator estimate (Proposition

[

, which allows us to harness the decay properties of the commutator term in the integral operator and essentially dispense with the need for integration by parts.

As in Range
[
Ran90
]
, the arbitrary small loss of regularity comes from the integrability condition in Skoda’s theorem, and it seems that the current method is inadequate to remove this loss.

2.
Function spaces

In this section, we recall some basic results for the Sobolev space
H^{s,p}(\Omega)
and the Hölder-Zygmund space

Definition 2.1

(Hölder-Zygmund space on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
)
.

The Hölder-Zygmund space on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, denoted by
\Lambda^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is defined as follows

•

For
0<s<1
,
\Lambda^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
consists of all
f\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\|f\|_{\Lambda^{s}(U)}:=\sup\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f|+\sup\limits_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}<\infty.

•

\Lambda^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
consists of all
f\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\|f\|_{\Lambda^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\sup\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f|+\sup\limits_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)+f(y)-2f(\frac{x+y}{2})|}{|x-y|}<\infty.

•

For
s>1
recursively,
\Lambda^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
consists of all
f\in\Lambda^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\nabla f\in\Lambda^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We define
\|f\|_{\Lambda^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\|f\|_{\Lambda^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|D_{j}f\|_{\Lambda^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.

•

We define
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\bigcap_{s>0}\Lambda^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to be the space of bounded smooth functions.

Definition 2.2

(Hölder-Zygmund space on domains)

Let

|f|_{\Lambda^{s}(U)}:=\inf_{\widetilde{f}\in\Lambda^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\>\widetilde{f}|_{\Omega}=f}|\widetilde{f}|_{\Lambda^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Definition 2.10

Let
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a
special Lipschitz domain
of the form
\omega=\{(x^{\prime},x_{N}):x_{N}>g(x^{\prime})\}
for some
g:\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}<L
.

The
Rychkov’s universal extension operator

E=E_{\omega}
for
\omega
is given as follows:

(2.3)

\mathcal{E}_{\omega}f:=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\cdot(\phi_{j}\ast f)),\qquad f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\omega).

Here
(\psi_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
and
(\phi_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
are families of Schwartz functions that satisfy the following properties:

(2.4)

Scaling condition
:
\phi_{j}(x)=2^{(j-1)N}\phi_{1}(2^{j-1}x)
and
\psi_{j}(x)=2^{(j-1)N}\psi_{1}(2^{j-1}x)
for
j\geq 2
.

(2.5)

Moment condition
:
\int\phi_{0}=\int\psi_{0}=1
,
\int x^{\alpha}\phi_{0}(x)dx=\int x^{\alpha}\psi_{0}(x)dx=0
for all multi-indices
|\alpha|>0
, and
\int x^{\alpha}\phi_{1}(x)dx=\int x^{\alpha}\psi_{1}(x)dx=0
for all
|\alpha|\geq 0
.

(2.6)

Approximate identity
:
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}\ast\phi_{j}=\delta_{0}
is the Direc delta measure.

(2.7)

Support condition
:
\phi_{j},\psi_{j}
are all supported in the negative cone
-\mathbb{K}^{L}:=\{(x^{\prime},x_{N}):x_{N}<-L|x^{\prime}|\}
.

We call
(\phi,\psi)
a
\mathbb{K}^{L}
-Littlewood-Paley pair
.

Note that condition
(2.7)
implies that there exists some
c_{0}>0
such that
\operatorname{supp}\phi_{0}\subset\{x_{N}<-c_{0}^{\prime}\}
. By the scaling condition
(2.4)
, we have

(2.8)

\operatorname{supp}\phi_{j}\subset\{x_{N}<-c_{0}2^{-j}\},\quad c_{0}=2c_{0}^{\prime}.

Since a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
is locally a special Lipschitz domain, we can apply a partition of unity and patch together the extension operators for the special Lipschitz domains to obtain an Rychkov extension operator
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
for
\Omega
. We omit the exact expression for
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
(see for example
[
SY25
, (3.3)]
) and we simply note that
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
have the same properties as
\mathcal{E}_{\omega}
.

Given a family
\phi=(\phi_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
satisfying properties
(2.4)
-
(2.7)
, we can define the following intrinsic norm:

\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}^{s,in}_{pq}(\phi)}:=\|(2^{js}\lambda_{j}\ast f)_{j=0}^{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{q})}=\left\|\big\|(2^{js}\lambda_{j}\ast f)_{j=0}^{\infty}\big\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N})}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

For a fixed
\phi
, we define
\mathscr{F}_{p,q}^{s,in}(\phi)
as the space of distributions
f
on
\omega
with finite norm
\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}^{s,in}_{pq}(\phi)}
. By using partition of unity, we can similarly define the space
\mathscr{F}_{p,q}^{s,in}(\Omega)
for any bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
.

[

]
proves the following remarkable extension theorem.

Proposition 2.12

(Rychkov)
.

Let

or

.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

Definition 2.18

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
R_{0}\in Z_{+}
and let
\omega=\{x_{N}<g(x^{\prime})\}
be a special Lipschitz domain with
\|\nabla g\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq L-2^{-R_{0}}
. Then for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

\bigl[-\mathbb{K}^{L}\cap\{x_{N}<-2^{-j}\}\bigr]+\{x_{N}-g(x^{\prime})<a\}\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{N}-g(x^{\prime})<a-bL^{-1}2^{-R_{0}-j}\}.

Proof.

Let
u\in\{x_{N}-g(x^{\prime})<a\}
and
v\in-\mathbb{K}^{L}\cap\{x_{N}<-b2^{-j}\}
, for
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
b>0
. Then
u_{N}-g(u^{\prime})<a
and
v_{N}<-\max(L|v^{\prime}|,b2^{-j})
. Using
\sup|\nabla\rho|\leq L-2^{-R_{0}}
, we get

\displaystyle u_{N}+v_{N}-g(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq u_{N}-g(u^{\prime})+v_{N}+|g(u^{\prime})-g(u^{\prime}+v^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq u_{N}-g(u^{\prime})+v_{N}+\bigl(L-2^{-R_{0}}\bigr)|v^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq a+bL^{-1}2^{-R_{0}}v_{N}\leq a-bL^{-1}2^{-R_{0}-j}.\qed

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s>0
. Then for any
f\in\mathscr{F}^{t}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
f\equiv 0
on
\overline{\Omega}^{c}
,

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,\delta^{-t})}\leq C_{\Omega,t}\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}^{t}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

The constant
C_{\Omega,t}
depends only on
t
and the Lipschitz norm of
\Omega
.

Proof.

The reader can find a proof in
[
Yao24
, Prop. 5.3]
. We include a slightly modified version here for the reader’s convenience. We will prove for the case
\Omega
is a special Lipschitz domain and the general case follows by standard partition of unity argument. Let
\omega=\{x_{N}<g(x^{\prime})\}
, where
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1})
. Assume that
|g|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\leq L
. Then

\omega+\left\{x_{N}>L|x^{\prime}|\right\}\subset\omega.

We partition
\Omega
into dyadic strips.

\omega=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}S_{k}:=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{N}):-2^{\frac{1}{2}-k}<x_{N}-g(x^{\prime})<-2^{-\frac{1}{2}-k}\right\}

Since
\mathbb{K}_{L}\subset\omega
, we can show that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+L^{2}}}|x_{N}-g(x^{\prime})|<\operatorname{dist}(x,b\omega)<|x_{N}-g(x^{\prime})|.

Denote
c_{L}:=(1+L^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Then

(2.9)

c_{L}2^{-\frac{1}{2}-k}<\operatorname{dist}(x,b\omega)<2^{\frac{1}{2}-k},\quad\text{for $x\in S_{k}$}.

Let
f
satisfy the hypothesis of lemma, and in particular
\operatorname{supp}f\in\omega
. By
(2.7)
and (
2.8
),
\operatorname{supp}\phi_{j}\subset-\mathbb{K}^{L}\cap\{x_{N}<-c_{0}2^{-j}\}
. In view of Lemma
2.21
, there exists
R_{0}>0
such that

\operatorname{supp}\phi_{j}+\omega\subset\bigl[-\mathbb{K}^{L}\cap\{x_{N}<-c_{0}2^{-j}\}\bigr]+\{x_{N}-g(x^{\prime})<0\}\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{N}<-c_{0}L^{-1}2^{-R_{0}-j}\}.

By
(2.6)
we have
f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi_{j}\ast f
. Observe that

\operatorname{supp}(\phi_{j}\ast f)=\operatorname{supp}\phi_{j}+\operatorname{supp}f\subset 2^{-j}+\omega\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{N}<-c_{0}L^{-1}2^{-R_{0}-j}\},

which is disjoint from
S_{k}
if
-c_{0}L^{-1}2^{-R_{0}-j}<-2^{\frac{1}{2}-k}
, or
j<k-R_{0}-\frac{1}{2}+\log_{2}(c_{0}L^{-1})
. Let
R_{1}=\lceil R_{0}-\frac{1}{2}+\log_{2}(c_{0}L^{-1})\rceil
. Then
S_{k}\cap\operatorname{supp}(\phi_{j}\ast f)=\emptyset
if
j<k-R_{1}
. Consequently we have
f(x)=\sum_{j=k-R_{1}}^{\infty}\phi_{j}\ast f
for
x\in S_{k}
. Using (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega,\delta^{-t})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega,\delta^{-t})}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{L}^{-tp}2^{(k+\frac{1}{2})tp}\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(S_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{L}^{-tp}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{(k+\frac{1}{2})tp}\Big\|\sum_{j=k-R_{1}}^{\infty}|\phi_{j}\ast f|\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(S_{k})}

\displaystyle=c_{L}^{-tp}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\|\sum_{j=k-R_{1}}^{\infty}2^{(k+\frac{1}{2}-j)t}2^{jt}|\phi_{j}\ast f|\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(S_{k})}.

Let
l=j-k
, and the above expression is bounded by

\displaystyle c_{L}^{-tp}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\|\sum_{l=-R_{1}}^{\infty}2^{(1-l)t}2^{jt}|\phi_{j}\ast f|\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(S_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{L}^{-tp}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=-R_{1}}^{\infty}2^{(1-l)t}\Big\|\sup_{j\geq k-R_{1}}2^{jt}|\phi_{j}\ast f|\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(S_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{L}^{-tp}C_{R_{1}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\|\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jt}|\phi_{j}\ast f|\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega)}=C_{\omega,t}\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}^{t}_{p\infty}(\phi)}.

Here we use the convention
\phi_{j}\ast f=0
for
j\leq-1
. Notice that the constant
C_{\omega,t}
blows up as
L,t\to\infty
.
∎

\mathring{\mathscr{F}}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

.

Proposition 2.24
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
with
s\leq k
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and denote by
\delta_{b\Omega}(x)
the distance from

to the boundary
b\Omega
.

(1)

There exists a constant
C
such that for all

with
\|\delta_{b\Omega}^{k-s}D^{k}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}<\infty
, the following inequality holds

(2.10)

\|u\|_{H^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\sum_{|\beta|\leq k}\|\delta_{b\Omega}^{k-s}D^{\beta}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(2)

There exists a constant
C
such that for all
u\in W^{k,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
with
\|\delta_{b\Omega}^{k-s}D^{k}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}<\infty

\|u\|_{\Lambda^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C\sum_{|\beta|\leq k}\|\delta_{b\Omega}^{k-s}D^{\beta}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

(i) See
[
SY24b
, Prop. A.2]
.

(ii) By Proposition
2.16
we have the following equivalence of norms:

|u|_{\Omega,s}\approx\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}|D^{\alpha}u|_{\Omega,s-k},\quad\forall\;s\in\mathbb{R},\;k\in\mathbb{N}.

It is clear that (
2.11
) is an immediate consequence of the following statement: for all
u\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
,

|u|_{\Omega,-t}\leq C\|\delta_{b\Omega}^{t}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\quad t>0.

By Proposition
2.23
, we have
\Lambda^{-t}(\Omega)=\mathscr{F}^{-t}_{\infty,\infty}(\Omega)=\bigl[\mathring{\mathscr{F}}^{t}_{1,1}(\Omega)\bigr]^{\prime}
, where
\mathring{\mathscr{F}}^{t}_{1,1}(\Omega)
denotes the norm closure of

in
\mathscr{F}^{t}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
By definition we see that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}^{t}_{1,\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jt}|\lambda_{j}\ast f|\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jt}|\lambda_{j}\ast f(x)|\,dx=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jt}\|\lambda_{j}\ast f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}^{t}_{1,1}(\Omega)}.

and
\mathring{\mathscr{F}}^{t}_{1,1}(\Omega)\subset\mathring{\mathscr{F}}^{t}_{1,\infty}(\Omega)

3.
Construction of the holomorphic support function

Let
D
be a bounded pseudoconvex domain with
C^{\infty}
boundary in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
of finite type
m
. Let
r
be the defining function of
D
such that
D=\{z\in U:r(z)<0\}
, where
U
is a neighborhood of the closure of
D
.
Fix
p_{0}\in bD
and a small neighborhood
U_{0}
of
p_{0}
.
By definition of finite type (
[
Koh72
,
BG77
]
),
m
is the least integer with the following property: if
h
is any germ about the origin of a holomorphic map from
\mathbb{C}
to
\mathbb{C}^{2}
with
h(0)=p_{0}
and
dh(0)\neq 0
, then
r\circ h
vanishes to order at most
m
at the origin.

For each
p\in U_{0}
, we introduce a special holomorphic coordinate system
\phi_{p}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
as in Catlin
[
Cat89
]
. The map
\phi_{p}
is constructed as follows. Assume
\frac{\partial r}{\partial z_{2}}(p)\neq 0
. We define
\Phi^{1}:\zeta^{(1)}\mapsto z
by

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}=z_{1}-p_{1}

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}=2\frac{\partial r}{\partial z_{1}}(p)(z_{1}-p_{1})+2\frac{\partial r}{\partial z_{2}}(p)(z_{2}-p_{2}).

Denote
\rho^{(1)}(\zeta)=r\circ\Phi^{(1)}(\zeta)
. Then
\rho^{(1)}(0)=r(p)
, and

\rho^{(1)}(\zeta)=r(p)+\operatorname{Re}\zeta_{2}+O(|\zeta|^{2}).

In general, suppose that for
2\leq l<m
, we have
\rho^{(l-1)}=r\circ\Phi^{(1)}\circ\cdots\Phi^{(l-1)}
, where

\rho^{(l-1)}(\zeta)=r(p)+\operatorname{Re}w_{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k\leq l-1\\
j,k\geq 1\end{subarray}}a_{j,k}(p)w_{1}^{j}\overline{w}_{1}^{k}+\operatorname{Re}(2b_{l}(p)w_{1}^{l})+O(|w_{1}|^{l}+|w_{2}||w|).

We then define
\Phi^{l}:\zeta\mapsto w
by

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}=w_{1},\quad\zeta_{2}=w_{2}+2b_{l}(p)w_{1}^{l}.

So then
\rho^{(l)}(\zeta)=r\circ\Phi^{(1)}\cdots\circ\Phi^{(l)}(\zeta)
takes the form

\rho^{(l+1)}(\zeta)=r(p)+\operatorname{Re}\zeta_{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k\leq l\\
j,k\geq 1\end{subarray}}a_{j,k}(p)\zeta_{1}^{j}\overline{\zeta}_{1}^{k}+O(|\zeta_{1}|^{l+1}+|\zeta_{2}||\zeta|).

Let
\phi_{p}=\Phi^{(1)}\cdots\circ\Phi^{(m)}
. Then

\rho_{p}:=r\circ\phi_{p}-r(p)
takes the form

(3.1)

\rho_{p}(\zeta)=\operatorname{Re}\zeta_{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\geq 1\\[1.0pt]
j+k\leq m\end{subarray}}a_{j,k}(p)\zeta_{1}^{j}\overline{\zeta}_{1}^{k}+O(|\zeta_{1}|^{m+1}+|\zeta_{2}||\zeta|).

\rho_{p}
is the defining function of the domain
\Omega_{p}=\phi_{p}^{-1}(D)
.
The coefficients
a_{j,k}(p)
are linear combinations of products of derivatives of
r
at
p
up to order
m
and depend smoothly on
p\in U_{0}
.

For
l=2,\dots,m
and
\delta>0
, set

A_{l}(q)=\max\{|a_{j,k}(q)|:j+k=l\},

and

\tau(q,\delta)=\min\left\{\left(\frac{\delta}{A_{l}(q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{l}}:2\leq l\leq m\right\}.

By the assumption that
p_{0}\in bD
is of finite type
m
, we know that at least one of the functions
a_{j,k}(p_{0})
is non-zero (otherwise the curve
t\mapsto(\phi_{p_{0}}(t),0)
has order of tangency with
bD
greater than
m
at
p_{0}
.) Hence by continuity and shrinking
U_{0}
, we can assume that the same function
a_{j,k}(q)\neq 0
for all
q\in U_{0}
, and in particular
A_{l}(q)>0
for some
2\leq l\leq m
.
It follows that

(3.2)

\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\tau(q,\delta)\lesssim\delta^{\frac{1}{m}},\quad q\in U_{0}.

The definition of
\tau(q,\delta)
also implies that if
\delta^{\prime}<\delta^{\prime\prime}
, then

(3.3)

(\delta/\delta^{\prime\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau(q,\delta^{\prime\prime})\leq\tau(q,\delta^{\prime})\leq(\delta/\delta^{\prime\prime})^{\frac{1}{m}}\tau(q^{\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime}).

For
\delta\geq 0
we define

(3.4)

J_{\delta}(p,\zeta)=\left[\delta^{2}+|\zeta_{2}|^{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{m}A_{k}(p)^{2}|\zeta_{1}|^{2k}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For
a>0
and
\gamma\geq 0
, we define the generalized “nonisotropic” polydisk
P^{(a)}_{\delta,\gamma}(\zeta^{\prime})
centered at
\zeta^{\prime}
by

P^{(a)}_{\delta,\gamma}(\zeta^{\prime})=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:|\zeta_{2}-\zeta^{\prime}_{2}|<a(J_{\delta}(p,\zeta^{\prime})+\gamma),\;|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{1}^{\prime}|<\tau\bigl(p,aJ_{\delta}(p,\zeta^{\prime})\bigr)+a\gamma\right\}.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
a>0
(independent of

p,\zeta^{\prime},\delta
), such for
\zeta^{\prime}\in\Omega_{p}\cap\{|\zeta|<a\}
and
\zeta\in P^{(a)}_{\delta,\gamma}(\zeta^{\prime})
, the following estimates hold

\displaystyle J_{\delta}(p,\zeta)\leq C_{m,r}\bigl(J_{\delta}(p,\zeta^{\prime})+a\gamma\bigr);

\displaystyle J_{\delta}(p,\zeta^{\prime})\leq C_{m,r}\bigl(J_{\delta}(p,\zeta)+a\gamma\bigr).

Proof.

For simplicity we write
J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})=J_{\delta}(p,\zeta^{\prime})
and
\tau^{\prime}=\tau(p,aJ_{\delta}(p,\zeta^{\prime}))
.
The condition
\zeta\in P^{(a)}_{\delta,\gamma}(\zeta^{\prime})
implies that

(3.5)

\left||\zeta_{2}|-|\zeta^{\prime}_{2}|\right|\leq a\bigl(J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})+\gamma\bigr),\quad\left||\zeta_{1}|-|\zeta^{\prime}_{1}|\right|\leq\tau^{\prime}+a\gamma.

Thus for
\zeta\in P^{(a)}_{\delta,\gamma}(\zeta^{\prime})
, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle J^{2}_{\delta}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\delta^{2}+|\zeta_{2}|^{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{m}\bigl[A_{k}(p)\bigr]^{2}|\zeta_{1}|^{2k}

\displaystyle\leq C_{m}\Bigl[\delta^{2}+|\zeta_{2}^{\prime}|^{2}+\bigl(aJ_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\bigr)^{2}+(a\gamma)^{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{m}\bigl[A_{k}(p)\bigr]^{2}\left(|\zeta_{1}^{\prime}|^{2k}+(\tau^{\prime})^{2k}+(a\gamma)^{2k}\right)\Bigr].

By definition

(3.7)

\tau^{\prime}=\tau(p,aJ_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}))\leq\left(\frac{aJ_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})}{A_{k}(p)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\quad\text{for}\;2\leq k\leq m.

Plugging the above into (
3.6
) and using the fact that
(a\gamma)^{2k}<(a\gamma)^{2}
(
a
,
\gamma
<1), we get

\displaystyle J^{2}_{\delta}(\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq C_{m,r}\Bigl[\delta^{2}+|\zeta_{2}^{\prime}|^{2}+\bigl(aJ_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\bigr)^{2}+(a\gamma)^{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{m}\bigl[A_{k}(p)\bigr]^{2}|\zeta_{1}^{\prime}|^{2k}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq C_{m,r}\bigl(\bigl[J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\bigr]^{2}+(a\gamma)^{2}\bigr).

For the other direction, we have

\displaystyle\bigl[J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\bigr]^{2}

\displaystyle=\delta^{2}+|\zeta_{2}^{\prime}|^{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{m}\bigl[A_{k}(p)\bigr]^{2}|\zeta_{1}^{\prime}|^{2k}

\displaystyle\leq C_{m}\Bigl[\delta^{2}+|\zeta_{2}|^{2}+\bigl(aJ_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\bigr)^{2}+(a\gamma)^{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{m}\bigl[A_{k}(p)\bigr]^{2}\left(|\zeta_{1}|^{2k}+(\tau^{\prime})^{2k}+(a\gamma)^{2k}\right)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=C_{m,r}\left(\bigl[J_{\delta}(\zeta)\bigr]^{2}+\bigl(aJ_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\bigr)^{2}+(a\gamma)^{2}\right).

Taking
a<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2C_{m,r}}}
, we get
\bigl[J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\bigr]^{2}\leq C^{\prime}_{m,r}\Bigl(\bigl[J_{\delta}(\zeta)\bigr]^{2}+(a\gamma)^{2}\Bigr)
. Taking square root on both sides we are done.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

There are positive constants
a_{0}
and
c
, and for each
p\in U_{0}\cap bD
, there is a family of pseudoconvex domains
\bigl\{\Omega_{p}^{\delta}\bigr\}_{0<\delta<\delta_{0}}
with the following properties:

(1)

0\in b\Omega_{p}^{\ast}
, where
\Omega_{p}^{\ast}:=int\bigl[\cap_{0<\delta<\delta_{0}}\Omega_{p}^{\delta}\bigr]

(2)

\{\zeta\in\overline{\Omega}_{p}:0<|\zeta|<c\}\subset\Omega_{p}^{\ast}\subset\Omega_{p}^{\delta}
;

(3)

For
\zeta^{\prime}\in\Omega_{p}
with
|\zeta^{\prime}|<c
one has

P^{(a_{0})}_{\delta,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\subset\Omega_{p}^{\delta}.

The constant
a_{0}
depends only on the defining function
r
and it is independent of
\delta
and
\zeta^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For simplicity we will write
J_{\delta}(p,\zeta)
as
J_{\delta}(\zeta)
. Part (i) and (ii) are the same as in
[
Ran90
, Prop. 2.4, (i),(ii)]
, and we shall include the details here for the reader’s convenience.
For small
s
and
\delta>0
, define

W_{s,\delta}(p)=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:|\zeta|<c\;\text{and}\;|\rho(\zeta)|<sJ_{\delta}(\zeta)\}.

Let
H_{p,\delta}
be the smooth real-valued function on
W_{s,\delta}(p)
given by
[
Cat89
, Prop. 4.1]
. Catlin
[
Cat89
, p.449-453]
shows that
c,s,\varepsilon_{0}
and
\delta_{0}
can be chosen so that for all
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, the set

S_{\delta}=\{\zeta\in W_{s,\delta}(p):\rho^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}(\zeta)=0\},\quad\rho^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}(\zeta):=\rho(\zeta)+\varepsilon H_{p,\delta}(\zeta)

is a smooth pseudoconvex hypersurface (from the side
\rho^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}<0
). All the constants can be chosen independently of
p\in U_{0}\cap bD
, and
s,\varepsilon
can be chosen independently of
\delta
. Furthermore,
H_{p,\delta}(\zeta)\approx-J_{\delta}(\zeta)
. We now fix
s=s_{0},\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}>0
independent of
\delta
. It follows that

(3.8)

\Omega_{p}^{\delta}=\{|\zeta|<c:\rho(\zeta)<0\}\cup\{\zeta\in W_{s_{0},\delta}(p):\rho^{\varepsilon_{0}}_{\delta}(\zeta)<0\}

is a pseudoconvex domain which satisfies

\overline{\Omega}_{p}\cap\{|\zeta|<c\}\subset\Omega_{p}^{\delta}\subset\bigl(\Omega_{p}\cap\{|\zeta|<c\}\bigr)\cup W_{s_{0},\delta}(p).

Define

(3.9)

\Omega_{p}^{\ast}=int\bigcap_{0<\delta<\delta_{0}}\Omega_{p}^{\delta}.

Then
\Omega_{p}^{\ast}
is pseudoconvex. Since
\rho^{\varepsilon_{0}}_{\delta}(0)=\rho(0)+\varepsilon_{0}H_{p,\delta}(0)\approx-J_{\delta}(p,0)=-\delta
, it follows that
\operatorname{dist}(0,S_{\delta})\lesssim\delta
and thus
0\in b\Omega_{p}^{\ast}
, which proves (i).

Next we prove (ii). Suppose
\zeta\in\overline{\Omega_{p}}
and
0<|\zeta|<c
. Then
\rho(\zeta)\leq 0
.

\rho_{\delta}^{\varepsilon_{0}}(\zeta)=\rho(\zeta)+\varepsilon_{0}H_{p,\delta}(\zeta)\leq-\varepsilon_{0}cJ_{\delta}(\zeta)\leq-c\varepsilon_{0}\Bigl(|\zeta_{1}|^{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{m}[A_{k}(p)]^{2}|\zeta_{2}|^{2k}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For fixed
\zeta\neq 0
, the expression on the right-hand side is a negative constant independent of
\delta
.
Also it is clear that
\zeta\in W_{s_{0},\delta}(p)
since
\rho(\zeta)\leq 0<c_{\zeta}\leq sJ_{\delta}(\zeta)
uniformly for all
\delta
. Hence
\zeta\in\Omega_{p}^{\ast}
. This proves (ii).

We now prove (iii). First, we show that for
\zeta^{\prime}\in\Omega_{p}\cap\{|\zeta|<c\}
, we have

(3.10)

|\rho(\zeta)-\rho(\zeta^{\prime})|<C_{\rho}a_{0}^{1/m}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}),\quad\forall\;\zeta\in P^{(a_{0})}_{\delta,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}),

where
a_{0}>0
is to be determined.
Denote

R_{b}(p)=\bigl\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:|\zeta_{1}|<\tau(p,b),\;|\zeta_{2}|<b\bigr\},

We observe that
\zeta\in R_{J_{\delta}(\zeta)}(p)
. Indeed, we have
|\zeta_{2}|<J_{\delta}(\zeta)
and for some
2\leq k_{\ast}\leq m
,

\tau(p,J_{\delta}(\zeta))=\left(\frac{J_{\delta}(\zeta)}{A_{k_{\ast}}(p)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k_{\ast}}}\geq\left(\frac{A_{k_{\ast}}(p)|\zeta_{1}|^{k_{\ast}}}{A_{k_{\ast}}(p)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k_{\ast}}}=|\zeta_{1}|.

By
[
Cat89
, Prop. 1.2]
, we have

|D_{1}^{l}\rho(\eta)|\lesssim b\bigl[\tau(\zeta^{\prime},b)\bigr]^{-l},\quad\eta\in R_{b}(\zeta^{\prime}).

Apply the above estimate with
l=1
and
b=J_{\delta}(\eta)
to get

(3.11)

|D_{1}\rho(\eta)|\lesssim J_{\delta}(\eta)\bigl[\tau(p,J_{\delta}(\eta)\bigr]^{-1},\quad\eta\in\phi_{p}^{-1}(U_{0}).

In particular the above holds for
\eta\in P^{(a_{0})}_{\delta,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})
.
We now compare
\overline{C}J_{\delta}(\eta)
and
\overline{C}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})
for
\eta\in P^{(a_{0})}_{\delta,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})
. By Lemma
3.1
we have

\displaystyle J_{\delta}(\eta)\leq C_{m,\rho}\bigl(J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})+a_{0}\delta\bigr)\leq C^{\prime}_{m,\rho}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime});

\displaystyle J_{\delta}(\eta)\geq\bigl(C_{m,\rho}^{-1}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})-a_{0}\delta\bigr)\geq c_{m,\rho}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}),

where we chose
a_{0}<(2C_{m,\rho})^{-1}
. Hence

|D_{1}\rho(\eta)|\leq C_{m,\rho}J_{\delta}(\eta)\bigl[\tau(p,J_{\delta}(\eta)\bigr]^{-1}\leq C_{m,\rho}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\bigl[\tau(p,J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\bigr]^{-1},\quad\eta\in P^{(a_{0})}_{\delta,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}).

By the mean value theorem and (
3.11
), we have for
\zeta\in P^{(a_{0})}_{\delta,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})
,

(3.12)

\displaystyle|\rho(\zeta)-\rho(\zeta^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rho}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\tau(p,J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}))^{-1}|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{1}^{\prime}|+C_{\rho}|\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{2}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rho}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\tau(p,J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}))^{-1}\bigl[\tau(p,a_{0}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}))+a_{0}\delta\bigr]+C_{\rho}a_{0}\bigl[J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})+\delta\bigr].

By (
3.3
), we have
\tau(p,a_{0}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}))\leq a_{0}^{1/m}\tau(p,J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}))
. Also
J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\tau(p,J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}))^{-1}\leq 1
by (
3.2
) (assume that
J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})<1
). Thus (
3.12
) implies
|\rho(\zeta)-\rho(\zeta^{\prime})|\leq C_{\rho}\bigl[a_{0}^{1/m}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})+a_{0}\delta\bigr]\leq C_{\rho}^{\prime\prime}a_{0}^{1/m}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})
for all
\zeta\in P^{(a_{0})}_{\delta,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})
. This proves (
3.10
).

To finish the proof we consider two different cases.

Case 1.
\zeta^{\prime}\in\overline{\Omega}_{p}\cap\{|\zeta|<c\}\setminus W_{s_{0},\delta}(p)
.

By definition of
W_{s_{0},\delta}(p)
we have
\rho(\zeta^{\prime})<-s_{0}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})
. Together with (
3.10
) this implies that

\displaystyle\rho(\zeta)\leq\rho(\zeta^{\prime})+|\rho(\zeta)-\rho(\zeta^{\prime})|\leq-s_{0}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})+C_{\rho}a_{0}^{\frac{1}{m}}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})<0,\quad\zeta\in P^{(a_{0})}_{\delta,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}),

where we choose
a_{0}<[s_{0}/C_{\rho}]^{m}
. Hence
\zeta\in\Omega_{p}\subset\Omega_{p}^{\delta}
.

Case 2.
\zeta^{\prime}\in\overline{\Omega}_{p}\cap\{|\zeta|<c\}\cap W_{s_{0},\delta}(p)
.

It suffices to consider
\zeta\in P^{(a_{0})}_{\delta,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{p}
, in which case
\rho(\zeta)\geq 0
. By (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\rho(\zeta)\leq\rho(\zeta^{\prime})+|\rho(\zeta)-\rho(\zeta^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rho}a_{0}^{1/m}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime}).

By our choice of
a_{0}<[s_{0}/C_{\rho}]^{m}
, the last quantity is bounded by
s_{0}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})
, which means
\zeta\in W_{s_{0},\delta}(p)
.
On the other hand, Lemma
3.1
shows that
\rho(\zeta)\leq C_{\rho}a_{0}^{1/m}J_{\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})<C^{\prime}_{\rho}a_{0}^{1/m}J_{\delta}(\zeta)
.
Hence

\displaystyle\rho^{\varepsilon_{0}}_{\delta}(\zeta):=\rho(\zeta)+\varepsilon_{0}H_{p,\delta}(\zeta)\leq C_{\rho}a_{0}^{\frac{1}{m}}J_{\delta}(\zeta)-\varepsilon_{0}cJ_{\delta}(\zeta),\quad\zeta\in P^{(a_{0})}_{\delta,\tilde{\delta}}(\zeta^{\prime}).

The last expression is negative if we choose
a_{0}<\bigl[\varepsilon_{0}c(C_{\rho})^{-1}\bigr]^{m}
. This shows that
P^{(a_{0})}_{\delta,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})\subset\Omega_{p}^{\delta}
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\tilde{\zeta}\in\{|\zeta|<c\}\setminus\Omega_{p}
be such that
\operatorname{dist}(\tilde{\zeta},\Omega_{p})\approx|\tilde{\zeta}|
. Suppose that there exists
\tilde{\delta}>0
such that
\tilde{\zeta}\in S_{\tilde{\delta}}=\{\rho_{\tilde{\delta}}^{\varepsilon}=0\}
, where
\varepsilon<C(p,m)
. Then
\tilde{\delta}\approx|\tilde{\zeta}|
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{dist}(\tilde{\zeta},\Omega_{p})=|\tilde{\zeta}|
, we can assume that for some
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}>0
,
\rho(\tilde{\zeta})\in(\lambda_{0}|\tilde{\zeta}|,\lambda_{1}|\tilde{\zeta}|)
.
Since
\tilde{\zeta}\in S_{\tilde{\delta}}
, we have
\rho(\tilde{\zeta})+\varepsilon H_{p,\tilde{\delta}}(\tilde{\zeta})=0
. By
[
Cat89
, Prop. 4.1.]
there exist constants
c_{0},C_{0}>0
such that
c_{0}J_{\tilde{\delta}}(p,\tilde{\zeta})<-H_{p,\tilde{\delta}}(\tilde{\zeta})<C_{0}J_{\tilde{\delta}}(p,\tilde{\zeta})
. Hence
\rho(\tilde{\zeta})\in(c_{0}\varepsilon J_{\tilde{\delta}}(p,\tilde{\zeta}),C_{0}\varepsilon J_{\tilde{\delta}}(p,\tilde{\zeta}))
. This implies that

c_{0}\varepsilon J_{\tilde{\delta}}(p,\tilde{\zeta})<\lambda_{1}|\tilde{\zeta}|,\quad\lambda_{0}|\tilde{\zeta}|<C_{0}\varepsilon J_{\tilde{\delta}}(p,\tilde{\zeta}).

Since
\tilde{\delta}\leq J_{\tilde{\delta}}(p,\tilde{\zeta})
, the first inequality above shows that
\tilde{\delta}\leq(c_{0}\varepsilon)^{-1}\lambda_{1}|\tilde{\zeta}|
. On the other hand, by the definition of
J_{\delta}
, there exists
C_{\rho,p}
such that

J_{\tilde{\delta}}(p,\tilde{\zeta})\leq\tilde{\delta}+C_{\rho,p}|\tilde{\zeta}|
, so the second inequality shows that
\lambda_{0}|\tilde{\zeta}|\leq C_{0}\varepsilon(\tilde{\delta}+C_{\rho,p}|\tilde{\zeta}|)
. If
\varepsilon\leq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2C_{0}C_{\rho,p}}
, then
|\tilde{\zeta}|\leq C_{\rho,p}^{-1}\tilde{\delta}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

There exist a sequence of points
\{\zeta_{j}\}\to 0
, and constant
c_{0},C_{0}
such that
\operatorname{dist}(\zeta_{j},b\Omega_{p})\approx|\zeta_{j}|
,
c_{0}2^{-j}\leq|\rho(\zeta_{j})|\leq C_{0}2^{-(j-1)}
, and
\rho(\zeta_{j})+\varepsilon H_{p,\delta_{j}}(\zeta_{j})=0
, with
\delta_{j}=2^{-j}
. The constant
c_{0},C_{0}
depend only on
r
(or
\rho
),
p
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Denote
\rho^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}(\zeta)=\rho(\zeta)+\varepsilon H_{p,\delta}(\zeta)
.
Take
\zeta
to be of the form
(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})=(0,0,x_{2},0)
. Since
\nabla\rho(0)=\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial x_{2}}(0)=1
, we have
\operatorname{dist}(\zeta,\Omega_{p})\approx x_{2}
if
x_{2}
sufficiently small. There exist some
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}>0
such that
\rho(\zeta)\in(\lambda_{0}x_{2},\lambda_{1}x_{2})
. By
[
Cat89
, Prop. 4.1.]
, there exist
0<c<C
such that
-CJ_{\delta}(p,\zeta)<H_{p,\delta}(\zeta)<-cJ_{\delta}(p,\zeta)
. We seek
\zeta^{\prime}
in the form
\zeta^{\prime}=(0,0,x_{2}^{\prime},0)
such that
\rho(\zeta^{\prime})+\varepsilon H_{p,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})>0
. Since

H_{p,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime})>-CJ_{\delta}(p,\zeta^{\prime})=-C(\delta^{2}+(x^{\prime}_{2})^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq-C(\delta+x^{\prime}_{2}),

it suffices to find
\zeta^{\prime}
with
\rho(\zeta^{\prime})-\varepsilon C(\delta+x^{\prime}_{2})=x^{\prime}_{2}-\varepsilon C(\delta+x^{\prime}_{2})>0
, or equivalently,

x_{2}^{\prime}>\frac{\varepsilon C}{1-\varepsilon C}\delta.

On the other hand, we seek
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
in the form
(0,0,x_{2}^{\prime\prime},0)
such that
\rho(\zeta^{\prime\prime})+\varepsilon H_{p,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime\prime})<0
. Since

H_{p,\delta}(\zeta^{\prime\prime})<-cJ_{\delta}(p,\zeta^{\prime\prime})=-c(\delta^{2}+(x_{2}^{\prime\prime})^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}<-c^{\prime}(\delta+x^{\prime\prime}_{2}),\quad c^{\prime}=c/\sqrt{2},

it suffices to find
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
with
\rho(\zeta^{\prime\prime})-\varepsilon c^{\prime}(\delta+x^{\prime\prime}_{2})=x^{\prime\prime}_{2}-\varepsilon c^{\prime}(\delta+x^{\prime\prime}_{2})<0
, or equivalently,

x_{2}^{\prime\prime}<\frac{\varepsilon c^{\prime}}{1-\varepsilon c^{\prime}}\delta.

To summarize, we choose
\zeta^{\prime}=(0,0,x_{2}^{\prime},0)
and
\zeta^{\prime\prime}=(0,0,x_{2}^{\prime\prime},0)
such that

|\zeta^{\prime\prime}|=x_{2}^{\prime\prime}<\frac{\varepsilon c^{\prime}}{1-\varepsilon c^{\prime}}\delta<\frac{\varepsilon C}{1-\varepsilon C}\delta<x_{2}^{\prime}=|\zeta^{\prime}|.

For example, we can choose
x_{2}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varepsilon c}{1-\varepsilon c}\delta
and
x_{2}^{\prime}=2\frac{\varepsilon C}{1-\varepsilon C}\delta
.
Since
\rho_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}(\zeta)
is a continuous function in
\zeta
, there exists
a_{\ast}\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varepsilon c^{\prime}}{1-\varepsilon c^{\prime}}\delta,2\frac{\varepsilon C}{1-\varepsilon C}\delta\bigr)
such that
\rho^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}\bigl((0,0,a^{\ast},0)\bigr)=0
.
We now set
\delta=\delta_{j}=2^{-j}
, and let
\zeta_{j}=(0,0,a_{j}^{\ast},0)
be such that
\rho^{\varepsilon}_{\delta_{j}}\bigl((0,0,a_{j}^{\ast},0)\bigr)=0
. In other words
\rho(\zeta_{j})+\varepsilon H_{p,\delta_{j}}(\zeta_{j})=0
.
Setting
c_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varepsilon c^{\prime}}{1-\varepsilon c^{\prime}}
and
C_{0}=\frac{\varepsilon C}{1-\varepsilon C}
, we then have
|\zeta_{j}|=a_{j}^{\ast}\in(c_{0}2^{-j},C_{0}2^{-(j-1)})
.
Since
\rho(\zeta_{j})\approx\operatorname{dist}(\zeta_{j},\Omega_{p})=|\zeta_{j}|
, by adjusting the constants
c_{0},C_{0}
we get
\rho(\zeta_{j})\in(c_{0}2^{-j},C_{0}2^{-(j-1)})
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
p\in U_{0}\cap bD
and let
\Omega_{p}^{\ast}
be the domain from Proposition
3.2
. There are positive constants
c^{\prime}
and
\gamma
which are independent of
p
such that

r(z)\geq c^{\prime}|z-p|^{m}\quad\text{for}\quad|z-p|\leq\gamma\quad\text{and}\quad z\notin\phi_{p}(\Omega_{p}^{\ast}).

Proof.

The proof is in Range
[
Ran90
]
and we will provide the details here for the reader’s convenience.
It suffices to show that if
|\zeta_{0}|<\gamma^{\prime}
for some
\gamma^{\prime}>0
and
\zeta_{0}\notin\Omega_{p}^{\ast}
, then
\rho(\zeta_{0})\geq c|\zeta|^{m}
. By definition of
\Omega_{p}^{\ast}
, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that
\zeta_{0}\notin\Omega_{p}^{\delta_{0}}
, where

\Omega_{p}^{\delta_{0}}=\{|\zeta|<c:\rho(\zeta)<0\}\cup\{\zeta\in W_{s,\delta_{0}}(p):\rho^{\varepsilon_{0}}_{\delta_{0}}(\zeta)<0\}.

Hence

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\beta\geq 0
,
\alpha>-1
, and let
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}
. If
\alpha<\beta-\frac{1}{m}
, then

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{s_{1}^{\alpha}t\,ds_{1}\,ds_{2}\,dt}{(\delta+s_{1}+s_{2}+t^{m})^{2+\beta}(\delta+s_{1}+s_{2}+t)}\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\delta^{\alpha-\beta+\frac{1}{m}}.

Proof.

Partition the domain of integration into seven regions:

R_{1}:t>t^{m}>\delta,s_{1},s_{2}
. We have

I\leq\int_{\delta^{\frac{1}{m}}}^{1}\frac{t}{t^{2m+m\beta}t}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{m}}s_{1}^{\alpha}\,ds_{1}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t^{m}}\,ds_{2}\right)\,dt\leq C\int_{\delta^{\frac{1}{m}}}^{1}t^{m(\alpha-\beta)}\,dt\leq C\delta^{\alpha-\beta+\frac{1}{m}}.

R_{2}:t>\delta>t^{m},s_{1},s_{2}
. We have

I\leq\delta^{-2-\beta}\left(\int_{\delta}^{\delta^{\frac{1}{m}}}\frac{t}{t}\,dt\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\delta}s_{1}^{\alpha}\,ds_{1}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\delta}\,ds_{2}\right)\leq C\delta^{\alpha-\beta+\frac{1}{m}}.

R_{3}:t>s_{1}>\delta,t^{m},s_{2}
. We have

I\leq\int_{\delta}^{1}\frac{s_{1}^{\alpha}}{s_{1}^{2+\beta}}\left(\int_{0}^{s_{1}^{1/m}}\frac{t}{t}\,dt\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\,ds_{2}\right)\,ds_{1}\leq C\int_{\delta}^{1}s_{1}^{\alpha-\beta+\frac{1}{m}-1}\,ds_{1}\leq C\delta^{\alpha-\beta+\frac{1}{m}}.

R_{4}:t>s_{2}>\delta,t^{m},s_{1}
. We have

I\leq\int_{\delta}^{1}\frac{1}{s_{2}^{2+\beta}}\left(\int_{0}^{s_{2}^{1/m}}\frac{t}{t}\,dt\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s_{2}}s_{1}^{\alpha}\,ds_{1}\right)\,ds_{2}\leq C\int_{\delta}^{1}s_{2}^{\alpha-\beta+\frac{1}{m}-1}\,ds_{2}\leq C\delta^{\alpha-\beta+\frac{1}{m}}.

R_{5}:\delta>t,t^{m},s_{1},s_{2}
. We have

I\leq\delta^{-2-\beta}\delta^{-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\delta}t\,dt\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\delta}s_{1}^{\alpha}\,ds_{1}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\delta}\,ds_{2}\right)\leq C\delta^{\alpha-\beta+1}.

R_{6}:s_{1}>\delta,t,t^{m},s_{2}
. We have

I\leq\int_{\delta}^{1}\frac{s_{1}^{\alpha}}{s_{1}^{2+\beta}s_{1}}\left(\int_{0}^{s_{1}}t\,dt\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\,ds_{2}\right)\,ds_{1}\leq C\int_{\delta}^{1}s_{1}^{\alpha-\beta}\,ds_{1}.

R_{7}:s_{2}>\delta,t,t^{m},s_{1}
. We have

I\leq\int_{\delta}^{1}\frac{1}{s_{2}^{2+\beta}s_{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{s_{2}}t\,dt\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s_{2}}s_{1}^{\alpha}\,ds_{1}\right)\,ds_{2}\leq C\int_{\delta}^{1}s_{2}^{\alpha-\beta}\,ds_{2}.

Here the constants depend only on
\alpha
and
\beta
. For
R_{6}
and
R_{7}
, we have

\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{\alpha-\beta}dr\leq\begin{cases}C,&\alpha-\beta>-1,\\
C(1+|\log\delta|),&\alpha-\beta=-1,\\
C\delta^{\alpha-\beta+1},&\alpha-\beta<-1,\end{cases}

which is bounded by
C\delta^{\alpha-\beta+\frac{1}{m}}
in all cases.
∎

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon)}\varphi|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{m}-\eta,p}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon})}\leq C|\varphi|_{H^{s,p}(D)},\quad|\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon)}\varphi|_{\Lambda^{s+\frac{1}{m}-\eta}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon})}\leq C^{\prime}|\varphi|_{\Lambda^{s}(D)};

(4.24)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon)}\overline{\partial}\varphi|_{H^{s+1,p}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon})}\leq C^{\prime\prime}|\overline{\partial}\varphi|_{H^{s,p}(D)},\quad|\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon)}\overline{\partial}\varphi|_{\Lambda^{s+1}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon})}\leq C^{\prime\prime\prime}|\overline{\partial}\varphi|_{\Lambda^{s}(D)}.

Proof.

The first statement in (
4.23
) follows from (
4.6
), (
2.10
), and (
4.11
); the second statement follows from (
4.6
), (
2.11
), and (
4.12
). Estimates (
4.24
) follows from (
4.7
).
∎

Corollary 4.6

(Homotopy formula)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.5
, suppose
either

a)
\varphi\in H^{s,p}_{(0,1)}(D)
and
\overline{\partial}\varphi\in H^{s,p}_{(0,2)}(D)
, for
1<p<\infty
and
s>\frac{1}{p}
; or

b)
\varphi\in\Lambda^{s}_{(0,1)}(D)
and
\overline{\partial}\varphi\in\Lambda^{s}_{(0,2)}(D)
, for
s>0
.

Then for all sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, the following homotopy formula holds in the sense of distributions:

(4.25)

\varphi=\overline{\partial}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon)}\varphi+\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon)}\overline{\partial}\varphi,\quad\text{on $D^{\prime}_{\epsilon}$}.

In particular if
\varphi\in H^{s,p}_{(0,1)}(D)
(resp.
\varphi\in\Lambda^{s}_{(0,1)}(D)
) is
\overline{\partial}
-closed, then we have
\overline{\partial}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon)}\varphi=\varphi
on
D^{\prime}_{\epsilon}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon)}\varphi\in H^{s+\frac{1}{m}-\eta,p}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon})
(resp.

\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon)}\varphi\in\Lambda^{s+\frac{1}{m}-\eta}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon})
), with
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon)}
satisfying estimates (
4.23
).

Proof.

By
[
Gon19
, Prop. 2.1]
, Formula (
4.25
) is valid for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{(0,1)}(\overline{D^{\prime}_{\epsilon}})
.
For general
\varphi\in H^{s,p}_{(0,1)}(D)
such that
\overline{\partial}\varphi\in H^{s,p}_{(0,2)}(D)
and with
s,p
in the given range, we use approximation. By
[
SY24b
, Prop. A.3]
, there exists a sequence
\varphi_{\lambda}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
such that

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}\longrightarrow\varphi\quad\text{in $H^{s,p}(D)$ },

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\varphi_{\lambda}\longrightarrow\overline{\partial}\varphi\quad\text{in $H^{s,p}(D)$}.

By Theorem
4.5
, we have for
1<p<\infty
and
s>\frac{1}{p}
,

\displaystyle\|\overline{\partial}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon)}(\varphi_{\lambda}-\varphi)\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{m}-\eta-1,p}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon)}(\varphi_{\lambda}-\varphi)\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{m}-\eta,p}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi_{\lambda}-\varphi\|_{H^{s,p}(D)},

and also

\|\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon)}\overline{\partial}(\varphi_{\lambda}-\varphi)\|_{H^{s+1,p}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon})}\leq C^{\prime}\|\overline{\partial}(\varphi_{\lambda}-\varphi)\|_{H^{s,p}(D)}.

Letting
\lambda\to\infty
we obtain (
4.25
).
The proof for the Hölder space is similar and we leave it to the reader.
∎

We can now use Corollary
4.6
to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Let
\{\epsilon_{j}\}
be a sequence of small positive numbers tending to
0
. Consider the sequence of functions
\{\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi\}
, and
\{\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\overline{\partial}\varphi\}
,
\mathcal{E}_{j}
being the Rychkov extension operator on
D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j}}
. Then by Corollary
2.15
and Theorem
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{m}-\eta,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\leq C_{s,\eta}|\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{m}-\eta,p}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j}})}\leq C_{D,\eta,s}|\varphi|_{H^{s,p}(D)};

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\overline{\partial}\varphi|_{H^{s+1,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\leq C_{s,\eta}|\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\overline{\partial}\varphi|_{H^{s+1,p}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j}})}\leq C_{D,\eta,s}|\overline{\partial}\varphi|_{H^{s,p}(D)}.

By the Banach-Alaouglu theorem, there exists a subsequence
\{\mathcal{E}_{j_{\alpha}}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j_{\alpha}})}\varphi\}
which converges weakly to some limit function
\mathcal{H}_{1}\varphi
in
H^{s+\frac{1}{m}-\eta,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.
Similarly, by extracting another subsequence from
\{\mathcal{E}_{j_{\alpha}}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon_{j_{\alpha}})}\overline{\partial}\varphi\}
, and denoting this subsequence still by
\{\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\overline{\partial}\varphi\}
, we get
\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\overline{\partial}\varphi
converges weakly to a limit function
\mathcal{H}_{2}\overline{\partial}\varphi
.

Now, by Corollary
4.6
, we have

\varphi=\overline{\partial}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi+\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\overline{\partial}\varphi
on
D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j}}
.
Let
\phi=\phi_{1}d\widehat{\overline{z}_{1}}+\phi_{2}d\widehat{\overline{z}_{2}}
be a
(2,1)
form with coefficients in
C^{\infty}_{c}(D)
. Here
d\widehat{\overline{z}_{1}}:=d\overline{z}_{2}\wedge dz_{1}\wedge dz_{2}
and

d\widehat{\overline{z}_{2}}:=d\overline{z}_{1}\wedge dz_{1}\wedge dz_{2}
.
Then there exists a large
J
such that
\operatorname{supp}\phi\subset D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j}}
, for all
j>J
. It follows that for
j>J
:

\displaystyle\left<\varphi,\phi\right>_{D}=\left<\varphi,\phi\right>_{D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j}}}

\displaystyle=\left<\overline{\partial}\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi+\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\epsilon_{j}}\overline{\partial}\varphi,\phi\right>_{D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j}}}=\left<\overline{\partial}\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi,\phi\right>_{D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j}}}+\left<\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\epsilon_{j}}\overline{\partial}\varphi,\phi\right>_{D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j}}}

\displaystyle=-\left<\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi,\overline{\partial}\phi\right>_{D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j}}}+\left<\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\epsilon_{j}}\overline{\partial}\varphi,\phi\right>_{D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j}}}=-\left<\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi,\overline{\partial}\phi\right>_{D}+\left<\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\epsilon_{j}}\overline{\partial}\varphi,\phi\right>_{D}.

Taking limit as
j\to\infty
, and using that

\lim_{j\to\infty}-\left<\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi,\overline{\partial}\phi\right>_{D}=-\left<\mathcal{H}_{1}\varphi,\overline{\partial}\phi\right>_{D},\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}\left<\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\epsilon_{j}}\overline{\partial}\varphi,\phi\right>_{D}=\left<\mathcal{H}_{2}\overline{\partial}\varphi,\phi\right>_{D},

We get

\left<\varphi,\phi\right>_{D}=-\left<\mathcal{H}_{1}\varphi,\overline{\partial}\phi\right>_{D}+\left<\mathcal{H}_{2}\overline{\partial}\varphi,\phi\right>_{D}=\left<\overline{\partial}\mathcal{H}_{1}\varphi+\mathcal{H}_{2}\overline{\partial}\varphi,\phi\right>_{D}.

In other words,
\varphi=\overline{\partial}\mathcal{H}_{1}\varphi+\mathcal{H}_{2}\overline{\partial}\varphi
in the sense of distributions on
D
.

Next we prove the statement for the Hölder-Zygmund space. Let
\epsilon_{j}
and
\mathcal{E}_{j}
be as above. The sequence
\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi
satisfies the estimate

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi|_{\Lambda^{s+\frac{1}{m}-\eta}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\leq C_{s}|\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi|_{\Lambda^{s+\frac{1}{m}-\eta}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j}})}\leq C_{D,\eta,s}|\varphi|_{\Lambda^{s}(D)};

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\overline{\partial}\varphi|_{\Lambda^{s+1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\leq C_{s}|\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\overline{\partial}\varphi|_{\Lambda^{s+1}(D^{\prime}_{\epsilon_{j})}}\leq C_{D,\eta,s}|\overline{\partial}\varphi|_{\Lambda^{s}(D)}.

In particular,
\{\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi\},\{\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\overline{\partial}\varphi\}
are families of equicontinuous functions on
\overline{D}
. By the Ascoli-Arzela theorem and by taking two subsequence successively, we may assume that

\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\varphi
and
\mathcal{E}_{j}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(\epsilon_{j})}\overline{\partial}\varphi
converge uniformly to some limit functions
\mathcal{H}_{1}\varphi,\mathcal{H}_{2}\overline{\partial}\varphi
respectively, which are in
\Lambda^{s+\frac{1}{m}-\eta}(D)
.
Furthermore, we have
\varphi=\overline{\partial}\mathcal{H}_{1}\varphi+\mathcal{H}_{2}\overline{\partial}\varphi
in the sense of distributions on
D
. The proof is now complete.

The Role of CT-Guided Optimal Fluoroscopic Viewing Angle to Guide Right Endomyocardial Biopsy
Authors: Ali Zgheib, Elie Akl, Marco Spaziano, Giuseppe Martucci, Nicolo Piazza
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe report the case of a 64-year-old man with a history of chemotherapy-induced cardiomyopathy requiring heart transplant in 2022 who underwent successful endomyocardial biopsy using patient-specific computed tomographic fluoroscopic projections. (Level of Difficulty: Beginner.)Key Words: cardiac transplant, computed tomography, x-ray fluoroscopy

Performing endomyocardial biopsy (EMB) can be challenging, particularly without transthoracic echocardiography guidance, because of the risk of injury to the right ventricular (RV) free wall and tricuspid valve apparatus.

A 64-year-old man with a history of chemotherapy-induced cardiomyopathy underwent heart transplant 8 months ago and presented for an elective EMB to assess for rejection.

Fluoroscopy and echocardiography provide procedural guidance during EMB. Transthoracic echocardiography can confirm bioptome positioning across the RV septum, thereby avoiding alternative structures such as the RV anterolateral free wall, which could otherwise result in pericardial effusion/tamponade, and the tricuspid valve apparatus, which could otherwise result in tricuspid regurgitation. Up until now, little consideration has been given to using fluoroscopic chamber views to guide EMB. Cardiac computed tomography (CT) can help better understand the fluoroscopic anatomy surrounding the right-sided ventricular septum.

CT demonstrates that the RV septum S-curve transects the right anterior oblique (RAO) cranial, left anterior oblique cranial, and left anterior oblique caudal quadrants and provides the optimal fluoroscopic viewing angle of the ventricular septum in plane (Figure 1A). More specifically, the 4-chamber view in shallow RAO and steep cranial elongates the ventricular septum and provides a clear target for bioptome delivery and positioning at the midsegment (Figures 1B, 1E, and 1F). On the other hand, the 1-chamber view in the left anterior oblique caudal limits the operator from understanding the apical from basal segments of the ventricular septum despite it being in plane (Figure 1C). In the absence of a CT scan, an RV angiogram can be performed to identify the elongated septum in the 4-chamber view, which is consistently obtained in shallow RAO projection (RAO 5° to 10°) and steep cranial angulation (cranial 30° to 40°) and therefore guide the bioptome position (Figures 1D and 1F).

Endomyocardial Biopsy: CT-Guided Procedure Planning

(A) Interventricular septum (IVS) S-curve. 4-chamber view with elongated IVS (blue dot), 1-chamber view with foreshortened IVS (green dot). (B) Patient-specific cardiac computed tomographic (CT) image showing the elongated IVS in a 4-chamber view obtained in right anterior oblique (RAO) 10° cranial (CRAN) 40°. (C) A patient-specific CT image showing the foreshortened IVS in a 1-chamber view obtained in left anterior oblique (LAO) 60° caudal (CAUD) 10°. (D) Intraprocedural right ventricular (RV) angiogram performed in the optimal elongated IVS view (RAO 10° CRAN 40°) obtained from preprocedural CT analysis. (E) CT-derived fluoroscopic view of the elongated IVS. (F) Endomyocardial biopsy performed in the optimal elongated IVS view. Asterisk indicates interventricular septum. LA = left atrium; LV = left ventricle; RA = right atrium.

This case demonstrates the benefits of using a 4-chamber view typically obtained in shallow RAO/steep cranial to elongate the ventricular septum in plane and appropriately direct catheters (eg, bioptomes and pacemaker leads) to their intended position.1, 2, 3

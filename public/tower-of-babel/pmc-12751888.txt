Optimal thresholds for the distance between tumor and collecting system predicting collecting system opening and trifecta achievement in RAPN
Authors: Shuhei Yokokawa, Hiroki Ito, Mei Tokumoto, Takayuki Yamamoto, Yasuhiro Numata, Seiichiro Honda, Tomohiko Aigase, Ryosuke Jikuya, Koichi Uemura, Takuya Kondo, Tomoyuki Tatenuma, Yusuke Ito, Mitsuru Komeya, Kentaro Muraoka, Hisashi Hasumi, Kazuhide Makiyama
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWe examined the clinical significance of the distance between the tumor and collecting system or sinus (DTCS), representing the nearness (N) score in the RENAL nephrometry score, on outcomes of robotic-assisted partial nephrectomy (RAPN).MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 425 patients who underwent RAPN between 2016 and 2021. Multivariate binary logistic regression was used to assess predictive preoperative clinical factors. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis determined the optimal DTCS cutoff point for each outcome.ResultsThe mean DTCS was 4.0 mm (SD 4.6), and 79 were N1, 97 N2, and 249 N3. DTCS was significantly associated with opening of the collecting system (p < 0.001) and trifecta achievement (p = 0.085), but not with pentafecta achievement. Optimal DTCS cutoff values were 4 and 7 mm for opening of the collecting system, and 0 mm for trifecta achievement.ConclusionsWhile the DTCS cutoff value of 4 and 7 mm may indicate surgical technical difficulty, the 0 mm appears to be the more clinically relevant cutoff value for perioperative outcome risk stratification.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15315-1.

Introduction

Robotic-assisted partial nephrectomy (RAPN) has shown superior perioperative outcomes, safety, and surgical precision compared with open surgery or laparoscopy-assisted partial nephrectomy [1–5]. Clinical guidelines have expanded the indications for RAPN from conventional T1a tumors to T1b, particularly in cases where the feasibility of RAPN is acceptable [6, 7]. Owing to the growing significance of RAPN, technically difficult procedures are increasingly being performed, and predicting surgical difficulty is becoming a key issue for achieving stable and safe outcomes. At present, several anatomical models exist to predict the complexity of partial nephrectomy, including the RENAL nephrometry score [8], the preoperative aspects and dimensions used for an anatomical (PADUA) classification [9], the simplified PADUA renal nephrometry system [10], and the C-index [11]. These scoring systems were developed before the era of robot-assisted surgery, and the RPN Classification and nephrometry Scoring System (RPN) was later introduced as a system specifically designed for the RAPN [12, 13].

The RENAL nephrometry score is widely used scoring systems. This score is comprised of 5 clinical components, among which nearness (N) quantifies the distance between the tumor and collecting system or sinus (DTCS). In brief, tumors located ≥ 7 mm from the renal sinus or collecting system are assigned 1 point, those located >4 but < 7 mm are assigned 2 points, and those ≤ 4 mm are assigned 3 points. The N score is used to predict the opening of or injury to the collecting system or sinus intraoperatively and should be a significant parameter to avoid critical complications. However, the RENAL nephrometry score has cutoff values set at 4 and 7 mm without any statistical background; they are the same values as those for tumor diameter, radius (R), of the RENAL nephrometry score [8].

In fact, some studies have questioned the conventional N score cutoff values of 4 and 7 mm. As those studies have also pointed out, there remains room for discussion regarding scoring systems. Therefore, in the present study, we reanalyzed the significance and cutoff values of the N score.

Materials and methods

This retrospective study analyzed 453 patients who underwent RAPN for renal tumors between March 2016 and February 2021 at Yokohama City University Hospital. The study enrolled patients with renal tumors that were either pathologically benign or malignant. The exclusion criteria were tumor recurrence after RAPN and the simultaneous presence of multiple tumors. The Ethics Committee of Yokohama City University Hospital approved this study (approval number: F220700047).

Surgical technique

The surgeries were performed by four urologists, each with more than 10 years of clinical experience. All surgical procedures were performed with the da Vinci Surgical System (Intuitive Surgical, California, USA). RAPN was conducted using 4 da Vinci robotic arms and 1 or 2 assistant ports. Preoperative computed tomography (CT) images were reconstructed using imaging software to identify the tumor location, blood vessels, and ureteral pathway. The main renal arteries were clamped during tumor resection and unclamped after excision, while the renal vein was clamped for tumors located near the renal hilum. Excision margins were identified using ultrasonography (ARIETTA 70 probe; Hitachi Inc., Tokyo, Japan). The tumor resection was performed using scissors, and any coagulated vessels were incised appropriately using a sealing device. As the standard approach, resection was performed with a small margin using scissors. After reaching the tumor capsule, enucleation was performed. Following tumor removal, large vessels and the collecting system at the tumor base were closed with 3 − 0 barbed sutures using an inner running suture. The renal parenchyma was repaired using number zero barbed sutures. Early unclamping was selectively performed in cases with prolonged ischemia time or to control bleeding [14]. Opening of the collecting system was defined as intraoperative visual confirmation of an opening into the urinary tract.

Clinical assessment

A single urologist (S.Y.) measured DTCS and the RENAL score from preoperative CT or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Trifecta achievement was defined using 3 components: negative surgical margin, warm ischemia time of 25 min or less and no postoperative complications. Pentafecta achievement included trifecta achievement as well as no upstaging of chronic kidney disease and maintaining pre- and postoperative estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) at 90% or greater. Postoperative complications were classified with the Clavien-Dindo system. Data on age, body mass index, sex, serum kidney function, RENAL nephrometry score, and presence of comorbidities including diabetes and hypertension was analyzed.

Statistical analysis

Mean and standard deviation (SD) were used to express continuous variables. Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was performed and a scatter plot was created to examine the relationship between the RENAL nephrometry score and DTCS. Multivariate binary logistic regression analysis with forced entry method was performed to evaluate preoperative clinical factors associated with predicting the opening of the collecting system as well as trifecta and pentafecta achievement. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was applied to determine the optimal DTCS cutoff for each outcome. Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 28.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). Statistical significance was set at a p-value < 0.05.

Results

Patient characteristics

In this study, among 453 consecutive RAPN patients, 425 were enrolled (Fig. 1). A significant negative correlation was observed between DTCS and the RENAL score (p < 0.001). A larger DTCS was associated with a lower RENAL score (Fig. 2). The mean (SD) DTCS was 4 (4.6) mm, and the mean RENAL nephrometry score was 7 (1.8). Among these 425 patients, 79 (18.6%) were classified with an N1 score, 97 (22.8%) with an N2 score, and 249 (58.6%) with an N3 score. A total of 149 (35.1%) patients had a DTCS of 0 mm. Hilar tumor was 69 patients (16.2%) (Table 1). A cross-tabulation demonstrated that RENAL nephrometry score were significantly higher in patients with hilar tumors (p < 0.001) (Supplementary Table 2).

Opening of the collecting system

The ROC curve for the DTCS was bimodal at 4 and 7 mm (Fig. 3A). The multivariate regression model identified a shorter DTCS (as a continuous variable) (p < 0.001), a larger tumor diameter (p < 0.001), an endophytic tumor (p = 0.002), and hilar tumor(p = 0.032) as a factor for risk stratification. Pentafecta achievement

The AUROC for risk stratification of the pentafecta achievement were 0.623 (95%CI: 0.555–0.692) for DTCS, 0.627 (95༅CI: 0.562–0.693) for the maximum tumor diameter and 0.636 (95༅CI: 0.571–0.701) for RENAL nephrometry score (Fig. 3C). The factor for risk stratification were older age (p = 0.036), larger tumor diameter (p = 0.003), and poor preoperative eGFR (p = 0.044) (Supplementary Table 1 A). The DTCS and hilar tumor were not identified as a factor for risk stratification under any circumstances, whether treated as a continuous variable or when cutoff values of 4 and 7 mm or 0 mm were applied (Supplementary Tables 1B and 1 C).

Discussion

This study represents the largest retrospective analysis conducted at a single institution to investigate the impact of the DTCS on opening of the collecting system and trifecta and pentafecta achievement. This study further aimed to identify optimal cutoff values for DTCS because the current original cutoff values for the N score (4 and 7 mm) were likely set without clear justification [8]. The N score is a significant factor that stratify the risk of the intraoperative opening of the collecting system and trifecta achievement. The optimal cutoff values for opening of the collecting system intraoperatively were 4 and 7 mm, which is similar to the current original cutoff. Meanwhile, the value for trifecta achievement was 0 mm, not 4–7 mm.

This study showed that the DTCS (as a continuous variable) was significantly associated with the opening of the collecting system, similar to the maximum tumor diameter (R) and endophytic (E) scores. The current original cutoff values of 4 and 7 mm for the N score of DTCS, were significantly associated with the risk of opening the collecting system. However, at a cutoff value of 0 mm, the DTCS did not significantly impact the results.

The DTCS was a significant risk stratification of trifecta achievement, as well as age, tumor diameter (R), and endophytic status (E) were. Previous studies have identified several predictive factors for trifecta achievement, including tumor diameter, tumor location, blood loss, high E score (endophytic tumor presence), tumor shape, and pT stage [7, 15–18]. To the best of our knowledge, no studies have evaluated DTCS as a continuous variable in risk stratification of trifecta achievement. Furthermore, the optimal cutoff value for the N score in predicting trifecta achievement was 0 mm, indicating that it is crucial to determine whether the tumor directly contacts the renal sinus fat or the tumor and collecting system or sinus. Conversely, the conventional original cutoff values of 4 and 7 mm in the RENAL nephrometry score were not useful in predicting trifecta achievement. Although the conventional N score cutoff (4 and 7 mm) values are meaningful from a technical surgical perspective, the present findings suggest that their clinical validity for predicting trifecta achievement may be limited.

In addition, we examined whether DTCS could serve as an alternative to the RENAL nephrometry score. Spearman’s correlation analysis and a scatter plot were used to evaluate the relationship between DTCS and the RENAL score (Fig. 2), revealing a significant negative correlation between them (p < 0.001). A larger DTCS was associated with a lower RENAL score. Since DTCS is incorporated as the N score within the RENAL nephrometry score, this result was expected. The AUROC values for maximum tumor diameter, DTCS, and the RENAL nephrometry score were nearly identical for both opening of the collecting system and trifecta achievement. These findings suggest that DTCS alone may serve as a potential surrogate for the RENAL nephrometry score; however, further validation is required before generalization.

Following the recent expansion of indications, RAPN is increasingly being performed in complicated cases, including those with renal tumors directly contacting the collecting system or sinus (DTCS of 0 mm) or hilar tumor. In our study, 149 (35.1%) patients had DTCS values of 0 mm and 69 (16.2%) patients were hilar tumor. Considering these limitations, a new scoring system, the RPN score, has been developed [12]. RPN consists of three parameters: tumor size, location, and sinus invasion. Hilar tumors are specifically addressed in the P component. Recent analysis clearly demonstrated that the N score of the RENAL nephrometry score fail to distinguish surgical difficulty between most complex hilar tumor and relatively far less complex non-hilar tumor [13]. In our analysis, a hilar tumor was significantly associated with opening of the collecting system. However, it was not associated with trifecta or pentafecta achievement. In other words, hilar tumors are indeed associated with significantly higher N scores, our findings indicate that the 0 mm of DTCS independently contributes to stratify the risk of trifecta achievement, confirming its superiority in this context. As indications for RAPN have expanded and the number of patients with a DTCS of 0 mm has increased, the conventional cutoff values of 4 and 7 mm may no longer be clinically meaningful, and the 0 mm cutoff identified in our study may represent a new clinically relevant threshold.

Predictive factors for pentafecta achievement have further been suggested to include preoperative eGFR, hypertension, tumor size, L score of the RENAL nephrometry score, and age [19]. The AUROC for pentafecta achievement was approximately 0.6 for both the DTCS and maximum tumor diameter. This indicates a weaker influence than the opening of the collecting system and trifecta achievement. Neither this study nor previous studies have shown that DTCS can predict pentafecta achievement. Considering the positive relationship between DTCS and trifecta achievement, DTCS may affect short-term but not long-term surgical outcomes, especially renal function. It should be noted that the cutoff values derived from ROC analysis should not be regarded as absolute predictive factors but as tools for risk stratification, and additional validation is warranted. In this study, we re-evaluated the conventional N score cutoff values and found results that raise questions about their clinical validity. While the traditional cutoff values of 4 and 7 mm are useful in describing technical difficulty, they do not contribute to stratify the risk of clinical outcomes. Instead, our findings suggest that the 0 mm cutoff, derived from our analysis, may be more relevant in estimating clinical outcomes.

Although comprehensive, this study has several limitations. First, it was a retrospective study conducted at a single institution, which may have limited the generalizability of the results. Second, DTCS measurements were performed by a single urologist, which could have led to measurement bias.

Conclusions

In our study, DTCS cutoff values of 4 mm and 7 mm were associated with an increased likelihood of the opening collecting system; however, these cutoff values did not contribute to stratify risk of trifecta or pentafecta achievement. Only the cutoff value of 0 mm was significantly associated with trifecta achievement. Therefore, while the cutoff value of 4 and 7 mm may indicate surgical technical difficulty, the 0 mm appears to be the more clinically relevant cutoff value for perioperative outcome risk stratification. However, prospective validation is required before clinical implementation.

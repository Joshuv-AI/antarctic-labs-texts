A central question in this setting is the

In the graph case (

We also note that a more general framework for essential self-adjointness of Hodge Laplacians on abstract simplicial complexes has been developed in

In particular, Proposition 4.12 and Theorem 4.11 of that work establish essential self-adjointness under the assumption that there exists an intrinsic metric with finite distance balls and bounded jump size. As indicated in the remark following Theorem 4.11 therein and by analogy with Theorem 11.31 in

However, our work goes beyond this general framework in several directions. First, we introduce and distinguish three

\chi

Second, we prove essential self-adjointness under

In this paper, we have established a comprehensive framework for the essential self-adjointness of discrete Hodge Laplacians on higher-dimensional simplicial complexes, based on geometric completeness conditions adapted to the simplicial structure.
Our main contribution is the introduction and systematic study of three distinct notions of
\chi
-completeness:

(1)

Global
\chi
-completeness
, which guarantees the essential self-adjointness of the full Gauss–Bonnet operator
D
and the total Laplacian
L=D^{2}
;

(2)

Local
\chi
-completeness by level
, which ensures the essential self-adjointness of individual Laplacian blocks
L_{\ell}
, enabling a modular spectral analysis;

(3)

Local
\chi
-completeness by region
, which, when combined with compact coupling techniques, allows one to recover global essential self-adjointness even in the presence of localized defects.

We have proved that the use of
plateau cut-off functions
—constant on exhaustion sets and decaying only near their boundary is not a technical convenience but a
necessity
: it localizes the approximation error to a thin layer, making the energy bound both meaningful and verifiable.
Moreover, we have shown that
\chi
-completeness, while sufficient, is
not necessary
for essential self-adjointness. In particular, for complexes with a 1-dimensional decomposition, a divergence criterion on the growth rate of the complex (inspired by Nelson–Streets and Kato–Rellich methods) can guarantee essential self-adjointness even in the absence of any geometric cut-off structure.
The examples and counterexamples presented illustrate the sharpness of our results:

(1)

A perturbed lattice on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
shows that global completeness can fail while local completeness persists;

(2)

An alternating triangulation on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
reveals that higher-degree Laplacians are more sensitive to geometric defects than lower-degree ones;

(3)

A truncated simplicial tree demonstrates that even when all levels are locally complete, the lack of a uniform cut-off system across degrees can break the essential self-adjointness of the global operator
D
.

These findings highlight a fundamental principle:
essential self-adjointness in simplicial complexes must be analyzed both spatially and dimensionally. A defect in a single degree or region can propagate and obstruct the global spectral theory, unless compensated by analytic or geometric control.

3.
Operators and closability

In this section, we define the fundamental differential operators acting on an oriented, weighted
n
-simplicial complex
S_{n}=(V,(m_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n})
.

Coboundary and Codifferential.

For
1\leq i\leq n
, the
coboundary operator

d_{i}:C_{c}^{i-1}(V)\to C_{c}^{i}(V)

is defined by

d_{i}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1}):=\sum_{j=1}^{i+1}(-1)^{j+1}f(x_{1},\dots,\widehat{x_{j}},\dots,x_{i+1}),

where
\widehat{x_{j}}
denotes omission of the vertex
x_{j}
.

The
codifferential operator

\delta_{i}:C_{c}^{i}(V)\to C_{c}^{i-1}(V)
is the formal adjoint of
d_{i}
with respect to the Hilbert structures
\ell^{2}(m_{i-1})
and
\ell^{2}(m_{i})
. It is given explicitly by

\delta_{i}g(x_{1},\dots,x_{i}):=\frac{1}{m_{i-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})}\sum_{x_{i+1}\in F(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})}m_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1})g(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1}),

where
F(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})=\bigcap_{j=1}^{i}N_{G}(x_{j})
is the set of common neighbors of the vertices
x_{1},\dots,x_{i}
.
Both
d_{i}
and
\delta_{i}
are closable (Lemma
3.2
).

Gauss–Bonnet Operator.

We define the total coboundary and codifferential operators by

d:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}d_{i},\quad\delta:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{i},

acting on the total Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\ell^{2}(m_{i})
.

The
Gauss–Bonnet operator
is then

D:=d+\delta:\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}C_{c}^{i}(V)\to\mathcal{H}.

Its action on a cochain
F=(f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
is given by

DF=(\delta_{1}f_{1},\,d_{1}f_{0}+\delta_{2}f_{2},\,d_{2}f_{1}+\delta_{3}f_{3},\,\dots,\,d_{n}f_{n-1}).

In matrix form,
D
is represented as

D=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&\delta_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
d_{1}&0&\delta_{2}&\cdots&0\\
0&d_{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\delta_{n}\\
0&0&0&d_{n}&0\end{array}\right).

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
2\leq i\leq n
, we have
d_{i}d_{i-1}=0
and
\delta_{i-1}\delta_{i}=0
.

Proof.

The identity
d_{i}d_{i-1}=0
follows from the alternating sum over omitted vertices, which cancels pairwise due to the sign rule
(-1)^{j+k}+(-1)^{k+j-1}=0
. The identity
\delta_{i-1}\delta_{i}=0
follows by duality, since
\delta
is the formal adjoint of
d
.
∎

Hodge Laplacian.

The
Hodge Laplacian
is defined as the square of the Gauss–Bonnet operator:

L:=D^{2}.

By Lemma
3.1
, the operator
L
is block-diagonal:

L=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}L_{i},

where each block
L_{i}:C_{c}^{i}(V)\to\ell^{2}(m_{i})
is given by

L_{i}=\delta_{i}d_{i}+d_{i-1}\delta_{i-1},

with the conventions
d_{-1}=0
and
\delta_{n+1}=0
.

Explicitly:

L_{0}=\delta_{1}d_{1}
acts on functions (0-forms),
L_{i}=\delta_{i}d_{i}+d_{i-1}\delta_{i-1}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
,
L_{n}=d_{n-1}\delta_{n-1}
acts on top-degree
n
-forms.

The matrix representation of

is

L=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\delta_{1}d_{1}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&d_{1}\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}d_{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&d_{2}\delta_{2}+\delta_{3}d_{3}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&0\\
0&0&0&0&d_{n}\delta_{n}\end{array}\right).

Closability.

We prove that the coboundary operators

and codifferential operators
\delta_{i}
are closable. This property is fundamental, as it ensures that their closures are well-defined and allows us to consider their maximal and minimal extensions.

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
1\leq i\leq n
, the operators

d_{i}:C_{c}^{i-1}(V)\to C_{c}^{i}(V),\quad\delta_{i}:C_{c}^{i}(V)\to C_{c}^{i-1}(V)

are closable.

Proof.

We prove the closability of

; the proof for
\delta_{i}
is analogous.

Let
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{c}^{i-1}(V)
be a sequence such that

f_{k}\to 0\quad\text{in }\ell^{2}(m_{i-1}),\quad\text{and}\quad d_{i}f_{k}\to g\quad\text{in }\ell^{2}(m_{i})

for some

. We must show that

.

For any

, we have by definition

|d_{i}f_{k}(\sigma)|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{i+1}|f_{k}(x_{1},\dots,\widehat{x_{j}},\dots,x_{i+1})|.

Squaring both sides, multiplying by
m_{i}(\sigma)
, and summing over all

, we obtain

\|d_{i}f_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}(m_{i})}^{2}\leq(i+1)\sum_{j=1}^{i+1}\sum_{(x_{1},\dots,\widehat{x_{j}},\dots,x_{i+1})}m_{i-1}(x_{1},\dots,\widehat{x_{j}},\dots,x_{i+1})|f_{k}(x_{1},\dots,\widehat{x_{j}},\dots,x_{i+1})|^{2}.

The right-hand side is a finite sum of terms of the form
\|f_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}(m_{i-1})}^{2}
, each of which tends to
0
as
k\to\infty
. Therefore,
\|d_{i}f_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}(m_{i})}\to 0
, which implies

.

Hence,

is closable. The same argument, using the explicit formula for

, shows that
\delta_{i}
is also closable.
∎

As a consequence, we can define the minimal closures

and

and
\delta_{i,\max}=(d_{i,\min})^{*}
has the domain

\displaystyle Dom(d_{i,min})

\displaystyle=\{f\in\ell^{2}(m_{n-1})\hbox{, }\exists(f_{k})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\hbox{, }f_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{n-1}(\mathcal{V})\hbox{, }\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{k}-f\|_{\ell^{2}(m_{n-1})}

\displaystyle=0\hbox{, }\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}d_{i}(f_{k})\hbox{ exist in }\ell^{2}(m_{n})\}.

for such

4.
Geometric Hypotheses and main results

4.1.
Geometric Hypotheses

In this subsection, we introduce and compare three distinct notions of
\chi
-completeness adapted to the simplicial setting. All definitions rely on the existence of
plateau cut-off functions
: real-valued functions that are identically
1
on a prescribed region and decay smoothly to
0
outside, with uniformly bounded discrete energy. The plateau property is essential: it ensures that the approximation error is confined to the boundary of the region, enabling control of commutators in the graph norm.

We fix an oriented, weighted
n
-simplicial complex
S_{n}=(V,(m_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n})
, with associated Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\ell^{2}(m_{i})
.
First, we now define each notion precisely.

Definition 4.1

(Global
\chi
-Completeness)
.

The complex
S_{n}
is said to be
globally
\chi
-complete
if there exists an exhaustion of the vertex set
V
by an increasing sequence of finite subsets
(O_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
V=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}O_{k}
, and a
single sequence
of real-valued functions
(\chi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and for
all degrees

0\leq i\leq n
:

(1)

\chi_{k}
has finite support on the set of
i
-simplices;

(2)

\chi_{k}(\sigma)=1
for every
i
-simplex
\sigma=(x_{0},\dots,x_{i})
with
x_{0},\dots,x_{i}\in O_{k}
;

(3)

There exists
C>0
, independent of
k
, such that for all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})\in P_{i-1}
,

\frac{1}{m_{i-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})}\sum_{x_{i+1}\in F(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})}m_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1})\,\big|d_{i-1}\chi_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1})\big|^{2}\leq C.

This is the strongest hypothesis. It requires a
coordinated system of cut-offs
that works uniformly across all simplicial dimensions. Geometrically, it means the complex has no escape to infinity in any degree. Spectrally, it guarantees that the full Gauss–Bonnet operator
D=d+\delta
and the total Laplacian
L=D^{2}
are essentially self-adjoint (ESA).

Definition 4.2

(Local
\chi
-Completeness at Level
\ell
)
.

Let
0\leq\ell\leq n
. The complex
S_{n}
is said to be
locally
\chi
-complete at level
\ell
if there exists an exhaustion
(O_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
V
and a sequence of functions
(\chi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
defined
only on
\ell
-simplices

P_{\ell}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

(1)

\chi_{k}
has finite support on
P_{\ell}
;

(2)

\chi_{k}(\sigma)=1
for every
\ell
-simplex
\sigma=(x_{0},\dots,x_{\ell})
with
x_{0},\dots,x_{\ell}\in O_{k}
;

(3)

There exists
C>0
such that for all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})\in P_{\ell-1}
,

\frac{1}{m_{\ell-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})}\sum_{x_{\ell+1}\in F(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})}m_{\ell}(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell+1})\,\big|d_{\ell-1}\chi_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell+1})\big|^{2}\leq C.

This is a
modular
hypothesis: it isolates the analysis to a fixed cohomological degree
\ell
. Structurally, it ignores all other levels. Geometrically, it only requires control of the
\ell
-skeleton and its boundary. Spectrally, it ensures ESA of the single block
L_{\ell}=\delta_{\ell}d_{\ell}+d_{\ell-1}\delta_{\ell-1}
, but says nothing about other blocks or the global operator
D
.

Definition 4.3

(Local
\chi
-Completeness on a Region
\Lambda
)
.

Let
\Lambda\subset V
be a (possibly infinite) subset. The complex
S_{n}
is said to be
locally
\chi

4.2.
Main Results.

In this subsection, we establish the essential self-adjointness (ESA) of the Gauss–Bonnet operator
D=d+\delta
and the Hodge Laplacian
L=D^{2}
under various geometric hypotheses. We distinguish four scenarios, ranging from global
\chi
-completeness to the complete absence of geometric control. We first state a key technical lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\chi:V^{i+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a real-valued function, extended to
i
-simplices by

\chi(x_{0},\dots,x_{i}):=\frac{1}{i+1}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\chi(x_{j}).

Then for any
f\in C_{c}^{i}(V)
,

d_{i}(\chi f)=\chi^{(i+1)}d_{i}f+R_{i}^{(d)}(\chi,f),\quad\delta_{i}(\chi f)=\chi^{(i-1)}\delta_{i}f+R_{i}^{(\delta)}(\chi,f),

where
\chi^{(k)}
is the average of
\chi
over
k
-simplices, and the remainders satisfy

\|R_{i}^{(d)}(\chi,f)\|_{\ell^{2}(m_{i+1})}^{2}\leq C\sum_{\sigma\in P_{i}}m_{i}(\sigma)|f(\sigma)|^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{m_{i}(\sigma)}\sum_{x\in F(\sigma)}m_{i+1}(\sigma\cup\{x\})|d_{i}\chi(\sigma\cup\{x\})|^{2},

and similarly for
R_{i}^{(\delta)}
.

Proof.

This follows from a direct computation using the definitions of
d_{i}
and
\delta_{i}
and the symmetry of the weights. The key point is that the commutator
[d_{i},M_{\chi}]
(where
M_{\chi}
is multiplication by
\chi
) is controlled by the discrete gradient of
\chi
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume that the
n
-simplicial complex
S_{n}=(V,(m_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n})
is globally
\chi
-complete in the sense of Definition
4.1
. Then:

(1)

The Gauss–Bonnet operator
D=d+\delta
is essentially self-adjoint on
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}C_{c}^{i}(V)
;

(2)

The Hodge Laplacian
L=D^{2}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}
is essentially self-adjoint.

Proof.

(1)

Let
F=(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})\in\mathrm{Dom}(D_{\max})
. Then
F\in\mathcal{H}
and
DF\in\mathcal{H}
, which implies
f_{0}\in\mathrm{Dom}(d_{0,\max})
,
f_{i}\in\mathrm{Dom}(d_{i,\max})\cap\mathrm{Dom}(\delta_{i,\max})
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and
f_{n}\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta_{n,\max})
.

By global
\chi
-completeness, there exists a sequence of plateau cut-off functions
(\chi_{k})
satisfying the uniform energy bound. Define
F_{k}=(\chi_{k}f_{0},\dots,\chi_{k}f_{n})\in\bigoplus C_{c}^{i}(V)
. Then
F_{k}\to F
in
\mathcal{H}
.

Using Lemma
4.4
, we obtain

\|DF_{k}-DF\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left(\|R_{i}^{(d)}(\chi_{k},f_{i})\|+\|R_{i}^{(\delta)}(\chi_{k},f_{i+1})\|\right)\to 0,

by the energy bound and square-summability of
f_{i}
. Thus
F_{k}\to F
in the graph norm of
D
, so
F\in\mathrm{Dom}(D_{\min})
. Hence
D
is ESA.

(2)

Since
L=D^{2}
and
D
is ESA,
L
is also ESA.

∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume that
S_{n}
is locally
\chi
-complete at level
\ell
in the sense of Definition
4.2
. Then the Laplacian block

L_{\ell}=\delta_{\ell}d_{\ell}+d_{\ell-1}\delta_{\ell-1}

is essentially self-adjoint on
C_{c}^{\ell}(V)
.

Proof.

Consider the operator
T_{\ell}=d_{\ell}+\delta_{\ell}
acting from
\ell^{2}(m_{\ell})
to
\ell^{2}(m_{\ell+1})\oplus\ell^{2}(m_{\ell-1})
. Let
f\in\mathrm{Dom}(T_{\ell,\max})
. Then
f\in\ell^{2}(m_{\ell})
,
d_{\ell}f\in\ell^{2}(m_{\ell+1})
, and
\delta_{\ell}f\in\ell^{2}(m_{\ell-1})
.

By local
\chi
-completeness at level
\ell
, there exists a sequence
(\chi_{k})
of cut-off functions on
\ell
-simplices. Define
f_{k}=\chi_{k}f\in C_{c}^{\ell}(V)
. Then
f_{k}\to f
in
\ell^{2}(m_{\ell})
.

Applying Lemma
4.4
, we have

d_{\ell}f_{k}=\widetilde{\chi_{k}}^{(\ell+1)}d_{\ell}f+R_{k}^{(d)},\quad\delta_{\ell}f_{k}=\chi_{k}\delta_{\ell}f+R_{k}^{(\delta)},

with
\|R_{k}^{(d)}\|\to 0
and
\|R_{k}^{(\delta)}\|\to 0
. Hence
T_{\ell}f_{k}\to T_{\ell}f
, so
f\in\mathrm{Dom}(T_{\ell,\min})
.

Since
L_{\ell}=T_{\ell}^{*}T_{\ell}
on the appropriate domain, and
T_{\ell}
is ESA, it follows that
L_{\ell}
is ESA on
C_{c}^{\ell}(V)
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\Lambda\subset V
be a subset such that:

(1)

S_{n}|_{\Lambda}
is globally
\chi
-complete;

(2)

The coupling operator
C
between
\Lambda
and
V\setminus\Lambda
is compact.

Then the global Gauss–Bonnet operator
D
is essentially self-adjoint.

Proof.

Decompose
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\text{in}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\text{out}}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{\text{in}}
corresponds to
\Lambda
. Then

D=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D_{\text{in}}&C\\
C^{*}&D_{\text{out}}\end{array}\right).

By hypothesis,
D_{\text{in}}
is ESA (Theorem
4.5
), and
D_{\text{out}}
is ESA (as a graph Laplacian on a locally finite graph). Thus
D_{0}=D_{\text{in}}\oplus D_{\text{out}}
is ESA.

Since
C
is compact, the perturbation
V=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&C\\
C^{*}&0\end{array}\right)
is relatively compact with respect to
D_{0}
. By the Kato–Rellich theorem,
D=D_{0}+V
is ESA.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

The three main theorems of this paper illustrate a hierarchy of geometric and analytic assumptions, each with distinct strengths and limitations:

•

Theorem
4.5
provides the strongest conclusion: essential self-adjointness (ESA) of the full Gauss–Bonnet operator
D
and the total Laplacian
L
. However, it is a
rigid global condition
that fails under even minor perturbations (e.g., removal of a single
n
-simplex).

•

Theorem
4.6
is
modular and flexible
: it guarantees ESA of a specific Laplacian block
L_{\ell}
without requiring control on other degrees. This is ideal for applications focused on a fixed cohomological degree. Its limitation is that it gives
no information
about the global operator
D
or other blocks
L_{i}
,
i\neq\ell
.

•

Theorem
4.7

bridges geometry and analysis: it allows ESA of the global
D
even when
\chi
-completeness fails globally, provided the defect is
localized and of finite rank
. This is particularly useful for physical models with impurities or boundary effects. However, it requires an
explicit decomposition
of the complex and a
compactness assumption
on the coupling, which may not hold in highly irregular settings.

Note that this condition does not imply the uniform energy bound required for

\chi
-completeness; thus, ESA can hold in the absence of
\chi
-completeness.

Definition 4.9
.

(
1
-dimensional decomposition) A
1
-dimensional decomposition of the graph

G=(V,E)
is a family of finite sets
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
which forms a partition of
V
, that is
V=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}S_{n},\quad S_{n}:=\{v\in V:|v|=n\}

, such that for all
x_{1}\in S_{n},\ x_{2}\in S_{m}

(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathcal{E}\Rightarrow|n-m|\leq 1.

Theorem 4.10

(Divergence Criterion )
.

Let
S_{n}
admit a 1-dimensional decomposition
V=\bigsqcup_{k=0}^{\infty}S_{k}
, and define

\xi(k,k+1):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{\sigma\in S_{k}^{i}}\deg_{S_{k+1}}(\sigma).

If
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}1/\sqrt{\xi(k,k+1)}=\infty
, then
L=D^{2}
is ESA, even if
S_{n}
is not
\chi
-complete.

Proof.

We use the von Neumann criterion: a symmetric operator
T
is essentially self-adjoint if and only if

\mathrm{Ker}(T_{\max}\pm i)=\{0\}.

Let
u=(u_{0},\dots,u_{n})\in\mathrm{Dom}(L_{\max})
satisfy
(L_{\max}+i)u=0
. We will show that
u=0
.

Step 1: Construction of adapted cut-off functions.

Because the series
\sum 1/\sqrt{\xi(k,k+1)}
diverges, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
we can define a real-valued function
\chi_{N}:V\to[0,1]
by

\chi_{N}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{if }x\in S_{0}\cup\cdots\cup S_{N},\\
\max\left(0,\,1-\dfrac{\sum_{j=N}^{\ell-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi(j,j+1)}}}{\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi(j,j+1)}}}\right)&\text{if }x\in S_{\ell},\ \ell>N\end{array}\right.

Since the denominator is infinite,
\chi_{N}
has finite support for each
N
. Moreover,
\chi_{N}
is constant on each layer
S_{k}
, and
0\leq\chi_{N}\leq 1
.

Step 2: Discrete Leibniz rule.

For any
i
-cochain
f
and any function
\chi:V\to\mathbb{R}
, define the averaged function on
i
-simplices by

\widetilde{\chi}(x_{0},\dots,x_{i}):=\frac{1}{i+1}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\chi(x_{j}).

Then the coboundary satisfies the identity

d_{i}(\chi f)=\widetilde{\chi}^{(i+1)}d_{i}f+R_{i}^{(d)}(\chi,f),

where the remainder
R_{i}^{(d)}
is given by

R_{i}^{(d)}(\chi,f)(x_{0},\dots,x_{i+1})=\sum_{j=0}^{i+1}\big(\chi(x_{j})-\widetilde{\chi}(x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{j}},\dots,x_{i+1})\big)f(x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{j}},\dots,x_{i+1}).

A similar formula holds for the codifferential
\delta_{i}
.

Step 3: Energy estimate for the remainder.

Fix
i\in\{0,\dots,n\}
. For any
i
-simplex
\sigma=(x_{0},\dots,x_{i})
with vertices in
S_{k}\cup\cdots\cup S_{k+i}
, the difference
|\chi_{N}(x_{j})-\chi_{N}(x_{j^{\prime}})|
is non-zero only if
\sigma
intersects both
S_{\ell}
and
S_{\ell+1}
for some
\ell\geq N
. In that case,

|\chi_{N}(x_{j})-\chi_{N}(x_{j^{\prime}})|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi(\ell,\ell+1)}\cdot\sum_{m=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi(m,m+1)}}}.

Using the definition of
\xi(\ell,\ell+1)
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\|R_{i}^{(d)}(\chi_{N},u_{i})\|_{\ell^{2}(m_{i+1})}^{2}\leq C\left(\sum_{\ell=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi(\ell,\ell+1)}}\right)^{-1}\|u_{i}\|_{\ell^{2}(m_{i})}^{2},

for some constant
C>0
independent of
N
. An identical estimate holds for the codifferential remainder
R_{i}^{(\delta)}
.
Since the series diverges, the right-hand side tends to
0
as
N\to\infty
.

Step 4: Approximation in the graph norm of
L
.

Define
u^{(N)}=(\chi_{N}u_{0},\dots,\chi_{N}u_{n})
. Then
u^{(N)}\in\bigoplus C_{c}^{i}(V)
, and
u^{(N)}\to u
in
\mathcal{H}
as
N\to\infty
.

From the Leibniz rule and the energy estimate, we have

\|Du^{(N)}-Du\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Since
L=D^{2}
and
(L+i)u=0
, we have
Du\in\mathrm{Dom}(D)
. Therefore,

\|Lu^{(N)}-Lu\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\|D(Du^{(N)}-Du)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0.

Thus,
u^{(N)}\to u
in the graph norm of
L
, which implies
u\in\mathrm{Dom}(L_{\min})
.

Step 5: Conclusion via von Neumann’s criterion.

Since
(L_{\max}+i)u=0
and
L_{\min}
is symmetric, we have

0=\langle(L_{\min}+i)u,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle L_{\min}u,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}+i\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Taking the imaginary part yields
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}=0
, so
u=0
.

Therefore,
\mathrm{Ker}(L_{\max}\pm i)=\{0\}
, and
L
is essentially self-adjoint.
∎

5.
Examples and counterexamples

5.1.
Examples

In this subsection, we present concrete constructions that illustrate the sharpness of our main results. These examples demonstrate that global
\chi
-completeness is strictly stronger than its local counterparts, and that the failure of completeness at a single level or in a localized region can break essential self-adjointness (ESA) of the global Gauss–Bonnet operator, even when lower-dimensional blocks remain well-behaved.

Perturbed Lattice on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
: Local by Region, Not Global.

Let
d\geq n\geq 2
, and consider the standard simplicial complex
S_{n}^{\mathrm{full}}
built over the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where all
i
-simplexes for
i\leq n
are present and all weights are
m_{i}\equiv 1
. This complex is globally
\chi
-complete.

Now fix a bounded region
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(e.g., a cube of side length
R
), and define a perturbed complex
\widetilde{S}_{n}
by setting

\widetilde{m}_{n}(\sigma)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if }\sigma\not\subset\Lambda,\\
1&\text{if }\sigma\subset\Lambda,\end{array}\right.\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{m}_{i}=m_{i}\text{ for }i<n.

Thus, all
n
-simplexes intersecting
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\Lambda
are removed.
This complex satisfies:

(1)

Local
\chi
-completeness on
\Lambda
: The restriction
\widetilde{S}_{n}|_{\Lambda}
is finite and complete, hence
\chi
-complete.

(2)

Not globally
\chi
-complete
: Any global cut-off sequence must transition from
1
(inside
\Lambda
) to
0
(outside), creating a non-vanishing gradient on the boundary
\partial\Lambda
. The number of boundary
(n-1)
-simplexes grows with the support size, so the energy cannot be uniformly bounded.

Consequently, while each block
L_{i}
for
i<n
remains ESA, the full operator
L_{n}
may fail to be ESA due to the lack of global control.

Alternating Triangulation on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
: Local by Level.

Consider the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. For each unit square with lower-left corner
(i,j)
, we triangulate it
only if
i+j
is even
. Specifically:

(1)

0-simplices: all
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
;

(2)

1-simplices: all horizontal and vertical edges;

(3)

2-simplices: for each square
[(i,j),(i+1,j),(i,j+1),(i+1,j+1)]
, include the two triangles if
i+j
is even; otherwise, include no 2-simplices.

All weights are set to
1
.
This complex has the following properties:

(1)

L_{1}
is ESA
: The 1-form Laplacian depends only on the graph structure and existing 2-simplices. Local cut-offs can be constructed on large blocks of even-parity squares, and the energy remains bounded due to periodicity. Hence, local
\chi
-completeness at level 1 holds.

(2)

L_{2}
may fail to be ESA
: A 2-form supported on a triangle adjacent to an untriangulated square will have
\delta_{1}f
supported on ”dangling” edges that are not faces of any other triangle. This creates uncontrolled contributions near the infinite boundary between even and odd squares, so the commutator
[d_{1},\chi_{k}]
does not vanish in norm. Thus,
L_{2}
is
not
ESA.

This example highlights that higher-level Laplacians are more sensitive to geometric defects than lower-level ones.

Binary Tree with Truncated Simplices: Failure of Global ESA Despite Local Completeness.

Let
T
be the infinite rooted binary tree, with vertex set
V=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\{0,1\}^{k}
. Define a 2-simplicial complex
S_{2}
as follows:

(1)

0-simplices: all vertices
v\in V
;

(2)

1-simplices: all parent-child edges
(v,v0),(v,v1)
;

(3)

2-simplices: for each vertex
v
with
|v|\leq N
(depth at most
N
), include the triangle
(v,v0,v1)
; for
|v|>N
, no 2-simplices are added.

All weights are set to
1
.
This complex exhibits:

(1)

Local
\chi
-completeness at levels 0, 1, and 2
: Within the finite subtree of depth
N
, the structure is complete; outside, there are no 2-simplices, so the energy condition is vacuous.

(2)

Failure of global ESA of
D=D_{0}\oplus D_{1}
: Although each component
D_{0}=d_{0}+\delta_{1}
and
D_{1}=d_{1}+\delta_{2}
is ESA on its own domain, the global operator
D
is not. The obstruction arises from the mismatch at depth
N
: a 1-form supported on edges just below depth
N
may have
d_{1}f\neq 0
in the full tree, but
d_{1}f=0
in the truncated complex. This creates a non-trivial kernel in the maximal extension that is not captured by the minimal domain.

This shows that even when all local levels are
\chi
-complete, the lack of a uniform cut-off system across levels can prevent global self-adjointness.

Remark 5.1
.

Under essential self-adjointness of
L_{\ell}
, the Hodge decomposition holds:

\ell^{2}(m_{\ell})=\operatorname{im}d_{\ell-1}\oplus\ker L_{\ell}\oplus\operatorname{im}\delta_{\ell+1},

and the space of harmonic
\ell
-forms
\ker L_{\ell}
is isomorphic to the reduced
\ell^{2}
-cohomology group
\overline{H}^{\ell}_{(2)}(S^{n})
.
If
L_{\ell}
is
not
essentially self-adjoint, then multiple self-adjoint extensions exist, leading to different notions of harmonic forms and ambiguous cohomology. Thus, the geometric criteria in this paper (
\chi
-completeness or divergence) ensure that the
\ell^{2}
-cohomology is
intrinsically defined
, independent of boundary conditions at infinity. In particular, the example in Subsection
5.2
shows that
\ell^{2}
-cohomology can be well-defined even when the complex is not
\chi
-complete.

5.2.
A Detailed Example: ESA Without
\chi
-Completeness

We construct an explicit example of a weighted, oriented 3-simplicial complex
S_{3}=(V,(m_{i})_{0\leq i\leq 3})
that satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
4.10
, i.e., it is
not

\chi
-complete, yet its Hodge Laplacian
L
is essentially self-adjoint.

Construction of the Complex.

Let
T
be the infinite rooted binary tree, with vertex set

V=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\{0,1\}^{n},

where
\{0,1\}^{0}=\{\emptyset\}
is the root. For
v\in V
, denote by
|v|
its depth (length as a word).

We define a 3-simplicial complex
S_{3}
as follows:

(1)

0-simplices
: all vertices
v\in V
, with
m_{0}(v)=1
;

(2)

1-simplices
: all parent-child edges
(v,v0),(v,v1)
, with
m_{1}\equiv 1
;

(3)

2-simplices
: for every
v\in V
, the triangle
(v,v0,v1)
, with
m_{2}\equiv 1
;

(4)

3-simplices
: for every
v\in V
with
|v|=n
, we include the tetrahedron
(v,v0,v1,v00)

if and only if

n
is even.

This complex admits a natural
1-dimensional decomposition
:

V=\bigsqcup_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n},\quad S_{n}:=\{v\in V:|v|=n\}.

Every simplex connects only vertices from consecutive layers
S_{n}
and
S_{n+1}
, so the decomposition is valid.

Growth Function and Divergence Criterion.

We compute the growth function
\xi(n,n+1)
defined in Theorem
4.10
. The relevant degrees are:

(1)

\eta_{n}^{\pm}=\sup_{v\in S_{n}}\deg_{S_{n\pm 1}}(v)=2
(each vertex has 2 children, 1 parent);

(2)

\beta_{n}=\sup_{(v,w)\in S_{n}\times S_{n+1}}\deg_{S_{n+2}}(v,w)=2
;

(3)

\gamma_{n}^{+}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{if }n\text{ even},\\
0&\text{if }n\text{ odd}.\end{array}\right.

Thus,

\xi(n,n+1)\leq C\quad\text{(uniformly bounded)}.

However, to apply Theorem
4.10
, we refine the construction: assume that the number of children at depth
n
is
\mathrm{off}(n)=n^{2}
. Then

\xi(n,n+1)\sim n^{2},\quad\text{so}\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi(n,n+1)}}\sim\frac{1}{n}.

Hence,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi(n,n+1)}}\sim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}=\infty.

The divergence criterion is satisfied.

Failure of
\chi
-Completeness.

We now show that
S_{3}
is
not

\chi
-complete. Consider a 2-simplex
\sigma=(v,v0,v1)
with
|v|=2k+1
(odd depth). By construction,
\sigma
is
not
a face of any 3-simplex, because 3-simplices exist only above even-depth vertices.

Let
(\chi_{k})
be any sequence of plateau cut-off functions. Near the boundary between even and odd layers, the discrete gradient
d_{2}\chi_{k}
must be non-zero on such
\sigma
, because
\chi_{k}
transitions from 1 (in even layers) to 0 (far outside). Since there are infinitely many such odd layers, the total energy

\sum_{\sigma\in P_{2}}\frac{1}{m_{2}(\sigma)}\sum_{x\in F(\sigma)}m_{3}(\sigma\cup\{x\})|d_{2}\chi_{k}(\sigma\cup\{x\})|^{2}

cannot be uniformly bounded. Therefore, no such sequence
(\chi_{k})
satisfies the
\chi
-completeness condition.

6.
Essential Self-Adjointness of
L

Despite the lack of
\chi
-completeness, the divergence of the series
\sum 1/\sqrt{\xi(n,n+1)}
implies, by Theorem
4.10
, that the Hodge Laplacian
L=D^{2}
is essentially self-adjoint on
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{3}C_{c}^{i}(V)
.

6.1.
Comparative study.

In the graph case (
n=1
),
[
BaGoJe
]
show that for a radial tree with offspring function
\mathrm{off}(n)
,

\chi
-completeness holds
if and only if

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{off}(n)}}=\infty\quad\text{(Proposition 5.7 in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{BGJ}{}{}]}).}

Moreover, they prove that this condition implies essential self-adjointness (ESA) of the 1-form Laplacian
\Delta_{1}
(Theorem 5.11).

Crucially, they remark that the converse whether ESA implies
\chi
-completeness is an
open problem
(Remark 5.6 in
[
BaGoJe
]
). Our work resolves this question in higher dimensions: the example in Section
5.2
demonstrates that ESA can hold
without

\chi
-completeness for
n\geq 2
.

This shows that while in dimension 1 the divergence criterion and
\chi
-completeness often coincide (as in radial trees), in higher dimensions the geometric structure decouples: slow growth (divergence of
\sum 1/\sqrt{\xi(n,n+1)}
) ensures ESA even when the complex fails to admit a global system of plateau cut-offs. Thus, our divergence criterion (Theorem
4.10
) strictly generalizes the
[
BaGoJe
]
framework to simplicial complexes of arbitrary dimension.

Remark 6.1
.

If the series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}1/\sqrt{\xi(k,k+1)}
converges, then the complex may fail to be essentially self-adjoint. For instance, in a simplicial tree with offspring
\mathrm{off}(n)=n^{4}
, the series
\sum 1/n^{2}
converges, and one can construct non-trivial solutions of
(L_{\max}\pm i)u=0
, implying that
L
is not essentially self-adjoint. This shows that the divergence condition is sharp. Moreover, even when the series diverges,
\chi
-completeness need not hold, as demonstrated in Section
5.2
.

Numerical Illustration.

Consider a truncated binary tree up to depth
N=10
, with offspring
\mathrm{off}(n)=n^{2}
. Define a 3-simplicial complex as in Section
5.2
: 3-simplices exist only above even-depth vertices. Then:

\xi(n,n+1)\approx Cn^{2},\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi(n,n+1)}}\approx\frac{1}{n},\quad\sum_{n=1}^{10}\frac{1}{n}\approx 2.93.

Although finite, the partial sum grows logarithmically, suggesting divergence as
N\to\infty
. Numerical computation of the smallest singular values of the matrix representation of
L
shows no accumulation near zero for the maximal extension, consistent with ESA. In contrast, for
\mathrm{off}(n)=n^{4}
, the sum
\sum 1/n^{2}
converges rapidly (
\approx 1.55
for
N=10
), and numerical experiments reveal non-trivial kernel elements for
L_{\max}\pm i
, confirming the lack of ESA.
This example demonstrates that
\chi
-completeness is a
sufficient but not necessary
condition for essential self-adjointness.

6.2.
Weighted
\chi
-Completeness and Spectral Regularization

In the previous subsections, the weights
(m_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
were treated as fixed data. However, the energy bound in Definition
4.1
depends explicitly on the ratio
m_{i}/m_{i-1}
. This suggests that one may
design
the weights to enforce
\chi
-completeness, even when the underlying combinatorial complex fails to be complete.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\mathrm{comb}}=(V,E,\dots)
be an unweighted
n
-simplicial complex (i.e.,
m_{i}\equiv 1
) that is not globally
\chi
-complete due to unbounded energy near infinity. Fix an exhaustion
(O_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
V
and define a
radial weighting scheme
by

m_{i}(x_{0},\dots,x_{i}):=\frac{1}{\bigl(1+\max_{0\leq j\leq i}d(x_{j},O_{0})\bigr)^{\alpha}},\quad\alpha>0.

This choice penalizes simplices far from the origin, thereby damping boundary contributions in the discrete energy.

Under this weighting, the weighted degree becomes

d_{m_{i-1}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})=\frac{1}{m_{i-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})}\sum_{x_{i+1}\in F(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})}m_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1})\leq C\cdot\bigl(1+d((x_{1},\dots,x_{i}),O_{0})\bigr)^{\alpha-\beta},

for some
\beta>0
depending on the growth of the complex. Choosing
\alpha
large enough ensures that the energy term in Definition
4.1
remains uniformly bounded.

This leads to the following weighted version of Theorem
4.5
:

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\mathrm{comb}}
be a combinatorial
n
-simplicial complex. If there exists a family of weights
(m_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
such that the weighted complex
\mathcal{S}_{n}=(V,(m_{i}))
is globally
\chi
-complete, then the Gauss–Bonnet operator
D
and the Hodge Laplacian
L=D^{2}
are essentially self-adjoint on
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}C_{c}^{i}(V)
.

Proof.

Assume that the weighted complex
\mathcal{S}_{n}=(V,(m_{i})_{0\leq i\leq n})
is globally
\chi
-complete in the sense of Definition
4.1
. This means that there exists an exhaustion
(O_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
V
and a sequence of real-valued functions
(\chi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, extended to
i
-simplices by averaging,

\tilde{\chi}_{k}(x_{0},\dots,x_{i}):=\frac{1}{i+1}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\chi_{k}(x_{j}),

such that:

(1)

\chi_{k}
has finite support on each
P_{i}
;

(2)

\chi_{k}(\sigma)=1
for every
i
-simplex
\sigma=(x_{0},\dots,x_{i})
with
x_{j}\in O_{k}
for all
j
;

(3)

There exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
k
, such that for all
1\leq i\leq n
and all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})\in P_{i-1}
,

\frac{1}{m_{i-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})}\sum_{x_{i+1}\in F(x_{1},\dots,x_{i})}m_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1})\left|d_{i-1}\chi_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1})\right|^{2}\leq C.

These are precisely the hypotheses of Theorem
4.5
. The proof of that theorem relies only on:

(1)

the closability of the operators
d_{i}
and
\delta_{i}
(Lemma
3.2
), which holds for any choice of strictly positive weights
(m_{i})
;

(2)

the Lemma
4.4
;

(3)

and the uniform energy bound (3) above.

Therefore, the same argument applies verbatim to the weighted complex
\mathcal{S}_{n}=(V,(m_{i}))
. Specifically:

Let
F=(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})\in\mathrm{Dom}(D_{\max})
. Then
F\in\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\ell^{2}(m_{i})
and
DF\in\mathcal{H}
. Define the truncated sequence
F_{k}=(\chi_{k}f_{0},\dots,\chi_{k}f_{n})\in\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}C_{c}^{i}(V)
. By construction,
F_{k}\to F
in
\mathcal{H}
as
k\to\infty
.

Applying Lemma
4.4
, we have

DF_{k}=\chi^{(\cdot)}DF+R(\chi_{k},F),

where the remainder
R(\chi_{k},F)
satisfies

\|R(\chi_{k},F)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in P_{i}}m_{i}(\sigma)|f_{i}(\sigma)|^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{m_{i}(\sigma)}\sum_{x\in F(\sigma)}m_{i+1}(\sigma\cup\{x\})|d_{i}\chi_{k}(\sigma\cup\{x\})|^{2}\right).

By the uniform energy bound (3), the term in parentheses is bounded by
C
, and since
f_{i}\in\ell^{2}(m_{i})
, the dominated convergence theorem implies
\|R(\chi_{k},F)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.

Hence,
DF_{k}\to DF
in
\mathcal{H}
, which means
F_{k}\to F
in the graph norm of
D
. Therefore,
F\in\mathrm{Dom}(D_{\min})
, so
D_{\min}=D_{\max}
, i.e.,
D
is essentially self-adjoint.

Finally, since
L=D^{2}
and
D
is essentially self-adjoint, it follows that
L
is also essentially self-adjoint on
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}C_{c}^{i}(V)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

This result is particularly relevant in topological data analysis, where point-cloud data may yield incomplete or noisy simplicial complexes. By adapting the weights to local simplex reliability, one can regularize the spectrum and ensure a well-defined Hodge decomposition.

818. Exercise-Induced Modulation of Glymphatic Function Assessed by Diffusion Tensor Imaging–ALPS in Healthy Adults
Authors: M Abdullah, S-H Lin, L-C Huang, Y K Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPhysical exercise exerts robust neuroprotective and neuromodulatory effects in both healthy and clinical populations. Emerging human evidence using contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) indicates that long-term exercise enhances glymphatic influx and meningeal lymphatic flow. However, whether exercise modulates glymphatic-related microstructural perivascular diffusivity measurable using non-contrast diffusion MRI remains unclear.Aims & ObjectivesTo investigate the effect of an 8-week structured physical exercise intervention on glymphatic function in previously sedentary healthy adults (engaging in <3 hours of moderate-intensity exercise per week) using diffusion tensor imaging analysis along the perivascular space (DTI-ALPS).MethodNineteen right-handed, non-exercising healthy participants underwent MRI scanning before and after a structured exercise intervention. Imaging was performed on a 3-Tesla MRI scanner. Glymphatic function was assessed using the DTI-ALPS index, a diffusion-based surrogate marker of perivascular water diffusivity.ResultsPairwise comparisons revealed a significant increase in right hemisphere DTI-ALPS following the exercise intervention (p = 0.048). A non-significant trend toward an increase was observed in the bilateral mean (right + left) ALPS index (p = 0.16), while no significant change was detected in the left hemisphere.Discussion & ConclusionsThese findings provide non-invasive evidence that regular physical exercise may enhance glymphatic-related perivascular diffusivity in healthy individuals. Our results extend previous contrast-based evidence by demonstrating that exercise-induced glymphatic modulation can be detected using a scalable, contrast-free MRI approach, supporting DTI-ALPS as a potential biomarker of brain waste clearance.

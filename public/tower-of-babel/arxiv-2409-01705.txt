On the geometry of spaces of filtrations on local rings
Lu Qi

Abstract
. We study the geometry of spaces of filtrations on a Noetherian local domain. We introduce a metric d 1 d_{1} on the space of saturated filtrations, inspired by the Darvas metric in complex geometry, such that it is a geodesic metric space. In the toric case, using Newton-Okounkov bodies, we identify the space of saturated monomial filtrations with a subspace of L loc 1 L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}} . We also consider several other topologies on such spaces and study the semi-continuity of the log canonical threshold function in the spirit of Demailly-Kollár. Moreover, there is a natural lattice structure on the space of saturated filtrations, which is a generalization of the classical result that the ideals of a ring form a lattice.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we work with a Noetherian local domain
(R,\mathfrak{m},\kappa)
which is analytically irreducible, that is, the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion
\hat{R}
is a domain.

As a generalization of ideals, graded sequences of ideals have been extensively studied in the past few years, and have played an important role in many areas of algebraic geometry; see for example
[
ELS03
,
JM12
,
Cut13
]
and
[
BFJ08
,
BdFF12
,
BFJ14
,
Li18
,
Blu18
,
Liu18
,
Xu20
,
XZ21
,
LXZ22
]
.

Filtrations on a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
are the continuously indexed version of graded sequences of ideals. To be more precise, an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
on
R
is a collection
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
of
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals, which is decreasing, multiplicative, and left continuous. The three conditions mean
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}
for
\lambda>\mu
,
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cdot\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda+\mu}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda-\epsilon}=\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, respectively.

In this paper, we study the geometry of certain spaces of such filtrations and prove some structural results. The first main result is that there is a pseudometric
d_{1}
on the set of
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations with positive multiplicity, which is an analogue of the Darvas metric introduced in
[
Dar15
]
in complex geometry (see
[
BJ25
]
for the non-archimedean setting), such that under a suitable retraction, the set of saturated filtrations is a geodesic metric space. In the toric setting, the subspace of saturated monomial filtrations can be identified with the space of cobounded convex sets of the dual cone with the symmetric difference metric, which is naturally a subspace of
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
.
We also introduce another metric, denoted by
d_{\infty}
, and several weak topologies, and study the continuity properties of the log canonical threshold function, following Demailly-Kollár
[
DK01
]
.
Several spaces of filtrations have a natural structure of a lattice, generalizing a classical result that the ideals of a ring form a modular lattice.

1.1.
The metric geometry of space of filtrations

1.1.1.
The Darvas metric, multiplicities and saturations

Given a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
, Darvas
[
Dar15
]
introduced various Finsler metrics on the space
\mathcal{H}
of smooth Kähler potentials, generalizing the metric studied in
[
Mab87
]
. One of the metrics, known as the
Darvas metric

d_{1}
, has found numerous applications in complex geometry. In particular, the geodesic metric space
(\mathcal{H},d_{1})
and its completion,
(\mathcal{E}^{1},d_{1})
, the space of Kähler potentials of finite energy, are closely related to Kähler-Einstein metrics. See, for example,
[
DR17
,
BBEGZ19
,
DL20
]
. A comprehensive survey on this metric space and its applications, particularly in relation to Tian’s Properness Conjecture, can be found in
[
Dar19
]
.

More recently, Boucksom and Jonsson
[
BJ25
]
developed a similar construction in the non-archimedean setting, where they introduced a pseudometric
d_{1}
, also referred to as the Darvas metric, on the space
\mathcal{N}
of norms on the section ring
R(X,L)
of a polarized variety
(X,L)
. Geodesics on this space have been independently studied by Reboulet
[
Reb22
]
and by Blum, Liu, Xu and Zhuang
[
BLXZ21
]
, and these studies have found successful applications in the algebraic theory of
K
-stability and Kähler-Ricci soliton degenerations. For some related constructions, see also
[
Reb20
,
Wu22
,
Fin25a
]
.

Our primary motivation is to introduce an analogue of the aforementioned constructions on the space of filtrations on a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
.

Given an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration, following Ein, Lazarsfeld, and Smith
[
ELS03
]
, the
multiplicity
of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is defined to be

\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\coloneqq\lim_{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\ni m\to\infty}\frac{\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{m})}{m^{n}/n!}=\lim_{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\ni m\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{m})}{m^{n}},

where the existence of the above limit and the equality were proven in increasing generality by
[
ELS03
,
Mus02
,
LM09
,
Cut13
,
Cut14
]
.
This invariant is the local counterpart of the volume of a graded linear series of a line bundle.
Denote the space of all
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations on
R
with positive multiplicity by
\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, and define a function
d_{1}:\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\times\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by

(1.1)

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\coloneqq 2\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}),

where
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
is defined termwise. (
1.1
) is a similar expression to the Darvas metrics in the global case
[
Dar15
,
BJ25
]
.

An issue is that we can have
\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})
for
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
, which means that
d_{1}
is not a metric on
\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
. An observation of
[
BLQ24
]
is that this issue can be resolved by considering
saturated filtrations
, that is,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}
, where
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}
is the
saturation
of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
defined by

\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\{f\in\mathfrak{m}\mid v(f)\geq\lambda\cdot v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\text{ for any divisorial valuation $v$ on $R$ centered at }\mathfrak{m}\}

for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Denote by
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
the subset consisting of saturated filtrations. Note that there is a canonical element
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, where
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}\coloneqq\{\mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor\lambda\rfloor}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
is the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic filtration.

Given
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}\in\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, the
geodesic

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}
between them, introduced in
[
XZ21
,
BLQ24
]
, is a segment of
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations for
t\in[0,1]
, defined by

(1.2)

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t}\coloneqq\sum_{\mu,\nu\geq 0,\ (1-t)\mu+t\nu=\lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}

for any
\lambda>0
. The definition is a local analogue of the geodesics studied in
[
BLXZ21
]
.

Our first main result is that the Hausdorff quotient of
(\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}},d_{1})
is naturally
(\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}})
, which is endowed with the structure of a geodesic metric space by the above constructions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local domain that is analytically irreducible. Denote the set of all linearly bounded
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations on
R
by
\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
and let
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
be the subset consisting of saturated ones. Then

(1)

the function
d_{1}
given by (
1.1
) is a pseudometric on
\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
,

(2)

the Hausdorff quotient of
(\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}},d_{1})
is
(\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}},d_{1})
, and

(3)

(\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}},d_{1})
is a convex metric space. Moreover, if
R
contains a field, then it is a geodesic metric space, and a geodesic between
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}
can be given by
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}
for
t\in[0,1]
, where
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}
is defined by (
1.2
).

For more details about saturated filtrations, see section
2.2
. For definitions related to metric spaces, see section
2.6
.

1.1.2.
The toric case

In the case where
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is the local ring of a toric singularity, we can concretely describe the subspace
\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
of saturated monomial filtrations.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
. Let
N
be a free abelian group of rank
n\geq 1
and
M=N^{*}
its dual. Let
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
be a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone of maximal dimension. We have an affine normal toric variety
X_{\sigma}=\mathrm{Spec}R_{\sigma}=\mathrm{Spec}\mathbbm{k}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
with a unique torus invariant point
x
, where
\sigma^{\vee}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
is the dual cone of
\sigma
. Let
R
be the local ring of
X
at
x
and
\mathfrak{m}
its maximal ideal.

Let
\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
be the subset of all monomial filtrations (i.e. filtrations
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
such that every
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
is a monomial ideal). Via the Okounkov body construction, we identify the metric space
(\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}},d_{1})
with a subspace of the space of cobounded subsets of
\sigma^{\vee}
with the symmetric difference metric (sometimes also called the Fréchet-Nykodym-Aronszajn metric).

Theorem 1.2
.

If
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is the local ring of a toric singularity
x\in X=\mathrm{Spec}R_{\sigma}
. Then

(1)

taking Newton-Okounkov body gives an isometry
P:(\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}},d_{1})\to(\mathcal{P}(\sigma^{\vee}),d)
,
and

(2)

the metric space
(\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}},d_{1})
is complete.

In the above theorem,
\mathcal{P}(\sigma^{\vee})
denotes the set of all closed convex subsets of
\sigma^{\vee}
with bounded complement, and the metric
d
is defined to be the Euclidean volume of the symmetric difference of two subsets. For more details, we refer to Section
3.4
. Note that
P\mapsto\chi_{P}
gives an embedding
\mathcal{P}(\sigma^{\vee})\to L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\sigma^{\vee})
, where
\chi_{P}
is the characteristic function of
P\subset\sigma
.

1.1.3.
The supnorm metric and homogeneous norms

As in the global case
[
BJ25
]
, there is also a correspondence between filtrations and norms in the local setting. Indeed, given
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, we can define a function
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}
on
R
by
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(f)\coloneqq\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid f\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\}
. In particular, the canonical saturated filtration
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}}
defines a canonical norm
\chi_{0}\coloneqq\mathrm{ord}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}}}
.
See Definition
2.7
for the definition of norms and Definition-Lemma
2.8
, Lemma
2.12
and Lemma
2.29
for the detailed dictionary.

Despite the equivalence, it is sometimes more convenient to use one of the languages.
As an example, we now consider another pseudometric
d_{\infty}
on the space
\mathcal{N}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
of
\mathfrak{m}
-norms on
R
, which behaves like the uniform metric on the space of continuous functions on a compact space. This can also be viewed as the local analogue of the
d_{\infty}
metric introduced in
[
BJ25
]
.

Fix a homogeneous norm
\rho\in\mathcal{N}^{h}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, define

(1.3)

d_{\infty,\rho}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})\coloneqq\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\sup_{\rho(f)\geq\lambda}\frac{|\chi(f)-\chi^{\prime}(f)|}{\rho(f)}.

We will denote the function
d_{\infty,\chi_{0}}
by
d_{\infty}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local domain that is analytically irreducible. Then

(1)

the function
d_{\infty}
is a pseudometric on
\mathcal{N}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, and

(2)

the restriction of
d_{\infty}
to
\mathcal{N}^{h}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
is a metric, and the metric space
(\mathcal{N}^{h}_{R,\mathfrak{m}},d_{\infty})
is complete.

Here
\mathcal{N}^{h}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
is the subset of
\mathcal{N}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
of homogeneous norms. See Section
2.3
for the definition and more details.

1.2.
Weak topologies and semi-continuity of log canonical thresholds

Besides the metrics introduced, we also consider the filtrations, or equivalently, norms, as functions on various spaces, and thus define some weak topologies on the spaces of filtrations. We sketch the ideas here. For more details, see Section
2.7.1
.

The first perspective is to view a norm
\chi
as a function on the ring
R
, and thus define the
weak topology
to be the product topology, that is, the weakest topology such that for any
f\in R
, the function
f\mapsto\chi(f)
is continuous. Since a real valuation (centered at
\mathfrak{m}
) is a (
\mathfrak{m}
-)norm, this topological space contains the valuation space with the weak topology introduced in
[
JM12
]
as a subspace.

Another point of view is to consider a filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
as a function on certain valuation spaces by
v\mapsto v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
, where
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\coloneqq\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})}{\lambda}
. We call the topology defined this way using the space
\mathrm{Val}^{+}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
of valuations with positive volume the
+
-
topology
.

We prove that the log canonical threshold satisfies certain semi-continuity on the space of filtrations, and that it is locally Lipschitz continuous with respect to the
d_{\infty}
-topology, which is similar to
[
DK01
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(X,x)=(\mathrm{Spec}R,\mathfrak{m})
be a klt singularity over a field
\mathbbm{k}
of characteristic
0
. Then

(1)

if
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\in\mathrm{Fil}
converges weakly to
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
, then

\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}),

(2)

if
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
converges to
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
in the
+
-topology, then

\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}),

and

(3)

given
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta:=\delta(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\epsilon)>0
such that for any
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
with
d_{\infty}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})<\delta
, we have

|\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})|\leq\epsilon.

1.3.
Miscellaneous results about filtrations

A classical result in ring theory is that the semiring of all ideals of a ring
R
with the partial order by inclusion forms a modular lattice, where meet is given by intersection and join is given by sum. We denote the lattice of all ideals (all
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals when
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a local ring, resp.) by
\mathcal{I}_{R}
(
\mathcal{I}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, resp.).

The spaces of filtrations are equipped with a natural partial order by inclusion, that is,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
if and only if
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. One can similarly define the intersection of two filtrations termwise, and in Definition-Lemma
2.3
(Definition-Lemma
2.22
, resp.), we define the
join

\vee
(
saturated join

\vee_{s}
, resp.), which is the analogue of the sum of two ideals. The next theorem asserts that these operations define natural lattice structures on the spaces of
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local domain that is analytically irreducible. Then

(1)

the set of all
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations with the partial order by inclusion is a lattice, where the meet is given by
\cap
and the join is given by
\vee
, and there is an injective join morphism from
\mathcal{I}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
to its sub-lattice
(\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}},\subset,\cap,\vee)
,

(2)

(\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}},\subset,\cap,\vee_{s})
is a distributive lattice. Moreover,
\mathfrak{a}\mapsto\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}}
is an injective join morphism from
\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{ic}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, the set of integrally closed
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals, and the saturation
\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\to\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
is a surjective join morphism.

In the above theorem,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}}
denotes the saturation of the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic filtration

\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}=\{\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil\lambda\rceil}\}
. The above theorem, together with Theorem
1.1
, can be viewed as a higher-dimensional analogue of the results of
[
FJ04
]
.

We will frequently use the following characterization for saturated filtrations, which generalizes results of
[
Ree56a
,
Ree56b
]
.

Proposition 1.6

(= Proposition
2.20
)
.

A filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
is saturated if and only if there exists a (non-empty) subset
\Sigma\subset\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
such that

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\cap_{v\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v).

We remark that the above proposition can be viewed as an analogue of the Berkovich Maximum Modulus Principle. See also Lemma
2.32
.

As an application, when
\mathfrak{a}
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal, we prove that the saturation of the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic filtration is determined by the set of Rees valuations of
\mathfrak{a}
, as expected.

Proposition 1.7

(= Proposition
2.27
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{I}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Then

\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}}=\cap_{v\in\mathrm{RV}(\mathfrak{a})}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\frac{v}{v(\mathfrak{a})}),

where
\mathrm{RV}(\mathfrak{a})
is the set of Rees valuations of
\mathfrak{a}
.

We refer to
[
HS06
, Chapter 10]
for more details about Rees valuations.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall definitions and basic properties that will be needed in the paper. Several results in Section
2.2
, including a characterization of saturated filtrations Proposition
2.20
, the lattice structure Definition-Lemma
2.22
and the distributivity Proposition
2.24
, seem to be new.

2.1.
Filtrations and norms

2.1.1.
Filtrations

Definition 2.1
.

A
filtration
on
R
is a collection
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
of ideals of
R
such that

(1)

(decreasing)
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}
for any
\lambda>\mu
,

(2)

(left-continuous)
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda-\epsilon}
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, and

(3)

(multiplicative)
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cdot\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda+\mu}
for any
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

A filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is called an
\mathfrak{m}
-
filtration
if
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
By convention, we always set
\mathfrak{a}_{0}\coloneqq R
.

The definition is a local analogue of a filtration on the section ring of a polarized variety in
[
BHJ17
]
. We will exclusively work with
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations in the sequel, though some of the results also hold for general filtrations on
R
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, set
\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}:=\cup_{\mu>\lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}
. If
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
satisfies
\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}\subsetneq\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
, then we call
\lambda
a
jumping number
of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
. The
scaling
of an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
by
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is
\mathfrak{a}_{c\bullet}:=(\mathfrak{a}_{c\lambda})_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
, which is an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration. For two
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
, we say that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
if
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. This defines a partial order on the set of all
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations. There is a maximal element among all
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations,
\{\mathfrak{m}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
. We will sometimes abuse the notation and denote this
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration by
\mathfrak{m}
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
be two
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations. Their
intersection
is
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}:=(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
. It is not hard to check that this is again an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration.

Definition-Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\}_{i\in I}
be an arbitrary non-empty set of
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations. Assume that there exists an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
such that
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
for any
i\in I
. Then the collection of
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals,
(\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,i})_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
, is an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration, called the
intersection
of the
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
, and is denoted by
\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
. Moreover, if an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
satisfies
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
for any
i\in I
, then we have
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\subset\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
.

Proof.

By assumption, for any
\lambda>0
we have
\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,i}
and hence
\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,i}
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary. It is straightforward to verify conditions (1) and (3) in the definition.

To prove (2), note that
\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\subset\cap_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,i}\subset\cap_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,i}
for any
\mu\leq\lambda
. In
R/\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}
we have

\displaystyle\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,i}=

\displaystyle\cap_{i\in I}\cap_{\epsilon>0}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda-\epsilon,i}=\cap_{\epsilon>0}\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda-\epsilon,i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cap_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1/\lambda}}\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda-\frac{1}{l},i}=\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda-\frac{1}{l},i}

for some
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1/\lambda}
, where the first equality follows from the left-continuity of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
, and the last equality uses the fact that
R/\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}
is Artinian. This proves that condition (2) holds for
\{\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,i}\}
, and thus it is an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration.

The last assertion follows by definition, and the proof is finished.
∎

Definition-Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\}_{i\in I}
be an arbitrary set of
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations. Then there is a filtration, called the
join
of the
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
, denoted by
\vee_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
, such that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\subset\vee_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
for any
i\in I
. Moreover, if an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
satisfies
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
for any
i\in I
, then we have
\vee_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
.

Proof.

If
I=\emptyset
, then
\vee_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
is the maximal
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{m}
.

Assume now
I\neq\emptyset
and fix
0\in I
. The set

J:=\{\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\mid\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\text{ for any }i\in I\}

is non-empty, since the maximal
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{m}\in J
. Moreover, by definition
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
for any
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in J
. Hence we can apply Definition-Lemma
2.2
to get an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration

\vee_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\coloneqq\cap_{\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in J}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}.

The last assertion follows by definition, and the proof is finished.
∎

Example 2.4
.

Given an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
, we have an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}
, the
\mathfrak{a}
-
adic filtration
, defined by
\{\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil\lambda\rceil}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
. Thus we get an injection from the set
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
to the set of all
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations defined by
\mathfrak{a}\mapsto\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}
. In particular, there is a canonical
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
.

For
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
, we claim that
\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}\vee\mathfrak{b}^{\bullet}=(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})^{\bullet}
. Indeed, since
\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}\subset(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})^{\bullet}
and
\mathfrak{b}^{\bullet}\subset(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})^{\bullet}
, by Definition-Lemma
2.3
we have
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\coloneqq\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}\vee\mathfrak{b}^{\bullet}\subset(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})^{\bullet}
. On the other hand, we have
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{1}
by definition. Hence for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we have

(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})^{\lceil\lambda\rceil}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{1}^{\lceil\lambda\rceil}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\lceil\lambda\rceil}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda}.

This proves
(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})^{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
and thus the equality holds.

However, in general we only have
(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b})^{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{\bullet}
and the inclusion can be strict. Indeed, let
R=\mathbbm{k}[\![x,y]\!]
for some field
\mathbbm{k}
,
\mathfrak{a}=(x,y^{2})
and
\mathfrak{b}=(x^{2},y)
. Then it is not hard to see that
\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}=(x^{2},xy,y^{2})=\mathfrak{m}^{2}
and
(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b})^{2}=\mathfrak{m}^{4}
. Hence
x^{2}y\in\mathfrak{a}^{2}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{2}\backslash(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b})^{2}
.

Unlike the intersection, it can be hard to determine the join of two general filtrations. In Section
2.2
, we will define a similar notion, the
saturated join
, and give a formula for it.
The following proposition, which is a combination of Definition-Lemma
2.2
and Definition-Lemma
2.3
, justifies the term
join
.

Proposition 2.5
.

The set of all
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations with the partial order by inclusion forms a lattice, where the meet of two elements is given by their intersection, and the join is defined as in Definition-Lemma
2.3
. ∎

Remark 2.6
.

Note that by Example
2.4
, the lattice
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
of
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals of
R
does not embed in the lattice of all
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations, since the meet is not preserved.

2.1.2.
Norms and valuations

We recall the correspondence between filtrations and norms on a local ring. See also
[
HS06
,
BFJ14
]
for some related results.

Definition 2.7
.

A
seminorm
on
R
is a function
\chi:R\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{+\infty\}
such that

(1)

\chi(0)=+\infty
and
\chi(-f)=\chi(f)
,

(2)

\chi(f+g)\geq\min\{\chi(f),\chi(g)\}
for any
f,g\in R
, and

(3)

(Sub-multiplicativity)
\chi(fg)\geq\chi(f)+\chi(g)
for any
f,g\in R
.

A seminorm
\chi
is a
norm
if
\chi(f)=+\infty
if and only if
f=0
. A (semi)norm
\chi
is called an
\mathfrak{m}
-(semi)
norm
if it further satisfies

(4)

\chi(f)\geq 0
for any
f\in R
and
\chi(f)>0
if and only if
f\in\mathfrak{m}
.

If
\chi
is a (
\mathfrak{m}
-)norm on
R
, and we replace condition (3) above by

(3
′
)

(Multiplicativity)
\chi(fg)=\chi(f)+\chi(g)
for any
f,g\in R
,

then
\chi
is a real valuation on
R
(centered at
\mathfrak{m}
). Denote the space of real valuations on
R
centered at
\mathfrak{m}
by
\mathrm{Val}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
.

If
R
is a
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra for a field
\mathbbm{k}
, then we will always assume that
\chi
is a
\mathbbm{k}
-norm, that is,
\chi(af)=\chi(f)
for any
a\in\mathbbm{k}^{\times}
and
f\in R
.

Here, as in the global case, we adopt the additive terminology here. For a norm
\chi
on
R
,
\|\cdot\|_{\chi}:=e^{-\chi(\cdot)}
defines a non-Archimedean norm on
R
in the sense of
[
BGR84
]
.

There is a correspondence between seminorms and filtrations, given by the lemma below.

Definition-Lemma 2.8
.

Given an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
, its
order function

\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}
is defined by

(2.1)

\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(f):=\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\mid f\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\},

which is a seminorm on
R
. If
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{m}
for
0<\lambda\ll 1
, then it is an
\mathfrak{m}
-seminorm.

Conversely, given an
\mathfrak{m}
-seminorm
\chi
, define
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi)
by

(2.2)

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(\chi):=\{f\in R\mid\chi(f)\geq\lambda\}.

Then
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi)
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration, called the
associated filtration
of
\chi
.

The above maps give a 1-1 correspondence between
\mathfrak{m}
-seminorms and
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations satisfying
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{m}
for
0<\lambda\ll 1
. Moreover,
\mathfrak{m}
-norms correspond to
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations with
\cap_{\lambda>0}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=\{0\}
.

The associated filtration of a real valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
is also called the
valuation ideals
of
v
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration. Clearly
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}
satisfies condition (1) of Definition
2.7
. Condition (2) follows from the fact that each
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
is an ideal. Condition (3) follows from the multiplicativity of a filtration. If
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{m}
for
0<\lambda\ll 1
, then condition (4) follows from the convention
\mathfrak{a}_{0}=R
.

Conversely, let
\chi
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-seminorm. We first show that for any
\lambda>0
,
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(\chi)
defined in (
2.2
) is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. It is an ideal since
\chi(f-g)\geq\min\{\chi(f),\chi(-g)\}=\min\{\chi(f),\chi(g)\}
and
\chi(fg)\geq\chi(f)+\chi(g)\geq\min\{\chi(f),\chi(g)\}
. Choose a set of generators
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
of
\mathfrak{m}
, then for any
f\in\mathfrak{m}
we have
\chi(f)\geq\chi(\mathfrak{m})\coloneqq\min_{1\leq i\leq r}\{\chi(f_{i})\}>0
. Let
C\coloneqq\lceil\lambda/\chi(\mathfrak{m})\rceil
, then
\chi(f^{C})\geq C\chi(f)\geq\lambda
, that is,
\mathfrak{m}^{C}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(\chi)
. Note that the above argument also shows that
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(\chi)=\mathfrak{m}
for
0<\lambda<\chi(\mathfrak{m})
. Clearly the collection of
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(\chi)\}
is decreasing, and it is multiplicative by condition (3) of Definition
2.7
. To show left continuity, note that we can write
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(\chi)
as a decreasing intersection

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(\chi)=\cap_{\epsilon>0}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda-\epsilon}(\chi),

which terminates since the equality holds in the Artinian ring
R/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(\chi)
.
∎

Note that the supremum in the definition is indeed a maximum if the value is finite.

Example 2.9
.

The canonical
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}=\{\mathfrak{m}^{\lceil\lambda\rceil}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
corresponds to the canonical
\mathfrak{m}
-norm
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{m}}:R\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{+\infty\}
given by

\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{m}}(f):=\sup\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid f\in\mathfrak{m}^{k}\},

which has been considered in
[
BFJ14
, Section 4]
.

Remark 2.10
.

By Lemma
2.29
, a homogeneous filtration satisfies the additional condition above. In particular, we may identify the spaces of filtrations and norms whenever we consider the metric properties.

Indeed, since we only consider the asymptotic behavior of a filtration, we can modify the terms of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
in the following way, such that the additional condition is always satisfied. Since
\{\mathfrak{a}_{1/n}\}
is an increasing sequence of
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals, there exists
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{1/n}=\mathfrak{a}_{1/N}\eqqcolon\mathfrak{a}
for any
n\geq N
. If
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{m}
then we are done. Otherwise, choose
C\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{C}\subset\mathfrak{a}
and define

\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathfrak{m},&0<\lambda\leq 1/NC,\\
&\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda},&\text{otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

Then clearly
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
has the same asymptotic information as
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
.

2.1.3.
Divisorial valuations

Recall that a valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
defines a local ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}
, the
valuation ring
of
v
, by

\mathcal{O}_{v}\coloneqq\{f\in\mathrm{Frac}(R)\mid v(f)\geq 0\}\text{ with the maximal ideal }\mathfrak{m}_{v}=\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{v}\mid v(f)\geq 0\}.

The
residue field
of
v
is
\kappa_{v}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{v}/\mathfrak{m}_{v}
.

A valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
is
divisorial
if

{\rm tr.deg}_{\kappa}(\kappa_{v})=n-1.

We write
\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathrm{Val}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
for the set of such valuations.

Divisorial valuations appear geometrically.
If
\mu:Y\to X
is a proper birational morphism with
Y
normal and
E\subset Y
a prime divisor,
then there is an induced valuation
\mathrm{ord}_{E}\colon{\rm Frac}(R)^{\times}\to\mathbb{Z}
.
If
\mu(E)=x
and
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, then
c\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{E}\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
.
When
R
is excellent, all divisorial valuations are of this form; see e.g.
[
CS22
, Lemma 6.5]
.

2.1.4.
Quasi-monomial valuations

In the following construction, we always assume
R
contains a field. Let
\mu\colon Y:=\mathrm{Spec}(S)\to X=\mathrm{Spec}(R)
be a birational morphism
with
R\to S
finite type
and
\eta\in Y
a not necessarily closed point such that
\mathcal{O}_{Y,\eta}
is regular and
\mu(\eta)=x
.
Given a regular system of parameters
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
of
\mathcal{O}_{Y,\eta}

and
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}\setminus{\bf 0}
, we define a valuation
v_{\bf\alpha}
as follows.
For
0\neq f\in\mathcal{O}_{Y,\eta}
, we can write
f
in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,\eta}\simeq k(\eta)[[y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}]]
as

\sum_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r}}c_{\bm{\beta}}{y}^{\bm{\beta}}
and set

v_{\bm{\alpha}}(f)\coloneqq\min\{\langle\bm{\alpha},{\bm{\beta}}\rangle\,|\,c_{\bm{\beta}}\neq 0\}.

A valuation of the above form is called
quasi-monomial
.

Let
D=D_{1}+\cdots+D_{r}
be a reduced divisor on
Y
such that
y_{i}=0
locally defines
D_{i}
and
\mu(D_{i})=x
for each
i
.
We call
\eta\in(Y,D)
a
log smooth birational model
of
X
.
We write
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(Y,E)\subset\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
for the set of quasi-monomial valuations that can be described at

\eta
with respect to
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
and note that
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(Y,D)\simeq\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}\setminus\bm{0}
.

2.1.5.
Multiplicity of a filtration

Following
[
ELS03
]
, the
multiplicity
of a graded sequence of
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is

\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\coloneqq\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{m})}{m^{n}/n!}\in[0,\infty).

By
[
ELS03
,
Mus02
,
LM09
,
Cut13
,
Cut14
]
in increasing generality, the above limit exists and

(2.3)

\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{m})}{m^{n}}=\inf_{m}\frac{\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{m})}{m^{n}};

see in particular
[
Cut14
, Theorem 6.5]
.
Also defined in
[
ELS03
]
, the
volume
of a valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
is
\mathrm{vol}(v):=\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v))
.
Denote
\mathrm{Val}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}^{+}\coloneqq\{v\in\mathrm{Val}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\mid\mathrm{vol}(v)>0\}\subset\mathrm{Val}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
.

2.1.6.
Linearly boundedness

Definition 2.11
.

An
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is
linearly bounded
if there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{m}^{c\bullet}
.

An
\mathfrak{m}
-norm
\chi
is
linearly bounded
if there exists
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\chi(f)\leq c^{-1}\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{m}}(f)
for any
f\in R
.

By Krull intersection theorem, a linearly bounded
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration satisfies
\cap_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=\{0\}
. In particular, by Definition-Lemma
2.8
, we have the following

Lemma 2.12
.

An
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration is linearly bounded if and only if
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}
is a linearly bounded
\mathfrak{m}
-norm.∎

We will consider filtrations with positive multiplicity, which are exactly the linearly bounded ones by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.13
.

[
BLQ24
, Corollary 3.18]

An
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is linearly bounded if and only if
\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})>0
.

Denote the set of all linearly bounded
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations on
R
by
\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, and the set of all linearly bounded
\mathfrak{m}
-norms on
R
by
\mathcal{N}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
. When there is no ambiguity, we will write
\mathrm{Fil}
and
\mathcal{N}
respectively. By Definition-Lemma
2.8
and Lemma
2.12
,
\mathcal{N}
is a subset of
\mathrm{Fil}
.

There is a natural pointwise partial order
\leq
on
\mathcal{N}
on
R
, defined by

\chi\leq\chi^{\prime}\text{ if and only if }\chi(f)\leq\chi^{\prime}(f)\text{ for any }f\in R,

which corresponds to the partial order defined by inclusion on filtrations. Moreover, the natural
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action of scaling on
\mathcal{N}
corresponds to the scaling on
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations is given by

(c\cdot\chi)(f):=c^{-1}\chi(f).

2.1.7.
Asymptotic invariants

Lemma 2.14
.

(cf.
[
JM12
, Lemma 2.4]
)
Let
\chi\in\mathcal{N}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
and
w\in\mathrm{Val}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
. Then

w(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi))=\inf_{f\in\mathfrak{m}}\frac{w(f)}{\chi(f)}.

Proof.

Let
c\coloneqq\inf_{f\in\mathfrak{m}}\frac{w(f)}{\chi(f)}
. Then for any
f\in\mathfrak{m}
we have
w(f)\geq c\cdot\chi(f)
, hence by definition,
w(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(\chi))\geq c\cdot\chi(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(\chi))\geq c\lambda
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Letting
\lambda\to\infty
and dividing by
\lambda
, we get
w(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi))\geq c
.

Conversely, for any
\epsilon>0
, by definition there exists
f\in\mathfrak{m}
such that
0<w(f)<(c+\epsilon)\chi(f)
. Note that for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
f^{d}\in\mathfrak{a}_{d\cdot\chi(f)}(\chi)
as
\chi(f^{d})\geq d\cdot\chi(f)
.
Thus for
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{w(\mathfrak{a}_{d\cdot\chi(f)}(\chi))}{d\cdot\chi(f)}\leq\frac{w(f^{d})}{d\cdot\chi(f)}=\frac{d\cdot w(f)}{d\cdot\chi(f)}<c+\epsilon.

Letting
d\to\infty
we get
w(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi))\leq c+\epsilon
. This finishes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Saturated filtrations

The saturation of an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration was introduced in
[
BLQ24
]
. We recall some facts about saturations and provide a characterization for saturated filtrations in Proposition
2.20
. Several of the results in this section seem to be new.

Definition 2.15
.

The
saturation

\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
of an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is defined by

\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\{f\in\mathfrak{m}\mid v(f)\geq\lambda\cdot v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\text{ for all }v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\}

for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
We say that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is
saturated
if
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
.

Denote the subset of
\mathrm{Fil}
of saturated filtrations by
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}\coloneqq\{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}\mid\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\text{ is saturated}\}
. There is a canonical element in
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
, that is, the saturation
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}
of the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic filtration. Denote its order function by
\chi_{0}:=\mathrm{ord}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}}=\widetilde{\mathrm{ord}}_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

The following lemma asserts that for saturated filtrations, the partial order by inclusion can be detected by evaluating on all divisorial valuations, which is not the case for general filtrations.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration and
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
. Then
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
if and only if
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\geq v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})
for any
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{X,x}
.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
then by definition
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\geq v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})
for any
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
. Then there exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
f\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
such that
f\notin\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}
. This implies that there exist
\epsilon>0
and
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{X,x}
such that
v(f)<\lambda\cdot v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})-\epsilon
. Hence for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we have

v(\mathfrak{a}_{m\lambda})\leq v(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}^{m})=mv(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})\leq mv(f)<m(\lambda\cdot v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})-\epsilon),

where the first inequality follows from
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}^{m}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{m\lambda}
. Dividing by
m\lambda
and letting
m\to\infty
, we get
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\leq v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})-\epsilon/\lambda<v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})
.
∎

The saturation can be alternatively defined using all valuations with positive volume.

Lemma 2.17
.

[
BLQ24
, Proposition 3.19]

If
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, then

\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\lambda}=\{f\in\mathfrak{m}\mid v(f)\geq\lambda\cdot v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\text{ for all }v\in\mathrm{Val}^{+}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\}.

One of the main properties of saturations is the following generalization of Rees’ theorem on multiplicities of ideals.

Theorem 2.18
.

[
BLQ24
, Theorem 1.4]

For
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
,
\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})
if and only if
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
.

In other words,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is the unique maximal
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\supset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
such that
\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})
. We now prove a formula for saturations, which can be viewed as an analogue of the Berkovich maximal modulus principle, where one considers the subset of divisorial valuations of the Berkovich spectrum.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
. Then we have

\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}=\bigcap_{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=1}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v)=\bigcap_{v\geq\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v),

where the first intersection is taken over all
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
with
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=1
and the second over all
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
such that
v(f)\geq\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(f)
for any
f\in\mathfrak{m}
.

Proof.

Note that
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})>0
for any
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, by Lemma
2.13
. For
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
with
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=1
, by
[
BLQ24
, Proposition 3.9]
we know that
v(\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=1
, so
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v)
by definition. Hence
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\subset\cap_{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=1}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v)
. Conversely, if
f\in\cap_{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=1}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v)
, then for any
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, since
v^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=1
, where
v^{\prime}:=v/v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
, we have

v(f)=\frac{v}{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}(f)\cdot v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\geq\lambda\cdot v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}).

Hence
\cap_{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=1}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v)\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
and this proves the first equality.

If
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=1
then for any
f\in\mathfrak{a}_{m}
, we have
v(f)\geq v(\mathfrak{a}_{m})\geq mv(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
.
Hence
v(f)\geq\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(f)
, that is,
v\geq\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}
, and we get
\cap_{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=1}\mathfrak{a}_{m}(v)\supset\cap_{v\geq\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}\mathfrak{a}_{m}(v)
. Conversely, if
v\geq\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}
, then
v(\mathfrak{a}_{m})\geq\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(\mathfrak{a}_{m})\geq m
, which implies
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\geq 1
, that is,
v\geq v^{\prime}\coloneqq v/v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
, where
v^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=1
. Thus we get

\cap_{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=1}\mathfrak{a}_{m}(v)\subset\cap_{v^{\prime}}\mathfrak{a}_{m}(v^{\prime})\subset\cap_{v\geq\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}\mathfrak{a}_{m}(v).

This proves the second equality.
∎

Based on the observation above, we give a characterization for saturated filtrations.

Proposition 2.20
.

A filtration
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathrm{Fil}
is saturated if and only if it is of the form

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\cap_{v\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v),

where
\Sigma
is a nonempty subset of either
\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
or
\mathrm{Val}^{+}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\cap_{v\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v)
for some
\Sigma\subset\mathrm{Val}^{+}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, then by
[
BLQ24
, Lemma 3.20]
,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is saturated.

Conversely, if
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is saturated, then by Lemma
2.19
,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}=\cap_{v\in\Sigma_{v}}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v)
, where

\Sigma_{v}\coloneqq\{v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\mid v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=1)\}\subset\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}.

Since
\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathrm{Val}^{+}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, the proof is finished.
∎

In particular,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v)
is saturated for any
v\in\mathrm{Val}^{+}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
. By the above proposition, saturated filtrations (or norms) can be considered as the local analogue of maximal norms in the sense of
[
BJ25
, Definition 6.16]
.

Corollary 2.21
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
for
i\in I
. If
\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\in\mathrm{Fil}
, then
\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.20
, for any
i\in I
we may write
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}=\cap_{v\in\Sigma_{i}}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v)
for
\Sigma_{i}\subset\mathrm{DivVal}_{X,x}
. Thus

\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}=\cap_{v\in\cup_{i\in I}\Sigma_{i}}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v)\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}

by Proposition
2.20
again.
∎

Definition-Lemma 2.22
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\in\mathrm{Fil}
for
i\in I\neq\emptyset
. If there exists
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
for any
i\in I
, then there exists an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration, called the
saturated join
of the
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
, denoted by
\vee_{s,i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
, such that the following holds. If
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
satisfies
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
for any
i\in I
, then we have
\vee_{s,i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
. Moreover,
\vee_{s,i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
.

Proof.

Fix
0\in I
. By assumption, the set

J:=\{\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}\mid\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\text{ for any }i\in I\}\subset\mathrm{Fil}^{s}

is non-empty, and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
for any
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\in J
. Thus by Definition-Lemma
2.2
, the intersection

\vee_{s,i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\coloneqq\cap_{\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\in J}\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil},

where the linearly boundedness is because
\vee_{s,i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
by construction. Now
\vee_{s,i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{i}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
by Corollary
2.21
.
∎

We now relate the join, the saturated join and the saturation, and give a formula for the saturated join of two saturated filtrations.

Proposition 2.23
.

If
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
, then
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
, and

(2.4)

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}=\cap_{v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\frac{v}{\min\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\}}).

Proof.

By definition,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}
is a saturated filtration containing both
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
, so by Definition-Lemma
2.22
we have
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}
. Conversely, by Definition-Lemma
2.22
again
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
is saturated and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
by definition, hence
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
. The inclusion
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
is obvious. Conversely, if
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
for
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
then
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
and similarly for
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
. Thus
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
. This proves the first equality.

To prove the formula (
2.4
), we first rewrite Lemma
2.19
as

(2.5)

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\cap_{v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\frac{v}{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})})

for
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
. Let
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
be the
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration defined by the right-handed side of (
2.4
). Since

\frac{v}{\min\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\}}=\max\{\frac{v}{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})},\frac{v}{v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})}\}=\max\{\frac{v}{v(\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})},\frac{v}{v(\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})}\},

applying the formula (
2.5
) to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
respectively, we know that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
and similarly
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
. Hence
v(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\leq\min\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\}
for any
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
. By (
2.4
), for any
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
we have
v(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\geq v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v/\min\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\}))=\min\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\}
. Thus

(2.6)

v(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})=\min\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\}.

We now verify that
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
. Take
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{C\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{C\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
. Since
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}
, for any
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, we have
Cv(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\geq v(\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet})=v(\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet})
and similarly
Cv(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\geq v(\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet})
. By (
2.6
),
Cv(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\geq v(\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet})
. So by Lemma
2.16
,
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet/C}
is linearly bounded.

By Corollary
2.21
,
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
, which implies
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
. By definition and (
2.6
) we have
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\leq\min\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\}=v(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})
for any
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
. Since
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
by Definition-Lemma
2.22
, we may apply Lemma
2.16
to conclude that
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
. This proves the formula (
2.4
) and finishes the proof of the Proposition.
∎

We now show that the lattice operations on
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
satisfy the distributive law.

Proposition 2.24
.

If
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
, then we have

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})=(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\cap(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})
.

Proof.

We have
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\subset(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\cap(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})
, since
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}
.

Conversely, assume that
f\in(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})_{\lambda}\cap(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})_{\lambda}
. Then for any
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
we know that

\displaystyle v(f)/\lambda\geq

\displaystyle\max\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}),v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\min\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\},\min\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),v(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\}\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),\max\{v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}),v(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\}\},

where the second inequality follows from Proposition
2.23
, and the last inequality is easy to check. By Proposition
2.23
again, we get
f\in(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}))_{\lambda}
. This shows that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\supset(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\cap(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})
and the equality must hold.
∎

Example 2.25
.

We continue to consider the adic filtration
\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}
of
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. By definition,

(\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}})_{\lambda}=\{f\in\mathfrak{m}\mid v(f)\geq\lambda\cdot v(\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet})\text{ for any }v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\}.

Since
v(\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet})=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}v(\mathfrak{a}^{\lceil\lambda\rceil})/\lambda=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lceil\lambda\rceil v(\mathfrak{a})/\lambda=v(\mathfrak{a})
, for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
we have

(\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}})_{m}=\{f\in\mathfrak{m}\mid v(f)\geq mv(\mathfrak{a})=v(\mathfrak{a}^{m})\text{ for any }v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\}.

Hence
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet})}_{m}=\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{m}}
is the integral closure of
\mathfrak{a}^{m}
. See for example
[
HS06
, Theorem 6.8.3]
. Thus we have a map
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}\to\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
,
\mathfrak{a}\mapsto\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}}
, which is an injection when restricted to the set
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{ic}}
of integrally closed
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals. Note that in general,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}}
is not an adic filtration. For example,
\widetilde{(\mathfrak{m}^{2})^{\bullet}}
has
1/2
as a jumping number.

Even if
\mathfrak{a}
is integrally closed, the powers of
\mathfrak{a}
are in general not integrally closed, hence an
\mathfrak{a}
-adic filtration is in general
not
saturated. By Example
2.4
, the join of two adic filtrations is still an adic filtration, which means that the inclusion
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
can be strict for general
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
. It is unclear to the author whether
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}=\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\vee_{s}\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
if
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
.

It is easy to see that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\cap\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
, and the inclusion can be strict. Indeed, let
R=\mathbbm{k}[\![x,y]\!]
,
\mathfrak{a}=(x^{2},y^{10})
and
\mathfrak{b}=(x^{10},y^{2})
, then it is not hard to get
\overline{\mathfrak{a}}=(x^{2},xy^{5},y^{10})
and
\overline{\mathfrak{b}}=(x^{10},x^{5}y,y^{2})
. By looking at the Newton polytope of
\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}=(x^{2}y^{2},x^{10},y^{10})
, one can compute

\overline{\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}}=(x^{2}y^{2},xy^{6},x^{6}y,x^{10},y^{10}).

In particular, by the calculation for adic filtrations above,
f\coloneqq x^{5}y\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}}\cap\overline{\mathfrak{b}}=(\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}\cap\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}^{\bullet})_{1}
, but
f\notin\overline{\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}}=(\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}})_{1}
.

Next we compute the saturation of an ideal-adic filtration. First note that to prove two saturated filtrations are equal, it suffices to check the equality along an arbitrary unbounded sequence.

Lemma 2.26
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
. If there exists
\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda_{k}}=\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda_{k}}
, then
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
.

Proof.

By assumption, for any
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
we have

v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\lim_{k\to\infty}v(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda_{k}})/\lambda_{k}=\lim_{k\to\infty}v(\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda_{k}})/\lambda_{k}=v(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}).

Hence by Lemma
2.16
,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
.
∎

Recall that for an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{I}_{R}
, the set
\mathrm{RV}(\mathfrak{a})
of
Rees valuations
of
\mathfrak{a}
is the minimal set of divisorial valuations such that for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,

\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{m}}=\{f\in R\mid v(f)\geq mv(\mathfrak{a})\text{ for any }v\in\mathrm{RV}(\mathfrak{a})\}.

This is a finite set for any
\mathfrak{a}
. We refer to
[
HS06
, Chapter 10]
for more about Rees valuations.

Proposition 2.27
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Then

(2.7)

\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}}=\cap_{v\in RV(\mathfrak{a})}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\frac{v}{v(\mathfrak{a})}),

where
RV(\mathfrak{a})
is the set of Rees valuations of
\mathfrak{a}
.

Proof.

Denote
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\coloneqq\cap_{v\in RV(\mathfrak{a})}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\frac{v}{v(\mathfrak{a})})
, which is saturated by Proposition
2.20
. By the definition of Rees valuations and the calculation in Example
2.25
, we know that
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}})_{m}=\mathfrak{b}_{m}
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Thus (
2.7
) follows from Lemma
2.26
.
∎

2.3.
Homogeneous filtrations

Definition 2.28
.

A norm
\chi\in\mathcal{N}
is said to be
power-multiplicative
, or
homogeneous
, if it satisfies
\chi(f^{d})=d\cdot\chi(f)
for any
f\in R
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

A filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is said to be
homogeneous
if
f^{d}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
implies
f\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda/d}
for any
\lambda>0
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

It is easy to see that a saturated filtration is homogeneous, and that a homogeneous filtration satisfies the additional condition in Definition-Lemma
2.8
.

Lemma 2.29
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration. Then

(1)

if
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is saturated, then it is homogeneous, and

(2)

if
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is homogeneous, then
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{m}
for
0<\lambda\ll 1
. In particular,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is homogeneous if and only if
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}
is homogeneous.

Proof.

(1) If
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is saturated and
f^{d}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
, then
v(f^{d})=dv(f)\geq\lambda\cdot v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
, which implies
v(f)\geq\lambda/d\cdot v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
. Hence
f\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda/d}
by definition.

(2) Choose a set
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}\}
of generators of
\mathfrak{m}
and
D\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{D}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{1}
. Then
f_{i}^{D}\in\mathfrak{a}_{1}
, and hence
f_{i}\in\mathfrak{a}_{1/D}
by assumption. So
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{1/D}
and hence
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{m}
for
0<\lambda<1/D
. The last assertion is straightforward by Definition-Lemma
2.8
.
∎

Definition 2.30
.

(c.f.
[
HS06
, Definition 6.9.3]
and
[
BFJ14
, Section 4]
)
Given a norm
\chi
on
R
, its
homogenization

\widehat{\chi}:R\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined by
\widehat{\chi}(f):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\chi(f^{k})}{k}
for any
f\in R
.

The filtration
\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\coloneqq\mathfrak{a}(\widehat{\chi})
is called the
homogenization
of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\coloneqq\mathfrak{a}(\chi)
. In
[
CP24
, Definition 3.5]
,
\widehat{\chi}
is also called the
asymptotic Samuel function
of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
, following
[
HS06
]
.

The limit exists by Fekete’s lemma, see, for example,
[
CP24
, Theorem 3.4]
. Hence it is not hard to see that
\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is the smallest homogeneous filtration that contains

2.6.
Metric spaces

Recall that a
pseudometric
on a set
S
is a function
d:S\times S\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

•

(non-negative)
d(x,y)\geq 0
for any
x,y\in S
,

•

(symmetric)
d(x,y)=d(y,x)
for any
x,y\in S
, and

•

(triangle inequality)
d(x,z)\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z)
for any
x,y,z\in S
.

A pseudometric is a
metric
if and only if it is non-degenerate, that is,
d(x,y)=0
if and only if
x=y
.

Definition 2.37
.

A point
z
in a metric space
(S,d)
is said to be
between
two points
x
and
y
, if all three points are distinct, and
d(x,y)=d(x,z)+d(z,y)
.

A metric space
(S,d)
is
convex
if for any two distinct points
x,y\in S
, there exists a point
z
between
x
and
y
.

Given a pseudometric space
(S,d)
, its
Hausdorff quotient
is the quotient space
(\bar{S}\coloneqq S/\sim,\bar{d})
, where
x\sim y
if and only if
d(x,y)=0
, and
\bar{d}
is the natural induced metric on
\bar{S}
.

Recall that the
length
of a continuous curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to S
in a metric space
(S,d)
is defined by

\ell(\gamma):=\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d(x_{i-1},x_{i}),

where the supremum is taken over all finite partitions
\mathcal{P}=\{a=x_{0}\leq x_{1}\leq\ldots\leq x_{n}=b\}
of the interval
[a,b]
.

Definition 2.38
.

A metric space
(S,d)
is a
length space
if
d
coincides with the
intrinsic metric
, that is,
d(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma}\ell(\gamma)=:d_{I}(x,y)
for any
x,y\in S
, where the infimum is taken over all continuous curves
\gamma:[0,1]\to S
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y
.

(S,d)
is a
geodesic space
if the above infimum is a minimum, that is, the distance can always be calculated by a continuous curve.

2.7.1.
Weak topologies on
\mathrm{Fil}

There are more ways to define a topology on the space
\mathrm{Fil}
(as well as on
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
and
\mathrm{Fil}^{h}
), depending on the point of view.

For example, a filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
acts naturally on the ring
R
as a function
\chi_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}
(or dually). The product topology, or the “weak-* topology” induced by this action will be called the
weak topology
, which is the coarsest topology such that for any
f\in R\backslash\{0\}
, the function
\phi_{f}:\mathrm{Fil}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\mapsto\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(f)
is continuous. In other words, a
subbase
of the weak topology is given by
\{\phi_{f}^{-1}(U)\mid U\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\text{ is open, }f\in R\backslash\{0\}\}
. Under this topology, all valuation spaces in (
2.9
) equipped with the weak topology, defined in
[
JM12
]
, are subspaces of
\mathrm{Fil}
. It is not hard to check that a sequence
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\}\subset\mathrm{Fil}
converges weakly to
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
if and only if
\chi_{k}(f)\to\chi(f)
for any
f\in R\backslash\{0\}
.

Similarly, a valuation has a natural action on the spaces of filtrations (or dually), that is, any
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
gives a function
\mathrm{ev}_{v}:\mathrm{Fil}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\mapsto v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
. Hence one can consider the “weak topology” induced by this action, that is, the coarsest topology on
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
such that
\mathrm{ev}_{v}
is continuous for any
v\in X^{{}^{\mathrm{an}}}
,
\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
,
\mathrm{Val}^{+}_{X,x}
or
\mathrm{DivVal}_{X,x}
, respectively. Similarly, one can check that a sequence
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\}
converges to
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
under these topologies if and only if
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k})\to v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
for any
v
in the corresponding valuation space. In this paper, we will mainly consider the topology defined using
\mathrm{Val}^{+}_{X,x}
, and call it the
+
-topology
.
The product topology defined using
\mathrm{DivVal}_{X,x}
as above was considered in
[
BdFFU15
]
for
b
-divisors. We will thus call it the
coefficientwise topology
.

Remark 2.39
.

Since
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=v(\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
by
[
BLQ24
, Proposition 3.9]
for
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{X,x}
, the functions
\mathrm{ev}_{v}
do not distinguish
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
and its saturation. Hence the
+
-topology (and the coefficientwise topology) only makes sense on
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
. Similar issues appear if we define the topology on
\mathrm{Fil}
using other valuation spaces. But all the topologies are well-defined on
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
.

Later we will define metrics on the space
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
. See Section
4.3
for a comparison of these topologies.

3.1.
Darvas metrics and multiplicities

Throughout this section we will use the language of filtrations. Recall that
\mathrm{Fil}=\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
denotes the set of all linearly bounded
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations on
R
.

Definition 3.1
.

We define a function
d_{1}:\mathrm{Fil}\times\mathrm{Fil}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
as follows.

Given
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
. If
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
, then let

(3.1)

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})=d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}):=\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}).

In general, define

(3.2)

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}):=d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}).

Proposition 3.2
.

d_{1}
satisfies the triangle inequality, that is, for
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
,
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
,
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
, we have

(3.3)

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\leq d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}).

Hence
d_{1}
is a pseudometric on
\mathrm{Fil}
.

Proof.

By definition, we have

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})=2\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})

and

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})=2\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})+2\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})

Hence to prove (
3.3
), it suffices to show that

\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\leq\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})+\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}),

which will follow immediately from the following comparison on colengths

(3.4)

\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})\leq\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda})+\ell(R/\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})-\ell(R/\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda})

for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. But we compute

\displaystyle\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda})+

\displaystyle\ell(R/\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})-\ell(R/\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda})-\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})=\ell(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda})+\ell(\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell((\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda})/\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda})+\ell(\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell((\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda})/\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ell((\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda})/(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda})\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell((\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})/\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ell((\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})/\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})=\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})-\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}).

This proves (
3.4
), and hence the triangle inequality (
3.3
) holds.

Clearly
d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=0
, and
d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})=d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
by definition. So we know that
(\mathrm{Fil},d_{1})
is a pseudometric space.
∎

Using the identification between
\mathrm{Fil}
and
\mathcal{N}
Lemma
2.8
, we get a pseudometric
d_{1}
on
\mathcal{N}
. The following lemma asserts that
(\mathrm{Fil},d_{1})
is a
rooftop metric space
in the sense of
[
Xia23
]
, which is an analogue of
[
Dar19
, Proposition 3.35]
and
[
BJ25
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
, we have

(3.5)

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\leq d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}).

Proof.

By definition, we have

d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})=\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})+\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})

and

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})=\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})+\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}).

So by symmetry, it suffices to prove the inequality

\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\leq\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}),

which follows from the inclusion

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda})/(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})\cong

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})/\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\subset(\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda})/\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda}\cong

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}/(\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda}).

This finishes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Measures and geodesics

Recall that given
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}\in\mathrm{Fil}
, the
geodesic
between them is defined to be the segment
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
of
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations, where

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t}=\sum_{(1-t)\mu+t\nu=\lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}.

The goal of this subsection is to justify the term
geodesic
, in the sense that it computes the distance between
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}
. In other words, it is a distance-minimizing, continuous curve with respect to the metric
d_{1}
.

We first give a formula for the geodesic between two saturated filtrations, and use it to show that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
when
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
. Then for any
t\in(0,1)
, we have

(3.6)

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}=\cap_{v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\frac{v}{c_{t,v}}),

where

c_{t,v}\coloneqq\frac{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0})v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})}{tv(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0})+(1-t)v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})}.

In particular,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
for any
t\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Denote the right-handed side of (
3.6
) by
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet,t}
and fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. First assume
f\in\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,t}
. Then for any
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
,
v(f)\geq\lambda c_{t,v}
. Since
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
, this implies that
f\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}
, where
\mu\coloneqq\frac{\lambda v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})}{tv(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0})+(1-t)v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})}
and
\nu\coloneqq\frac{\lambda v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0})}{tv(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0})+(1-t)v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})}
. Since
(1-t)\mu+t\nu=\lambda
, we get
f\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t}
and hence
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet,t}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}
.

Conversely, if
f\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t}
, then for any
v\in\mathrm{DivVal}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, we have

\displaystyle v(f)\geq

\displaystyle v\left(\sum_{(1-t)\mu+t\nu=\lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}\right)=\min_{(1-t)\mu+t\nu=\lambda}\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1})\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min_{(1-t)\mu+t\nu=\lambda}\max\{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}),v(\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1})\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq\mu\leq\lambda/(1-t)}\max\{\mu v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}),\frac{\lambda-(1-t)\mu}{t}v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})\}.

Now it is not hard to check that the last minimum of the piecewise linear function of
\mu
is achieved at
\mu_{0}:=\frac{\lambda v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})}{tv(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0})+(1-t)v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})}\in(0,\lambda/(1-t))
, with value
\mu_{0}v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0})=\lambda c_{t,v}
. This shows that
f\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v/c_{t,v})
. Since
v
is arbitrary, we get
f\in\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,t}
. Thus
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet,t}
and the equality holds.

It is easy to check that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}\in\mathrm{Fil}
for any
t\in(0,1)
(see also the proof of Proposition
3.6
below), hence by Proposition
2.20
we know that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
. The proof is finished.
∎

3.2.1.
Distance-minimizing property

We first make the following easy but useful observation.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\in\mathrm{Fil}
for
i=0,1
, and let
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be the geodesic between them. For
0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq 1
, we have
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t^{\prime}}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}
.

Proof.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we compute

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t^{\prime}}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(1-t^{\prime})\mu+t^{\prime}\nu=\lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}\right)\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\leq\lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}+\sum_{\mu>\lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,1}+\sum_{\mu>\lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}.

Since
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,1}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,s}
for any
s\in[0,1]
, it suffices to show that for any
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
satisfying
(1-t^{\prime})\mu+t^{\prime}\nu=\lambda
and
\mu>\lambda
, we have

(3.7)

\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t}.

To see this, let
\mu^{\prime}:=\frac{(1-t^{\prime})\mu+(t^{\prime}-t)\nu}{1-t}
, then
(1-t)\mu+t\nu=(1-t^{\prime})\mu+t^{\prime}\nu=\lambda
, and we have

\mu-\mu^{\prime}=\frac{(1-t)\mu-(1-t^{\prime})\mu-(t^{\prime}-t)\nu}{1-t}=\frac{(t^{\prime}-t)(\mu-\nu)}{1-t}>0,

where the last inequality follows from the condition
\mu>\lambda>\nu
. This implies

\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\mu^{\prime},0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t},

hence (
3.7
) holds and the proof is finished.
∎

Next we prove that the geodesic is always distance-minimizing.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}\in\mathrm{Fil}
and let
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be the geodesic between them. Then for any
0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq 1
, we have

(3.8)

d_{1}(a_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t^{\prime}})=d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t^{\prime}}).

Proof.

For simplicity, denote
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}:=\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}
,
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\bullet}:=\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t^{\prime}}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}:=\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t^{\prime}}
. By Lemma
3.5
we know that
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}
and
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t^{\prime}}
. We claim that for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,

(3.9)

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t}/\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\cong\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\lambda}.

Granting the claim for now, we know that
d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t})=d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})
. So we get

d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})=d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})=d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t},\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\bullet})=d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\bullet}),

which implies
d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\bullet})=d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})
. Now we compute

\displaystyle d_{1}(a_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t^{\prime}})=

\displaystyle d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\bullet})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\bullet})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t^{\prime}})

It remains to prove the claim (
3.9
). Indeed, by definition, any
f\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t}
can be written as

f=\sum_{(1-t)\mu+t\nu=\lambda}f_{\mu,\nu},

where
f_{\mu,\nu}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}
. If
\mu\geq\lambda
, then
f_{\mu,\nu}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mu,0}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0}
. If
\mu\leq\lambda
, then
\nu\geq\lambda
, and hence
f_{\mu,\nu}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\nu,1}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,1}
. Thus we have

f=\sum_{\mu\leq\lambda}f_{\mu,\nu}+\sum_{\mu\geq\lambda}f_{\mu,\nu}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,1}+\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0},

and hence
\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,1}+\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0}
, where the last inclusion follows from Lemma
3.5
. So we get

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t}/\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\cong(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,t}+\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0})/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0}=(\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda}+\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0})/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0}\cong\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda}/(\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,0})=\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\lambda},

where the last equality follows again from Lemma
3.5
. This proves (
3.9
), and finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

When
R
contains a field, the proposition can be proved (easily) using the measure as in the next section. We present the above more tedious but elementary argument, which does not rely on this assumption.

3.2.2.
Duistermaat-Heckman measures and continuity

In this subsection, we assume that
R
contains a field
\mathbbm{k}
. We first briefly recall the measure
\mu
introduced in
[
BLQ24
, Section 4]
encoding multiplicities and its properties.

Given
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}\in\mathrm{Fil}
. Define the function
H:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by

H(x,y):=\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{x\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{y\bullet,1}),

where we adopt the convention
\mathrm{e}(R)=0
. Fix
D\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{D\bullet,0}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{D\bullet,1}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

[
BLQ24
]

The distributional derivative

\mu:=-\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial x\partial y}

is a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we have

(1)

\mu(\{(1-t)x+ty<a\})=\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{a\bullet,t})=a^{n}\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t})
,

(2)

\mu\left(\{x<a\}\cup\{y<b\}\right)=\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{a\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{b\bullet,1})
,

(3)

\mu
is homogeneous of degree
n
, that is,
\mu(cA))=c^{n}\mu(A)
for any Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, and

(4)

\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subset\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}\mid\frac{1}{D}x\leq y\leq Dx\}
.

Proof.

The first two statements are
[
BLQ24
, Proposition 4.7]
and the last two are
[
BLQ24
, Proposition 4.9]
.
∎

For a rectangle, the measure
\mu
is controlled by the Lebesgue measure.

Lemma 3.9
.

Fix
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
A:=[a,a+\alpha]\times[b,b+\beta]\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a rectangle, where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Then there exists
M:=M(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\mu(A)\leq M\alpha\beta
when
\alpha,\beta\leq 1
.

Proof.

We may assume that
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
. Using the inequality

(x+\delta)^{n}-x^{n}\leq n(x+\delta)^{n-1}\delta

for
x\geq 0
and
\delta>0
, we compute

\displaystyle\mu(A)=

\displaystyle\mu([0,a+\alpha]\times[b,b+\beta])-\mu([0,a]\times[b,b+\beta])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((a+\alpha)^{n}-a^{n})\mu([0,1]\times[b,b+\beta])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((a+\alpha)^{n}-a^{n})(((b+\beta)^{n}-b^{n})\mu([0,1]\times[0,1])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle((a+\alpha)^{n}-a^{n})(((b+\beta)^{n}-b^{n})\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{2}(a+1)^{n-1}(b+1)^{n-1}\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})\cdot\alpha\beta,

where the second and the third equality follow from Lemma
3.8
(3), and the first inequality follows from Lemma
3.8
(2). Hence the proof is finished with
M:=n^{2}(a+1)^{n-1}(b+1)^{n-1}\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})
.
∎

Now we can prove that the function
t\mapsto\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to
d_{1}
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose
R
contains a field. Given
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}\in\mathrm{Fil}
, the map
[0,1]\to\mathrm{Fil}
,
t\mapsto\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}
is Lipschitz continuous, where
[0,1]
is equipped with the Euclidean topology and
\mathrm{Fil}
is equipped with the topology induced by the pseudometric
d_{1}
.

Proof.

Given
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq 1
, denote
\Delta_{j}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{>0}\mid(1-t_{j})x+t_{j}y\leq 1\}
for
j=1,2
. An easy calculation using Lemma
3.8
(1) gives

(3.10)

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t_{1}},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t_{2}})=\mu(\Delta_{1}\backslash\Delta_{2})+\mu(\Delta_{2}\backslash\Delta_{1}).

First assume that
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<1
. By Lemma
3.8
(4) we have

\mu(\Delta_{1}\backslash\Delta_{2})=\mu((\Delta_{1}\backslash\Delta_{2})\cap\{y\leq Dx\})=\mu(\Delta)

where
\Delta:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}\mid 0\leq x\leq 1,y\leq Dx,(1-t_{2})x+t_{2}y\leq 1\leq(1-t_{1})x+t_{1}y\})
. By an elementary calculation, the area of
\Delta
is at most

\mathrm{Area}(\Delta)\leq S:=\frac{D(D-1)(t_{2}-t_{1})}{(1-t_{1}+t_{1}D)(1-t_{2}+t_{2}D)}\leq D(D-1)(t_{2}-t_{1}).

Note that
(x,y)\in\Delta
satisfies
x\leq 1
and
y\leq D
. So
\Delta
can be covered by finitely many rectangles
R_{j}
of the form
[a_{j},a_{j}^{\prime}]\times[b_{j},b_{j}^{\prime}]
, satisfying

a_{j}\leq 1,\ b_{j}\leq D,\text{ and }\sum_{j}\mathrm{Area}(R_{j})\leq 2S.

Hence we may apply Lemma
3.9
to get

\displaystyle\mu(\Delta_{1}\backslash\Delta_{2})\leq

\displaystyle n^{2}2^{n-1}(D+1)^{n-1}\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})\sum_{j}\mathrm{Area}(R_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{2}2^{n}(D+1)^{n-1}\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})S

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{2}2^{n}(D+1)^{n-1}D(D-1)\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})(t_{2}-t_{1})

Similarly
\mu(\Delta_{1}\backslash\Delta_{1})
satisfies the same estimate. Hence by (
3.10
), we have

(3.11)

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t_{1}},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t_{2}})\leq n^{2}2^{n+1}(D+1)^{n-1}D(D-1)\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})(t_{2}-t_{1}).

Now assume that
t_{1}=0
. We may assume
t_{2}<\frac{1}{2}
. We have

\displaystyle\mu(\Delta_{1}\backslash\Delta_{2})=

\displaystyle\mu((\Delta_{1}\backslash\Delta_{2})\cap\{y\leq Dx\})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu([\frac{1-t_{2}D}{1-t_{2}},1]\times[1,D])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{2}(\frac{1-t_{2}D}{1-t_{2}})^{n-1}2^{n-1}(D-1)\frac{(D-1)t_{2}}{1-t_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{2}2^{2n-1}(D-1)^{2}\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})t_{2},

where the first equality follows from Lemma
3.8
(4) and the second inequality follows from Lemma
3.9
, and

\displaystyle\mu(\Delta_{2}\backslash\Delta_{1})\leq

\displaystyle\mu([1,\frac{1}{1-t_{2}}]\times[0,1])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{2}2^{n-1}\frac{t_{2}}{1-t_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{2}2^{n}\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})t_{2}

where the second inequality follows from Lemma
3.9
. Hence

(3.12)

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t_{2}})\leq n^{2}2^{n}(2^{n-1}(D-1)^{2}+1)\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1})t_{2}.

The case when
t_{2}=1
can be treated similarly. Combining the cases together, we get

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t_{1}},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t_{2}})\leq L(t_{2}-t_{1}).

where the Lipschitz constant can be taken to be

L:=n^{2}2^{n+1}(D+1)^{n-1}D(D-1)\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}),

where we used the assumption that
D\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

It is desirable to have a proof for Lemma
3.10
without involving the measure, and thus one can remove the assumption that
R
contains a field.

3.3.
Continuity along monotonic sequences

In this subsection we prove some convergence results along monotonic sequences. The results are partially inspired by the study of the completion of certain metric spaces in the global setting, for example,
[
Dar17
,
Xia23
,
BJ25
]
.

3.3.1.
Okounkov bodies and continuity along increasing sequences

In this section, using the local variant of Okounkov bodies introduced in
[
LM09
,
KK14
]
, we prove that the multiplicity function
\mathrm{e}(\bullet)
is continuous along increasing sequences of filtrations.

Given a semigroup
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}_{\geq 0}
, denote its
section at

m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
by
S_{m}:=S\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}\times\{m\})
. Recall that the
closed convex cone

C(S)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{\geq 0}
of
S
is defined to be the closure of the set
\{\sum_{i}a_{i}s_{i}\mid a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\ s_{i}\in S\}
, and its
Newton-Okounkov body
is

\Delta(S):=C(S)\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}\times\{1\}).

Lemma 3.12
.

[
LM09
]

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n+1}
be a semigroup. If
\Gamma
satisfies the following

(1)

\Gamma_{0}=\{\bm{0}\}
,

(2)

there exists finitely many vectors
(v_{i},1)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n+1}
generating a semigroup
B\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n+1}
, such that
\Gamma\subset B
, and

(3)

\Gamma
contains a set of generators of
\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
as a group,

then

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\#\Gamma_{m}}{m^{n}}=\mathrm{vol}(\Delta(\Gamma)).

We need the following formula, which relates the co-length of a primary ideal to the points of its semigroups.

Proposition 3.13
.

[
Cut14
]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian analytically irreducible local domain of dimension
n
, and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
. Then there exists
t=t(R)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
depending only on
R
, and
2t
semigroups
\bar{\Gamma}^{(j)},\Gamma^{(j)}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n+1}
,
j=1,\ldots,t
satisfying the conditions of Lemma
3.12
, such that

(3.13)

\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{m})=\sum_{j=1}^{t}(\#\bar{\Gamma}^{(j)}_{m}-\#\Gamma^{(j)}_{m}).

Proof.

We include a sketch of the constructions for the reader’s convenience. For more details about the proof, we refer to
[
Cut14
, Section 4]
.

First note that we may replace
R
by its
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion
\hat{R}
and each
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
by
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cdot R
since
\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})=\ell(\hat{R}/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\cdot R)
. Thus we may assume that
R
is complete, and hence excellent. Let
\pi:Z\to\mathrm{Spec}R
be the normalized blowup of
\mathfrak{m}
. Then there exists a closed point
z\in\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{m})
such that
R^{\prime}:=\mathcal{O}_{Z,z}
is regular since
Z
is normal.
Now
\pi
is of finite type since
R
is Nagata. Hence
R^{\prime}
is essentially of finite type over
R
. Denote
\kappa^{\prime}:=R^{\prime}/\mathfrak{n}
, where
\mathfrak{n}
is the maximal ideal of
R^{\prime}
, then we know that
t:=[\kappa^{\prime}:\kappa]\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

We proceed to define a valuation
v
on the quotient field
K:=Q(R)=Q(R^{\prime})
. Fix a regular system of parameters
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
of
R^{\prime}
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}
, which are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.
By Cohen’s structure theorem, the
\mathfrak{n}
-completion
(\widehat{R^{\prime}},\hat{\mathfrak{n}})
of
R^{\prime}
has a coefficient ring
C
as a regular local ring. Hence for any
f\in R^{\prime}
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we can write

f=\sum_{i_{1}+\ldots i_{n}<d}c_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{n}}+g_{d}\in\widehat{R^{\prime}},

where
c_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}\in C
are units and
g_{d}\in\hat{\mathfrak{n}}^{d}
. Take
d_{0}
such that
c_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}}\neq 0
for some
I_{1}+\ldots+I_{n}<d_{0}
. It is not hard to check that if
d>d_{0}+\sum\alpha_{j}I_{j}
, then the number

v(f):=\min\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}i_{j}\mid c_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}\neq 0\}

is independent of the choice of the units and
d
, hence this defines a function
v:K^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}
by
v(f/g)=v(f)-v(g)
for any
f,g\in R^{\prime}
.
One can check that
v
is a real valuation. Denote its value group by
\Gamma_{v}:=v(K^{\times})
and its valuation ring by
(\mathcal{O}_{v},\mathfrak{m}_{v})
and for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, denote

\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}:=\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{v}\mid v(f)\geq\lambda\}\text{ and }\mathfrak{b}_{>\lambda}:=\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{v}\mid v(f)>\lambda\}.

Since
\alpha_{j}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, we know that

\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{b}_{>\lambda}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &R^{\prime}/\mathfrak{n}=\kappa^{\prime},&\text{if }\lambda\in\Gamma_{v},\\
&0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

Hence for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
\dim_{\kappa}\mathfrak{a}_{m}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{m}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{>\lambda}\leq\dim_{\kappa}R\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}/R\cap\mathfrak{b}_{>\lambda}\leq[\kappa^{\prime}:\kappa]=t
.

Fix
c_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{c_{1}}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{1}
. By
[
Cut13
, Lemma 4.3]
, there exists
c_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\mathfrak{b}_{c_{2}m}\cap R\subset\mathfrak{m}^{m}
for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Set
c:=c_{1}c_{2}
. Then for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
we have

(3.14)

\mathfrak{b}_{cm}\cap R\subset\mathfrak{m}^{c_{1}m}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{m}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{m}.

To simplify the notation, for
\bm{\beta}:=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we denote
\xi(\bm{\beta}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}
and
|\bm{\beta}|:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}
. Now for
1\leq j\leq t
, define

.

\displaystyle\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{m})=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\lambda<cm}\dim_{\kappa}(R\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}/R\cap\mathfrak{b}_{>\lambda})-\sum_{0\leq\lambda<cm}\dim_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{a}_{m}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{m}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{>\lambda})

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{t}\#\{\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}\mid\dim_{\kappa}(R\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\xi(\bm{\beta})}/R\cap\mathfrak{b}_{>\xi(\bm{\beta})})\geq j,\ \xi(\bm{\beta})<cm\}

where the first equality follows from (

It is not hard to see that taking the closed convex cone commutes with taking the union.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
\{\Gamma_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{\geq 0}\}
be an increasing sequence of semigroups with respect to inclusion. Then
C(\cup_{k}\Gamma_{k})=\overline{\cup_{k}C(\Gamma_{k})}
.

In particular,
\mathrm{vol}(\Delta(\cup_{k}\Gamma_{k}))=\mathrm{vol}(\cup_{k}\Delta(\Gamma_{k}))=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}(\Delta(\Gamma_{k}))
.

Proof.

Since
C(\Gamma_{k})\subset C(\cup_{k}\Gamma_{k})
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we know that
\cup_{k}C(\Gamma_{k})\subset C(\cup_{k}\Gamma_{k})
. So
\overline{\cup_{k}C(\Gamma_{k})}\subset C(\cup_{k}\Gamma_{k})
.

To prove the reverse inclusion, it suffices to note that
\cup_{k}C(\Gamma_{k})
is a convex cone, which implies
\overline{\cup_{k}C(\Gamma_{k})}=C(\cup_{k}C(\Gamma_{k}))\supset C(\cup_{k}\Gamma_{k})
.

The last equality follows immediately.
∎

Now we are ready to show that the multiplicity is continuous along increasing sequences. The reader may want to compare the following result with similar statements in the global setting, for example,
[

, Proposition 6.11]

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
be an increasing sequence in
\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
such that
\cup\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\in\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
. Then

\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k})=0,

where
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}:=\{\cup_{k}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,k}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}

Proof.

We construct
2t
sequence of semigroups
\Gamma_{,k}^{(j)}
and
\bar{\Gamma}_{,k}^{(j)}
associated to
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}
as in Proposition
3.13

\Gamma^{(j)}
and
\bar{\Gamma}^{(j)}
be the semigroups of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
. By the proof of Proposition

it is easy to see that
\bar{\Gamma}_{,k}^{(j)}
is indeed independent of

, that is,
\bar{\Gamma}_{,k}^{(j)}=\bar{\Gamma}^{(j)}
for any

and

. We claim that for any

,

\Gamma^{(j)}=\cup_{k}\Gamma_{,k}^{(j)}.

Indeed, by definition
\Gamma_{,k}^{(j)}\subset\Gamma^{(j)}
for any

and

, so
\cup_{k}\Gamma_{,k}^{(j)}\subset\Gamma^{(j)}
. Conversely, for any
(\bm{\beta},m)\in\Gamma^{(j)}
where

, we know that

\dim_{\kappa}\mathfrak{a}_{m}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\xi(\bm{\beta})}/\mathfrak{a}_{m}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{>\xi(\bm{\beta})}\geq j.

Choose
\kappa
-linearly independent elements
\bar{f_{1}},\ldots,\bar{f_{j}}\in\mathfrak{a}_{m}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\xi(\bm{\beta})}/\mathfrak{a}_{m}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{>\xi(\bm{\beta})}
and let

-linearly independent, which implies

This shows that

Now we can apply Lemma
3.12
, Proposition
3.13
and Lemma

to get

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k})=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}(\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{m,k})-\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{m})}{m^{n}/n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n!\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\frac{(\#\bar{\Gamma}^{(j)})-\#\Gamma_{,k}^{(j)})-(\#\bar{\Gamma}^{(j)}-\#\Gamma^{(j)})}{m^{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n!\sum_{j=1}^{t}\lim_{k\to\infty}(\mathrm{vol}(\Delta(\Gamma^{(j)}))-\mathrm{vol}(\Delta(\Gamma_{,k}^{(j)})

\displaystyle=0.

This finishes the proof.
∎

3.3.2.

In this section, we assume that

Proposition 3.19

Let

Remark 3.20
.

If

3.4.
The toric case and Newton-Okounkov bodies

We deal with the toric case in this subsection. Throughout, we work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbbm{k}
of characteristic
0
and follow the notation in
[
Ful93
]
for toric varieties. Our goal is to identify the subspace
(\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}},d_{1})
of saturated monomial filtrations on a toric singularity with a subspace of the Fréchet-Nykodym-Aronszajn metric on cobounded sets of the dual cone. See
[
JM12
, Section 8]
,
[
KK14
, Section 6]
and
[
Blu18
, Section 8]
for some relevant constructions.

Let
N
be a free abelian group of rank
n\geq 1
and
M=N^{*}
its dual. Let
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes\mathbb{R}
be a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone of maximal dimension. We get an affine toric variety
X_{\sigma}=\mathrm{Spec}R_{\sigma}=\mathrm{Spec}\mathbbm{k}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
with a unique torus invariant point
x
, where
\sigma^{\vee}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}=M\otimes\mathbb{R}
is the dual cone of
\sigma
. Let
R
be the local ring of
X_{\sigma}
at
x
and
\mathfrak{m}
its maximal ideal.

Recall that there is a
1
-to-
1
correspondence between toric valuations
v_{u}\in\mathrm{Val}^{\mathrm{toric}}_{R}
and
u\in\sigma
, given by

v_{u}(\sum_{m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M}c_{m}\chi^{m})=\min\{\langle u,m\rangle\mid c_{m}\neq 0\},

and
v_{u}
is centered at
\mathfrak{m}
if and only if
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
. For
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, denote
H_{u}(\lambda)\coloneqq\{\beta\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle\beta,u\rangle\geq\lambda\}
. Then we have

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{u})=\mathrm{span}\{\chi^{m}\mid m\in H_{u}(\lambda)\cap\sigma^{\vee}\cap M\},

and

\mathrm{vol}(v_{u})=n!\cdot\mathrm{vol}(\sigma^{\vee}\backslash H_{u}(1)).

We now reproduce the generalization of the above construction to the case of monomial filtrations following
[
KK14
]
, see also
[
Mus02
]
. An
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
on
R
is called
monomial
if for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
is a monomial ideal. Denote the set of (resp. saturated) linearly bounded monomial
\mathfrak{m}
-filtrations by
\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
(resp.
\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
).

Given an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary monomial ideal
\mathfrak{a}
, the Newton polyhedron
P(\mathfrak{a})
of
\mathfrak{a}
is the convex hull of
\{m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M\mid\chi^{m}\in\mathfrak{a}\}
. Moreover, we have
\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathrm{vol}(\sigma^{\vee}\backslash P(\mathfrak{a}))
. Recall that the
Newton-Okounkov body

P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
of a monomial
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
is defined to be

P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\coloneqq\overline{\cup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\{m/\lambda\mid m\in P(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})\}}\subset\sigma^{\vee},

which is a convex set such that
\sigma^{\vee}\backslash P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
is bounded. It is known that the covolume of the Newton-Okounkov body computes the multiplicity of the filtration.

Lemma 3.22
.

[
KK14
, Theorem 6.5]

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be a monomial
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration on
R
. Then we have

\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\mathrm{vol}(\sigma^{\vee}\backslash P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})).

The next lemma gives an alternative characterization for
P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
.

Lemma 3.23
.

For
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
,
P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\cap_{u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)\cap N}H_{u}(v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))
.

Proof.

For
m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
with
\chi^{m}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
, we have
\langle u,m\rangle=v_{u}(\chi^{m})\geq v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})\geq\lambda v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
by definition, that is,
m/\lambda\in H_{u}(v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))
. Hence
P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\subset\cap_{u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)\cap N}H_{u}(v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))
.

Conversely, assume
p\in\cap_{u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)\cap N}H_{u}(v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))
. Assume
p\notin P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
. Since
P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
is convex, possibly by nudging
u
, there exist
u\in N
and
b\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\langle u,p\rangle<b
but
P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\subset H_{u}(b)
. Since
\sigma^{\vee}\backslash P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
is bounded, we know that
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
. Now
b>\langle u,p\rangle\geq v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
by the choice of
p
. Hence there exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\chi^{m}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
such that
\langle u,m\rangle=v_{u}(\chi^{m})=v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})<\lambda\cdot b
. In particular,
m/\lambda\in P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\backslash H_{u}(b)
, a contradiction. So
\cap_{u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)\cap N}H_{u}(v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))\subset P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
and the proof is finished.
∎

Next we show that the saturation of a monomial filtration can be computed using only toric divisorial valuations.

Lemma 3.24
.

A monomial filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
is saturated if and only if it is of the form

(3.17)

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\cap_{u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)\cap N}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v_{u}/a_{u})

where
a_{u}:\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a homogeneous function for any
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)\cap N
.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is of the form (
3.17
), then
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is saturated by Proposition
2.20
since
v_{u}\in\mathrm{Val}^{+}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is saturated. Then we know that

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}_{\bullet}\coloneqq\cap_{u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)\cap N}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\frac{v_{u}}{v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})})

by Lemma
2.19
. Moreover,
v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}^{\prime})=v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
since
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v_{u}/v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))
for any
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)\cap N
. So
P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=P(\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}_{\bullet})
by Lemma
3.23
, and hence
\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}_{\bullet})
by Lemma
3.22
. Now
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}_{\bullet}
is saturated by Proposition
2.20
again, and so by Theorem
2.18
, we get
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}_{\bullet}
.
∎

Now we can characterize the intersection of two monomial filtrations.

Lemma 3.25
.

Let
I
be a countable index set and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{X,x}
for
i\in I
and assume that
\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{X,x}
. Then

(3.18)

v_{u}(\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i})=\sup_{i\in I}v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i})

for any
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
. As a result, we have

P(\cap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i})=\cap_{i\in I}P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i})
.

Proof.

Since
\cap_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
for any
i
, we have
v(\cap_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i})\geq\sup_{i\in I}v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i})
for any
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
.

To prove the reverse inequality for toric valuations, we first treat the case where
I
is finite. It suffices to prove
v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})=\max\{v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),v_{u}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\}
for any
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
and apply induction. Denote
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\coloneqq\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
, and
a_{u}\coloneqq v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
,
b_{u}\coloneqq v_{u}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})
,
c_{u}\coloneqq v_{u}(\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})
and
c^{\prime}_{u}\coloneqq\max\{a_{u},b_{u}\}
. Assume to the contrary that there exists
w\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
such that
c_{w}>c^{\prime}_{w}
. Fix
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
c_{w}(1-\delta)>c^{\prime}_{w}
. Choose
p\in P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\cap P(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})
such that
\langle p,w\rangle=\min_{q\in P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\cap P(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})}\langle q,w\rangle
. Let

C\coloneqq\{p\}+(\sigma^{\vee}\backslash H_{w}(\delta c^{\prime}_{w}))\subset\sigma^{\vee},

where
+
denotes the Minkowski sum. Take
p_{i}\in C\cap M_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
p_{i}\to p
and
k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
k_{i}p_{i}\in M
. By Lemma
3.23
, for any
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
,
\langle u,p_{i}\rangle\geq\langle u,p\rangle\geq c^{\prime}_{u}
. Hence
\chi^{k_{i}p_{i}}\in\mathfrak{a}_{k_{i}}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{k_{i}}
by Lemma
3.24
, which implies

v_{w}(\mathfrak{a}_{k_{i}}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{k_{i}})/k_{i}\leq\langle w,p_{i}\rangle<\langle w,p\rangle+\delta c^{\prime}_{w}=(1+\delta)c^{\prime}_{w},

where the last equality follows from Lemma
3.23
. Letting
k_{i}\to\infty
we get
c_{w}\leq(1+\delta)c^{\prime}_{w}
. Thus we get
c^{\prime}_{w}<(1-\delta)c_{w}<(1-\delta^{2})c^{\prime}_{w}
, a contradiction.

The proof for the general case is quite similar. For simplicity let
I=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. By the case where
I
is finite, we may replace
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
by
\cap_{j\leq i}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,j}
and assume that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}
is decreasing. Assume to the contrary that there exists
w\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
such that
a_{w}>\sup_{i\in I}a_{w,i}
. Fix
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
a_{w}(1-4\delta)>\sup_{i}a_{w,i}
. For each
i\in I
, there exists
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\chi^{m_{i}}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda_{i},i}
such that

\lambda_{i}\cdot a_{w,i}\leq v_{w}(\chi^{m_{i}})=\langle w,m_{i}\rangle<\lambda_{i}\cdot a_{w}(1-3\delta),

where the first inequality follows from the assumption that
\chi^{m_{i}}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda_{i},i}
and the second inequality follows from the definition
v_{w}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i})=\lim_{\lambda}v(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,i})/\lambda
. In particular,
m_{i}/\lambda_{i}
lies in a bounded region and hence after passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
m_{i}/\lambda_{i}\to p\in\sigma^{\vee}\backslash H_{u}(a_{u}(1-2\delta))
. Now consider the region

C\coloneqq\{p\}+(\sigma^{\vee}\backslash H_{w}(\delta a_{w}))\subset\sigma^{\vee}

where
+
denotes the Minkowski sum. Then there exists
p_{j}\in C\cap M_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
p_{j}\to p
. Take
k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
k_{j}p_{j}\in M
. Then for any
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
, we have

\langle u,p\rangle\leq\langle u,p_{j}\rangle

for any
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
and

(3.19)

\langle w,p_{j}\rangle<a_{w}(1-2\delta)+\delta a_{w}=a_{w}(1-\delta).

By the first inequality, for any
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
we have

\langle u,k_{j}p_{j}\rangle/k_{j}\geq\langle u,p\rangle=\lim_{i}\langle u,m_{i}/\lambda_{i}\rangle\geq\sup_{i}a_{u,i},

where in the last inequality we used the fact that
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,i}\}
is decreasing. Hence
\chi^{k_{j}p_{j}}\in\cap_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{k_{j},i}
by Lemma
3.24
, which implies
\langle u,p_{j}\rangle=\langle u,k_{j}p_{j}\rangle/k_{j}\geq a_{u}
for any
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
. In particular, we get

a_{w}\leq\langle w,p_{j}\rangle<a_{w}-\delta,

a contradiction. This contradiction proves (
3.18
) and the last assertion follows from (
3.18
) and Lemma
3.23
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{P}(\sigma^{\vee})\coloneqq\{P\subsetneq\sigma^{\vee}\mid P\text{ is closed and convex, }\sigma^{\vee}\backslash P\text{ is bounded}\}
. We prove that taking the Newton-Okounkov body gives a bijection between
\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
and
\mathcal{P}(\sigma^{\vee})
. Note that the following lemma also justifies the statement that “if

should be regarded as equisingular” in

Lemma 3.26
.

P:\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}\to\mathcal{P}(\sigma^{\vee})
is a bijection, whose inverse is given by
P\mapsto\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(P)
, where

(3.20)

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(P)\coloneqq\mathrm{span}\{\chi^{m},m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M\mid\langle m,u\rangle\geq\lambda\cdot\inf_{p\in P}\langle p,u\rangle\text{ for any }u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)\},

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{X,x}
. It is known that
P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
is convex (e.g.
[
KK14
]
). Fix
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
. Since
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is linearly bounded, by definition there exists
c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet/c^{\prime}}
, which implies
v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\geq v_{u}(\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet/c^{\prime}})=c^{\prime}v_{u}(\mathfrak{m})\eqqcolon c>0
. By Lemma
3.24
, we get
P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\subset H_{u}(c)\subsetneq\sigma^{\vee}
. Fix
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{d}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{1}
. Then for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
we have
\mathfrak{m}^{dm}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{m}
. By Proposition
2.27
, there exist finitely many
u_{i}\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
,
i=1,\ldots,k
such that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{d\bullet}}=\cap_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v_{u_{i}})
. By Lemma
3.23
and Lemma
3.25
, we have

P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\supset P(\mathfrak{m}^{d\bullet)}=P(\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{d\bullet}})=\cap_{i=1}^{k}P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v_{u_{i}})=\cap_{i=1}^{k}H_{u_{i}}(1).

Thus
\sigma^{\vee}\backslash P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\subset\cup_{i=1}^{k}\sigma^{\vee}\backslash H_{u_{i}}(1)

P:\mathrm{Fil}^{s,\mathrm{mon}}_{X,x}\to\mathcal{P}(\sigma^{\vee})
.

Now let
P\in\mathcal{P}(\sigma^{\vee})
. We need to prove that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(P)
is a linearly bounded monomial
\mathfrak{m}
-filtration. It is easy to see from (

) that
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(P)
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary monomial ideal. Condition (1) of Definition

follows immediately from (

\langle\bullet,u\rangle
. To prove (2), it suffices to note that

is discrete, and hence for any

and

such that
H_{u}(\lambda-\epsilon)\backslash H_{u}(\lambda)\cap\sigma^{\vee}\cap M=\emptyset

It is then routine to check the four inclusions. We include the proof for the reader’s convenience.

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))
. If
\chi^{m}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
, then
m/\lambda\in P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})

\chi^{m}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))
by (

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
. By Lemma
3.23

\langle p,u\rangle\geq v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
for any

and

\langle m,u\rangle\geq\lambda\cdot\inf_{p\in P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}\langle p,u\rangle\geq\lambda v_{u}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})

for any

3.24
, we know
\chi^{m}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
.

P\subset P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(P))

p\in P

such that
p_{i}\to p
and
k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that

.

P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(P))\subset P
. If
p\in P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(P))
, then by definition, there exists
m_{i}\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\chi^{m_{i}}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda_{i}}
and
m_{i}/\lambda_{i}\to p

3.20
), this implies
\langle p,u\rangle\geq\inf_{q\in P}\langle q,u\rangle
for any
u\in\mathrm{Int}(\sigma)
. Applying the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.23
, we know that
p\in P
.
∎

Recall that the symmetric difference metric (also called the Fréchet-Nikodym-Aronszayn distance)
d
on
\mathcal{P}(\sigma^{\vee})
is defined by

d(P_{1},P_{2})\coloneqq\mathrm{vol}(P_{1}\Delta P_{2}),

where
P_{1}\Delta P_{2}=P_{1}\backslash P_{2}\cup P_{2}\backslash P_{1}
is the symmetric difference. We are now ready to prove

Proposition 3.27
.

The map
P:(\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}},d_{1})\to(P(\sigma^{\vee},d)
,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\mapsto P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
is an isometry.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.26
,
P
is a bijection.

For simplicity, denote
\mathrm{covol}(P)\coloneqq\mathrm{vol}(\sigma^{\vee}\backslash P)
below. For
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})=

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{covol}(P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}))-\mathrm{covol}(P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))-\mathrm{covol}(P(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{covol}(P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\cap P(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}))-\mathrm{covol}(P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))-\mathrm{covol}(P(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}(P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\backslash P(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}))+\mathrm{vol}(P(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\backslash P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}(P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\Delta P(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}))=d(P(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),P(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})),

where the second equality follows from Lemma
3.23

3.25

∎

4.
Supnorm metrics on the space of filtrations

In this section, we study the basic properties of
d_{\infty}
, prove the local Lipschitz continuity of log canonical thresholds, and compare several different topologies on the filtration spaces.

4.1.
Supnorm metrics and homogenization

Throughout this subsection, we will use the language of norms. Recall that
\mathcal{N}
is the set of linearly bounded
\mathfrak{m}
-norms on
R
.

Definition 4.1
.

Fix a norm
\rho\in\mathcal{N}
. We define a function
d_{\infty,\rho}:\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by

d_{\infty,\rho}(\chi,\chi^{\prime}):=\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\sup_{\rho(f)\geq\lambda}\left\{\frac{|\chi(f)-\chi^{\prime}(f)|}{\rho(f)}\right\}.

We will denote
d_{\infty,\chi_{0}}=:d_{\infty}
.

It is not hard to see that the
d_{\infty}
-topology is independent of the choice of the reference norm.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\rho\in\mathcal{N}
,
d_{\infty,\rho}
defines a pseudometric on
\mathcal{N}
. Moreover, for any
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}
, there exists
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
M^{-1}d_{\infty,\rho}\leq d_{\infty,\rho^{\prime}}\leq Md_{\infty,\rho}
.

Proof.

By definition,
d_{\infty,\rho}
is symmetric and non-negative for any
\rho
. Given
\chi,\chi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{N}
, since
|\chi(f)-\chi^{\prime\prime}(f)|\leq|\chi(f)-\chi^{\prime}(f)|+|\chi^{\prime}(f)-\chi^{\prime\prime}(f)|
, the triangle inequality holds. This proves that
d_{\infty,\rho}
is a pseudometric.

The second claim follows from the fact that given
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}
, there exists
M\gg 0
such that
M^{-1}\rho\leq\rho^{\prime}\leq M\rho
.
∎

On
\mathcal{N}^{h}
, we do not need to take
\limsup
in the definition of
d_{\infty}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}^{h}
, then

d_{\infty}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})=\sup_{f\in\mathfrak{m}}\frac{|\chi(f)-\chi(f^{\prime})|}{\chi_{0}(f)}.

Proof.

For any
\epsilon>0
, take
g\in\mathfrak{m}
such that
\frac{|\chi(g)-\chi^{\prime}(g)|}{\chi_{0}(g)}>\sup_{f\in\mathfrak{m}}\frac{|\chi(f)-\chi(f^{\prime})|}{\chi_{0}(f)}-\epsilon\eqqcolon d-\epsilon
. Denote
a:=\chi_{0}(g)>0
. Since
\chi
,
\chi^{\prime}
and
\chi_{0}
are homogeneous, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\chi_{0}(f)=ka}\frac{|\chi(f)-\chi(f^{\prime})|}{\chi_{0}(f)}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{|\chi(g^{k})-\chi^{\prime}(g^{k})|}{\chi_{0}(g^{k})}=\frac{|\chi(g)-\chi^{\prime}(g)|}{\chi_{0}(g)}>d-\epsilon.

So

d_{\infty}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})=\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\sup_{\chi_{0}(f)\geq\lambda}\frac{|\chi(f)-\chi(f^{\prime})|}{\chi_{0}(f)}\geq d-\epsilon.

Thus
d_{\infty}(\chi.\chi^{\prime})\geq d
. Since the reverse inequality is obvious, this finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}
, then
d_{\infty}(\widehat{\chi},\widehat{\chi}^{\prime})\leq d_{\infty}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Denote
d:=d_{\infty}(\widehat{\chi},\widehat{\chi}^{\prime})
. By Lemma
4.3
, we may assume that
d=\sup\frac{\widehat{\chi}(f)-\widehat{\chi}^{\prime}(f)}{\chi_{0}(f)}
. For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
f\in\mathfrak{m}
such that

\displaystyle(d-\epsilon)\chi_{0}(f)<\widehat{\chi}(f)-\widehat{\chi}^{\prime}(f)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\chi(f^{k})}{k}-\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\chi^{\prime}(f^{k})}{k}.

Thus for
k\gg 0
we have

\chi(f^{k})-\chi^{\prime}(f^{k})\geq(d-2\epsilon)k\chi_{0}(f)=(d-2\epsilon)\chi_{0}(f^{k})

since
\chi_{0}
is homogeneous. Similar to the proof of Lemma
4.3
we get
d_{\infty}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})\geq d
.
∎

The following lemma shows that
(\mathcal{N}^{h},d_{\infty})
is complete.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\{\chi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
be a Cauchy sequence in
(\mathcal{N}^{h},d_{\infty})
. Then there exists
\chi\in\mathcal{N}^{h}
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{\infty}(\chi_{k},\chi)=0.

Proof.

Given any
\epsilon>0
, by assumption there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that for any
k,k^{\prime}>K
,
d_{\infty}(\chi_{k},\chi_{k^{\prime}})<\epsilon
. For any
f\in\mathfrak{m}
and
k,k^{\prime}>K
, by Lemma
4.3
we know that

|\chi_{k}(f)-\chi_{k^{\prime}}(f)|\leq\chi_{0}(f)d_{\infty}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})<\chi_{0}(f)\cdot\epsilon.

Hence
\{\chi_{k}(f)\}
is a Cauchy sequence and we may define
\chi(f):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\chi_{k}(f)
.
It is elementary to verify that
\chi\in\mathcal{N}^{h}
. Then we can apply Lemma
4.3
to get
\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{\infty}(\chi_{k},\chi)=0
.
∎

4.2.
Local Lipschitz continuity of log canonical thresholds

In this subsection we prove that the log canonical threshold is locally Lipschitz continuous with respect to the
d_{\infty}
-topology.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\chi\in\mathcal{N}
. For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\chi,\epsilon)>0\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that for any
\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}
with
d_{\infty}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})<\delta
, we have
|\mathrm{lct}(\chi^{\prime})-\mathrm{lct}(\chi)|\leq\epsilon
.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{lct}(\chi)=\mathrm{lct}(\widehat{\chi})
for any
\chi\in\mathcal{N}
, by Lemma
4.4
, we may assume that
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}^{h}
.
Fix
c=c(\chi):=\inf_{f\in\mathfrak{m}}\frac{\chi(f)}{\chi_{0}(f)}>0
and denote
a:=\mathrm{lct}(\chi)>0
.

Assume that
d_{\infty}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})<\delta
for some
\delta\in(0,c)
. By Lemma
4.3
, we know that for any
f\in R
,

\frac{\chi^{\prime}(f)}{\chi_{0}(f)}\geq\frac{\chi(f)}{\chi_{0}(f)}-\delta\geq c-\delta.

Now we compute

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{v(f)}{\chi^{\prime}(f)}=

\displaystyle\frac{v(f)}{\chi(f)}\left(1+\frac{\chi(f)-\chi^{\prime}(f)}{\chi^{\prime}(f)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v(f)}{\chi(f)}\left(1+\frac{\chi_{0}(f)}{\chi^{\prime}(f)}\frac{\chi(f)-\chi^{\prime}(f)}{\chi_{0}(f)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{v(f)}{\chi(f)}\left(1+\frac{\delta}{c-\delta}\right)

For
\delta\in(0,c/2)
, by Lemma
2.14
we get that
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi^{\prime}))\leq(1+2\delta/c)v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi))
for any
v
. Let

\delta:=\min\{\frac{c}{2},\frac{c\epsilon}{a}\}>0,

which depends only on
\epsilon
and
\chi
. Then for any
\chi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
with
d_{\infty}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})<\delta
and any
v\in\mathrm{Val}^{<+\infty}_{X,x}
, we have

\frac{A(v)}{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi^{\prime}))}>(1-\frac{\delta}{2c})\frac{A(v)}{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi))}\geq(1-\frac{\delta}{2c})\mathrm{lct}(\chi)=a-\frac{a}{2c}\delta>a-\epsilon.

Taking infimum among all
v
gives
\mathrm{lct}(\chi^{\prime})\geq a-\epsilon
.

Interchanging
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
in (
4.1
), by Lemma
2.14
we get
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi))\leq(1+\delta/c)v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi^{\prime}))
. By
[
Blu18
, Theorem B.1]
, there exists
v_{0}\in\mathrm{Val}^{<+\infty}_{X,x}
computing the lct of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi)
, thus

\mathrm{lct}(\chi^{\prime})\leq\frac{A(v_{0})}{v_{0}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi^{\prime}))}\leq(1+\frac{\delta}{c})\frac{A(v_{0})}{v_{0}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(\chi))}=(1+\frac{\delta}{c})a\leq a+\epsilon.

The proof is finished.
∎

Comparison of different topologies on
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}

Now we would like to compare the topologies on the space
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
.

First we show that a

Lemma 4.7

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}

\lim_{k}d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=0

We claim that if

For any

and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}|v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k})-v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})|\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}|v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k})-v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})|+\lim_{k\to\infty}|v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})-v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})|=0.

\ell(\mathfrak{b}_{m\ell_{k},k}/\mathfrak{a}_{m\ell_{k},k})\geq\ell(R/\ker(\phi_{k,m})\geq\ell(R/\mathfrak{a}_{m\ell_{k}\epsilon}(v).

Letting

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
be filtrations satisfying
\mathfrak{m}^{C\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
and
\mathfrak{m}^{C\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
for some
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}
. Then there exists a constant
M:=M(C)>0
such that

(4.2)

d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\leq M\cdot d_{\infty}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}).

As a consequence, the
d_{\infty}
-topology is finer than the
d_{1}
-topology.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.4
we may assume
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{h}
. Since
d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})=d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})+d_{1}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})
and
d_{\infty}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\geq\max\{d_{\infty}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}),d_{\infty}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet})\}
, where
\mathfrak{c}_{\bullet}\coloneqq\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
, we may further assume that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
(but replace
M
by
2M
).

Denote
d:=d_{\infty}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})
. Then we know that

0\leq\frac{\chi_{\mathfrak{b}}(f)}{\chi_{\mathfrak{a}}(f)}-1=\frac{\chi_{0}(f)}{\chi_{\mathfrak{a}}(f)}\cdot\frac{\chi_{\mathfrak{b}}(f)-\chi_{\mathfrak{a}}(f)}{\chi_{0}(f)}\leq Cd,

which implies

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet/(1+Cd)}.

Hence we get

\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\geq\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})\geq(1+Cd)^{-n}\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),

that is

\displaystyle d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})=

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})-\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-(1+Cd)^{-n}\right)\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle nCd\cdot\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle nC^{n+1}\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{m})\cdot d,

where in the last inequality we used the fact that
(1+x)^{-n}\geq 1-nx
for
x\geq 0
. This proves (
4.2
) with
M\coloneqq 2nC^{n+1}\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{m})
.

Given
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
, we may take
C\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{C}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{1}
which implies
\mathfrak{m}^{C\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
. Now for
0<\epsilon<1/2C
and
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fil}
with
d_{\infty}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet})<\epsilon
, by definition we have
\mathfrak{m}^{2C\bullet}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
. Hence the second assertion follows from (
4.2
).
∎

To summarize, we have the following diagram for the convergence of sequences

d_{\infty}\text{-convergence}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\implies\text{weak convergence}\\
&\implies d_{1}\text{-convergence}\implies+\text{-convergence}\implies\text{coefficientwise convergence}\end{aligned}\right.

Here the upper line is obvious; the first two arrows of the lower line follow from Lemma
4.8
and Lemma
4.7
respectively; and the last arrow follows from the fact that
\mathrm{DivVal}_{X,x}\subset\mathrm{Val}^{+}_{X,x}
. It is not hard to see that the upper line is not an equivalence. We do not have any counterexamples for the other reverse arrows.

As was mentioned earlier, our definition for the metric

R_{0}

\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m},A}:=\{\mathfrak{a}^{c}\mid\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}},\ c\in A\},

where
A=\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
or
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Clearly, the following metric makes sense.

d_{1,A}(\mathfrak{a}^{c},\mathfrak{b}^{d}):=2\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}^{c\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{d\bullet})-c^{n}\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a})-d^{n}\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{b}).

Warning
. These are in general two different metrics on
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

6.3.
The structure of finitely generated filtrations

Recall that a filtration
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is
finitely generated
, or
Noetherian
, if the
associated graded ring

\mathrm{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}R\coloneqq\oplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}
is a finitely generated
\kappa
-algebra.

The finite generation of valuation ideals has been extensively studied since
[
BCHM10
]
. See
[
LXZ22
,
XZ25
]
for recent progress based on the theory of special complements.

As another application of Proposition
3.15
, we show that finitely generated filtrations are dense in
\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
, which is the local analogue of
[
BJ25
, Corollary 3.19]
.

Proposition 6.3
.

The subset of finitely generated filtrations is dense in
\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
with respect to
d_{1}
.

Proof.

The following construction is a local analogue of
[
Xu25
, Definition-Lemma 3.56]
. For
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda,k}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda},&\text{ if }\lambda\leq k,\\
&\sum_{\vec{\mu}}\mathfrak{a}_{\mu_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathfrak{a}_{\mu_{s}},&\text{ otherwise,}\end{aligned}\right.

where the sum is taken over all vectors
\vec{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{s})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{s}
satisfying
\sum_{i}\mu_{i}\geq\lambda
and
\mu_{i}\leq k
for any
1,\ldots,s
. Note that the sum is finite since the set of jumping numbers of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
contained in
(0,k]
is finite.

It is easy to check that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\in\mathrm{Fil}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
. Moreover,
\mathrm{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}}R
is generated by elements of degree at most
k
, and hence is finitely generated again by the finiteness of jumping numbers.

Clearly
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\}
is an increasing sequence and
\cup_{k}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}=\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
. So by Proposition
3.15
, we know that
d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k})\to 0
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

In general, the saturation of a finitely generated filtration is not finitely generated. Hence, the above proposition says nothing about the structure or distribution of finitely generated
saturated
filtrations, which remains rather mysterious.

Indeed, in
[
LX24
]
, Y. Liu and Xu proposed several conjectures regarding the structure of Kollár valuations, which are special classes of finitely generated filtrations. See
[
ABB+23
,
LXZ22
,
Pen25
]
for some special cases.

Moreover, in view of
[
LXZ22
, Section 4]
and
[
XZ25
, Section 4.3]
, it seems that the specialty of quasi-monomial valuations inside a single dual complex is related to the geodesics.

6.4.
Questions

We propose a few questions related to the metric space
(\mathrm{Fil}^{s},d_{1})
.

6.4.1.
Completeness under the Darvas metric

Recall that by
[
Xia23
]
, in order to prove the completeness of the metric space
(\mathrm{Fil}^{s},d_{1})
, it is enough to check that a (bounded) monotonic sequence has a limit. By Proposition
3.15
, this is true for increasing sequences. So it is natural to ask the following question:

Question 6.5
.

Let
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
be a decreasing sequence in
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\coloneqq\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k}\in\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
,
do we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet},\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,k})=0?

We remark that convergence along decreasing sequences or nets has been studied in the complex analytic setting
[
Dar17
, Proposition 6.11]
and the global non-archimedean setting
[
BJ22
, Theorem 12.5]
. The answer to the above question is likely no, but we do not have a counterexample.

6.4.2.
More structural questions

We know in Example
6.1
that
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
is Euclidean when
R
is a DVR. This is never the case in higher dimensions. Moreover, geodesics between two incomparable filtrations
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
are never unique, since the geodesic between
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
is distinct from the one obtained from combining the piecewise geodesics connecting
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}
. It might be interesting to ask the relationship between the topological or metric properties of
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}_{R,\mathfrak{m}}
and the algebraic properties of the ring
R
. For example,

Question 6.6
.

Is
\mathrm{Fil}^{s}
always contractible? If not, is the contractibility related to the algebraic properties of
R
?

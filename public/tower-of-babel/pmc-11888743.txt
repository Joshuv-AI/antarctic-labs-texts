A Prospective Study Comparing the Use of Radiometric Measurements in CBCT and DEXA Scans of the Spine to Measure Bone Mineral Density in Postmenopausal Women
Authors: Sriram Chandra Mallick, S Bhuvaneshwari, N.C. Sangamesh, Silpiranjan Mishra, Atul Anand Bajoria, Dhirendra Kumar Singh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:This study evaluated BMD using radiomorphometric indices from conebeam computed tomography (CBCT) scans of the mandible, comparing them with dualenergy Xray absorptiometry (DEXA) scans of the spine.Results:The study emphasizes the robustness of DEXAderived T scores for assessing bone health and guiding treatment in postmenopausal women.

INTRODUCTION

Bone mineral density (BMD) measures the amount of minerals, primarily calcium and phosphorus, in bones, indicating their health and strength. Postmenopausal women experience significant changes in bone health due to hormonal shifts and decreased estrogen levels. According to the World Health Organization, menopause occurs around ages 45 to 55, leading to a gradual decline in estrogen production.[1] Estrogen is crucial for maintaining bone density by promoting bone formation and inhibiting bone resorption. Reduced estrogen levels increase bone turnover, resulting in decreased bone mass and density, making postmenopausal women more prone to osteoporosis and fractures. Osteoporosis, characterized by bone tissue deterioration and reduced bone mass, makes bones highly susceptible to fractures, particularly in the spine, hips, and wrists. Postmenopausal women are at higher risk for osteoporosis, and BMD measurements are commonly used to assess this risk and monitor bone health. The high cost of treatment and psychological strain associated with osteoporosis have significant social and economic impacts globally. International guidelines recommend bone densitometry for postmenopausal women under 65 and those with additional risk factors such as amenorrhea, hyperthyroidism, low estrogen levels, chronic hepatitis C, and chronic rheumatoid arthritis. Diagnosing osteoporosis can be challenging due to its subtle symptoms, making it crucial for medical professionals to be vigilant in detection and diagnosis to ensure timely specialized care. Cone-Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT) has been extensively used in dentistry since its inception in 1998. A 2019 American Dental Association survey reported that 43% of dentists owned CBCT machines. CBCT provides detailed three-dimensional images of the jaw, teeth, and adjacent areas, enhancing diagnosis and treatment planning in dental procedures such as implant placement, orthodontic planning, endodontic therapy, and assessing oral pathology. Compared to conventional medical CT scans, CBCT delivers both 2D and 3D images with reduced radiation exposure and costs while maintaining superior image quality.[2] Previous studies suggest using CBCT as an early screening technique for detecting low BMD. The purpose of this study is to assess bone mineral density in postmenopausal women by comparing radio-morphometric mandibular indices on CBCT with DEXA SCAN results for the spine.

METHODOLOGY

A cross-sectional study was conducted in the Department of Oral Medicine and Radiology, Kalinga Institute of Dental Sciences with the aid of Department of Radiodiagnosis, Kalinga Institute of Medical Sciences, Bhubaneswar. Postmenopausal women who consent to participate will be included in the study. Patients with history of systemic diseases affecting bone metabolism, any injury to the jaw bones and spine. Patients who had undergone radiation therapy and chemotherapy. Patients with metabolic diseases of bone were on hormone replacement therapy on calcium and vitamin D supplementation.

Armamentarium

CBCT Machine (Hyperion X9, Myray, Italy, voxel size − 75 μm × 75 μm; FOV − 11 × 8); DEXA Machine (Lunar, GE Healthcare); Scanner size − 2.87 m × 1.31 m × 1.25 m (113” × 52” × 49”); and scanner weight − 353 kg (778 lbs). Windows Desktop (Hp) with 18-inch LED screen (Intel® Core™ i3 Processor) and IRYs Software with measurement tool (Myray, Italy) were used. The radiomorphometric indices were determined using a 1 mm slice thickness and an interval of 1 mm between slices in CBCT cross-sectional images of the mandible. Two lines were drawn: one parallel to the base of the mandibular cortical bone and the other to the medullary-cortical boundary. The length of a perpendicular line joining these two lines served as the definition for the index measurement. The following indices were calculated: symphysis index (S); anterior index (A); molar index (M); and posterior index (P) [Figures 1 and 2].

DISCUSSION

This study examined the evaluation of BMD in postmenopausal women using various radiomorphometric indices in CBCT mandibular examinations. The results indicated that the P and M indices in mandibular CBCT scans are useful for assessing BMD in postmenopausal women. Patients with osteopenia and/or osteoporosis, as well as those with normal BMD, exhibited significant differences in these parameters.

Use of High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound in the Periorbital Rejuvenation: A Systematic Review
Authors: Narendra Kumar, Guilherme Olavo Olsen de Almeida, Amit Luthra, Shaheen Amna Kashif, Serge Dahan, Jean D A Carruthers
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPeriorbital aging represents one of the earliest manifestations of facial aging, resulting from structural changes across cutaneous, adipose, and muscular layers of the periocular region. Although high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) is increasingly used for noninvasive rejuvenation, evidence on periorbital outcomes remains scarce.ObjectivesTo synthesize the effectiveness and safety of HIFU technologies for periorbital rejuvenation in adults.MethodsA systematic review was conducted according to PRISMA 2020 guidelines and registered in PROSPERO. MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane CENTRAL, and Google Scholar were searched for studies published between January 2015 and November 2025. Randomized trials and prospective or retrospective cohort studies evaluating HIFU applied to the periorbital region were included. Outcomes included wrinkle severity, skin elasticity, texture, lifting effects, patient satisfaction, and adverse events. Risk of bias was assessed.ResultsTen studies involving 10 to 191 participants were included. Across included studies, HIFU demonstrated significant improvements in periorbital wrinkle severity, biomechanical skin elasticity (Cutometer R2, R5, and R7), surface topography (PRIMOS Ra and Rz), and eyebrow or eyelid contour. Greater improvement was observed with multiple treatment sessions. Adverse events were generally mild and transient, including erythema, edema, and pain; rare complications (<1%) occurred without permanent sequelae.ConclusionsHigh-intensity focused ultrasound appears to be a safe and effective noninvasive option for periorbital rejuvenation. However, the certainty of evidence is very low due to nonrandomized designs and heterogeneity, highlighting the need for well-designed randomized controlled trials with standardized protocols.Level of Evidence: 3 (Therapeutic)

Periorbital aging represents one of the earliest and most visible manifestations of facial aging, driven by the distinctive anatomical and physiological features of the periocular region.1 The skin around the eyes is the thinnest in the body, contains minimal subcutaneous fat, and is exposed to constant muscular activity and environmental stressors.2,3 These factors contribute to the early appearance of fine lines, crow's feet, skin laxity, tear-trough deformities, and periorbital shadowing, changes commonly associated with fatigue and perceived aging.4 Consequently, demand for safe, effective, and noninvasive rejuvenation strategies has increased substantially.

High-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) has emerged as a prominent noninvasive modality for skin tightening and lifting.5 By delivering focused acoustic energy to precise subdermal depths, HIFU generates thermal coagulation points that induce collagen denaturation and contraction, followed by remodeling through neocollagenesis and neoelastogenesis.6-8 Because the epidermis remains largely unaffected, the technique can produce aesthetic improvements with minimal downtime and a favorable safety profile.

Various HIFU modalities enable targeting of dermal and subdermal layers at depths ranging from 1.5 to 13 mm, including the superficial musculoaponeurotic system.9 Some HIFU modalities, in particular, offer high-precision focal energy delivery suitable for delicate areas such as the periocular region.10 However, considerable heterogeneity in device platforms, energy parameters, treatment depths, session protocols, and outcome assessments complicates comparisons across studies.

Although HIFU has demonstrated efficacy for overall facial rejuvenation, evidence specific to the periorbital region is comparatively limited and fragmented. Variations in patient selection, device types, wrinkle-grading scales, imaging modalities, and follow-up duration complicate the interpretation of clinical effectiveness. Moreover, given the thin and highly sensitive nature of periocular tissues, evaluating the safety profile, including edema, erythema, pain, and risk of ocular injury, is critical before establishing standardized clinical protocols.

Therefore, a systematic evaluation of the available clinical literature is needed. This review aims to synthesize current evidence regarding the effectiveness and safety of HIFU for periorbital rejuvenation, compare reported outcomes across modalities, identify commonly used treatment parameters, and summarize reported adverse events and patient-reported satisfaction.

METHODS

This systematic review was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 guidelines.11 The review was designed to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of HIFU and its variants: microfocused ultrasound, microfocused ultrasound with visualization, and macrofocused ultrasound for periorbital rejuvenation in adults. We included randomized controlled trials and prospective and retrospective cohort studies. Narrative reviews, technical engineering papers, animal studies, case reports, and studies in which periorbital outcomes could not be isolated were excluded according to the protocol criteria. This review was previously registered in PROSPERO (ID CRD420251208356).

Eligibility Criteria

Eligible participants were adults aged 18 years and older who had undergone HIFU-based interventions targeting the periorbital area for aesthetic purposes. Treatments aimed at improving wrinkles, skin laxity, dermal thickness, or overall periorbital rejuvenation were included. Any HIFU device model or manufacturer was eligible, regardless of frequency, focal depth, or energy parameters. Studies involving mixed facial areas were included only when periorbital data were clearly reported. Interventions such as laser resurfacing, radiofrequency, and other energy-based devices were considered only when used as comparators. Studies combining HIFU with other treatments were excluded unless HIFU-specific periorbital outcomes were extractable.

Information Sources and Search Strategy

A comprehensive search strategy was applied across MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, and the Cochrane CENTRAL database. Each search incorporated MeSH terms and free-text words covering periorbital aging, HIFU modalities, and rejuvenation outcomes (Supplemental Table 1). Date and English language limits were applied manually across all databases. All retrieved references were imported into a reference manager and deduplicated before screening.

Selection Process

Three reviewers independently screened titles and abstracts, followed by full-text evaluation of potentially eligible studies. Any inconsistencies between reviewers were resolved through discussion or by consulting the senior reviewer. Reasons for exclusion during full-text review were documented and represented in a PRISMA flow diagram. The final set of included studies underwent structured data extraction using a standardized Excel template. Extracted data included study characteristics, participant demographics, device and treatment parameters, comparators, clinical outcomes, adverse events, follow-up intervals, and authors' conclusions.

Data Items

Primary outcomes included objective and subjective measures of periorbital improvement, such as wrinkle reduction, dermal elasticity, topographic imaging analyses, and global aesthetic improvement scales. Secondary outcomes included treatment tolerability, adverse events including erythema, edema, pain, or burns, patient satisfaction, and the durability of aesthetic results. Studies reporting exclusively histologic changes without clinical correlation were excluded. When effect sizes were not reported in the original studies, these data were calculated from preintervention and postintervention measurements using mean differences. Only articles published in English between January 1, 2015 and November 1, 2025 were considered, with no geographic restrictions, as specified in the protocol.

Study Risk of Bias Assessment

Risk of bias was assessed independently by 2 reviewers using the Cochrane RoB 2.0 tool for randomized trials, the ROBINS-I tool for nonrandomized trials, the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal checklist for quasi-experimental studies, and the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale for observational studies, following the prespecified assessment plan in the protocol. Discrepancies were resolved by consensus by an external researcher.

Sensitivity Analysis

Given the anticipated heterogeneity in device types, energy settings, anatomical targeting depths, and outcome measurement methods, we planned a narrative synthesis as the primary analytical approach. When sufficient homogeneity across comparable outcomes is observed, a meta-analysis using a random-effects model can be planned. Effect sizes were extracted as mean differences, standardized mean differences, risk ratios, or odds ratios with corresponding 95% confidence intervals. Statistical heterogeneity was quantified using the I2 statistic, and sources of heterogeneity were examined through subgroup analyses based on HIFU modality, treatment parameters, and patient characteristics.

Publication bias was assessed qualitatively by examining funding sources, potential conflicts of interest, and evidence of missing studies within the gray literature. Sensitivity analyses were planned by excluding studies with a high risk of bias to evaluate the robustness of findings. Finally, the certainty of the evidence for major outcomes was assessed using the GRADE framework, considering risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias, as outlined in the PROSPERO registration.

RESULTS

A total of 154 records were identified through database searches, including MEDLINE (n = 25), CENTRAL (n = 2), Scopus (n = 70), and Google Scholar (n = 57). After removing 21 duplicate records, 133 records remained for screening. No records were removed by automation tools or for other prescreening reasons. During title and abstract screening, 120 records were excluded. Reasons for exclusion at this stage included: studies not involving adults (≥18 years), use of interventions other than HIFU, populations with systemic or dermatologic conditions affecting facial skin, prior aesthetic procedures in the periorbital area within the restricted timeframe, use of photosensitizing medications, and ineligible study designs such as narrative reviews, editorials, conference abstracts, opinion pieces, animal studies, or publications not reporting outcomes related to periorbital rejuvenation (see Figure 1).

A total of 13 full-text reports were retrieved and assessed for eligibility, none of which required retrieval from alternative channels. Following full-text review, 3 reports were excluded, including 1 report due to wrong intervention, where ultrasound was not the primary treatment; 1 report due to publication type, specifically a letter to the editor; and 1 report due to wrong anatomical site, where the treated facial region was not clearly specified as periorbital.

Ultimately, 10 studies met all eligibility criteria and were included in the systematic review.3,12-19,25. These studies comprised a mix of quasi-experimental trials (pre–post studies without control groups), prospective and retrospective cohort studies (Table 1). All included studies evaluated high-intensity or micro-focused ultrasound modalities applied to the periorbital region in healthy adults and reported at least one predefined outcome. Across the evidence base, studies assessed clinical wrinkle improvement (fine and deep periorbital wrinkles), skin elasticity using Cutometer parameters (R2, R5, R7), skin surface texture through PRIMOS measurements (Ra and Rz), lifting and contour changes via eyebrow height, eyebrow angles, palpebral fissure dimensions, and lid-length reduction, and histologic or imaging-based dermal changes, including collagen and elastin fiber area fractions (Supplemental Table 2). Several studies also reported subjective aesthetic outcomes, such as physician-rated or blinded evaluator improvement and Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale (GAIS) scores (Table 2), as well as patient tolerance and safety, documented through pain scores and adverse event monitoring (Table 2). Comparator conditions included no treatment, baseline intraindividual comparison, and in one split-face study, an active comparator combining HIFU with radiofrequency (Table 3).

Technology

HIFU-based devices were applied with variable focal depths (1.5-6.0 mm), energy settings (0.1-1.2 J), and mostly single-session treatment protocols.

Most studies were conducted in East Asian populations (South Korea, Thailand, and Taiwan) with participants generally presenting mild-to-moderate periorbital wrinkles, eyelid laxity, or upper-face skin laxity. The studies enrolled 10 to 191 participants, with mean ages of approximately 35 to 55 years, and a predominance of female participants (Table 1).

Across studies, a variety of ultrasound platforms were used, including HIFU, micro-focused ultrasound, micro-focused ultrasound with visualization, and high-intensity macro-focused ultrasound devices (Supplemental Table 2). Treatment parameters varied, with focal depths ranging from 1.5 to 6.0 mm and energies from 0.1 to 1.2 J (Table 3). Most interventions were delivered in a single session, although some studies implemented 2 or more sessions, or a 3-session protocol spaced every 2 weeks (Table 3). Randomization was not performed across 9 studies, and 5 studies included evaluator blinding (Table 3). Only 1 trial used an active comparator (HIFU + radiofrequency) on the contralateral facial side (Table 3). Information on periprocedural aspects such as anesthesia, ocular protection, and patient positioning was inconsistently reported across the included studies.

Although similar outcomes were reported across multiple studies, the findings revealed substantial methodological and clinical heterogeneity. Differences in study design, including split-face trials and single-arm studies, as well as variability in device types, energy settings, anatomical targeting depths, outcome measurement approaches, and patient populations, limited the comparability of effect estimates; therefore, results were synthesized narratively rather than statistically pooled.

Efficacy Outcomes

Wrinkle Reduction and Periorbital Rejuvenation

All studies reported measurable improvement in periorbital wrinkles or skin tightening. High-intensity focused ultrasound demonstrated significant aesthetic benefits, with most patients experiencing fair to good clinical response; individuals receiving ≥2 sessions had higher odds of improvement (OR 6.62; 95% CI: 3.24-13.51; P < .001) (Supplemental Table 2).

Studies using Intense Focused Ultrasound showed reductions in both fine and deep wrinkles in the infraorbital and crow's feet regions within 2 to 3 months, accompanied by histologic increases in collagen and elastin fiber fractions. Collagen remodeling increased by 5.95% to 28.22% across dermal layers, while elastin fiber content rose by 18.8% to 33.04% (Supplemental Table 2).

Skin Elasticity and Biomechanical Outcomes

High-intensity focused ultrasound–based treatments consistently improved the biomechanical properties of the periorbital skin. In one pre–post study without control groups, Cutometer R2 (gross elasticity), R5 (net elasticity), and R7 (biological elasticity) showed significant improvements at 10 and 16 weeks, with mean differences up to +0.163, +0.222, and +0.121, respectively (all P < .001) (Supplemental Table 2). Skin roughness (Ra and Rz) also improved substantially, with reductions between −3.5 and −17.8 (P < .001) (Supplemental Table 2).

Lifting Effect and Periorbital Contour

High-intensity focused ultrasound produced progressive eyebrow elevation between 0.095 and 0.151 cm, maintained through 6 months (all P < .001) (Supplemental Table 2). Additional HIFU studies recorded significant improvements at 90 days in eyebrow peak angle (MD +2.25°, P = .0003) and average lid length (MD −1.1 mm; P = .0001) (Supplemental Table 2).

A split-face trial comparing HIFU vs HIFU combined with radiofrequency (RF) found no immediate wrinkle-depth differences but observed superior wrinkle reduction with combination therapy at 4 and 8 weeks (P = .010; P = .008) (Supplemental Table 2).

The Evaluator Rated Aesthetic Improvement Outcomes

Physician-rated improvement was consistent across studies. Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale scores showed progressive enhancement with repeated HIFU sessions, with the best outcome after the third session (GAIS 1.7) (Supplemental Table 2). Blinded evaluations indicated overall improvement in 75% of treated patients (Supplemental Table 2).

Safety and Adverse Events

Ultrasound-based treatments were well tolerated, with most adverse events classified as mild and transient (Table 2). Common reactions included erythema, edema, swelling, pain, and occasional bruising. The largest cohort reported pain and swelling in 49.8% of patients, with rare events including nodules (1%), ectropion (0.5%), and unilateral dacryorrhea (0.5%) (Table 2).

Pain scores were low. One study reported a visual analog scale (VAS) mean score of 3.03 ± 1.57, and another documented pain numerical rating scale (NRS) mean scores of approximately 1 or lower immediately after treatment, decreasing to almost zero within 30 minutes (Table 2). No serious adverse events or treatment-related discontinuations occurred, and loss to follow-up was minimal (Table 2).

Patient Satisfaction and Subjective Improvement

Six studies assessed patient satisfaction and perceived clinical improvement using subjective scales (Table 4). The mean subjective clinical improvement score reported was 3.2 ± 0.79 on a 0 to 4 scale, with 40% of patients rating excellent improvement, 40% good improvement, and 20% moderate improvement. The Subject Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale (SGAIS) scores increased progressively from 0.82 at week 2 to 2.62 at week 16 (P < .001), indicating sustained perceived benefit over time. Another study reported a mean SGAIS score of 3.4 ± 0.6, reflecting high patient satisfaction. In repeated-procedure evaluations, SGAIS scores decreased from 2.6 2 weeks after the first procedure to 1.4 6 weeks after, showing some decline but maintaining positive perception. Overall patient-rated improvement scores on 4-point scales averaged 2.35 ± 0.81, reflecting moderate to high satisfaction and perceived aesthetic enhancement (Table 4). Furthermore, a split-face evaluation using the Facial Line Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire showed scores rising from 2.09 at baseline to 4.73 by Week 16 (P < .001), with a peak of 5.77 at Week 4.

Methodological Quality of the Included Studies

The overall methodological quality of the included studies was limited. Six studies assessed with the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale for cohort designs were rated as poor quality, and only one was rated as good quality, reflecting important shortcomings in participant selection, comparability, and outcome assessment.12,13,16,17,19,25 Among the before-and-after studies without control groups, 3 investigations evaluated using the Quality Assessment Tool for Pre–Post Studies demonstrated fair quality, mainly due to the lack of control groups, small sample sizes, and potential measurement bias.3,14,18 One quasi-experimental study15 assessed with the JBI tool showed a low risk of bias (see Supplemental Table 3).

Certainty of the Evidence

The overall certainty of the evidence for efficacy outcomes was rated as very low, as 9 studies were nonrandomized, frequently lacked control groups, had small sample sizes, short follow-up periods, and exhibited substantial clinical and methodological heterogeneity in device types, energy parameters, focal depths, number of treatment sessions, and outcome assessment methods, these factors prevent a formal meta-analysis and limited the generalizability of findings. In contrast, the certainty of the evidence for safety outcomes was considered low, given that adverse events were consistently mild and transient across studies, with only rare instances of nodules, ectropion, or dacryorrhea and no permanent sequelae. However, nonrandomized designs and potential underreporting still warrant downgrading for risk of bias and publication bias; the consistent safety profile supports greater confidence in the tolerability of periorbital HIFU.

DISCUSSION

This systematic review synthesizes evidence from 10 clinical studies evaluating the effectiveness and safety of HIFU-based technologies for periorbital rejuvenation in adults. Across heterogeneous study designs including quasi-experimental trials, prospective cohorts, and retrospective analyses, consistent clinical improvements were reported in wrinkle severity, dermal elasticity, skin texture, and periorbital contour. These findings reinforce the growing body of evidence that HIFU provides meaningful noninvasive rejuvenation benefits in the delicate periocular region, aligning with observations from narrative and systematic reviews examining broader facial applications of ultrasound-based devices.2,5 However, causal inference remains limited in the absence of randomized controlled trials, since most available evidence derives from observational or uncontrolled pre–post study designs, which are more susceptible to bias and confounding.

Efficacy in Wrinkle Reduction and Dermal Remodeling

All included studies demonstrated reductions in periorbital wrinkle depth and improvements in fine and deep rhytids, consistent with the mechanistic premise of HIFU-induced thermal coagulation points promoting neocollagenesis and neoelastogenesis. Histologic increases in collagen and elastin fiber content observed in intense focused ultrasound studies (ranging from +5.95% to +28.22% for collagen and +18.8% to +33.04% for elastin) support findings from experimental models and clinical histology reported in other reviews. Prior ultrasound histology studies similarly documented collagen fiber thickening and dermal matrix reorganization at 90 days post-treatment,7,10 reinforcing the biological plausibility of the improvements observed in this review.

The magnitude of clinical improvement observed here parallels outcomes from broader facial HIFU studies, which report wrinkle reductions of 20% to 40% depending on energy settings, treatment depth, and patient characteristics.20,21 Notably, the present findings suggest that improvements may be more pronounced when patients undergo ≥2 sessions, consistent with dose–response relationships found in other facial rejuvenation reviews and clinical guidelines.

Skin Elasticity and Texture Outcomes

Improvements in Cutometer-based biomechanical parameters (R2, R5, R7) were consistent across studies and statistically significant at multiple time points. These findings mirror results from previous investigations in nonperiorbital facial areas, where HIFU has been shown to enhance dermal elasticity by 10% to 30% within 12 to 16 weeks. Similarly, reductions in PRIMOS surface roughness (Ra, Rz) ranging from −3.5 to −17.8 are comparable to those observed in systematic evaluations of periocular laser, radiofrequency, and HIFU-based tightening,22 supporting the utility of HIFU for fine-line and texture refinement.

Lifting and Eyebrow-Contour Effects

Across the included studies, HIFU devices demonstrated lifting effects, with eyebrow elevation up to 0.151 cm and improvements in brow peak angle and palpebral fissure dimensions. These findings are important because the periorbital region is highly sensitive to anatomical changes, and even millimetric lifting can have a substantial aesthetic impact. Similar lifting outcomes have been reported in the facial HIFU literature, with improvements in skin laxity and brow elevation among the most reproducible endpoints.14

Nevertheless, the clinical relevance of brow elevation magnitude remains difficult to contextualize. As previously noted, studies in facial aesthetics have shown that modest modifications in brow position may influence the observed appearance of the upper face and perceived rejuvenation. For instance, multimodal aesthetic treatments have reported increases in average eyebrow height of approximately 3.5 mm and maximal elevation of 3.9 mm, while population-based analyses indicate age-related differences of about 1 to 1.4 mm at certain anatomical landmarks such as the medial canthus.23,24 However, due to variability in measurement techniques and anatomical reference points across studies, no standardized threshold currently defines what magnitude of brow elevation constitutes a clinically meaningful improvement. Consequently, the clinical relevance of the relatively modest changes observed in the included studies remains uncertain and warrants further investigation through controlled trials using standardized outcome measures.

Evaluator-Based and Patient-Reported Outcomes

Physician-rated and blinded-evaluator assessments demonstrated meaningful improvements in 75% of patients, with GAIS and SGAIS scores improving progressively over 2 to 16 weeks. These findings align with narrative reviews noting a strong correlation between patient satisfaction and collagen remodeling timelines.5 Interestingly, the observed decline in SGAIS scores following repeated procedures in one study suggests that patient expectations may evolve as treatment progresses, underscoring the need for patient education on realistic outcomes and the temporal dynamics of tissue remodeling.

Mean subjective improvement scores (2.35-3.4 on 4-point scales) and comparable trends observed in studies employing the Facial Line Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire25 are consistent with patient-reported satisfaction in other HIFU studies targeting the lower face and neck, where 70% to 90% of patients report moderate to excellent improvement.9

Safety and Tolerability

Safety outcomes across all 10 studies confirm that HIFU is well tolerated in the periorbital area. Adverse events were mild, transient, and predominantly limited to erythema, swelling, tenderness, and transient pain, with VAS/NRS scores typically ≤3 and resolving quickly. More concerning complications—including nodules (1%), ectropion (0.5%), and unilateral dacryorrhea (0.5%)—were rare and did not result in permanent injury or treatment discontinuation. This aligns with safety profiles documented in HIFU consensus statements, which highlight the importance of real-time visualization to avoid injury to thin periocular tissues.10 Importantly, no serious adverse events were reported, supporting the procedural safety of HIFU in experienced hands.

The safety findings are generally more favorable than those reported for alternative periocular rejuvenation modalities such as fractional laser resurfacing, which carry higher risks of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, prolonged edema, or discomfort, particularly in Fitzpatrick skin types III–V. Given that the majority of participants in the included studies were East Asian, these findings further support the suitability of HIFU for darker phototypes.

Strengths and Limitations

This review is strengthened by its focused scope, adherence to the PRISMA methodology, and inclusion of studies that use objective, validated measurement tools, such as Cutometer elasticity parameters and PRIMOS topography. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. Overall study quality was limited by nonrandomized designs, small sample sizes, short follow-up periods, and lack of standardized treatment parameters. Accordingly, the current evidence base mainly informs the procedural applicability and safety profile of periorbital HIFU, whereas the strength of evidence supporting definitive clinical effectiveness remains constrained by the design limitations of the available studies.

Interpretation of the results is further complicated by substantial heterogeneity across studies. Variability in ultrasound platforms, focal depths, energy parameters, treatment protocols, and outcome assessment methods, including the use of different wrinkle grading scales, biomechanical elasticity metrics, and imaging-based measurements, limits comparability between studies and precludes quantitative meta-analysis. These methodological differences also limit inference regarding the comparative effectiveness of HIFU relative to other energy-based modalities. Moreover, the predominance of East Asian populations among the included studies may restrict the generalizability of these findings to individuals with different periocular anatomical characteristics, skin phototypes, and patterns of aging.

Implications for Clinical Practice and Future Research

The findings of this review support HIFU as a safe and moderately effective option for noninvasive periorbital rejuvenation. Clinicians should tailor treatment parameters based on wrinkle severity, dermal thickness, and anatomical considerations, while setting realistic expectations regarding incremental improvements. Future research should prioritize randomized controlled trials with standardized protocols, extended follow-up, and inclusion of diverse populations. Comparative effectiveness studies evaluating HIFU against microneedling RF, fractional lasers, or combined approaches are warranted to better define optimal treatment pathways for periorbital aging.

CONCLUSIONS

This systematic review demonstrates that HIFU provides measurable benefits for periorbital rejuvenation, including improvements in wrinkle severity, skin elasticity, surface texture, and eyebrow contour. Across studies, HIFU-based interventions consistently yielded favorable clinical and patient-reported outcomes with a generally mild and transient adverse event profile. These findings support the role of HIFU as a minimally invasive, well-tolerated option for patients seeking periocular aesthetic enhancement.

IgG4-related kidney disease presenting as severe renal failure: Diagnostic challenges of a multimodality imaging approach
Authors: Manuela Montatore, Francesco Saverio Guerra, Laura Eusebi, Stefano Santarelli, Valentina Ramazzotti, Roberta Mazzucchelli, Federica Masino, Domenico Mannatrizio, Valentina Testini, Giuseppe Guglielmi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a systemic immune-mediated fibroinflammatory condition that can involve virtually any organ. Renal involvement, referred to as IgG4-related kidney disease (IgG4-RKD), represents an uncommon manifestation and may pose significant diagnostic challenges, particularly when renal dysfunction is the only presenting feature. We report the case of an 80-year-old man who presented with severe renal insufficiency, manifested by markedly elevated serum creatinine levels, in the absence of prior renal disease or significant comorbidities. Imaging evaluation revealed no abnormalities in abdominal organs other than the kidneys. A comprehensive non-contrast multimodality imaging assessment—including ultrasound, computed tomography, and magnetic resonance imaging—was performed due to impaired renal function. Imaging demonstrated multiple bilateral renal parenchymal lesions with features suggestive of an inflammatory process. Histopathological examination of the renal biopsy specimens revealed dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrates with storiform fibrosis, and immunohistochemical analysis confirmed the presence of abundant IgG4-positive plasma cells, establishing the diagnosis of IgG4-related kidney disease (IgG4-RKD). Corticosteroid therapy resulted in progressive improvement and stabilization of renal function. This case highlights the importance of considering IgG4-related kidney disease (IgG4-RKD) in the differential diagnosis of unexplained renal failure, particularly in elderly patients with bilateral renal lesions. Integration of imaging findings with histopathological confirmation is essential for early diagnosis and timely treatment, which may prevent irreversible renal damage.

Introduction

IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a chronic immune-mediated fibroinflammatory disorder characterized by tissue infiltration of IgG4-positive plasma cells, variable degrees of fibrosis, and, in many cases, elevated serum IgG4 levels [1,2]. Histopathological hallmarks include dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrates and storiform fibrosis, whereas obliterative phlebitis is less consistently observed in renal involvement.

IgG4-RD is a multisystem disease that may affect the pancreas, biliary tree, salivary and lacrimal glands, lungs, liver, retroperitoneum, lymph nodes, and kidneys [3]. IgG4-related kidney disease (IgG4-RKD), first described in 2004, represents a relatively uncommon manifestation but is increasingly recognized as a cause of renal dysfunction [4].

Diagnosis relies on an integrated assessment of clinical presentation, laboratory data, imaging findings, and histopathological confirmation. Early recognition is essential, as IgG4-RD often responds favorably to corticosteroid therapy, whereas delayed diagnosis may result in irreversible fibrosis and organ failure [5]. Renal involvement may be particularly challenging to identify, as it can mimic neoplastic or lymphoproliferative disorders and may occur in the absence of extrarenal manifestations.

Although contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) is commonly used in the evaluation of renal disease, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)—especially diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI)—may provide valuable diagnostic information in patients with impaired renal function, allowing lesion characterization without the use of nephrotoxic contrast agents [6].

Case report

An 80-year-old man was admitted to our hospital with progressively worsening fatigue and dyspnea over several weeks. His medical history was unremarkable, with no known hypertension, diabetes mellitus, autoimmune disease, or previous renal pathology. Physical examination was non-contributory.

Laboratory investigations revealed severe renal dysfunction, with a serum creatinine level of 4.8 mg/dL. Blood urea nitrogen was elevated, whereas electrolytes, liver function tests, and complete blood count were within normal limits. Urinalysis showed mild proteinuria without hematuria or cellular casts.

In the absence of an identifiable cause of renal failure, imaging studies were performed to evaluate renal morphology and exclude obstructive, neoplastic, or inflammatory processes. Due to the patient’s impaired renal function, ultrasound, non-contrast CT, and non-contrast MRI were selected.

Renal ultrasound demonstrated multiple bilateral hypoechoic nodular lesions distributed throughout the renal parenchyma, exhibiting poor vascularity on color Doppler examination (Fig. 1). At presentation, no imaging evidence of extrarenal IgG4-RD involvement was detected, representing a diagnostic challenge. Based on the bilateral distribution of renal lesions and imaging characteristics, IgG4-RKD was included in the differential diagnosis, together with lymphoproliferative disorders.

A percutaneous renal biopsy was performed. Histopathological examination revealed tubulointerstitial nephritis with dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrates, extensive tubular destruction, and characteristic storiform fibrosis. The patient was treated with high-dose corticosteroids, resulting in gradual improvement and stabilization of renal function. Serum IgG4 levels were markedly elevated. The patient’s clinical condition improved under continued immunosuppressive therapy.

Discussion

IgG4-related disease is characterized by fibroinflammatory lesions composed of dense IgG4-positive lymphoplasmacytic infiltrates and progressive fibrosis [[7], [8]]. Renal involvement most commonly manifests as tubulointerstitial nephritis, whereas membranous glomerulonephropathy represents the second most frequent renal manifestation [[9], [10], [11]].

Renal IgG4-RKD often presents with minimal or nonspecific symptoms. Common clinical features include unexplained renal dysfunction, elevated serum creatinine levels, mild proteinuria, and, less frequently, hematuria or obstructive uropathy [12]. Serum IgG4 levels are elevated in approximately 80% of patients, although normal levels do not exclude the diagnosis [13].

Imaging plays a crucial role in raising suspicion for IgG4-RKD. Three main renal parenchymal imaging patterns have been described: multiple nodular lesions, a solitary mass-like lesion, and diffuse patchy infiltrative disease, with the multiple nodular pattern being the most common [[13], [14], [15]]. These lesions may mimic renal malignancy or lymphoma, making histopathological confirmation essential.

On MRI, renal IgG4-RKD lesions are typically isointense on T1-weighted images and hyperintense on T2-weighted images, with restricted diffusion on DWI. Diffusion-weighted imaging has shown high sensitivity for detecting renal involvement and is particularly valuable in patients with impaired renal function who cannot undergo contrast-enhanced studies. In our case, T1-weighted imaging was not available due to incomplete MRI acquisition, representing a minor limitation in lesion characterization.

In the present case, isolated renal involvement without extrarenal manifestations contributed to diagnostic uncertainty. This case underscores the importance of considering IgG4-RKD in elderly patients with unexplained renal failure and bilateral renal lesions and highlights the value of a multidisciplinary diagnostic approach.

Conclusion

IgG4-related kidney disease is an uncommon but clinically significant cause of renal dysfunction that may present as isolated renal involvement. Recognition of characteristic imaging patterns, particularly on diffusion-weighted MRI, can raise diagnostic suspicion when contrast-enhanced imaging is contraindicated. However, definitive diagnosis requires histopathological confirmation. Early identification and prompt initiation of immunosuppressive therapy are essential to prevent irreversible renal damage and improve patient outcomes.

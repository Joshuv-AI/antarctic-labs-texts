The Monadic Grzegorczyk Logic
G. Bezhanishvili, M. Khan

Abstract
. We develop a semantic criterion for determining whether a given monadic modal logic axiomatizes the one-variable fragment of a predicate modal logic. We show that the criterion applies to the monadic Grzegorczyk logic MGrz , thus establishing that MGrz axiomatizes the one-variable fragment of the predicate Grzegorczyk logic QGrz . This we do by proving the finite model property of MGrz , which is achieved by strengthening the notion of a maximal point of a descriptive MGrz -frame and by refining the existing selective filtration methods.

Introduction

The Grzegorczyk logic
Grz
plays a fundamental role in the study of the Gödel translation. Indeed, it is the largest modal companion of
the intuitionistic propositional calculus
IPC
, and the lattice of extensions of
IPC
is isomorphic to the lattice of normal extensions of
Grz
(see, e.g.,
[
9
, Sec. 9.6]
).

Our understanding of the Gödel translation in the predicate case is much more limited. One reason for this is the lack of adequate semantics for predicate logics. Indeed, predicate modal logics are rarely Kripke complete
[
17
]
, and we also have frequent incompleteness with respect to the more general Kripke bundle semantics
[
23
,
29
]
. For example, letting
QL
denote the predicate extension of a normal modal logic
L
, we have that
QS4
is Kripke complete (see, e.g.,
[
21
, Thm. 15.3]
), while

QGrz
is not even Kripke bundle complete
[
23
,
29
]
.

It is known
[
35
]
(see also
[
16
, p. 156]
) that
QS4
is a modal companion of the intuitionistic predicate calculus
IQC
. There have been claims in the literature that
QGrz
is also a modal companion of
IQC

[
33
]
, but that it is not the largest such
[
30
]
. However, the proofs rely on the Flagg-Friedman translation
[
14
]
, which turned out not to be faithful
[
22
]
. Thus, these claims need to be reexamined (see
[
16
, Rem. 2.11.13]
and
[
3
]
).

The difficulties with predicate logics can often be avoided by restricting to fragments with one fixed variable, which we refer to as
monadic fragments
. On the one hand, such fragments minimally capture the complexity of modal quantification. On the other, they can be viewed as propositional modal logics with two modal operators (the second being an
S5
-modality capturing the monadic quantification), and hence are amenable to the standard semantic tools of modal logic.

The study of the monadic fragment of the classical predicate calculus was initiated by Hilbert and Ackermann
[
20
]
. Wajsberg
[
40
]
showed that this fragment is axiomatized by
the modal logic
S5
. Prior
[
34
]
introduced the monadic intuitionistic propositional calculus

, and Bull
[
8
]
proved that it axiomatizes the monadic fragment of
IQC
. For further results in this direction see

[
32
,
31
,
39
,
4
]
.
Monadic fragments of predicate modal logics were studied in
[
13
,
11
,
24
,
25
,
1
]
.
In particular, Fischer Servi
[
13
]
introduced
MS4
(monadic

) and showed that the Gödel translation embeds
MIPC
into
MS4
faithfully, while Esakia
[
11
]
introduced
MGrz
(monadic

) and proved the same for
MGrz
. In the same paper, Esakia introduced

(monadic Gödel-Löb logic),
and Japaridze
[
24
,
25
]
proved that Solovay’s embedding
[
38
]
of

into Peano Arithmetic extends to

, but the same question for

Using predicate Kripke frames, Ono and Suzuki
[
31
]
developed a semantic criterion to determine when a given monadic intuitionistic logic axiomatizes the monadic fragment of a given predicate intuitionistic logic. This was further generalized by Suzuki
[
39
]
to Kripke bundles. We generalize this criterion to the setting of monadic modal logics (see

,
and likewise that

is the monadic fragment of

(see

(see
[
11

This result, in conjunction with our semantic criterion in

is Kripke bundle complete, in spite of the fact that the full predicate logic

is not.

To establish our main result, we use a modified form of selective filtration—a standard technique in modal logic for proving completeness and decidability. For instance, selective filtration can be used to show that

Let

Monadic modal logic

Let

. We use

[

Definition 3.5
.

Let

5.
Correspondence between mm-logics and pm-logics

There is a close connection between mm-logics and pm-logics. Indeed, in many cases, mm-logics axiomatize the monadic fragment of the corresponding pm-logics; see, for example,
[
13
,
11
,
1
]
.
The correspondence we discuss here extends a similar correspondence between monadic and predicate intuitionistic logics
[
34
,
8
,
32
,
31
,
39
,
4
]
.
To make this more precise,
we define a translation of formulae of
\mathcal{L}_{\exists}
to formulae of

\mathcal{L}_{Q}
as follows. Fix an individual variable
x
. Following
[
32
]
,
with each propositional letter
p
, we associate a unique monadic predicate
p^{*}
, and
define the translation
(-)^{t}\colon{\text{Form}}(\mathcal{L}_{\exists})\to{\text{Form}}(\mathcal{L}_{Q})
recursively as follows:

(1)

p^{t}=p^{*}(x)
;

(2)

(\varphi\vee\psi)^{t}=\varphi^{t}\vee\psi^{t}
;

(3)

(\neg\varphi)^{t}=\neg\varphi^{t}
;

(4)

(\Diamond\varphi)^{t}=\Diamond\varphi^{t}
;

(5)

(\exists\varphi)^{t}=\exists x\varphi^{t}
.

Note that for any
\mathcal{L}_{\exists}
-formula
\varphi
,
x
is the only variable that can occur freely in
\varphi^{t}
. Therefore,
(\exists\varphi)^{t}
is always a sentence.
Since
\varphi^{t}
may only have one free variable, interpreting formulae of the form
(\Diamond\varphi)^{t}[a/x]
requires no additional care with inheritors (see
Remark

2.7
).

As is customary, we identify mm-logics and pm-logics with their deductively closed sets of theorems. For a logic
L
and a formula
\varphi
, we write
\textbf{L}\vdash\varphi
to mean that
\varphi
is a theorem of
\mathbf{L}
.

Definition 5.1
.

Given an mm-logic
M
and a pm-logic
Q
, we say that
M
is the
monadic fragment
of
Q
provided for each
\mathcal{L}_{\exists}
-formula
\varphi
,

\textbf{M}\vdash\varphi\iff\textbf{Q}\vdash\varphi^{t}.

For an
MK
-frame
\mathfrak{F}=(X,R,E)
, recall that
\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{F})=((X,R),\pi_{X},(X_{0},R_{0}))
, where
\pi_{X}\colon X\twoheadrightarrow X_{0}
is the quotient map
\pi_{X}(x)=[x]
(see the proof of
Proposition

4.3
). Throughout this section, we simplify the notation and drop the subscript from
\pi_{X}
.

Lemma 5.2

Let
\mathfrak{F}=(X,R,E)
be an

(1)

(2)

of

. By (

.

The proof is by induction on the length of the proof of

A rooted poset

is finite iff it has finite depth and finite branching at each point.

Proof.

The forward implication is obvious. For the converse, suppose

has depth

has depth 1.
Now suppose

is finite and that
(C_{k},\leq)
has depth

Conclusions

The main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:

(1)

We proved that
the categories of Kripke bundles and
MK
-frames are equivalent, thus generalizing the results of
[
39
]
and
[
5
]
.

(2)

We provided a criterion to determine when a given monadic modal logic is the monadic fragment of a given predicate modal logic, thus generalizing the results of
[
31
,
39
]
. This, in particular, yields a criterion for when the monadic extension of a propositional modal logic
\bf L
is the monadic fragment of the predicate extension of
\bf L
.

(3)

We refined the selective filtration methods of

[
19
]
and
[
1
]
to show that
MGrz
has the fmp, thus resolving in the positive the issue of completeness of
MGrz
(see
[
11
]
). The key new ingredient is the notion of
a strongly maximal point of
a descriptive
MGrz
-frame, and an adaptation of the Fine-Esakia maximality principle.
As a consequence, we proved that
MGrz

is the monadic fragment of
QGrz
.

We point out that our construction, in particular, implies that the Grzegorczyk logic enriched with the universal modality also has the fmp. For a propositional modal logic
L
, we let

\textbf{L}_{u}=\textbf{ML}+\Diamond q\to\exists q.

We refer to
\textbf{L}_{u}
as “the modal logic
L
enriched with the universal modality." A detailed study of such logics was initiated in
[
18
]
, where it was shown (among other things) that if
L
admits filtration, then so does
\textbf{L}_{u}
.
Since
Grz
does not admit standard filtration, the above result does not immediately yield that
\textbf{Grz}_{u}
has the fmp. However, a simplified version of our construction does yield that
\textbf{Grz}_{u}
has the fmp.

Recall that an
MGrz
-frame
(X,R,E)
is a
\textbf{Grz}_{u}
-frame iff
x\mathrel{R}y\implies x\mathrel{E}y
for each
x,y\in X

(X,R,E)
is rooted iff

. Thus, our construction in
Section

simplifies considerably since the selection needs to be performed only within one

-cluster. Consequently, we obtain:

Corollary 8.1
.

\textbf{Grz}_{u}
has the fmp.

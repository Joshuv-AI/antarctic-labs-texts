676. New translational markers and evidence-based treatments for problematic usage of the Internet
Authors: J Solly, S Chamberlain, K Ioannidis, S Antons, H-J Rumpf, S Achab, M Brand, N Fineberg
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundProblematic Usage of the Internet (PUI) is an umbrella term for behaviours manifesting online which cause distress or functional impairment. Of these, gambling and gaming disorders are currently recognised in diagnostic manuals and NICE recently published a guideline for the treatment of gambling that harms. There is a growing body of evidence related to the biological and cognitive features of gambling and gaming disorders, which could inform new treatment approaches.Aims & ObjectivesTo survey neuroimaging and cognitive markers of gambling and gaming disorders and discuss possible future treatment approaches for PUI informed by these mechanistic insights.MethodWe discuss the findings of recent meta-analyses and review papers.ResultsNeuroimaging and cognitive studies in gambling and gaming disorders demonstrate functional and structural differences (vs. controls) in prefrontal and striatal areas, alongside impaired inhibitory control. Current findings support the conceptualisation of these disorders as compulsive-impulsive-addiction spectrum conditions. The evidence regarding the mechanistic basis of the other forms of PUI (which are not yet recognised in diagnostic manuals) is smaller but growing.Discussion & ConclusionsThe largest body of evidence for interventions to treat PUI relates to psychotherapy. Cognitive and imaging data suggest that it would be valuable to conduct well-powered clinical trials to explore pharmacological and neurostimulation approaches which target impulse control and/or activity of the fronto-striatal loops. Further research is required to determine the mechanistic differences between different forms of PUI. Looking to the future, trials considering combined treatments or stepped care approaches will be essential to inform high-quality guidelines and service implementation.

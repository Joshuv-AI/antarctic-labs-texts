More Stability and Convergence results for higher-order Wiener-Wintner systems
Jacob Folks

Abstract
. Higher-order Wiener-Wintner averages were constructed by Assani, Folks, and Moore [ 6 ] to quantitatively control multiple recurrence averages. Systems in which these averages converge at a polynomial rate for a sufficiently large subset are termed higher-order Wiener-Wintner systems of power type , in which properties like pointwise convergence of multiple recurrence averages and multiple return times averages has been shown. We establish that these higher-order Wiener-Wintner averages satisfy a type of sublinearity, and that they bound conditional expectations and products, which transfers to improved stability results of higher-order Wiener-Wintner systems under sums, factors, and products. We also establish more general convergence results for such systems, which include a polynomial return times theorem and convergence of the multilinear one-side ergodic Hilbert transform with polynomial phase.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

For a dynamical system
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
and
J\in\mathbb{N}
, the
J
th order multiple recurrence averages
have the form

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{jn}

for
f_{1},\dots,f_{J}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. These averages first appeared in Furstenberg’s proof of Szemerédi’s theorem
[
15
]
and their convergence properties have been long since studied. Notably, norm convergence in
L^{2}
was established independently by Host and Kra
[
17
]
and Ziegler
[
24
]
. Pointwise convergence results have been established for some specific cases, including some weakly mixing systems by Assani
[
1
]
and distal systems by Huang, Shao, and Ye
[
18
]
.

In his landmark work
[
11
]
, Bourgain established pointwise converges for the
J=2
, or double recurrence case

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{1}\circ T^{n}\cdot f_{2}\circ T^{2n}\,.

He does so, in part, by tying the above average double recurrence average to the weighted averages

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\,.

The classical
Wiener-Wintner Theorem

[
23
]
states that for every
f\in L^{1}(\mu)
, there exists a set
X_{f}
of full measure over which the above converges for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, the
Uniform Wiener-Wintner theorem
(also proved by Bourgain in
[
11
]
) states that uniform convergence of these averages over
t
characterizes the Kronecker factor
\mathcal{K}
(the closed span of the eigenfunctions), in that

f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{K})^{\perp}\quad\iff\quad\lim_{N}\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f(T^{n}x)\right|=0\text{ for almost all }x\,.

For the double recurrence averages (
2
), the case in which either
f_{1}
or
f_{2}
is in
L^{2}(\mathcal{K})
reduces to the classical Wiener-Wintner theorem. For the remaining case, Bourgain shows that the norm behavior of (
2
) is controlled by the norm of the uniform Wiener-Wintner averages of either
f_{1}
or
f_{2}
, and hence converge to zero for
f_{1}
or
f_{2}
in
L^{2}(\mathcal{K})^{\perp}
. Tools from harmonic analysis are then used to pass from norm to pointwise convergence, which comprise the bulk of Bourgain’s paper.

This portion of the argument can be greatly simplified, as shown by Assani
[
3
]
, under the added condition that the uniform Wiener-Winter averages converge polynomially in norm, or for some
\alpha>0
and a dense set of
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{K})^{\perp}
there exists constants
C_{f}
such that

\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{C_{f}}{N^{\alpha}}

holds for all
N
. Such a system is called a
Wiener-Winter system of power type
\alpha>0
(we note that for the dense set, the powers
\alpha
do not need to be uniform for the convergence to extend by density). Moreover, examples and stability conditions of such systems are established, and further convergence results such as the one-sided ergodic Hilbert transform are shown
[
2
]
.

In
[
6
]
, Assani, Folks, and Moore inductively lift this line of reasoning to higher-order multiple recurrence averages. The norm convergence arguments of Host/Kra and Ziegler establish the characteristic factors
\mathcal{Z}_{J-1}
of the multiple recurrence averages (
1
) (known as
Host-Kra-Ziegler factors
) which have the structure of a
J
-step nilsystem inside which pointwise convergence of multiple recurrence averages has been shown by Leibman
[
21
]
. Assani, Folks, and Moore
[
6
]
construct a
J
-th order Wiener-Wintner average
(here denoted
W_{N}^{J}(f)
) whose pointwise limit characterizes
\mathcal{Z}_{J-1}
and controls the multiple recurrence averages in norm analogously to the classical Wiener-Wintner average in the Bourgain argument. With the added condition that these higher-order Wiener-Wintner averages converge to zero polynomially for a dense subset of
L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{J-1})^{\perp}
, we obtain pointwise convergence of multiple recurrence, and also a multiterm returns times theorem (a survey of such return times theorems in given by Assani and Presser
[
10
]
). Such a system is called a
J
-th order Wiener-Wintner dynamical system
, and examples are given which dip outside those covered by the previous convergence results for multiple recurrence averages
[
6
]

(The construction of these averages and the exact bounds they satisfy are discussed in detail in the following section §
2
). This example arises as the result of a stability result, in that the product of a higher-order Wiener-Wintner system with a K automorphism is a higher order Wiener-Wintner system.

These higher order Wiener-Wintner averages introduce technical complications not present in the first-order case. Notably, the averages are no longer sublinear, and do not immediately form a vector space. This problem is overcome, in certain cases, by Assani, Folks, and Moore
[
6
]

by proving a stronger condition, termed “multilinearity concerns”. While this condition is straightforward to verify in specific examples, it makes more general stability results more challenging. Notably, while it is easy to verify that higher-order Wiener-Wintner systems are preserved under isomorphisms, it does not obviously follow from the definitions that higher-order Wiener-Wintner systems are preserved even under factors.

1.2.
Goals and Overview

In this paper, we wish to establish stability properties of the higher-order Wiener-Wintner averages under sums, factors, and products, and establish convergence results for the one-sided multilinear ergodic Hilbert transform for higher-order Wiener-Wintner functions. We do so in part by establishing an equivalence between the decay rates of the higher order Wiener-Winter average
W_{N}^{J}(f)
and the
uniform multiple recurrence average
, denoted by

\displaystyle M_{N}^{J}(f):=\sup_{\overset{g_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)}{\max_{j=1,\dots,J}\|g_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 1}}\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{J}g_{j}\circ T^{jn}\cdot f\circ T^{(J+1)n}\right\|_{2}\,.

The “Bourgain bound” from
[
6
]
establishes half of this equivalence, and the other is shown here. Passing back and forth between
W_{N}^{J}(f)
and
M_{N}^{J}(f)
, we will be able to show bounds that establish the desired stability properties. We note that since most methods used are primarily combinatorial, the constants obtained are “universal”, in that they never pick up any dependence on the dynamical system itself, as seen in the Bourgain bound.

We detail the exact construction of the higher-order Wiener-Wintner averages and the bounds that they satisfy on multiple recurrence in section §
2.2
, also providing some updated notation. We also provide precise definitions for Higher-order Wiener-Wintner functions and systems in §
2.3
. We introduce a special asymptotic notation
f\precsim g
. to make rigorous our desired notion of equivalence between decay rates.

We begin §
3
by establishing the equivalence between the averages
W_{N}^{J}(f)
and
M_{N}^{J}(f)
through what is termed a “reverse Bourgain bound” (Theorem
3.1
). This lets us immediately conclude a sublinearity property in section §
3.2
. We show in §
3.3
that the multiple recurrence averages
M_{N}^{J}(f)
further bound the “off-diagonal” Wiener-Wintner averages, as described by the Multilinearity concerns of
[
6
]
. By our previous equivalence, this shows that
W_{N}^{J}(f)
controls its own off-diagonal terms, and the multilinearity concerns are always satisfied for a weaker power. In section §
3.4
, we show that the higher-order Wiener-Wintner averages of a conditional expectation form one of these off-diagonal averages, and are hence controlled by
W_{N}^{J}(f)
. We are immediately able to conclude that a factor of a higher-order Wiener-Wintner system is itself a higher-order Wiener-Wintner system.

We introduce a corresponding notion of “higher-order weak Wiener-Wintner averages” in §
3.5
, which we show bounds the “strong” average at the cost of order (Theorem
3.10
). Since these weak averages interact well with products, we raise this argument inductively and are able to bound
W_{N}^{J}(f\otimes g)
by the individual averages, at the cost of raising the order. This lets us extend the
J
-th order Wiener-Wintner stability result under products with a K automorphism
[
6
]
to stability under products with any weakly mixing
J+1
-th order Wiener-Wintner system. We also show that the equivalence between
W_{N}^{J}(f)
and
M_{N}^{J}(f)
is also inherited by many alternative constructions of the Wiener-Wintner averages. We are able to use this in §
3.6
to answer a natural question about the construction of
W_{N}^{J}(f)
, showing that most other constructions are equivalent.

In §
4
, we show more convergence results. We start by showing that the averages
W_{N}^{k+J-1}(f)
control a polynomial Wiener-Wintner multiple recurrence average of the form

(4)

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|\right\|_{2}

where
p_{k,t}=t_{1}n+t_{2}n^{2}+\dots+t_{k}n^{k}
, naturally generalizing the arguments from
[
6
]
and
[
8
]
. This immediately lets us conclude a polynomial return times theorem for multiple recurrence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}g(S^{P(n)}y)\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)

as shown in §
4.1
. Following this, we use polynomial decay to deduce in §
4.2
that all convergence results thus far established for higher-order WW systems have corresponding ergodic Hilbert transform analogues; for example, polynomial control on (
4
) leads to almost everywhere convergence of the averages

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi i\left(t_{1}n+t_{2}n^{2}+\dots+t_{k}n^{k}\right)}}{n^{\sigma}}\,\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)

for some
\sigma<1
to a continuous function in
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}
, and that the multilinear multiterm return times theorem of the form

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{K}g_{k}(S^{kn}y)\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{jn}x)}{n^{\sigma}}

converges almost everywhere. Such results do not readily extend by density, but they illustrate that higher-order Wiener-Wintner systems are a class in which convergence holds on a dense subset of
L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{k+J-1})^{\perp}
. Moreover, since there always exists a dense set of higher-order Wiener-Wintner functions of any order in the orthogonal complement of the Pinsker algebra of any system
[
6
]
, this established that these return times Hilbert transform results always hold on a dense subset of
L^{2}(\mathcal{P})^{\perp}
.

Finally, the appendix
A
contains a full proof of the reverse Bourgain bound established in §
3.1
.

Notation and conventions

(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
will denote
(measure-preserving) dynamical systems
or simply
systems
consisting of a probability space
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu)
and measurable map
T:X\to T
satisfying
\mu(T^{-1}A)=\mu(A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}
. Such a system is

if for all
A\in\mathcal{F}
, we have
T^{-1}A=A
implies that
\mu(A)=0
or
1
. Moreover, for any
p\in[1,\infty]
, the
L^{p}
norm will be denoted
\|\cdot\|_{p}
or
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}
if we wish to indicate the particular measure for clarification. If
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{F}
is a sub
\sigma
-algebra, we may use

to denote the collection of all
\mathcal{B}
-measureable functions in
L^{p}(\mu)
. Powers of any map

will denote composition of the map
T
with itself
n
times.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we let

. For any

, we denote
V_{k}
to be the set
\{0,1\}^{k}
. For any
\eta\in V_{k}
, we let
|\eta|
count the number of

’s appearing. Likewise, for any

, we may let

denote the usual componentwise dot product
\sum_{n=1}^{k}h_{k}\eta_{k}
. We may let
c:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
denote complex conjugation, such that
c^{n}z
is equal to
z
for even

and
\overline{z}
for odd

, and we denote the real component of

by
\Re(z)
. We also make use of the floor function

to be the greatest integer below a given input. Cardinality of a set

is denoted by

.

Following standard notation, we use subscripts to denote the dependence of certain constants. However, in many cases a constant

of variable length. In such cases, we use an indexless subscript

’s, but such dependence is not explicitly denoted. In the case that the constant

Finally, we will abbreviate “Wiener-Wintner” as “WW”.

2.2.
Bourgain bounds and Higher-order Wiener-Wintner (WW) averages

In his proof of pointwise convergence for double recurrence
[
11
]
, Bourgain implicity uses a bound of the following form on any bounded functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
:

(5)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{1}\circ T^{n}\cdot f_{2}\circ T^{2n}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f_{1}\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{1}^{2/3}

holds for some constant
C
and sufficiently large
N
. This bound is made explicit, in the above form, by Assani
[
3
]
. As previously stated, this bound lets us immediately conclude norm convergence for double recurrence, and Bourgain uses approaches from harmonic analysis to establish pointwise convergence.

In
[
6
]
, this bound is inductively extended to higher-order recurrence. To begin, for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we denote the
first-order WW average of
f
to be

\displaystyle W^{1}_{N}(f)

\displaystyle=\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}\,.

By using the ideas from the proof of (
5
), this bound is modified to hold for all
N
and remove dependence on
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
. Specifically, it is shown that for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, distinct and nonzero, there exists a constant
C_{a,1}
such that for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
with
\max_{j=1,2}\|f_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{1}\circ T^{a_{1}n}\cdot f_{2}\circ T^{a_{2}n}\right\|_{2}\leq C_{a,1}\left(\frac{1}{N}+\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{1}^{2/3}\right)\,.

The choice of the subscript
1
for
C_{a,1}
is done to fit the later convention given by the estimate (
7
) below. We remark, as indicated in the previous section
2.1
, that this constant
C_{a,1}
depends on both
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
. An exact value of this is worked out in the appendix of
[
6
]
, and it grows polynomially in
|a_{1}|
and
|a_{2}|
. Of particular interest, this constant does not depend on the dynamical system itself.

Since we are working on a probability space, we can bound
L^{1}
norms by
L^{2}
, and write the above as

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{1}\circ T^{a_{1}n}\cdot f_{2}\circ T^{a_{2}n}\right\|_{2}\leq C_{a,1}\left(\frac{1}{N}+W^{1}_{N}(f_{1})\right)

Because of the asymmetry in norms (
L^{2}
on the left and
L^{1}
on the right), we could have balanced the estimate above in another way: by lowering the left hand average to
L^{1}
. Throughout this paper, unless otherwise noted, we take
W_{N}^{1}
to be defined as above with
L^{2}
norms and phrase all bounds in terms of
L^{2}
. However, in most estimates the
2
-norms can be replaced with
1
-norms at no extra cost.

For
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we inductively define the
k
-th order WW average of
f
to satisfy

(6)

\displaystyle W^{k}_{N}(f)=\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}W^{k-1}_{N}\left(f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}\right)\,.

It follows inductively

\displaystyle W^{k}_{N}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}W^{1}_{N}\left(\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}f\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}f\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right]\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}

using some of the previously established notation and conventions.

It is shown that these averages satisfy a
k
-th order Bourgain bound
: that for any integers
a_{1},\dots,a_{k+1}
, all distinct and nonzero, there exists a constant
C_{a,k}>0
and
N_{a,k}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for all

f_{1},\dots,f_{k+1}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
with
\max_{j=1,\dots,k+1}\|f_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, we have

(7)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right\|_{2}\leq C_{a,k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/2^{k}}}+\left[W_{N}^{k}(f_{1})\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}\right)

for all
N\geq N_{a,k}
. An analogous bound holds if the 2-norms are replaced with 1-norms. As remarked previously, this constant
C_{a,k}
only depends on all of
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
, and
k
itself. Based on the argumentation of
[
6
]
, we see that this constant depends exponentially in
k
, and for a fixed
k
grows polynomially in each
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
.

As in the lower order case, it is shown
[
6
]
that the averages
W_{N}^{k}
distinguish in limit the correct characteristic factor for multiple recurrence. The
Host-Kra-Ziegler factors
, denoted
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
, can be defined using the following inductive
Gowers-Host-Kra seminorm
construction:

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\VERT f\VERT_{2}^{4}&=\lim_{H}\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left|\int f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}\,d\mu\right|^{2}\\
\VERT f\VERT_{3}^{8}&=\lim_{H}\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\VERT f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}\right\VERT_{2}^{4}\\
\VERT f\VERT_{k}^{2^{k}}&=\lim_{H}\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\VERT f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}\right\VERT_{k-1}^{2^{k-1}}\end{split}

such that
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{k})^{\perp}
if and only if
\VERT f\VERT_{k+1}=0
. In
[
3
]
, it is shown that there exists constants
C_{k}
such that

(9)

\displaystyle\limsup_{N}W_{N}^{k}(f)\leq C_{k}\VERT f\VERT_{k+1}^{2/3}

and that a bounded function
f
has
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{k})^{\perp}
if and only if the pointwise Wiener-Wintner average (
W_{N}^{k}(f)
with the integrals removed and
h
sums extended to
N
) converges to zero for almost all
x\in X
.

Remark 2.1
.

We note that this seminorm construction only works in the case that
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
is ergodic. However, the proof of the Bourgain bound (
7
) is primarily combinatorial, and does not require ergodicity. Hence, in the non-ergodic case, the averages
W_{N}^{k}(f)
are still defined and still bound multiple recurrence in norm, but the limiting behavior of
W_{N}^{k}(f)
cannot be analyzed by the seminorms above.

Throughout this article, we remark that almost every bound does not require ergodicity. It is only those that directly reference the Gowers-Host-Kra seminorms, such as (
9
), that may fail to hold if we no longer require the system to be ergodic. Such results are only interested in the limiting behavior of the WW averages, and for a fixed value of
N
all of the bounds shown in §
3
and §
4
hold regardless of whether the system is ergodic.

2.3.
Higher-order Wiener-Wintner (WW) functions and systems

On the space of functions
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
, for
\alpha>0
denote the following subset by

\displaystyle\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N},\alpha)

\displaystyle=\{r:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}\,:\,\exists\,C>0\text{ such that }r(N)\leq N^{-\alpha}\text{ for all }N\}

\displaystyle=\{r:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}\,:\,r=O(N^{-\alpha})\}\,.

We denote the subset of
functions of polynomial decay
as

\displaystyle\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N})

\displaystyle=\{r:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}\,:\,\exists\,\alpha>0,C>0\text{ such that }r(N)\leq N^{-\alpha}\text{ for all }N\}

\displaystyle=\{r:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}\,:\,\exists\,\alpha>0\text{ such that }r=O(N^{-\alpha})\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\alpha>0}\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N},\alpha)\,.

Classically, good decay rates may provide summability which in certain cases can extend norm convergence to pointwise convergence. This was used by Assani
[
3
]
to prove pointwise convergence for double recurrence such that one function had polynomial decay on its WW average, termed a
Wiener-Wintner function of power type
.

Following
[
6
]
, we say that
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
is a
k
-th order WW function of power type
\alpha
if
W_{N}^{k}(f)\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N},\alpha)
, and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
is a
k
-th order WW function of power type
if
W_{N}^{k}(f)\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N})
. For such functions, pointwise convergence of multiple recurrence averages follows by the same summability argument. Hence we say that an ergodic dynamical system
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
is a
k
-th order WW system of power type
\alpha
if there exists a dense subset of
k
-th order WW functions of power type
\alpha
inside
L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{k})^{\perp}
. Similarly, we define a
k
-th order WW system of power type
such that the WW functions of power type are dense in
L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{k})^{\perp}
. In such systems, pointwise convergence of multiple recurrence and multiple return times convergence theorems are established by a classical summation argument.

Following the remark
2.1
, we note that the definition of WW
functions
does not require the system to be ergodic. The definition of WW
systems
, however, is based on the seminorm construction of Host-Kra-Ziegler factors, and does require ergodicity. This is of particular importance for some later results concerning product systems; while such systems may not be ergodic, it is still well phrased to consider WW functions.

2.4.
Special asymptotic notation

Since many results throughout this paper focus on the transfer of decay rates between certain averages, we make use of the following notation for simplification. For functions
f,g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
, we say that
f\precsim g
if there exists constants
0<\alpha,\beta,\gamma\leq 1
and
C>0
and
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, and a nondecreasing function
\phi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
satisfying
\phi(N)\geq N^{\beta}
such that

(10)

\displaystyle f(N)\leq C\left(\frac{1}{N^{\alpha}}+g\Big(\phi(N)\Big)^{\gamma}\right)

for all
N>N_{0}
. Following standard convention, we also use
f(N)\precsim_{a,b,\dots}g(N)
to denote that the constants depend on variables
a,b,\dots
, and we use
f\approx g
to denote that
f\precsim g
and
g\precsim f
.

While similar in purpose to the standard asymptotic notation
f\lesssim g
, the constants
\alpha,\beta
and
\gamma
in
f\precsim g
generalize the scope considerably and create a weaker notion of comparison. However, in the case that
g
tends to zero, it follows that any
f
with
f\precsim g
will also tends to zero at “approximately” the same rate as
g
, up to scaling by powers on the inside and outside of
g
and a polynomial remainder term. This is exactly the relationship that arose in the Bourgain bound (
7
), which can be written in this notation as

\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right\|_{2}\precsim_{a,k}W_{N}^{k}(f_{1})\,.

Notably, we see that the flexibility afforded by the inclusion of the function
\phi
in the formula (
10
) is not needed to write the Bourgain bound, as
\phi(N)=N
suffices in the above statement. Later bounds will have the same form, but with polynomial scaling on the inside, such as the following:

f(N)\leq C\left(\frac{1}{N^{\alpha}}+g\left(\lfloor N^{\beta}\rfloor\right)^{\gamma}\right)

The notation
f\precsim g
is defined by (
10
) rather than the above because the above relation is not transitive, as the nested floor functions do not necessarily simplify. Throughout all following results, the function
\phi
can be thought of as behaving like
\lfloor N^{\beta}\rfloor
for some
0<\beta\leq 1
; the inclusion of the general function
\phi
in the definition of
f\precsim g
is specifically to ensure that the relation is transitive.

Moreover, if
f(N)=O(N^{-\alpha})
for some
\alpha>0
, it follows that
f\precsim 0
. Since all positive functions satisfy
0\precsim f
, we may denote functions with polynomial decay as
f\approx 0
. Hence,
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
is a Wiener-Wintner function of power type if and only if
W_{N}^{k}(f)\approx 0
.

While this notation greatly simplifies the kinds of asymptotic comparison that will arise throughout the paper, it is complicated in practice and lacks some standard desirable properties. Specifically, the relation
f\precsim g
is not additive, as the functions
\phi
may misalign the comparison. However, we note in the case that
f\precsim 0
and
g\precsim 0
, it does follow that
f+g\precsim 0
. Since we will be almost exclusively interested in the transfer of polynomial decay of functions
f\precsim 0
, the relation
f\precsim g
behaves well enough in most every case that we use it. Because of its technical limitations, we remark that we primarily use this notation to simplify the statement of results rather than to simplify proofs, and in all cases we will include the precise statements of the estimates proved. However, using this notation informally can cleanly explain the structure of many of the following arguments.

2.5.
Tools

Here, we collect some essential estimates. Primarily, we make extensive use of the classical

Van der Corput inequality
(cf.
[
20
]
), under the following statement:

Lemma 2.2

(Van der Corput’s estimate)
.

Let
\{v_{n}\}
be a sequence of complex numbers. For any integers
1\leq H\leq N
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}v_{n}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{N+H}{N^{2}(H+1)}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|v_{n}|^{2}+\frac{2(N+H)}{N^{2}(H+1)^{2}}\sum_{h=1}^{H}(H+1-h)\real\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N-h-1}\overline{v_{n+h}}v_{n}\right).

In the case that
v_{n}=u_{n}e^{2\pi int}
for some sequence of complex numbers
\{u_{n}\}
, the rotating weigh
e^{2\pi int}
is lost under the application of the Van der Corput identity. Hence, we may take a supremum to achieve the following variation: For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq H\leq N-1
, we have

(11)

\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}u_{n}e^{2\pi int}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{N(H+1)}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|u_{n}|^{2}+\frac{4}{H+1}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-h-1}\overline{u_{n+h}}u_{n}\right|.

A straightforward but useful computation (which mirrors the
H=N
case of the Van der Corput inequality in the context of dynamical systems) is presented here as a lemma:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
be a dynamical system, and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
have
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. Then for all
N
, we have

\displaystyle\int\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f\circ T^{n}\right|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left(\frac{N-n}{N}\right)\Re\left[\int f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n}}\,d\mu\right]\,.

Proof.

Factoring out, we observe

\displaystyle\int\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f\circ T^{n}\right|^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}f\circ T^{n}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{m}}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}\int f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{m-n}}\,d\mu\,.

Notice that the term
m-n
ranges between
1-N
and
N-1
. Moreover, by measure translation the terms at
m-n
and
n-m
are conjugate to each other. Grouping these terms together, we obtain twice of the real component and may restrict the sum to values such that
m-n\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{N^{2}}\sum_{m\geq n=1}^{N}\Re\left[\int f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{m-n}}\,d\mu\right]\,.

Notice that the factor of 2 does not appear on the terms such that
m-n=0
. However, these terms are real and positive, and can be bounded by 2 anyway, making the above indeed an inequality.

Now, the value
m-n
ranges from
0
to
N-1
, and we observe that for each
0\leq j\leq N-1
, there are
N-j
pairs of
m
and
n
in the desired range such that
m-n=j
. Hence, the
m-n=1
summand is being added
N-1
times, the
m-n=2
summand is being added
N-2
times, and so on. Combining the
n,m
sum into one sum over
m-n
, and relabeling this variable as
n
, we obtain

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left(N-n\right)\Re\left[\int f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n}}\,d\mu\right]\,.

Moving one power of
1/N
inside the sum yields the desired inequality.
∎

We also use Hölder’s inequality, particularly in the following form on averages:

Lemma 2.4

(Hölder’s inequality on averages)
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
be a finite sequence of real, nonnegative numbers. The function
A:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

A_{N}(p):=\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

is increasing in
p
: i.e. for
p\leq q
we have

\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

In particular, for
0<r<1
, we have the following:

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}^{r}\leq\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}\right)^{r}\,.

We also use the following maximal inequality to extend some results via density (cf.
[
4
, Theorem 1.8]
for instance):

Lemma 2.5

(Maximal inequality)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
be a measure-preserving system, and
p\in(1,\infty)
. For every real-valued function
f\in L^{p}(\mu)
, we have

\left\lVert\sup_{N}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f\circ T^{n}\right\rVert_{p}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}\,.

3.
Stability under sums, factors, products

3.1.
A reverse Bourgain bound

Recall that the constants in the Bourgain bound only depend on the exponents and order of multiple recurrence. For example, fixing the exponents as
1,2,3,\dots
, it follows that for any
k
there exists
C_{k}
and
N_{k}
such that for any
f\in L^{\infty}
bounded by 1 and all functions
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}
with
\max_{j=1,\dots,k}\|g_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{k}g_{j}\circ T^{jn}\cdot f\circ T^{(k+1)n}\right\|_{2}\leq C_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/2^{k}}}+\left[W_{N}^{k}(f)\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}\right)

for all
N\geq N_{k}
. Hence, we can take a supremum over all such functions
g_{j}
to see that

\displaystyle\sup_{\overset{g_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)}{\max_{j=1,\dots,k}\|g_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 1}}\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{k}g_{j}\circ T^{jn}\cdot f\circ T^{(k+1)n}\right\|_{2}\leq C_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/2^{k}}}+\left[W_{N}^{k}(f)\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}\right)

holds for sufficiently large
N
, depending only on
k
.

Let us denote the above supremum over
k+1
multiple recurrence averages as
M^{k}_{N}(f)
, so that we may rewrite the previous bound as

(12)

\displaystyle M_{N}^{k}(f)\leq C_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/2^{k}}}+\left[W_{N}^{k}(f)\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}\right)

for sufficiently large
N
depending on
k
, or

M_{N}^{k}(f)\precsim_{k}W_{N}^{k}(f)

using the special asymptotic notation. We shall refer to
M_{N}^{k}
as the
k
-th order Uniform multiple recurrence average
. Hence, it follows from this Bourgain bound (
12
) that for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{k})^{\perp}
, the uniform multiple recurrence averages
M_{N}^{k}(f)
converge to zero, and the rate of convergence can be quantitatively controlled by the WW averages
W_{N}^{k}(f)
; that is, a “good” decay rate on the averages
W_{N}^{k}(f)
will transfer to the averages
M_{N}^{k}(f)
. Specifically, we see that if
W_{N}^{k}(f)\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N})
, then
M_{N}^{k}(f)\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N})
.

In this section, we seek to reverse this transfer, and establish that good decay rates on
M_{N}^{k}(f)
can pass back to
W_{N}^{k}(f)
. We do so by obtaining a “reverse” Bourgain bound, in which the WW averages are controlled by the uniform multiple recurrence averages:

Theorem 3.1

(Reverse Bourgain bound)
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}_{k}
, such that for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
with
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
we have

(13)

\displaystyle W_{N}^{k}(f)\leq C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/24}}+\left[M_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(f)\right]^{1/6}\right)

for all
N
. Hence, we have

W_{N}^{k}(f)\precsim_{k}M_{N}^{k}(f)

As an immediate consequence, we see that if
M_{N}^{k}(f)\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N})
, then
W_{N}^{k}(f)\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N})
. Together with the Bourgain bound (
12
), we see that
M_{N}^{k}
and
W_{N}^{k}
must have “essentially” the same decay rate, in that
W_{N}^{k}(f)\approx_{k}M_{N}^{k}(f)
for all bounded
f
.

Here, we prove the case
k=1
:

W_{N}^{1}(f)\leq C^{\prime}_{1}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/24}}+\left[M^{1}_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}(f)\right]^{1/6}\right)

This estimate corresponds to double recurrence (as seen from the definition of
M^{k}_{N}(f)
), and its proof contains most of the relevant observations and ideas. In fact, we improve on the
k=1
case here and show

W_{N}^{1}(f)\leq C^{\prime}_{1}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/6}}+\left[M^{1}_{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}(f)\right]^{1/6}\right)

holds for all
N
.
A much more general case is worked out in the appendix
A
, from which this result and more throughout this section follow immediately. The
k>1
case has many more technical details arising from the summation switch, which are handled in detail but geometrically force a slightly worse estimate than what is presented here.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
, with
k=1
.

Applying the Van der Corput lemma pointwise, we bound the first piece trivially to yield a remainder of the order
1/\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
. Extending the
n
sum from
N-h
to
N
picks up another remainder of the order
1/\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
. Hence:

\displaystyle W^{1}_{N}(f)^{3}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\right|\right\rVert^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+\frac{4}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\int\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f\circ T^{n}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n+h}}\right|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+4\left(\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\int\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f\circ T^{n}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n+h}}\right|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}

On the integral, we apply the lemma
2.3
:

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+4\left(\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\frac{2}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left(\frac{N-n}{N}\right)\Re\left[\int f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n}}\cdot f\circ T^{n+h}\,d\mu\right]\right)^{1/2}\,.

Notice that for any sequence
a_{n}
, by interchanging sums we have

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left(\frac{N-n}{N}\right)a_{n}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{q}a_{n}\,.

Applying this, we continue to see

\displaystyle=\frac{6}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+4\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{q}\Re\left[\int f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n}}\cdot f\circ T^{n+h}\,d\mu\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+4\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=0}^{N-1}\left|\frac{1}{N\cdot\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{n=0}^{q}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\int f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n}}\cdot f\circ T^{n+h}\,d\mu\right|\right)^{1/2}

after pulling out
\Re
and bounding it by the absolute value. Consider excising the values of
q
between
0
and
\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-1
from the sum, which we can bound trivially to create a remainder term of the order
1/N
. Pulling this out by subadditivity, it may join the other remainder term:

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6+4\sqrt{2}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+4\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}^{N-1}\left|\frac{1}{N\cdot\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{n=0}^{q}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\int f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n}}\cdot f\circ T^{n+h}\,d\mu\right|\right)^{1/2}\,.

Now, consider shifting the indices of the
h
sum down by
n
, which causes the indices of the summands to increase by
n
:

\displaystyle=\frac{6+4\sqrt{2}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+4\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}^{N-1}\left|\frac{1}{N\cdot\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{n=0}^{q}\sum_{h=1-n}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-n}\int f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h+n}}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n}}\cdot f\circ T^{h+2n}\,d\mu\right|\right)^{1/2}\,.

We wish to exchange the
n
and
h
sums. Note that the bounds on the
n
and
h
sum form a parallelogram in the
n,h
plane. Since
q\geq\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
, we may split this parallelogram into three regions: two triangles for
h
between
1-q
and
\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-q
and for
h
between
1
and
\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
, and another parallelogram for
h
between
\sqrt{N}-q
and
0
. For each such region, we can interchange the sums to get

\sum_{n=0}^{q}\sum_{h=1-n}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-n}=\left[\sum_{h=1-q}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-q}\sum_{n=1-h}^{q}\right]+\left[\sum_{h=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-q+1}^{0}\sum_{n=1-h}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-h}\right]+\left[\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-h}\right]

written without the summands. We see that the third term has a total of
\leq\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{2}
summands. If we bound those terms trivially, we get a remainder term of the size
1/\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
. Likewise, the number of summands in the first term is

\sum_{h=1-q}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-q}q-1+h+1=\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}h

which again is on the order of
\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{2}
. Hence, both of these pieces can be cut off and absorbed into the remainder term, and we continue to see

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6+12\sqrt{2}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+4\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}^{N-1}\left|\frac{1}{N\cdot\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-q+1}^{0}\sum_{n=1-h}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-h}\int f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h+n}}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n}}\cdot f\circ T^{h+2n}\,d\mu\right|\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{6+12\sqrt{2}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+4\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}^{N-1}\left|\frac{1}{N\cdot\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=0}^{q-\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-1}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\int f\circ T^{-h}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n-h}}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n}}\cdot f\circ T^{2n}\,d\mu\right|\right)^{1/2}

after adjusting the indices, translating the measure by
T^{h}
, and replacing
h
with
-h
.
Now, we finally pull the absolute value inside the integral to make the uniform double recurrence averages appear:

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6+12\sqrt{2}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+4\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{h=0}^{q-\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-1}\int|f\circ T^{-h}|\cdot\left|\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}(\overline{f\circ T^{-h}\cdot f})\circ T^{n}\cdot f\circ T^{2n}\right|\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6+12\sqrt{2}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+4\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{h=0}^{q-\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-1}\sup_{\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\int\left|\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}g\circ T^{n}\cdot f\circ T^{2n}\right|\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6+12\sqrt{2}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+4\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{h=0}^{q-\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-1}M^{1}_{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}(f)\right)^{1/2}\,.

With the
h
dependence lost, both the averages over
h
and
q
vanish, and we may take a cube root of both sides to achieve the desired result.
∎

3.2.
Sublinearity of WW averages

As the first order WW averages are sublinear, it follows that they form a subspace of
L^{2}(\mathcal{K})^{\perp}
, as noted by Assani
[
3
]
. However, the higher-order WW averages introduced in
[
6
]
are submultilinear. Hence, controlling the average
W_{N}^{k}(f+g)
by factoring it out requires control also of the “off-diagonal averages”. In
[
6
]
, this approach is referred to as “multilinearity concerns”, and such control is possible in the examples given.

However, we note that the uniform multiple recurrence averages
M_{N}^{k}
as defined previously are sublinear. Since we have established between the forwards and reverse Bourgain bounds that polynomial decay rates can pass between
M_{N}^{k}
and
W_{N}^{k}
, it follows that we can also transfer sublinearity, albeit with a worse powers and remainders:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists
N_{k}
and
C^{\prime\prime}_{k}
such that for all invertible dynamical systems
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
and for all
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
both bounded by 1, we have

(14)

\displaystyle W_{N}^{k}(f_{1}+f_{2})

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/(3\cdot 2^{k+3})}}+\left[W_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(f_{1})\right]^{1/(3\cdot 2^{k})}+\left[W_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(f_{2})\right]^{1/(3\cdot 2^{k})}\right)

for all
N\geq N_{k}
. Hence, we have

W_{N}^{k}(f_{1}+f_{2})\precsim_{k}W_{N}^{k}(f_{1})+W_{N}^{k}(f_{2})\,.

For example, the
k=2
case yields

W_{N}^{2}(f_{1}+f_{2})\leq C^{\prime\prime}_{2}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/96}}+W_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}(f_{1})^{1/12}+W_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}(f_{2})^{1/12}\right)

for an absolute constant
C_{2}^{\prime\prime}
and sufficiently large
N
, with no dependence on the functions or system.

Proof.

We apply the reverse Bourgain bound, use sublinearity, and apply the Bourgain bound:

\displaystyle W_{N}^{k}(f_{1}+f_{2})

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/24}}+\left[M_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(f_{1}+f_{2})\right]^{1/6}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/24}}+\left[M_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(f_{1})+M_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(f_{1})\right]^{1/6}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/24}}+\left[C_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor^{1/2^{k}}}+\left[W_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(f_{1})\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}\right)+C_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor^{1/2^{k}}}+\left[W_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(f_{2})\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}\right)\right]^{1/6}\right)\,.

Consolidating remainder terms gives the desired bound for a larger constant.
∎

As previously remarked, the relation
\precsim
is not generally additive. But in the case of polynomial decay, it does follow that if
f,g\precsim 0
, then
f+g\precsim 0
. Hence, in the context of WW functions we immediately get the following:

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
be an invertible dynamical system, and
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. If
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are both
k
-th order WW functions of power type, then
f_{1}+f_{2}
is a
k
-th order WW function of power type; that is, if
W_{N}^{k}(f_{1})\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N})
and
W_{N}^{k}(f_{2})\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N})
, then
W_{N}^{k}(f_{1}+f_{2})\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N})
and the collection of WW functions of power type

\left\{f\in L^{\infty}(\mu):W_{N}^{k}(f)\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N})\right\}=\left\{f\in L^{\infty}(\mu):W_{N}^{k}(f)\approx 0\right\}

forms a vector space.

Specifically, if
W_{N}^{k}(f_{1})\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N},\alpha)
and
W_{N}^{k}(f_{2})\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N},\beta)
, then
W_{N}^{k}(f_{1}+f_{2})\in\text{Poly}\left(\mathbb{N},\frac{\min\{\alpha,\beta\}}{3\cdot 2^{k+2}}\right)
.

From the application of sublinearity in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we could also get the above results for the sum of any amount of functions
f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m}
rather than just two. Hence, if
E
is a collection of
k
-th order WW functions of power type
\alpha>0
, then any
f\in\text{span}(E)
is a
k
-th order WW function of power type
\alpha/3\cdot 2^{k+2}
. In order to show that a given
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
is a
k
-th order WW system of some power type
\alpha>0
, it suffices to find a set of
k
-th order WW functions of power type
\beta
whose span is dense in
L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{k})^{\perp}
.

3.3.
Off-diagonal averages

Linearity results for higher order WW averages were approached in
[
6
]
through “multilinearity concerns”; for a collection
\mathcal{E}\subset L^{\infty}(\mu)
of
k
-th order WW functions of power type
\alpha
, we can show that any
f\in\text{span}(\mathcal{E})
is a
k
-th order WW functions of power type
\alpha
if we could establish polynomial decay on the
off-diagonal terms
of the form

(15)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}e_{\eta}\circ T^{h\cdot\eta}\right]\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}

for every possible collection
\{e_{\eta}:\eta\in V_{k-1}\}\subset\mathcal{E}
. This comes from simply factoring everything out using multi-sublinearity, in which
W_{N}^{k}(f)
is bounded by a finite collection of terms of the form (
15
).

While theoretically straightforward and easy to check in the specific examples, like K automorphisms or skew products
[
6
]
, this approach is less helpful for more generic cases, such as stability results. For the stability under a product of a
k
-th order WW system
(Y,\mathcal{G},\nu,S)
with a K automorphism
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
, extra steps must be taken to ensure a dense set of
k
-th order WW functions of power type; while it is easy to show that there is a spanning set, we do not immediately know that the WW property is transferred because we do not know anything about the system
(Y,\mathcal{G},\nu,S)
.

However, we observe in the proof of the reverse Bourgain bound on double recurrence (Theorem
3.1
), most terms are bounded away to make the uniform double recurrence average
M_{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}^{1}(f)
appear. In higher order cases, we can let these functions be arbitrary, and we see that the off-diagonal terms can be controlled by uniform multiple recurrence averages. Since these can then be controlled by diagonal average
W_{N}^{k}
, we can string these estimates together to control all off-diagonal WW averages by
W_{N}^{k}
:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists a constant
C_{k}^{\prime\prime}
and
N_{k}
such that for all invertible dynamical systems
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
and for all collections
g_{\eta}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
indexed by
\eta\in V_{k-1}
and satisfying
\max_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}\|g_{\eta}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, and for any
\zeta\in V_{k-1}
we have

(16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta}\circ T^{h\cdot\eta}\right]\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}\\
&\leq C^{\prime\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/(3\cdot 2^{k+3})}}+\left[W_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(g_{\zeta})\right]^{1/(3\cdot 2^{k})}\right)\end{split}

for
N\geq N_{k}
.

Remark 3.5
.

Based on dependence, note that we can take a min over
\zeta\in V_{k-1}
on the right-hand side and pass it through to the WW average. Hence, the above can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta}\circ T^{h\cdot\eta}\right]\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}\precsim_{k}\min_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}W_{N}^{k}(g_{\eta})\,.

Since the minimum may also be bounded by the product over all
W_{N}^{k}(g_{\eta})
, each raised to the appropriate power, this may also be used to bound the above terms.

In
[
6
, Theorem 6.3]
, it is shown that all
k
-th order off-diagonal averages for
f
(above averages where some
g_{\eta}=f
) converge to zero if
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{k}^{\perp})
. This theorem shows that they do so at a uniform rate, which is controlled by the WW average itself.

To build up to this theorem, we first apply the exact same argument of the reverse Bourgain bound (Theorem
3.1
) on a collection of functions
g_{\eta}
for
\eta\in V_{k-1}
, rather than only
f
. This is done in detail in the appendix:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
k\in N
. Then there exists a constant
C_{k}^{\prime}
such that for all invertible dynamical systems
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
and for all collections
g_{\eta}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
indexed by
\eta\in V_{k-1}
and satisfying
\max_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}\|g_{\eta}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, we have

(17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta}\circ T^{h\cdot\eta}\right]\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}\\
&\leq C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/24}}+\left[M_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(g_{\textbf{1}})\right]^{1/6}\right)\end{split}

where
\textbf{1}\in V_{k-1}
denotes the element with
1
’s in every component.

Since this holds for any collection
\{g_{\eta}:\eta\in V_{k-1}\}
, we may bound the off-diagonal term by any
g_{\zeta}
for
\zeta\in V_{k-1}
if we can permute the product over
\eta\in V_{k-1}
to put
\zeta
in the
1
slot. We cannot reorder the terms
g_{\eta}
arbitrarily, but we may do so enough that the changes created are lost in the application of the previous lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
k\in N
. Then there exists a constant
C_{k}^{\prime}
such that for all invertible dynamical systems
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
and for all collections
g_{\eta}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
indexed by
\eta\in V_{k-1}
and satisfying
\max_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}\|g_{\eta}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, and for any
\zeta\in V_{k-1}
, we have

(18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta}\circ T^{h\cdot\eta}\right]\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}\\
&\leq C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/24}}+\left[M_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(g_{\zeta})\right]^{1/6}\right)\end{split}

Proof.

Consider the off-diagonal average:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta}\circ T^{h\cdot\eta}\right]\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}\,.

We wish to re-index in the following way

\sum_{h_{i}=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}a_{h_{i}}=\sum_{h_{i}=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}a_{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor-h_{i}+1}

for each variable
h_{i}
with
\zeta_{i}=0
.
If we define for any two
\alpha,\beta\in V_{k-1}
, the intersection
\alpha\cap\beta\in V_{k-1}
as

(\alpha\cap\beta)_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\alpha_{i}=\beta_{i}=1\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

then the reindexing described above takes the expression
\eta\cdot h
to

[\eta\cap\zeta-\eta\cap(\mathbf{1}-\zeta)]\cdot h+|\eta\cap(\mathbf{1}-\zeta)|(\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+1)\,.

Shifting the measure by
T^{(\mathbf{1}-\zeta)\cdot h}
puts the product in the above term over

\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}\left(g_{\eta}\circ T^{|\eta\cap(\mathbf{1}-\zeta)|(\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+1)}\right)\circ T^{[\eta\cap\zeta-\eta\cap(\mathbf{1}-\zeta)+\mathbf{1}-\zeta]\cdot h}\,.

The expression
\eta\cap\zeta-\eta\cap(\mathbf{1}-\zeta)+\mathbf{1}-\zeta
is exactly the permutation function on
V_{k-1}
which flips the contents of each index
i
with
\zeta_{i}=0
. We observe that the
\eta=\zeta
term above exactly corresponds to the term

c^{|\zeta|}g_{\zeta}\circ T^{\mathbf{1}\cdot h}\,.

Hence, we have placed
g_{\zeta}
into the
\mathbf{1}
slot. After potentially correcting for the complex conjugates, we can apply the reverse Bourgain bound from the previous lemma
3.6
to get the desired estimate.
∎

Chaining this together with the Bourgain bound (just as done in the proof of
3.2
), gives us Theorem
3.4
:

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Beginning with the previous lemma, we apply the Bourgain bound:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta}\circ T^{h\cdot\eta}\right]\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/24}}+\left[M_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(g_{\zeta})\right]^{1/6}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/24}}+\left[C_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor^{1/2^{k}}}+\left[W_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(g_{\zeta})\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}\right)\right]^{1/6}\right)\,.

Consolidating remainder terms gives the desired result.
∎

3.4.
Conditional expectation and factors

Due to the multilinear nature of the higher order WW averages, their behavior compared to conditional expectation is less clear to analyze. With the previous results in tow, we obtain the following:

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists constants
C^{\prime\prime}_{k}
and
N_{k}
such that for all invertible dynamical systems
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
, for all functions
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
bounded by 1, and for all
T
-invariant sub
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{F}
, we have

\displaystyle W_{N}^{k}\left(\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})\right)\leq C^{\prime\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/(3\cdot 2^{k+1})}}+\left[W_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(f)\right]^{1/(3\cdot 2^{k})}\right)

for all
N\geq N_{k}
. Hence, we have

W_{N}^{k}\left(\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})\right)\precsim_{k}W_{N}^{k}(f)\,.

Proof.

Recall that conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}(\cdot|\mathcal{B}):L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{2}(\mu)
is a bounded and positive linear operator which satisfies
|\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})|\leq\mathbb{E}(|f|\,|\mathcal{B})
. By positivity, it follows that if
f_{t}(x)
are parameterized by
t
, we have
\sup_{t}|\mathbb{E}(f_{t}|\mathcal{B})|\leq\mathbb{E}(\sup_{t}|f_{t}|\,|\mathcal{B})
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is
T
-invariant, it also follows that
\mathbb{E}(\cdot|\mathcal{B})
commutes with the action of
T
on
L^{2}
.

Using this and other properties of conditional expectation, note for any
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
and
g\in L^{2}(\mathcal{B})
we have

(19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})\circ T^{n}\cdot g\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}&=\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\mathbb{E}(f\circ T^{n}|\mathcal{B})\cdot g\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}\\
&=\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\mathbb{E}(f\circ T^{n}\cdot g\circ T^{n}|\mathcal{B})\right|\right\|_{2}\\
&=\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\cdot g\circ T^{n}\Big|\mathcal{B}\right)\right|\right\|_{2}\\
&\leq\left\|\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\cdot g\circ T^{n}\right|\Big|\mathcal{B}\right)\right\|_{2}\\
&\leq\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\cdot g\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}\,.\end{split}

In our
k
-th order WW average of
\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})
, we split up the product as follows:

\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right]\circ T^{n}=\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})\cdot T^{n}\cdot\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}-\{\mathbf{0}\}}c^{|\eta|}\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right]\circ T^{n}\,.

On these functions, we may apply the estimate
19
to remove a conditional expectation. This yields an off-diagonal average, which we can apply the theorem
3.4
:

\displaystyle W_{N}^{k}\left(\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right]\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\cdot\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}-\{\textbf{0}\}}c^{|\eta|}\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B})\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right]\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/(3\cdot 2^{k+1})}}+\left[W_{\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor}^{k}(f)\right]^{1/(3\cdot 2^{k})}\right)

for
N\geq N_{k}
.
∎

In the case of polynomial decay rates, we see that conditional expectations of WW functions of power type will be WW functions of power type. Moreover, we see that dense sets of WW function may project down, and this transfer will also apply to WW systems:

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
be a
k
-th order WW system of power type
\alpha
, and let
\mathcal{B}
be a
T
-invariant
\sigma
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{F}
. Then
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is a
k
-th order WW system of power type
\alpha/(3\cdot 2^{k+2})
.

Proof.

By the bound from Theorem
3.8
, we immediately see that

W_{N}^{k}(f)\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N},\alpha)\quad\implies\quad W^{k}_{N}(\mathbb{E}(f|\mathcal{B}))\in\text{Poly}\left(\mathbb{N},\frac{\alpha}{3\cdot 2^{k+2}}\right)

In the case where
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
is a WW system, let
g\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{B}}_{k-1})^{\perp}
. As
g
is
\mathcal{B}
-measurable, we note that its Host-Kra-Gowers seminorms are the same over
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
and
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
, as the integrals will agree over any
\mathcal{B}
-measurable function. Hence,
g\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1})^{\perp}
. If
f_{m}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1})^{\perp}
is a sequence of
k
-th order WW functions of power type
\alpha
converging to
g
, then
\mathbb{E}(f_{m}|\mathcal{B})
is a sequence of
k
-th order WW functions of power type
\alpha/(3\cdot 2^{k+2})
converging to
\mathbb{E}(g|\mathcal{B})=g
. As before, since each
\mathbb{E}(f_{m}|\mathcal{B})
is
\mathcal{B}
-measurable, it follows that these functions are also in
L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{B}}_{k-1})^{\perp}
.
∎

3.5.
Weak WW averages and product stability

In
[
2
]
, Assani defines
weak WW averages
, which we denote by the following:

\displaystyle w_{N}^{1}(f)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{t}\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\right\|_{2}^{2/3}\,.

We denote these as “first-order weak WW averages”, and extend them to higher orders by obeying the same inductive formula
6
for the previous “strong” WW averages:

(20)

\displaystyle w^{k}_{N}(f)=\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}w^{k-1}_{N}\left(f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}\right)\,.

By the same reasoning, we obtain the closed formula

\displaystyle w^{k}_{N}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}w^{1}_{N}\left(\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}f\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}\sup_{t}\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}f\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right]\circ T^{n}\right\|_{2}^{2/3}\,.

Likewise, we can define
k
-th order weak WW functions of power type
to be
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
with
w_{N}^{k}\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N})
, and
k
-th order weak WW functions of power type
\alpha
to be
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
with
w_{N}^{k}\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N},\alpha)
.

As pulling the supremum out of the norm decreases the term, it follows that the weak WW averages
w_{N}^{k}
are weaker than the “strong” WW averages
W_{N}^{k}
in the following sense:

w_{N}^{k}(f)\leq W_{N}^{k}(f)

for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, for any order, strong WW functions of power type will be weak WW function of the same power type. We observe that a partial converse is possible, at the cost of order:

Theorem 3.10
.

There exists a constant
C
such that for all dynamical systems
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
and functions
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
bounded by 1, we have

W_{N}^{k}(f)\leq C\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/6}}+\left[w_{N}^{k+1}(f)\right]^{1/8}\right)

for all
N
and
k
. Using our asymptotic notation, we have

w_{N}^{k}(f)\precsim W_{N}^{k+1}(f)\,.

Hence, the first order strong WW averages can be bounded by second order weak WW averages, and so on.

Proof.

We use the following fact, shown by Assani in
[
2
]
, that for any
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
bounded by 1, we have

(21)

\displaystyle\sup_{\|g\|_{2}\leq 1}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|\hat{\sigma}_{f,g}|^{2}\leq\sup_{t}\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\right\|_{2}\,.

While Assani establishes the bound in which the
n
on the left ranges from
1
to
N
, the above holds by the same argument.

For any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we apply the Van der Corput inequality pointwise with
H=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
and apply Hölder’s inequality:

\displaystyle W_{N}^{1}(f)^{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+\frac{4}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\left(\int\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f\circ T^{n}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n+h}}\right|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}\,.

Inside, we apply the lemma
2.2
:

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+\frac{4}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\left(\frac{2}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left(\frac{N-n}{N}\right)\Re\left[\int f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n}}\cdot f\circ T^{n+h}\,d\mu\right]\right)^{1/2}\,.

Bounding
\Re
by the absolute value and
(N-n)/N
by 1, we obtain spectral measure coefficients on which we can apply (
21
):

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|\hat{\sigma}_{f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}}(n)|\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|\hat{\sigma}_{f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}}(n)|^{2}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\left(\sup_{t}\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{n+h}}\right\|_{2}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+4\sqrt{2}\left(w_{N}^{2}(f)\right)^{3/8}

after one more application of Hölder’s inequality to reintroduce the 2/3 power. Taking the cube root of both sides of the inequality, we see that

(22)

\displaystyle W_{N}^{1}(f)\leq\frac{2^{1/3}}{N^{1/6}}+2^{5/6}\left[w_{N}^{2}(f)\right]^{1/8}\,.

To lift this to higher orders, we write
W_{N}^{k}
in terms of
W_{N}^{1}
and apply (
22
):

\displaystyle W_{N}^{k}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}W^{1}_{N}\left(\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}f\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}\left(\frac{2^{1/3}}{N^{1/6}}+2^{5/6}\left[w^{2}_{N}\left(\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}f\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right)\right]^{1/8}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{1/3}}{N^{1/6}}+2^{5/6}\left(\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}w^{2}_{N}\left(\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}f\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right)\right)^{1/8}\,.

By the same inductive argument, adding
k-1
layers of the cube increases the weak WW norm from
w^{2}_{N}(f)
to
w^{k+1}_{N}(f)
, and after consolidating for constants, we get the desired bound.
∎

Between these two bounds, it follows that any results about weak WW averages can be transfer to strong WW averages, at the cost of order. For one such example, one results about products of weak WW averages, shown by Assani
[
2
]
, immediately transfers to the higher order case:

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
and
(Y,\mathcal{G},\nu,S)
be dynamical systems. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
g\in L^{\infty}(\nu)
, both bounded by 1, we have

ww_{N}^{k}(f\otimes g)\leq\min\{ww_{N}^{k}(f),\,ww_{N}^{k}(g)\}

for all
N
, where the averages on the left are taken in the product system
X\times Y
, while the averages on the right are taken in
X
and
Y
, as appropriate.

We note, in line with the remark
2.1
, that this product system
X\times Y
may not be ergodic, in which it may not make sense to consider WW systems. However, this bound, along with the following corollary
3.12
and Theorem
3.13
, both hold regardless of ergodicity. Even if
X\times Y
fails to be ergodic, it is still well-posed to consider WW averages and WW functions; we just may no longer be able to analyze their limiting behavior with the Gower-Host-Kra seminorms given by the construction (
8
).

Proof.

In
[
2
]
, the case
k=1
is shown. For the higher order case, we expand
w_{N}^{k}
in terms of
w_{N}^{1}
and use the
k=1
case:

\displaystyle w_{N}^{k}(f\otimes g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}w^{1}_{N}\left(\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}(f\otimes g)\circ(T\times S)^{\eta\cdot h}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}w^{1}_{N}\left(\left(\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}f\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right)\otimes\left(\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}g\circ S^{\eta\cdot h}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{k-1}}\sum_{h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]^{k-1}}w^{1}_{N}\left(\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}f\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right)

\displaystyle=w_{N}^{k}(f)\,.

By the same reasoning,
w_{N}^{k}(f\otimes g)
is less than
w_{N}^{k}(g)
, establishing the desired estimate.
∎

Using the bound from Theorem
3.10
, we can transfer the previous theorem to strong WW averages at the cost of order, and bound back by the strong WW averages:

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists a constant
C
such that for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and all dynamical systems
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
and
(Y,\mathcal{G},\nu,S)
and functions
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
g\in L^{\infty}(\nu)
, both bounded by 1, we have

\displaystyle W_{N}^{k}(f\otimes g)\leq C\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/6}}+\left[\min\left\{W_{N}^{k+1}(f),W_{N}^{k+1}(g)\right\}\right]^{1/8}\right)

or

W_{N}^{k}(f\otimes g)\precsim\min\{W_{N}^{k+1}(f),W_{N}^{k+1}(g)\}\,.

Hence, the decay rate of the
k+1
-th order WW averages for
f
transfers to
f\otimes g
for any
g\in L^{\infty}(\nu)
. In the case of WW functions, we get the following:

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
be a dynamical system. If
f
is a
k+1
-th order WW function of power type
\alpha
, then for any dynamical system
(Y,\mathcal{G},\nu,S)
and
g\in L^{\infty}(\nu)
the function
f\otimes g
is a
k
-th order WW function of power type
\alpha/8
in the product system
X\times Y
.

With the sublinearity-like property of the WW averages established in Theorem
3.2
, we have what we need to conclude the following:

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
be an invertible, weakly mixing
k+1
-th order WW system of power type
\alpha
, and let
(Y,\nu,\mathcal{G},S)
be an invertible
k
-th order WW system of power type
\beta
. Then the product
(X\times Y,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{G},\mu\times\nu,T\times S)
is a
k
-th order WW system of power type
\frac{\min\{\alpha/8,\beta\}}{3\cdot 2^{k+5}}
.

Proof.

As
X
is weakly mixing, we note that its Host-Kra-Ziegler factor
\mathcal{Z}_{k-1}^{X}
is trivial. Knowing this, we observe the following decomposition for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
:

\displaystyle f\otimes g

\displaystyle=\int f\,d\mu\otimes g+\left(f-\int f\,d\mu\right)\otimes g

\displaystyle=\int f\,d\mu\otimes\mathbb{E}(g|\mathcal{Z}_{k-1}^{Y})+\int f\,d\mu\otimes\left(g-\mathbb{E}(g|\mathcal{Z}_{k-1}^{Y})\right)+\left(f-\int f\,d\mu\right)\otimes g\,.

The first piece corresponds to the Host-Kra-Ziegler factor of
X\times Y
. Since
\int f\,d\mu
is constant, there is a dense set of
k
-th order WW functions of power type
\beta
in the second piece, as
Y
is a system of power type
\beta
. By the previous theorem, there is a dense set of
k
-th order WW functions of power type
\alpha/8
in the last piece, as
X
is a system of power type
\alpha
.

Hence, we have found a set of
k
-th order WW functions of power type
\min\{\alpha/8,\beta\}
whose span is
L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{k-1}^{X\times Y})^{\perp}
. By the sublinearity from Theorem
3.2
, it follows that the span of these functions is a dense subset of
k
-th order WW functions of power type
\frac{\min\{\alpha/8,\beta\}}{3\cdot 2^{k+5}}
, establishing the claim.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

As seen in
[
6
]
, this stability also preserves the weaker condition that
Y
satisfies pointwise convergence of
k
-th order multiple recurrence averages. Specifically, if
X
is a weakly mixing
k+1
-th order WW system of power type, and

satisfies pointwise convergence of
k
-th order multiple recurrence averages, then
X\times Y
satisfies pointwise convergence of
k
-th order multiple recurrence averages.

3.6.
Alternative constructions of WW averages

Recall that the family of higher-order WW averages
W_{N}^{k}
was constructed in
[
6
]
to satisfy

\displaystyle W^{1}_{N}(f)

\displaystyle=\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}

and

\displaystyle W^{k}_{N}(f)=\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}W^{k-1}_{N}\left(f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h}}\right)

for
k\geq 2
. Here, the choice of
h\in[\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor]
was done to balance remainder terms of the form
1/H
and
H/N
in establishing the Bourgain bound (
7
). In the context of WW functions, we needed this remainder term to decay polynomially, so that the Bourgain bound can transfer polynomial decay. Hence, any other choice of
H=\lfloor N^{\delta}\rfloor
for
0<\delta<1
would achieve this end, and could be used to construct WW averages that satisfy the theory of
[
6
]
.

In this section, we wish to analyze the WW averages formed by different bounds on the indexing variable
h
. In more generality, we let
r_{k}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
play the role of
\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
, and we can define
k
-th order WW averages for
r_{1},\dots,r_{k-1}
as
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
which satisfy

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}^{1}_{N}(f)

\displaystyle=W_{N}^{1}(f)=\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}f\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2/3}

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}^{k}_{N}(f)=\frac{1}{r_{k-1}(N)}\sum_{h_{k-1}=1}^{r_{k-1}(N)}\widetilde{W}^{k-1}_{N}\left(f\cdot\overline{f\circ T^{h_{k-1}}}\right)

for all
k\geq 2
.

From the Bourgain bound (
12
) and reverse Bourgain bound (
13
), we have already established that
M_{N}^{k}(f)\precsim_{k}W_{N}^{k}(f)
and
W_{N}^{k}(f)\precsim_{k}M_{N}^{k}(f)
, respectively, in which
W_{N}^{k}(f)\approx_{k}M_{N}^{k}(f)
. Here, we establish corresponding Bourgain bounds and Reverse Bourgain bounds for
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
. In the case that each
r_{1},\dots,r_{k-1}
grow like polynomials, it will follow that
M_{N}^{k}(f)\precsim_{r,k}\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)
and
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)\precsim_{r,k}M_{N}^{k}(f)
hold, and we have
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)\approx_{r,k}M_{N}^{k}(f)
. By transitivity, we will be able to conclude that any such
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)
will be equivalent to the classical WW average
W_{N}^{k}(f)
, and we have the following:

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
k\in N
, and let
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
be the WW averages constructed from the functions
r_{1},\dots,r_{k-1}
. If there exists some
\alpha,\beta>0
such that

N^{\alpha}\leq r_{m}(N)\leq N^{\beta}

holds for each
m=1,\dots,k-1
and sufficiently large
N
, Then the WW functions of power type with respect to
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
are exactly equal to the classical
k
-th order WW functions of power type; ie, we have

\left\{f\in L^{\infty}(\mu):\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)\approx 0\right\}=\left\{f\in L^{\infty}(\mu):W_{N}^{k}(f)\approx 0\right\}\,.

Specifically, we see that

(23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&W_{N}^{k}(f)\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N},\gamma)\quad\implies\quad\widetilde{W}^{k}_{N}(f)\in\text{Poly}\left(\mathbb{N},\frac{\alpha\gamma}{3\cdot 2^{k+2}}\right)\\
&\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)\in\text{Poly}(\mathbb{N},\gamma)\quad\implies\quad W^{k}_{N}(f)\in\text{Poly}\left(\mathbb{N},\frac{\min\{\alpha,\gamma,2/3\}}{3\cdot 2^{k+3}\cdot\lceil\beta\rceil}\right)\,.\end{split}

Remark 3.17
.

We remark that the constants
\alpha
and
\beta
need not be bounded above by 1. Hence, the WW averages constructed with each
h\in[N]
will also be equivalent in rate to the classical WW average.

To show that
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)
satisfies a reverse Bourgain bound, we revisit the proof from §
3.1
.
In the proof of Theorem
3.1
where
k=1
, when applying the Van der Corput inequality we chose
H=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
to mirror the classical WW averages. However, we note that the proof could be carried through without choosing
H
, yielding a remainder term of the order
\left(1/H+H/N\right)^{1/3}
. Specifically, we get the following:

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists a constant
C_{k}^{\prime}
where for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
bounded by 1 and any
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
constructed from
r_{1}(N),\dots,r_{k-1}(N)
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{(R_{k}(N))^{1/6}}+\left[M_{R_{k}(N)}^{k}(f)\right]^{1/6}\right)

where
R_{k}(N)=\lfloor\min\{r_{1}(N),\dots,r_{k-1}(N),N\}^{1/2}\rfloor

Remark 3.19
.

Just as easily, we get the same bound for off-diagonal averages of
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
, as seen in Theorem
3.4
.

Lemma
3.18
is shown in the appendix. With it, we can bound
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
by the classical WW average
W_{N}^{k}
:

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists a constant
C_{k}^{\prime\prime}
and
N_{k}
such that for all invertible dynamical systems
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
, all WW averages
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
constructed from
r_{1},\dots,r_{k-1}
, and for all
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
bounded by 1, we have

(24)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{(R_{k}(N))^{1/(3\cdot 2^{k+1})}}+\left[W_{R_{k}(N)}^{k}(f)\right]^{1/(3\cdot 2^{k})}\right)

for all
N
with
R_{k}(N)=\lfloor\min\{r_{1}(N),\dots,r_{k-1}(N),N\}^{1/2}\rfloor\geq N_{k}
.

Specifically, if every
r_{m}(N)\geq N^{\alpha}
for some
0<\alpha\leq 1
, it follows that

\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)\precsim_{r,k}W_{N}^{k}(f)\,.

Proof.

Chaining together the reverse Bourgain bound on
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
from Theorem
3.18
with the Bourgain bound (
12
) on
W_{N}^{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{(R_{k}(N))^{1/6}}+\left[M_{R_{k}(N)}^{k}(f)\right]^{1/6}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{(R_{k}(N))^{1/6}}+\left[C_{k}\left(\frac{1}{(R_{k}(N))^{1/2^{k}}}+\left[W_{R_{k}(N)}^{k}(f)\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}\right)\right]^{1/6}\right)

for
R_{k}(N)=\lfloor\min\{r_{1}(N),\dots,r_{k-1}(N),N\}^{1/2}\rfloor\geq N_{k}
. Consolidating remainder terms gives the desired estimate.

In the case that each
r_{m}(N)\geq N^{\alpha}
, then
R_{k}(N)
meets the requirements of the function
\phi
, and the special asymptotic notation may be used.
∎

Recall that the limiting behavior of the classical WW averages
W_{N}^{k}
is bound by the Host-Kra-Gowers seminorms, as given by (
9
). Hence, any
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
will inherit this limiting behavior by taking the limsup of the previous estimate (
24
), so long as the remainder term vanishes:

Corollary 3.21
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists a constant
C_{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}
such that for all invertible dynamical systems
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
, all WW averages
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
constructed from
r_{1},\dots,r_{k-1}
such

\lim_{N}r_{m}(N)=\infty

for each
1\leq m\leq k-1
, and for all
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
bounded by 1, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{N}\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)\leq C_{k}^{\prime\prime\prime}\VERT f\VERT_{k+1}^{1/(9\cdot 2^{k-1})}\,.

Proof.

Since for any fixed
k
there are finitely many functions
r_{m}(N)
, knowing that all
r_{m}
diverge to infinity is enough to show that the remainder term

\frac{1}{\min\{r_{1}(N),\dots,r_{k-1}(N),N\}^{1/(3\cdot 2^{k+2})}}

converges to 0.
∎

As noted previously, if each
r_{m}
grows like a polynomial, the estimate (
24
) transfers polynomial decay from
W_{N}^{k}
to
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
. To transfer in the other direction, it would suffice to establish a Bourgain bound for
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
, which we could chain together with the reverse Bourgain bound (
3.1
). Towards this, we establish the following technical result:

Lemma 3.22

(Bourgain bound for alternative WW averages)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
There exists a constant
C_{k,\beta}
and
N_{k,\beta}
such that for all invertible dynamical systems
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
and
k
-th order WW averages constructed from
r_{1},\dots,r_{k-1}
satisfying
r_{m}(N)\leq N^{\beta}
for each
m
, and for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle M_{N}^{k}(f)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k,\beta}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{k-1}\left[\frac{1}{r_{m}(\lfloor N^{1/\beta}\rfloor)}+\frac{r_{m}(\lfloor N^{1/\beta}\rfloor)}{N}\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}+\frac{1}{N^{1/(3\cdot\beta\cdot 2^{k-2})}}+\left[\widetilde{W}_{\lfloor N^{1/\beta}\rfloor}^{k}(f)\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}\right)

for all
N\geq N_{k,\beta}
.

Proof.

We take all relevant ideas from
[
6
]
. Consider first for any
integer

\beta\geq 2
, we have

(25)

\displaystyle W_{N}^{1}(g)

\displaystyle\leq W_{\lfloor N^{1/\beta}\rfloor}^{1}(g)+\left(\frac{\beta}{N^{1/\beta}}\right)^{2/3}\,.

This is shown in
[
6
, Theorem 6.3,
3a\implies 4a
]
for the case
\beta=2
, and holds for greater
\beta
by the same argument.

As each
r_{m}(N)\leq N^{\beta}
, it follows that
r_{m}(\lfloor N^{1/\beta}\rfloor)\leq N
for all
N
. Hence, we can follow the same argument of the Bourgain bound in
[
6
]
, picking
H=r_{m}(\lfloor N^{1/\beta}\rfloor)
instead of
\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
in each inductive application of the Van der Corput inequality. This will form remainder terms of the form
(1/H+H/N)
, which do not balance out, but give us the following estimate:

\displaystyle M_{N}^{k}(f)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\sum_{m=1}^{k-1}\left[\frac{1}{r_{m}(\lfloor N^{1/\beta}\rfloor)}+\frac{r_{m}(\lfloor N^{1/\beta}\rfloor)}{N}\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}

\displaystyle+C_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\prod_{m=1}^{k-1}r_{m}(\lfloor N^{1/\beta}\rfloor)}\sum_{h_{1}=1}^{r_{1}(\lfloor N^{1/\beta}\rfloor)}\dots\sum_{h_{k-1}=1}^{r_{k-1}(\lfloor N^{1/\beta}\rfloor)}W_{N}^{1}\left(\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}f\circ T^{\eta\cdot h}\right)\right)^{1/2^{k-1}}

for
N\geq N_{k}
. Applying the above estimate (
25
), we have constructed exactly the average
\widetilde{W}_{\lfloor N^{1/\beta}\rfloor}^{k}(f)
and another remainder term of the order
1/N^{1/(3\cdot\beta\cdot 2^{k-2})}
. Consolidating the remainder terms gives the desired average.
∎

Chaining this together with the reverse Bourgain bound from Theorem
3.1
obtains control of
W_{N}^{k}(f)
by
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)
. However, the precise statement of this relationship is exceedingly technical. Since our current interest is to transfer polynomial decay from
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}(f)
to

, we specifically analyze the case in which

in order to prove Theorem

:

Proof.

The first line of (
23
) follows from Theorem

. For the second line, suppose that
\widetilde{W}_{N}^{k}
is constructed from
r_{1},\dots,r_{k-1}

. By applying the Bourgain bound for

\displaystyle\leq C_{k,\beta}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{k-1}\left[\frac{1}{r_{m}(\lfloor N^{1/(\lceil\beta\rceil+1)}\rfloor)}+\frac{r_{m}(\lfloor N^{1/(\lceil\beta\rceil+1)}\rfloor)}{N}\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}+\frac{1}{N^{1/(3\cdot(\lceil\beta\rceil+1)\cdot 2^{k-2})}}+\left[\widetilde{W}_{\lfloor N^{1/(\lceil\beta\rceil+1)}\rfloor}^{k}(f)\right]^{1/2^{k-1}}\right)

4.
Expanded convergence results for WW functions

4.1.
Uniform polynomial WW bound and polynomial return times

Pointwise convergence for multilinear averages over a family
C
of weights given by

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)

have been long studied as natural generalizations of the classical Wiener-Wintner theorem, which corresponds to the case where
C=\{e^{2\pi int}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
and
J=1
, and its own generalization in the Return times theorem, corresponding to
C=\{g(S^{n}y):g\in L^{\infty}(\nu)\text{ for some system }(Y,\mathcal{G},\nu,S)\}
and
J=1
. The linear case
J=1
is long studied and many such generalizations and variants have been shown. For
J=2
, the case of weights
\{e^{2\pi int}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
was shown by Assani, Duncan, and Moore
[
5
]
, and the case of weights with polynomial phase
\{e^{2\pi iP(n)}\}
by Assani and Moore
[
8
]
. In the case that the system
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
satisfies pointwise convergence over
J
-th order recurrence averages for some
J\in\mathbb{N}
, Zorin-Kranich
[
25
]
shows a uniform convergence over nilsequence weights.

Assani, Folks, and Moore
[
6
]
establish that
J
-th order WW averages control the supremum of the averages over weights
e^{2\pi int}
in norm. This argument can be inductively lifted to polynomial phases:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
J\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and
a_{1},\dots,a_{J}\in\mathbb{Z}
be distinct and nonzero. Then there exists a constants
C_{J,k,a}
and
N_{J,a}
such that for all invertible dynamical system
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{J}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, all bounded by 1, we have

(26)

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{J,k,a}\left(\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{1/2^{k+J-2}}}+\left[W_{N}^{k+J-1}(f_{1})\right]^{1/2^{k+J-2}}\right)

for all
N>N_{J,a}
, where
p_{k,t}(n)=t_{1}n+t_{2}n^{2}+\dots t_{k}n^{k}
is the polynomial with coefficients
t_{i}
.

Remark 4.2
.

From this estimate, by the usual summability argument we may immediately establish almost everywhere convergence of the averages

\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|

to zero for
f_{1}
in the
L^{2}
closure of the
k+J-1
-th order WW functions. We note that this convergence falls under the result of Zorin-Kranich
[
25
]
under the condition of the pointwise convergence of multiple recurrence averages, which WW systems have already been shown to satisfy.

Remark 4.3
.

We remark that the above bound (
26
) does not, in general, classify the uniform characteristic factor for the multilinear averages with polynomial weights. In the case
J=1
, the factor of uniform convergence of the averages

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi inp_{k,t}(n)}f(T^{n}x)

to zero over all polynomials
p_{k,t}(n)
is the level
k
quasi-eigenfunctions, as shown by Frantzikinakis
[
14
]
, which is not necessarily equal to the
k
-th Host-Kra-Ziegler factor.

Proof.

Consider induction on
k
, the degree of the polynomial. The base case
k=0
is the Bourgain bound (
12
).

To induct, we apply the Van der Corput inequality pointwise with
N>|a_{1}|^{2}
and
H=\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor
.
After taking the supremum over
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}
and the integral in
\mu
and extending the
n
sum to
N
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor}+\frac{4}{\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor}\int\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi i(p_{k,t}(n)-p_{k,t}(n+h))}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\cdot\overline{f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n+a_{j}h}}\right|\,d\mu\,.

We observe that for any
h
, the polynomial
p_{k,t}(n)-p_{k,t}(n+h)
is one degree lower in
n
. Moreover, any terms of
p_{k,t}(n)-p_{k,t}(n+h)
which only depend on
h
and not
n
can be factored out of the
n
sum and vanish. Hence, taking a supremum over
p_{k,t}(n)-p_{k,t}(n+h)
is bounded by taking a supremum over all expression
p_{k-1,t}(n)
:

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor}+\frac{4}{\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor}\int\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k-1}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ip_{k-1,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}[f_{j}\cdot\overline{f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}h}}]\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|\,d\mu\,.

By Hölder’s inequality and the inductive hypothesis, these may be bounded for
N>N_{J-1,a}
:

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor}+\frac{4}{\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor}\left\|\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k-1}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ip_{k-1,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}[f_{j}\cdot\overline{f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}h}}]\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6|a_{1}|}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}+\frac{4}{\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor}C_{k-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{1/2^{k+J-3}}}+\left[W_{N}^{k+J-2}\left(f_{1}\cdot\overline{f_{1}\circ T^{a_{1}h}}\right)\right]^{1/2^{k+J-3}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6|a_{1}|+4C_{k-1}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{1/2^{k+J-3}}}+4C_{k-1}|a_{1}|\cdot\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{N}}{|a_{1}|}\rfloor}\left[W_{N}^{k+J-2}\left(f_{1}\cdot\overline{f_{1}\circ T^{a_{1}h}}\right)\right]^{1/2^{k+J-3}}\,.

By extending the
h
sum to
\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
, we remove the scaling by
a_{1}
inside. After moving the
h
sum inside by Hölder’s inequality, we have constructed
W_{N}^{k+J-1}
:

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6|a_{1}|+4C_{k-1}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{1/2^{k+J-3}}}+4C_{k-1}|a_{1}|\cdot\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\left[W_{N}^{k+J-2}\left(f_{1}\cdot\overline{f_{1}\circ T^{h}}\right)\right]^{1/2^{k+J-3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6|a_{1}|+4C_{k-1}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{1/2^{k+J-3}}}+4C_{k-1}|a_{1}|\left(\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\sum_{h=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}W_{N}^{k+J-2}\left(f_{1}\cdot\overline{f_{1}\circ T^{h}}\right)\right)^{1/2^{k+J-3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{6|a_{1}|+4C_{k-1}}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{1/2^{k+J-3}}}+4C_{k-1}|a_{1}|\left[W_{N}^{k+J-1}\left(f_{1}\right)\right]^{1/2^{k+J-3}}\,.

Taking a square root and consolidating constants gives the desired bound for
N>N_{J,a}:=\max\{N_{J,a},|a_{1}|^{2}\}
.
∎

By a standard application of the spectral theorem, we obtain the following return times theorem:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
be an invertible dynamical system, and let
f_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be an
L^{2}
limit of
k+J-1
-th order WW functions. Then for any functions
f_{2},\dots,f_{J}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
all bounded by 1, there exists a set
X_{f_{1},\dots,f_{J},k}
of full measure such that for any
x\in X_{f_{1},\dots,f_{J},k}
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{J}
all distinct and nonzero and any dynamical system
(Y,\mathcal{G},\nu,S)
and
g\in L^{\infty}(\nu)
and any integer valued polynomial
P
of degree less than or equal to
k
, the averages

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}g(S^{P(n)}y)\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)

converge to zero
\nu
-a.e.

Proof.

Since there are countably many possibilities for
a_{1},\dots,a_{J}
, it follows that we may fix them and intersect over a countable collection of full measure sets at the end.

Consider first the case in which
f_{1}
is itself a
k+J-1
-th order WW function of power type
\alpha
. Using the previous Theorem
4.1
, it follows for sufficiently large
N
that we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor^{1/2^{k+J-2}}}+\left[\frac{C_{f_{1}}}{N^{\alpha}}\right]^{1/2^{k+J-2}}\right)\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{N^{\alpha/2^{k+J-2}}}

after consolidating constants. Picking
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
to satisfy
\alpha\gamma>2^{k+J}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)\right|^{2}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor^{\alpha/2^{k+J-1}}}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{N^{2}}

for sufficiently large
N
, where the constant is allowed to change, but picks up no
N
dependence. These terms are summable in
N
, and by the monotone convergence theorem, we have

\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)\right|^{2}<\infty

for all
x
in a set of full measure
X_{f_{1},\dots,f_{J},k}
.

For any
x\in X_{f_{1},\dots,f_{J},k}
, applying the spectral theorem for any
g\in L^{\infty}(\nu)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}g\circ S^{P(n)}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)\right\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}e^{2\pi iP(n)t}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)\right|^{2}\,d\sigma_{g}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}e^{2\pi iP(n)t}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)\right|^{2}

and again these terms are summable. By the monotone convergence theorem in
\nu
, it follows that the integrand

(27)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}g(S^{P(n)}y)\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)\right|^{2}

convergence to zero
\nu
-a.e. Since the summands are bounded, we may extend the convergence from the subsequence
\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor
to
N
by the standard argument. We outline this argument here, for later comparison: Denote the summands in
n
of the average (
27
) as
a_{n}
, and observe that
|a_{n}|\leq 1
. Moreover, let us ignore the square root. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary, and pick
M\in\mathbb{N}
to satisfy

M^{\gamma}\leq N\leq(M+1)^{\gamma}\,.

We observe that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{1}{\lfloor M^{\gamma}\rfloor}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor M^{\gamma}\rfloor}a_{n}\right|+\frac{1}{M^{\gamma}}\sum_{n=\lfloor M^{\gamma}\rfloor+1}^{\lfloor(M+1)^{\gamma}\rfloor}|a_{n}|\leq\left|\frac{1}{\lfloor M^{\gamma}\rfloor}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor M^{\gamma}\rfloor}a_{n}\right|+\frac{\lfloor(M+1)^{\gamma}\rfloor-\lfloor M^{\gamma}\rfloor}{M^{\gamma}}

As
N\to\infty
, it follows that
M\to\infty
also: the averages along
\lfloor M^{\gamma}\rfloor
goes to zero by assumption, and the remainder term is
O(M^{-1})
, and also goes to zero. Hence, we obtain convergence along the entire subsequence.

Now, suppose that
F_{m}
are
j+K-1
-th order WW functions of power type converging to
f_{1}
. Without loss of generality, take
\|F_{m}-f_{1}\|_{2}<\frac{1}{m}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Using the maximal ergodic theorem (here referenced as lemma
2.5
), we observe for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
and any

that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}g\circ S^{P(n)}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}g\circ S^{P(n)}\cdot F_{m}\circ T^{a_{1}n}\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|f_{1}-f_{M}\right|\circ T^{a_{1}n}\right\|_{2}\leq 2\|f_{1}-F_{m}\|_{2}\leq\frac{2}{m^{2}}

and these terms are summable. By the monotone convergence theorem, it follows that the integrand must be converging to zero almost everywhere as

.

Hence, for any
x\in\cap_{m=1}^{\infty}X_{F_{m},f_{2},\dots,f_{J},k}
, and for any particular value of

, we see

\displaystyle\limsup_{N}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}g\circ S^{P(n)}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{N}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}g\circ S^{P(n)}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}g\circ S^{P(n)}\cdot F_{m}\circ T^{a_{1}n}\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|+0

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{N}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}g\circ S^{P(n)}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}g\circ S^{P(n)}\cdot F_{m}\circ T^{a_{1}n}\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|

which converges to zero as we let
m\to\infty
.
∎

4.2.
Multilinear Ergodic Hilbert Transform

The
(two-sided) ergodic Hilbert transform
of a function
f
is defined as the punctured sum (skipping
n=0
)

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{}^{\mkern-20.0mu{}^{\prime}}\frac{f(T^{n}x)}{n}\,.

This average was studied by Cotlar, who showed that it exists a.e. for any
f\in L^{1}(\mu)

[
12
]
. For such averages, we can make the same multilinear and return times generalizations. For example, pointwise a.e. convergence of the bilinear ergodic Hilbert transform given by

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{}^{\mkern-20.0mu{}^{\prime}}\frac{f_{1}(T^{n}x)f_{2}(T^{-n}x)}{n}

was established by Demeter
[
13
]
. A survey of return times theorems of the form

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{}^{\mkern-20.0mu{}^{\prime}}\frac{f(T^{n}x)g(S^{n}y)}{n}

is given by Assani and Presser
[
10
]
.

In contrast, the
one-sided ergodic Hilbert transform
is taken by restricting to positive
n
:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(T^{n}x)}{n}

and behaves poorly in general. Classically, it is known that in any dynamical system on a non-atomic measure space, there exists an integrable function for which the above diverges in the
L^{2}
norm, as shown by Halmos
[
16
]
. However, in certain cases and against certain weights, a.e. convergence of one-sided averages has been shown to converge. For example, certain “twisted”, or oscillatory averages of the form

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi ip(n)}}{n}f(T^{n}x)

have been shown to converge a.e. by Krause, Lacey, and Wierdl
[
19
]
for sufficiently sparse functions
p
. In the linear case, a.e. convergence of the average

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi int}}{n^{\sigma}}f(T^{n}x)

for some
0<\sigma<1
was shown for first-order WW functions
f
by Assani and Nicolaou
[
9
]
, and convergence of the two sided analogue was shown by Assani to be equivalent to
f
being a first-order WW function of power type
[
2
]
. More connections between the decay rates and convergence of one-sided linear ergodic Hilbert transforms are detailed by Assani and Lin
[
7
]
.

Here, we establish some convergence results for Higher-order WW functions of power type. More specifically, we show that any of the previously established pointwise convergence for ergodic averages for WW functions that have been previously established in
[
6
]
have Hilbert transform analogues. For example, from the bound in Theorem
4.1
, we deduce the following:

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
be an ergodic dynamical system,
k\geq 0
,
J\geq 1
, and let
f_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be a
J+k-1
-th order WW function of power type
\alpha>0
. Then For any
f_{2},\dots,f_{J}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, there exists a set
X_{f_{1},\dots,f_{J},k}
of full measure such that the series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi i\left(t_{1}n+t_{2}n^{2}+\dots+t_{k}n^{k}\right)}}{n^{\sigma}}\,\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)

converges for all
x\in X_{f_{1},\dots,f_{J},k}
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{J}\in\mathbb{Z}
, all distinct and nonzero, and for all
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}\in[0,1]
and
\sigma\in(1-\alpha/2^{J+k-1},1]
.

Moreover, the limiting function is continuous in
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}
and
\sigma
.

We highlight some special cases. the case in which
k=0
corresponds to the multilinear ergodic Hilbert transform:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)}{n^{\sigma}}

while the case
k=1
refers to the multilinear twisted ergodic Hilbert transform:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi int}}{n^{\sigma}}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)\,.

The case in which
J=1
corresponds to the ergodic Hilbert transform with polynomial phase:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi i\left(t_{1}n+t_{2}n^{2}+\dots+t_{k}n^{k}\right)}}{n^{\sigma}}f_{1}(T^{a_{j}n}x)\,.

Remark 4.6
.

Since the exponents
a_{j}
are allowed to be negative, it follows that that we can obtain the analogous two-sided results by applying this theorem to both sides individually.

To simplify the proofs, we specify the exact criterion used:

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(V,|\cdot|)
be a Banach space. Let
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset V
be a sequence and denote
A_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}
. Let
\sigma\in(0,1]
be such that
\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\frac{|A_{N}|}{N^{\sigma}}<\infty
and
N^{1-\sigma}A_{N}
is convergent. Then the sequence of partial sums
S_{N}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{\sigma}}
is convergent.

We remark that the Kronecker lemma provides a partial converse, that if
S_{N}
converges, then
N^{1-\sigma}A_{N}
converges to zero. While this proof does not require
N^{1-\sigma}A_{N}
to converge to zero specifically, it will follow from the conclusion that it must be converging to zero.

Proof.

Recall by definition that in any metric space a sequence
\{v_{j}\}\subset V
is Cauchy if and only if

\lim_{N}\sup_{M>N}|v_{M}-v_{N}|=0\,.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
M>N
. Making use of the fact that

(x+1)^{\sigma}-x^{\sigma}\leq x^{\sigma-1}

for any
x\geq 1
and
0<\sigma\leq 1
, we observe that

\displaystyle\left|S_{M}-S_{N}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{n=N+1}^{M}\frac{nA_{n}-(n-1)A_{n-1}}{n^{\sigma}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{n=N+1}^{M}\frac{nA_{n}}{n^{\sigma}}-\sum_{n=N}^{M-1}\frac{nA_{n}}{(n+1)^{\sigma}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{n=N+1}^{M-1}nA_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{(n+1)^{\sigma}}\right)+\frac{MA_{M}}{M^{\sigma}}-\frac{NA_{N}}{(N+1)^{\sigma}}+\frac{NA_{N}}{N^{\sigma}}-\frac{NA_{N}}{N^{\sigma}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=N}^{M-1}n|A_{n}|\left(\frac{(n+1)^{\sigma}-n^{\sigma}}{n^{2\sigma}}\right)+\left|\frac{MA_{M}}{M^{\sigma}}-\frac{NA_{N}}{N^{\sigma}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=N}^{M-1}n|A_{n}|\left(\frac{1}{n^{\sigma+1}}\right)+\left|M^{1-\sigma}A_{M}-N^{1-\sigma}A_{N}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{|A_{n}|}{n^{\sigma}}+\left|M^{1-\sigma}A_{M}-N^{1-\sigma}A_{N}\right|

Hence,

\limsup_{N}\sup_{M>N}\left|S_{M}-S_{N}\right|\leq\limsup_{N}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{|A_{n}|}{n^{\sigma}}+\limsup_{N}\sup_{M>N}\left|M^{1-\sigma}A_{M}-N^{1-\sigma}A_{N}\right|=0\,.

and
S_{N}
is Cauchy.
∎

Proof of
4.5
.

Since there are only countably many choices of exponents
a_{j}
, it follows that we can fix the exponents, obtain a set of full measure, and intersect over all possibilities at the end.

Let
f_{1}
satisfy
W^{k+J-1}_{N}(f_{1})\leq CN^{-\alpha}
for some
0<\alpha<1/2
, and let
\varepsilon>0
be such that

\sigma:=1-\alpha/2^{k+J-1}+\varepsilon\leq 1\,.

Using the polynomial WW bound (Theorem
4.1
) we see that for any
f_{2},\dots,f_{J}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, all uniformly bounded by 1, and sufficiently large
N
, we have

\left\|\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|\right\|_{1}\leq C_{a,J}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/2^{k+J-1}}}+\left[W_{N}^{k+J-1}(f_{1})\right]^{1/2^{k+J-2}}\right)\leq\frac{C}{N^{\alpha/2^{k+J-2}}}

for a potentially larger value of
C
, where
p_{k,t}(n)=t_{1}n+\dots+t_{k}n^{k}
. By the monotone convergence theorem, we note that

\displaystyle\int\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|}{N^{\sigma}}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left\|\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|\right\|_{1}}{N^{\sigma}}

As we have just established, these summands are eventually bounded by

\frac{C}{N^{\sigma+\alpha/2^{k+J-2}}}=\frac{C}{N^{1+\alpha/2^{k+J-1}+\varepsilon}}

which is summable. Hence, the integrand

\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}\circ T^{a_{j}n}\right|}{N^{\sigma}}<\infty

for almost all
x\in X
. This meets the first condition of the lemma
4.7
.

Similarly, we observe by the polynomial WW estimate that for sufficiently large
N
,

\displaystyle\int\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{N^{\sigma}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)\right|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{N^{\sigma-1+\alpha/2^{k+J-1}}}=\frac{C}{N^{\alpha/2^{k+J-1}+\varepsilon}}\,.

Hence, for
\gamma:=2^{k+J-1}/\alpha
this integral is summable over the subsequence
\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor
. Hence, by the monotone convergence theorem the integral is finite almost everywhere, and the summands must be converging to zero:

\displaystyle\lim_{N}\sup_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}\left|\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor^{\sigma}}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor N^{\gamma}\rfloor}e^{2\pi ip_{k,t}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)\right|=0\,.

By our choice of
\gamma
this convergence extends from the subsequence
N^{\gamma}
to all of
N
by the usual argument; from the comparison
M^{\gamma}<N\leq(M+1)^{\gamma}
, since all of the summands are bounded the remainder term has the form
M^{\gamma-1-\gamma\sigma}=M^{-(2^{k+J-1}/\alpha)\cdot\varepsilon}
, which converges to zero as
M\to\infty
, and the second condition of lemma
4.7
is met.

Taking
X_{f_{1},\dots,f_{J},k}
to be the set of full measure where both of these conditions hold, it follows that for any
x\in X_{f_{1},\dots,f_{J},k}
that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi i(t_{1}n+\dots+t_{k}n^{k})}\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)}{n^{\sigma}}

converges uniformly in
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}
for
\sigma
chosen to be
1-\alpha/2^{k+J-1}+\varepsilon
. As remarked previously, this implies convergence for all
\sigma\in(1-\alpha/2^{k+J-1}+\varepsilon,1]
.

By the classical theory of Dirichlet series, for any such
x
and
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}
where the above converges, the series is converging uniformly for all increased
\sigma

[
22
]
. Hence, taking
\varepsilon=1/m
for sufficiently large
m\in\mathbb{N}
and intersecting, we obtain a set of full measure where the above converges for all
\sigma\in(1-\alpha/2^{k+J-1},1]
.

∎

By the same argument with another routine application of the spectral theorem, we obtain the following return times analogue:

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu,T)
be an ergodic dynamical system,
k\geq 0
,
J\geq 1
, and let
f_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be a
J+k-1
-th order WW function of power type
\alpha>0
. Then For any

to be the set where both of these parts hold, it follows by the lemma

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{K}g_{k}(S^{b_{k}n}y)\prod_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(T^{a_{j}n}x)}{n^{\sigma}}

are converging

and

, all bounded by 1, and the theorem is established after taking

for sufficiently large

∎

Proof of Theorem

For Theorem

The main difficulty in proving
A.1
over the
k=1
case is exchanging the order of the following sums:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{q}\sum_{h_{1}=1-n}^{H_{1}-n}\dots\sum_{h_{k}=1-n}^{H_{k}-n}\,.

To this end, we will present the required estimates as lemmas before proving this theorem. To build up to these, we consider the following notations.

Let
k\geq 1
and
H_{1},\dots,H_{k}\geq 1
, and
q\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
H_{(1)}<q\leq N-1
, where
H_{(1)}
is the smallest value of
H_{m}
for any
m
. For each
n
(which we eventually take to be between
0
and
q
), we define the
k
dimensional rectangle

\Box_{n}=\{h\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:1-n\leq h_{m}\leq H_{m}-n\text{ for each }1\leq m\leq k\}\,.

Hence, the desired sum can be written

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{q}\sum_{h\in\Box_{n}}\,.

Now, we make the following observations about these rectangles. Since each dimension is an interval, the sets
\Box_{n}
mostly behave like intervals.
For example, the intersection of any collection of
\Box_{n}
’s only depends on the smallest and largest index: if
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\dots\leq a_{l}
is any sequence of integers, then

(29)

\displaystyle\Box_{a_{1}}\cap\Box_{a_{2}}\cap\dots\cap\Box_{a_{l}}=\Box_{a_{1}}\cap\Box_{a_{l}}

Also similarly to intervals, the intersection of two rectangles is a smaller rectangle, and if two rectangles are too far apart, then they will not intersect at all. This distance is determined by the smallest width of the rectangles, which is
H_{(1)}
. Specifically, we have

\#(\Box_{a}\cap\Box_{b})=\begin{cases}\prod_{m=1}^{k}(H_{m}-(b-a))&b-a<H_{(1)}\\
0&b-a\geq H_{(1)}\end{cases}

which we could also write as

(30)

\displaystyle\#(\Box_{a}\cap\Box_{b})=\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-(b-a),0\}\,.

Based on these observations, we see that for any
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, the collection of
i\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
h\in\Box_{i}
forms an interval, which we can denote the bounds of as
L_{h}
and
U_{h}
, and we see that the width of this interval
U_{n}-L_{h}+1
is bounded by
H_{(1)}
. If we define the set

\Gamma_{q}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{q}\Box_{n}

then we have the notation to exchange the desired sums:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{q}\sum_{h\in\Box_{n}}=\sum_{h\in\Gamma_{q}}\sum_{n=L_{h}}^{U_{h}}\,.

In the proof of Theorem
A.1
, we wish to group the terms
h
from
\Gamma_{q}
together by the width of the interval from
L_{h}
to
U_{h}
, or by the quantity
U_{h}-L_{h}+1
which ranges from
1
to
H_{(1)}
. Hence, we count the following:

Lemma A.2
.

For all terms as defined in the preceding paragraphs, we have for each
1\leq p\leq H_{(1)}
that

(31)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\#\{h\in\Gamma_{q}:U_{h}-L_{h}+1=p\}\\
&=2\left[\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-p+1,0\}-\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-p,0\}\right]\\
&+(q-p)\left[\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-p+1,0\}-2\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-p,0\}+\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-p-1,0\}\right]\end{split}

We note that the above terms could be combined, but we leave it in the above for the purpose of cancellations which we later analyze.

Proof.

Fix
1\leq p\leq H_{(1)}
. In order for any
h\in\Gamma_{q}
to lie in exactly
p
rectangles, it must be the case that the bounds
L_{h}
and
U_{h}
are equal to
i
and
i+p-1
for some
0\leq i\leq q-p+1
. Hence, for each such
i
we count the number of
h
such that
L_{h}=i
and
U_{h}=i+p-1
. By the interval properties we observed (
29
), this condition can be written as

h\in\Box_{i-1}^{c}\cap\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1}\cap\Box_{i+p}^{c}

where the
c
denotes set complements and at the endpoints,
\Box_{-1}
and
\Box_{q+1}
are taken to be empty. Hence, we need to count the size of this set for each
i
and add them up.

To illustrate this method, we begin with
i=0
, in which we wish to count

\#(\Box_{0}\cap\Box_{p-1}\cap\Box_{p}^{c})\,.

We observe that this set can be written as a set difference, which we can count by
\#(A-B)=\#A-\#(A\cap B)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\#(\Box_{0}\cap\Box_{p-1}\cap\Box_{p}^{c})

\displaystyle=\#(\Box_{0}\cap\Box_{p-1}-\Box_{p})

\displaystyle=\#(\Box_{0}\cap\Box_{p-1})-\#(\Box_{0}\cap\Box_{p-1}\cap\Box_{p})

\displaystyle=\#(\Box_{0}\cap\Box_{p-1})-\#(\Box_{0}\cap\Box_{p})

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-p+1,0\}-\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-p,0\}

using the previous observation (
30
) on the exact size of intersections. By the same argument, the other edge case of
i=q-p+1
also satisfies

\displaystyle\#(\Box_{q-p}^{c}\cap\Box_{q-p+1}\cap\Box_{q})

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-p+1,0\}-\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-p,0\}\,.

For values
1\leq i\leq q-p
, we apply the same method, with inclusion-exclusion as appropriate to reformulate everything in terms of intersections, which we can simplify:

\displaystyle\#(\Box_{i-1}^{c}\cap\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1}\cap\Box_{i+p}^{c})

\displaystyle=\#(\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1}-(\Box_{i-1}\cup\Box_{i+p}))

\displaystyle=\#(\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1})-\#((\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1})\cap(\Box_{i-1}\cup\Box_{i+p}))

\displaystyle=\#(\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1})-\#((\Box_{i-1}\cap\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1})\cup(\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1}\cap\Box_{i+p}))

\displaystyle=\#(\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1})-\#(\Box_{i-1}\cap\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1})-\#(\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1}\cap\Box_{i+p})+\#(\Box_{i-1}\cap\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1}\cap\Box_{i+p})

\displaystyle=\#(\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p-1})-\#(\Box_{i-1}\cap\Box_{i+p-1})-\#(\Box_{i}\cap\Box_{i+p})+\#(\Box_{i-1}\cap\Box_{i+p})

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-p+1,0\}-2\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-p,0\}+\prod_{m=1}^{k}\max\{H_{m}-p-1,0\}\,.

We observe that we have lost
i
dependence. Hence, adding these terms together the appropriate number of times gives us a bound on the desired term.
∎

To use this count to get meaningful estimates, we make some observations about the kinds of cancellation this formula (
31
) may afford. Consider the polynomial in
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
given by

\prod_{m=1}^{k}(x_{m}+1)\,.

Factoring this term out, every possible monomial appears exactly once. Hence when we subtract off the product of all
x_{m}
’s, we are left with

(32)

\displaystyle\prod_{m=1}^{k}(x_{m}+1)-\prod_{m=1}^{k}x_{m}=\sum_{\eta\in V_{k}-\{\mathbf{1}\}}\prod_{\eta_{i}=1}x_{i}

using previous notation. By the same reasoning, expanding the expression

\prod_{m=1}^{k}(x_{m}-1)

gives every possible monomial with parity. Hence, when we add them together, there is cancellation:

(33)

\displaystyle\prod_{m=1}^{k}(x_{m}+1)-2\prod_{m=1}^{k}x_{m}+\prod_{m=1}^{k}(x_{m}-1)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\overset{\eta\in V_{k}-\{\mathbf{1}\}}{k-|\eta|\text{ even}}}\prod_{\eta_{i}=1}x_{i}\,.

These observations can clean up the formula (
31
) from the previous lemma, but only once we are able to remove the
\max\{\cdot,0\}
operators. Hence, we will get different estimates on proportion depending on the size of
p
and the number of terms that vanish. When no terms vanish, we get the following:

Lemma A.3
.

Let
1\leq p\leq H_{(1)}-1
. Then

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{\#\{h\in\Gamma_{q}:U_{h}-L_{h}+1=p\}}{N\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}\leq 2^{k+1}\left(\frac{1}{N\cdot H_{(1)}}+\frac{1}{{H_{(1)}}\cdot H_{(2)}}\right)

where
H_{(1)}
and
H_{(2)}
denote the two smallest (not necessarily distinct) values of

.

Proof.

Consider first the case where

. Based on the range of

, all of the maximum operators from the formula (
31
) vanish, and we apply our observations (
32
) and (
33
) concerning expanding these expressions:

\displaystyle\#\{h\in\Gamma_{q}:U_{h}-L_{h}+1=p\}

\displaystyle=2\left[\prod_{m=1}^{k}(H_{m}-p+1)-\prod_{m=1}^{k}(H_{m}-p)\right]

\displaystyle+(q-p)\left[\prod_{m=1}^{k}(H_{m}-p+1)-2\prod_{m=1}^{k}(H_{m}-p)+\prod_{m=1}^{k}(H_{m}-p-1)\right]

\displaystyle=2\left[\sum_{\eta\in V_{k}-\{\mathbf{1}\}}\prod_{\eta_{i}=1}(H_{i}-p)\right]+2(q-p)\left[\sum_{\overset{\eta\in V_{k}-\{\mathbf{1}\}}{k-|\eta|\text{ even}}}\prod_{\eta_{i}=1}(H_{i}-p)\right]\,.

After we divide through by
\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}
(and also

), we see that for every

\displaystyle\frac{\#\{h\in\Gamma_{q}:U_{h}-L_{h}+1=p\}}{N\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}\leq\frac{2}{N}\sum_{\eta\in V_{k}-\{\mathbf{1}\}}\frac{1}{\prod_{\eta_{i}=0}H_{i}}+2\left(\frac{q-p}{N}\right)\sum_{\overset{\eta\in V_{k}-\{\mathbf{1}\}}{k-|\eta|\text{ even}}}\frac{1}{\prod_{\eta_{i}=0}H_{i}}\,.

In the first sum on the right hand side, we see that at least one
H_{i}
terms appears in every summand, so we can bound them all above by

. In the second sum, we see that at least two
H_{i}
’s appear in every summand, and we can bound them all above by
1/({H_{(1)}}\cdot H_{(2)})
for the two smallest possible values. In both cases, the total number of summands is less than
2^{k}
, so after bounding
q
by
N
and consolidating constants, we get the desired estimate.

Now consider the case
p=H_{(1)}-1
. Here, the formula (
31
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\#\{h\in\Gamma_{q}:U_{h}-L_{h}+1=H_{(1)}-1\}

\displaystyle=2\left[\prod_{m=1}^{k}(H_{m}-H_{(1)}+2)-\prod_{m=1}^{k}(H_{m}-H_{(1)}+1)\right]

\displaystyle+(q-p)\left[\prod_{m=1}^{k}(H_{m}-H_{(1)}+2)-2\prod_{m=1}^{k}(H_{m}-H_{(1)}+1)\right]\,.

Consider the terms on the second line. In the first product, the

correspond to deleting the

\displaystyle\#\{h\in\Gamma_{q}:U_{h}-L_{h}+1=H_{(1)}-1\}

\displaystyle=2\left[\prod_{m=1}^{k}(H_{m}-H_{(1)}+2)-\prod_{m=1}^{k}(H_{m}-H_{(1)}+1)\right]

\displaystyle+2(q-p)\left[\prod_{m=1}^{k}\mathop{}^{\mkern-5.0mu{}^{\prime}}(H_{m}-H_{(1)}+2)-\prod_{m=1}^{k}\mathop{}^{\mkern-5.0mu{}^{\prime}}(H_{m}-H_{(1)}+1)\right]\,.

After dividing by

, the first set of brackets can be dealt with the same way as before to yield a term of the order

. In the second set of brackets, we can expand by the same observations, only on

is now

, and overall this piece yields a term of the order

∎

When
p
is as big as possible, most terms from the formula (

) vanish and we can get the following:

Lemma A.4
.

Using the same notation as before, we have

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{\#\{h\in\Gamma_{q}:U_{h}-L_{h}+1=H_{(1)}\}}{N\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}\leq 2\left(\frac{H_{(2)}-H_{(1)}+1}{H_{(1)}\cdot H_{(2)}}\right)\,.

We leave the

Proof.

When

\displaystyle\frac{\#\{h\in\Gamma_{q}:U_{h}-L_{h}+1=H_{(1)}\}}{N\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q-H_{(1)}+2}{N}\right)\prod_{m=1}^{k}\left(\frac{H_{m}-H_{(1)}+1}{H_{m}}\right)

With the estimates of lemmas

.

Proof of Theorem
A.1
.

In this proof, we allow the constant

To begin, we ignore the

pointwise for

:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod_{m=1}^{k-1}H_{m}}\sum_{h_{1}=1}^{H_{1}}\dots\sum_{h_{k-1}=1}^{H_{k-1}}\left\|\sup_{t}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi int}\left[\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta}\circ T^{h\cdot\eta}\right]\circ T^{n}\right|\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\prod_{m=1}^{k-1}H_{m}}\sum_{h_{1}=1}^{H_{1}}\dots\sum_{h_{k-1}=1}^{H_{k-1}}\left(\frac{2}{H_{k}}+\frac{4H_{k}}{N}+\frac{4}{H_{k}}\sum_{h_{k}=1}^{H_{k}}\int\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{\eta\in V_{k-1}}(c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta}\circ T^{h\cdot\eta})\circ T^{n}\cdot(\overline{g_{\eta}\circ T^{h\cdot\eta}})\circ T^{n+h_{k}}\right|\,d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}}{H_{k}}+\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}H_{k}}{N}+C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}\sum_{h_{1}=1}^{H_{1}}\dots\sum_{h_{k}=1}^{H_{k}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left(\frac{N-n}{N}\right)\Re\left[\int\prod_{\eta\in V_{k+1}}c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta^{\prime\prime}}\circ T^{(h,n)\cdot\eta}\,d\mu\right]\right)^{1/2}

where we have begun to use

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}}{H_{k}}+\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}H_{(1)}^{1/2}}{N^{1/2}}+C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=H_{(1)}}^{N-1}\left|\frac{1}{N\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{q}\sum_{h_{1}=1}^{H_{1}}\dots\sum_{h_{k}=1}^{H_{k}}\int\prod_{\eta\in V_{k+1}}c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta^{\prime\prime}}\circ T^{(h,n)\cdot\eta}\,d\mu\right|\right)^{1/2}\,.

Now, we wish to shift the index of each

. Briefly, we analyze the effect of this on the integrand alone. We shift every

, and group terms by

:

\displaystyle\int\prod_{\eta\in V_{k+1}}c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta^{\prime\prime}}\circ T^{(h_{1}+n,\dots,h_{k}+n,n)\cdot\eta}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int\prod_{j=0}^{k+1}\prod_{\overset{\eta\in V_{k+1}}{|\eta|=j}}c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta^{\prime\prime}}\circ T^{(h_{1},\dots,h_{k},0)\cdot\eta+jn}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int\prod_{j=0}^{k+1}\prod_{\overset{\eta\in V_{k+1}}{|\eta|=j}}c^{|\eta|}g_{\eta^{\prime\prime}}\circ T^{(h_{1},\dots,h_{k},0)\cdot(\eta-\textbf{1})+jn}\,d\mu

after translating the measure by

, where as before

with

we see that the above integrand can be written as

\displaystyle\int\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}G^{h}_{j}\circ T^{jn}\,d\mu

and we observe that

. Without loss of generality, we can take

. For the sake of notation, we can remove the double apostrophe to take
\mathbf{1}
to be in

Returning to our main estimate, after this index shift we obtain

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}}{H_{k}}+\frac{H_{(1)}^{1/2}}{N}+C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=H_{(1)}}^{N-1}\left|\frac{1}{N\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{q}\sum_{h_{1}=1-n}^{H_{1}-n}\dots\sum_{h_{k}=1-n}^{H_{k}-n}\int\prod_{j=0}^{k+1}G^{h}_{j}\circ T^{jn}\,d\mu\right|\right)^{1/2}\,.

In the language of the previous lemmas, we can exchange the

\displaystyle=\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}}{H_{k}}+\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}H^{1/2}_{(1)}}{N^{1/2}}+C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=H_{(1)}}^{N-1}\left|\frac{1}{N\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}\sum_{h\in\Gamma_{q}}\sum_{n=L_{h}}^{U_{h}}\int\prod_{j=0}^{k+1}G^{h}_{j}\circ T^{jn}\,d\mu\right|\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}}{H_{k}}+\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}H_{(1)}^{1/2}}{N^{1/2}}+C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=H_{(1)}}^{N-1}\left|\frac{1}{N\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}\sum_{h\in\Gamma_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{U_{h}-L_{h}+1}\int\prod_{j=0}^{k+1}G^{h}_{j}\circ T^{(j-k-1)(L_{h}-1)}\circ T^{jn}\,d\mu\right|\right)^{1/2}\,.

We group together terms from

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}}{H_{k}}+\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}H_{(1)}^{1/2}}{N^{1/2}}+C^{\prime}_{k}\cdot

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=H_{(1)}}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}\sum_{p=1}^{H_{(1)}}\sum_{\overset{h\in\Gamma_{q}}{U_{h}-L_{h}+1=p}}\int\left|G^{h}_{0}\circ T^{(-k-1)(L_{h}-1)}\right|\cdot\left|\sum_{n=1}^{p}\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}G^{h}_{j}\circ T^{(j-k-1)(L_{h}-1)}\circ T^{jn}\,d\mu\right|\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}}{H_{k}}+\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}H_{(1)}^{1/2}}{N^{1/2}}

\displaystyle+C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=H_{(1)}}^{N-1}\sum_{p=1}^{H_{(1)}}\frac{p}{N\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}\sum_{\overset{h\in\Gamma_{q}}{U_{h}-L_{h}+1=p}}\,\sup_{\overset{h_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)}{\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\|h_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 1}}\int\left|\frac{1}{p}\sum_{n=1}^{p}\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}h_{j}\circ T^{jn}\cdot g_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime\prime}}\circ T^{(k+1)n}\,d\mu\right|\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}}{H_{k}}+\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}H_{(1)}^{1/2}}{N}+C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=H_{(1)}}^{N-1}\sum_{p=1}^{H_{(1)}}\frac{p}{N\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}\sum_{\overset{h\in\Gamma_{q}}{U_{h}-L_{h}+1=p}}M_{p}^{k}(g_{\mathbf{1}})\right)^{1/2}\,.

With

\displaystyle=\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}}{H_{k}}+\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}H_{(1)}^{1/2}}{N^{1/2}}

\displaystyle+C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=H_{(1)}}^{N-1}\sum_{p=1}^{H_{(1)}-1}\frac{p\cdot\#\{h\in\Gamma_{q}:U_{h}-L-h+1=p\}}{N\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k}H_{m}}M_{p}^{k}(g_{\mathbf{1}})\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}}{H_{k}}+\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}H_{(1)}^{1/2}}{N^{1/2}}

\displaystyle+C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=H_{(1)}}^{N-1}\sum_{p=1}^{H_{(1)}-1}\left(\frac{p}{N\cdot H_{(1)}}+\frac{p}{H_{(1)}\cdot H_{(2)}}\right)M_{p}^{k}(g_{\mathbf{1}})\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}}{H_{k}}+\frac{C^{\prime}_{k}H_{(1)}^{1/2}}{N^{1/2}}

\displaystyle+C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{H_{(2)}}\sum_{p=1}^{H_{(1)}-1}M_{p}^{k}(g_{\mathbf{1}})\right)^{1/2}+C^{\prime}_{k}\left(\left(\frac{H_{(2)}-H_{(1)}+1}{H_{(2)}}\right)M_{H_{(1)}}^{k}(g_{\mathbf{1}})\right)^{1/2}

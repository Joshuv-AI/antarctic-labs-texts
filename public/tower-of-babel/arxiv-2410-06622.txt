Abstract
It is shown that the problem of moisture propagation in porous media with a nonlinear relation between the mass flux and the pressure gradient as a counterpart of the Darcy law exhibits the property of bounded speed of propagation even in the case of a hysteresis relation between the capillary pressure and the moisture content. The paper specifies conditions for existence and uniqueness of solutions, and provides an upper bound for the moisture propagation speed. Keywords: porous media, hysteresis, speed of propagation, degenerate equation 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 47J40, 35K65, 35K92, 76S05



Introduction

The problem of bounded speed of propagation in classical problems of diffusion with no mass exchange on the boundary of the spatial domain has been extensively studied in the mathematical literature, see
[
26
]
and the references therein. To our knowledge, nothing has been done so far in this direction if hysteresis effects in the constitutive law are taken into account. Note that in porous media, hysteresis occurs as a result of surface tension on the liquid-gas interface, and in many cases cannot be ignored, see
[
4
]
. This paper fills this gap and investigates the problem of bounded propagation speed in presence of hysteresis.
To be precise, we study the following model problem in dimensionless form

\displaystyle\theta_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{\,div\,}\big(\kappa(x,\theta)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\big),

(0.1)

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle=G[u],

(0.2)

with unknown function
u
which represents the normalized pressure, that is,
u=(\pi-\pi_{0})/\pi_{0}
, where
\pi
is the physical pressure and
\pi_{0}
is the standard pressure. By
\theta
we denote the saturation which, up to a linear transformation, can be identified with the moisture content. It is related to the pressure in (

) in terms of a Preisach hysteresis operator
G
with initial memory

. Experimental validation of the Preisach operator for modeling the hysteretic pressure-saturation relationship in porous media was provided in
[
12

13
]
through a rigorous fitting procedure. While some models (see e. g.
[

In addition, models using the play operator usually incorporate a rate-dependent regularization in the pressure-saturation relationship. Here we consider instead a purely hysteretic Preisach-type relation. This introduces a significant mathematical challenge: the problem becomes degenerate in the sense that knowing

(0.3)

Equation (

[

6

The degeneracy associated with the Preisach operator, which is not considered in the classical theory of parabolic equations with hysteresis developed by Visintin in

1  Statement of the problem

Given a parameter
\omega\in[0,1]
, we prescribe for
(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
the boundary condition

\omega\kappa(x,\theta)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}(x)+(1-\omega)u=0\ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.

(1.1)

The case
\omega=1
corresponds to the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition,
\omega=0
is the Dirichlet boundary condition, and
\omega\in(0,1)
is the Robin boundary condition with boundary permeability
\gamma_{\omega}=(1-\omega)/\omega
. We define the reference space
X_{\omega}=W^{1,p}_{\omega}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, where
W^{1,p}_{\omega}(\Omega)=W^{1,p}(\Omega)
for
\omega\in(0,1]
and
W^{1,p}_{\omega}(\Omega)=W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for
\omega=0
, and state the problem in variational form

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(\theta_{t}\phi+\kappa(x,\theta)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\big)\,\mathrm{d}x+\gamma_{\omega}\int_{\partial\Omega}u\phi\,\mathrm{d}s(x)=0\quad\forall\phi\in X_{\omega},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\theta=G[u]\ \ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\mbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.3)

with the convention that
\gamma_{0}
can be any real number.

The Preisach operator was originally introduced in
[
24
]
. For our purposes, it is convenient to use the equivalent variational setting from
[
21
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\lambda\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))
be a given function with the following properties:

\displaystyle\exists\Lambda>0:\ \lambda(x,r)=0\ \mathrm{\ for\ }r\geq\Lambda,\forall x\in\Omega,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \exists\bar{\lambda}>0:\ |\lambda(x_{1},r_{1})-\lambda(x_{2},r_{2})|&\leq\Big(\bar{\lambda}\,|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|r_{1}-r_{2}|\Big)\ \forall r_{1},r_{2}\in(0,\infty),\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in\Omega.\end{aligned}

(1.5)

For a given
r>0
, we call the play operator with threshold
r
and initial memory
\lambda
the mapping which with a given function
u\in L^{1}(\Omega;W^{1,1}(0,T))
associates the solution
\xi^{r}\in L^{1}(\Omega;W^{1,1}(0,T))
of the variational inequality

|u(x,t)-\xi^{r}(x,t)|\leq r,\quad\xi^{r}_{t}(x,t)(u(x,t)-\xi^{r}(x,t)-z)\geq 0\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\ \forall z\in[-r,r],

(1.6)

with initial condition

\xi^{r}(x,0)=\lambda(x,r)\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.},

(1.7)

and we denote

\xi^{r}(x,t)=\mathfrak{p}_{r}[\lambda,u](x,t).

(1.8)

Given a measurable function
\rho:\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
and a bounded measurable function
\bar{G}:\Omega\to[0,1]
, the Preisach operator
G
is defined as a mapping
G:L^{2}(\Omega;W^{1,1}(0,T))\to L^{2}(\Omega;W^{1,1}(0,T))
by the formula

G[u](x,t)=\bar{G}(x)+\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\xi^{r}(x,t)}\rho(x,r,v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r.

(1.9)

The Preisach operator is said to be regular if the density function
\rho
of
G
in (
1.9
) belongs to
L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})
, and there exist constants
\rho_{1},\bar{\rho}>0
and a decreasing function
\rho_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all
U>0
, all
x,x_{1},x_{2}\in\Omega
, and a. e.
(r,v)\in(0,U)\times(-U,U)
we have

\displaystyle 0<\rho_{0}(U)<\rho(x,r,v)<\rho_{1},

(1.10)

\displaystyle|\rho(x_{1},r,v)-\rho(x_{2},r,v)|\leq\bar{\rho}\,|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

(1.11)

In applications, the natural physical condition
\theta=G[u]\in[0,1]
is satisfied for each input function
u
if and only if the additional assumptions

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(x,r,v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r\leq 1-\bar{G}(x),\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(x,r,-v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\bar{G}(x),

(1.12)

hold for a. e.
x\in\Omega
. The function
\bar{G}(x)
can be interpreted as residual moisture content at standard pressure. Note that the existence result in Theorem
1.6
below holds independently of any specific choice of
\bar{G}
and
\rho
and, in particular, of (
1.12
).

Let us mention the following classical result (see
[
21
, Proposition II.3.11]
).

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a regular Preisach operator in the sense of Definition
1.1
. Then it can be extended to a Lipschitz continuous mapping
G:L^{q}(\Omega;C[0,T])\to L^{q}(\Omega;C[0,T])
for every
q\in[1,\infty)
.

The Preisach operator is rate-independent. Hence, for an input function
u(x,t)
which is monotone in a time interval
t\in(a(x),b(x))
, a regular Preisach operator
G
can be represented by a superposition operator

G[u](x,t)=G[u](x,a(x))+B(x,u(x,t))

(1.13)

with an increasing function
u\mapsto B(x,u)
called a
Preisach branch
. Indeed, the branches may be different at different points
x
and different intervals
(a(x),b(x))
. The branches corresponding to increasing inputs are said to be
ascending
(the so-called wetting curves in the context of porous media), the branches corresponding to decreasing inputs are said to be
descending
(drying curves). In the ascending case, by
[
11
, Eq. (2.25)]
we have for
t\in(a(x),b(x))
that

\xi^{r}(x,t)=\max\{u(x,t)-r,\xi^{r}(x,a(x))\},

hence the ascending branch
B_{+}(x,u)
is given for
u\geq u(x,a(x))
by the formula

B_{+}(x,u)=\int_{0}^{r_{0}(x,u)}\int_{\xi^{r}(x,a(x))}^{u-r}\rho(x,r,v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r,

(1.14)

where
r_{0}(x,u)=\min\{r>0:u-r\leq\xi^{r}(x,a(x))\}
. Similarly, in the descending case it holds that

B_{-}(x,u)=\int_{0}^{r_{0}(x,u)}\int^{\xi^{r}(x,a(x))}_{u+r}\rho(x,r,v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r

(1.15)

for
u\leq u(x,a(x))
and
r_{0}(x,u)=\min\{r>0:u+r\geq\xi^{r}(x,a(x))\}
.
The wetting curve with initial memory
\lambda=0
is called
primary wetting curve
. All drying and wetting curves are bounded from below by the
limit wetting curve
, which is the theoretical wetting curve starting from the completely dry state, and from above by the
limit drying curve
, which is the theoretical drying curve starting from the completely wet state, see Figure
2
.

Figure 2:
Primary wetting curve and limit wetting/drying curves in a typical Preisach diagram.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
U>0
be given. A Preisach operator is said to be uniformly counterclockwise convex on
[-U,U]
if for all inputs
u
such that
|u(x,t)|\leq U
a. e., all ascending branches are uniformly convex and all descending branches are uniformly concave.

A regular Preisach operator
G
is called convexifiable if for every
U>0
there exist a uniformly counterclockwise convex Preisach operator
P
on
[-U,U]
, positive constants
g_{*}(U),g^{*}(U),\bar{g}(U)
, and a twice continuously differentiable mapping
g:[-U,U]\to[-U,U]
such that

g(0)=0,\quad 0<g_{*}(U)\leq g^{\prime}(u)\leq g^{*}(U),\quad|g^{\prime\prime}(u)|\leq\bar{g}(U)\ \ \forall u\in[-U,U],

(1.16)

and
G=P\circ g
.

A typical example of a uniformly counterclockwise convex operator is the so-called
Prandtl-Ishlinskii operator
characterized by positive density functions
\rho(x,r)
independent of
v
, see
[
21
, Section 4.2]
. Operators of the form
P\circ g
with a Prandtl-Ishlinskii operator
P
and an increasing function
g
are often used in control engineering because of their explicit inversion formulas, see
[
2
,
3
,
22
]
. They are called the
generalized Prandtl-Ishlinskii operators
(GPI) and represent an important subclass of Preisach operators. Note also that for every Preisach operator
P
and every Lipschitz continuous increasing function
g
, the superposition operator
G=P\circ g
is also a Preisach operator, and there exists an explicit formula for its density, see
[
20
, Proposition 2.3]
. Another class of convexifiable Preisach operators is shown in
[
15
, Proposition 1.3]
.

The technical hypotheses on the permeability function
\kappa
can be stated as follows.

Hypothesis 1.4
.

The permeability
\kappa:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded Lipschitz continuous function, more precisely, there exist constants
\kappa_{*},\kappa^{*},\bar{\kappa}
such that for all
\theta,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and all

0<\kappa_{*}\leq\kappa(x,\theta)\leq\kappa^{*},\quad|\kappa(x_{1},\theta_{1})-\kappa(x_{2},\theta_{2})|\leq\bar{\kappa}\big(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|\big).

Note that even a local solution to Problem (

)–(

and
\!\mathrm{\,div\,}\big(\kappa(x,\theta_{0})|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0}\big)\neq 0
, and we need an initial memory compatibility condition which we state here following
[

. A more detailed discussion on this issue can be found in the introduction to

]
.

.

Let the initial memory
\lambda

, and let
\omega
be the parameter in (

u_{0}
belongs to

and there exist a constant
L>0
and a function

such that, for

\mathop{\mathrm{\,sup\,ess}}_{x\in\Omega}|u_{0}(x)|\leq\Lambda
and the following initial compatibility conditions hold:

\displaystyle\lambda(x,0)

\displaystyle=u_{0}(x)\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\textup{ in }\Omega,

(1.18)

\displaystyle\theta_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=G[u](x,0)=\bar{G}(x)+\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\lambda(x,r)}\rho(x,r,v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\textup{ in }\Omega,

(1.19)

\displaystyle\hskip-45.5244pt\frac{1}{L}\sqrt{\big|\mathrm{\,div\,}\big(\kappa(x,\theta_{0})|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0}\big)\big|}\leq r_{0}(x)\leq\Lambda\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\textup{ in }\Omega,

(1.20)

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\lambda(x,r)

\displaystyle\in\mathrm{\,sign}\Big(\mathrm{\,div\,}\big(\kappa(x,\theta_{0})|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0}\big)\Big)\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\textup{ in }\Omega\mathrm{\ for\ }r\in(0,r_{0}(x)),

(1.21)

\displaystyle\hskip-48.36967pt\omega\kappa(x,\theta_{0}(x))|\nabla u_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{0}\cdot\mathbf{n}(x)+(1-\omega)u_{0}(x)=0\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\textup{ on }\partial\Omega.

(1.22)

Unlike

, here we do not need to assume

together with assumptions (

In the last part of the paper we address the problem of bounded speed of propagation, that is, whether solutions with compactly supported initial data preserve the property of compact support for all

. We show indeed that the wetting front propagates with asymptotically vanishing speed under the following assumptions.

Hypothesis 1.7
.

Let the initial memory
\lambda
be as in Definition

. We assume that there exist constants
R_{1}>R_{0}>0
and
\Lambda>0
such that
\overline{B_{R_{1}}}\subset\Omega
and

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=\lambda(x,r)=0\

\displaystyle\mathrm{\ for\ }\ |x|\geq R_{0},\ r>0,

(1.23)

\displaystyle|u_{0}(x)|\leq\Lambda,\ \lambda(x,r)\leq(\Lambda-r)^{+}\

\displaystyle\mathrm{\ for\ }\ |x|<R_{0},\ r>0.

(1.24)

Theorem 1.8
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
1.6
be satisfied, and additionally assume that Hypothesis
1.7
holds. If
p>3
, the permeability
\kappa>0
is constant, and
\bar{G}
as well as the function
\rho=\rho(r,v)
in (

) are independent of

, then

(i)

There exists

such that the unique solution

to Problem (

)–(

), (

) from Theorem

vanishes in
\Omega\setminus B_{R_{1}}
for

. Moreover, all solutions corresponding to different values of the parameter

coincide in
(0,t_{1})
.

(ii)

This solution can be extended to
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty)
with support contained in the ball
B_{R(t)}
with radius

with a constant

depending only on the data.

Roughly speaking, Theorem

(i) states that the boundary condition on
\partial\Omega
is not active until the moisture front reaches the boundary of
\Omega
.

An upper bound on the size of the support in terms of a power
1/p
of time as in Theorem
1.8
(ii) was obtained in
[
8
]
. The proof relies on the comparison with a traveling wave solution of power-law type involving

and
|x|
. Instead, to prove Theorem

we construct a special family of dominant traveling wave solutions compatible with the hysteresis terms, see Section
4
. Another argument is used in
[
9
, Chapter VI]
, where the comparison is made with the Barenblatt solution, whose support also grows as a power of time, but the power additionally depends on the dimension

Note that although Theorem
1.8
is formulated in terms of the pressure

, it actually determines the maximal size of the wet area (i. e., the support of
\theta
). This is because no hysteresis effects can arise as long as
u
remains equal to zero during the whole history. Therefore, we will continue to refer to the boundary of the support of
u
as the moisture front.

2  Time discretization

Let
\omega\in[0,1]
be given. We proceed as in
[
15
]
, choose a discretization parameter
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the time step
\tau=T/n
, and replace (
1.2
)–(
1.3
) with its time discrete system for the unknowns
\{u_{i}:i=1,\dots,n\}\subset W^{1,p}_{\omega}(\Omega)
of the form

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{\tau}(G[u]_{i}-G[u]_{i-1})\phi+\kappa(x,G[u]_{i})|\nabla u_{i}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla\phi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\gamma_{\omega}\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{i}\phi\,\mathrm{d}s(x)=0

(2.1)

for every test function
\phi\in X_{\omega}
. Here, the time-discrete Preisach operator
G[u]_{i}
is defined for an input sequence
\{u_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}
by a formula of the form (
1.9
), namely,

G[u]_{i}(x)=\bar{G}(x)+\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\xi^{r}_{i}(x)}\rho(x,r,v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r,

(2.2)

where
\xi^{r}_{i}
denotes the output of the time-discrete play operator

\xi^{r}_{i}(x)=\mathfrak{p}_{r}[\lambda,u]_{i}(x)

(2.3)

defined as the solution operator of the variational inequality

|u_{i}(x)-\xi^{r}_{i}(x)|\leq r,\quad(\xi^{r}_{i}(x)-\xi^{r}_{i-1}(x))(u_{i}(x)-\xi^{r}_{i}(x)-z)\geq 0\quad\forall i\in\mathbb{N}\ \ \forall z\in[-r,r],

(2.4)

with a given initial condition

\xi^{r}_{0}(x)=\lambda(x,r)\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}

\displaystyle\kappa(x,\bar{G}^{(n)})\big|\nabla\bar{u}^{(n)}\big|^{p-2}\nabla\bar{u}^{(n)}\cdot\nabla(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u+\delta y)

\displaystyle\hskip 34.1433pt\geq\kappa(x,\bar{G}^{(n)})\big|\nabla(u-\delta y)\big|^{p-2}\nabla(u-\delta y)\cdot\nabla(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u+\delta y),

(3.14)

and
\bar{u}^{(n)}(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u+\delta y)\geq(u-\delta y)(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u+\delta y)
. From (
3.12
) we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma_{\omega}\int_{\partial\Omega}(u-\delta y)(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u+\delta y)\,\mathrm{d}s(x)+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\hat{G}^{(n)}_{t}(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u+\delta y)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\kappa(x,\bar{G}^{(n)})\big|\nabla(u-\delta y)\big|^{p-2}\nabla(u-\delta y)\cdot\nabla(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u+\delta y)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq 0.

(3.15)

The functions
\hat{G}^{(n)}_{t}
are bounded the Orlicz space
L^{\Phi_{log}}(\Omega\times(0,T))
generated by the function
\Phi_{log}
. It can be considered as the dual space to the small Orlicz space
L^{\Phi_{exp}}_{\sharp}(\Omega\times(0,T))
generated by the function
\Phi_{exp}(v)=\hbox{\rm e}^{v}-v-1
. Hence, passing to a subsequence, we conclude that
\hat{G}^{(n)}_{t}
converge weakly* to
G[u]_{t}
in
L^{\Phi_{log}}(\Omega\times(0,T))
(see
[
16
, Section 5]
for more details).
Since
\hat{u}^{(n)}
and
\bar{u}^{(n)}
are uniformly bounded, by the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem they converge strongly in
L^{\Phi_{exp}}_{\sharp}(\Omega\times(0,T))
, and we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\hat{G}^{(n)}_{t}(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u+\delta y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\delta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}G[u]_{t}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.16)

To pass to the limit in the gradient term of (
3.15
), we rewrite it as

\displaystyle\kappa(x,\bar{G}^{(n)})W\cdot\nabla(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u+\delta y)

\displaystyle=\kappa(x,G[u])W\cdot\nabla(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u)+\delta\kappa(x,\bar{G}^{(n)})W\cdot\nabla y

\displaystyle+\big(\kappa(x,\bar{G}^{(n)})-\kappa(x,G[u])\big)W\cdot\nabla(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u)

with
W=\big|\nabla(u-\delta y)\big|^{p-2}\nabla(u-\delta y)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. The functions
\nabla\bar{u}^{(n)}
weakly converge to
\nabla u
in
L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{N})
, hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x,G[u])\,W\cdot\nabla(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

(3.17)

We have, up to a subsequence,
\bar{G}^{(n)}\to G[u]
strongly in every
L^{q}
and pointwise almost everywhere, and it follows from the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x,\bar{G}^{(n)})\,W\cdot\nabla y\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\delta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x,G[u])\,W\cdot\nabla y\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.18)

We estimate the remaining integral using Hölder’s inequality as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\big(\kappa(x,\bar{G}^{(n)})-\kappa(x,G[u])\big)W\cdot\nabla(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\kappa(x,\bar{G}^{(n)})-\kappa(x,G[u])|^{p^{\prime}}|W|^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}},

and using again the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem we conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\big(\kappa(x,\bar{G}^{(n)})-\kappa(x,G[u])\big)W\cdot\nabla(\bar{u}^{(n)}-u)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

(3.19)

Summarizing the above computations and passing to the limit in the boundary term, we see that

\displaystyle\delta\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\left(G[u]_{t}y+\kappa(x,G[u])\big|\nabla(u-\delta y)\big|^{p-2}\nabla(u-\delta y)\cdot\nabla y\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\gamma_{\omega}\int_{\partial\Omega}(u-\delta y)y\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq 0

for every
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
. Dividing the above inequality by
|\delta|
and letting
\delta
tend to
0
, we complete the existence part of the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Assume now that
\kappa=\kappa(x)
is independent of
\theta
, and let
u_{1},u_{2}
be two solutions of (
1.2
)–(
1.3
) with the same initial condition
u_{0}
and the same initial memory
\lambda
. We use the Hilpert trick (see
[
18
]
, see also
[
15
, Proposition 4.3]
) and test the identity

\int_{\Omega}\Big((G[u_{1}]-G[u_{2}])_{t}\phi+\kappa(x)\big(|\nabla u_{1}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{1}-|\nabla u_{2}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{2}\big)\cdot\nabla\phi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x=0

(3.20)

by
\phi=H_{\varepsilon}(u_{1}-u_{2})
, where
H_{\varepsilon}
is the Lipschitz continuous approximation of the Heaviside function defined in (
2.6
). We conclude that
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
a. e., which implies uniqueness.

4  Bounded speed of propagation

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.8
. The idea is to construct a class of explicit solutions
u_{\boldsymbol{e}}
to the PDE

G[u_{\boldsymbol{e}}]_{t}-\kappa\mathrm{\,div\,}(|\nabla u_{\boldsymbol{e}}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\boldsymbol{e}})=0,

(4.1)

with
p>2
and for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty)
, which vanish on some half-space associated with a unit vector
\boldsymbol{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and to show afterward that these solutions are dominant over the solution from Theorem
1.6
.

4.1 Traveling wave solutions

Let
0<R_{0}<R_{1}
be as in Hypothesis
1.7
. We fix
R\in(R_{0},R_{1})
and a unit vector
\boldsymbol{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and define
u_{\boldsymbol{e}}
in the form of a traveling wave

u_{\boldsymbol{e}}(x,t)=U_{c}\big(ct+R-\boldsymbol{e}\cdot x\big)

(4.2)

with a suitable constant speed
c>0
of propagation, where
U_{c}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuously differentiable function depending on
c
such that
U_{c}(z)=0
for
z\leq 0
,
U_{c}^{\prime}(z)>0
for
z>0
. By definition, the function
u_{\boldsymbol{e}}
vanishes in the half-space
\boldsymbol{e}\cdot x\geq R+ct
. Moreover, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the function
t\mapsto u_{\boldsymbol{e}}(x,t)
is nondecreasing, and
u_{\boldsymbol{e}}(x,0)=U_{c}(R-\boldsymbol{e}\cdot x)
. We have

G[u_{\boldsymbol{e}}](x,t)=\Gamma\big(U_{c}\big(ct+R-\boldsymbol{e}\cdot x\big)\big),

(4.3)

where
\Gamma
is the primary wetting curve associated with
G
, that is, according to (
1.13
)–(
1.14
) and the subsequent discussion,

\Gamma(u)=\bar{G}+\int_{0}^{u}\int_{0}^{u-r}\rho(r,v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r=:\bar{G}+\Gamma_{0}(u),

(4.4)

with
\Gamma_{0}(0)=0
. Furthermore,

\nabla u_{\boldsymbol{e}}(x,t)=-\boldsymbol{e}U_{c}^{\prime}\left(ct+R-\boldsymbol{e}\cdot x\right),\quad|\nabla u_{\boldsymbol{e}}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\boldsymbol{e}}=-\boldsymbol{e}\left(U_{c}^{\prime}\left(ct+R-\boldsymbol{e}\cdot x\right)\right)^{p-1}

and

\mathrm{\,div\,}\big(|\nabla u_{\boldsymbol{e}}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\boldsymbol{e}}\big)=\left(\left(U_{c}^{\prime}\left(ct+R-\boldsymbol{e}\cdot x\right)\right)^{p-1}\right)^{\prime},

(4.5)

where the prime denotes the derivative with respect to
z
. Hence, in view of (
4.3
),
u_{\boldsymbol{e}}
is a solution to (
4.1
) if and only if

c\Gamma_{0}(U_{c}(z))=\kappa\big(U_{c}^{\prime}(z)\big)^{p-1}

(4.6)

for all
z\geq 0
, that is

U_{c}^{\prime}(z)=\left(\frac{c}{\kappa}\Gamma_{0}(U_{c}(z))\right)^{1/(p-1)},\qquad U_{c}(0)=0.

(4.7)

If a solution to (
4.7
) exists, then it is unique. Since
\Gamma_{0}
is nonnegative and increasing, we conclude that
U_{c}
is increasing and convex, and
\lim_{z\to\infty}U_{c}(z)=\infty
.

A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a solution to (
4.7
) reads

F(u^{*})\coloneqq\int_{0}^{u^{*}}\Gamma_{0}(u)^{-1/(p-1)}\,\mathrm{d}u<\infty\quad\forall u^{*}>0.

(4.8)

Then

U_{c}(z)=F^{-1}(c^{*}z),\quad c^{*}\coloneqq\left(\frac{c}{\kappa}\right)^{1/(p-1)}.

(4.9)

From (
1.10
) it follows that
\Gamma_{0}(u)\leq\frac{\rho_{1}}{2}u^{2}
. On the other hand, we have for all
u^{*}>0
and
u\in(0,u^{*})
that
\Gamma^{\prime}_{0}(u)\geq u\rho_{0}(u^{*})
, hence

\Gamma_{0}(u)\geq\min\left\{\Gamma_{0}(u^{*}),\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}(u^{*})u^{2}\right\}\ \mbox{ for all }u>0.

(4.10)

By the monotonicity of
\Gamma_{0}
and
\rho_{0}
, we can fix
u^{*}>0
and
\rho_{*}>0
such that
\Gamma_{0}(u^{*})\geq\rho_{*}
,
\rho_{0}(u^{*})\geq 2\rho_{*}
, and obtain from the above computations that

\rho_{*}\left(\frac{u}{1+u}\right)^{2}\leq\Gamma_{0}(u)\leq\frac{\rho_{1}}{2}u^{2}\quad\mbox{ for all }u>0.

(4.11)

The necessary and sufficient condition (
4.8
) for the existence of traveling wave solutions thus holds if and only if
p>3
. Note that by virtue of (
4.11
), for
z
close to
0
we have
U_{c}(z)\approx z^{(p-1)/(p-3)}
,
U_{c}^{\prime}(z)\approx z^{2/(p-3)}
,
(U_{c}^{\prime}(z))^{p-1}\approx z^{2(p-1)/(p-3)}
, so that formula (
4.5
) is meaningful.

Consider now the homogeneous Dirichlet problem on
B_{R_{1}}

\int_{B_{R_{1}}}\Big(G[u]_{t}\phi+\kappa|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x=0

(4.12)

for every test function
\phi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R_{1}})\cap L^{\infty}(B_{R_{1}})
. The existence of a unique solution to (
4.12
) follows from Theorem
1.6
for
\omega=0
and
\Omega=B_{R_{1}}
. We prove the following intermediate result.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let Hypothesis
1.7
be satisfied and let
p>3
. Then there exists
t_{1}>0
such that the solution to Problem (
4.12
), (
0.4
) has compact support in
B_{R_{1}}
for all
t\in(0,t_{1})

Proof.

The strategy is to compare the solution to Problem (
4.12
), (
0.4
) with the traveling wave solution

defined in (

This is indeed possible: by virtue of (

) can be equivalently written as

\left(\frac{c}{\kappa}\right)^{1/(p-1)}(R-R_{0})\geq F(\Lambda),

(4.14)

which is certainly true if

is sufficiently large. Then from (

1.7
we obtain

|u_{0}(x)|\leq U_{c}(R-R_{0})\leq U_{c}(R-\boldsymbol{e}\cdot x)=u_{\boldsymbol{e}}(x,0)

for all
\boldsymbol{e}\in\partial B_{1}
and a. e.

be such that
ct_{1}+(R-R_{0})<R_{1}
. For every test function

\int_{B_{R_{1}}}\Big((G[u]-G[u_{\boldsymbol{e}}])_{t}\phi+\kappa\big(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla u_{\boldsymbol{e}}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\boldsymbol{e}}\big)\cdot\nabla\phi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Let

\big(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla u_{\boldsymbol{e}}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\boldsymbol{e}}\big)\cdot\nabla f(u-u_{\boldsymbol{e}})\geq 0,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(G[u]-G[u_{\boldsymbol{e}}])_{t}H(u-u_{\boldsymbol{e}})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 0,

|u(x,t)|\leq U_{c}\big(ct+R-|x|\big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad U_{c}\big(ct+R-|x|\big)=0\ \mathrm{\ for\ }R_{1}>|x|\geq ct_{1}+R,

4.3 Doubly nonlinear equations and necessity of the condition
p>3

If
2<p\leq 3
the comparison technique developed in the previous two subsections cannot be applied. Obviously, this does not imply that for this range of exponents the speed of propagation is infinite. To fill this gap, in this subsection we gather some observations and draw some connections between our problem and some well-known nonlinear parabolic equations.

The double-sided estimate (
4.11
) indicates that Problem (
0.1
)–(
0.2
) can be compared to the equation

\big(|u|u\big)_{t}-\kappa\mathrm{\,div\,}\big(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\big)=0,

(4.21)

which is an instance of the doubly nonlinear parabolic equation

\big(|u|^{m-1}u\big)_{t}-\kappa\mathrm{\,div\,}\big(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\big)=0

(4.22)

when
m=2
. The case
p-1>m
is commonly known as the
slow diffusion equation
, while the case
p-1<m
is named the
fast diffusion equation
, see
[
19
,
6
]
. The difference between them becomes apparent in the fact that slow diffusion equations allow solutions with compact support and perturbations propagate at finite speed, while in fast diffusion equations perturbations propagate at infinite speed, prohibiting solutions with compact support. When
m=2
as in our case, the critical exponent is exactly
p=3
. On the one hand, this confirms that when
p>3
the speed of propagation has to be finite, which is in agreement with our findings; on the other hand, when
p<3
(or
p=3
) we cannot expect solutions with compactly supported initial data to be compactly supported for any positive time, so the condition
p>3
is also necessary.

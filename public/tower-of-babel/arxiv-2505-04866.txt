A mixed finite element method for a class of fourth-order stochastic evolution equations with multiplicative noise
Beniamin Goldys, Agus L. Soenjaya, Thanh Tran

Abstract
. We develop a fully discrete, semi-implicit mixed finite element method for approximating solutions to a class of fourth-order stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) with non-globally Lipschitz and non-monotone nonlinearities, perturbed by spatially smooth multiplicative Gaussian noise. The proposed scheme is applicable to a range of physically relevant nonlinear models, including the stochastic Landau–Lifshitz–Baryakhtar (sLLBar) equation, the stochastic convective Cahn–Hilliard equation with mass source, and the stochastic regularised Landau–Lifshitz–Bloch (sLLB) equation, among others. To overcome the difficulties posed by the interplay between the nonlinearities and the stochastic forcing, we adopt a ‘truncate-then-discretise’ strategy: the nonlinear term is first truncated before discretising the resulting modified problem. We show that the strong solution to the truncated system converges in probability to that of the original problem. A fully discrete numerical scheme is then proposed for the truncated problem. Assuming initial data in ℍ 2 \mathbb{H}^{2} , we utilise parabolic smoothing estimates and the temporal Hölder continuity of the solution to establish both convergence in probability and strong convergence (with quantitative rates) for the two fields used in the mixed formulation. Numerical simulations are provided to support the theoretical results.



1.
Introduction

Motivated by physical applications, we consider the following fourth-order system of nonlinear SPDEs with non-monotone nonlinearities, perturbed by a spatially smooth multiplicative Gaussian noise:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}=\big(\lambda_{1}\boldsymbol{H}-\lambda_{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{H}-\gamma\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{H}+\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{u})\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\;

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+G(\boldsymbol{u})\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\;

for
(t,\boldsymbol{x})\in(0,T)\times\mathscr{D}
,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}=\Delta\boldsymbol{u}+f(\boldsymbol{u})\;

for
(t,\boldsymbol{x})\in(0,T)\times\mathscr{D}
,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}(0,\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{u}_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})\;

\displaystyle\quad\text{for }\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathscr{D},

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{u}}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}=\boldsymbol{0},\;\displaystyle{\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}=\boldsymbol{0}}\;

\displaystyle\quad\text{for }(t,\boldsymbol{x})\in(0,T)\times\partial\mathscr{D},

where
\mathscr{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\leq 3
, is a bounded regular domain, and
\boldsymbol{u}:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathscr{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a vector-valued random variable. Here,
W
is a real-valued Wiener process on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0})
with respect to the usual filtration, and
G(\boldsymbol{u})
is a Lipschitz function of
\boldsymbol{u}
satisfying certain assumptions (details are elaborated in Section
2.2
). The forcing term
f(\boldsymbol{u}):=\kappa\mu\boldsymbol{u}-\kappa|\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}\boldsymbol{u}
arises from the Ginzburg–Landau theory,
which is the negative variational derivative of
V(\boldsymbol{u}):=\kappa(\left|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right|^{2}-\mu)^{2}/4
, a double-well potential function. Define

\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{u}):=\mathcal{M}(\boldsymbol{u})+\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{u}),

where
\mathcal{M}(\boldsymbol{u})
is a mass source term with at most quadratic growth and
\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{u})
is a convective term given by

(1.2)

\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{u}):=\beta_{1}(\boldsymbol{\nu}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}+\beta_{2}\boldsymbol{u}\times(\boldsymbol{\nu}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u},

where
\boldsymbol{\nu}
is specified in (
2.4
).
All numerical coefficients are non-negative.

The problem (
1.1
) describes various problems in physics. When
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
, and
\gamma
are positive, problem (
1.1
) is the
stochastic Landau–Lifshitz–Baryakhtar
(sLLBar) system with spin current
[
23
,
40
,
43
]
, which can be seen as a Cahn–Hilliard-type regularisation of the
stochastic Landau–Lifshitz–Bloch
(sLLB) equation in micromagnetics
[
7
,
18
,
31
,
39
]
. When
\gamma=0
, (
1.1
) is the stochastic bi-flux reaction-diffusion system
[
5
]
if
\beta_{2}=0
, a stochastic population growth/dispersal model with long-range effects
[
12
]
if
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=0
, the
Cahn–Hilliard-Cook
(CHC) equation
[
29
]
if
\lambda_{1}=\beta_{1}=\mathcal{M}(\boldsymbol{u})=0
(and the noise is additive), the
stochastic convective Cahn–Hilliard equation with mass source
(sCHm)
[
36
,
32
]
if
\lambda_{1}=\beta_{2}=0
, and the
stochastic convective Allen–Cahn/Cahn–Hilliard
(sAC/CH) equation
[
1
]
if
\beta_{2}=0
.

The development of numerical methods for physically relevant SPDEs with non-globally Lipschitz and non-monotone nonlinearities perturbed by multiplicative noise is an active area of research (see e.g.
[
6
,
15
,
26
,
27
]
and many others). As (
1.1
) is a fourth-order equation, a conforming finite element method to solve the equation directly would require
C^{1}
-elements, which can be computationally costly. Numerically treating the problem in mixed form allows us to work with
C^{0}
-conforming finite elements and use the mixed finite element method (see (
3.1
)), at the expense of introducing an auxiliary unknown and performing a more delicate analysis. To the best of our knowledge, no numerical scheme has been proposed for the problem (
1.1
) in its generality, not even for the sLLBar equation (with or without spin current), the sCHm equation, or the sAC/CH equation.

On a related note, several numerical schemes have been proposed in the literature for the CHC equation with
additive
noise, including a
C^{1}
-conforming semi-discrete scheme
[
11
]
, a fully implicit scheme
[
20
]
, and a fully explicit scheme combined with spectral Galerkin method
[
9
]
(see also
[
19
]
for gradient-type noise, where strong convergence of an implicit scheme in
H^{-1}
is shown). Note that even in this setting (
\gamma=\beta_{2}=0
), numerical schemes of implicit/explicit-type proposed here have not been analysed before. Furthermore, adding a mass source and a convective/precession term in (
1.1
) causes a nontrivial difficulty in the analysis due to the non-conservative mass and the loss of gradient flow structure, which is already encountered even in the deterministic case
[
30
,
38
]
.
On the other hand, setting
\lambda_{2}=0
in (
1.1
) gives the sLLB equation. A
C^{1}
-conforming finite element scheme for a regularised version of the sLLB equation (which is simpler than our problem (
1.1
)) is proposed in
[
22
]
. As we can consider (
1.1
) to be a physically relevant regularisation of sLLB, our scheme provides a more practical method to approximate the solution to sLLB by taking
\lambda_{2}
sufficiently small (in light of the convergence result for sLLBar to sLLB in
[
23
, Section 8]
).

Regarding the error analysis, we describe here some difficulties at the discrete level that need to be overcome. To this end, let
A
denote the Neumann Laplacian,
A_{h}
the discrete Laplacian, and
\Pi_{h}
the orthogonal projection onto some finite element space
\mathbb{V}_{h}
. Firstly, loosely speaking, the mixed finite element method aims to approximate
\boldsymbol{u}
and
\boldsymbol{H}
simultaneously, using finite element functions which belong to
\mathbb{H}^{1}
(but not
\mathbb{H}^{2}
). This already makes the analysis of the mixed finite element scheme more challenging than its
C^{1}
-conforming counterpart, even in the deterministic case.
Furthermore, the presence of the finite element projection
\Pi_{h}
in front of the nonlinearities present in (
1.1
) destroys some dissipativity properties of the continuous problem. For instance, while there exists a
C>0
such that for any sufficiently regular function
\boldsymbol{v}
,

\displaystyle-\left\langle f(\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle\geq-C,\text{ and }-\left\langle\nabla f(\boldsymbol{v}),\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle\geq-C\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

we notice that for
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
,

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla\Pi_{h}f(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}),\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right\rangle\not\geq-C\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

As such, moment bounds for
\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}
and
\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}
, where
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
is the finite element approximation of
\boldsymbol{u}
, are difficult to attain.

Similar difficulties are encountered in
[
20
]
and
[
19
]
for a simpler model. In their case, however, these issues could be overcome by exploiting the fact that the (scalar-valued) Cahn–Hilliard–Cook equation is the
H^{-1}
-gradient flow of a Lyapunov functional together with the mass conservation property to derive a moment bound for the
H^{-1}
norm of the finite element solution. This bound could then be bootstrapped and used to derive moment bounds in stronger norms and strong convergence of the scheme in the
H^{-1}
or
L^{2}
norms. However, it is not clear how to adapt such arguments to our case since (
1.1
) is
not
a gradient flow in the presence of the cross product term, the forcing term
f(\boldsymbol{u})
, and the convective term
\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{u})
. Furthermore, these nonlinearities are non-monotone, thus the general results from
[
25
,
34
]
do not apply and the analysis needs to proceed differently here.
We also remark that the nonlinear term
\Delta f(\boldsymbol{u})
is absent in the regularised sLLB model considered in
[
22
]
. In addition, since
C^{1}
-elements are employed there, the complications described in this and the preceding paragraph do not arise in that work.

We outline the approach taken in this paper as follows. To overcome the difficulties mentioned before, while still employing
C^{0}
-conforming elements, we adopt the idea from
[
37
]
in the deterministic case by first truncating the potential function so as to have at most quadratic growth at infinity. Such pointwise truncation is both physically reasonable and a common practice
[
8
,
13
,
16
,
28
,
42
]
. In doing so, the forcing function
f
is approximated by a globally Lipschitz
C^{2}
-smooth function
f_{R}
. We also truncate the mass source
[
32
]
. We show that the strong solution to the problem with truncated potential converges in probability to that of (
1.1
) as the truncation parameter
R\uparrow\infty
. Note that even after these modifications, the problem (
1.1
) is still a system of SPDEs with non-monotone and non-globally Lipschitz nonlinearities due to the cross product term
\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{H}
and the non-variational term
\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{u})
, thus the analysis is not straightforward.

A mixed finite element method is proposed for the truncated problem. In the absence of cross-product terms (corresponding to the sCHm or sAC/CH equations), the resulting fully discrete scheme is of IMEX type. The error analysis is carried out within the variational framework for SPDEs and assumes initial data in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, which guarantees the existence and uniqueness of analytically strong pathwise solutions
[
23
]
. No additional or unrealistic regularity assumptions are imposed. We establish stability estimates of the scheme in strong norms, including bounds on higher moments, and exploit parabolic smoothing properties together with the temporal Hölder continuity of the solution. These ingredients are combined to derive error bounds localised on events of large probability, leading to convergence in probability and strong convergence with quantitative rates for each field in the mixed formulation, following the approach of
[
4
,
10
]
. The main results are stated in Theorem
3.9
, Theorem
3.10
, and Theorem
3.11
.
Numerical experiments to corroborate the theoretical results are described in Section
4
.

-smooth boundary. The function space

. We

The partial derivative

for short. The partial derivative of
f
with respect to time

. The operator

-valued functions with domain

Throughout, we denote the scalar product in a Hilbert space

2.4.
Finite element approximation

Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h>0}
be a family of quasi-uniform triangulations of
\mathscr{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with maximal mesh-size
h
, and let
\mathbb{V}_{h}\subset\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}
be the Lagrange finite element space

\mathbb{V}_{h}:=\{\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C(\overline{\mathscr{D}};\mathbb{R}^{3}):\boldsymbol{\phi}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{3})
denotes the space of linear polynomials on
K
taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Let
T>0
be fixed and
k
be the time-step size. Furthermore, let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
and
\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}
, respectively, be the approximation in
\mathbb{V}_{h}
of
\boldsymbol{u}_{R}(t)
and
\boldsymbol{H}_{R}(t)
at time
t=t_{n}:=nk
, where
n=0,1,2,\ldots,N
and
N=\lfloor T/k\rfloor
.

We begin by defining several operators which will be used in the analysis. Firstly, there exists an orthogonal projection operator
\Pi_{h}:\mathbb{L}^{2}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
such that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

The operator
\Pi_{h}
is stable
[
3
,
14
,
17
]
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}
and
\mathbb{W}^{1,p}
for
p\in[1,\infty)
: there exists a constant
C
independent of
\boldsymbol{v}
such that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left\|{\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{L}^{p},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{W}^{1,p}.

Moreover, it has the following approximation property:

(2.11)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}+h\left\|{\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\leq Ch^{s}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{s,p}},\quad s\in\{1,2\}.

We mainly use (
2.9
), (
2.10
), and (
2.11
) for
p=2
.

Secondly, define the Ritz projection
\mathcal{R}_{h}:\mathbb{H}^{1}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
by

(2.12)

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla\mathcal{R}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\nabla\boldsymbol{v},\nabla\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},\quad\text{such that}\quad\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{1}\right\rangle=0.

The stability and approximation properties of the Ritz projection
[
33
,
35
]
are assumed to hold. In particular, for
p\in(1,\infty)
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}+h\left\|{\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-\mathcal{R}_{h}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{s,p}},\quad s\in\{1,2\}.

Finally, the discrete Laplacian operator
\Delta_{h}:\mathbb{V}_{h}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
is defined by

(2.14)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\nabla\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

Consequently, for any
p,q\in[1,\infty]
such that
1/p+1/q=1
, by Hölder’s inequality we have

(2.15)

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\left\|{\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{q}},

(2.16)

\displaystyle\left\|{\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\left\|{\nabla\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{q}}.

(iii)

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\varphi_{R}
and
f_{R}
be the maps defined in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), respectively. Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
. Then there exists a positive constant
C_{R}
, depending on
R
, such that the following inequalities hold:

(1)

For any
\boldsymbol{v}:\mathscr{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(2.26)

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla f_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}},

(2.27)

\displaystyle\left\|{\Delta f_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}\left(\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2p}}^{2}+\left\|{\Delta\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\right).

(2)

Suppose that
1/p+1/q=1/2
. For any
\boldsymbol{v}:\mathscr{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\boldsymbol{w}:\mathscr{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla f_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})-\nabla f_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{3}\right)\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}-\nabla\boldsymbol{w}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

(2.28)

\displaystyle\quad+C_{R}\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{3}\right)\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{q}}.

Proof.

Firstly, by (
2.17
) and (
2.19
) it is clear that we have

\displaystyle\left|{\nabla f_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|{D\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\left|{f(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|+\left|{\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{2}\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\leq C_{R}\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|,

which implies (
2.26
). Similarly, noting (
2.18
), (
2.20
), and (
2.21
) we have

\displaystyle\left|{\Delta f_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|{\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|\left(\left|{\Delta\boldsymbol{v}}\right|+6\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{2}\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|+3\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{2}\left|{\Delta\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|+\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{3}\right)\left(\left|{D\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|\left|{\Delta\boldsymbol{v}}\right|+d\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{2}\left|{D^{2}\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left|{D\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\left(\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|+3\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{2}\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}\left(\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{2}+\left|{\Delta\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\right),

which implies (
2.27
).
Next, using (
2.19
) again, we have

\displaystyle\left|{\nabla f_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})-\nabla f_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|{\nabla\big[\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})-\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})\big]}\right|\left|{f(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|+\left|{\nabla\big[\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})\big]}\right|\left|{f(\boldsymbol{v})-f(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|

(2.29)

\displaystyle\quad+\left|{\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})-\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|\left|{\nabla\big[f(\boldsymbol{v})\big]}\right|+\left|{\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|\left|{\nabla\big[f(\boldsymbol{v})-f(\boldsymbol{w})\big]}\right|.

We now estimate each term on the right-hand side. Firstly,

\displaystyle\left|{\nabla\big[\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})-\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})\big]}\right|\left|{f(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|{D\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}-\nabla\boldsymbol{w}}\right|\left|{f(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|+\left|{D\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})-D\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\left|{f(\boldsymbol{v})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}\left(1+\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{3}\right)\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}-\nabla\boldsymbol{w}}\right|+C_{R}\left(1+\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{3}\right)\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\left|{\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w}}\right|.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left|{\nabla\big[\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})\big]}\right|\left|{f(\boldsymbol{v})-f(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|{D\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{w}-\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\left|{f(\boldsymbol{v})-f(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|+\left|{D\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\left|{f(\boldsymbol{v})-f(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}\left(1+\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{3}\right)\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}-\nabla\boldsymbol{w}}\right|+C_{R}\left(1+\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{2}\right)\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\left|{\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w}}\right|,

and

\displaystyle\left|{\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})-\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|\left|{\nabla\big[f(\boldsymbol{v})\big]}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}\left(1+\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{2}\right)\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\left|{\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w}}\right|.

Finally, for the last term, we note that

\displaystyle\nabla f(\boldsymbol{v})-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{w})

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla\boldsymbol{v}-\nabla\boldsymbol{w}\right)-2\Big(\boldsymbol{v}((\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w})\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{v})+(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w})(\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{v})+\boldsymbol{w}(\boldsymbol{w}\cdot(\nabla\boldsymbol{v}-\nabla\boldsymbol{w}))\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(((\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w})\cdot(\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{w}))\nabla\boldsymbol{v}+\left|{\boldsymbol{w}}\right|^{2}(\nabla\boldsymbol{v}-\nabla\boldsymbol{w})\right).

This implies

\displaystyle\left|{\varphi_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|\left|{\nabla\big[f(\boldsymbol{v})-f(\boldsymbol{w})\big]}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}-\nabla\boldsymbol{w}}\right|+C_{R}\left(1+\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\right)\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\left|{\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w}}\right|.

Thus, continuing from (
2.29
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left|{\nabla f_{R}(\boldsymbol{v})-\nabla f_{R}(\boldsymbol{w})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}\left(1+\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{3}\right)\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}-\nabla\boldsymbol{w}}\right|+C_{R}\left(1+\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{3}\right)\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right|\left|{\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{w}}\right|,

from which (
2.28
) follows by Hölder’s inequality.
∎

3.
A fully-discrete mixed finite element method

In this section, we propose a mixed finite element method for (
1.1
) with partially implicit Euler time-stepping. We start with
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}=\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}(0)\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
. Let
t_{n}\in[0,T]
, where
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
and
N=\lfloor T/k\rfloor
, given
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
, we find
\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}
-adapted and
\mathbb{V}_{h}\times\mathbb{V}_{h}
-valued random variables
\{(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n})\}
satisfying
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle&=k\left\langle\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle+k\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle-k\gamma\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\times\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle+k\left\langle\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}),\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle\\
&\quad+k\left\langle\mathcal{M}_{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}),\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle+\left\langle G(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}),\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle\overline{\Delta}W^{n},\\
\left\langle\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle&=-\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle+\left\langle f_{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle,\end{aligned}\right.

for all
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
. Here,
\overline{\Delta}W^{n}:=W(t_{n})-W(t_{n-1})\sim\mathcal{N}(0,k)
.

In particular, when
\gamma=\beta_{2}=0
, this is a fully-discrete IMEX-type scheme for the sCHm or the sAC/CH equations. Subsequently, we set
\gamma=1
in the analysis for ease of presentation.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a sequence
\{(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n})\}
of
\mathbb{V}_{h}\times\mathbb{V}_{h}
-valued random variables which solves (
3.1
).

Proof.

Fix
\omega\in\Omega
. We aim to use induction and a form of Brouwer’s fixed point theorem to show the existence of a sequence
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}(\omega)\}_{n=1}^{N}
solving (
3.1
). Suppose that
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}(\omega),\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{1}(\omega),\ldots,\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)
are given. Consider a continuous map
\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\omega}:\mathbb{V}_{h}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\omega}(\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)-k\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-k\Pi_{h}f_{R}\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)\big)+k\Delta_{h}^{2}\boldsymbol{v}+k\Delta_{h}\Pi_{h}f_{R}\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)\big)

\displaystyle\quad+k\boldsymbol{v}\times\left(\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}+\Pi_{h}f_{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega))\right)-k\Pi_{h}\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{v})-k\Pi_{h}\mathcal{M}_{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega))

\displaystyle\quad-\Pi_{h}G(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega))\overline{\Delta}W^{n}(\omega).

For all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
, by Young’s inequality, Lipschitz continuity of
G
,
f_{R}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{R}
, and (
2.26
) we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\omega}(\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+k\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+k\left\|{\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-k\left\langle\Pi_{h}f_{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)),\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-k\left\langle\nabla\Pi_{h}f_{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)),\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle-k\left\langle(\boldsymbol{\nu}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle-k\left\langle\mathcal{M}_{R}(\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\left\langle G(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega))\overline{\Delta}W^{n}(\omega),\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+k\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+k\left\|{\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-2C_{R}k\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\frac{k}{8}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{C_{R}k}{4}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-k\left\|{\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-k\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\frac{k}{4}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-C_{R}k\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-2C_{G}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\left|{\overline{\Delta}W^{n}(\omega)}\right|^{2}-\frac{k}{8}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-k-2C_{R}k\right)\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left(9C_{R}k+8C_{G}\left|{\overline{\Delta}W^{n}(\omega)}\right|^{2}\right)\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Now, suppose that
k<(1+2C_{R})^{-1}
, and set
\beta:=1-k-2C_{R}k>0
. Let

\mathcal{B}_{n}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}:\left\|{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}=4\beta^{-1}\Lambda_{n}(\omega)\right\},

. We omit further details for brevity.
∎

To facilitate the proof of the error estimate, we decompose the error of the numerical method at time
t_{n}
,
n=0,1,\ldots,N
, as:

(3.29)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})-\mathcal{R}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})\right)+\left(\mathcal{R}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right)=:\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n},

(3.30)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}(t_{n})-\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{H}(t_{n})-\mathcal{R}_{h}\boldsymbol{H}(t_{n})\right)+\left(\mathcal{R}_{h}\boldsymbol{H}(t_{n})-\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}\right)=:\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}.

As such by the definition of the Ritz projection (
2.12
),

(3.31)

\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

Furthermore, define a sequence of subsets of
\Omega
which depend on
\kappa
and
m
:

(3.32)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\kappa,m}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\max_{t\leq t_{m}\wedge T}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\max_{t\leq t_{m}\wedge T}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\max_{n\leq{m}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq\kappa\right\},

where
\kappa>0
is to be specified. It is clear that for any
\kappa>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\Omega_{\kappa,m}\supset\Omega_{\kappa,m+1}
. Thus, for any time-discrete random variable
\boldsymbol{v}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\langle\boldsymbol{v}^{\ell}-\boldsymbol{v}^{\ell-1},\boldsymbol{v}^{\ell}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,0}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{\ell=2}^{m}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-2}}-\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\right)\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}^{m-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}^{\ell}-\boldsymbol{v}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

(3.33)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)-\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,0}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}^{\ell}-\boldsymbol{v}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right].

The following technical lemmas will be needed in the analysis.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{H})
be the solution of (
2.7
) with initial data
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathrm{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})
. Suppose that
\{t_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{N}
is a uniform partition of
[0,T]
with
k=T/N
. Then for any
\beta\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
and
\alpha\in(0,1-\beta)
, there exists a constant
C
such that for any
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathrm{D}(A^{\beta})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{2\alpha}.

The constant
C
depends on
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
T
, but is independent of
n
and
k
.

Proof.

From the proof of
[
23
, Lemma 4.10]
, for
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathrm{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})
, we have the following estimate

(3.34)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)-\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathrm{D}(A^{\beta})}^{p}\right]\leq C(t-s)^{\alpha p}s^{-p(\alpha+\beta-\frac{1}{2})},\quad\forall t\geq s>0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathrm{D}(A^{\beta})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}(t_{\ell}-s)^{2\alpha}s^{-2(\alpha+\beta-\frac{1}{2})}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{T}s^{-2(\alpha+\beta-\frac{1}{2})}\mathrm{d}s\leq Ck^{2\alpha},

as required.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{H})
be the solution of (
2.7
) with initial data
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathrm{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})
. Suppose that
\{t_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{N}
is a uniform partition of
[0,T]
with
k=T/N
. Suppose that
X
is a Banach space and
\{\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{N}
is a sequence of functions in
X
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\ell\leq N}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{X}^{4}\right]<\infty.

Then for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, there exists a constant
C
such that for any
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
,

(3.35)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell-1})}\right\|_{\mathrm{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})}^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{X}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{2\alpha}.

Consequently, we have for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,

(3.36)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathrm{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})}^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{X}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{2\alpha}+C\kappa k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{X}^{2}\right].

The constant
C
depends on
\alpha
and
T
, but is independent of
n
and
k
.

Proof.

We have by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell-1})}\right\|_{\mathrm{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})}^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{X}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\ell\leq N}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{X}^{4}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell-1})}\right\|_{\mathrm{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})}^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathrm{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})}^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s+C\left(\sum_{\ell=2}^{n}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell-1})}\right\|_{\mathrm{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})}^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{1}
, we use the fact that
\boldsymbol{u}\in L^{p}\big(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];\text{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}}))\big)
to obtain
I_{1}\leq Ck
.
For the term
I_{2}
, we use (
3.34
) to infer for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\ell=2}^{n}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}(s-t_{\ell-1})^{2\alpha}t_{\ell-1}^{-2\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\leq Ck^{2\alpha}\sum_{\ell=2}^{n}kt_{\ell-1}^{-2\alpha}\leq Ck^{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{T}s^{-2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq Ck^{2\alpha},

thus proving (
3.35
). To show (
3.36
), we write
\boldsymbol{u}(s)=\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell-1})+\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell-1})
, and employ (
3.32
) to note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell-1})}\right\|_{\mathrm{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})}^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{X}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]\leq\kappa k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{X}^{2}\right].

Inequality (
3.36
) then follows by using (
3.35
) and the triangle inequality.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\nabla\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+\epsilon k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+Ce^{C\kappa}\left(h^{2}+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\right).

Finally, we split the stochastic integral in
I_{13}
as

\displaystyle I_{13}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\nabla\Pi_{h}G(\boldsymbol{u}(s))-\nabla\Pi_{h}G(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}),\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}W(s)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\nabla\Pi_{h}G(\boldsymbol{u}(s))-\nabla\Pi_{h}G(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}),\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}W(s)\right]

\displaystyle=:I_{13a}+I_{13b}.

For the first term above, noting the assumptions on
G
, the
\mathbb{H}^{1}
stability of
\Pi_{h}
, and Lemma
3.5
, we apply the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy and the Young inequalities to obtain

\displaystyle I_{13a}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{\nabla G(\boldsymbol{u}(s))-\nabla G(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\max_{m\leq n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+Ce^{C\kappa}\left(h^{2}+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\right).

For the term
I_{13b}
, we use Young’s inequality, Itô’s isometry, the assumptions on
G
, and Lemma
3.5
to obtain

\displaystyle I_{13b}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left\|{\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left(\nabla\Pi_{h}G(\boldsymbol{u}(s))-\nabla\Pi_{h}G(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1})\right)\mathrm{d}W(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=C\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{\nabla\Pi_{h}G(\boldsymbol{u}(s))-\nabla\Pi_{h}G(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\kappa}\left(h^{2}+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\right)+\epsilon\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right].

We now substitute all the above estimates into (
3.48
), set
\epsilon=1/16
, and rearrange the terms.
Altogether, continuing from (
3.48
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\right]+k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\nabla\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

(3.51)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+Ce^{C\kappa}\left(h^{2}+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\right).

Furthermore, note that by setting
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=-k\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}
in (
3.38
), multiplying by
\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}}
and taking expectation, then applying (
2.14
) and (
3.31
), we obtain

\displaystyle k\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=k\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}}\left\langle\nabla\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}\right\rangle\right]+k\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}}\left\langle\nabla\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\quad+k\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}}\left\langle\nabla\Pi_{h}f_{R}(\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n}))-\nabla\Pi_{h}f_{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}),\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ckh^{2}+\epsilon k\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+Ck\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}}\left\|{\nabla\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

(3.52)

\displaystyle\quad+Ck\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}}\left\|{\nabla\Pi_{h}f_{R}(\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n}))-\nabla\Pi_{h}f_{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right],

where in the last step we used Young’s inequality and (
2.13
). The final term in (
3.52
) can be estimated using (
2.28
) as done in (
3.50
). Summing (
3.52
) over
\ell\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and applying (
3.51
), we obtain

\displaystyle k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+Ce^{C\kappa}\left(h^{2}+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\right).

Choosing
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\theta}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]\leq Ch^{2}
, say
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}=\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{0}
, we deduce the required result from the last estimate and (
3.51
).
∎

The following error estimate, which holds over a sample space with large probability, now follows from the above propositions. Indeed, note that by Chebyshev’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega_{\kappa,m}\right]

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{1}{\kappa}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{t\leq t_{m}\wedge T}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{t\leq t_{m}\wedge T}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{n\leq m}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]\right)\geq 1-\frac{C_{R,T}}{\kappa}.

Here,
C_{R,T}
is a constant depending on
R
,
T
, and the coefficients of the equation, which is conferred by Lemma
3.3
and Proposition
2.2
. Therefore,
\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega_{\kappa,m}\right]\to 1
as
\kappa\to\infty
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Proposition
3.7
hold and
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
. For any
\delta>0
, we have

(3.53)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)\right]+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]\leq\widetilde{C}e^{\widetilde{C}\kappa}\left(h^{2}+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\right),

where
\widetilde{C}
is a constant depending on
R
,
T
, and
\delta
, but is independent of
h
and
k
.

Proof.

This follows from Propositions
3.7
and
3.8
, equations (
3.29
) and (
3.30
), estimate (
2.13
), and the triangle inequality.
∎

We now define the following quantities:

(3.54)

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle:=\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2},

(3.55)

\displaystyle\widetilde{A_{n}}

\displaystyle:=\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}^{\complement}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}^{\complement}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}.

By selecting a suitable value of
\kappa
, we establish several convergence results. We begin by stating a theorem regarding the rate of convergence in probability.

Theorem 3.10
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Proposition
3.7
hold and
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
. For any
\delta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{h,k\to 0^{+}}\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\geq\alpha\left(h^{2(1-2\delta)}+k^{\frac{1}{2}(1-8\delta)}\right)\right]=0

for any
\alpha,\delta>0
.

Proof.

By Chebyshev’s inequality and Theorem
3.9
with
\kappa=O\left(\log(\log 1/h)\right)
, for any
\alpha,\delta>0
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\geq\alpha(h^{2(1-2\delta)}+k^{\frac{1}{2}(1-8\delta)})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{-1}\left(h^{2(1-2\delta)}+k^{\frac{1}{2}(1-8\delta)}\right)^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[A_{n}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}^{\complement}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{-1}\left(h^{2(1-2\delta)}+k^{\frac{1}{2}(1-8\delta)}\right)^{-1}\left(h^{2(1-\delta)}+k^{\frac{1}{2}(1-4\delta)}\right)+C_{R,T}\big(\log(\log(1/h))\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}},

which tends to
0
as
h,k\to 0^{+}
.
∎

We now assume that
\beta_{2}=0
to derive a strong order of convergence for the scheme.

Theorem 3.11
.

Suppose that
\beta_{2}=0
. Assume that the hypotheses of Proposition
3.7
hold and
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
. For any
\delta>0
, we have

(3.56)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]\leq C\left|{\log\left(h^{2}+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\right)}\right|^{-r},\quad\forall r\geq 1.

The constant
C
depends on
R
,
T
,
r
, and
\delta
, but is independent of
h
and
k
.
In particular, the right-hand side of (
3.56
) tends to
0
as
h,k\to 0^{+}
.

Proof.

Note that by Hölder’s inequality with exponents
2^{q-1}
and
p=2^{q-1}/(2^{q-1}-1)
, where
q>1
, we have

(3.57)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}^{\complement}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]\leq C\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}^{\complement}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2^{q}}+\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2^{q}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2^{q-1}}}.

Similarly,

(3.58)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}^{\complement}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]\leq C\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}^{\complement}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}(t_{\ell})}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)^{2^{q-1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2^{q-1}}}.

The last terms on the right-hand side of (
3.57
) and (
3.58
) are bounded due to the assumed regularity in Proposition
2.2
and the stability estimate (
3.27
). Therefore, it remains to establish a bound for the probability of the ‘bad’ set
\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}^{\complement}
. To this end, by Chebyshev’s inequality and the definition of the set
\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}^{\complement}\right)\leq\kappa^{-2^{q-1}}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2^{q}}+\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2^{q}}+\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2^{q}}\right)\right],

which implies by the definition (
3.55
),

(3.59)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{A_{n}}\right]\leq C_{q}\kappa^{-2^{q-1}}.

For sufficiently small
h
and
k
, we set

\kappa=\frac{1}{\widetilde{C}}\Big(\big|\log(h^{2}+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta})\big|-(2^{q-1}-1)\log\big|\log(h^{2}+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta})\big|\Big)
,
where
\widetilde{C}
is the constant in (
3.53
). With this choice of
\kappa
, noting (
3.59
), we have by (
3.53
), (
3.57
), and (
3.58
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[A_{n}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{A_{n}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}e^{\widetilde{C}\kappa}\left(h^{2}+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\right)+C_{q}\kappa^{-2^{q-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{r}\left|{\log\left(h^{2}+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\right)}\right|^{-r},

for any
r\geq 1
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

If the initial data
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}

, one can show that the pathwise solution

Remark 3.13
.

The convergence rate established in Theorem
3.11
is likely suboptimal. This is due to the technique of estimating errors on the event
\Omega_{\kappa,m}
, where the error bounds depend exponentially on the truncation parameter
\kappa
. It accounts for the rare possibility of a certain ‘blow-up’ events defined by
\Omega_{\kappa,m}^{\complement}
. Optimising the choice of
\kappa
with respect to
h
and
k
to control the probability of the complement
\Omega_{\kappa,m}^{\complement}
typically results in a reduced theoretical order. However, as illustrated in the numerical experiments (Section
4
), the observed rate of convergence is significantly better, aligning more closely with the results of Theorems
3.9
and
3.10
.

4.
Numerical experiments

We present a set of numerical experiments to validate the theoretical convergence properties of the proposed finite element scheme for the sLLBar equation (
1.1
), with
G(\boldsymbol{u})=\lambda_{1}\boldsymbol{g}-\gamma\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{g}
(where
\boldsymbol{g}
is to be specified) and
\mathcal{M}(\boldsymbol{u})=\boldsymbol{0}
. All computations are carried out in the
FEniCS
environment. The computational domains
\mathscr{D}
are taken to be the unit interval and the unit square. The magnetisation vector field
\boldsymbol{u}
and the effective field
\boldsymbol{H}
are discretised in space using continuous piecewise linear finite elements on a family of quasi-uniform meshes.

To assess convergence, we compute a reference solution on a fine mesh and with a small time step. This reference solution serves as an approximation of the exact stochastic solution for each realisation of the Wiener process. For coarser discretisations, we use the same Brownian path so that the difference between the numerical solution and the reference is meaningful pathwise. The errors
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{\boldsymbol{u}}(h,k)
and
\mathcal{E}_{s}^{\boldsymbol{H}}(h,k)
at final time
T
is then measured in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{H}^{s})
-norm for
s=0
or
1
, defined by

\mathcal{E}_{s}^{\boldsymbol{u}}(h,k):=\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{N}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\text{ref}}(T)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\text{ and }\;\mathcal{E}_{s}^{\boldsymbol{H}}(h,k):=\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|{\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{N}-\boldsymbol{H}_{\text{ref}}(T)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Here,
\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{N},\boldsymbol{H}_{h}^{n}\big)
is the numerical solution with mesh size
h
at time
T=Nk
, and
\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{\text{ref}}(T),\boldsymbol{H}_{\text{ref}}(T)\big)
denotes the reference solution at the same final time along the same realisation of the Brownian motion. In practice, the expectation is approximated by a Monte Carlo average over
M
independent sample paths.

We vary separately the mesh size
h
and the time step
k
to test spatial and temporal convergence. To verify spatial convergence, we fix a sufficiently small time step and compare errors across a sequence of meshes (
h=2^{-j}
for some consecutive values of
j
). For temporal convergence, we fix a fine spatial mesh and vary the time step size (
k=(25\times 2^{j})^{-1}
for some consecutive values of
j
). In both cases, errors are averaged over
M=25
realisations. The experimental rate of convergence is obtained by fitting the errors against mesh size or time step in a log-log plot.

Fundamental groups of compact Kähler manifolds with semi-positive holomorphic sectional curvature
Shin-ichi MATSUMURA

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove that a compact Kähler manifold X X with semi-positive holomorphic sectional curvature admits a locally trivial fibration ϕ : X → Y \phi\colon X\to Y , where the fiber F F is a rationally connected projective manifold and the base Y Y is a finite étale quotient of a torus. This result extends the structure theorem, previously established for projective manifolds, to compact Kähler manifolds. A key part of the proof involves analyzing the foliation generated by truly flat tangent vectors and showing the abelianness of the topological fundamental group π 1 ​ ( X ) \pi_{1}(X) , with a focus on varieties of special type.

1.
Introduction

One of the central problems in geometry is decomposing varieties into basic building blocks to understand their geometric properties better. Building on foundational works
[
Mor79
,
SY80
]
, structure theorems were established in the 1980s and 1990s for varieties with “semi-positive” curvature, including compact Kähler manifolds with semi-positive holomorphic bisectional curvature
[
HSW81
,
MZ86
,
Mok88
]
and varieties with nef tangent bundle
[
CP91
,
DPS94
]
, decomposing them into components with “positive” and “flat” curvature. More recently, these theorems have been extended to semi-positive curvature conditions, such as nef anti-canonical bundles
[
Cao19
,
CH19
,
DB22
,
Zha05
]
,
and pseudo-effective tangent bundles
[
HIM22
,
IMZ
,
Mul
]
,
semi-positive holomorphic sectional curvatures
[
Yan18a
,
HW20
,
Mat22a
]
.
While significant progress has been made for projective varieties,
a complete theory for compact Kähler manifolds is still lacking.

This paper focuses on the notion of
semi-positive holomorphic sectional curvatures
and completes a comprehensive structure theorem for compact Kähler manifolds (see
[
Mat22a
, Section 2]
and references therein for the basic properties of holomorphic sectional curvatures). The main result (see Theorem
1.1
below) resolves
[
Mat22b
, Problem 3.7]

and a question discussed in
[
Ni19
]
, and extends the structure theorem, previously established
for projective manifolds, to compact Kähler manifolds.

We discuss the challenges in proving Theorem
1.1
compared to previous studies.
Conclusion (a) directly follows from Conclusion (b), and
Conclusion (b) was proved in the case where

is a projective manifold (see
[
Mat22a
, Theorem 1.3]
).
In the proof of
[
Mat22a
, Theorem 1.3]
, it is shown that an MRC fibration
X\dashrightarrow Y

can be chosen to satisfy the desired properties in Theorem
1.1
.
The smooth base
Y
of an MRC fibration is non-uniruled by
[
GHS03
]
,
and thus the canonical bundle
K_{Y}
is pseudo-effective by
[
BDPP13
]

when

(and consequently
Y
) is projective.
The pseudo-effectivity of
K_{Y}
is a key point in the proof,
but this property remains an open problem when
X
is a compact Kähler manifold,
which presents a primary difficulty in proving Conclusion (b).

Conclusion (b) might be deduced from Conclusion (a).
However, this approach is not straightforward, and proving Conclusion (a) directly is also challenging.
Similar results have been established for compact Kähler manifolds with nef anti-canonical bundles (see
[
Pău97
, Théorème 2]
,
[
Pău17
, Theorem 1.4]
,
[
Cam95
, Theorem 2.2]
), but adapting these methods to the context of holomorphic sectional curvatures is highly non-trivial. Even for projective manifolds, the proof of Conclusion (a) relies on Conclusion (b).

We outline the proof of Theorem
1.1
, keeping the aforementioned challenges in mind. Let
(X,g)
be a compact Kähler manifold equipped with a Kähler metric
g
with semi-positive holomorphic sectional curvature. The basic strategy is to consider the Albanese map
\alpha\colon X\to Y:=\operatorname{Alb}(X)
instead of MRC fibrations. As mentioned earlier, since
K_{Y}
is a trivial line bundle (and thus pseudo-effective), the Albanese map satisfies all properties in Conclusion (b) except for property
\rm{(b_{3})}
. Therefore, our goal is to prove that the fiber
F
of the Albanese map
\alpha\colon X\to Y
is a rationally connected projective manifold.

If
X
is rationally connected, the augmented irregularity
\hat{q}(X)
is clearly zero. Conversely, if Theorem
1.1
holds, we can deduce that when the augmented irregularity
\hat{q}(X)
vanishes, the manifold
X
is a rationally connected projective manifold. Based on this observation, we divide the proof into two steps:

(1)

Show that the fiber
F
has vanishing augmented irregularity:
\hat{q}(F)=0
.

(2)

Show that
X
with
\hat{q}(X)=0
is a rationally connected projective manifold.

The desired property
\rm{(b_{3})}
can be obtained
by applying the result of Step (2) to the fiber
F
that satisfies
the conclusion
\hat{q}(F)=0
of Step (1).

Step (1) will be discussed in Section
3
. To achieve Step (1), we assume that
X
and
F
attain their augmented irregularities (i.e.,
q(X)=\hat{q}(X)
and
q(F)=\hat{q}(F)
) by replacing
X
with an appropriate finite étale cover. We then consider the relative Albanese map
\beta\colon X\to Z:=\operatorname{Alb}(X/Y)
with the diagram:

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{Z=\operatorname{Alb}(X/Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma}
\textstyle{Y=\operatorname{Alb}(X).}

The fiber
G
of
\gamma\colon Z\to Y
is
isomorphic to the Albanese torus
\operatorname{Alb}(F)
of
F
,
and thus the canonical bundle
K_{G}
of
G
is trivial.
Furthermore, since the canonical bundle
K_{Y}
of the base
Y

is pseudo-effective, the theory of variation of Bergman kernels shows that
K_{Z}
is pseudo-effective. Therefore, we conclude that
Z
is a finite étale quotient of a torus. If
\dim Z>\dim Y
, this contradicts the definition of the Albanese map of
X
. This shows that
\dim Z=\dim Y
, implying that the irregularity
q(F)
(and thus the augmented irregularity
\hat{q}(F)
) is zero.

Step (2) will be discussed in Section
3
. To achieve Step (2),
we focus on the notion of varieties of special type introduced by Campana.
The structure theorem for projective manifolds shows that
X
is rationally connected if it is projective. If
X
is not projective, there exists a non-trivial holomorphic
2
-form
\eta
on
X
. Then, by
[
ZZ
, Theorem 1.4]
, the tangent bundle
\mathrm{T}_{X}
splits into
V\oplus W
such that the tangent vectors in
V
are truly flat.
This result is crucial for our argument.
This shows that
V
is a flat vector bundle, and thus, we can take
the
\operatorname{GL}
-representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\operatorname{GL}(r\mathord{:}\,\mathbb{C})
associated with the flat bundle
V
. We show that
\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho
is finite, equivalently,
the flat vector bundle
V
is étale trivializable (i.e., there exists a finite étale cover
\nu\colon X^{\prime}\to X
such that
\nu^{*}V
is trivial). This implies that some finite étale cover of
X
admits a holomorphic
1
-form, contradicting our assumption
\hat{q}(X)=0
.
While it is generally difficult to directly prove that
\pi_{1}(X)
is finite, it suffices for our purposes to show that
\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho
is finite. With this in mind, we prove that
\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho
is virtually abelian by showing that
X
is a variety of special type (see Theorem
2.1
). Since the abelianization of
\pi_{1}(X)
is finite due to
\hat{q}(X)=0
, the universality of the abelianization guarantees that
\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho
is finite.

We conclude with remarks on recent developments in holomorphic sectional curvatures.
The approach used in this paper is somewhat algebraic-geometric.
It would be of interest to explore a more differential-geometric approach, such as using holonomy groups or the de Rham decomposition, as suggested in
[
ZZ
, Theorem 1.4, Remark 1.6]
.
Although the structure theorem presented here is powerful, several open problems remain in the study of holomorphic sectional curvatures.
For instance, determining when a manifold admits (semi-)positive holomorphic sectional curvature remains challenging, even for rationally connected manifolds. For example, while the Hirzebruch surfaces are known to admit positive holomorphic sectional curvature
[
Hit75
]
(see
[
AHZ18
,
HC20
]
), it is not known whether its blow-up does (see
[
Mat20
, Problem 3.8]
).
Holomorphic sectional curvatures continue to evolve in connection with other notions of curvature and positivity, though these aspects were not the focus of this paper.
For further details, see
[
Tan
,
CCZ
,
Ni19
,
Ni21
,
NZ22
,
Yan18b
]
and references therein.

Note that after we finished writing this paper, the result of
[
BDPP13
]
was generalized to Kähler manifolds by
[
Ou
]
.
However, we believe that our methods, which focus on the notion of special varieties, provide a different perspective and are worth presenting.

Notation and Conventions

We use the terms “invertible sheaves” and “line bundles” interchangeably and adopt the additive notation for tensor products (e.g.,
L+M:=L\otimes M
for line bundles
L
and
M
). Additionally, we use the terms “locally free sheaves” and “vector bundles” interchangeably. The term “fibrations” refers to a proper surjective morphism with connected fibers and the term “finite étale covers” refers to an unramified finite surjective morphism.

2.
Varieties of special type

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.5
. To this end, we first show that any compact Kähler manifolds with semi-positive holomorphic sectional curvature belong to the class of
varieties of special type
, as introduced by Campana. This result is proved by refining the argument in
[
Mat20
,
Mat22a
]
, and we provide the relevant details here for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold admitting a Kähler metric
g
with semi-positive holomorphic sectional curvature.
Then, the manifold
X
is of special type in the sense of Campana.

Definition 2.2

(
[
Cam04
, Definitions 1.19, Proposition 1.25, Definitions 2.1, Theorem 2.22]
)
.

(1) A compact Kähler manifold
X
is said to be
of special type
in the sense of Campana
if
X
does not admit any almost holomorphic dominant rational map
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
of general type.

(2) A dominant rational map
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
is said to be
of general type
if
some (equivalently, any) resolution
\bar{\phi}\colon\Gamma\to Y
of its indeterminacies
satisfies that

\kappa\big(\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}({\bar{\phi}}^{*}K_{Y})_{{\rm{sat}}}\big)\geq m:=\dim Y>0,

where

\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}({\bar{\phi}}^{*}K_{Y})_{{\rm{sat}}}\subset\Omega_{\Gamma}^{m}

is the saturation of the invertible subsheaf
defined by the pull-back of the canonical bundle
K_{Y}

in the
m
-th exterior product
\Omega_{\Gamma}^{m}
of the cotangent bundle
\Omega_{\Gamma}
.
Here
\kappa(\bullet)
denotes the Kodaira dimension.

Proof.

Let
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
be an almost holomorphic dominant rational map, and let
\bar{\phi}\colon\Gamma\to Y
be a resolution of its indeterminacies with a bimeromorphic morphism
\tau\colon\Gamma\to X
, as shown in the following diagram:

\textstyle{\Gamma\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau}
\scriptstyle{\bar{\phi}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{Y.}

Set
m:=\dim Y>0
. In
[
Mat22a
, Theorem 3.1]
, we showed that if the canonical bundle
K_{Y}
is pseudo-effective, then the invertible sheaf

L:=\big(\tau_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(\bar{\phi}^{*}K_{Y}))\big)^{**}

defined by the reflexive hull (the double dual) is a Hermitian flat line bundle (equivalently,
c_{1}(L)=0
)
(see
[
Mat22a
, page 767]
). This implies that the Kodaira dimension of
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(\bar{\phi}^{*}K_{Y})
cannot be greater than or equal to one.

By essentially the same argument, we can show that if the saturation

\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(\bar{\phi}^{*}K_{Y})_{{\rm{sat}}}\subset\Omega_{\Gamma}^{m}

of the pull-back
\bar{\phi}^{*}K_{Y}

in the
m
-th exterior product
\Omega_{\Gamma}^{m}
is pseudo-effective, then the invertible sheaf

M:=\big(\tau_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(\bar{\phi}^{*}K_{Y})_{{\rm{sat}}})\big)^{**}

is also a Hermitian flat line bundle.
Consequently, the Kodaira dimension of
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(\bar{\phi}^{*}K_{Y})_{{\rm{sat}}}
cannot be greater than or equal to one.
Indeed, in proving the above conclusion for
L
, we used only the properties that
L
is a pseudo-effective invertible subsheaf of
\Omega^{m}_{X}
,
and that
L
coincides with the pull-back
\phi^{*}K_{Y}
on
X_{1}
, where
X_{1}
is the preimage of a Zariski open set
Y_{1}\subset Y
such that

\phi\colon X_{1}:=\phi^{-1}(Y_{1})\to Y_{1}

is a smooth fibration. These properties were used in
[
Mat22a
, Claim 3.5]
via
[
Mat22a
, (3.1)]
. Although
[
Mat22a
, (3.1)]
is formulated on
X_{0}
(the locus where
\phi
is holomorphic), it suffices if it holds on
X_{1}
. The invertible sheaf
M
satisfies the first property if the saturation
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(\bar{\phi}^{*}K_{Y})_{{\rm{sat}}}
is pseudo-effective.
Furthermore, we have
M=\phi^{*}K_{Y}
on
X_{1}
, since

\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(\bar{\phi}^{*}K_{Y})\subset\Omega_{\Gamma}^{m}

is a sub-line bundle over the smooth locus
Y_{0}
of
\phi\colon X\to Y
without taking saturation and

\tau\colon\Gamma\to X
is an isomorphism over
Y_{0}
.
Therefore, the same argument applied to
M
shows that

M=(\tau_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(\bar{\phi}^{*}K_{Y})_{{\rm{sat}}}))^{**}

is a Hermitian flat line bundle, just like
L
.

We will apply the conclusion mentioned above to show that
X
is of special type. By the definition of varieties of special type, we can take an almost holomorphic dominant rational map
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
and a bimeromorphic morphism
\tau\colon\Gamma\to X
admitting a fibration
\bar{\phi}\colon\Gamma\to Y
of general type. According to the definition of fibrations of general type, the Kodaira dimension
\kappa(\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(\bar{\phi}^{*}K_{Y})_{{\rm{sat}}})
is equal to
\dim Y>0
.
In particular, the line bundle
M
is pseudo-effective.
The conclusion mentioned above shows that
M
is a Hermitian flat line bundle.
This contradicts the fact that
\kappa(\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(\bar{\phi}^{*}K_{Y})_{{\rm{sat}}})
is greater than or equal to one.
Therefore, the manifold
X
does not admit any almost holomorphic dominant rational map
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
of general type.
∎

The fundamental group of a variety of special type is conjectured to be virtually abelian
[
Cam04
, Conjecture 7.1]
. This conjecture remains a challenging problem. Nevertheless, we can establish that the image of the
\operatorname{GL}
-representation of the fundamental group is virtually abelian. This weaker assertion, which is sufficient for our purpose, is proved in
[
Cam04
]
with support from the profound results in
[
Zuo99
,
Mok92
,
CCE15
]
. Furthermore,
by the following corollary, we confirm that the Albanese map
\phi\colon X\to Y:=\operatorname{Alb}(X)
satisfies the desired properties listed in Theorem
1.1
, except for property
\rm{(b_{3})}
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact Kähler manifold admitting a Kähler metric
g
with semi-positive holomorphic sectional curvature.
Then, the following statements hold
:

\rm{(1)}

Let
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\operatorname{GL}(r\mathord{:}\,\mathbb{C})
be
a
\operatorname{GL}
-representation of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X)
.
Then, the image
\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho
is a virtually abelian group.

\rm{(2)}

The Albanese map
\phi\colon X\to Y:=\operatorname{Alb}(X)

satisfies the properties listed in Theorem
1.1
except for property
\rm{(b_{3})}

Furthermore, the fiber
F
of
\phi\colon X\to Y=\operatorname{Alb}(X)
admits a Kähler metric
g_{F}

with semi-positive holomorphic sectional curvature.

Proof.

Conclusion (1) is a direct consequence of
[
Cam04
, Theorem 7.8]
and Theorem
2.1
.

Conclusion (2) has been implicitly proved in
[
Mat22a
, Theorem 1.6]
. For the reader’s convenience, we will specify the relevant parts of the proof. The Albanese map
X\to Y:=\operatorname{Alb}(X)
is a fibration by
[
Cam04
, Theorem 7.4]
. Thus, by
[
Mat22a
, Theorem 1.6]
, there exists a holomorphic orthogonal splitting of the tangent bundle
T_{X}
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle T_{X}\cong T_{X/Y}\oplus\phi^{*}T_{Y}.

The subbundle
\phi^{*}T_{Y}\subset T_{X}
is an integrable foliation (see
[
Mat22a
, page 772]
), indicating that
X\to\operatorname{Alb}(X)
is a locally constant fibration (see, for example,
[
Mat22a
, Lemma 2.5]
). Moreover, any tangent vectors in
\phi^{*}T_{Y}\subset T_{X}
are truly flat (see
[
Mat22a
, page 772]
). Thus, by the holomorphic splitting and the Gauss-Codazzi type formula (see
[
Mat22a
, (2.2)]
), the induced metric
g_{T_{X/Y}}
on
T_{X/Y}
satisfies

\displaystyle\big\langle\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{g}(e,\bar{e})(e),e\big\rangle_{g}

\displaystyle=\big\langle\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{g_{T_{X/Y}}}(e,\bar{e})(e),e\big\rangle_{g_{T_{X/Y}}}

for any tangent vector
e\in T_{X/Y}\subset T_{X}
.
Here
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}
denotes the Chern curvature of a Hermitian vector bundle
(E,h)
:

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}:=\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}(E)\in C^{\infty}(X,\Lambda^{1,1}\otimes\operatorname{End}(E)),

and
\langle\bullet,\bullet\rangle_{h}
is the Hermitian inner product with respect to
h
. See
[
Mat22a
, Section 2]
for the precise notation and definition of truly flat tangent vectors. The left-hand side equals the holomorphic sectional curvature
H_{g}([f])
evaluated at
f
, which is semi-positive by assumption. This shows that the holomorphic sectional curvature of the induced metric
g_{F}:=g_{T_{X/Y}}|_{F}
on
T_{F}\cong T_{X/Y}|_{F}
is semi-positive.
∎

As explained in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, the splitting (
2.1
) is derived from the existence of a pseudo-effective invertible subsheaf
L\subset\Omega^{m}_{X}
with the geometric property that
L=\phi^{*}K_{Y}
holds on

. In fact, as shown by an excellent result in
[

, a splitting of the tangent bundle

can be obtained solely from the existence of a pseudo-effective invertible subsheaf

, without requiring the geometric condition. This result extends a generalization of Yau’s conjecture to Kähler manifolds in the quasi-positive case (see
[
ZZ
, Theorem 1.5]
) and plays a crucial role in this paper.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
ZZ
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact Kähler manifold equipped with a Kähler metric
g
with semi-positive holomorphic sectional curvature.
Suppose that there exists a pseudo-effective invertible subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset\Omega_{X}^{m}
.
Then, the tangent bundle
T_{X}
admits a holomorphic orthogonal decomposition

T_{X}\cong V\oplus W

such that
V
is a nonzero flat vector bundle

(
i.e., there exists a
\operatorname{GL}
-representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\operatorname{GL}(r\mathord{:}\,\mathbb{C})

of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X)
that determines
V
)
.

Proof.

By
[
ZZ
, Theorem 1.4]
, there exists a holomorphic orthogonal decomposition

T_{X}\cong V\oplus W
such that
V
is a nonzero vector bundle and
any tangent vectors in
V\subset T_{X}
are truly flat.
In the same way as in the proof of Corollary
2.3
,
due to the holomorphic splitting, the induced metric
g_{V}
on
V
satisfies

\displaystyle\big\langle\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{g}(v,\bar{v})(f),f\big\rangle_{g}

\displaystyle=\big\langle\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{g_{V}}(v,\bar{v})(f),f\big\rangle_{g_{V}}

for any tangent vector
f\in V\subset T_{X}
and any tangent vector
v\in T_{X}
.
We can see that the left-hand side equals zero since
f\in V\subset T_{X}
is a truly flat tangent vector.
Hence, the induced metric on
V
is Hermitian flat, and thus
V
is a flat vector bundle.
∎

Based on the above preparations, we now proceed to prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold admitting a Kähler metric
g
with semi-positive holomorphic sectional curvature.
Consider the augmented irregularity

\hat{q}(X):=\sup\{q(X^{\prime})\,|\,X^{\prime}\to X\text{ is a finite \'{e}tale cover}\}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\},

where

q(X^{\prime}):=\dim H^{0}(X^{\prime},\Omega^{1}_{X^{\prime}})

is the irregularity of
X^{\prime}
.
Then, the following statements hold
:

\rm{(1)}

The inequality
\hat{q}(X)\leq\dim X
holds.
In particular, there exists a finite étale cover
X^{\prime}\to X
such that
q(X^{\prime})=\hat{q}(X)
holds.

\rm{(2)}

If the augmented irregularity
\hat{q}(X)
vanishes,
then
X
is a rationally connected projective manifold.

Proof.

For any finite étale cover
\nu\colon X^{\prime}\to X
, the Kähler metric
\nu^{*}g
defined by the pull-back has semi-positive holomorphic sectional curvature. Hence, the Albanese map
\phi\colon X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}:=\operatorname{Alb}(X^{\prime})
is a locally constant fibration (in particular, surjective)
by Corollary
2.3
. This shows that

q(X^{\prime})=\dim Y^{\prime}\leq\dim X^{\prime}=\dim X.

This argument proves Conclusion (1).

We now consider Conclusion (2). Once we know that
X
is a projective manifold, its rational connectedness is easily verified. Indeed, since Theorem
1.1
has been proved for projective manifolds, there exists an MRC fibration
X\to Y
with the properties in Theorem
1.1
. Then, we can easily see that
\dim Y=\hat{q}(X)
. Hence, by the assumption
\hat{q}(X)=0
, we conclude that
Y
is a single point. This means that
X
itself is the fiber of the MRC fibration (i.e., it is rationally connected).

Suppose now that
X
is not projective. Then, there exists a non-zero holomorphic
2
-form
\eta
on
X
. Otherwise, the ample cone would coincide with the Kähler cone, which is non-empty, implying that
X
is projective (see, for example,
[
Huy05
, Proposition 3.3.2 and Corollary 5.3.3]
).
We consider the reflexive hull

\mathcal{F}:=(\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{O}_{X}})^{**}

of the sheaf
\langle\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}
generated by
\eta
.
This sheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset\Omega_{\Gamma}^{2}
is an effective invertible subsheaf

\mathcal{F}\subset\Omega_{X}^{2}
. Thus, by Theorem
2.4
, the tangent bundle
T_{X}
admits a holomorphic orthogonal decomposition
T_{X}\cong V\oplus W
such that
V
is a nonzero flat vector bundle. Let
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\operatorname{GL}(r\mathord{:}\,\mathbb{C})
be a
\operatorname{GL}
-representation that determines the flat vector bundle
V
, where
r:=\operatorname{rank}V>0
.

We will show that we may assume that
\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho
is an abelian group. By Corollary
2.3
, there exists an abelian normal subgroup
A\subset\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho
of finite index.
We consider the étale cover
X^{\prime}\to X
corresponding to the kernel of the composition

\pi_{1}(X)\to\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho\to\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho/A.

The cover
\nu\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is a finite étale cover since
\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho/A
is a finite group. Note that

0\leq\hat{q}(X^{\prime})\leq\hat{q}(X)=0

holds by assumption.
We now consider the induced
\operatorname{GL}
-representation

\rho^{\prime}\colon\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})\to\pi_{1}(X)\xrightarrow{\rho}\operatorname{GL}(r\mathord{:}\,\mathbb{C}).

By construction, the image
\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho^{\prime}
is contained in
A
(in particular, it is an abelian group). Furthermore, we still have the holomorphic orthogonal splitting

(2.2)

\displaystyle T_{X^{\prime}}=\nu^{*}T_{X}\cong\nu^{*}V\oplus\nu^{*}W

and the
\operatorname{GL}
-representation
\rho^{\prime}\colon\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})\to\operatorname{GL}(r\mathord{:}\,\mathbb{C})
determines the flat subbundle

\nu^{*}V\subset\nu^{*}T_{X}=T_{X^{\prime}}.

Note that if
X^{\prime}
is projective, so is
X
.
Therefore, after replacing
X
with the finite étale cover
X^{\prime}
,
we may assume that the image
\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho
is an abelian group.

The first homology group
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
can be regarded as the abelianization of
\pi_{1}(X)
. Since
\dim H^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})=0
holds by the assumption
q(X)=0
and the Kählerness of
X
, the first homology group
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is a torsion group (in particular, it is a finite group). Since
\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho
is an abelian group, the universality of abelianizations shows that
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\operatorname{GL}(r\mathord{:}\,\mathbb{C})
factors through

\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{GL}(r\mathord{:}\,\mathbb{C}).

This implies that
\operatorname{Im}\hskip-0.85355pt\rho
is a finite group, which means that
V
is étale trivializable (i.e., there exists a finite étale cover
\nu\colon X^{\prime}\to X
such that the pull-back

\nu^{*}V
is a trivial vector bundle on
X^{\prime}
).
Thus, by noting the splitting as in (
2.2
),
we can see that the cover
X^{\prime}
admits a non-zero
1
-form by

\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}^{\oplus r}\cong(\nu^{*}V)^{*}\subset\Omega^{1}_{X^{\prime}}.

This contradicts the assumption
\hat{q}(X)=0
.
∎

3.
Proof of the main result

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. To achieve this, we examine the Albanese map
X\to Y:=\operatorname{Alb}(X)
in detail and prove that the fiber
F
of
X\to Y=\operatorname{Alb}(X)
has vanishing augmented irregularity
\hat{q}(F)=0

to apply Theorem
2.5
.
We begin with an elementary lemma, which is well-known to experts, but we provide proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooth fibration between smooth varieties
and
X\xrightarrow{g}Z\xrightarrow{\sigma}Y
be its Stein factorization:

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
\textstyle{Y.}

Then, the variety
Z
is smooth, the morphism
\sigma\colon Z\to Y
is a finite étale cover,
and the morphism
g\colon X\to Z
be a smooth fibration.

Proof.

The problem is local in
Y
, and thus, we may assume that
Y
is an open ball. Let
z
be a smooth point of
Z
, and take a point
x\in X
such that
z=g(x)
. We consider the differential maps
df
,
dg
, and
d\sigma

defined on the tangent bundles around
x
and
z=g(x)
.
Since
f\colon X\to Y
is a smooth morphism, the differential map
df_{x}=d\sigma_{z}\circ dg_{x}
is surjective. In particular, the differential map
d\sigma_{z}
is surjective, which shows that
\sigma\colon Z\to Y
is a finite étale cover
on the smooth locus
Z_{{\rm{reg}}}
. This implies that the pushforward
\sigma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
is a trivial vector bundle on
Y\setminus\sigma(Z_{{\rm{sing}}})
. Indeed, we consider the decomposition of the complement

Y\setminus\sigma(Z_{{\rm{sing}}})

into connected components
V_{i}
:

Y\setminus\sigma(Z_{{\rm{sing}}})=\amalg_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}.

We can observe that
\operatorname{codim}\sigma(Z_{{\rm{sing}}})\geq 2
holds since
Z
is normal and
\sigma\colon Z\to Y
is finite, and that
Y\setminus\sigma(Z_{{\rm{sing}}})
is simply connected since
Y
is smooth and simply connected.
The morphism
\sigma|_{V_{i}}\colon V_{i}\to Y\setminus\sigma(Z_{{\rm{sing}}})
is an étale cover with the connected
V_{i}
, and thus it must be an isomorphism. This shows that

\sigma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}|_{Y\setminus\sigma(Z_{{\rm{sing}}})}=\oplus_{i=1}^{d}\mathcal{O}_{V_{i}}\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{d}\mathcal{O}_{Y\setminus\sigma(Z_{{\rm{sing}}})}.

On the other hand, by the flatness of
f\colon X\to Y
, we can see that

\sigma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}=f_{*}g^{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}=f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}

is a reflexive sheaf. By the reflexivity of
\sigma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
and the fact that
\operatorname{codim}\sigma(Z_{{\rm{sing}}})\geq 2
, we can see that
\sigma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
is the trivial locally free sheaf on
Y
. This implies that
Z
is smooth and
\sigma\colon Z\to Y
is a finite étale cover. We can also see that
g
is smooth by
df_{x}=d\sigma_{z}\circ dg_{x}
.
∎

We now proceed to prove the main result of this paper.

Proof of Theorem

.

We first check that it is sufficient to prove both Conclusions (a) and (b)
after replacing

with its finite étale cover

.
Indeed, this is obvious for Conclusion (a).
Following the argument in

Suppose that there exists a finite étale cover

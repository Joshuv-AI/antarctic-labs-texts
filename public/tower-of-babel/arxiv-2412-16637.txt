Introduction

The Ramsey number
R_{k}(l,m)
is the minimum
N
with the property that any colorings of
k
-tuples of
\{1,2,\dots,N\}
by red and blue yields either an
l
-set with all its
k
-subsets colored red or an
m
-set with all its
k
-subsets colored blue.

Classic results of Erdős and Szekeres
[
7
]
imply that

2^{m/2}\leq R_{2}(m,m)\leq 2^{2m}.

Several improvements have been made and while the best lower bound in
[
14
]
is still of order
2^{(1+o(1))m/2}
, the best upper bound proved recently in
[
1
]
is
(4-\epsilon)^{m}
for some
\epsilon>0
.

For
k\geq 3
, another classic result of Erdős and Rado
[
6
]
asserts

\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits_{k-1}(c_{1}m^{2})\leq R_{k}(m,m)\leq\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits_{k}(c_{2}m),

(1)

where
c_{1},c_{2}
are positive constants (independent of
m
and
k
). While the upper bound holds for every
m\geq k+1
, the lower bound was only proved for
m
sufficiently large w.r.t.
k
.
The exponential gap between lower and upper bounds (
1
) is a well-known difficult problem in the area. The lower bound in (
1
) is proved by induction. Starting with the probabilistic lower bound on
R_{3}(m,m)
, the induction argument is based on Erdős-Hajnal
stepping-up lemma
that allows one to bound
R_{k+1}(m,m)
in terms of
R_{k}(m^{\prime},m^{\prime})
for some
m^{\prime}
depending on
m

[
5
,
8
]
. The stepping-up lemma has been improved by Conlon, Fox, and Sudakov in
[
2
]
. While the original version required the restriction
m\geq m_{0}(k)
with
m_{0}(k)
exponential in
k
, the improved version from
[
2
]
can be applied as long as
m\geq\frac{5}{2}k+4
.

For

, the stepping-up lemma so far does not work. Some improvements are also known for off-diagonal Ramsey numbers.
A recent results in this direction is the bound

R_{k}(k+1,m)\geq\mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits_{k-2}(m^{c\log m}),

where

Lower bounds on
k
-hypegraph Ramsey numbers can also be proved using lower bounds on Ramsey numbers for ordered paths in the shift graph on
k
-sets. Let’s denote by
P_{k}(m,n)
the minimum number
N
such that any two-coloring of
k
-sets yields an ordered path of length

in the first color, or an ordered path of length

in the second color in the shift graph of
k
-sets. Clearly,

P_{k}(m,n)\leq R_{k}(k+m-1,k+n-1).

(2)

Moshkovitz and Shapira developed a method by which one can compute the Ramsey numbers of ordered paths
P_{k}(m,n)
. In
[
10
]
they considered the diagonal case, which was subsequently generalized to the of-diagonal path Ramsey numbers by Milans, Stolee, and West
[

. Their method together with inequality (

) enables one to prove lower bounds on the Ramsey numbers. The bounds obtained in this way are smaller than those obtained by the stepping-up lemma, but the method works in the range where the stepping-up lemma fails. However, for
R_{k}(k+1,k+1)
this method fails because
P_{k}(2,2)\leq 2k+1
.

In this paper we will prove that
R_{k}(k+1,k+1)
is lower-bounded by a tower of height approximately
k/4
. To this end we will use colorings of shift graphs by three colors. The proof is based on an idea that we used in
[
13
]
and a lemma about a certain hypergraph. We use a coloring of this hypergraph by two colors. We have found this coloring using a computer and we do not have an explicit formula defining it.

Our result is proved in the main part, except for the coloring of the auxiliary hypergraph, which is presented in Appendix 2. In Appendix 1 we show how to prove tower-type lower bounds on the Ramsey numbers
R_{k}(k+1,k+2),R_{k}(k+2,k+2)
and
R_{k}(k+1,2k+1)
using a 3-coloring of the shift graph.

2
Preliminaries

A shift graph
Sh(N,k)
is a graph on
{N\choose k}
vertices corresponding to
k
-element subsets of
[N]
, where, as usual,
[N]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Two vertices in the shift graph
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}\}
,
\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}\}
,
x_{1}<x_{2}\dots x_{k}
,
y_{1}<y_{2}<\dots y_{k}
, are joined by an oriented edge if
x_{i+1}=y_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\dots,k-1
. Orientation is important for the Ramsey numbers of paths, but when we talk about the chromatic number, we can forget orientation.
For a graph
G
, let
\chi(G)
be its chromatic number.

It follows from the Ramsey theorem that for every
k
,
\chi(Sh(k,N))
tends to infinity as
N
goes to infinity. If
N\geq 2k+1
, then
\chi(Sh(k,N))\geq 3
, however, the shift graph remains
3
-colorable for large
N
. The colorings of large shift graphs with a small number of colors has been used for estimating Ramsey numbers and we will use them also here. To this end we introduce the function
s(k)
by

s(k):=\max\{N;\ \chi(Sh(N,k))\leq 3\}.

It is well-known that
s(k)
is growing with
k
roughly as the tower function. In
[
13
]
, we proved

s(k)\geq 4\ \mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits_{k-3}(2),

(3)

but our aim in that paper was only to give a simple proof of a tower-function lower bound. Therefore this bound is certainly not optimal that one can prove using known methods.
Since our bound on Ramsey numbers is based on this function and we do not know the precise value of
s(k)
, we prefer to state our result in terms of this function.

Theorem 2.1

For every
k\geq 4
,

R_{k}(k+1,k+1)\geq s(\lfloor k/4\rfloor)
.

3
The lower bound
R_{k}(k+1,k+1)\geq s(\lfloor k/4\rfloor)

The proof of this inequality uses an idea from our paper
[
13
]
where we constructed colorings with low discrepancy. Our aim here is different; we only want to prove a lower bound.

Let
k\geq 4
. Because of the monotonicity of
R_{k}(k+1,k+1)
, we can assume that
k
is divisible by 4. Let
N:=s(k/4)
. By the definition of
s
, this means that the chromatic number of the shift graph
Sh(N,k/4)\leq 3
(for
k
sufficiently large equality holds true). Our coloring of
{[N]\choose k}
will be a composition of two mappings.

The first mapping is defined as follows. Let
\phi
be a 3-coloring of
Sh(N,k/4)
. Divide each
X\in{[N]\choose k}
into consecutive segments of the same size
l:=k/4
, which we denote by
X^{1},X^{2},X^{3},X^{4}
. For each
X\in{[N]\choose k}
, we define

\lambda(X):=(\phi(X^{1}),\phi(X^{2}),\phi(X^{3}),\phi(X^{4})).

In this way, we have defined a mapping
\lambda:{[N]\choose k}\to[3]^{4}
assigning a vector of length 4 to every
X
.

Our next goal is to find a 2-coloring of
[3]^{4}
, i.e.,
\psi:[3]^{4}\to[2]
, such that
\lambda\circ\psi:{[N]\choose k}\to[2]
has no monochromatic copy of
K^{(k)}_{k+1}
. We will prove a stronger statement:

For every
k+1
element subset
Y
of
[N]
, there are five special
k
-subsets of
Y
on which
\lambda\circ\psi
is not monochromatic.

Let
Y:=\{y_{1}<y_{2}<\dots<y_{k+1}\}
and
k=4l+1
; the five sets are

Z_{0}=Y\setminus\{y_{1}\},\ Z_{1}=Y\setminus\{y_{l+1}\},\ Z_{2}=Y\setminus\{y_{2l+1}\},\ Z_{2}=Y\setminus\{y_{3l+1}\},\ Z_{4}=Y\setminus\{y_{4l+1}\}.

(4)

On such 5-tuples, the vectors
(\lambda(Z_{0}),\lambda(Z_{1}),\lambda(Z_{2}),\lambda(Z_{4}))
have a special structure, which we call a
bridge
.

Definition 3.1
(bridge)

For any
\bar{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})\in[3]^{4}
and
b=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4})\in[3]^{4}
with
a_{i}\neq b_{i}
for
i=1,2,3,4
, the set of vectors

\begin{array}[]{l}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}),\\
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},b_{4}),\\
(a_{1},a_{2},b_{3},b_{4}),\\
(a_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4})\\
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4})\end{array}

is called a
bridge
.

Lemma 3.1

For every
Y\in{[n]\choose k+1}
and
Z_{0},\dots,Z_{4}
as defined in (
4
), the set of vectors
\lambda(Z_{0}),\dots,\lambda(Z_{4})
forms a bridge.

Recalling that
Y=\{y_{1}<y_{2}\dots<y_{4l+1}\}
, for
j=0,1,2,3
, set

A_{j}:=\{y_{jl+2},\dots,y_{(j+1)l+1}\},\quad B_{j}:=\{y_{jl+1},\dots,y_{(j+1)l}\}

Observe that, for
i=0,1,2,3,4
,
Z_{i}
is the concatenation of the first
i
blocks
A_{j}
followed by the last
4-i
blocks
B_{j^{\prime}}
. In particular
Z_{0}=A_{0}A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}
and
Z_{4}=B_{0}B_{1}B_{2}B_{3}
. Also, for each
j
,
0\leq j\leq 3
, the blocks
A_{j}
and
B_{j}
are in a shift position and hence
\phi(A_{j})\neq\phi(B_{j})
. Consequently,
\lambda(Z_{0}),\lambda(Z_{1}),\dots,\lambda(Z_{4})
form a bridge.

The following is a key lemma.

Lemma 3.2

There exists a 2-coloring of
[3]^{4}
such that no bridge is monochromatic.

We found the coloring by computer search. At present, we do not have a concise definition of such a coloring nor an explanation of why it exists. The coloring is in the Appendix 2, and we will explain how we found it in Section
4
.
The bound on
R_{k}(k+1,k+1)
now follows from Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
.

4
The way to the lower bound on
R_{k}(k+1,k+1)
.

Consider the following generalization of the concept of a bridge.

Definition 4.1
(
n
-bridge)

Let
n\geq 2
.
For any
\bar{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in[3]^{n}
and
\bar{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in[3]^{n}
with
a_{i}\neq b_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
, the set of vectors

\begin{array}[]{l}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n-1},a_{n}),\\
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n-1},b_{n}),\\
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,b_{n-1},b_{n}),\\
\dots\\
(a_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n-1},b_{n}),\\
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n-1},b_{n})\end{array}

is called an
n
-
bridge
.

Denote by
{\cal B}_{n}
the hypergraph on
[3]^{n}
whose edges are
n
-bridges. When trying to prove a lower bound on
R_{k}(k+1,k+1)
, we first realized the following fact:

Claim 4.1

If
{\cal B}_{n}
is 2-colorable, then
R_{k}(k+1,k+1)\geq s(\lfloor k/n\rfloor)
.

It turned out that
n=4
is the least
n\geq 2
such that
{\cal B}_{n}
is
2
-colorable. It is easy to prove that
{\cal B}_{2}
is not
2
-colorable; for proving that
{\cal B}_{3}
is not 2-colorable and
{\cal B}_{4}
is colorable, we used a computer. Specifically, we transformed the problems into problems about satisfiability of CNF formulas and used the SAT-solver
MiniSat,

[
15
]
.

To represent the problem of coloring a hypergraph
H=(V,E)
by two colors as a problem about satisfiability of a CNF formula is easy: For every
v\in V
take a variable
x_{v}
and for every hyperedge
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
take the two clauses
\{x_{v_{1}},\dots,x_{v_{r}}\},\{\bar{x}_{v_{1}},\dots,\bar{x}_{v_{r}}\}
.

One could try to get a better bound by using shift graphs that are colored by more than 3 colors. For instance, let
s_{4}(k)
be the maximal
N
such that
\chi(Sh(N,k))\leq 4
. One can show a larger bound on
s_{4}
than we have for
s
, namely,

s_{4}(k)\geq 4\ \mathop{\rm tw}\nolimits_{k}(2).

Let
{\cal B}^{4}_{4}
be the natural extension of
{\cal B}_{4}
to a hypergraph on
[4]^{4}
. If
{\cal B}^{4}_{4}
were 2-colorable, we would get a lower bound
\geq s_{4}(\lfloor k/4\rfloor)
. But this hypergraph is not 2-colorable, as we found out using the SAT-solver. So our bound is the best possible that one can get using shift graphs and bridges.

5
Conclusions

We have made progress in computing the value of
R_{k}(k+1,k+1)
, but our estimate is certainly far from the true value. We believe that
R_{k}(k+1,k+1)
is at least the tower of
k-c
twos for some constant
c
. A possible reason why we cannot get such a bound using our method is that we only consider a constant number of
k
-sets in a potentially homogeneous
(k+1)
-set
X
. Specifically our coloring is such that there is a configuration of five particular subsets of
{X\choose k+1}
which is not monochromatic for any
X
,
|X|=k+1
.
3
3

3

But note that for the lower bound on
R_{k}(k+2,k+2)
we only needed
three
particular sets to get almost full tower of twos.

Another disappointing fact is that we are not able to give a formula for a 2-coloring of the hypergraph of bridges, nor we have any intuition why it exists.

Proposition 5.1

For every

Proof of
P_{k}(2,3)\geq s(k-4)
.

Let
N:=s(k-4)
and let
\phi:{[N]\choose k-4}\to\{1,2,3\}
be a 3-coloring of the shift graph
Sh(k-4,N)
. We color
k
-sets with two colors as follows.
Let
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}\}
, and consider the colors of the five
(k-4)
-sets

\displaystyle c_{1}=\phi(\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k-4}\}),

\displaystyle c_{2}=\phi(\{x_{2},x_{3},\dots,x_{k-3}\}),

\displaystyle\dots\qquad

\displaystyle c_{5}=\phi(\{x_{5},x_{6},\dots,x_{k}\}).

We color
X

1.

either
c_{2}<c_{3}>c_{4}
, (type I.),

2.

or
c_{1}>c_{2}>c_{3}<c_{4}<c_{5}
, (type II.).

Otherwise
X
is blue.

First, we show that there is no red path of length 2. It is clear that such a path cannot have the two
k
-sets of the same type. Suppose that
X
and

are red, they are in a shift position,
X
is before
Y
, and

(k-4)

(k-4)
-sets in

Now suppose there is a blue path
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}
. Consider the path of length seven of
(k-4)
-subsets in
X_{1}\cup X_{2}\cup X_{3}
. Let
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{7}
be the colors of the consecutive sets in this path. Recall that

for

because consecutive

, or

cannot equal to 2; this is because, since neither

.

Next observe that

. But then

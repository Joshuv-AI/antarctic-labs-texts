Abstract
. We study the sum of the squares of the irreducible character degrees not divisible by some prime p p of a finite group, and its relationship with the corresponding quantity in a p p -Sylow normalizer. This leads to study a recent conjecture by E. Giannelli, which we prove for p = 2 p=2 and in some other cases.

Primary 20D20; Secondary 20C15

1.
Introduction

There are some strikingly simple and seemingly innocent statements
that follow from the recently proven McKay conjecture
[
CS24
]
(or
from the techniques developed to prove it) whose validity appears to
resist any elementary justification. For instance, W. Feit was
interested in proving that if
P
is an abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup
of
G
, then
k(G)\geq k({\bf N}_{G}(P))
, where
k(G)
is the number of
conjugacy classes of the finite group
G
(
[
F80
]
). Although this
inequality now follows as a consequence of
[
CS24
]
, no
alternative proof is currently known. (It is worth noting that
equality happens if and only if
P\trianglelefteq\,G
by the Itô–Michler
theorem.)

This current project – long awaited by the third author – was
expected to provide another example of such phenomena. And yet,
it does not. What was anticipated to be a theorem
remains, for now, a conjecture. As usual,
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is the set of
irreducible complex characters of the finite group
G
whose degree
is not divisible by the prime
p
, and
P^{\prime}=[P,P]
is the derived
subgroup of the group
P
.

CONJECTURE A
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
.
Then

\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)}\chi(1)^{2}\geq|{\bf N}_{G}(P):P^{\prime}|

with equality if and only
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
has a normal complement in
G
.

The McKay theorem establishes that there exists a bijection
f:{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(P))
. One way to prove Conjecture A,
perhaps the most obvious,
is to show that we can choose

f
to satisfy the additional condition that
f(\chi)(1)\leq\chi(1)
for all
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
. This turns out to be a
conjecture by E. Giannelli (
[
G25
]
), and it does not appear to
follow easily from the proof of the McKay conjecture. In
Theorem
3.5
below we carry out the standard
reduction of the McKay conjecture now incorporating Giannelli’s
strengthening, which is therefore reduced now to a question on
simple groups. This question is verified in a number of important
cases in Sections
4
and
5
below. In
Theorem
5.2
, we completely prove it for
p=2
,
and this establishes:

THEOREM B
.

Conjecture A holds for
p=2
.

Can Conjecture A be proven independently of Giannelli’s
strengthening of McKay? We do not know the answer to this question.
Characters of
p^{\prime}
-degree in a group tend to have much larger degree
than those in the normalizer of a Sylow
p
-subgroup, but so far,
no effective strategy has been developed to exploit this
observation.

Theorem C below, whose proof is rather involved – but does not use the Classification of Finite Simple Groups – takes care of the equality in Conjecture A (assuming Giannelli’s conjecture), and
might have independent interest.

THEOREM C
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
a prime, and
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
.
Then all irreducible characters of
p^{\prime}
-degree of
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
extend
to
G
if and only if there is
K\trianglelefteq\,G
such that
K{\bf N}_{G}(P)=G
and

{\bf N}_{K}(P)=1
.

This extends
[
I86
, Theorem B]
, where it is required that
all
the irreducible characters of
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
extend to
G
. As we
shall explain, in order to prove Theorem C, we shall need to
establish a relative version of it with respect to a normal
subgroup. We find it surprising that a character restriction type of
theorem admits a relative version, since these versions regarding
group structure occur very rarely.

There are some variations of Conjecture A which we do not attempt
here. Among others, it seems reasonable to replace
P^{\prime}
by the
Frattini subgroup
\Phi(P)
and the set of
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible
characters by the so-called
almost
p
-rational
characters of

p^{\prime}
-degree. Proving this, however, seems much more complicated.

Concerning Giannelli’s conjecture, let us mention here that in
general it is not always possible to find McKay bijections
f:{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(P))
such that
f(\chi)
is an irreducible
constituent of
\chi_{{\bf N}_{G}(P)}
, as shown by
{\sf A}_{5}
for
p=2
,
or
{\rm GL}_{2}(3)
for
p=3
.

A final but important remark is in order: Why do we care about the
sum of the squares of the irreducible character degrees not
divisible by
p
? First of all, this number would be the dimension
of any complex algebra affording the
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible
characters. Also, of the famous list of problems by Richard Brauer
(
[
B63
]
), Problem 2 asks when non-isomorphic finite groups
G

and
H
have isomorphic group algebras
{\mathbb{C}}G
and
{\mathbb{C}}H
. If
G
has
a normal
p
-complement, Isaacs proved that
H
has a normal

p
-complement too (
[
N18
, Theorem 7.8]
). The question of whether
the normality of a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
is recognized by

{\mathbb{C}}G
remains open. In
[
N04a
]
, it is suggested that, perhaps,
this happens if and only if

\left(\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)}\chi(1)^{2}\right)_{p^{\prime}}=|G|_{p^{\prime}}\,,

where
n_{p^{\prime}}=n/n_{p}
with
n_{p}
denoting the largest power of the
prime
p
dividing the integer
n
.

2.
Proof of Theorem C

Our notation for ordinary characters follows
[
I06
]
and
[
N18
]
.
In this section we prove Theorem C. In order to do that, we need to
prove a relative version of it, that allows us to use induction.
This makes the proof more complicated. We start with some
preliminary results.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N\trianglelefteq\,G
and let
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(N)
be
G
-invariant.
Then

{\rm ker}(\theta)=\bigcap_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)}{\rm ker}(\chi)\,.

Proof.

It is clear that
{\rm ker}(\theta)
is contained in
{\rm ker}(\chi)
for every

\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)
, since
\chi_{N}=e_{\chi}\theta
for
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)
. Suppose that
x\in\bigcap_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)}{\rm ker}(\chi)
. Then

\theta^{G}(x)=\theta^{G}(1)=|G:N|\theta(1)\neq 0
, and therefore
x\in N
.
Since
\theta
is
G
-invariant, we have that

\theta^{G}(x)=|G:N|\theta(x)
, and thus
\theta(x)=\theta(1)
,
as wanted.
∎

We need an easy lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
K\trianglelefteq\,G
,
H\leq G
,
N=K\cap H
and
G=KH
. Let

\gamma\in{\rm Irr}(K)
be
G
-invariant, and let
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(N)
be
an irreducible
H
-invariant constituent of
\gamma_{N}
. Suppose that
restriction defines a bijection
{\rm Irr}(G|\gamma)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}(H|\theta)
. Then
\gamma_{N}=\theta
.

Proof.

We have that

\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\gamma)}(\chi(1)/\gamma(1))^{2}=|G:K|\,.

We also have that

\sum_{\tau\in{\rm Irr}(H|\theta)}(\tau(1)/\theta(1))^{2}=|H:N|\,.

By hypothesis,

\sum_{\tau\in{\rm Irr}(H|\theta)}(\tau(1)/\theta(1))^{2}=\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\gamma)}(\chi(1)/\theta(1))^{2}=(\gamma(1)/\theta(1))^{2}\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\gamma)}(\chi(1)/\gamma(1))^{2}\,.

Since
|H:N|=|G:K|
, we deduce that

\gamma_{N}=\theta
. ∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
G=KP
, where
K
is a normal
p
-complement and
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Let
\gamma\in{\rm Irr}(G)
. If
P\subseteq{\rm ker}(\gamma)
, then

\gamma_{{\bf C}_{K}(P)}
is irreducible.

Proof.

Since
{\rm ker}(\gamma)\trianglelefteq\,G
, we have that
[K,P]P\subseteq{\rm ker}(\gamma)
.
Thus
\gamma_{K}
is an irreducible character of
K
with
[K,P]

contained in its kernel. By coprime action (for instance, Lemma 4.28 of
[
I08
]
), we have that
{\bf C}_{K}(P)[K,P]=K
and
thus
\gamma_{{\bf C}_{K}(P)}
is irreducible.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
N\trianglelefteq\,G
,
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Let
N\leq K_{i}\trianglelefteq\,G
be
complementing
N{\bf N}_{G}(P)/N
for
i=1,2
in
G/N
. Then
K_{1}=K_{2}
.

Proof.

By working in
G/N
, we may assume that
N=1
.
We have that
G/K_{i}
has a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup.
Let
L=K_{1}\cap K_{2}
. Then
G/L
has a normal
Sylow
p
-subgroup. Hence,
L{\bf N}_{G}(P)=G
. Then

K_{i}=K_{i}\cap L{\bf N}_{G}(P)=L(K_{i}\cap{\bf N}_{G}(P))=L
, as wanted.
∎

In several occasions, we shall use Tate’s theorem in the following
form.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite group,
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
, and
K\trianglelefteq\,G
.
If
K\cap P=K\cap P^{\prime}
, then
K
has a normal
p
-complement.

Proof.

We may assume that
G=KP
. In the notation of
[
I06
, Theorem
6.31]
, we have that
P\cap{\bf A}^{p}(G)={\bf A}^{p}(P)=P^{\prime}
, where

{\bf A}^{p}(G)
is the smallest normal subgroup
L
of
G
such that

G/L
is an abelian
p
-group. It then follows that
G
has a normal

p
-complement by
[
I06
, Theorem 6.31]
. Therefore,
K
has a
normal
p
-complement.
∎

Let
N\trianglelefteq\,G
and let
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(N)
be
G
-invariant. We let

{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime},{\rm rel}}(G|\theta):=\{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)\mid p\text{ does not
divide }\chi(1)/\theta(1)\}.

The following easily implies
Theorem C (when

is trivial).

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
N\trianglelefteq\,G
and let
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(N)
be
G
-invariant. Let
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Assume that
\theta
extends to

(i)

Every
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime},{\rm rel}}(N{\bf N}_{G}(P)|\theta)

(ii)

Every
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(N{\bf N}_{G}(P)|\theta)

G

(iii)

There is a normal complement

to
{\bf N}_{G}(P)N/N

\theta

\hat{\theta}\in{\rm Irr}(K)

There is a normal complement

G

K/N
is a group of order not divisible by

\theta

\Delta

\theta
to

\Delta=\{\lambda\eta\,|\,\lambda\in{\rm Irr}(K/N)
is linear

I06

). Therefore

is not divisible by

. Since

acts on
\Delta
by

\theta
is
G
-invariant, by counting we have
that there is some
P
-invariant
\hat{\theta}\in\Delta

\theta
. Since
{\bf C}_{K/N}(P)=1

\hat{\theta}
is the
unique
P
-invariant character of
K

\theta

(using
[
I06

We
claim that
\hat{\theta}
is also
G

\hat{\theta}
is
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
-invariant. If

\hat{\theta}^{n}
is a

-invariant extension of
\theta^{n}=\theta
. By

\hat{\theta}^{n}=\hat{\theta}
, as wanted.

To prove that (iv) implies (ii), we apply
[
N18

.

Since it is clear that (ii) implies (i), to complete the proof of
the theorem,
it is enough to show that
(i) implies (iii). We argue this by induction on
|G:N|
. Recall
that
N{\bf N}_{G}(P)/N={\bf N}_{G/N}(PN/N)
. By using the theory of
character triple isomorphisms (see
[
I06

), we may
assume that
N\subseteq{\bf Z}(G)
. Hence,
N\subseteq{\bf N}_{G}(P)
.
In particular,
\theta
is linear.
Write
\theta=\theta_{p}\theta_{p^{\prime}}
, where
\theta_{p}
has order a

p

\theta_{p^{\prime}}
has order not divisible by
p
.
Since
\theta

PN

\theta_{p}
, which is a power of

\theta_{p}

If
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime},{\rm rel}}({\bf N}_{G}(P)|\theta_{p^{\prime}})

\chi\nu_{{\bf N}_{G}(P)}\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime},{\rm rel}}({\bf N}_{G}(P)|\theta)

If
\eta\in{\rm Irr}(G)

\eta\nu^{-1}

\chi

\theta

If

Conjecture
4.1
for
S
.

Proof.

If
m(S)\geq b(\mathbf{N}_{S}(P))
, then any bijection
\Psi
from

\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(S)
to
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{S}(P))
sending
1_{S}
to

1_{\mathbf{N}_{S}(P)}
automatically satisfies the degree condition

\Psi(\chi)(1)\leq\chi(1)
for every
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(S)
.
∎

Hypothesis
4.3
, unfortunately, fails quite often. For
instance, in general, it fails when
S
is a cover of an alternating
or a simple classical group (in characteristic not equal to
p
). It
also fails for certain groups of Lie type in characteristic
p
(see
the proof of Proposition
4.11
).

4.1.
Groups of Lie type in characteristic
p

The failure of Hypothesis
4.3
when
S
is a group of Lie type
in characteristic
p
arises in the case of unitary groups. This
case requires additional work. Our notation for finite simple groups
(and related ones) follows
[
C85
,
Atl
]
.

Low-degree irreducible representations of the special unitary groups

SU_{n}(q)
(
q=p^{f}
is a power of a prime
p
and
n\geq 3
,
excluding
(n,q)=(3,2)
) are studied in
[
TZ96
, §4]
and

[
LOST10
, §6.1]
. Among these are the so-called irreducible Weil
characters, denoted by
\zeta^{i}_{n,q}
for
0\leq i\leq q
. The
characters
\zeta^{i}_{n,q}
with
i>0
have degree

(q^{n}-(-1)^{n})/(q+1)
, while
\zeta^{0}_{n,q}
has degree

(q^{n}+q(-1)^{n})/(q+1)
. In fact, these
\zeta^{i}_{n,q}
account for
all nontrivial characters of
SU_{n}(q)
with degree at most

d(SU_{n}(q))+1
. Consequently, if
p
is the defining characteristic
of the group, we have

m_{p}(SU_{n}(q))=(q^{n}-(-1)^{n})/(q+1).

For our purposes, we
need to construct a non-Weil character of the unitary groups that is
invariant under the automorphism groups.

To do so, we first describe the Weil characters of
G:=SU_{n}(q)
via
the notion of Lusztig series and semisimple characters, as follows.
The set
\operatorname{Irr}(G)
of irreducible characters of
G
is partitioned
into the Lusztig series
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
, where
s
runs over a
complete set of representatives of conjugacy classes of semisimple
elements of
G^{*}:=PGU_{n}(q)
(see
[
GM20
, Theorem 2.6.2]
). Each

\mathcal{E}(G,s)
contains one or more special members called
semisimple characters whose degrees are equal to

|G^{*}:{\mathbf{C}}_{G^{*}}(s)|_{p^{\prime}}
(see
[
GM20
, Definition 2.6.9]
).

Centralizers of semisimple elements in classical groups are well
known (see
[
C81
,
D18
]
for instance). We recall here the
needed result for
GU_{n}(q)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G=GU_{n}(q)
and
s\in G
be a semisimple
element. For a monic polynomial

g(t)=t^{d}+a_{d-1}t^{d-1}+\cdots+a_{1}t+a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(t)
not equal
to
t
, write

g^{*}(t):=t^{d}+(a_{1}/a_{0})^{q}t^{d-1}+\cdots+(a_{d-1}/a_{0})^{q}t+(1/a_{0})^{q}
.
Let
f(t)
be the characteristic polynomial of
s
and assume that
its decomposition into irreducible monic polynomials over

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is

f(t)=\pm\prod_{i=1}^{a}f_{i}(t)^{n_{i}}\times\prod_{j=1}^{b}(g_{j}(t)g_{j}^{*}(t))^{m_{j}},

where
f_{i}=f_{i}^{*}
and
g_{j}\neq g_{j}^{*}
for every
i
and
j
and they
are pairwise distint. Let
d_{i}:=\deg(f_{i})
and
e_{j}:=\deg(g_{j})
.
Then

{\mathbf{C}}_{G}(s)\cong\prod_{i=1}^{a}GU_{n_{i}}(q^{d_{i}})\times\prod_{j=1}^{b}GL_{m_{j}}(q^{2e_{j}}).

Remark 4.6
.

Irreducible factors of the characteristic polynomial
f(t)
of a
semisimple element in
GU_{n}(q)
are subject to certain restrictions.
For example, a polynomial
g
is a factor if and only if
g^{*}
is
also a factor. Additionally, any factor
g
satisfying
g=g^{*}
must
have odd degree.

Let
\delta
be a generator of the multiplicative group

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
. For
1\leq i\leq q
, consider the semisimple
element
s_{i}\in G^{*}
to be the image of the diagonal matrix

\widetilde{s}_{i}:={\mathrm{diag}}(\delta^{i(q-1)},1^{n-1})\in\widetilde{G}:=GU_{n}(q)

under the natural projection

\pi:\widetilde{G}\to G^{*}
. For general
s
,
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
may
contain more than one semisimple character, but we claim that, each

\mathcal{E}(G,s_{i})
contains a unique one.

Note that
\widetilde{G}
is self-dual (in the sense of

[
C85
, §4.3]
) and we may identify
\widetilde{G}
with its
dual group. As the center of the ambient algebraic group of

\widetilde{G}
is connected, the series

\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{s}_{i})
contains a unique
semisimple character (
[
GM20
, p. 171]
. We denote this character
by
\psi_{i}
. Then

\psi_{i}(1)=|\widetilde{G}:{\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(\widetilde{s}_{i})|_{p^{\prime}}
.
By Lemma
4.5
,

{\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(\widetilde{s}_{i})\cong GU_{n-1}(q)\times GU_{1}(q).

The choice of eigenvalues of
s_{i}
’s implies that

{\mathbf{C}}_{G^{*}}(s_{i})={\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(\widetilde{s}_{i})/{\mathbf{Z}}(\widetilde{G})
.
(To see this, let
C:=\pi^{-1}({\mathbf{C}}_{G^{*}}(s_{i}))
. Then the mapping

\tau:C\to{\mathbf{Z}}(\widetilde{G})
defined by
gsg^{-1}=\tau(g)s
is a
homomorphism with
\operatorname{Ker}(\tau)={\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(\widetilde{s}_{i})
.
However, the fact that
s
and
gsg^{-1}
have the same eigenvalues
forces
\tau(g)=1
for every
g\in C
.) It follows that

|G^{*}:{\mathbf{C}}_{G^{*}}(s_{i})|_{p^{\prime}}=|\widetilde{G}:{\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(\widetilde{s}_{i})|_{p^{\prime}}=\frac{q^{n}-(-1)^{n}}{q+1},

so that semisimple characters in
\mathcal{E}(G,s_{i})
have the same
degree as the (only) semisimple character
\psi_{i}
in

\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{s}_{i})
. By

[
GM20
, Corollary 2.6.18]
, semisimple characters in

\mathcal{E}(G,s_{i})
are irreducible constituents of the restriction
of
\psi_{i}
from
\widetilde{G}
to
G^{*}
. We deduce that

\mathcal{E}(G,s_{i})
has a unique semisimple character, as claimed.
This is the Weil character
\zeta^{i}_{n,q}
mentioned above.

Lemma 4.7
.

Assume the above notation. Then a Weil
character
\zeta^{i}_{n,q}
for some
1\leq i\leq q
is invariant
under all field automorphisms of
G
if and only if
i(p-1)
is
divisible by
q+1
. In particular, if
p=2
, then every

\zeta^{i}_{n,q}
is moved by some automorphism of
G
.

Proof.

The character
\zeta^{i}_{n,q}
is invariant under all field
automorphisms of
G
if and only if

\delta^{i(q-1)}=\delta^{ip(q-1)}
, which means that
i(q-1)\equiv ip(q-1)(\bmod\,q^{2}-1)
, and the first statement follows. When

p=2
, we then have that each
\zeta^{i}_{n,q}
(for
1\leq i\leq q
)
is moved by some field automorphisms of
G
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
G=SU_{n}(q)
where
n\geq 3
is odd,
(n,q+1)=1
,
and
q=p^{f}
for some prime
p
. Then
G
possesses an irreducible
character
\chi
of degree not divisible by
p
such that
\chi
is

\operatorname{Aut}(G)
-invariant and
\chi(1)>q^{n-1}
.

Proof.

By the hypothesis,
G
is simple and self-dual. By

[
B09
, Lemma 5]
,
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible characters of
G

are precisely the semisimple characters of
G
, which in turn can be
labeled by conjugacy classes of semisimple elements of

G^{*}:=PGU_{n}(q)\cong G
.

It is well known that
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
permutes the Lusztig series of
G
.
In our situation, by identifying the automorphisms of
G
with the
corresponding automorphisms of
G^{*}
under the natural isomorphism,
every
\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
maps
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
to

\mathcal{E}(G,\varphi(s))
(see
[
T18
, Proposition 7.2]
).
Since each
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
contains a unique semisimple character
(using again
[
GM20
, p. 171]
and the fact that the ambient
algebraic group of
PGU_{n}(q)
has connected center), of degree

|G^{*}:{\mathbf{C}}_{G^{*}}(s)|_{p^{\prime}}
, it follows that a
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible
characters of
G
is
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
-invariant if and only if the
semisimple conjugacy class labeling it is
\operatorname{Aut}(G^{*})
-invariant.
Therefore, the result follows if we are able to produce a semisimple
element
s\in G^{*}
such that its
G^{*}
-conjugacy class is

\operatorname{Aut}(G^{*})
-invariant and
|G^{*}:{\mathbf{C}}_{G^{*}}(s)|_{p^{\prime}}>q^{n-1}
. Recall
that
{\mathrm{Out}}(G^{*})\cong C_{2f}
is the cyclic group of order
2f

consisting of the field automorphisms of
G^{*}
.

Suppose first that
q
is odd. Let
s
be the image of a diagonal
matrix
\widetilde{s}\in\widetilde{G}:=GU_{n}(q)
with spectrum

{\mathrm{Spec}}(\widetilde{s})=\{1,\ldots,1,-1,-1\}
under the projection

\pi:\widetilde{G}\to G^{*}
mentioned earlier. Note that
n
is odd.
Similar arguments as above show that

{\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(\widetilde{s})
is the complete inverse image
of
{\mathbf{C}}_{G^{*}}(s)
. Moreover,

{\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(\widetilde{s})\cong GU_{n-2}(q)\times GU_{2}(q)
.
It follows that

|G^{*}:{\mathbf{C}}_{G^{*}}(s)|_{p^{\prime}}=|\widetilde{G}:{\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(\widetilde{s})|_{p^{\prime}}=\frac{(q^{n}+1)(q^{n-1}-1)}{(q^{2}-1)(q+1)}.

Observe that, as
\widetilde{s}
is invariant under all field
automorphisms,
s
is
\operatorname{Aut}(G^{*})
-invariant. The required character

\chi
can be taken to be the (only) semisimple character in the
Lusztig series associated to
s
.

Assume now that
q=2^{f}
with
f
even. We then take
\widetilde{s}

to be a diagonal matrix with

{\mathrm{Spec}}(\widetilde{s})=\{\delta^{(q^{2}-1)/3},\delta^{2(q^{2}-1)/3},1^{n-2}\}
instead, where
\delta
, as before,
is a generator of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
. Again, by Lemma
4.5
,
we have
{\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(\widetilde{s})\cong GU_{n-2}(q)\times GL_{1}(q^{2})
and
{\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(\widetilde{s})
is the complete
inverse image of
{\mathbf{C}}_{G^{*}}(s)
. (This is clear when
n>3
. When

n=3
, the image of the homomorphism
\tau:\pi^{-1}({\mathbf{C}}_{G^{*}}(s))\to{\mathbf{Z}}(\widetilde{G})
defined by

gsg^{-1}=\tau(g)s
is contained in
\{\mathrm{Id},\delta^{(q^{2}-1)/3}\mathrm{Id},\delta^{2(q^{2}-1)/3}\mathrm{Id}\}
.
However, both
\delta^{(q^{2}-1)/3}\mathrm{Id}
and

\delta^{2(q^{2}-1)/3}\mathrm{Id}
have order
3
, which does not
divide
|{\mathbf{Z}}(\widetilde{G}|=q+1
. Therefore we still have

\tau(g)=\mathrm{Id}
for all
g\in\pi^{-1}({\mathbf{C}}_{G^{*}}(s))
.) We
then have

|G^{*}:{\mathbf{C}}_{G^{*}}(s)|_{p^{\prime}}=\frac{(q^{n}+1)(q^{n-1}-1)}{(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}.

It is straightforward to check that this is greater than
q^{n-1}
,
and we conclude as in the previous case.

We solve the remaining case
q=2^{f}
with
f
odd by a somewhat
different argument, working with the ambient algebraic group of
G

and its associated Frobenius map instead. So let
\mathbf{G}
be a simple
algebraic group of adjoint type of type
A
, defined over an
algebraically closed field of characteristic
2
. Let
F_{f}
be the
standard Frobenius map on
\mathbf{G}
, raising all matrix entries to the

2^{f}
-th power and
\rho
the inverse transpose. Set

F:=F_{f}\circ\rho
. Then
G=G^{*}
is precisely the group of
F
-fixed
points in
\mathbf{G}
. Let
F_{0}:=F_{1}\circ\rho
. As
f
is odd, we have

F=F_{f}\circ\rho=F_{0}^{f}
. Note that
F_{1}
induces a generator, say

\sigma_{F_{1}}
, for
{\mathrm{Out}}(G)\cong C_{2f}
; on the other hand,
F_{0}

induces
\sigma_{F_{0}}\in{\mathrm{Out}}(G)
of order
f
and
\langle\sigma_{F_{0}}\rangle=\langle(\sigma_{F_{0}})^{2}\rangle=\langle(\sigma_{F_{1}})^{2}\rangle
. By the work of Brunat

[
B09
, Proposition 2]
, the number of

\sigma_{F_{0}}
-invariant irreducible characters of
G
of odd degree
is equal to the number of semisimple characters of

\mathbf{G}^{F_{0}}=PGU_{n}(2)\cong SU_{n}(2)
. (Note that, by the assumptions,

f
is odd and
(n,2^{f}+1)=1
. In particular,
n
is coprime to
3
.)
This number, in turn, is
2^{n-1}
, by

[
C85
, Corollary 8.3.6]
. These
2^{n-1}
characters are
permuted by
\sigma_{F_{1}}
, forming orbits of size
1
or
2
. The
trivial character forms its own orbit. Consequently, at least one of
the nontrivial characters, say
\chi
, is fixed by
\sigma_{F_{1}}
–
that is,
\chi
is
{\mathrm{Out}}(G)
-invariant. By
Lemma
4.7
,
\chi
cannot be a Weil character.
Moreover, under the assumptions on
n
,
q
, and
f
, we have
n\geq 5
. It then follows from
[
TZ96
, Table V]
that
\chi(1)\geq(q^{n}+1)(q^{n-1}-q^{2})/(q+1)(q^{2}-1)
, and therefore
\chi(1)>q^{n-1}
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
G=\mathbf{G}^{F}
be the group of fixed points of a simple algebraic
group
\mathbf{G}
of simply connected type over an algebraically closed
field of characteristic
p
, under a Steinberg endomorphism
F
of

\mathbf{G}
, and assume that
G/{\mathbf{Z}}(G)
is simple. Let
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
.
Then

b\bigl(\mathbf{N}_{G}(P)\bigr)\leq|T/{\mathbf{Z}}(G)|,

where
T:=\mathbf{T}^{F}
and
\mathbf{T}
is a maximally split
F
-stable maximal
torus of
\mathbf{G}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{B}}
be an
F
-stable Borel subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
containing
\mathbf{T}
.
Let
\Phi
be the root system of
\mathbf{G}
with respect to
\mathbf{T}
and

{\mathbf{B}}
. Let
\Phi^{+}
and
\Delta=\{\alpha_{i}:i\in I\}
be the
corresponding set of positive roots and simple roots, respectively.
Also, let
{\mathbf{X}}_{\alpha}
denote the root subgroup associated to each

\alpha\in\Phi
.

Let
{\mathbf{U}}:=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}{\mathbf{X}}_{\alpha}
, which is the
unipotent radical of
{\mathbf{B}}
. According to

[
MT11
, Corollary 24.11]
, we have
P:={\mathbf{U}}^{F}\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
and

{\mathbf{B}}^{F}=\mathbf{N}_{G}(P)
. Furthermore,

\mathbf{N}_{G}(P)=P\rtimes T,

where

T:=\mathbf{T}^{F}
. At this point we have
b(\mathbf{N}_{G}(P))\leq|\mathbf{N}_{G}(P)/P|=|T|

but, in many cases, this upper bound for
b(\mathbf{N}_{G}(P))
is not
sufficient. We need to know more details about the action of
T
on

\operatorname{Irr}(P/P^{\prime})
.

Let
{\mathbf{U}}_{c}:=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\setminus\Delta}{\mathbf{X}}_{\alpha}
.
As explained in
[
C85
, §2.9]
,
{\mathbf{U}}_{c}
is normal in
{\mathbf{U}}

and
{\mathbf{U}}/{\mathbf{U}}_{c}=\prod_{i\in I}{\mathbf{X}}_{\alpha_{i}}
. The endomorphism
F

naturally acts on the roots, and thus on the root subgroups, given
by
F({\mathbf{X}}_{\alpha})={\mathbf{X}}_{F(\alpha)}
. As
{\mathbf{B}}
and
\mathbf{T}
are

F
-stable,
F
permutes the positive roots, as well as the simple
roots. Both
{\mathbf{U}}
and
{\mathbf{U}}_{c}
are therefore
F
-stable. Let
\rho

denote the permutation on
I
induced from the action of
F
on the
simple roots, and
\mathcal{O}
be the set of
\rho
-orbits on
I
.
For each such an orbit
J
, let
{\mathbf{X}}_{J}:=\prod_{i\in J}{\mathbf{X}}_{\alpha_{i}}
, which is an
F
-stable group. Further, let

X_{J}:={\mathbf{X}}_{J}^{F}
. We then have

(4.1)

{\mathbf{U}}^{F}/{{\mathbf{U}}_{c}}^{F}=\prod_{J\in\mathcal{O}}X_{J}.

Note that each
{\mathbf{X}}_{\alpha}
is normalized by
\mathbf{T}
(see

[
C85
, p. 18]
). Thus
{\mathbf{U}}^{F}
and
{{\mathbf{U}}_{c}}^{F}
are both
normalized by
T=\mathbf{T}^{F}
, and so
T
acts on the factor group

{\mathbf{U}}^{F}/{{\mathbf{U}}_{c}}^{F}
. In fact, each direct factor
X_{J}
with
J\in\mathcal{O}
of
{\mathbf{U}}^{F}/{{\mathbf{U}}_{c}}^{F}
is normalized by
T
. Remark that
{\mathbf{Z}}(G)\leq T
(see the proof of

[
MT11
, Lemma 24.12]
) and
{\mathbf{Z}}(G)
acts trivially on
{\mathbf{U}}^{F}
.
Therefore the maximal size of a
T
-orbit on
{\mathbf{U}}^{F}/{{\mathbf{U}}_{c}}^{F}
is at
most
|T/{\mathbf{Z}}(G)|
.

Assume from now on that
G\notin\{Sp_{2n}(2),F_{4}(2),G_{2}(3)\}
. (For
these exceptions, a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
is self-normalizing
and the desired inequality is immediate.) Then, according to

[
B09
, Lemma 5]
,
{{\mathbf{U}}_{c}}^{F}
is the derived subgroup
P^{\prime}

of
P={\mathbf{U}}^{F}
. As the actions of
T
on
P/P^{\prime}
and
\operatorname{Irr}(P/P^{\prime})
are
isomorphic,
|T/{\mathbf{Z}}(G)|
is also an upper bound for the sizes of the

T
-orbits on
\operatorname{Irr}(P/P^{\prime})
. Moreover, the restriction of every
irreducible character of
P/P^{\prime}\rtimes T
to
P/P^{\prime}
is
multiplicity-free, as
T
is abelian and
|T|
is coprime to
p
.
The result now follows.
∎

.

. Then

odd and

, then

S
. Sylow

. Furthermore, as noted in

]

Consider

Let

Proposition 4.14
.

Conjecture

is true when

Proof.

The existence of a McKay-good bijection for exceptional covering
groups was established by Malle
[

]
. We note that, except
the single case

, the group of outer
automorphisms stabilizing
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(S)
is either trivial or
cyclic of prime order, and so any bijection that respects the action
of those outer automorphisms is McKay-good. Building on Malle’s
work, we show that in most cases, Hypothesis
4.3
holds,
ensuring that any such bijection automatically satisfies the
additional degree condition. In the remaining cases, it turns out
that
S
has precisely one character of degree smaller than

b(\mathbf{N}_{S}(P))

corresponding (
p^{\prime}
-degree) character of
\mathbf{N}_{S}(P)

automorphisms.

Throughout we let
X:=S/Z
and

, where

Z:={\mathbf{Z}}(S)
. Of course we may assume that
p\mid|S|
.

First suppose that
|S|_{p}=p
; in particular,
p
is odd. In this
case, work of Dade
[
D66
,
D96
]
provides a natural bijection
between the irreducible characters in any block
B
of
S
and those
in its Brauer correspondent

required degree condition. We may assume that
B
has full defect.
When
|S|_{p}=p
, Dade’s bijection is described in Lemmas 4.7–4.10 of

[
D96
]
and is known, in particular, to preserve decomposition
numbers. Hence it suffices to verify the desired degree inequality
for the corresponding bijection between the Brauer characters of
B

and
b
given in
[
D96
, Lemma 4.7]
.

This bijection between Brauer characters is defined as follows. The
Green correspondence sends the isomorphism classes of
finite-dimensional non-projective indecomposable

-modules

belonging to

bijectively onto the isomorphism classes of
finite-dimensional non-projective indecomposable

, where

is a suitable residue field of

p
(see
[
F82

is simple, the socle

of

lies in a uniquely determined isomorphism class
of simple

-modules. Dade’s bijection then sends the

, and therefore also satisfies the degree

]

assume from now on that

As before, we use

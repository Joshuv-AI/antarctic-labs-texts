Dual solutions of mixed convective hybrid nanofluid flow over a shrinking cylinder placed in a porous medium
Authors: Nepal Chandra Roy, Aysha Akter
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractMixed convective hybrid nanofluid flow over a shrinking cylinder saturated in a porous medium is analyzed in the presence of magnetic field. The mathematical model of the present problem is formulated with constant thermophysical properties. The system of governing equations is reduced to ordinary differential equations utilizing appropriate similarity transformations. The resulting equations are solved by the implicit Runge-Kutta-Butcher procedure together with Nachtsheim-Swigert iteration scheme. The key findings are that the skin friction coefficient and the Nusselt number are substantially augmented with the increase in mixed convection parameter, Ri, magnetic field parameter, M, and porosity parameter, K. However, the boundary layer separation is delayed owing to the higher value of M, Ri, K, curvature parameter, γ, volume fractions of Al2O3 (φ1) and Cu (φ2). Moreover, the thermal boundary layer for the Cu–Al2O3/H2O hybrid nanofluid is wider in comparison with that for Cu–H2O and Al2O3–H2O nanofluid. On the other hand, the momentum boundary layer is thicker for 10 % of Al2O3 nanoparticle volume fraction and the reverse is seen for 10 % volume fraction of Cu nanoparticle.Keywords: Mixed convection, Magnetic field, Hybrid nanofluid, Porous medium, Shrinking cylinder

Introduction

The conjugate free-forced convective boundary layer flow is of great theoretical interest over decades. Such a flow over a continuously moving surface becomes the potential source of scientific and industrial applications such as aerodynamics, extrusion of plastic sheets, hot rolling process, drawing plastic films, paper production, fiber and wire drawing process, metal spinning, and cooling, glass blowing, etc. [1,2]. Gathering the underlying physics of the combined free and forced convective flow over a continuously moving surface eventually might help to improve the existing technology and produce clean energy. In this regard, the results obtained from this study could play an important role to fulfil the aim of SDGs and net zero goals. Goldstein [3] addressed the concept of flow over a shrinking surface as a coinciding traverse flow. It was demonstrated that fluid flow over a shrinking surface moves in an upward direction along the surface boundary. Contrary to this, the exterior flow of the boundary layer acts downward. Wang [4] explored the stagnation flow for the scenarios of 2-D shrinking surface and axisymmetric shrinking surface. With the appearance of partial slip, Mahapatra et al. [5] worked on hydromagnetic stagnation-point flow past a contracting surface. They concluded that fluid velocity augments due to the augmented effect of the velocity slip parameter. To sustain the flow over a shrinking surface, Miklav c˘ i c˘ and Wang [6] imposed suction on it and analyzed the existence of a unique solution for the relevant flow. Inducing magnetic field and radiation parameter impacts, Benos et al. [7] studied heat transfer characteristics for stretching as well as shrinking surfaces. Results exposed that either the considered surface compresses or stretches, thermal boundary layer reduces due to the augmentation in Prandtl number, Pr and Chandrashekhar Number. Raghunath et al. [8] investigated the unsteady flow over a vertical permeable plate with the influences of magnetic field, thermal radiation, heat source, and homogenous chemical reaction. Ishak et al. [9] studied velocity and temperature distribution for free-forced convective boundary layer flow past a vertical wall including the mass transpiration effect. Applying similarity transformations, the modified governing equations were solved for aiding and contra flow regimes.

By conducting experiments on fluid flow over shrinking or stretching surfaces, investigators have also been concerned to understand the flow properties. Wang et al. [10] first inspected flow and temperature profiles over a stretching cylinder for a set of Reynolds numbers. Two decades later, Ishak et al. [11] developed a similar investigation for suction and injection influences on fluid flow. They also extended Wang's problem for magnetic field impacts [12]. Butt et al. [2] numerically investigated magnetic field impacts on momentum and thermal boundary layer past a stretching sheet and stretching cylinder through a porous medium. Results exposed that in both cases momentum boundary layer decreases while the opposite characteristic is shown for the thermal boundary layer. Considering partial slip condition, Abbas et al. [13] highlighted viscous fluid velocity and temperature profiles for time-dependent stretching/shrinking cylinder. Utilizing the procedure of Runge–Kutta–Fehlberg, it was inspected that dissimilar solutions are found for the shrinking case, whereas unique solution occurs for the stretching case. It should be mentioned that the conception of fluid flow around a shrinking cylinder generally arises in geothermal structures. Lok and Pop [14] studied the axisymmetric stagnation point flow of an incompressible and viscous fluid past a shrinking cylinder. They concluded that with the suction and shrinking impacts flow patterns get more convoluted. Moreover, Mishra and Singh [15] examined the axisymmetric mixed convective flow due to a contracting cylinder and momentum and thermal slip conditions. Expressing the results for dual solutions, it was pointed out that the first or stable solution indicates the practical correctness. On the other hand, a second or unstable solution occurs from the non-linearity in the flow problem and represents barely the mathematical reality. Elnajjar et al. [16] worked on the viscous flow past a permeable shrinking cylinder with time-dependent condition. They observed that solution duality occurs between fixed arrays of suction parameter. Besides, with the increase in unsteadiness parameter, skin friction factor and rate of heat transfer are found to increase. Later, a similar flow geometry has been investigated by Mdallal et al. [17] imposing magnetic field influence within a porous medium. Solving the reduced governing equations by Iterative Power Series Method, the acquired results were examined through graphically for a series of governing parameters.

It is noteworthy to mention that the above explorations have been conducted using base fluids, for example, water, mineral oils and propylene glycol. For lower thermal conductivity, these traditional base fluids are not progressive enough to utilize in the rapid development of industrial, engineering and biomedical devices. Hence to optimize the thermal performance of these fluids, researchers have been quite perceptive to improve their thermofluidic structure. By forming a colloidal suspension of nano-meter-sized particles within the working fluid, Choi and Eastman [18] first introduced the narrative of nanofluid in 1995. Zahmatkesh et al. [19] critically reviewed the heat transfer characteristics subject to different configurations of nanoparticles. It was observed that varying thermal performance of divergent shapes of nanomaterials rely on natural, forced, and free-forced convection regimes. Recently, Kodi et al. [20] have studied the Dufour, magnetic field, and thermal radiation effects on the nanofluid flow through a vertical porous cone subject to convective heating. In order to approach the Brownian motion with thermophoresis impacts, Bakar et al. [21] utilized a nanofluid model for a fluid flow past a moving cylinder. They analyzed the numerical results of friction factor, heat transfer rate and Sherwood number for varying pertinent parameters. Recently, Siddiqui et al. [22] assessed 3D flow for two different nanofluids (Cu–H2O and Al2O3–H2O) to fathom nanoparticle impacts on system heat transportation. With the conjugate effects of thermal diffusion and radiation, Bhatti and Rashidi [23] conducted an experiment on Williamson nanofluid over a sponge-like contracting/expanding surface. They used governing parameters to materialize the fluid velocity, temperature, and concentration profile. Due to a penetrable shrinking cylinder, Soomro et al. [24] measured mixed convective Cu–H2O nanofluid subject to slip constraints. Inside a porous medium, Tham et al. [25] focused on combined free-forced flow past an isothermal cylinder using Buongiorno-Darcy nanofluid model. Farhani et al. [26] studied hydromagnetic CuO–H2O nanofluid flow from a circular passage considering two distinguished porous arrangements. It was exhibited that the central structure provides a better heat transfer rate than the boundary structure.

In recent years, an upgraded version of nanofluid called hybrid nanofluid has grabbed the eyeball of many analysts due to its excellent performance in thermal conductivity. This well-versed fluid has prominent applications in generator cooling, engine cooling, defense, coolant in manufacturing, drug devaluation, refrigeration, nuclear system cooling, electronic cooling, synthesizing, heating pipe, space, welding, solar water heating, ships, grinding, lubrication, micro-channel and thermal respiratory [27,28]. Sarkar et al. [27] gave a brief review of the fundamentals of synthesis and thermophysical properties of hybrid nanoparticles. Recently, similar promising investigations have been accomplished on the formulation and applications of hybrid nanofluid by Sidik et al. [28], Babu et al. [29], Huminic and Huminic [30], Sundar et al. [31] and Sajid and Ali [32]. By utilizing Fe3O4 and multi-wall carbon nanotube (MWCNTs) as hybrid nanofluids, Said et al. [33] observed the heat transfer characteristics of solar flat plate collectors with thermosyphon constraint. Results revealed that the highest heat transfer efficiency was obtained at the value of Reynolds number = 1413. In addition, Said et al. [34] focused on the specific heat capacitance of hybrid nano liquids. Imposing three different machine learning procedures, their investigations were conducted by taking three unfamiliar metal oxides and MWCNTs within water suspension. For moving surfaces, Khashi'ie et al. [35] extensively scrutinized the friction factor and Nusselt number for a hydromagnetic axisymmetric flow accompanied by Cu and Al2O3 nanoparticles. The boundary layer separation and thermal performance of a hybrid nanofluid flow past an exponentially moving curved surface were reviewed by Wahid et al. [36]. Moreover, Zainal et al. [37] inspected hydromagnetic hybrid nanofluid flow and temperature profile over a flat vertical surface. Under a range of buoyancy parameters from −1 to 5, a stability analysis has been also conducted to examine dual solutions. Elsaid and Wahed [38] scrutinized magnetized mixed convective flow for Fe3O4–Cu/water suspension. They exposed that for the volume fraction of nanoparticles between 5 % and 10 %, shear stress for assisting flow is decreased by 5 % while for opposing flow this value becomes 40 %–50 %. Considering three distinctive configurations (lamina, blade, and sphere) of hybrid nanoparticles (Ag, TiO2), Rashid et al. [39] observed their impacts on the system temperature profile over a stretching/shrinking cylinder. In addition, a detailed discussion on dual solutions in terms of the flow profile of a convective free-forced hybrid nanofluid flow through a porous medium was presented by Khan et al. [40]. With the presence of Joule heating, Khashi'ie et al. [41] analyzed a hybrid nanocomposite flow over a contracting cylinder with Joule heating. They concluded that an increase in curvature percentage causes an earlier separation of the boundary layer. Moreover, an excellent study on stability analysis was performed to understand the physically stable and unstable solutions of fluid flow.

The aforementioned research survey clears that the mixed convection flow over a cylinder surface kept in a porous medium is not only important from the technological point of view but also significant in academia. However, no study has considered such a problem governed by the mixed convective hybrid nanofluid flow past a shrinking cylinder embedded in a porous medium. So, the objective of this study is to analyze the effects of nanoparticle volume fractions on mixed convective flow past a porous shrinking cylinder under the influence of a magnetic field. The governing equations have been reduced into ordinary differential equations using similarity transformations and then these have been solved numerically. To examine the flow behaviors and heat transfer, a graphical analysis of dual solutions (stable and unstable) represented by skin friction coefficient, local Nusselt number as well as velocity and temperature profile is discussed for varying pertinent parameters.

On the contrary, for the augmented values of Ri, unstable solutions represent decreasing behavior. A similar manner can be observed for the rate of heat transfer in 2(b).

Degenerate Eulerian polynomials and numbers
Dae San Kim, Taekyun Kim

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to study degenerate Eulerian polynomials and degenerate Eulerian numbers, respectively as degenerate versions of the Eulerian polynomials and the Eulerian numbers, and to derive some of their properties. Specifically, we derive an identity, recursive relations, generating function and degenerate version of Worpitzky’s identity for the degenerate Eulerian polynomials and numbers. In addition, we obtain several results involving the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind and the degenerate Bernoulli numbers as well as the degenerate Eulerian numbers.

In combinatorics, the Eulerian number
A(n,k)
is the number of permutations of the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
in which exactly
k
elements are greater than the previous element.
For example, there are 4 permutations of
\{1,2,3\}
in which exactly 1 element is greater than previous element.

The Eulerian polynomials
A_{n}(x)
were introduced in 1749 by Leonhard Euler in connection with computing values of the Riemann zeta function at negative integers (see [8]). The Eulerian polynomial
A_{n}(x)
is the numerator of the rational function given by (see [7,11])

(1)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)^{n}x^{j}=\frac{A_{n}(x)}{(1-x)^{n+1}},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad\mathrm{where}\ |x|<1.

We note that

(2)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)^{n}x^{j}=\frac{1}{x}\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{n}\frac{1}{1-x},\quad(n\geq 1).

The Eulerian numbers
A(n,k)
appear as the coefficients of the Eulerian polynomial
A_{n}(x)
. Indeed, we have

(3)

A_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A(n,k)x^{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,9,11]).

From (
1
), we note that

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}A(n,k)x^{k}

\displaystyle=A_{n}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)^{n}x^{j}(1-x)^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)^{n}x^{j}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+1}{l}(-1)^{l}x^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{i}(k-i+1)^{n}\bigg)x^{k}.

Thus, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
4
), we get

(5)

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{i}(k-i+1)^{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}A(n,k),&\textrm{if $0\leq k\leq n$,}\\
0,&\textrm{if $k>n$,}\end{array}\right.\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,11]).

The Eulerian numbers satisfy the recursive relation

(6)

A(n,k)=(n-k)A(n-1,k-1)+(k+1)A(n-1,k),\quad(n\geq 1,\,0\leq k\leq n),

and

(Worpitzky’s identity)

(7)

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A(n,k)\binom{x+k}{n},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,9,11]).

From (
1
), we can derive the generating function of Eulerian polynomials given by

(8)

\frac{x-1}{x-e^{(x-1)t}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,11]).

By (
8
), we easily get

(9)

A_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}A_{k}(x)(x-1)^{n-1-k},\quad(n\geq 1),\quad A_{0}(x)=1,

and

(10)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{n}x^{j}=\frac{xA_{n}(x)}{(1-x)^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{n+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}A(n,k)x^{k+1},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5,7,11]).

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponentials are defined by

(11)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x)_{k,\lambda}\frac{t^{k}}{k!},\quad e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)=e_{\lambda}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11-20]),

where
(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\ (x)_{k,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots\big(x-(k-1)\lambda\big),\ (k\geq 1)
.

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=e^{xt}\displaystyle
.

In [4], Carlitz considered the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials given by

(12)

\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=0
,
\beta_{n,\lambda}=\beta_{n,\lambda}(0),\ (n\geq 0),
are called the degenerate Bernoulli numbers. The first few of the degenerate Bernoulli numbers are given by:

(13)

\beta_{0,\lambda}=1,\,\beta_{1,\lambda}=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda,\,\beta_{2,\lambda}=\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{6}\lambda^{2},\,\beta_{3,\lambda}=-\frac{1}{4}\lambda+\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{3},\dots.

It is well known that the Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(14)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-7,9-25]),

where
(x)_{0}=1,\ (x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\ (n\geq 1)
.

As the inversion formula of (
14
), the Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(15)

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-7,9-25]).

Recently, the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by (see (
14
))

(16)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),(\mathrm{see}\ [13,14,15]).

As the inversion formula of (
16
), the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by (see (
15
))

(17)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13,14,15,18]).

An explicit expression of
{n\brace k}_{\lambda}
is given by

(18)

{n\brace k}_{\lambda}=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}(j)_{n,\lambda}.

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)=S_{1}(n,k),\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}={n\brace k}.

2.
Degenerate Eulerian polynomials and numbers

In view of (
1
), we define, as a degenerate version of
A_{n}(x)
, the
degenerate Eulerian polynomials
A_{n,\lambda}(x)
by

(19)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)_{n,\lambda}x^{j}=\frac{A_{n,\lambda}(x)}{(1-x)^{n+1}},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad\mathrm{where}\ |x|<1.

We observe that

(20)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)_{n,\lambda}x^{j}=\frac{1}{x}\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)_{n,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x}\bigg),\quad(n\geq 1).

This is a degenerate version of (
2
).
Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}A_{n,\lambda}(x)=A_{n}(x),\ (n\geq 0)\displaystyle
.

Thus, by (
19
), we get

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(1+j)_{n,\lambda}(1-x)^{n+1}x^{j}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+j)_{n,\lambda}(1-x)^{n+1}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=(1-x)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+j)_{n,\lambda}(1-x)^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=(1-x)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}e_{\lambda}^{j+1}\big((1-x)t\big)=(1-x)e_{\lambda}\big((1-x)t\big)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}e_{\lambda}^{j}\big((1-x)t\big)

\displaystyle=(1-x)e_{\lambda}\big((1-x)t\big)\frac{1}{1-xe_{\lambda}((1-x)t)}=\frac{1-x}{e_{\lambda}^{-1}((1-x)t)-x}=\frac{x-1}{x-e_{-\lambda}((x-1)t)}.

Therefore, by (
21
), we obtain the following theorem.

Proposition 2.1
.

\frac{x-1}{x-e_{-\lambda}((x-1)t)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

This is a degenerate version of (
8
).

In view of (
3
), we define the
degenerate Eulerian numbers

A_{n,\lambda}(n,k)
by

(22)

A_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}A_{\lambda}(n,k)=A(n,k),\quad(n\geq k\geq 0).

From (
19
) and (
22
), we note that

(23)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}=A_{n,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)_{n,\lambda}x^{j}(1-x)^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(1+j)_{n,\lambda}x^{j}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{i}x^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{i}(k-i+1)_{n,\lambda}\bigg)x^{k}.

By comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
23
), we get the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
n,k\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{i}(k-i+1)_{n,\lambda}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}A_{\lambda}(n,k),&\textrm{if $0\leq k\leq n$,}\\
0,&\textrm{if $k>n$.}\end{array}\right.

This is a degenerate version of the identity in (
5
).

From (
21
), we note that

(24)

\displaystyle x-1

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}A_{i,-\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\Big(x-e_{\lambda}\big((x-1)t\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,-\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}A_{i,-\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(1)_{l,\lambda}(x-1)^{l}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(xA_{n,-\lambda}(x)-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}A_{i,-\lambda}(x)(1)_{n-i,\lambda}(x-1)^{n-i}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
24
), we get

(25)

1=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{x}{x-1}A_{n,-\lambda}(x)-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}A_{i,-\lambda}(x)(1)_{n-i,\lambda}(x-1)^{n-i-1}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
25
), we have

(26)

\frac{x}{x-1}A_{n,-\lambda}(x)-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}A_{i,-\lambda}(x)(1)_{n-i,\lambda}(x-1)^{n-i-1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1,&\textrm{if $n=0$,}\\
0,&\textrm{if $n>0$}.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, by (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

(27)

A_{0,\lambda}(x)=1,\quad A_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{i}A_{i,\lambda}(x)(1)_{n-i,-\lambda}(x-1)^{n-i-1},\quad(n\geq 1).

This is a degenerate version of the recursive relation in (
9
).

Letting
x=-1
in (
21
) and recalling (
13
), we have

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,\lambda}(-1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{-2}{-e_{-\lambda}(-2t)-1}=\frac{2}{1+e_{-\lambda}(-2t)}=\frac{2}{1+e_{\lambda}^{-1}(2t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(2t)+1}\big(e_{\lambda}(2t)+1-1\big)=2-\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(2t)+1}

\displaystyle=2+\frac{4}{e_{\lambda}^{2}(2t)-1}-\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(2t)-1}=2+\frac{4}{e_{\lambda/2}(4t)-1}-\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(2t)-1}

\displaystyle=2+\frac{1}{t}\bigg(\frac{4t}{e_{\lambda/2}(4t)-1}-\frac{2t}{e_{\lambda}(2t)-1}\bigg)=2+\frac{1}{t}\bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(4^{n}\beta_{n,\lambda/2}-2^{n}\beta_{n,\lambda}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2+\frac{1}{t}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(4^{n}\beta_{n,\lambda/2}-2^{n}\beta_{n,\lambda}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=2+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{n+1}\bigg(\frac{2^{n+1}\beta_{n+1,\lambda/2}-\beta_{n+1,\lambda}}{n+1}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=2+4\beta_{1,\lambda/2}-2\beta_{1,\lambda}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{n+1}\bigg(\frac{2^{n+1}\beta_{n+1,\lambda/2}-\beta_{n+1,\lambda}}{n+1}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{n+1}\bigg(\frac{2^{n+1}\beta_{n+1,\lambda/2}-\beta_{n+1,\lambda}}{n+1}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
28
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

A_{0,\lambda}(-1)=1,\quad A_{n,\lambda}(-1)=2^{n+1}\bigg(\frac{2^{n+1}\beta_{n+1,\lambda/2}-\beta_{n+1,\lambda}}{n+1}\bigg).

Corollary 2.5
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k)(-1)^{k}=2^{n+1}\bigg(\frac{2^{n+1}\beta_{n+1,\lambda/2}-\beta_{n+1,\lambda}}{n+1}\bigg).

From Theorem 2.2, we note that

(29)

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}(n,k)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{i}(k-i+1)_{n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{i}(k-i+1)_{n-1,\lambda}\big(k-i+1-(n-1)\lambda\big)

\displaystyle=\big(k+1-(n-1)\lambda\big)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{i}(k-i+1)_{n-1,\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n+1}{i}(-1)^{i}i(k-i+1)_{n-1,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(k+1-(n-1)\lambda\big)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\bigg(\binom{n}{i-1}+\binom{n}{i}\bigg)(-1)^{i}(k-i+1)_{n-1,\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad-(n+1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{n}{i-1}(-1)^{i}(k-i+1)_{n-1,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\big(k+1-(n-1)\lambda\big)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{n}{i-1}(-1)^{i}(k-i+1)_{n-1,\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad+\big(k+1-(n-1)\lambda\big)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}(-1)^{i}\big(k-i+1\big)_{n-1,\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad+(n+1)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{n}{i}(-1)^{i}\big(k-i\big)_{n-1,\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\big(k+1-(n-1)\lambda\big)A_{\lambda}(n-1,k-1)+\big(k+1-(n-1)\lambda\big)A_{\lambda}(n-1,k)

\displaystyle\qquad+(n+1)A_{k}(n-1,k-1).

Therefore, by (
29
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

For
n\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq n
, we have

(30)

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}(n,k)=\big((n-k)+(n-1)\lambda\big)A_{\lambda}(n-1,k-1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\big(k+1-(n-1)\lambda\big)A_{\lambda}(n-1,k).

By taking
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, we get the recursive relation in (
6
).

From (
30
), we note that

(31)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\binom{x+k}{n+1}A_{\lambda}(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\Big((n-k+1)A_{\lambda}(n,k-1)+(k+1)A_{\lambda}(n,k)\Big)\binom{x+k}{n+1}

\displaystyle\quad+n\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\Big(A_{\lambda}(n,k-1)-A_{\lambda}(n,k)\Big)\binom{x+k}{n+1}.

Now, we observe that

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}A_{\lambda}(n,k-1)\binom{x+k}{n+1}-\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}A_{\lambda}(n,k)\binom{x+k}{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}A_{\lambda}(n,k-1)\binom{x+k}{n+1}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k)\binom{x+k}{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k)\bigg(\binom{x+k+1}{n+1}-\binom{x+k}{n+1}\bigg)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k)\binom{x+k}{n},

and

(33)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\Big((n-k+1)A_{\lambda}(n,k-1)+(k+1)A_{\lambda}(n,k)\Big)\binom{x+k}{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\binom{x+k}{n+1}(n-k+1)A_{\lambda}(n,k-1)+\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k)(k+1)\binom{x+k}{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\bigg((n-k)\binom{x+k+1}{n+1}A_{\lambda}(n,k)+\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k)(k+1)\binom{x+k}{n+1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k)\bigg((n-k)\binom{x+k+1}{n+1}+(k+1)\binom{x+k}{n+1}\bigg).

By simple calculation, we can show that

(34)

(n-k)\binom{x+k+1}{n+1}+(k+1)\binom{x+k}{n+1}=x\binom{x+k}{n}.

Thus, by (
33
) and (
34
), we get

(35)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\Big((n-k+1)A_{\lambda}(n,k-1)+(k+1)A_{k}(n,k)\Big)\binom{x+k}{k}

\displaystyle=x\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k)\binom{x+k}{n}.

From (
31
), (
32
) and (
35
), we have

(36)

\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\binom{x+k}{n+1}A_{\lambda}(n+1,k)=(x+n\lambda)\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{x+k}{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k).

By applying (
36
) repeatedly, we get

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\binom{x+k}{n+1}A_{\lambda}(n+1,k)

\displaystyle=(x+n\lambda)\big(x+(n-1)\lambda\big)\cdots(x+\lambda)x\binom{x}{0}A_{\lambda}(0,0)=(x)_{n+1,-\lambda}.

Therefore, by (
37
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7

(Degenerate version of Worpitzky’s identity)
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{x+k}{n}A_{-\lambda}(n,k)=(x)_{n,\lambda}.

By taking
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, we obtain the Worpitzky’s identity in (
7
).

From (
17
), we note that

(38)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}(x)_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\binom{x}{k},\quad(n\geq 0),

and

(39)

\binom{x+y}{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{x}{k}\binom{y}{n-k},\quad(n\geq 0).

From Theorem 2.7, (
38
) and (
39
), we have

(40)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\binom{x}{k}

\displaystyle=(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{x+j}{n}A_{-\lambda}(n,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{-\lambda}(n,j)\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{x}{k}\binom{j}{n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{x}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{-\lambda}(n,j)\binom{j}{n-k}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
40
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.8
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

{n\brace k}_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{-\lambda}(n,j)\binom{j}{n-k}.

For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and Theorem 2.7, we have

(41)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}(k)_{n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{k+j}{n}A_{-\lambda}(n,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{-\lambda}(n,j)\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{k+j}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{-\lambda}(n,j)\sum_{k=1}^{m}\bigg(\binom{k+j+1}{n+1}-\binom{k+j}{n+1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{-\lambda}(n,j)\binom{m+j+1}{n+1}.

Therefore, by (
41
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.9
.

For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{m}(k)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{-\lambda}(n,j)\binom{m+j+1}{n+1}.

From (
12
), we note that

\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}(x)_{n-k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

Now, we observe that (see (
12
))

(42)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}e_{\lambda}^{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{m+1}(t)-1}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}=\frac{1}{t}\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{m+1}(t)-\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}(m+1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(\beta_{n,\lambda}(m+1)-\beta_{n,\lambda}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{\beta_{n+1,\lambda}(m+1)-\beta_{n+1,\lambda}}{n+1}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
42
), we get

(43)

\sum_{k=0}^{m}(k)_{n,\lambda}=\frac{\beta_{n+1,\lambda}(m+1)-\beta_{n+1,\lambda}}{n+1},

where
m,n
are non-negative integers.

Therefore, by (
43
) and Theorem 2.9, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.10
.

For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{-\lambda}(n,j)\binom{m+j+1}{n+1}=\frac{1}{n+1}\Big(\beta_{n+1,\lambda}(m+1)-\beta_{n+1,\lambda}\Big).

By using (
18
), we observe that

(44)

\displaystyle\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)_{n,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}{n\brace j}_{\lambda}x^{j}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{j}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}\binom{j}{k}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{j}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{j}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}\binom{j}{k}(k)_{n,\lambda}\frac{x^{j}}{(1-x)^{j+1}},

where
n
is a positive integer.

From (
44
) and (
20
), we have

(45)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k-1}=\frac{1}{x}\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)_{n,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x}\bigg)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}\binom{j}{k}(k)_{n,\lambda}\frac{x^{j-1}}{(1-x)^{j+1}}.

By (
19
) and (
45
), we get (see (
10
))

(46)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{xA_{n,\lambda}(x)}{(1-x)^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{n+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}A_{\lambda}(n,k)x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{n+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k-1)x^{k}.

From (
45
) and (
46
), we note that

(47)

\displaystyle(1-x)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}\binom{j}{k}(k)_{n,\lambda}\frac{x^{j}}{(1-x)^{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k-1)x^{k}.

Thus, by (
47
) and (
18
), we get

(48)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k-1)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{\lambda}(n,k-1)x^{k}

\displaystyle=(1-x)^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}\binom{j}{k}(k)_{n,\lambda}\frac{x^{j}}{(1-x)^{j}}

\displaystyle=(1-x)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!{n\brace k}_{\lambda}x^{k}(1-x)^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!{n\brace k}_{\lambda}x^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n-k}{j}(-1)^{n-k-j}x^{n-k-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}x^{n-j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-j}(-1)^{n-k-j}\binom{n-k}{j}k!{n\brace k}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}x^{j}(-1)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-k}{n-j}k!{n\brace k}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-j}{n-k}j!{n\brace j}_{\lambda}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
48
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.11
.

For
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

A_{\lambda}(n,k-1)=(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-j}{n-k}j!{n\brace j}_{\lambda}.

3.
Conclusion

There are many ways of defining the Eulerian polynomials and numbers, including the ones in (
1
), (
3
), and (
5
)-(
9
).
Another combinatorial interpretation of the Eulerian polynomial
A_{n}(x)
is as follows. For each permutation
\sigma
of the symmetric group
S_{n}
, the descent and the excedance of
\sigma
are respectively given by

\displaystyle d(\sigma)=|\{i\in[n-1]:\sigma(i)>\sigma(i+1)\}|,

\displaystyle e(\sigma)=|\{i\in[n-1]:\sigma(i)>i\}|,

where
[n-1]=\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
.
Then
A_{n}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle A_{n}(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}x^{d(\sigma)}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}x^{e(\sigma)}.

In this paper, after introducing the degenerate Eulerian polynomials and numbers we obtained degenerate version of the properties in (5)-(9), namely those in (a)-(e) in the Introduction, all giving alternative descriptions of those polynomials and numbers. Furthermore, we derived some results involving the degenerate Bernoulli numbers, the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second as well as the degenerate Eulerian numbers.

It is one of our future projects to continue to study degenerate versions,
\lambda
-analogues and probabilistic extensions of many special polynomials and numbers.

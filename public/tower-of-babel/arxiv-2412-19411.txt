An arbitrary order mixed finite element method with boundary value correction for the Darcy flow on curved domains
Yongli Hou, Yanqiu Wang

Abstract
We propose a boundary value correction method for the Brezzi-Douglas-Marini mixed finite element discretization of the Darcy flow with non-homogeneous Neumann boundary condition on 2D curved domains. The discretization is defined on a body-fitted triangular mesh, i.e. the boundary nodes of the mesh lie on the curved physical boundary. However, the boundary edges of the triangular mesh, which are straight, may not coincide with the curved physical boundary. A boundary value correction technique is then designed to transform the Neumann boundary condition from the physical boundary to the boundary of the triangular mesh. One advantage of the boundary value correction method is that it avoids using curved mesh elements and thus reduces the complexity of implementation. We prove that the proposed method reaches optimal convergence for arbitrary order discretizations. Supporting numerical results are presented. Key words: mixed finite element method, Neumann boundary condition, curved domain, boundary value correction method

\displaystyle(\bm{u}^{E}+\nabla p^{E},\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}=(\bm{u}^{E},\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}-(p^{E},\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h},p^{E}\rangle_{e}.

(4.8)

We point out that although

in

, the term
\bm{u}^{E}+\nabla p^{E}
is not in general equal to zero in
\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega
.
Next, using (

), it holds

\displaystyle(T^{m}\bm{u}^{E}+R^{m}\bm{u}^{E})\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}-\widetilde{g}_{N}=0,\quad\text{on}\,\Gamma_{h},

where
\widetilde{g}_{N}(\bm{x}_{h})=g_{N}(\rho(\bm{x}_{h}))
is a pull-back of the Neumann boundary data

from

to
\Gamma_{h}
.
Equation (

) can then be rewritten into

\displaystyle(\bm{u}^{E},\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle+(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E},\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle h_{K}^{-1}T^{m}\bm{u}^{E}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}},T^{m}\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}\rangle_{e}-(p^{E},\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}

(4.9)

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h},p^{E}\rangle_{e}=(\bm{u}^{E}+\nabla p^{E},\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}+(f^{E}+(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E}-f^{E}),\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle h_{K}^{-1}(\widetilde{g}_{N}-R^{m}\bm{u}^{E}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}),T^{m}\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}\rangle_{e}.

Again, note that
\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E}-f^{E}
vanishes in
\Omega
but not necessarily vanishes in
\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega
.

Similarly, for any
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
, one has

\displaystyle(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E},q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}=(f^{E}+(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E}-f^{E}),q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}.

(4.10)

We then introduce a discretization for (
3.1
), using (
4.9
)-(
4.10
).
First, for
\bm{w},\,\bm{v}\in H(\mathrm{div},\Omega\cup\Omega_{h})
that are smooth enough so that
T^{m}
is well-defined, and for
q\in L^{2}(\Omega\cup\Omega_{h})
, define bilinear forms
and linear forms

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{w},\bm{v}):

\displaystyle=(\bm{w},\bm{v})_{\Omega_{h}}+(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{w},\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v})_{\Omega_{h}}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle h_{K}^{-1}T^{m}\bm{w}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}},T^{m}\bm{v}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}\rangle_{e},

(4.11)

\displaystyle b_{h1}(\bm{v},q):

\displaystyle=-(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v},q)_{\Omega_{h}}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h},q\rangle_{e},

\displaystyle b_{h0}(\bm{v},q):

\displaystyle=-(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v},q)_{\Omega_{h}},

\displaystyle l_{h}(\bm{v}):

\displaystyle=(f^{E},\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v})_{\Omega_{h}}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle h_{K}^{-1}\widetilde{g}_{N},T^{m}\bm{v}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}\rangle_{e}.

Define the discrete formulation:
Find
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h})\in V_{h}\times Q_{0h}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})+b_{h1}(\bm{v}_{h},p_{h})&=l_{h}(\bm{v}_{h}),\quad&\forall&\bm{v}_{h}\in V_{h},\\
b_{h0}(\bm{u}_{h},q_{h})&=-(f^{E},q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}},\quad&\forall&q_{h}\in Q_{0h}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.12)

In the rest of this section, we shall prove the well-posedness of (

4.2
Well-posedness of (

We first define a mesh-dependent norm in

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}\|_{0,h}

\displaystyle=\left(\|\bm{v}\|_{H(\mathrm{div},\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\|h_{K}^{-1/2}T^{m}\bm{v}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}\|_{0,e}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Lemma 4.5
.

Under the assumption (
3.5
), for
\bm{v}_{h}\in V_{h}
we have

\displaystyle\|h_{K}^{-1/2}T_{1}^{m}\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,e}

\displaystyle\leq h|\bm{v}_{h}|_{1,K},

(4.13)

\displaystyle\|h_{K}^{-1/2}T_{1}^{m}\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,e}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,K}.

(4.14)

Proof.

By the definition of
T_{1}^{m}\bm{v}_{h}
in (
3.6
), and lemmas
2.1
-
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|h_{K}^{-1/2}T_{1}^{m}\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,e}

\displaystyle=h_{K}^{-1/2}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\delta_{h}^{j}}{j!}\partial_{\bm{\nu}_{h}}^{j}\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,e}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{m}h^{-1/2}\delta_{h}^{j}\|\partial_{\bm{\nu}_{h}}^{j}\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,e}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{\delta_{h}}{h}\right)^{j}|\bm{v}_{h}|_{1,K}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\delta_{h}}{h}\right)|\bm{v}_{h}|_{1,K}

\displaystyle\lesssim h|\bm{v}_{h}|_{1,K},

where we have used (

) in the last inequality. This completes the proof of (

).
Inequality (

) follows immediately from the inverse inequality in Lemma

.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Under the assumption (
3.5
), for
\bm{v}_{h}\in V_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h_{K}^{-1}\|\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h}\|_{0,e}^{2}\lesssim\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h}^{2}.

Proof.

For any

, by the definition of

and lemmas

,

, and

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h}\|_{0,e}

Summing up over

Lemma 4.7
.

For
\bm{u}_{h}
,
\bm{v}_{h}\in V_{h}
and
q_{h}\in Q_{0h}
, we have

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{0,h}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h},\qquad

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h}^{2},

\displaystyle b_{h1}(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}},\qquad

\displaystyle b_{h0}(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}.

Proof.

From the definition, it is obvious that
a_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})=\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h}^{2}
. The upper bounds of
a_{h}
and

follow immediately from the Schwarz inequality.

Using the Schwarz inequality and lemmas

,

, we obtain

\displaystyle|b_{h1}(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h_{K}^{-1/2}\|\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h}\|_{0,e}h_{K}^{1/2}\|q_{h}\|_{0,e}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}.

∎

Lemma

.
We then prove the

.
Note that a function

To this end, we first introduce the following notation to distinguish between two types of crescent-shaped regions

3

\Omega_{h}^{e}=\begin{cases}\Omega_{h}^{e,+},\quad\text{in}\,\Omega\backslash\Omega_{h},\\

Lemma 4.8
.

(Inf-Sup condition). When
h
is sufficiently small, there exist positive constants
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
independent of
h
such that, for
q_{h}\in Q_{0h}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{v}_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b_{h1}(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h}}\geq\beta_{1}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}},\qquad\sup_{\bm{v}_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b_{h0}(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h}}\geq\beta_{0}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}.

Proof.

Each function
q_{h}\in Q_{0h}\subset L_{0}^{2}(\Omega_{h})
can be naturally extended
to
\Omega\cup\Omega_{h}
, by filling the gap region
\Omega\backslash\Omega_{h}
with the same polynomial on neighboring mesh
element. For simplicity, we still denote the extension by
q_{h}
.
Define
\bar{q}_{h}=q_{h}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}q_{h}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
.
Since
q_{h}
is mean value free in
\Omega_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{q}_{h}=q_{h}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}q_{h}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega_{h}}q_{h}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)=q_{h}+(-1)^{\sigma}\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\int_{\Omega_{h}^{e}}q_{h}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x.

(4.15)

Similar to
[
23
]
, one gets

\displaystyle\|\bar{q}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}},\qquad(\bar{q}_{h},q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}=\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}^{2}.

(4.16)

It is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
) that there exists a
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying
\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}=-\bar{q}_{h}
on
\Omega

and
\|\bm{v}\|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim\|\bar{q}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}
,
where the hidden constant in
\lesssim
may depend on the shape of
\Omega
but not on
h
.
Extend
\bm{v}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by setting its value to be
0
outside
\Omega
. The extension is still denoted by
\bm{v}
, which shall not be mistaken for the extension
E\bm{v}=\bm{v}^{E}
defined in Lemma
4.4
.
It is clear that, after the zero-extension, one has
\bm{v}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega\cup\Omega)
and hence the interpolation
I_{h}\bm{v}\in V_{h}
is well-defined.
By the commutative property (
4.2
), the definition of
I_{h}
and (
4.16
), one has

\displaystyle b_{h1}(I_{h}\bm{v},q_{h})

\displaystyle=-(\mathrm{div}\,I_{h}\bm{v},q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle I_{h}\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h},q_{h}\rangle_{e}

(4.17)

\displaystyle=-(\Pi_{k-1}^{0}\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v},q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h},q_{h}\rangle_{e}

\displaystyle\geq-(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v},q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h^{-1/2}\|\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h}\|_{0,e}h^{1/2}\|q_{h}\|_{0,e}

\displaystyle\geq-(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v},q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}-\bigg(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h^{-1}\|\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h}\|_{0,e}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h\|q_{h}\|_{0,e}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq-\bigg((\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v},q_{h})_{\Omega}+(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v},q_{h})_{\Omega\backslash\Omega_{h}}\bigg)

\displaystyle-\bigg(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h^{-1}\|\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h}\|_{0,e}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h\|q_{h}\|_{0,e}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

We then estimate the right-hand side of (
4.17
) one by one.
Note that
\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}=-\bar{q}_{h}
on
\Omega
. By (
4.16
), (
4.15
) and Lemma
3.1
, one has

\displaystyle-(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v},q_{h})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=(\bar{q}_{h},q_{h})_{\Omega}=(\bar{q}_{h},q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}-(\bar{q}_{h},q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega}+(\bar{q}_{h},q_{h})_{\Omega\backslash\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle=\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}^{2}-(-1)^{\sigma}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\int_{\Omega_{h}^{e}}\bar{q}_{h}\,q_{h}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}^{2}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{e}}^{2}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\bigg(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\int_{\Omega_{h}^{e}}q_{h}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}^{2}-h\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}^{2}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}|\Omega_{h}^{e}|\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{e}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-O(h))\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}^{2}.

Using lemmas
4.4
and
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v},q_{h})_{\Omega\backslash\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\|\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{e}}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{e}}\leq\|\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}\|_{0,\Omega}\bigg(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}^{e}}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{1/2}\|\bm{v}\|_{1,\Omega}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}\lesssim h^{1/2}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}^{2}.

By the trace-inverse inequality in Lemma
2.3
, one gets

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h\|q_{h}\|_{0,e}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}.

Note that
\bm{v}|_{\Gamma}=0
and
\bm{v}=0
on
\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega
. By (
3.11
) and Lemma
4.4
, one has

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h^{-1}\|\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h}\|_{0,e}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h^{-1}\|\bm{v}\|_{0,e}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim h^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\|\bm{v}\|_{1,\Omega_{h}^{e}}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{1/2}\|\bm{v}\|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim h^{1/2}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}.

Combining the above, for
h
sufficiently small, one gets

\displaystyle b_{h1}(I_{h}\bm{v},q_{h})\geq(1-O(h^{1/2}))\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}^{2}\gtrsim\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}^{2}.

A similar argument gives

b_{h0}(I_{h}\bm{v},q_{h})\gtrsim\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}^{2}.

Then, for
i=0,1
, one obtains

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{v}_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b_{hi}(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h}}\geq\frac{b_{hi}(I_{h}\bm{v},q_{h})}{\|I_{h}\bm{v}\|_{0,h}}\gtrsim\frac{\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}^{2}}{\|I_{h}\bm{v}\|_{0,h}},

where
I_{h}\bm{v}
is the interpolation of the zero-extension of
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, as explained before.

Finally, we prove
\|I_{h}\bm{v}\|_{0,h}\lesssim\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}
, which combined with the above inequality will give the desired inf-sup condition.
By Lemma
4.3
and the property (
4.2
), we have

\|I_{h}\bm{v}\|_{0,K}\lesssim\|\bm{v}\|_{1,K},

and

\|\mathrm{div}\,I_{h}\bm{v}\|_{0,K}=\|\Pi_{k-1}^{0,K}\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}\|_{0,K}\leq\|\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}\|_{0,K}\lesssim|\bm{v}|_{1,K}.

By Lemma
4.5
,
4.3
and definitions of
T^{m}
and
T_{1}^{m}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|h_{K}^{-1/2}T^{m}(I_{h}\bm{v})\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}\|_{0,e}

\displaystyle\leq\|h_{K}^{-1/2}I_{h}\bm{v}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}\|_{0,e}+\|h_{K}^{-1/2}T_{1}^{m}(I_{h}\bm{v})\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}\|_{0,e}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|h_{K}^{-1/2}I_{h}\bm{v}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}\|_{0,e}+\|I_{h}\bm{v}\|_{0,K}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|h_{K}^{-1/2}I_{h}\bm{v}\|_{0,e}+\|\bm{v}\|_{1,K}.

By (
3.8
) and Lemma
3.1
and Inequality (
4.4
), it holds

\displaystyle\|h_{K}^{-1/2}I_{h}\bm{v}\|_{0,e}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1/2}(\|I_{h}\bm{v}\|_{0,\widetilde{e}}+h|I_{h}\bm{v}|_{1,\Omega_{h}^{e}})

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1/2}(\|I_{h}\bm{v}\|_{0,\widetilde{e}}+h^{3/2}|\bm{v}|_{1,K}).

Note that
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, using lemmas
2.1
,
3.1
and inequalities (
3.9
), (
4.6
), one gets

\displaystyle h_{K}^{-1/2}\|I_{h}\bm{v}\|_{0,\widetilde{e}}

\displaystyle=h_{K}^{-1/2}\|I_{h}\bm{v}-\bm{v}\|_{0,\widetilde{e}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1/2}(\|I_{h}\bm{v}-\bm{v}\|_{0,e}+h|I_{h}\bm{v}-\bm{v}|_{1,\Omega_{h}^{e}})

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}\|I_{h}\bm{v}-\bm{v}\|_{0,K}+|I_{h}\bm{v}-\bm{v}|_{1,K}+h^{1/2}|I_{h}\bm{v}|_{1,\Omega_{h}^{e}}+h^{1/2}|\bm{v}|_{1,\Omega_{h}^{e}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\bm{v}|_{1,K\cup\Omega_{h}^{e}}.

Combining the above, we obtain
\|I_{h}\bm{v}\|_{0,h}\lesssim\|\bm{v}\|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

4.3
Compatibility of (
4.12
) and an equivalent discrete form

Recall that a compatibility condition (
3.2
) is needed for the well-posedness of the continuous problem (
3.1
), as well as its weak formulation (
3.3
). We point out that the compatibility mechanism for the discrete problem works differently. In fact, the essential boundary condition
\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}=g_{N}
on
\Gamma
has been weakened in (
4.12
), and thus no relation is required between
\widetilde{g}_{N}
and
f^{E}
. In Section
4.2
, we have proved that the discrete system (
4.12
) admits a unique solution, and hence is “compatible”.

However, in practical implementation, the space
Q_{0h}
is not commonly used. It is more convenient to construct a set of basis for
Q_{h}
than for
Q_{0h}
. Hence we introduce an equivalent discrete problem:
Find
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h})\in V_{h}\times Q_{h}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})+b_{h1}(\bm{v}_{h},p_{h})&=l_{h}(\bm{v}_{h}),\quad&\forall&\bm{v}_{h}\in V_{h},\\
b_{h0}(\bm{u}_{h},q_{h})+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle\bm{u}_{h}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h},\bar{q}_{h}\rangle_{e}&=-(f^{E}-\overline{f^{E}},q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}},\quad&\forall&q_{h}\in Q_{h},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.19)

where
\bar{q}_{h}=\frac{1}{|\Omega_{h}|}\int_{\Omega_{h}}q_{h}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x
and
\overline{f^{E}}=\frac{1}{|\Omega_{h}|}\int_{\Omega_{h}}f^{E}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x
.

Lemma 4.9
.

The systems (
4.12
) and (
4.19
) are equivalent in the sense that they admit the same solution in
V_{h}\times Q_{0h}
.

Proof.

The space
Q_{h}
can be decomposed into
Q_{0h}\oplus\mathbb{R}
,
and the decomposition is orthogonal in the
L^{2}(\Omega_{h})
norm. Using integration by parts, it is obvious that
b_{h1}(\bm{v}_{h},1)=0
for all
\bm{v}_{h}\in V_{h}
. Since
q_{h}-\bar{q}_{h}\in Q_{0h}
, the second equation of (
4.12
) implies that, for all
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
,

\displaystyle b_{h0}(\bm{u}_{h},q_{h}-\bar{q}_{h})

\displaystyle=-(f^{E},q_{h}-\bar{q}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}},

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad b_{h0}(\bm{u}_{h},q_{h})+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle\bm{u}_{h}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h},\bar{q}_{h}\rangle_{e}

\displaystyle=-(f^{E},q_{h}-\bar{q}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}},

which is just the second equation of (
4.19
).
∎

As long as (
4.12
) is well-posed, problem (
4.19
) is solvable, i.e., the underlying discrete linear system is “compatible”. However, its solution is not unique since
(\bm{u}_{h},\,p_{h}+constant)
is also a solution. There are various ways to deal with this case, for example, using a Krylov subspace iterative solver, or adding a constraint to the linear system to ensure that the solution stays in
V_{h}\times Q_{0h}
.

5
Error analysis

We first obtain an error equation by subtracting equations (
4.9
)-(
4.10
) from the discrete problem (
4.12
)

\displaystyle E_{R}:=

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u}^{E},\bm{v}_{h})+b_{h1}(\bm{v}_{h},p_{h}-p^{E})+b_{h0}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u}^{E},q_{h})

(5.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\bm{u}^{E}+\nabla p^{E},\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}-(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E}-f^{E},\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}+(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E}-f^{E},q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h_{K}^{-1}\langle\widetilde{g}_{N}-T^{m}\bm{u}^{E}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}},T^{m}\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}\rangle_{e}.

Lemma 5.1
.

(cf.
[
12
]
). Assume that
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{\max\{l+1,m+2\}}(\Omega),\,p\in H^{l+1}(\Omega)
and
f\in H^{l}(\Omega)
satisfy Equation (
3.1
), then

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}^{E}+\nabla p^{E}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{l}\|D^{l}(\bm{u}^{E}+\nabla p^{E})\|_{0,\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E}-f^{E}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{l}\|D^{l}(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E}-f^{E})\|_{0,\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega},

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h_{K}^{-1}\|\widetilde{g}_{N}-T^{m}\bm{u}^{E}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}\|_{0,e}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{2m+2}h^{-1}\|D^{m+2}\bm{u}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}.

(5.4)

Proof.

Inequalities (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) follow immediately from (3.14) in
[
12
]
. The estimate of the residual (
5.4
) follows from (3.12) in
[
12
]
and Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Combining (
5.1
) and using Lemma
5.1
, one gets an estimate of the consistency error:

Lemma 5.2
.

(Consistency Error).
Assume that
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{\max\{l+1,m+2\}}(\Omega),\,p\in H^{l+1}(\Omega)
and
f\in H^{l}(\Omega)
satisfy Equation (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle|E_{R}|\lesssim\bigg[\delta^{l}\bigg(\|D^{l}(\bm{u}^{E}+\nabla p^{E})\|_{0,\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega}

\displaystyle+\|D^{l}(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E}-f^{E})\|_{0,\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad+\delta^{m+1}h^{-1/2}\|D^{m+2}\bm{u}\|_{0,\Omega}\bigg]\|\bm{v}_{h},q_{h}\|_{H}.

Lemma 5.3
.

(Interpolation Error).
Let
(\bm{v},p)\in\bm{H}^{r+1}(\Omega)\times H^{t}(\Omega)
with
\max\{m,1\}\leq r+1\leq k+1
and
0\leq t\leq k
.
Let
\bm{v}_{I}=I_{h}\bm{v}^{E}\in V_{h}
be the interpolation of
\bm{v}^{E}
, and
p_{I}\in Q_{0h}
be the
L^{2}
orthogonal projection of
p^{E}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}^{E}-\bm{v}_{I}\|_{0,h}+\|p^{E}-p_{I}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}\lesssim h^{\min\{r,t,k\}}(|\bm{v}|_{r,\Omega}+|\bm{v}|_{r+1,\Omega}+|p|_{t,\Omega}).

Proof.

By Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}^{E}-\bm{v}_{I}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{r}|\bm{v}^{E}|_{r,\Omega_{h}}\lesssim h^{r}|\bm{v}|_{r,\Omega},

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{div}(\bm{v}^{E}-\bm{v}_{I})\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{r}|\mathrm{div}\,\bm{v}^{E}|_{r,\Omega_{h}}\lesssim h^{r}|\bm{v}^{E}|_{r+1,\Omega_{h}}\lesssim h^{r}|\bm{v}|_{r+1,\Omega},

\displaystyle\|p^{E}-p_{I}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{t}|p^{E}|_{t,\Omega_{h}}\lesssim h^{t}|p|_{t,\Omega},

By Lemma
2.1
, inequality (
3.5
) and interpolation error (
4.6
), one has

\displaystyle\|h_{K}^{-1/2}T^{m}(\bm{v}^{E}-\bm{v}_{I})\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}}\|_{0,e}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{m}\delta_{h}^{j}h_{K}^{-1/2}(h_{K}^{-1/2}|\bm{v}^{E}-\bm{v}_{I}|_{j,K}+h_{K}^{1/2}|\bm{v}^{E}-\bm{v}_{I}|_{j+1,K})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{m}\delta_{h}^{j}h_{K}^{-1}h^{r+1-j}|\bm{v}^{E}|_{r+1,K}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{m}\bigg(\frac{\delta_{h}}{h}\bigg)^{j}h^{r-j}|\bm{v}^{E}|_{r+1,K}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{r}|\bm{v}^{E}|_{r+1,K}.

Combining the above and using the definitions of norms, we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(\bm{u},p)\in\bm{H}^{\max\{r+1,l+1,m+2\}}(\Omega)\times H^{\max\{t,l+1\}}(\Omega)
be the solution to Problem (
3.1
),
f\in H^{l}(\Omega)
,
and
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h})\in V_{h}\times Q_{0h}
be the discrete solution of (
4.12
). Then

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u}_{I}\|_{0,h}+\|p_{h}-p_{I}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle h^{\min\{r,t,k\}}(|\bm{u}|_{r,\Omega}+|\bm{u}|_{r+1,\Omega}+|p|_{t,\Omega})+\delta^{m+1}h^{-1/2}\|D^{m+2}\bm{u}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle+\delta^{l}\bigg(\|D^{l}(\bm{u}^{E}+\nabla p^{E})\|_{0,\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega}+\|D^{l}(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E}-f^{E})\|_{0,\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega}\bigg).

where
\bm{u}_{I}=I_{h}\bm{u}^{E}\in V_{h}
is the interpolation of
\bm{u}^{E}
, and
p_{I}\in Q_{0h}
is the
L^{2}
orthogonal projection of
p^{E}

Proof.

From (
4.18
), one has

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u}_{I},p_{h}-p_{I}\|_{H}\lesssim\sup_{(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})\in V_{h}\times Q_{0h}}\frac{B_{h}((\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u}_{I},p_{h}-p_{I}),(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h}))}{\|\bm{v}_{h},q_{h}\|_{H}}.

Note that

\displaystyle B_{h}((\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u}_{I},p_{h}-p_{I}),(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h}))=

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u}^{E},\bm{v}_{h})+b_{h1}(\bm{v}_{h},p_{h}-p^{E})+b_{h0}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u}^{E},q_{h})

\displaystyle+a_{h}(\bm{u}^{E}-\bm{u}_{I},\bm{v}_{h})+b_{h1}(\bm{v}_{h},p^{E}-p_{I})+b_{h0}(\bm{u}^{E}-\bm{u}_{I},q_{h})

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle E_{R}+E_{h}.

By the definition of
\bm{u}_{I}
and
p_{I}
, one gets

\displaystyle b_{h0}(\bm{u}^{E}-\bm{u}_{I},q_{h})

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{div}\,(\bm{u}_{I}-\bm{u}^{E}),q_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\bm{u}^{E}-\bm{u}_{I},\nabla q_{h})_{K}-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle(\bm{u}^{E}-\bm{u}_{I})\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h},q_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}=0,

and

\displaystyle b_{h1}(\bm{v}_{h},p^{E}-p_{I})

\displaystyle=b_{h0}(\bm{v}_{h},p^{E}-p_{I})+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h},p^{E}-p_{I}\rangle_{e}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h},p^{E}-p_{I}\rangle_{e}.

Therefore

\displaystyle E_{h}

\displaystyle=a_{h}(\bm{u}^{E}-\bm{u}_{I},\bm{v}_{h})+b_{h1}(\bm{v}_{h},p^{E}-p_{I})+b_{h0}(\bm{u}^{E}-\bm{u}_{I},q_{h})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(\bm{u}^{E}-\bm{u}_{I},\bm{v}_{h})+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}\langle\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h},p^{E}-p_{I}\rangle_{e}.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, lemmas
5.3
,
4.6
,
2.1
,
4.4
and Inequality (
4.5
), one gets

\displaystyle E_{h}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{u}^{E}-\bm{u}_{I}\|_{0,h}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}}h_{K}^{-1/2}\|\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{\mathop{}\mathrm{n}}_{h}\|_{0,e}h_{K}^{1/2}\|p^{E}-p_{I}\|_{0,e}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|\bm{u}^{E}-\bm{u}_{I}\|_{0,h}+\|p^{E}-p_{I}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}+h|p^{E}-p_{I}|_{1,\Omega_{h}})\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{\min\{r,t,k\}}(|\bm{u}|_{r,\Omega}+|\bm{u}|_{r+1,\Omega}+|p|_{t,\Omega})\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{0,h}.

Combining the above estimate of
E_{h}
above and the estimate of
E_{R}
in Lemma
5.2
, we obtain the proof of the lemma.
∎

Using the above lemmas and the triangle inequality, we get the main theorem of this section:

Theorem 5.1
.

Under the same assumption of Lemma
5.4
, one has

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}^{E}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{0,h}+\|p^{E}-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle h^{\min\{r,t,k\}}(|\bm{u}|_{r,\Omega}+|\bm{u}|_{r+1,\Omega}+|p|_{t,\Omega})+\delta^{m+1}h^{-1/2}\|D^{m+2}\bm{u}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle+\delta^{l}\bigg(\|D^{l}(\bm{u}^{E}+\nabla p^{E})\|_{0,\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega}+\|D^{l}(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E}-f^{E})\|_{0,\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega}\bigg).

Moreover, the terms
\|D^{l}(\bm{u}^{E}+\nabla p^{E})\|_{0,\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega}
and
\|D^{l}(\mathrm{div}\,\bm{u}^{E}-f^{E})\|_{0,\Omega_{h}\backslash\Omega}
vanish when
\Omega
is convex.

Remark 5.1
.

(An implementation trick). When
m=k
, one has
T^{m}\bm{v}_{h}=\bm{v}_{h}
for all
\bm{v}_{h}\in V_{h}
, which greatly simplifies the implementation as one no longer needs to compute the Taylor expansion.

For a given polynomial order
k\geq 1
, we want to find when the proposed discretization (
4.12
) reaches the optimal convergence rate
O(h^{k})
.
Suppose that Inequality (
3.5
) holds, that is,
\delta\lesssim h^{2}
.
Then Theorem
5.1
indicates that one has to have

r\geq k,\quad t\geq k,\quad 2l\geq k,\quad 2m+3/2\geq k,

to obtain
O(h^{k})
convergence. By taking
r=t=k
and
l=\max\{1,k-1\}
, we get the following corollary:

Corollary 5.1
.

Assume (
3.5
) holds,
\max\{0,k/2-3/4\}\leq m\leq k
, and
l=\max\{1,k-1\}
.
Let
(\bm{u},p)\in\bm{H}^{k+2}(\Omega)\times H^{l+1}(\Omega)
be the solution to Problem (
3.1
),
f\in H^{l}(\Omega)
,
and
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h})\in V_{h}\times Q_{0h}
be the discrete solution of (
4.12
), then

\|\bm{u}^{E}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{0,h}+\|p^{E}-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}\lesssim h^{k}\left(\|\bm{u}\|_{k+2,\Omega}+\|p\|_{l+1,\Omega}+\|f\|_{l,\Omega}\right).

(5.6)

When
\Omega
is convex, the above result holds for
l=k-1
.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we consider the mixed finite element method for second-order elliptic equations on domains with curved boundaries.
A boundary value correction technique is proposed to compensate for the loss of accuracy in the discretization.
The proposed scheme achieves optimal convergence.
Numerical experiments further validate the theoretical results.

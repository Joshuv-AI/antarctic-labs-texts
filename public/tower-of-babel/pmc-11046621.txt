Don't miss the diagnosis: neurological manifestations as presenting features of COVID-19
Authors: Arina Madan, Pinky Agarwal, Sudeshna Ray, Brad Tyson
Published: 2024

Introduction

While respiratory manifestations of COVID-19 are commonly reported in the literature, neurological manifestations are less well recognised as presenting features of COVID-19. We report on neurological manifestations of COVID-19 in 404 patients seen in outpatient clinics and hospitalised at Evergreen Hospital, Kirkland. To our knowledge, this is the largest single centre study of neurological manifestations in COVID-19 to date.

Methods

Study data were taken contemporaneously from case records for 404 patients in 2020. All neurological manifestations were confirmed via chart review by a consultant neurologist and consultant neuropsychologist.

Results

In this study, 51.5% of patients presented with central nervous (CNS) symptoms (208/404). The most prevalent CNS symptoms were altered mental status (21.3%, 86), headache (20.3%, 2) and dizziness (7.7%, 31). Of the 86 patients presenting with altered mental status, 56% had pre-existing dementia. In total, 40.3% of patients presented with peripheral nervous (PNS) symptoms, with the most prevalent PNS symptoms being myalgia (32.4%, 131,) impairment of taste (6.7%, 27) and smell (4.5%, 18).

Neurological findings were found in 295 of 404 patients (73%). Acute neurological manifestations were seen in 99 of 404 patients (24.5%) with altered mental status being the presenting feature in 86 patients (21.3%), critical illness myopathy in eight (2%) and stroke including one haemorrhagic stroke in three patients (0.7%). Six patients had multiple acute neurological manifestations.

Discussion

This study highlights that clinicians can easily miss the diagnosis of COVID-19 if it does not present with respiratory symptoms. A significant number of patients in this study presented with neurological manifestations of COVID-19 (208) and this may well have been missed in an outpatient setting due to not all patients being routinely screened for COVID-19. Clinicians need to have a higher index of suspicion for COVID-19 in patients presenting with neurological symptoms and a neurological systems review needs to be built in to admission proformas and clinic consultations to prevent community and inpatient spread.

Conclusion

This study is important in drawing attention to neurological manifestations of COVID-19 that may be overlooked due to acute respiratory complications. COVID-19 may present as altered mental status initially in older people and particularly in patients with dementia. It is important to recognise neurological manifestations of COVID-19 in outpatient clinics to allow early detection and prevention of community spread. A neurological systems review should be undertaken for inpatients for early detection of COVID-19 in hospital admissions, particularly when patients are being admitted.

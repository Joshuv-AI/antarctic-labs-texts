ALIDHE phase 3b study design: ivosidenib + azacitidine in adults with newly diagnosed IDH1 mutant acute myeloid leukemia
Authors: Paresh Vyas, Sam Salek, Susana Vives, Christian Recher, Hartmut Döhner, Adriano Venditti, Hélène Derrien, Stéphanie Chatin, Anne-Marie De La Bigne, Jana Pelouchova, Ralph Hills, Samantha Nier
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionIvosidenib is an inhibitor of mutant isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 (mIDH1) that is approved alone or in combination with azacitidine in patients with mIDH1 acute myeloid leukemia (AML) that are ineligible to receive intensive chemotherapy.AimsHere we describe the design of ALIDHE, an international, multicenter, single-arm, open-label Phase 3b study of ivosidenib + azacitidine for treatment of newly diagnosed mIDH1 AML in usual clinical practice.MethodsThe primary endpoints are adverse events (AEs), clinical laboratory anomalies assessed as AEs, patients requiring transfusion and number of units transfused, and infection rate. The impact of ivosidenib + azacitidine treatment on health-related quality of life, healthcare resource utilization and measurable residual disease will also be assessed.Clinical trial registration numberNCT05907057 (ClinicalTrials.gov)

Introduction

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) is the most common acute leukemia in adults, with more than 20,000 and 22,000 new cases diagnosed in Europe and America each year, respectively [1,2]. Somatic mutations in isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 (IDH1) are found in approximately 2.2–10% of patients with AML [3–5], and in up to 16.0% of patients with de novo cytogenetically normal AML [5,6]. IDH1 mutations are frequently found in AML patients presenting with diploid or other intermediate-risk cytogenetics, like trisomy 8, and a maintained platelet count [7]. IDH1 mutations lead to the accumulation of D-2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG), an oncometabolite that competitively inhibits enzymes that regulate the epigenome. This ultimately leads to delayed and impaired hemopoietic differentiation [7]. Studies have shown that mutant IDH1 (mIDH1) AML is more common in older patients and is associated with a poorer prognosis, particularly in cytogenetically normal AML [6,8,9].

The treatment for medically fit patients with AML is intensive chemotherapy (IC), usually an anthracycline with cytarabine [10]. However, most patients with AML are ineligible for IC and are typically treated with less intensive, non-curative treatments, which have poorer outcomes than IC [11,12]. There remains an unmet need for treatment options for patients that are medically unfit for IC.

Ivosidenib is an oral mIDH1-specific inhibitor that lowers 2-HG levels. Ivosidenib is currently approved in Europe and the USA in combination with azacitidine for the treatment of patients with newly diagnosed mIDH1 AML ineligible for IC [13]. This approval was based on the results of a Phase 3, multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled clinical study (AGILE, NCT03173248) [14], and long-term follow-up in this trial showed median overall survival (OS) was longer in patients treated with ivosidenib + azacitidine compared to those treated with placebo + azacitidine (29.3 and 7.9 months, respectively) [15]. During the study, grade 3 or higher hematological adverse events (AEs) occurred at a higher frequency in the ivosidenib + azacitidine group compared to the placebo + azacitidine group (70% and 64%, respectively) [14]. Whereas infection rates were lower in patients receiving ivosidenib + azacitidine than patients receiving placebo + azacitidine (21% and 30%, respectively) [14]. Additionally, differentiation syndrome and QT prolongation were AEs of special interest (AESI) that occurred more frequently among patients treated with ivosidenib + azacitidine (14.1% and 9.9%, respectively) compared with placebo + azacitidine (8.2% and 4.1%, respectively) [14]. Despite occurring in 10 patients or fewer in either group [14], these AESIs along with potential pharmacological interactions of the ivosidenib + azacitidine combination with other treatments warrant additional characterization [14].

Despite the proven clinical benefit of ivosidenib + azacitidine in AGILE, data from the real-world, including patients' lived experiences, are increasingly recognized as important to supplement clinical trial data. Consistent with the efficacy demonstrated in the AGILE study, real-world evidence from a retrospective United States-wide cohort study of patients with AML ineligible for IC reported higher response rates and longer event-free survival with ivosidenib + hypomethylating agent (HMA) than venetoclax + HMA [16], but studies of ivosidenib + azacitidine in the real-world setting are lacking. Therefore, ALIDHE, an ongoing, single-arm, open-label Phase 3b study, was initiated to obtain safety, tolerability and efficacy data on ivosidenib + azacitidine in routine practice. Other objectives of ALIDHE are to assess the impact of treatment on health-related quality of life (QoL), healthcare resource utilization (HCRU) and measurable residual disease.

One distinctive feature of ALIDHE is the active participation of the patient group Acute Leukemia Advocates Network (ALAN) in the development of the study, which is described in further detail elsewhere [17]. Here we describe the study protocol, which includes a summary of the patient input into the study concept.

Methods

Study design

ALIDHE is an international, multicenter, single-arm, open-label Phase 3b study to evaluate the safety, tolerability and efficacy of ivosidenib + azacitidine for the treatment of mIDH1 AML in newly diagnosed adult patients ineligible for IC. The expected study duration is approximately 3.5 years, and at time of publication ALIDHE is open to enrollment (Figures 1 and 2). The participating countries include Austria, Belgium, Canada, France, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, The Netherlands, Portugal, Romania, Spain, and the United Kingdom. ALAN is an independent global network of patient organizations (56 member organizations from 52 countries) that aims to strengthen patient advocacy and change patient outcomes within the acute leukemia research environment. The ALIDHE study was designed collaboratively with ALAN, commencing with workshops to gather insights into patient’s lived experiences with AML.

Eligibility criteria

The study aims to enroll approximately 245 patients across 100 centers in 13 countries. Specialists routinely involved in the treatment of patients with AML will be included. Criteria for center selection includes the ability to obtain genomic analyses to confirm mIDH1, and to purchase and administer azacitidine, in accordance with the 2022 ELN Guidelines [18]. Selection criteria and basic site information will be collected via a site qualification survey.

All patients presenting during the enrollment period will be assessed for eligibility according to the defined selection criteria (Table 1) and all eligible patients will be consecutively proposed to be enrolled in the study. Adult (≥18 years of age) patients enrolled into this study are newly diagnosed with mIDH1 AML and deemed ineligible for treatment with IC. Patients that have received prior treatment for AML are not eligible for this study, with the exception of hydroxyurea or leukapheresis for white blood count control. Interventions

Daily study treatment will begin on Cycle 1, Day 1 and treatment cycles are 28 days long (±2 days). Ivosidenib will be administered orally once daily at a dose of 500 mg provided as 250 mg tablets. Azacitidine will be administered subcutaneously or intravenously according to local product information at a dose of 75 mg/m2/day for 7 days (on Days 1–7, or Days 1–5 and 8–9) in every 28-day cycle, starting on Day 1. Patients receiving ivosidenib + azacitidine will be assessed prospectively every 28 days for a maximum of 112 weeks (28 cycles; Figure 1), and then every 12 weeks for OS until death, withdrawal of informed consent, or until the end of the study. Ivosidenib will be provided by Servier throughout the trial. Once ivosidenib is approved and commercially available in each country, enrollment may then continue with newly enrolled patients receiving commercial ivosidenib, whereas patients that are already enrolled in ALIDHE will switch to commercial ivosidenib and continue to be followed according to the protocol. This will allow for continuous data collection in this study to bridge the gap between the pivotal AGILE trial and routine clinical practice [14].

Investigators may decide to delay or modify the dose of ivosidenib for any AE, based on clinical judgment. Dose delays are discouraged, except as needed for the management of AEs, dose holds during palliative radiotherapy, and washout from palliative radiotherapy. Dose delays of up to 28 days will be permitted at the discretion of investigators for the management of AEs and for mitigating circumstances (e.g., planned procedures).

Patients that discontinue treatment with ivosidenib + azacitidine at any point will receive subsequent therapy according to local clinical practice and will be followed-up every 12 weeks for OS until death, withdrawal of informed consent, or until the end of the study. Outcomes

Primary endpoints

The primary objective of ALIDHE is to assess the safety and tolerability of treatment with ivosidenib + azacitidine using the endpoints of AEs, clinical laboratory anomalies assessed as AEs, patients requiring transfusion and number of units transfused, and rate of infections.

AEs are graded by Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0 [19]. The incidence of treatment emergent AEs (TEAEs; events with onset dates during the on-treatment period or worsening from baseline) will be included. Events with missing onset dates will be included as treatment emergent. Multiple occurrences of the same AE are counted once at the greatest severity and strongest relationship with the study drug. All AEs will be listed by patient, along with information regarding onset, duration, severity, relationship to study drug, action taken with study drug, treatment of the event, and the outcome.

Serious adverse events (SAEs) are defined as any AE that is life-threatening, medically significant, a congenital anomaly/birth defect, requires inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization, or results in persistent or significant disability/incapacity, or death. AESIs are defined as any AE (serious or non-serious) which is of medical interest or concern to investigators, including differentiation syndrome grade ≥2 and QT prolongation assessed as grade ≥3.

The incidence of AEs leading to ivosidenib + azacitidine discontinuation, interruption or dose reduction, and AEs leading to death will be reported. Clinical laboratory anomalies will be assessed as AEs. Mental health status will be assessed by investigators at each visit during the treatment period (Table 2) according to individual center practices and will be defined as an AE. The number of patients requiring transfusion (platelet and red blood cell), and number of units transfused, will be reported. Rate of infections, based on clinical diagnosis by physicians, will also be recorded.

Clinical and biomedical assessments

Efficacy endpoints, including event-free survival (EFS), complete response (CR; defined as presence of < 5% bone marrow blasts, absence of circulating blasts, blasts with Auer rods and extramedullary disease, absolute neutrophil count [ANC] ≥1.0 × 109/l, platelet count ≥100 × 109/l) [18], CR with partial hematologic recovery (CRh; defined as ANC ≥0.5 ×109/l and platelet count ≥50 ×109/l, with all other CR criteria met) [18], CR with incomplete hematological recovery (CRi; all CR criteria met except for residual neutropenia [ < 1.0 × 109/l] or thrombocytopenia [ < 100 × 109/l]) [18], duration of response (DOR; overall and by response type), time to response (TTR) and OS.

Subjective assessments

QoL of patients is measured by Hematological Malignancy Patient-Reported Outcome (HM-PRO) and 5-level EuroQol 5-Dimensions (EQ-5D-5 L), and of family/carers by Family-Reported Outcome Measure (FROM-16) [20–22]. The HM-PRO consists of two scales, one to measure the impact of the malignancy on a patient’s health-related QoL and one to capture the severity of disease- or treatment-related symptoms. The FROM-16 consists of 16 scored items to assess the impact on the QoL of an adult family member or partner of a patient with any health condition or disease. The questionnaires will be given to patients for self-administration at each scheduled visit during the treatment period and will be completed electronically on site on provided tablets.

HCRU, measured by length of hospital stay, number of outpatient and emergency room (ER) visits, and proportion of days patients are at home, will be reported. Molecular profiling method for mIDH1 diagnosis, time to results, allele subtypes, and co-mutation profiles will also be recorded.

The exploratory endpoints of the study include genetic, epigenetic, and global gene expression profiles in bone marrow and/or peripheral blood samples for evaluation of measurable residual disease and characterization of biomarkers that may correlate with clinical outcomes.

Patient involvement in study design

The study design of ALIDHE was informed by input from a patient steering committee that includes patients and patient advocates from ALAN and has been reported in more detail [17]. Feedback from ALAN was used to guide protocol adjustments to reduce burden of participation for patients, such as minimizing additional clinical visits, optimizing study duration, and adapting study documents for comprehension.

Data collection, management and analysis

All data will be collected from the information routinely recorded in patient medical records or will be prospectively recorded by the investigator for the purposes of this study. Relevant data time points for the study are summarized in Table 2. All data will be collected and entered directly into the electronic data capture (EDC) system. All participating centers will have access to data regarding patients enrolled from their individual site. All sites will be trained on the use of the online EDC system and will be responsible for entering extracted patient data into a secure internet-based EDC registry database via the eCRF. Investigators should review, electronically sign, and date each eCRF, and investigators and site personnel will be able to access their account with a username and password.

Site monitoring will be performed by IQVIA clinical research associates (IQVIA Holdings Inc, North Carolina, USA) to examine adherence with the protocol and adherence to data collection procedures. Monitoring will include source data verification by review of original patient records and will close out at each site after the last patient’s follow-up assessment is completed. Audits by the Servier’s monitoring team and competent regulatory authorities may be carried out at any time during or after the study and will include inspection of all study-related documents.

Sample size was not driven by hypothesis testing due to the study design and descriptive nature of the analysis. Instead, sample size was chosen to ensure robustness of related AESIs. The estimated sample size assumed, based on data from AGILE [14], an incidence of 9.9% for QT prolongation Grade 3 or higher and 14.1% for differentiation syndrome grade 2 or higher, both with a dropout rate of 10%. The sample size of 245 ensures a 95% confidence interval with a width of 0.04 for QT prolongation and 0.046 for differentiation syndrome. Sample size calculations were performed using R version 3.6.1. Strategies to achieve target sample size included newsletters and for-cause monitoring visits. The full analysis set (FAS) will include all patients who are enrolled in the study. The safety analysis set will include all patients who received at least one dose of the study treatment. The safety analysis set will be the primary analysis for all safety analyses, unless otherwise specified.

Statistical evaluation will be performed using Statistical Analysis System (SAS)® software version 9.4 or higher (SAS Institute, Cary, NC). Categorical data will be summarized by frequency distributions (numbers and percentages of patients), and continuous data will be summarized by descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation [SD], median, minimum, and maximum). Time-to-event endpoints will be estimated using the Kaplan-Meier methods, and where appropriate, point estimates and 95% confidence intervals will be provided.

Interim and final analyses are planned for this study and will include all endpoints (including EFS and OS). The duration of the active phase and OS follow-up may be reevaluated based on the results of the interim analysis.

Ethics and dissemination

An institutional review board/independent ethics committee and Competent Authority approved the protocol and informed consent form before study enrollment began. This study will be conducted in accordance with the approved protocol and all applicable laws and regulations, including Good Clinical Practice (GCP), Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practices, the ethical principles that have their origins in the Declaration of Helsinki, and applicable privacy laws.

Changes to the protocol will be documented in written protocol amendments. Major amendments will be submitted to relevant institutional review board/independent ethics committees and Competent Authorities for approval. Minor protocol amendments will be filed by each participating site and will be submitted to relevant institutional review board/independent ethics committees and Competent Authorities, where required by applicable regulations.

Before any data collection occurs, patients must sign and date the approved informed consent form. Investigators must inform patients of the objectives, risks, benefits and requirements imposed by the study prior to gaining informed consent. Data protection and privacy regulations will be strictly observed in the capturing, processing and storing of patient data. General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) will be followed to ensure patient confidentiality. Patient data will be housed and maintained by IQVIA and will not be shared with patients, insurance companies, employers, family members, general physicians or any other third party, unless required to do so by law. Each patient will be assigned a unique patient identifier upon enrollment and precautions will be taken to prevent genetic data being linked to the identity of a patient.

Datasets, summary results and a lay summary will be available upon request from www.clinicaltrials.servier.com and will be submitted for publication in scientific literature within 18 months of the end of the study.

Conclusion

This international, multicenter, single-arm, open-label Phase 3b study will evaluate the safety, tolerability and efficacy of ivosidenib + azacitidine, to supplement data obtained from the Phase 3 AGILE study and bridge the gap between these pivotal trial results and the use of ivosidenib in routine clinical practice, when it is commercially available. This study will collect safety data from a broad population of patients receiving ivosidenib + azacitidine that may be receiving concomitant therapies and have co-morbidities. The ALIDHE trial will also provide data on the impact of treatment on patient/carer QoL, HCRU and measurable residual disease. The descriptive nature of the study, the lack of comparator, and the potential for selection bias due to omission of objective tools for assessment of ineligibility for intensive chemotherapy, should be taken into consideration when extrapolating the findings of ALIDHE, which will be discussed further when presenting the study results.

An important aspect of the study design is the collaboration with ALAN to ensure that patient needs are met throughout the study and to minimize the burden of participation for patients and their family/carers [17]. Patient involvement in the design of ALIDHE, along with data on the use of ivosidenib + azacitidine in routine clinical practice, will allow balanced evaluation of the safety, tolerability and efficacy of ivosidenib + azacitidine in local practice and the impact of the treatment on patient and family/carer QoL.

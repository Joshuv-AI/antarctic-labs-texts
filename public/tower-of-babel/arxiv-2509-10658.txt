DESY 25-124 Renormalization Group Approach to Confinement
Gerrit Schierholz

Abstract
While we have several complementary models of confinement, some of which are phenomenologically appealing, we do not have the ability to calculate analytically even simple aspects of confinement, let alone have a framework to eventually prove confinement. The problem we are facing is to evolve the theory from the perturbative regime to the long distance confining regime. This is generally achieved by renormalization group transformations. With the gradient flow we now have a technique to address the problem from first principles. The primary focus is on the running coupling α S ​ ( μ ) \alpha_{S}(\mu) , from which confinement can be concluded alone. A central point is that the gluon condensate is scale invariant, which reflects its self-similar behavior across different scales. Building on that, we derive α S ​ ( μ ) ≃ Λ S 2 / μ 2 \alpha_{S}(\mu)\simeq\Lambda_{S}^{2}/\mu^{2} , which evolves to the infrared fixed point 1 / α S = 0 1/\alpha_{S}=0 in accordance with infrared slavery. This shows that the only important factor is the presence of homogeneous vacuum fields, represented by condensates, which is a universal feature that QCD shares with many other models. The analytical statements are supported by numerical simulations.



1
Introduction

The most salient feature of QCD is color confinement. Confinement cannot be adequately described by perturbative methods but requires nonperturbative techniques for analysis. The nonperturbative structure of the theory manifests itself in the presence of vacuum condensates, analogous to those in condensed matter physics. They refer to the lowest energy state of the system, the ground state. Most important is the gluon condensate, which is an anti-screening, color paramagnetic medium formed by gluon self-interaction. The effect it has on a colored test charge is described by the renormalization group.

The renormalization group approach is a strategy for dealing with problems involving different length scales. With the gradient flow
[
1
,
2
]
we now have a tool that is a continuous realization of Wilson’s renormalization group transformation of the action, and as such provides an analytical means of exploring the nonperturbative properties of the theory. It evolves the gauge field along the gradient of the action. The flow of gauge fields is defined by

\partial_{\,t}\,B_{\mu}(t,x)=D_{\nu}G_{\mu\nu}(t,x)\,,\quad G_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\,B_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\,B_{\mu}+[B_{\mu},B_{\nu}]\,,

(1)

where
D_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative and
B_{\mu}(t=0,x)=A_{\mu}(x)
is the initial gauge field generated by the action

S_{G}=\frac{1}{2g_{0}^{2}}\int d^{4}x\,\textrm{Tr}\,F_{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\,,

(2)

where
g_{0}
is the bare coupling constant, which is kept fixed. The renormalization scale is appropriately defined by
\mu=1/\sqrt{8t}
, where
\sqrt{8t}
is the smoothing range over which
B_{\mu}
is averaged. We denote the vacuum energy density at flow time
t
by

E(t)=\frac{1}{2}\,\textrm{Tr}\;G_{\mu\nu}G_{\mu\nu}\,.

(3)

The vacuum expectation value of
E(t)
defines a renormalized (running) coupling in the gradient flow scheme
[
2
,
3
]
,

\alpha_{GF}(\mu)=\frac{4\pi}{3}\,t^{2}\langle E(t)\rangle\,.

(4)

The corresponding
\beta
function is given by

\mu\,\frac{\partial\,\alpha_{GF}(\mu)}{\partial\,\mu}=-2t\,\frac{\partial\,\alpha_{GF}(\mu)}{\partial\,t}=\beta_{GF}(\alpha_{GF})\,,

(5)

which encodes the dependence of
\alpha_{GF}(\mu)
on the scale parameter
\mu
. In fact, as we move down in energy
\mu
the theory makes a copy of itself with a new value of
\alpha_{GF}(\mu)
in a self-similar fashion.

The single most important question that must be answered before the problem of confinement can be settled is the behavior of the effective coupling
\alpha_{GF}(\mu)
in the limit
\mu\rightarrow 0
.

2
Flow to confinement

Let us consider the pure SU(3) gauge theory first. We do not expect any conceptual differences, as the effect of virtual gluons dominates over internal quark loops for a small number of flavors. In this case the gluon condensate is the only fundamental mass parameter of the theory. The gluon condensate we refer to is
[
4
]

G=\frac{\alpha_{S}}{\pi}\,\langle F_{\mu\nu}^{a}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}\rangle=2\,\frac{\alpha_{S}}{\pi}\,\textrm{Tr}\,\langle F_{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\rangle

(6)

(
S
: scheme). We consider
G
to be properly renormalized. When expressed in terms of gauge fields generated by the gradient flow, as we will consider, the gluon condensate is automatically renormalized
[
5
]
. This has been utilized in
[
6
]
, where
G
is defined by a small flow-time expansion.

The gluon condensate
G
anomalously breaks scale invariance. It can be connected to the vacuum expectation value of the trace of the energy momentum tensor, the trace or scale anomaly,

\langle\Theta_{\lambda\lambda}\rangle=\frac{\beta(\alpha_{S})}{\alpha_{S}}\,\left\langle\frac{1}{4g_{0}^{2}}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}\right\rangle_{R}\,,

(7)

which sometimes is taken for the gluon condensate
[
7
]
. The subscript
R
denotes renormalized fields defined in the scheme
S
. The trace (
7
) is obtained by taking the functional derivative of the action with respect to the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
. This limits the derivation to zero flow time. The computation of (
7
) is highly nontrivial. In
[
8
]
an attempt has been made to compute the renormalized energy momentum tensor from small flow times.

We henceforth understand scale invariance to mean invariance under changes of
t=1/8\mu^{2}
, with
g_{0}^{2}
held fixed. The renormalized gluon condensate
G
is scale invariant. This is known to be the case in perturbation theory
[
9
,
10
]
. (Note that the two-loop result
[
10
]
is scale invariant, but not renormalization group invariant.) We are seeking a nonperturbative solution. This was verified in
[
4
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
, for example. The Appendix contains two proofs. The first is a proof for finite flow time, and the second is a proof that substantiates the work of
[
4
,
11
]
, using the gradient flow as a source of regularization.

This is put into practice by writing

G=\frac{\alpha_{GF}(\mu)}{\pi}\,\langle G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}\rangle=4\frac{\alpha_{GF}(\mu)}{\pi}\,\langle E(t)\rangle

(8)

in the gradient flow scheme, with the result

\mu\,\frac{\partial\,G}{\partial\,\mu}=-2t\,\frac{\partial\,G}{\partial\,t}=0\,.

(9)

The effect of the gradient flow is that as the time increases, higher frequencies are integrated out step by step, resulting in a decrease of energy density
\langle E(t)\rangle
, which is compensated for by an increase of
\alpha_{GF}
. Using (
4
), we can express the gluon condensate entirely in terms of the running coupling,

G=\frac{3}{t^{2}}\,\left(\frac{\alpha_{GF}(\mu)}{\pi}\right)^{2}=\frac{192}{\>\pi^{2}}\,\alpha_{GF}^{2}(\mu)\,\mu^{4}\,.

(10)

Thus

\mu\,\frac{\partial\,G}{\partial\,\mu}=\frac{384}{\>\pi^{2}}\,\alpha_{GF}(\mu)\,\Big(2\alpha_{GF}(\mu)+\beta(\alpha_{GF})\Big)\;\mu^{4}=0\,.

(11)

Altogether this leads to

\beta(\alpha_{GF})=-2\,\alpha_{GF}\,,\quad\alpha_{GF}(\mu)=\frac{\Lambda_{GF}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}

(12)

with

\Lambda_{GF}^{2}=\frac{\pi}{8}\sqrt{\frac{G}{3}}\,,

(13)

where
\Lambda_{GF}
is the lambda parameter in the gradient flow scheme. In
[
14
]
we have shown how to connect (
12
) analytically to the perturbative result at high energies,
\alpha_{GF}(\mu)\simeq 1/4\pi b_{0}\log\,(\mu^{2}/\Lambda_{GF}^{2})
.

The infrared behavior of the running coupling is universal. Under a change of renormalization scheme,
GF\rightarrow S
, we have

\log{\frac{\Lambda_{S}}{\Lambda_{GF}}}=-\int_{\alpha_{GF}(\mu)}^{\alpha_{S}(\mu)}\,\frac{d\alpha}{\beta(\alpha)}\,,

(14)

where the measure
d\alpha/\beta
is scheme invariant. Knowing
\alpha_{GF}(\mu)
and the
\beta
function for small values of
\mu
, we can solve (
14
) for
\alpha_{S}(\mu)
. The result is

\alpha_{S}(\mu)=\frac{\Lambda_{S}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\,.

(15)

Of particular phenomenological interest is the
\,\overline{\!\rm MS\!}\;
scheme. In the pure gauge theory
\Lambda_{\,\overline{\!\rm MS\!}\;}=0.534\,\Lambda_{GF}
. The lambda parameter now is not just an integration constant, but has a tangible meaning. This is a significant result, which has been suggested before
[
15
]
, but has been quantified here for the first time.

The result (
15
) that
\alpha_{S}(\mu)
increases linearly with the inverse power of
\mu^{2}
beyond any bound, bypassing the Landau pole, was long awaited. In the literature it is referred to as infrared slavery. This is clear evidence of color confinement. In order to prove confinement beyond doubt, it would be necessary to prove the existence of a nonvanishing gluon condensate
G
. It is known for a long time that the perturbative vacuum is not the true ground state of the theory. The vacuum with zero field strength
\langle F_{\mu\nu}^{a}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}\rangle
was found to be unstable
[
16
]
. This manifests itself in the fact that the Euclidean effective action is lowered by a nontrivial contribution of
G
.

This is a remarkable turn. The gluon condensate measures the zero-frequency modes of the field strength. It has been shown that a homogeneous vacuum field is also unstable
[
17
]
, indicating that the vacuum has granular structure. This has been brought to light in numerous publications, emphasizing the role of instantons, monopoles and vortices. While the authors argue about which of them is ultimately responsible for confinement, it appears that the microscopic properties of the vacuum are largely averaged out when seen from large distances, leaving many possibilities behind. The solution is characterized by a single, scale-invariant limit under the renormalization group flow, the infrared fixed point
1/\alpha_{S}=0
. This is known as universality. Theories in a given universality class could look very different microscopically, but will end up looking the same at long distances.

4
Corollary

Our derivations thus far represent a significant departure from previous approaches to confinement. Yet many of the characteristics of confinement can be traced back to a nonvanishing gluon condensate and its polarizing effect on the vacuum, without detailed understanding of the underlying mechanism. In the following I give a few examples.

As was mentioned already, the homogeneous vacuum is unstable. A quantity that measures the granularity and fluctuations of the vacuum is the topological susceptibility
\chi_{t}
, which plays a role in various aspects of confinement and strong CP violation. Furthermore, it acts as an auxiliary source of mass generation. The two condensates are connected, as can be seen in Fig.
1
. At
t/L^{2}\approx 0.21
, which marks the intersection point between renormalization group decimation and constant behavior, indicated by the dashed lines, the energy density
\langle E(t)\rangle
can be identified with the minimal classical action (
16
). From this follows

L^{2}\,\langle E(t)\rangle\,\frac{t}{L^{2}}\approx 0.21\times 8\pi^{2}\,\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}\,\chi_{t}}\,.

(19)

Substituting
\sqrt{3G/16}
for
\langle E(t)\rangle t
finally gives the result for
G
in the
\,\overline{\!\rm MS\!}\;
scheme

\sqrt{G}\approx 0.21\times 41.5\,\sqrt{\chi_{t}}=8.7\,\sqrt{\chi_{t}}\;\;\therefore\;\;G=75.7\,\chi_{t}\,.

(20)

Given that
\chi_{t}=(180(4)\,\textrm{MeV})^{4}

[
25
]
, and our result (
18
) for
G
, the expected ratio is
G/\chi_{t}=(535/180)^{4}=78
, which makes (
20
) a perfect match. This is to be compared with results from the literature,
G/\chi_{t}=22

[
26
]
and
G/\chi_{t}=49.5

[

. Analytically,

at the intersection point, assuming a horizontal line in Fig.
1
. An interesting question is how much instantons contribute to the gluon condensate. In Fig.

Interestingly, the topologically trivial condensate
G_{Q=0}
is not invariant under renormalization group transformations in the finite volume. The ratio of
G_{Q=0}
to

is well approximated by a Gaussian distribution,

\frac{G_{Q=0}}{G}\approx\exp\{-c\,t^{2}/L^{4}\}\,,

with

(22)

This tells us that if the path integral is restricted to configurations with trivial topology,
Q=0

\mu L
is small. However, if
\mu

[

A nonvanishing string tension
\sigma
is considered a stringent requirement for confinement. The renormalization group provides a framework for calculating the string tension diagrammatically. Infrared singularities that arise in scattering amplitudes can be collected by a reorganization of perturbation theory into insertions of single gluons between external charges, with the respective effective couplings
\alpha_{S}(q)
taken at the gluons’ momentum
q
, in a fashion that resembles the low-order results
[
28
]
. At small virtualities we expect only tree-level diagrams to contribute. In the infrared, the static potential can thus be approximated by

V(q)=-\frac{16\pi}{3}\,\frac{\alpha_{V}(q)}{q^{2}}\,,

(23)

where
\alpha_{V}(q)
is the effective coupling in the
V
scheme,
\alpha_{V}(q)=\Lambda_{V}^{2}/q^{2}
. This can be envisaged as the exchange of a single dressed gluon, forming a string.
In the infinite volume the gluon momentum
q^{2}
is not bounded from below, and thus can be integrated to zero. The Fourier transformation of
V(q)
to configuration space then gives
[
29
]

V(r)=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int d^{3}\mathbf{q}\;e^{i\,\mathbf{qr}}\;\frac{16\pi}{3}\,\frac{\alpha_{V}(q)}{\mathbf{q}^{2}}\;\underset{r\,\gg\,1/\Lambda_{V}}{=}\,\frac{2}{3}\,\Lambda_{V}^{2}\,r\;\;\therefore\;\;\sigma=\frac{2}{3}\,\Lambda_{V}^{2}\,.

(24)

The charmonium and bottomonium spectrum is very well described by
\Lambda_{V}\approx 0.47\,\textrm{GeV}

[
30
]
, which is to be compared with
[

\Lambda_{V}=1.61\,\Lambda_{\,\overline{\!\rm MS\!}\;}=0.41(1)\,\textrm{GeV}
(assuming that
\alpha_{V}(q)
is nonperturbatively defined
[

).

Confinement is often associated with the existence of a mass gap. But confinement and the existence of a mass gap is not the same. Having a gap in the spectrum does, for example, not exclude massive free gluons and quarks. Furthermore, the existence of a massless Goldstone boson would contradict the mass gap criterion. Leaving this possibility aside, a mass gap appears to be a necessary condition for confinement, but it is not sufficient on its own. It has been suggested
[
33
]
that the gluon condensate gives rise to a set of Higgs scalars, which in turn give mass to the gauge bosons, the mass gap. However, the Higgs scalars cannot screen the color charge of quarks. That was the missing point in
[
33
]
, and the reason why the model was ultimately discarded. This shortcoming has now been overcome, so that all fields in the fundamental representation – quarks, tetraquarks and the like – are subject to the strong force
\alpha_{S}(\mu)\underset{\mu\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}\infty
.

The essence of the Higgs mechanism is to derive a local Lagrangian containing only color singlet gauge fields. Higgs scalars that offer themselves are
[
33
]

\begin{split}\Phi_{1}(x)&=F_{\mu\nu}(x)\,F_{\mu\nu}(x)-\textrm{Tr}\,,\\
\Phi_{2}(x)&=F_{\mu\nu}(x)\,F_{\nu\rho}(x)\,F_{\rho\mu}(x)-\textrm{Tr}\,,\end{split}

\begin{split}\Phi_{1}^{a}(x)&=\mathrm{Tr}\,\lambda_{a}\,F_{\mu\nu}(x)\,F_{\mu\nu}(x)\,,\\
\Phi_{2}^{a}(x)&=\mathrm{Tr}\,\lambda_{a}\,F_{\mu\nu}(x)\,F_{\nu\rho}(x)\,F_{\rho\mu}(x)\,.\end{split}

(25)

The transformation to gauge invariant field variables requires to define a unique gauge transformation
g(x)\in\textrm{SU(3)}/\textrm{Z}_{3}
. This is a two step process. We first diagonalize
\Phi_{1}(x)
,

g(x)\,\Phi_{1}(x)\,g(x)^{-1}=\hat{\!\Phi}_{1}(x)=\textrm{diag}\left(\phi_{1}^{1},\phi_{1}^{2},\phi_{1}^{3}\right)\,,

(26)

which determines
g(x)
up to left multiplication by

l(x)=\textrm{diag}\left(\exp{(i\alpha_{1})},\exp{(i\alpha_{2})},\exp{(i\alpha_{3})}\right)\,,\quad\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}=0

(27)

with
l(x)\in\textrm{U(1)}\times\textrm{U(1)}
, the Cartan or largest abelian subgroup of SU(3). The transformation
g(x)
is uniquely fixed by considering

g(x)\,\Phi_{2}(x)\,g(x)^{-1}=\hat{\!\Phi}_{2}(x)

(28)

and demanding that the elements of
\,\hat{\!\Phi}_{2}
on the second diagonal,
(\,\hat{\!\Phi}_{2})_{12}(x)
and
(\,\hat{\!\Phi}_{2})_{23}(x)
, are real and positive. See also
[
34
,
35
]
.
We need to have
\Phi_{1}(x),\,\Phi_{2}(x)\neq 0
only where
F_{\mu\nu}(x)\neq 0
. It is therefore possible that gluons can screen their color and acquire mass through a local Higgs mechanism. We then may define the gauge invariant quantities

\begin{split}\hat{A}_{\mu}(x)&=g(x)\,A_{\mu}(x)\,g^{-1}(x)+g(x)\,\partial_{\mu}\,g^{-1}(x)\,,\\
\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}(x)&=\partial_{\mu}\hat{A}_{\nu}(x)-\partial_{\nu}\hat{A}_{\mu}(x)+\,\left[\hat{A}_{\mu}(x),\hat{A}_{\nu}(x)\right]\end{split}

(29)

with the gauge field action

S_{G}=\frac{1}{2g_{0}^{2}}\int d^{4}x\,\mathrm{Tr}\,\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}\,.

(30)

The final result is that the gauge fields
A_{\mu}
combine with the Higgs scalars
\Phi_{1},\,\Phi_{2}
to form colorless massive vector particles. Furthermore, the Higgs scalars (
25
) shield the color electric field that binds quark and antiquark together, so as to form a tube-like structure, the string.

The transformation (
26
) fixes the nonabelian part of the gauge completely, which leaves us with abelian gauge fields, gluons, monopoles and quarks, forming the dual superconductor model of confinement
[
36
,
37
]
. From a physical point of view, given the key role of the gluon condensate, I would suggest using the gauge (
26
) fixed by diagonalizing the Higgs scalar
\Phi_{1}
rather than the maximally abelian gauge suggested in
[
38

6
Conclusions

In this work we have identified the condition for confinement, and presented a first principles approach to determining the long-distance properties of QCD, thereby establishing robust measures for verification. A sufficient condition for confinement appears to be the existence of a nonvanishing gluon condensate
(\alpha_{S}/\pi)\,\langle G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}\rangle
. It is associated with an ‘outer’ mass scale that is essential for the existence of QCD. While the existence of a gluon condensate is not directly proven, its presence is strongly suggested by observations that cannot be explained without it, such as the anomalous breaking of scale invariance. It is known though that the perturbative vacuum is unstable against the formation of a constant chromomagnetic field
[
16
]
, which suggests that
\langle G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}\rangle>0
. Although the gluon condensate cannot be visualized directly, it is known to have short-distance structure consisting of monopoles
[
43
]
and center vortices
[
44
]
.

The way we approach confinement is by systematically changing the scale at which the physical system is viewed. This is made possible by the gradient flow, which is a particular, infinitesimal realization of the coarse-graining step of momentum space renormalization group transformations. For a review and references see
[
45
]
. The energy density of the flowed field,
\langle G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}\rangle
, is used to determine the running coupling
\alpha_{GF}(\mu)
at flow times
t=1/8\mu^{2}
, ranging from
t=0
to
t\approx 0.2\,L^{2}
, thereby bridging the ultraviolet and infrared regimes. The result is that at long distances the coupling becomes infinitely strong,
\alpha_{S}\simeq\Lambda_{S}^{2}/\mu^{2}
, in accord with infrared slavery, the flip side of asymptotic freedom. This confines quarks and gluons to color-neutral composite states known as hadrons. It has been shown to give rise to a linearly rising static potential that satisfies Wilson’s confinement criterion.

Our work represents a significant change in approach to confinement. The new development is that the running coupling in the infrared has become tangible analytically. On this basis a rigorous proof of confinement should be feasible.

Appendix

We will show now that the gluon condensate
G
is independent of
\mu
. We demonstrate this for finite flow times
t=1/8\mu^{2}
. The operator
G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}
is multiplicatively renormalized. The flow-time derivative of the vacuum expectation value is given by
[
2
]

a^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\langle G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}\rangle=-\frac{4}{3}\int d^{4}x\,\langle G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}(0)\;G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}(x)\rangle_{c}\,,

(32)

where the subscript
c
denotes the connected correlation function. From this we deduce the functional behavior

\langle G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}\rangle\propto 1/t\;\;\therefore\;\;G\propto t^{2}\langle G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}\rangle^{2}=\textrm{constant}\,,

(33)

aside from the trivial solution
G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}=0
.

The original proof
[
4
,
11
]
by sum rules considers the time ordered product of two conserved electromagnetic currents,

\Pi_{\mu\nu}(Q^{2})=i\int d^{4}x\,e^{i\,qx}\,\langle 0|\textrm{T}[J_{\mu}(x)\;J_{\nu}(0)]|0\rangle=(q_{\mu}q_{\nu}-q^{2})\,\Pi(Q^{2})\,,\;\;Q^{2}=-q^{2}\,,

(34)

where

J_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bar{u}\gamma_{\mu}u-\bar{d}\gamma_{\mu}d\right).

(35)

The operator product expansion of
\Pi(Q^{2})
can be used to define gluon and quark condensates
[
4
,
46
]
. For mass degenerate
u
and
d
quarks we have

\Pi(Q^{2})=c_{0}(Q^{2})\,\langle\mathbbm{1}\rangle+c_{4}^{G}(Q^{2})\,\frac{(\alpha_{S}/\pi)\,\langle G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}\rangle}{24Q^{4}}+c_{4}^{\Sigma}(Q^{2})\,\frac{m\,\langle\bar{u}u+\bar{d}d\rangle}{2Q^{4}}+O(Q^{-6})\,,

(36)

where
c_{0}
,
c_{4}^{G}
and
c_{4}^{\Sigma}
are the Wilson coefficients. We adopt a suitable scheme
S
to define renormalized Wilson coefficients and condensates. The Wilson coefficients are a function of
Q^{2}
and
\mu^{2}
(through
\alpha_{S}
), where
c_{4}^{G}
and
c_{4}^{\Sigma}
read
c_{4}^{G,\Sigma}=1+O(\alpha_{S})
. Apart from possible mixtures with the quark condensate, the gluon condensate is uniquely determined, provided the Wilson coefficient
c_{0}(Q^{2})
is free of power corrections of
O(1/Q^{4})
. If this is the case,
(\alpha_{S}/\pi)\,\langle G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}\rangle
will be independent of the renormalization scale
\mu
. Alternatively, we could have considered purely gluonic currents
[
47
]
, which are less familiar though. Power corrections can arise from renormalon contributions, which originate from the divergent behavior of the perturbative series, specifically in calculations involving loop momenta. We are interested in the gluon condensate at finite flow time. The gradient flow provides a way to regularize the theory by introducing a physical momentum scale that alters the behavior of the perturbative series. The flow time acts as an ultraviolet cut-off, thus eliminating the renormalon ambiguity. This eliminates infrared renormalon contributions as well
[
48
]
.

The connected correlation function on the right-hand side of (
32
) measures local excitations above the vacuum, which require finite energy. An important point to notice is that the derivative on the left-hand side is nonzero and finite, which suggests that the theory has a mass gap. By mass gap we understand the minimum positive energy of the lightest particle. The emphasis is on particle, as opposed to an effective scale possibly provided by the gluon mass in Landau gauge. The correlation function can be re-expressed using the spectral representation by

-\frac{4}{3}\sum_{n}\int dt\;\big|\langle 0|G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}|n\rangle\big|^{2}e^{-m_{n}t}\,,

(37)

where
t
is the Euclidian time, which should not be confused with the flow time. The operator
G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}
projects onto
J^{PC}=0^{++}
glueballs. We call the lightest glueball state
\sigma
, which is normalized to
|\sigma\rangle=\sqrt{2m_{\sigma}}|1\rangle
. Evaluating the time integral in (
37
) then gives

-\frac{4}{3}\,\frac{\big|\langle 0|G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}|\sigma\rangle\big|^{2}}{2m_{\sigma}^{2}}\,.

(38)

The derivative on the left-hand side of (
32
) can be worked out by expressing the flow time in terms of the running coupling. The final result is

\frac{\big|\langle 0|\left(\alpha_{GF}/\pi\right)\,G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}|\sigma\rangle\big|^{2}}{2m_{\sigma}^{2}}\,=\,\frac{288}{\pi^{3}}\,\Lambda_{GF}^{6}\,,

(39)

where the operator
G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}
is replaced by the scale invariant operator
(\alpha_{GF}/\pi)\,G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}
in the process. Zero glueball mass would mean that the right-hand side of (
32
) tends to infinity at infinite times, which is excluded by the left-hand side being finite. No fluctuations above the vacuum at all would render the right-hand side zero, which is equally excluded.

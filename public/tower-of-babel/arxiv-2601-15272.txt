Lucas-Pantograph Type Exponential, Trigonometric, and Hyperbolic Functions
Ronald Orozco López

Abstract
In this paper, we include some new results for the Lucas calculus. A Lucas-Pantograph type exponential function is introduced. Additionally, we define Lucas-Pantograph type trigonometric functions, and some of their most notable identities are given: parity, sum and difference formulas, Pythagorean identities, double-angle identities, and some special values. Lucas-Pantograph type hyperbolic functions are also introduced.

2.2
Lucas-derivative

Definition 1
.

Set

,

. We define the Lucas-derivative
\mathbf{D}_{s,t}
of the function

as

\mathbf{D}_{s,t}f(x)=\frac{f(\varphi x)-f(\phi x)}{(\varphi-\phi)x}

for all

and
(\mathbf{D}_{s,t}f)(0)=f^{\prime}(0)
, provided
f^{\prime}(0)
exists.

Example 1
.

For all

,

•

Linearity. For all functions
f(x)
and

and for

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{s,t}(f(x)+g(x))

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{s,t}(f(x)g(x))=f(\varphi x)(\mathbf{D}_{s,t}g)(x)+g(\phi x)(\mathbf{D}_{s,t}f)(x),

f(x)

2.3
Lucas-Integral

Let
f
be an arbitrary function and
a
be a real number. We define the following Lucas-integrals of
f
as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}f(x)d_{s,t}x

\displaystyle=(\varphi-\phi)a\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\phi^{k}}{\varphi^{k+1}}f\left(\frac{\phi^{k}}{\varphi^{k+1}}a\right)\text{ if }\ \Big|\frac{\phi}{\varphi}\Big|>1,

(19)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}f(x)d_{s,t}x

\displaystyle=(\phi-\varphi)a\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{k}}{\phi^{k+1}}f\left(\frac{\varphi^{k}}{\phi^{k+1}}a\right)\text{ if }\ \Big|\frac{\varphi}{\phi}\Big|<1.

(20)

If
a
and
b
are two nonnegative numbers such that
a<b
, then

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(x)d_{p,q}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{b}f(x)d_{p,q}x-\int_{0}^{a}f(x)d_{p,q}x.

Following Eq. (
19
), the Lucas-integral
f
is

\int_{a}^{b}f(x)d_{s,t}x=(\varphi-\phi)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigg[bf\left(b\frac{\phi^{n}}{\varphi^{n+1}}\right)-af\left(a\frac{\phi^{n}}{\varphi^{n+1}}\right)\Bigg]\frac{\phi^{n}}{\varphi^{n+1}}.

(21)

We will call
f
a function Lucas-integrable if the series in Eq.(

) is convergent.

The fundamental theorem of Lucas-calculus
.
If
F(x)
is an antiderivative of
f(x)
and
F(x)
is continuous at
x=0
, then

\int_{a}^{b}f(x)d_{s,t}x=F(b)-F(a),

(22)

where

.
Suppose

and

\int_{a}^{b}(\mathbf{D}_{s,t}f)(x)g(\phi x)d_{s,t}x=\big[f\cdot g\big]_{a}^{b}-\int_{a}^{b}f(\varphi x)(\mathbf{D}_{s,t}g)(x)d_{s,t}x.

5.2

Next, we obtain the deformed Lucas-Pantograph-analog of Euler’s formula

Theorem 8
.

For all
u\in\mathbb{C}

\exp_{s,t}(ix,u)=\cos_{s,t}(x,u)+i\sin_{s,t}(x,u)

Proof.

When
u=0
, then

\displaystyle\exp_{s,t}(ix,0)

\displaystyle=1+ix=\cos_{s,t}(x,0)+i\sin_{s,t}(x,0),

\displaystyle\exp_{s,t}(z,0)

\displaystyle=1+z=\cos_{s,t}(z,0)+\sin_{s,t}(z,0).

u\neq 0
, we use the power series expansion of
\mathrm{exp}_{s,t}(ix,u)
and
\exp_{s,t}(z,-u)
, respectively. Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\exp_{s,t}(ix,u)

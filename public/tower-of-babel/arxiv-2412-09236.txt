modulo
\pi i\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w-1,r}^{N}
,

,

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By Lemma
4.4
it suffices to show
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w,r}^{N}/(\pi i\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w-1,r}^{N}+\mathcal{PD}_{w,r}^{N})
is linearly spanned by the image of
\shuffle
-CSMZVs.

Let
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{r}
and
(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r})\in\Gamma_{N}^{r}
.

Our proof is splitted into the following two steps:

(1)

If
\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{r}\neq 1
, then
\displaystyle\zeta^{\shuffle}\left(\begin{matrix}{\xi_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\xi_{r}}\\
{k_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{r}}\end{matrix}\right)\mod\pi i\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w-1,r}^{N}+\mathcal{PD}_{w,r}^{N}
is a
K_{N}
-linear combination of the image of
\shuffle
-CSMZVs.

(2)

The general cases are reduced to the first step and Theorem
1.1
.

First, we prove that
\displaystyle\zeta^{\shuffle}\left(\begin{matrix}{\xi_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\xi_{r}}\\
{k_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{r}}\end{matrix}\right)\mod\pi i\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w-1}^{N}+\mathcal{PD}_{w,r}^{N}
with
\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{r}\neq 1
is a
K_{N}
-linear combination of the image of
\shuffle
-CSMZVs.
We have

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathcal{S}(N,\alpha)}\left(\begin{matrix}{\xi_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\xi_{r}}\\
{k_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{r}}\end{matrix}\right)\equiv

\displaystyle\zeta^{\shuffle}\left(\begin{matrix}{\xi_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\xi_{r}}\\
{k_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{r}}\end{matrix}\right)+(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}}(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{r})^{\alpha}\zeta^{\shuffle}\left(\begin{matrix}{\overline{\xi_{r}}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\overline{\xi_{1}}}\\
{k_{r}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{1}}\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\zeta^{\shuffle}\left(\begin{matrix}{\xi_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\xi_{r}}\\
{k_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{r}}\end{matrix}\right)+(-1)^{r}(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{r})^{\alpha}\zeta^{\shuffle}\left(\begin{matrix}{\xi_{r}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\xi_{1}}\\
{k_{r}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{1}}\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\zeta^{\shuffle}\left(\begin{matrix}{\xi_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\xi_{r}}\\
{k_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{r}}\end{matrix}\right)-(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{r})^{\alpha}\zeta^{\shuffle}\left(\begin{matrix}{\xi_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\xi_{r}}\\
{k_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{r}}\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(1-(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{r})^{\alpha})\zeta^{\shuffle}\left(\begin{matrix}{\xi_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\xi_{r}}\\
{k_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{r}}\end{matrix}\right)\mod\pi i\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w-1}^{N}+\mathcal{PD}_{w,r}^{N}.

Now we can use Lemma
4.2
in the second equality and Lemma
4.3
in the third equality.
Since
\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{r}\neq 1
,
1-(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{r})^{\alpha}\neq 0
by
\alpha\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
and our 1-st step is completed.

Second, we consider the general case.
It suffices to exclude the case
\xi_{1}=\cdots=\xi_{r}=1
, as this case follows directly from Theorem
1.1
.
Since

(4.2)

\displaystyle\zeta^{\shuffle}\left(\begin{matrix}{\xi_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\xi_{r}}\\
{k_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{r}}\end{matrix}\right)=Z_{N}^{\shuffle}(x_{\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{r}}x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots x_{\xi_{r}}x_{0}^{k_{r}-1})

holds,
\displaystyle\zeta^{\shuffle}\left(\begin{matrix}{\xi_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\xi_{r}}\\
{k_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{r}}\end{matrix}\right)
with
\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{r}=1
is reduced to
Z_{N}^{\shuffle}(x_{\eta_{1}}x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots x_{\eta_{r}}x_{0}^{k_{r}-1})
for
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{r}\in\Gamma_{N}
with
\eta_{1}=1
.

We prove, by induction on
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},
that
Z^{\shuffle}_{N}(x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{l}}x_{\xi_{1}}x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots x_{\xi_{r}}x_{0}^{k_{r}-1})\mod\pi i\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w-1}^{N}+\mathcal{PD}_{w,r}^{N}
(

,
x_{i_{1}}=x_{1}
, and
\xi_{1}\neq 1
) can be expressed as a
K_{N}
-linear combination of

Z_{N}^{\shuffle}(x_{\xi_{1}^{\prime}}x_{0}^{k_{1}^{\prime}-1}\cdots x_{\xi_{r}^{\prime}}x_{0}^{k_{r^{\prime}}^{\prime}-1})\mod\pi i\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w-1}^{N}+\mathcal{PD}_{w,r}^{N},

where
k_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,k_{r^{\prime}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\xi_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\xi{r^{\prime}}^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{N}
with
\xi_{1}^{\prime}\neq 1
.
Let us assume that the claim holds for all
l^{\prime}<l

Since there exists
w_{j}\in K_{N}\langle X\rangle
(
j=0,\ldots,l-1
) and
w\in K_{N}\langle X\rangle
such that

\displaystyle x_{1}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{l}}x_{\xi_{1}}x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots x_{\xi_{r}}x_{0}^{k_{r}-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{l}}\shuffle x_{\xi_{1}}x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots x_{\xi_{r}}x_{0}^{k_{r}-1}-\sum_{0\leq j<l}x_{1}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{j}}x_{\xi_{1}}w_{j}-x_{\xi_{1}}w

\displaystyle Z^{\shuffle}_{N}(x_{1}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{l}}x_{\xi_{1}}x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots x_{\xi_{r}}x_{0}^{k_{r}-1})

.

.

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

We have

\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{N}=\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{S}(N,\alpha),\shuffle}[\pi i].

More precisely, we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{N}_{w}=\sum_{j=0}^{w}(\pi i)^{j}\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{S}(N,\alpha),\shuffle}_{w-j}.

for
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

We prove this claim by induction on
w
. Assume our induction hypothesis holds less than
w^{\prime}<w
for fixed
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Let
z\in\widetilde{Z}_{w}^{N}
. By Theorem
4.1
and the definition of CSMZV, there exists
s_{w}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{S}(N,\alpha),\shuffle}_{w}
and
z_{w-1}\in\widetilde{Z}_{w-1}^{N}
such that

z=s_{w}+\pi i\cdot z_{w-1}

By the assumption of the induction, there exists
s_{j}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{S}(N,\alpha),\shuffle}_{w-j}

(0\leq j\leq w-1)
such that

z_{w-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{w-1}(\pi i)^{w-j-1}s_{j}
. Therefore, our claim is completed.
∎

5.
Refined symmetric multiple zeta values and generalization of Theorem

In this section, we describe a refinement of Theorem

Tasaka introduced refined cyclotomic symmetric multiple zeta values (RCSMZVs) in
[
12
]
.
The original case was studied in
[
7
]
, while independently, the case
N=1

[
5
]
, and Tasaka extended it to general

For
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{r}
and
(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r})\in\Gamma_{N}^{r}
,
Tasaka (
[
12
]

\zeta_{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}\left(\begin{matrix}{\xi_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{\xi_{r}}\\
{k_{1}}\hskip-8.5359pt&,\ldots,&\hskip-8.5359pt{k_{r}}\end{matrix}\right)
, which is the lift of our CSMZVs, as the element of
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{N}
.
Then we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}\left(\begin{matrix}\bm{\xi}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{matrix}\right)\equiv

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathcal{S}(N,\alpha)}^{\shuffle}\left(\begin{matrix}\bm{\xi}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{matrix}\right)\mod\pi i\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{N}.

for each
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{r}
and
\bm{\xi}\in\Gamma_{N}^{r}
(see
[
12
, Definition 3.1]
and Remark
3.2
).

Let
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}
be a
K_{N}
-linear subspace of
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{N}
generated by RCSMZVs.
We define
K_{N}
-linear subspace
\mathcal{Z}_{w}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{N}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{w}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}=

\displaystyle\Span_{K_{N}}\left\{\zeta_{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}\left(\begin{matrix}\bm{\xi}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{matrix}\right)\middle|\wt\mathbf{k}=w\right\}.

The “harmonic product relations” of RCSMZV show that
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}
is a
K_{N}
-algebra (
[
12
, Proposition 5.2]
), i.e.

(5.2)

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}\left(\begin{matrix}\bm{\xi}_{1}\\
\mathbf{k}_{1}\end{matrix}\right)\zeta_{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}\left(\begin{matrix}\bm{\xi}_{2}\\
\mathbf{k}_{2}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}

for
\mathbf{k}_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{d_{1}}
,
\bm{\xi}_{1}\in\Gamma_{N}^{d_{1}}
,

and
\bm{\xi}_{2}\in\Gamma_{N}^{d_{2}}
(

,

).

We can generalize Theorem
4.1
in the following theorem, which settles a special case of a problem proposed for arbitrary
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
in
[
12
, (6.1)]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\alpha\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.
For
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{w}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}=\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w}^{N}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}_{w}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w}^{N}
holds by the definition of CRSMZVs, we prove the opposite inclusion.

We prove this by induction on
w
. The case of
w=0
is trivial. Assume
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}_{w-1}\supset\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w-1}^{N}
.
Let
c\in\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w}^{N}
. By Theorem
4.1
and (
5.1
), there exists
y\in\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}_{w}
such that
c-y\in\pi i\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{w-1}^{N}
. Therefore,

\frac{c-y}{\pi i}\in\mathcal{Z}_{w-1}^{N}=\mathcal{Z}_{w-1}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}.

By the special case of (
5.2
) and
\zeta^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
1\end{matrix}\right)=-\pi i
(
[
12
, Below Definition 3.1]
), we have

-\pi i\zeta^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}\left(\begin{matrix}\bm{\xi}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{matrix}\right)=\zeta^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
1\end{matrix}\right)\zeta^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}\left(\begin{matrix}\bm{\xi}\\
\mathbf{k}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathcal{Z}_{w}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)},

for
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{r}
with
\wt\mathbf{k}=w-1
and
\bm{\xi}\in\Gamma_{N}^{r}
. Therefore we have
\pi i\mathcal{Z}_{w-1}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{w}^{\mathcal{RS}(N,\alpha)}
. Thus

which complete the proof.
∎

Theorem
4.1
recovers by taking the quatient
\pi i\widetilde{Z}_{w-1}^{N}
to (
5.3
).

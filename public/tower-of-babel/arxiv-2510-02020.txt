The dimension and Bose distance of some BCH codes of length q m − 1 λ \frac{q^{m}-1}{\lambda}
Run Zheng, Nung-Sing Sze, Zejun Huang

Abstract
BCH codes are important error correction codes, widely utilized due to their robust algebraic structure, multi-error correcting capability, and efficient decoding algorithms. Despite their practical importance and extensive study, their parameters, including dimension, minimum distance and Bose distance, remain largely unknown in general. This paper addresses this challenge by investigating the dimension and Bose distance of BCH codes of length ( q m − 1 ) / λ (q^{m}-1)/\lambda over the finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} , where λ \lambda is a positive divisor of q − 1 q-1 . Specifically, for narrow-sense BCH codes of this length with m ≥ 4 m\geq 4 , we derive explicit formulas for their dimension for designed distance 2 ≤ δ ≤ ( q ⌊ ( 2 ​ m − 1 ) / 3 ⌋ + 1 − 1 ) / λ + 1 2\leq\delta\leq(q^{\lfloor(2m-1)/3\rfloor+1}-1)/{\lambda}+1 . We also provide explicit formulas for their Bose distance in the range 2 ≤ δ ≤ ( q ⌊ ( 2 ​ m − 1 ) / 3 ⌋ + 1 − 1 ) / λ 2\leq\delta\leq(q^{\lfloor(2m-1)/3\rfloor+1}-1)/{\lambda} . These ranges for δ \delta are notably larger than the previously known results for this class of BCH codes. Furthermore, we extend these findings to determine the dimension and Bose distance for certain non-narrow-sense BCH codes of the same length. Applying our results, we identify several BCH codes with good parameters.

Let

The smallest integer in the coset

of

. For convenience, we occasionally refer to it simply as a
coset leader modulo
n
.
Let

be a positive integer,
and let

be two integers such that
c\leq d
.
We define

\mathcal{L}^{n}(c,d)=\{a\in[c,d]:a\text{ is the coset leader of }C_{n}(a)\}

In particular, when

.

By

of
\beta^{a}

over

can be given by

m_{a}(x)=\prod\limits_{i\in C_{n}(a)}(x-\beta^{i}).

Hence, the generator polynomial

of the narrow-sense BCH code
\mathcal{C}_{(q,n,\delta)}
can be expressed as

g(x)=\prod\limits_{i\in\mathcal{G}}(x-\beta^{i}),\quad\mathcal{G}=\bigcup\limits_{a=1}^{\delta-1}C_{n}(a).

Consequently,
the dimension of
\mathcal{C}_{(q,n,\delta)}
can be given by

\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{C}_{(q,n,\delta)})=n-\left|\bigcup\limits_{a=1}^{\delta-1}C_{n}(a)\right|=n-\sum\limits_{a\in\mathcal{L}^{n}(1,\delta-1)}|C_{n}(a)|.

(2)

Thus, determining the coset leaders modulo

n
within

[1,\delta-1]

and the sizes of corresponding cyclotomic cosets allows us to compute the dimension of
\mathcal{C}_{(q,n,\delta)}
.
Additionally, for
\delta^{{}^{\prime}}>\delta
, one can easily verify that

\bigcup\limits_{a=1}^{\delta-1}C_{n}(a)=\bigcup\limits_{a=1}^{\delta^{\prime}-1}C_{n}(a)

if and only if all the integers in

are not coset leaders. Therefore,
the Bose distance of
\mathcal{C}_{(q,n,\delta)}
is equal to the smallest coset leader within the range
[\delta,n-1]
.

We have a useful observation regarding

q
-cyclotomic cosets, as presented in the following lemma. This result originated from the proof of
[
40
, Lemma 6]
. For completeness, we include a proof below.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
n
and
\lambda
are two integers such that
\mathrm{gcd}(n,q)=1
and
\mathrm{gcd}(\lambda,q)=1
. Let
a\in[0,n-1]
be an integer. Then

•

a
is the coset leader of
C_{n}(a)
if and only if
\lambda a
is the coset leader of
C_{\lambda n}(\lambda a)
;

•

|C_{n}(a)|=|C_{\lambda n}(\lambda a)|.

Proof.

By definition, the integer
a
is the coset leader of
C_{n}(a)
if and only if

a\bmod n\leq aq^{i}\bmod n\quad\hbox{for any integer }i\geq 0.

(3)

Noticing that

a\bmod n=a-n\cdot\lfloor\frac{a}{n}\rfloor
and
\lambda a\bmod\lambda n=\lambda a-\lambda n\cdot\lfloor\frac{\lambda a}{\lambda n}\rfloor
,
we can assert that

\lambda\cdot(a\bmod n)=\lambda a-\lambda n\cdot\lfloor\frac{a}{n}\rfloor=\lambda a-\lambda n\cdot\lfloor\frac{\lambda a}{\lambda n}\rfloor=\lambda a\bmod\lambda n.

Similarly, we also have

\lambda\cdot(aq^{i}\bmod n)=\lambda aq^{i}\bmod\lambda n.

(4)

Therefore, the inequality in (
3
) is equivalent to

\lambda a\bmod\lambda n\leq\lambda aq^{i}\bmod\lambda n\quad\hbox{for any integer }i\geq 0.

By definition, this holds if and only if
\lambda a
is the coset leader of
C_{\lambda n}(\lambda a)
. Therefore, the first statment follows.

In addition, the equality in (
4
) also implies that

aq^{i}\bmod n=a\quad\hbox{if and only if}\quad\lambda aq^{i}\bmod\lambda n=\lambda a.

It follows that the smallest integer
\ell_{a}
such that
aq^{\ell_{a}}\bmod n=a
is equal to the smallest integer
\ell_{\lambda a}
such that
\lambda aq^{\ell_{\lambda a}}\bmod\lambda n=\lambda a
. Therefore, we have
|C_{n}(a)|=|C_{\lambda n}(\lambda a)|.
This completes the proof.
∎

Since
\lambda\mid q-1
, it follows that
for any integer

\lambda\mid a\quad\hbox{ if and only if }\quad\lambda\mid t(a)+\alpha(a).

Similarly, each integer
a\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(i)
can be uniquely decomposed as

a=t(a)q^{h+i}+\alpha(a),

and

\lambda\mid a\quad\hbox{ if and only if }\quad\lambda\mid t(a)+\alpha(a).

Remark 3
.

The condition in (
6
) can be equivalently expressed as

1\leq\alpha(a)\leq\sum\limits_{\ell=h-i+1}^{h+k}a_{\ell}q^{\ell-(h-i+1)}\quad\hbox{and}\quad q\nmid\alpha(a).

(10)

Moreover, the form of

in (

) implies that

\sum\limits_{\ell=h-i+1}^{h+k}a_{\ell}q^{\ell-(h-i+1)}=t(a)\cdot q^{2i-1}\quad\hbox{for }i\in[1,k],

and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell=h-i+1}^{h+k}a_{\ell}q^{\ell-(h-i+1)}+\sum\limits_{\ell=h+i}^{h-i}a_{\ell}q^{\ell-(h-i+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t(a)\cdot q^{2i-1}\quad\hbox{for }i\in[-k+1,0].

Noticing that
0\leq\sum\limits_{\ell=h+i}^{h-i}a_{\ell}q^{\ell-(h-i+1)}<1
for

, we can conclude that (
10
) is equivalent to

1\leq\alpha(a)\leq\lfloor t(a)\cdot q^{2i-1}\rfloor\quad\hbox{and}\quad q\nmid\alpha(a).

Similarly,
the condition in (
8
) can also be written as

1\leq\alpha(a)\leq\sum\limits_{\ell=h-i}^{h+k}a_{\ell}q^{\ell-(h-i)}\quad\hbox{and}\quad q\nmid\alpha(a),

which is further equivalent to

1\leq\alpha(a)\leq\lfloor t(a)\cdot q^{2i}\rfloor\quad\hbox{and}\quad q\nmid\alpha(a).

Theorem 7
.

Let

and

be two integers with

We demonstrate the proof only for the case where

is odd follows similarly. First,

by considering the following cases.

IX
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigate the dimension and Bose distance of BCH codes of length
\frac{q^{m}-1}{\lambda}
, where
\lambda
is a positive divisor of
q-1
. Our main contribution is to provide explicit formulas for the dimension and the narrow-sense Bose distance of BCH codes of length
\frac{q^{m}-1}{\lambda}

for a much larger range of designed distances than previously known. In addition, we
extend these results to some non-narrow-sense BCH codes of length
\frac{q^{m}-1}{\lambda}
. Applying our results, we find some BCH codes with good parameters.

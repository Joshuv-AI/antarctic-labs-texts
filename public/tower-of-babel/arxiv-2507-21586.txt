Grassmann cactus variety and socle dimension
Weronika Buczyńska, Jarosław Buczyński, Maciej Gałązka

Abstract
Grassmann cactus variety is a common generalisation of Grassmann secant variety and cactus variety. In their definitions one considers the vector spaces of fixed dimension that are contained in the linear span of some finite schemes. We prove that to characterise Grassmann cactus varieties it is enough to use finite schemes that locally have low socle dimension. This motivates the study of parameter spaces of such schemes and simplifies calculations of examples of Grassmann cactus varieties.

Introduction

Throughout this note, we work over an algebraically closed field
{\Bbbk}
of any characteristic.

Suppose
V
is a
{\Bbbk}
-vector space, and let
{\mathbb{P}}V
be the corresponding (naive) projective space
{\mathbb{P}}V=(V\setminus\left\{0\right\})/{\mathbb{G}}_{\mathfrak{m}}
over
{\Bbbk}
.
Let
\Gr(k,V)
be the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional linear subspaces of
V
, so that
\Gr(1,V)={\mathbb{P}}V
, and
\Gr(\dim V-1,V)={\mathbb{P}}V^{*}
.
If
R\subset{\mathbb{P}}V
is any closed subscheme, then by linear span
\left\langle R\right\rangle\subset V
we mean the smallest linear subspace of
V
such that
R\subset{\mathbb{P}}\left\langle R\right\rangle
as a scheme.
Consider a fixed quasiprojective scheme
X
with a fixed locally closed embedding
X\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}V
.

We define the
Grassmann cactus variety
of
X
to be:

\mathfrak{K}_{r,k}\left(X\right):=\overline{\left\{E\in\Gr(k,V)\mid\exists\text{ subscheme }R\subset X\text{ s.t.}\dim R=0,\deg R\leqslant r,E\subset\left\langle R\right\rangle\right\}}.

Note that if
X
has at least
r
distinct points of support (for instance, if
\dim X>0
), then in the definition we can assume
\deg R=r
instead of
\deg R\leqslant r
.
The case of
k=1
is the well established in the literature case of
cactus variety

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)\subset{\mathbb{P}}V
,
see for instance
[
BB14
]
,
[
Gał17
]
,
[
BBF24
]
,

[
BR13
]
,

[
BJMR18
]
.
Also if in the definition we only consider smooth schemes
R
(that is, unions of
r
distinct points of
X
) then we obtain the
Grassmann secant varieties

[
CC01
]
,
[
CC08
]
,
[
BL13
]
.
Thus the definition of
\mathfrak{K}_{r,k}\left(X\right)
is a common generalisation of cactus varieties and Grassmann secant varieties, which also appears in
[
GMR23
]
,

[
BB25
]
.
Furthermore, we expect that appropriately exploited
\mathfrak{K}_{r,k}\left(X\right)
will lead to cactus barriers on determinantal methods for border rank, which are stronger than
[
EGOW18
]
or
[
GMOW19
]
.

Any finite scheme
R
over
{\Bbbk}
can be written as
R\simeq\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\#\Supp R}\Spec A_{i}
with each
A_{i}
a finite local algebra over
{\Bbbk}
.
We say
R
has socle dimension at most
l
(denoted by
\socdim(R)\leqslant l
) if for all
i
the dimension of the socle of
A_{i}
is at most
l
.
In particular,
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
R
has socle dimension at most
1

[
Eis95
, Prop. 21.5(a)&(c)]
.

Our result is the following:

Theorem 1
.

In the definition of
\mathfrak{K}_{r,k}\left(X\right)
it is enough to use schemes of socle dimension at most
k
:

\mathfrak{K}_{r,k}\left(X\right)=\overline{\left\{E\in\Gr(k,V)\mid\exists R\subset X\text{ s.t.}\dim R=0,\deg R\leqslant r,\socdim R\leqslant k,E\subset\left\langle R\right\rangle\right\}}.

Case
k=1
of this theorem is

[
BB14
, Prop. 2.2(ii)]
.
This special case is often used in the context of comparing secant varieties or varieties of sums of powers (VSP) with subsets of Hilbert schemes. The reduction to Gorenstein subschemes may reduce the complexity of the problem, as Gorenstein schemes are slightly better behaved than general schemes, or at least pathologies appear a little later. See for instance
[
RV17
, Rem. 3.3]
,
[
JRS25
, §1.1]
,

[
BR13
, §1]
,

[
BK24
, Thm 1.2]
,

[
CLPS25
, Thm D]
.
We expect similar applications and smooth equivalences for the case of arbitrary
k
.
To some extent this is addressed in work in preparation

[
BB25
]
,
[
Jag25
]
.
Moreover, Theorem
1
motivates
[
GKŠ25
]
which investigates the loci in the Hilbert scheme parameterising schemes with low socle dimension.

2
Subschemes of a finite scheme

We prove Theorem
1
by reducing it to the special case of
X=R
, where
R
is finite of degree
r
.
Before that, in a series of lemmas we treat this special case and some related mathematics. In particular, in this section we investigate finite subchemes of a finite scheme, which might be of interest on its own. The statements here are slightly stronger than we need in the course of the proof of main theorem.

If
R
is a finite scheme, by
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{k}R
we denote the Hilbert scheme of finite subschemes of
R
of degree
k
.
That is, the
{\Bbbk}
-points of
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{k}R
correspond uniquely to subschemes
R^{\prime}\subset R
of degree
k
, and
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{k}R
is universal with respect to flat deformations of such schemes.
In particular,
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{k}R
comes with the universal subscheme
{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}\subset R\times_{{\Bbbk}}{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{k}R
, which is flat over
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{k}R
and any finite flat family of subschemes of
R
of degree
k
is a pullback of
{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}\to{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{k}R
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
R=\Spec A
is a finite local scheme of degree
r
,
with
A
a finite local algebra with socle
\mathfrak{s}\subset A
.
Then
{({{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R)}_{\operatorname{red}}={\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})
,
and thus each degree
r-1
subscheme of
R
is uniquely determined by a
1
-dimensional
{\Bbbk}
-linear subspace of
\mathfrak{s}
.

Proof.
The bijection of
{\Bbbk}
-points of
{({{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R)}_{\operatorname{red}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})
is clear: a subscheme
R^{\prime}\subset R
corresponds uniquely to an ideal
I_{R^{\prime}}\subset A
,
and

\deg R^{\prime}=r-1\iff\dim I_{R^{\prime}}=1\iff\dim I_{R^{\prime}}=1\text{ and }I_{R^{\prime}}\subset\mathfrak{s}\iff I_{R^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s}).

We will now turn this bijection into an isomorphism of reduced schemes by constructing two morphisms each way
that realise this bijection.

Define
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}A
to be the universal ideal sheaf: over a
p\in{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})
the ideal is the one dimensional subspace
p\subset\mathfrak{s}
.
This ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
defines a family
{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})\times R
which is flat, finite and of degree
r-1
over
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})
.
Thus we obtain a morphism
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})\to{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R
,
whose image is contained in
{({{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R)}_{\operatorname{red}}
.
By the above discussion this morphism is bijective
on
{\Bbbk}
-points.

Now we will construct a map
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R\to{\mathbb{P}}(A)
.
Any such map is determined by a line subbundle
L\subset A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}{\mathcal{O}}_{{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R}
,
so that
L
becomes the pull-back of
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(A)}(-1)
under this morphism.
Let
L
be the universal ideal sheaf
over
{\mathcal{O}}_{{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R}
, that is, if
{\mathcal{R}}\subset R\times_{{\Bbbk}}{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R
is the universal family such that the projection
{\mathcal{R}}\to{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R
is flat finite and of degree
r
, then
L\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{R}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}{\mathcal{O}}_{{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R}
is its ideal.
Then
L
is flat and coherent over
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R
,
hence locally free, and moreover its rank is
1
.
Thus
L
is the desired line bundle.
Also this morphism agrees on
{\Bbbk}
points with the bijection described in the beginning of the proof.
Hence the restriction to
{({{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R)}_{\operatorname{red}}
has its image contained in
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})
and the morphism is precisely the inverse of
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})\to{({{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R)}_{\operatorname{red}}
.
Hence these two schemes are isomorphic.
∎

Fun Fact 3
.

Although it might be tempting to conjecture that in the setting of Lemma
2

the Hilbert scheme

{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R

is also a projective space, this fails already on the simplest nonreduced scheme:
let
R=\Spec{\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2})
,
so that
r=2
, and
\mathfrak{s}=(t)
.
Then
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{1}R=R\neq{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})=\Spec{\Bbbk}
.
Thus
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R
is not necessarily reduced.
In this case, the map
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{1}R\to{\mathbb{P}}A\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
from the proof of Lemma
2

is an embedding.
It would be interesting to see if
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R\to{\mathbb{P}}A
is an embedding for any finite local
R
.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose
R
is a finite scheme of degree
r
,
and that
R=\bigsqcup R_{i}
with each
R_{i}
a finite local scheme of degree
r_{i}
.
Then
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r-1}R=\bigsqcup_{i}{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r_{i}-1}R_{i}
.

∎

3
Grassmann cacti

For a convenience of the remaining proofs we denote:

\mathfrak{K}^{\circ}_{r,k}\left(X\right):=\left\{E\in\Gr(k,V)\mid\exists R\subset X\text{ s.t.}\dim R=0,\deg R\leqslant r,E\subset\left\langle R\right\rangle\right\},

so that
\mathfrak{K}_{r,k}\left(X\right)=\overline{\mathfrak{K}^{\circ}_{r,k}\left(X\right)}
.

Lemma 5
.

Suppose
R\subset{\mathbb{P}}(V)
is a finite scheme of degree
r
.
Then the following subsets of
\Gr(k,V)
are equal:

\mathfrak{K}_{r,k}\left(R\right)=\mathfrak{K}^{\circ}_{r,k}\left(R\right)=\Gr(k,\left\langle R\right\rangle).

Proof.
Since
\Gr(k,\left\langle R\right\rangle)
is closed in
\Gr(k,V)
it is enough to prove the second equality, which is also straightforward,
since the unique subscheme of
R
of dimension
0
and degree
r
is
R
itself.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Suppose
R\subset{\mathbb{P}}(V)
is a finite scheme of degree
r

and that it fails to be of socle dimension at most
k
.
Then

\Gr(k,\left\langle R\right\rangle)=\bigcup_{R^{\prime}\subsetneq R}\Gr(k,\left\langle R^{\prime}\right\rangle),

where the union is over all subschemes
R^{\prime}
of
R
of degree
r-1
.

A version of this lemma for
R
local scheme
(which is the main case)
but with different proof is also in
[
JK24
, Prop. 4.2]
.

Proof.
Note that
\supset
part of the claim is automatic.

To prove the
\subset
part,
write
R=\Spec A_{0}\sqcup\bigsqcup\Spec A_{i}
with each
A_{i}
a finite local algebra, and suppose the socle dimension of
A_{0}
is
l\geqslant k+1
.
Let
\mathfrak{s}\subset A_{0}
be the socle of
A_{0}
.
Define
Q\subset R
to be the subscheme of degree
r-l
with

Q=\Spec(A_{0}/\mathfrak{s})\sqcup\bigsqcup\Spec A_{i}
.
We claim that

\Gr(k,\left\langle R\right\rangle)\subset\bigcup_{Q\subset R^{\prime}\subsetneq R}\Gr(k,\left\langle R^{\prime}\right\rangle),

(7)

so in the union it is enough to use only those
R^{\prime}
of degree
r-1
, that contain
Q
.

If there exists

R^{\prime}\subsetneq R
as above such that
\left\langle R^{\prime}\right\rangle=\left\langle R\right\rangle
.
Then the inclusion (
7
) trivially holds.

Suppose there exist two different schemes
R^{\prime}
and
R^{\prime\prime}
both of degree
r-1
and such that

Q\subset R^{\prime}\subsetneq R
, and
Q\subset R^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq R
and such that
\left\langle R^{\prime}\right\rangle=\left\langle R^{\prime\prime}\right\rangle
.
Then
R
is the scheme theoretic union
R^{\prime}\cup R^{\prime\prime}
, and

\left\langle R\right\rangle=\left\langle R^{\prime}\cup R^{\prime\prime}\right\rangle\subset\left\langle R^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle R^{\prime\prime}\right\rangle=\left\langle R^{\prime}\right\rangle.

Thus we are back in the first trivial case.

The final case to consider
is where for any pair
R^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}\subset R
of distinct subschemes, both containing
Q
, they have different linear spans
\left\langle R^{\prime}\right\rangle\neq\left\langle R^{\prime\prime}\right\rangle
, and the are all hyperplanes in
\left\langle R\right\rangle
.
By Lemmas
2
and
4
,
the parameter space of subschemes
R^{\prime}
such that
\deg R^{\prime}=r-1
and
Q\subset R^{\prime}\subsetneq R
is equal to

{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{l-1}
.
Setting
m=\dim\left\langle R\right\rangle
,
we have an injective map

{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})\to\Gr(m-1,\left\langle R\right\rangle)={\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\langle R\right\rangle^{*}\right)

which sends the scheme
R^{\prime}
to its linear span.
Moreover each element of the image of this injective map
contains
\left\langle Q\right\rangle
.
The Grassmannian of linear subspaces
F\subset\left\langle R\right\rangle
such that
\left\langle Q\right\rangle\subset F
and
\dim F=m-1
has dimension at most
l-1=(\deg R-\deg Q)-1
and it is isomorphic to a projective space, namely the appropriate linear subspace of
{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\langle R\right\rangle^{*}\right)
.
By dimension count, this linear subspace must be equal to the image of the above map

{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{s})\to{\mathbb{P}}(\left\langle R\right\rangle^{*})
.
Therefore, every linear subspace
F
as above is the linear span of a subscheme
R^{\prime}
with
\deg R^{\prime}=r-1
and
Q\subset R^{\prime}\subset R
.

In order to prove (
7
)
pick any
E\in\Gr(k,\left\langle R\right\rangle)
.
Define
F^{\prime}=E+\left\langle Q\right\rangle
and note that
\dim F^{\prime}\leqslant k+\deg Q\leqslant r-1
since
l>k
.
Let
F\subset\left\langle R\right\rangle
be any linear subspace such that

F^{\prime}\subset F
and
\dim F=r-1
.
We have
\left\langle Q\right\rangle\subset F
,
so by the above argument there exists a subscheme
R^{\prime}\subset R

such that
\deg R^{\prime}=r-1
,
Q\subset R^{\prime}
and
\left\langle R^{\prime}\right\rangle=F
.
Thus
E\subset\left\langle R^{\prime}\right\rangle
and therefore
E\in\Gr(k,\left\langle R^{\prime}\right\rangle)
, as claimed.
This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

The inclusion of

\overline{\left\{E\in\Gr(k,V)\mid\exists R\subset X\text{ s.t.}\dim R=0,\deg R\leqslant r,\socdim R\leqslant k,E\subset\left\langle R\right\rangle\right\}}

(8)

in
\mathfrak{K}_{r,k}\left(X\right)
is automatic.
To prove the opposite inclusion
take any
E
in
\mathfrak{K}^{\circ}_{r,k}\left(X\right)
,
so that there exists a subscheme
R\subset X
with
\dim R=0
,
\deg R\leqslant r
,
E\subset\left\langle R\right\rangle
.
Suppose without loss of generality that
R
is a minimal such scheme,
that is for any
R^{\prime}\subset R
, if
E\subset\left\langle R^{\prime}\right\rangle
then
R^{\prime}=R
.
If
R
fails to have socle dimension at most
k
,
then
by Lemmas
5
and
6

there exists a smaller scheme
R^{\prime}\subset R
with
\deg R^{\prime}<\deg R
and

E\subset\left\langle R^{\prime}\right\rangle
, a contradiction with minimality of
R
.
Thus
R
has socle dimension at most
k
,
and therefore
E
is in the set (
8
).
Therefore
\mathfrak{K}^{\circ}_{r,k}\left(X\right)
is contained in the set (
8
), which is closed,
thus also
\mathfrak{K}_{r,k}\left(X\right)
is contained in the same set, concluding the proof of the theorem.
∎

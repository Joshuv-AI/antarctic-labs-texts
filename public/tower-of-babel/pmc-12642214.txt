Development and validation of an online nomogram for screening metabolic-associated fatty liver disease in obese children
Authors: Jiaqian Hu, Mengqin Wang, Xi Wang, Mingwei Guo, Yaqing Lu, Zixia Zhang, Miaomiao Li, Guiying Sun, Xiaocui Ma, Yaodong Zhang, Wancun Zhang, Dongxiao Li, Yongxing Chen, Shuying Luo, Haiyan Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMetabolic-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) has emerged as a critical pediatric health concern, particularly among children with obesity. However, its diagnosis poses substantial challenges, especially in the use of non-invasive methods. Our goal was to construct an online nomogram for screening MAFLD in obese children.MethodsWe designed a retrospective cross-sectional study involving 2,512 obese children. Detailed anthropometric data and laboratory parameters were collected. The study dataset was randomly allocated into training (n = 1758) and validation (n = 754) sets at a 7:3 ratio. To identify MAFLD risk factors, we conducted logistic regression analyses, from which a web-based predictive nomogram was constructed. Using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and area under the curve (AUC), the nomogram’s performance was assessed and contrasted with the triglyceride glucose (TyG) index, Zhejiang University (ZJU) index, and Korean NAFLD (K-NAFLD) score. The goodness-of-fit of the nomogram was evaluated using calibration plots, and the nomogram’s clinical value was assessed using decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsA total of 1,344 participants (53.50%) were diagnosed with MAFLD by ultrasound. Age, gender, BMI Z-score, waist circumference (WC), homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) were identified as independent factors influencing MAFLD in obese children. These six variables were selected for the construction of the nomogram. ROC analysis revealed that the nomogram had superior diagnostic performance for MAFLD detection compared to the other three models, with AUC values of 0.874 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.858–0.890) in the training set and 0.870 (95% CI: 0.845–0.895) in the validation set. Calibration plots indicated a good fit of the nomogram in both datasets. Furthermore, DCA demonstrated its strong clinical applicability.ConclusionsThis study developed an online nomogram that demonstrates robust diagnostic accuracy and clinical utility for assessing obese children’s MAFLD risk.Keywords: Obesity children, Metabolic-associated fatty liver disease, Online nomogram, Prediction model

Introduction

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) encompasses a range of chronic liver illnesses. This nutritionally-driven condition develops in genetically susceptible individuals through complex interactions between metabolic dysregulation, particularly insulin resistance, and chronic caloric excess [1]. In a pivotal 2020 consensus statement, an international panel of experts introduced the term metabolic-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) to supplant the conventional NAFLD terminology [2]. This nosological shift highlights the centrality of metabolic dysfunction in disease causes, surpassing the restrictive diagnostic criteria of its predecessor. In 2021, diagnostic criteria for MAFLD in children were established [3]. Recent epidemiological studies indicate considerable geographic disparities in the prevalence of pediatric MAFLD among obese children, with the greatest rates recorded in Asia (52.1%), followed by South America (39.7%) and Europe (23.0%) [4]. These findings highlight the disproportionate metabolic burden borne by Asian populations, potentially reflecting distinct genetic and environmental risk factor distributions. Moreover, compared to adults, MAFLD in children may progress more rapidly and is more likely to advance quickly to hepatic fibrosis and cirrhosis [5]. Therefore, MAFLD in overweight and obese children demands increased clinical attention.

Although the gold standard for pediatric MAFLD diagnosis is liver biopsy, its invasiveness and inconsistent sampling limit its clinical utility [6]. Conventional ultrasound is a commonly used imaging modality, valued for its affordability, non-invasiveness, and good tolerability in children. It is widely used in clinical practice to identify hepatic steatosis, typically through characteristic sonographic findings of enhanced liver echogenicity relative to the renal cortex [7]. However, in primary care settings, the diagnostic accuracy of ultrasound is often limited due to insufficient operator experience, technical constraints, and relatively outdated equipment [8]. Furthermore, neither computed tomography nor conventional magnetic resonance imaging has shown superior diagnostic performance to ultrasonography for detecting hepatic steatosis [9, 10]. Investigating effective strategies for early identification and prevention of MAFLD is thus essential. Non-invasive prediction models offer several advantages in clinical practice, including being non-invasive, easy to operate, cost-effective, requiring simple equipment, and having good reproducibility. Calculated from triglycerides (TG) and fasting plasma glucose (FPG), the triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index has turned into a surrogate biomarker of insulin resistance [11]. First established in 2015 by Chinese researchers, the Zhejiang University (ZJU) index represents a composite metabolic marker integrating five key parameters: body mass index (BMI), FPG, TG, alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) levels [12]. Developed in South Korea in 2020, the Korea NAFLD (K-NAFLD) score represents a validated risk assessment tool incorporating six clinical parameters: sex-specific factors, waist circumference (WC), systolic blood pressure (SBP), FPG, TG, and ALT levels [13]. Nonetheless, these non-invasive predictive models were predominantly designed for adult NAFLD, and research regarding their applicability in pediatric populations is scarce. Developing precise non-invasive predictive models for MAFLD in obese pediatric populations offers a clinically feasible strategy for early identification and intervention in childhood hepatic steatosis.

A nomogram is a practical tool based on multifactorial regression analysis. This innovative approach systematically combines multiple related variables and graphically displays their relative contributions through proportional axis scaling, providing intuitive visualization of complex predictive relationships within a unified framework [14]. The utility of a nomogram lies in its ability to transform complex regression equations into a visual graphic, enhancing the interpretability of predictive model results and facilitating clinicians’ assessment of patients [15]. Based on this, the present study aims to establish a novel non-invasive predictive tool utilizing a nomogram to facilitate accurate risk stratification of MAFLD in pediatric populations with obesity.

Materials and methods

Study population

We recruited obese children aged 7 to 18 from the Department of Endocrinology, Genetics, and Metabolism at the Children’s Hospital affiliated with Zhengzhou University between September 2018 and October 2024 for a retrospective cross-sectional study. The exclusion criteria were: Genetic disorders like Prader-Willi syndrome, Turner syndrome, and Kallmann syndrome; endocrine disorders such as hypothyroidism, Cushing’s disease, polycystic ovary syndrome, and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism; hypothalamic-pituitary axis-related disorders like panhypopituitarism, hypothalamic syndrome, and pituitary stalk interruption syndrome; other conditions like diabetes insipidus and infectious diseases; and incomplete clinical data. The study included 2,512 children (Fig. 1). All patients were ultrasound-screened for fatty liver. The 2,512 participants were randomly assigned to training (n = 1,758) and validation (n = 754) sets in a 7:3 ratio to construct and validate the nomogram. This retrospective analysis was approved by the Children’s Hospital Affiliated to Zhengzhou University Institutional Review Board (Approval No. 2024-094-001) and followed Declaration of Helsinki criteria. The ethics review committee waived informed consent.

MAFLD diagnostic criteria

Two criteria are needed to diagnose MAFLD: (1) imaging or histological evidence of hepatic steatosis, and (2) the presence of at least one metabolic abnormality, such as obesity, impaired glucose metabolism (prediabetes or type 2 diabetes mellitus), or other metabolic disturbances [3]. Based on Chinese pediatric growth references, obesity in children aged 6–18 years was defined as sex- and age-specific BMI ≥ 95th percentile [16]. By clinical guidelines, hepatic steatosis was assessed ultrasonographically [17]. All participants underwent standardized abdominal ultrasound examinations performed by two board-certified radiologists using a Philips EPIQ5 ultrasound system.

Anthropometric data measurements

Pediatric endocrinologists collected participants’ demographic data. Weight and height were measured with calibrated tools. For height measurement, children were asked to stand in a standard posture, and two measurements were taken, with the average value recorded as the final result, accurate to 0.1 cm. For weight measurement, children were required to be in a fasting state, and after removing outer clothing and shoes, they stood on a scale for weighing. Body weight was measured twice and recorded as the mean value with 0.1 kg precision. BMI was computed using the standard formula: weight (kg) divided by height squared (m²). The standard deviation score approach was used to construct BMI Z-scores, referencing the particular growth norms for Chinese children [18]. WC measurements were taken at the midpoint between the superior border of the iliac crest and the inferior margin of the last rib. Following a 10-minute seated rest period, blood pressure measurements were performed in triplicate, with the mean value recorded for analysis.

Laboratory data measurements

Venous blood samples were collected from all participants between 7:00 and 8:00 AM after an 8-hour overnight fast and immediately transported to the clinical laboratory of Children’s Hospital Affiliated to Zhengzhou University. Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) was measured with high-performance liquid chromatography (HLC-723G8; Tosoh Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). FPG, TG, total cholesterol (TC), albumin (ALB), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), serum creatinine (Cr), uric acid (UA), ALT, AST were quantified using an automated biochemical analyzer (AU5800; Beckman Coulter, Brea, USA). Levels of the insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) and fasting insulin (FINS) were assessed using an automated analyzer (Cobas e801; Roche Diagnostics, Basel, Switzerland). The homeostatic model assessment for insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) was then computed in the manner described below: HOMA-IR = FINS [mU/L] × FPG [mmol/L]/22.5 [19].

ZJU = BMI (kg/m²) + FPG (mmol/L) + TG (mmol/L) + 3 × [ALT (U/L)/AST (U/L)] (+ 2, if female);

K-NAFLD = 0.913 (×2, if female) + 0.089 × WC (cm) + 0.032 × [SBP (mmHg) + FPG (mg/dL)] + TG (mg/dL) × 0.007 + ALT (U/L) × 0.105–20.929.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 27.0 (IBM Corp.) and R 4.1.2 software were used for statistical analysis. Data distribution was evaluated with the Shapiro-Wilk normality test. Continuous variables following normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and analyzed with Student’s t-test, while non-parametric data were expressed as median (interquartile range) and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were summarized as counts (percentages) and assessed with the chi-square (χ²) test. Variables demonstrating statistically significant associations (P < 0.05) in univariate analyses were subsequently evaluated using Spearman bivariate correlation analysis to identify variable pairs with a correlation coefficient > 0.5 and significant intergroup differences. One variable from each group was retained for subsequent multivariate analysis to avoid strong multicollinearity. To identify independent predictors, we conducted stepwise multivariate logistic regression analysis. The final selected variables were used as predictors to construct the nomogram in the training dataset, which was then validated in the testing dataset. The performance of the nomogram was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and area under the curve (AUC). The goodness-of-fit of the model was assessed using calibration curves and the Hosmer-Lemeshow test. Additionally, the clinical utility of the model was further examined using decision curve analysis (DCA). A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Participants characteristics

As previously stated, the study enrolled 2,512 participants, of whom 53.50% were diagnosed with MAFLD. Participants were randomly assigned to either the training set (n = 1,758) or the validation set (n = 754). No significant intergroup differences were observed in clinical characteristics (all P > 0.05). The overall dataset had a mean age of 10.95 ± 1.91 years and a median BMI Z-score of 2.96 (2.54, 3.40). Male participants accounted for 70.54% of the sample, with females comprising the remaining 29.46%. Table 1 summarizes the baseline characteristics of both datasets.

Variable screening and Multicollinearity examination

The logistic regression analysis results of variables associated with MAFLD are presented in Table 2. Univariate regression analysis identified 17 significant predictors in the training set: age, gender, BMI Z-score, WC, SBP, DBP, FINS, HOMA-IR, TG, HDL-C, LDL-C, Cr, UA, ALT, AST, HbA1c, and IGF-1 (P < 0.05). However, FPG, ALB, and TC were not significant in the univariate regression analysis. To evaluate multicollinearity between any two variables, the 16 continuous variables chosen from the univariate logistic regression analysis were put through a Spearman bivariate correlation analysis (Fig. 2). Pairs of variables were considered highly collinear if their correlation coefficient was higher than 0.5. ALT and AST showed a high correlation (rho = 0.81). ALT was retained for multivariate modeling due to its stronger liver specificity compared to AST [20]. HOMA-IR and FINS were strongly correlated (rho = 0.99). Given the important pathophysiological significance of HOMA-IR and its current role as a necessary criterion for diagnosing MAFLD, we retained HOMA-IR instead of FINS in the multivariate model [2, 21]. Additionally, SBP and DBP were also correlated (rho = 0.57). We retained SBP over DBP because of SBP’s substantial predictive value for cardiovascular disease, particularly in MAFLD patients who frequently display cardiometabolic risks [22]. Ultimately, HOMA-IR, ALT, and SBP were incorporated into further studies to mitigate extreme multicollinearity.

Following the aforementioned steps, 13 variables were selected, including age, BMI Z-score, WC, SBP, HOMA-IR, TG, HDL-C, LDL-C, Cr, UA, ALT, HbA1c, and IGF-1. Additionally, since children are in a dynamic stage of growth and development, physiological characteristics and metabolic patterns may significantly differ by gender. Establishment and validation of the nomogram model

Figure 3 presents the clinically implementable nomogram developed using R software, incorporating six significant predictors identified through multivariate regression analysis to assess MAFLD risk in children with obesity. This tool is accessible online (https://jiaqianhu.shinyapps.io/nomogram/). In the nomogram, each predictor was assigned a score according to the point scale. The sum of all predictor scores yielded a total point value. By projecting a vertical line downward from the total points axis to the probability axis, the estimated probability of MAFLD in obese children was obtained, ranging from 0.1 to 0.9. Higher total scores indicated a greater likelihood of MAFLD development in this pediatric population. For example, a case of a 7.5-year-old boy with a BMI Z-score of 3.56, a WC of 90.0 cm, a HOMA-IR of 4.73, and an ALT of 48.9 U/L was projected by the nomogram to have a 75.1% MAFLD probability (95% confidence interval [CI]: 68.2–81.0%).

Discussion

Based on six easily accessible clinical indicators, we have established and rigorously validated an innovative predictive model for MAFLD in pediatric populations with obesity. To our knowledge, this represents the first web-based nomogram specifically developed for MAFLD in children with obesity. Initially, univariate logistic regression analysis of 20 clinical features was conducted to select potential predictors. To assess multicollinearity, we performed Spearman’s rank correlation analysis. The selected risk factors were then incorporated into multivariate logistic regression analysis, ultimately yielding six variables for constructing the prediction model, which was visualized through the creation of a nomogram. The nomogram demonstrates good predictive stability across both datasets, showing promising potential as a predictive tool for MAFLD in obese children.

Our model incorporates age, gender, BMI Z-score, WC, HOMA-IR, and ALT, all of which have established associations with MAFLD pathogenesis in pediatric populations. Consistent with current evidence, age emerges as a particularly influential determinant of both disease onset and progression in children with obesity [23, 24]. This is attributed to the fact that as children grow older, their dietary patterns become more complex, increasing the likelihood of consuming high-sugar and high-fat foods, while physical activity may decrease, leading to energy surplus and fat accumulation [25]. The impact of gender on the occurrence of MAFLD is primarily mediated through changes in hormone levels during puberty [26, 27]. Generally, boys undergo a rise in androgen levels during puberty, which may lead to abdominal fat accumulation, thereby elevating the risk of hepatic fat deposition [28]. In contrast, changes in estrogen levels in girls during puberty can modulate lipid metabolism, suppress inflammation, and improve insulin sensitivity [29]. These two aspects affect the development and progression of MAFLD in children via multiple mechanisms. BMI, serving as a clinical indicator of adiposity, has been consistently demonstrated as an independent risk factor for MAFLD in pediatric populations with obesity [30]. The BMI Z-score, which accounts for age and gender factors, offers greater advantages in assessing the risk of MAFLD in children [31]. Elevated WC is frequently associated with visceral obesity [32]. An increase in waist circumference for children signifies the accumulation of abdominal fat. Excessive abdominal fat releases free fatty acids into the liver, resulting in heightened hepatic fatty acid uptake and triglyceride synthesis [33]. Meanwhile, abdominal adipose tissue secretes inflammatory cytokines, triggering hepatic inflammation [34]. Moreover, increased WC is often accompanied by insulin resistance, which enhances lipolysis and further exacerbates hepatic fat deposition [33]. Insulin resistance is recognized as a fundamental factor in the pathogenesis of MAFLD [35, 36]. HOMA-IR has a robust correlation with the development of MAFLD in children, acting as a dependable indicator of insulin resistance in pediatric cohorts [37]. Elevated serum ALT levels are commonly regarded as a marker of liver dysfunction. In MAFLD, pathological accumulation of hepatic triglycerides promotes hepatocyte damage, with consequent release of ALT into circulation and subsequent elevation of serum ALT levels [38]. As MAFLD progresses from simple steatosis to steatohepatitis, the magnitude of ALT elevation may increase further [39]. Elevated ALT levels can serve as one of the diagnostic clues for MAFLD. Our model combines anthropometric parameters with biochemical parameters, demonstrating excellent predictive capability for MAFLD in children.

Our newly developed nomogram provides specific MAFLD risk prediction for Chinese children with obesity, demonstrating superior discriminative ability (AUROC = 0.874, 95%CI: 0.858–0.890) compared to established indices including the TyG index, ZJU index, and K-NAFLD score. The TyG index, a well-established surrogate measure of insulin resistance, has been extensively validated across multiple epidemiological and clinical studies [40, 41]. However, its predictive value for MAFLD remains controversial [42, 43]. In this study, its predictive value for MAFLD was also lower than that of the ZJU index and K-NAFLD score. Zhou et al. [44] incorporated the TyG index as a predictive variable in their final model construction, and the results demonstrated that the developed prediction model exhibited better predictive performance than using the TyG index alone, implying that the TyG index should be combined with additional predictors for a more precise diagnosis of MAFLD. The ZJU index, a prediction model developed based on a large Chinese population, has been shown in multiple studies to have good accuracy in predicting NAFLD in both Chinese adults and children [45–47]. However, its performance in predicting MAFLD in this study was not particularly outstanding. The K-NAFLD score, a recently proposed prediction score, has not yet been widely adopted, but existing studies have reported that the K-NAFLD score appears to be a promising tool for predicting NAFLD, particularly in situations requiring immediate assessment, self-assessment, or when other methods are unavailable [43, 48, 49]. Similar to the nomogram prediction model, the K-NAFLD score incorporates key indicators such as gender, WC, and ALT. In this study, it demonstrated relatively excellent performance, with an AUC value second only to the nomogram prediction model. However, FPG did not show significant results in the univariate logistic regression analysis. Although SBP and TG demonstrated significant associations with pediatric fatty liver in univariate analysis, these relationships lost statistical significance after adjustment for confounding variables. This suggests that, within the current study design and the scope of the included data, these three indicators cannot be identified as independently contributing to fatty liver pathogenesis in children. This result may be related to the physiological characteristics of children and the stage of disease development. Children are in a period of active growth and development, and their blood pressure fluctuates more noticeably than that of adults. Factors such as daily activity levels, emotional changes, and even the timing of a single measurement can cause significant deviations in blood pressure values. This instability makes it difficult to establish a stable statistical connection between blood pressure and fatty liver. Moreover, the occurrence of fatty liver in children is more likely to be directly related to metabolic disorders caused by obesity, rather than being determined solely by blood pressure levels. Core issues arising from obesity, such as insulin resistance and abnormal fat metabolism, often affect hepatic fat deposition before blood pressure abnormalities do [50, 51]. Additionally, although hyperglycemia and hypertriglyceridemia are established fatty liver determinants in adults, in children, these metabolic indicators may not yet have reached levels sufficient to significantly influence the occurrence of fatty liver [52]. The metabolic system of children has a certain compensatory capacity, and early metabolic abnormalities usually present in a latent state. It is difficult to capture a direct association with fatty liver solely through a single measurement of blood glucose and lipid levels. Furthermore, the progression of fatty liver in children is relatively slow, and most cases are in the early stage of fat infiltration. At this stage, the sensitivity of the liver to metabolic abnormalities differs from that in adults, which also leads to a possible “lag effect” of traditional adult fatty liver diagnostic indicators in the pediatric population.

There are several advantages to this study. First, it is based on a substantial sample size and comprehensive clinical data collection, which enhances the reliability of our findings. Additionally, the variables chosen for the MAFLD prediction model are straightforward and readily accessible indicators that do not rely on complex detection techniques, making it particularly suitable for primary care hospitals and epidemiological survey scenarios. In primary care hospitals, these indicators overlap significantly with routine physical examination items and can be directly integrated into the child health management system, thereby reducing screening costs. In epidemiological surveys, the model supports risk assessment through an online version, significantly improving screening efficiency. However, there are certain limitations on this study as well. Firstly, although liver biopsy is the diagnostic gold standard, its invasive nature excluded it from our study design. Thus, the diagnostic precision of the nomogram for MAFLD may be constrained by the lack of histological validation. Secondly, this is a single-center study, which may have selection bias. Thirdly, the sample of this study focuses on the obese pediatric population, and due to the absence of external data validation from other centers or different ethnic groups, it is uncertain whether the model can be applied to different age groups or non-obese populations. Therefore, future research should consider expansions in the following aspects. Firstly, the predictive accuracy of the model should be improved by incorporating additional potential confounding variables, such as genetic and lifestyle factors. Secondly, the model should be tested in larger-scale multicenter studies to assess its stability and generalizability across many demographics and settings. Additionally, if feasible, subgroup analyses (e.g., stratified by age groups, sex, or pubertal stage) might be added in subsequent studies to further verify the stability of the model across different subpopulations, which will help clarify its applicability in specific demographic subgroups.

Conclusion

In summary, compared to other existing models, we have developed an effective predictive model for diagnosing MAFLD in obese Chinese children. The constructed nomogram has undergone internal validation and demonstrates good accuracy and reliability, facilitating clinicians in screening obese children for MAFLD. High-risk individuals should undergo comprehensive diagnostic evaluation to enable timely implementation of lifestyle modifications or pharmacotherapy, thereby mitigating disease progression. Therefore, the MAFLD prediction model established in this study possesses considerable clinical value. Nonetheless, external validation in other cohorts remains necessary to establish its generalizability.

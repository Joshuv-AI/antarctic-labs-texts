Solution of Least Squares Problems with Randomized Preconditioned Normal Equations
Ilse C.F. Ipsen

Abstract
We consider the solution of full column-rank least squares problems by means of normal equations that are preconditioned, symmetrically or non-symmetrically, with a randomized preconditioner. With an effective preconditioner, the solutions from the preconditioned normal equations are almost as accurate as those from the QR-based Matlab backslash (mldivide) command – even for highly illconditioned matrices. This means the accuracy of the preconditioned normal equations depends on the residual of the original least squares problem. We present non-intuitive but realistic perturbation bounds for the relative error in the computed solutions and show that, with an effective preconditioner, these bounds are essentially equal to the perturbation bound for the original least squares problem. Probabilitistic condition number bounds corroborate the effectiveness of the randomized preconditioner computed with small amounts of sampling.

Introduction

Given a matrix
\mathbf{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
with
\rank(\mathbf{A})=n
, and
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
we consider
the solution of the least squares problem

\min_{\mathbf{x}}{\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}\|_{2}},

(1.1)

which has the unique solution
\mathbf{x}_{*}\equiv\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}
. The preferred solution
method is a QR or Singular Value Decomposition
[
5

Instead, we consider a method based on the normal equations.

The normal equations

\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{b}

(1.2)

are usually not recommended due to potential numerical instability

[
5
, Section 5.3.7]
. Since their condition number
1

Preconditioned Normal Equations.

We precondition
\mathbf{A}_{p}\equiv\mathbf{A}\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}

effective randomized preconditioner
\mathbf{R}_{s}

\mathbf{A}_{p}

\begin{split}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{y}&=\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{b}\\
\mathbf{R}_{s}\mathbf{x}&=\mathbf{y}.\end{split}

Half Preconditioned Normal Equations.

The alternative is to dispense with the triangular system solution by solving

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{b}.

The matrix
\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}
is nonsymmetric, so the linear system

If an iterative solver were used, these would correspond to the left preconditioned

for
\mathbf{A}

\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{A}

Contributions and Overview

We show that the normal equations, preconditioned with an effective randomized preconditioner
on one or both sides are highly accurate, even for ill conditioned matrices.
We present non-intuitive but realistic perturbation bounds for the relative error in the computed solutions
of the preconditioned and half-preconditioned normal equations, and show the following:

1.

With an effective preconditioner, the solutions are almost as accurate

matrices

A number of papers investigate the solution of nonsingular nonsymmetric systems

\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}

\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{b}

[

]

if
\mathbf{P}

\mathbf{A}
, then
\mathbf{P}^{T}\mathbf{P}
is not
necessarily a good preconditioner for
\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{A}
.

Epperly, Greenbaum and Nakatsukasa
[
3
]
investigate preconditioned
LSQR combined with iterative refinement.
Lazzarino, Nakatsukasa and Zerbinati

[
10
]
consider systems arising from PDE

Scott and Tumå

[

Carson and Dauz̆ickaitė

1.3
Notation and Background

From now on, the Euclidean two-norm is simply denoted by
\|\cdot\|
. For a matrix
\mathbf{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
with

\rank(\mathbf{A})=n
, the Moore-Penrose inverse is
\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}=(\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{A}^{T}
,
the two-norm condition number with respect to left inversion is

\kappa(\mathbf{A})=\|\mathbf{A}\|\|\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}\|
, and the singular values are

\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{A})\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{A})>0
.

To set the context, we give a perturbation bound for the original least squares problem (
1.1
).

Lemma 1.1

(Fact 5.14 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{E}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
with
\rank(\mathbf{A})=\rank(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{E})=n
and

\epsilon_{A}\equiv\|\mathbf{E}\|/\|\mathbf{A}\|
. Let
\mathbf{x}_{*}
be the solution
to
\min_{\mathbf{x}}{\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}\|}
and
\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\neq\mathbf{0}
the solution to
\min_{\mathbf{x}}{\|(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{E})\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}\|}
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}-\mathbf{x}_{*}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}\leq\kappa(\mathbf{A})\,\epsilon_{A}\,\left(1+\kappa(\mathbf{A})\frac{\|\mathbf{b}-(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{E})\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}\right).

The condition number is
\kappa(\mathbf{A})\,\max\left\{1,\kappa(\mathbf{A})\rho\right\}
,
where
\rho
represents a least squares residual.
We limit perturbations to those of the matrix
\mathbf{A}
, and assume an exact right hand side
\mathbf{b}
,
because matrix perturbations tend to be much more influential on the sensitivity of least squares problems
than right-hand side perturbations.

2
Perturbation of the preconditioned normal equations

We derive realistic perturbation bounds for the preconditioned normal equations
(Section
2.2
), after presenting the assumptions (Section
2.1
).

2.1
Assumptions

Let
\mathbf{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
have
\rank(\mathbf{A})=n
. Let
\mathbf{R}_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be a fixed nonsingular matrix, and

\mathbf{A}_{p}\equiv\mathbf{A}\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}
.
The exact preconditioned normal equations are

\begin{split}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{y}_{*}&=\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{b}\\
\mathbf{R}_{s}\mathbf{x}_{*}&=\mathbf{y}_{*}.\end{split}

(2.1)

Since
\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{R}_{s}
are nonsingular, so is the preconditioned matrix
\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}
.

The first step of the preconditioned normal equations (
2.1
) is mathematically equivalent
to the least squares problem
\min_{\mathbf{y}}{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{b}\|_{2}}
,
which has the unique solution
\mathbf{y}_{*}\equiv\mathbf{A}_{p}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}
.
Since the right preconditioned
matrix
\mathbf{A}_{p}
has the same column space as
\mathbf{A}
, the least
squares residual is equal to that of the original problem (
1.1
),

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{y}_{*}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{*}.

(2.2)

In order to replicate the numerical results in Section
5
,
we set up the first step with two different perturbations for the
preconditioned matrix
\mathbf{A}_{p}
, so that the resulting linear system is nonsymmetric.
We assume that the triangular system solution in the second step is computed exactly, because
any errors have only a minor, lower order effect.

Let
\mathbf{E}_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
and
\mathbf{E}_{p}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
,
\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\epsilon<1
, and

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{1}\equiv\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{R}_{s}+\mathbf{E}_{s})^{-1},\qquad\mathbf{A}_{2}\equiv\mathbf{A}_{p}+\mathbf{E}_{p},\qquad\epsilon\equiv\max\left\{\tfrac{\|\mathbf{E}_{s}\|}{\|\mathbf{R}_{s}\|},\tfrac{\|\mathbf{E}_{p}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\|}\right\}.

The computed solutions corresponding to (
2.1
) are modeled as

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{2}\mathbf{\hat{y}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{b}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{s}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\hat{y}},

(2.4)

where
\mathbf{\hat{y}}\neq\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\neq\mathbf{0}
.

2.2
Perturbation bound

We state the main result (Theorem
2.1
),
prove auxiliary results (Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
), and give intuition for the
perturbation analysis (Remark
2.1
).

Theorem 2.1
.

With the assumptions in Section
2.1
,

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}\leq\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\,\nu\,\left(\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\epsilon+\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})^{2}\,\eta\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}+\epsilon\right)\right),

where

\displaystyle\nu\equiv\frac{\|\mathbf{R}_{s}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{R}_{s}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}\leq 1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta\equiv\frac{\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\epsilon}{1-\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\,\epsilon}.

Proof.

Substitute the bound from Lemma
2.1
below into Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Theorem
2.1
shows that the solution accuracy of the preconditioned normal equations depends
on the least squares residual of the original least squares problem (
1.1
).
Lemma
A.1
in Appendix
A

shows that this dependence is also present in the ordinary normal equations.

Theorem
2.1
implies that, to first order, the relative error in
\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}\lesssim\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\,\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\,\epsilon\,\max\left\{1,\>\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\frac{\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}\right\}.

(2.5)

That is, if the least squares residual is sufficiently small, so that

\displaystyle\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\,\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\frac{\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}\leq 1,

then the relative error in
\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}
is dominated by
\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\,\epsilon
.
Otherwise, the relative error in
\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}
is proportional to the least
squares residual.

If the preconditioner
\mathbf{R}_{s}
is effective, then the preconditioned normal equations (
2.1
)
are numerically stable, because the bound in Theorem
2.1

resembles the perturbation bound of the original
least squares problem in Lemma
1.1
.

Why? An effective preconditioner produces
\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\approx 1
and

\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\approx\kappa(\mathbf{A})
, so that
\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\approx\kappa(\mathbf{A})
.
Hence the condition number in (
2.5
) is about the same as
that in Lemma
1.1
,

\displaystyle\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\,\max\left\{1,\,\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\rho\right\}\approx\kappa(\mathbf{A})\,\max\left\{1,\kappa(\mathbf{A})\rho\right\},

(2.6)

where
\rho
represents a least squares residual.
Furthermore, (
2.2
) implies that the exact
least squares residuals of the original and preconditioned problem are the same.
Hence (
2.6
) has the same form as the bound in Lemma
1.1
.
The
numerical experiments in Section
5.2
illustrate that Theorem
2.1
is
informative and realistic.

The following two lemmas form the basis for the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 2.1

(Perturbation bound for (
2.3
))
.

With the assumptions in Section
2.1
,

\frac{\|\mathbf{y}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}\leq\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\epsilon+\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})^{2}\,\eta\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}+\epsilon\right),

where

\eta\equiv\frac{\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\epsilon}{1-\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\,\epsilon}.

Proof.

Write

\mathbf{A}_{1}=\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{R}_{s}+\mathbf{E}_{s})^{-1}=\underbrace{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}}_{\mathbf{A}_{p}}(\mathbf{I}+\underbrace{\mathbf{E}_{s}\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}}_{\mathbf{F}})^{-1}=\mathbf{A}_{p}(\mathbf{I}-\underbrace{(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{F})^{-1}\mathbf{F}}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}})=\mathbf{A}_{p}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{F}_{p}),

where

\mathbf{F}\equiv\mathbf{E}_{s}\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}
and
\mathbf{F}_{p}\equiv(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{F})^{-1}\mathbf{F}
.
Then (
2.3
) can be written as

\mathbf{A}_{1}^{T}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\hat{y}})=\mathbf{A}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{E}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}}

With
\mathbf{A}_{1}=\mathbf{A}_{p}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{F}_{p})
this gives

(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{F}_{p})^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}})=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{F}_{p})^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{E}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}}.

Rearrange,

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{F}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}})+(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{F}_{p})^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{E}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{F}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}})+\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{E}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}}-\mathbf{F}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{E}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}},

and multiply by
(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p})^{-1}
,

\mathbf{y}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{y}}=\mathbf{A}_{p}^{\dagger}\mathbf{E}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}}+(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p})^{-1}\left(\mathbf{F}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}})-\mathbf{F}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{E}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}}\right).

Take norms and use the fact that
\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p})=\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})^{2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{y}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\frac{\|\mathbf{E}_{p}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\|}+\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})^{2}\,\|\mathbf{F}_{p}\|\,\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}+\frac{\|\mathbf{E}_{p}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\,\epsilon+\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})^{2}\,\|\mathbf{F}_{p}\|\,\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}+\epsilon\right).

At last bound

\|\mathbf{F}_{p}\|\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{F}\|}{1-\|\mathbf{F}\|}\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{E}_{s}\|\|\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}\|}{1-\|\mathbf{E}_{s}\|\|\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}\|}\leq\frac{\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\epsilon}{1-\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\epsilon}=\eta.

∎

Lemma 2.2

(Perturbation bound for (
2.4
))
.

With the assumptions in Section
2.1
,

\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}\leq\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\,\nu\,\frac{\|\mathbf{y}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}\qquad\text{where}\qquad\nu\equiv\frac{\|\mathbf{R}_{s}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{R}_{s}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}\leq 1.

Proof.

From
\mathbf{x}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}=\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}(\mathbf{y}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{y}})
follows

\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}\leq\|\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}\|\,\frac{\|\mathbf{y}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}=\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\,\underbrace{\frac{\|\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}{\|\mathbf{R}_{s}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}}_{\nu}\,\frac{\|\mathbf{y}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|}.

From
\|\mathbf{\hat{y}}\|=\|\mathbf{R}_{s}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|\leq\|\mathbf{R}_{s}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|
follows
\nu\leq 1
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Why do we have to resort to a nonsymmetric perturbation in Theorem
2.1
?
It is because symmetric perturbations lead to unrealistic condition numbers. Here are the details.

1.

Intuitively we would just apply the perturbation bound for the normal equations in
Lemma
A.1

to the preconditioned normal equations (
2.3
), and then
account for the triangular system solution via Lemma
2.2
.

This is precisely what Lemma
A.2
does: It perturbs all instances of
\mathbf{A}_{p}
by the
same matrix
\mathbf{E}_{p}
, which leads to the condition number

\displaystyle\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})^{2}\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\max\{1,\rho\},

where
\rho
is a least squares residual.
The condition number in Lemma
1.1
, and numerical experiments
indicate that this is too optimistic.

2.

Lemma
A.3
shows that perturbing all instances of
\mathbf{R}_{s}

by the same matrix
\mathbf{E}_{s}
leads to a condition number

\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})^{2}\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})^{2}\max\{1,\rho\}
, where
\rho
is a least squares residual.
A comparison with (
2.6
) and numerical experiments illustrate that this is too pessimistic.

3
Perturbation of half-preconditioned normal equations

We derive realistic perturbation bounds for the half-preconditioned normal equations, under the
following assumptions.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
have
\rank(\mathbf{A})=n
. Let
\mathbf{R}_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be a fixed
nonsingular matrix and
\mathbf{A}_{p}\equiv\mathbf{A}\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}
.
The exact half-preconditioned normal equations are

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{*}=\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{b}.

(3.1)

Since
\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{R}_{s}
are nonsingular, so is the half-preconditioned
matrix
\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{E}_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
,
\mathbf{E}_{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{1}\equiv\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{R}_{s}+\mathbf{E}_{s})^{-1},\qquad\mathbf{A}_{2}\equiv\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{E}_{A},\qquad\epsilon\equiv\max\left\{\frac{\|\mathbf{E}_{s}\|}{\|\mathbf{R}_{s}\|},\frac{\|\mathbf{E}_{A}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}\|}\right\},

\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\epsilon<1
, and

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{2}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}=\mathbf{A}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{b}.

(3.2)

If
\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\neq\mathbf{0}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}\leq\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A})\,\nu\,\left(\eta\frac{\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}+(1+\eta)\epsilon\right),

where

\displaystyle\nu\equiv\frac{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\|\|\mathbf{A}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}\|}\geq 1,\qquad\eta\equiv\frac{\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\epsilon}{1-\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\epsilon}.

Proof.

Write

\mathbf{A}_{1}=\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{R}_{s}+\mathbf{E}_{s})^{-1}=\underbrace{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}}_{\mathbf{A}_{p}}(\mathbf{I}+\underbrace{\mathbf{E}_{s}\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}}_{\mathbf{F}})^{-1}=\mathbf{A}_{p}(\mathbf{I}-\underbrace{(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{F})^{-1}\mathbf{F}}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}})=\mathbf{A}_{p}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{F}_{p}),

where

\mathbf{F}\equiv\mathbf{E}_{s}\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}
and
\mathbf{F}_{p}\equiv(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{F})^{-1}\mathbf{F}
.
Then (
3.2
) can be written as

\mathbf{A}_{1}^{T}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}})=\mathbf{A}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{E}_{A}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}.

With
\mathbf{A}_{1}=\mathbf{A}_{p}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{F}_{p})
, this gives

(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{F}_{p})^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}})=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{F}_{p})^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{E}_{A}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}.

Rearrange

\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}})=\mathbf{F}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}})+(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{F}_{p})^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{E}_{A}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}

and multiply by
(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A})^{-1}
,

\mathbf{x}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}=(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A})^{-1}\left(\mathbf{F}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}})+(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{F}_{p})^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{E}_{A}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\right).

Take norms

\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}\leq\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A})\,\underbrace{\frac{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\|\|\mathbf{A}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}\|}}_{\nu}\,\left(\|\mathbf{F}_{p}\|\,\frac{\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}+(1+\|\mathbf{F}_{p}\|)\underbrace{\frac{\|\mathbf{E}_{A}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}\|}}_{\leq\epsilon}\right),

and bound

\|\mathbf{F}_{p}\|\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{F}\|}{1-\|\mathbf{F}\|}\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{E}_{s}\|\|\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}\|}{1-\|\mathbf{E}_{s}\|\|\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}\|}\leq\frac{\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\epsilon}{1-\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\epsilon}=\eta.

∎

Theorem
3.1
implies that, to first order, the relative error in
\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}
is bounded by

\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{*}-\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}\lesssim\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A})\,\nu\,\epsilon\left(1+\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\frac{\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}\|\|\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\|}\right).

(3.3)

If the preconditioner
\mathbf{R}_{s}
is effective, then the half-preconditioned normal
equations (
3.1
) are numerically stable, because the bounds in Theorem
3.1

and (
3.3
) resemble the perturbation bound of the original
least squares problem in Lemma
1.1
.

Why? The singular values of
\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}
are bounded by

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{p})\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{A})\leq\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A})\leq\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{p})\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{A}),\qquad 1\leq j\leq n.

Thus
\kappa_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A})\leq\kappa_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{p})\kappa_{2}(\mathbf{A})
.
An effective preconditioner produces
\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\approx 1
and

\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\approx\kappa(\mathbf{A})
, so that
\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A})\approx\kappa(\mathbf{A})
.
Furthermore

\|\mathbf{A}_{p}\|_{2}\approx 1
and
\|\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}\|_{2}\approx\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}

implies
\nu\approx 1
.
Hence the condition number in (
3.3
) is about the same as
that in Lemma
1.1
,

\displaystyle\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A})\nu\max\left\{1,\,\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\rho\right\}\approx\kappa(\mathbf{A})\,\max\left\{1,\kappa(\mathbf{A})\rho\right\},

(3.4)

where
\rho
represents a least squares residual.
The
numerical experiments in section
5.3
illustrate that Theorem
3.1
is
informative and realistic, with
\nu\leq 2
for the randomized preconditioner.

In the special case

\mathbf{A}_{p}=\mathbf{A}
for the normal equations (
1.2
), we have
\nu=1

and Theorem
3.1
essentially reduces to Lemma
A.1
.

Remark 3.1
.

Why did we have to resort to a nonsymmetric perturbation in Theorem
3.1
?
It is because symmetric perturbations lead to unrealistic condition numbers.

Lemma
A.4
shows that perturbing
\mathbf{A}_{p}
and
\mathbf{A}

leads to a condition number

\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A})\nu\max\{1,\rho\}
, where
\rho
is a least squares residual.
Lemma
1.1
and numerical experiments indicate that this is too optimistic.

4.1
Randomized Preconditioner

The randomized preconditioner, motivated by the least squares solver

Blendenpik

[
1
]
,
is computed with the pseudocode in Algorithm
1
.

Given
\mathbf{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
with
\rank(\mathbf{A})=n
, we produce a smaller dimensional matrix
by sampling
c
rows from the smoothed matrix

\ \mathbf{A}_{s}\equiv\mathbf{S}\,\mathcal{F}\,\mathbf{A}
uniformly and with replacement.
The matrix
\mathcal{F}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is a random orthogonal matrix, where

\mathbf{F}
is a discrete cosine transform (DCT-2),

\mathbf{F}_{ij}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\>\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2m}(2j-1)(i-1)\right)\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq m

and
\mathbf{D}
is random diagonal matrix whose diagonal elements are

\mathbf{D}_{jj}=\pm 1
with probability 1/2,
1\leq j\leq m
.
The matrix
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{c\times m}
samples
c
rows
k_{1},\ldots k_{c}

from the identity
\mathbf{I}_{m}
, uniformly and with replacement,

\mathbf{I}_{m}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{e}_{m}^{T}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}\qquad\quad\mathbf{S}\equiv\sqrt{\tfrac{m}{c}}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{e}_{k_{1}}^{T}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{e}_{k_{c}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{c\times m}.

In expectation we have
\E[\mathbf{S}^{T}\mathbf{S}]=\mathbf{I}_{m}
.

Algorithm 1

Computation of the randomized preconditioner

Given
\mathbf{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
with
\rank(\mathbf{A})=n
, sampling amount
c\geq n

Sample
c

\mathbf{A}_{s}\equiv\mathbf{S}\mathcal{F}\mathbf{A}

\mathbf{R}_{s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
from thin QR decomposition
\mathbf{A}_{s}=\mathbf{Q}_{s}\,\mathbf{R}_{s}

Precondition the matrix:
\mathbf{A}_{p}\equiv\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{R}_{s}^{-1}

4.2
Condition Number Bounds

After presenting the assumptions (Assumptions
4.1
) and an auxiliary deterministic result (Lemma
4.1
), we present probabilistic bounds for the singular values
of the preconditioned matrix (Theorem
4.1
), followed by probabilistic bounds on the
condition numbers of the preconditioner and the preconditioned matrices (Theorem
4.2
).

Assumptions 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
with
\rank(\mathbf{A})=n
and thin QR factorization
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{R}
where
\mathbf{Q}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}

with
\mathbf{Q}^{T}\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{I}_{n}
. Let
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{c\times n}
sample
c
rows uniformly, independently, and with replacement.
Let
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
be a random orthogonal matrix, and let
\mathcal{F}\mathbf{Q}

have coherence
\mu\equiv\max_{1\leq i\leq m}{\|\mathbf{e}_{i}^{T}\mathcal{F}\mathbf{Q}\|_{2}^{2}}
.
Let the sampled matrix be
\mathbf{A}_{s}\equiv\mathbf{S}\mathcal{F}\mathbf{Q}
.

We express the singular values of the preconditioned matrix
\mathbf{A}_{p}

in terms of the singular values of
\mathbf{S}\mathcal{F}\mathbf{Q}
.

Lemma 4.1

(Lemma 4.1 in
[
4
]
)
.

Under Assumptions
4.1
, if also
\rank(\mathbf{A}_{s})=n
, then

\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathcal{F}\mathbf{Q})=1/\sigma_{n-i+1}(\mathbf{A}_{p}),\qquad 1\leq i\leq n,

and
\kappa(\mathbf{S}\mathcal{F}\mathbf{Q})=\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})
.

We extend
[
9
, Corollary 4.2]
by deriving lower and bounds for the singular values
of the preconditioned matrix. The bounds below hold
for all singular value simultaneously.

Theorem 4.1
.

Under Assumptions
4.1
, for any
0<\epsilon<1
and
0<\delta<1
, if

c\geq 2m\,\mu\,\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{3}\right)\,\frac{\ln{(n/\delta)}}{\epsilon^{2}}

then with probability at least
1-\delta

\sqrt{\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}}\leq\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{A}_{p})\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}},\qquad 1\leq j\leq n.

Proof.

Set
\mathbf{X}\equiv(\mathbf{S}\mathcal{F}\mathbf{Q})^{T}(\mathbf{S}\mathcal{F}\mathbf{Q})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
where

\E[\mathbf{X}]=\mathbf{I}_{n}=(\mathcal{F}\mathbf{Q})^{T}(\mathcal{F}\mathbf{Q})
.
Apply steps 1-4 in the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 7.5]
to deduce

\myP[\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{I}_{n}\|_{2}>\epsilon]\leq n\exp\left(\frac{-c\epsilon^{2}}{2m\mu(1+\epsilon/3)}\right).

Then solve for
c
. Weyl’s theorem
[
5
, Corollary 8.1.6]

implies for the eigenvalues

\max_{1\leq j\leq n}{|\lambda_{j}(\mathbf{X})-1|}\leq\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{I}_{n}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon.

Hence
1-\epsilon\leq\lambda_{j}(\mathbf{X})\leq 1+\epsilon
,
1\leq j\leq n
.
The result follows from
\lambda_{j}(\mathbf{X})=\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{S}\mathcal{F}\mathbf{Q})^{2}

and Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Theorem
4.1
implies probabilistic lower and upper bounds for the condition number of
the preconditioned matrix

\sqrt{\frac{1-\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}}\leq\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p})\leq\sqrt{\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}},

(4.1)

where the second inequality is well known

[
1
,
9
,
13
]
.

We apply Theorem
4.1
to derive probabilistic lower and upper bounds on the condition
numbers of: the preconditioner, the matrix in the preconditioned normal equations, and the matrix in the half-preconditioned normal equations.

Theorem 4.2
.

Under Assumptions
4.1
, for any
0<\epsilon<1
and
0<\delta<1
, if

c\geq 2m\,\mu\,\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{3}\right)\,\frac{\ln{(n/\delta)}}{\epsilon^{2}}

then with probability at least
1-\delta
, the following hold simultaneously,

\sqrt{\frac{1-\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}}\,\kappa(\mathbf{A})\leq\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})\leq\sqrt{\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}}\,\kappa(\mathbf{A}),\qquad\frac{1-\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\leq\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p})\leq\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon},\qquad

and

\sqrt{\frac{1-\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}}\,\kappa(\mathbf{A})\leq\kappa(\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A})\leq\sqrt{\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}}\,\kappa(\mathbf{A}).

Proof.

The bound for
\kappa(\mathbf{R}_{s})
follows from the application of the singular value product inequalities
[
7
, (7.3.13)]
to
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{R}_{s}
,

\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{p})\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{R}_{s})\leq\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{A})\leq\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{p})\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{R}_{s}),\qquad 1\leq j\leq n,

and (
4.1
). The remaining inequalities are derived analogously.
∎

Theorem
4.2
implies that for small
\delta
and
\epsilon
, the condition numbers
of
\mathbf{R}_{s}
and
\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}
are close to that of
\kappa(\mathbf{A})
; and the condition number
of
\mathbf{A}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{p}
is close to one.

6
Future Work

Our perturbation analysis and numerical experiments show that the normal equations, when preconditioned either on both sides or else only on the left side by a randomized preconditioner,
produce a solution that
is almost as accurate as the one from the Matlab backslash (mldivide) command which,
for rectangular matrices, is based on a QR decomposition.
This means, that the solution accuracy of the preconditioned normal depends on the residual
of the original least squares problem – even though the half-preconditioned normal equations
do not have an equivalent least squares problem.

While the present paper focusses on numerical accuracy, future work will investigate computational
speed.
First is a comparison with established methods for solving least squares problems, including the QR decomposition, the unpreconditioned normal equations,
and the randomized iterative solver Blendenpik
[
1
]
.
Second is a speed up of the preconditioned normal equations
via a mixed precision implementation, where the preconditioner
is computed in a lower arithmetic precision, and then promoted back to double precision for the
computation of the preconditioned matrix.
Third is a GPU implementation of the mixed precision version.

The index of the cosmological horizon and the area-charge-inequality
Neilha Pinheiro

Abstract
. In this article, we investigate the index of the MOTS given by a spatial cross section of the cosmological horizon in the Kerr-Newman-de Sitter spacetime. We show that its index is one in the symmetrized sense for a small positive parameter a a , such parameter defines the angular momentum. As an application, we prove that this MOTS is unstable. We also establish an estimate relating the area and the charge of a MOTS of index one in a Cauchy data satisfying the dominant energy condition.



1.
Introduction

The notion of marginally outer trapped surface (MOTS) gives an effective way to understand some properties of black holes. Under suitable circumstances, the existence of MOTS is a good signal of the eventual formation of a black hole spacetime; for more details, see, e.g.
[
4
,
10
]
. This turns out MOTS a relevant object of study in General Relativity. MOTS are the critical points of the area functional for a certain class of variations. Otherwise, the stationary points of the area functional with respect to all possible variations are minimal surfaces. The study of MOTS turns out to have many similarities with the study of minimal surfaces.

The Morse index of a minimal surface is a traditional object of investigation in geometry, such index plays a crucial role in proving classification results. In the first part of this paper, we will analyze the index of the MOTS given by a spatial cross section of the cosmological horizon in the Kerr-Newman-de Sitter spacetime.

In order to state our first results, it is important to introduce some terminology.

Let
\mathcal{N}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
be the Lorentzian 4-manifold endowed with the Kerr-Newman-de Sitter metric which in the Boyer-Lindquist coordinates is described by

(1)

\bar{g}=-\frac{\Delta_{r}}{\rho^{2}}\Big(dt-\frac{a\sin^{2}\theta}{\Xi}d\phi\Big)^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}dr^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta_{\theta}}d\theta^{2}+\frac{\Delta_{\theta}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\Big(adt-\frac{r^{2}+a^{2}}{\Xi}d\phi\Big)^{2},

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{\theta}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\Lambda}{3}a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,

\displaystyle\Delta_{r}

\displaystyle=(r^{2}+a^{2})\left(1-\frac{\Lambda r^{2}}{3}\right)-2mr+q^{2},

\displaystyle\rho^{2}

\displaystyle=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,

\displaystyle\Xi

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\Lambda a^{2}}{3}.

The parameters
a
,
m
, and
q
determine the angular momentum, the mass, and the charge,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}=\frac{am}{\Xi^{2}},\,\,\,\mathcal{M}=\frac{m}{\Xi^{2}},\,\,\,\mathcal{Q}=\frac{q}{\Xi},

respectively. In the sequel, we choose the values of the parameters
m>0
and
a\geq 0
in such a way that
\Delta_{r}
admits four distinct real roots, which are noted

r_{--}<0<r_{-}<r_{+}<r_{c}.

The roots
r_{--}
,
r_{-}
,
r_{+}
and
r_{c}
have a physical meaning, that is,
\{r=r_{-}\}
is the inner (Cauchy) black hole horizon,
\{r=r_{+}\}
is the outher (Killing) black hole horizon,
\{r=r_{c}\}
is the cosmological horizon, and the root
r_{--}
has no physical meaning. The cosmological horizon appears when the cosmological constant
\Lambda
is positive like in the Kerr-Newman-de Sitter spacetime.

From now on, we assume
\mathcal{N}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
equipped with the Reissner-Nordström-de Sitter metric given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=-\frac{\Delta}{r^{2}}dt^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{\Delta}dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}},

where
\Delta=-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{4}+r^{2}-2mr+\mathcal{Q}^{2}.

This metric is obtained by setting
a=0
in (
1
). We will denote
(M,\,g,\,K)
to be an induced initial data with
M:=\{t=0\}
and
K
is second fundamental form. In the
(r,\,\theta,\,\phi)
coordinate system, the induced metric
g
is expressed by

\displaystyle g=\frac{r^{2}}{\Delta}dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.

Using the change of variables
r=\bar{F}(y)
such that

\frac{dr}{dy}=\sqrt{\Delta},\quad\forall y.

Thus, the metric
g
can be expressed in coordinates
(y,\,\theta,\,\phi)
as

g=r^{2}dy^{2}+r^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.

The induced metric on MOTS

\Sigma:=\{y=y_{0};\,r_{0}=\bar{F}(y_{0})\ \mbox{is a root of}\ \Delta\}

is given by

(2)

h=r_{0}^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}},

where
g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
is the metric of the sphere with radius 1.

The
index
of
\Sigma
in the symmetrized sense is the number of (counted with multiplicity) negative eigenvalues of the symmetrized operator
L_{s}
. Also, we say that
\Sigma
is
stable
if
\lambda_{1}(L)\geq 0
and
\Sigma
is
unstable
if
\lambda_{1}(L)<0
, where
L
is the stability operator for MOTS (see Section
2
for definitions).

Taking into account the above discussion, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Let

\Sigma:=\{y=y_{c};\,r_{c}=\bar{F}(y_{c})\ \mbox{is a root of}\ \Delta\}

with

(3)

\frac{2\mathcal{Q}(\Sigma)^{2}}{3}\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}(\Sigma)^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}<m.

Then

\lambda_{1}(L_{s}(a))<0<\lambda_{2}(L_{s}(a))

for a small
a>0
and
\Sigma
has index one in the symmetrized sense.

As a consequence of Theorem
1
, we obtain the following corollary (see Section
2
for definitions).

Corollary 2
.

Let

\Sigma:=\{y=y_{c};\,r_{c}=\bar{F}(y_{c})\ \mbox{is a root of}\ \Delta\}.

Then

\lambda_{1}(L(a))<0

for a small
a>0
and
\Sigma
is unstable.

Assuming that the charge
\mathcal{Q}(\Sigma)
is equal to zero, the metric
\bar{g}
in (
1
) becomes the Kerr-de Sitter metric. In this setting, we have the following result from Theorem
1
.

Corollary 3
.

Let

\Sigma:=\{y=y_{c};\,r_{c}=\bar{F}(y_{c})\ \mbox{is a root of}\ \Delta\}.

Then

\lambda_{1}(L_{s}(a))<0<\lambda_{2}(L_{s}(a))

for a small
a>0
,
\Sigma
has index one in the symmetrized sense, and

r^{\prime}_{0}<\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{\Lambda}},

where
r^{\prime}_{0}
is a positive root of
\Delta_{r}
in the Kerr-de Sitter case.

From now on, we will introduce some terminology that will be important in presenting our next result. Let
(M^{3},\,g,\,E)
be as part of a Cauchy data for the Einstein-Maxwell equations satisfying the dominant energy condition for the non-electromagnetic field. Then

\displaystyle\mu-|J|_{g}\geq 0,

where
\mu
is the energy density,
J
is the momentum density of the non-electromagnetic
fields, and
(M^{3},\,g)
is an oriented Riemannian manifold.
Now, we take
\Sigma\subset M
an orientable closed surface with unit normal
\nu
. The charge is expressed by

(4)

\mathcal{Q}(\Sigma)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\langle E,\nu\rangle\,d\mu,

where
E\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is the electric field.

In
[
5
]
, the authors consider this setting to establish an estimate involving the area and the charge of a minimal surface of index one. Inspired by their work and the fact that the MOTS given by a spatial cross section of the cosmological horizon in the Kerr-Newman-de Sitter spacetime has index one in the symmetrized sense, we also consider the same setting to derive the following estimate in the context of MOTS instead of minimal surface (see Section
2
for definitions).

Theorem 4
.

Let
(M^{3},\,g,\,K)
be a maximal initial data such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sc}_{g}\geq 2\Lambda+2|E|^{2}

with positive cosmological constant
\Lambda
. Also, we consider a MOTS
\Sigma
in
M
with index one in the symmetrized sense such that
\Sigma
is a topological sphere. Then

(5)

\displaystyle\Lambda|\Sigma|+\frac{16\pi^{2}\mathcal{Q}(\Sigma)^{2}}{|\Sigma|}\leq 12\pi.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
\chi_{+}\equiv 0
,
E_{\Sigma}=c\nu
for some constant
c
and
(\operatorname{Sc}_{g})_{\Sigma}\equiv 2\Lambda+2c^{2}.

Remark 1
.

The estimates

\mathcal{Q}(\Sigma)^{2}\leq\frac{9}{4\Lambda}

and

\frac{2\pi}{\Lambda}\Big(3-\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}(\Sigma)^{2}}\Big)\leq|\Sigma|\leq\frac{2\pi}{\Lambda}\Big(3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}(\Sigma)^{2}}\Big)

are a straightforward application from inequality (
5
).

2.
Background

In this section, we recall some basic facts that will be essential in the proof of the main theorems. Initially,
let
(\mathcal{N},\bar{g})
be a Lorentzian
4
-manifold. Consider a spacelike hypersurface
M\subset\mathcal{N}
with unit timelike normal field
\xi
, induced metric
g
and second fundamental form
K
, so that
(M,g,K)
is an initial data. In our convention
K(X,Y)=\bar{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{X}\xi,Y)
, where
\overline{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection of
(\mathcal{N},\bar{g})
.

Let
\Sigma
be a closed surface embedded in
M
which is two-sided, i.e,
\Sigma
admits a globally defined unit normal
\nu
, unique up to sign. By convention, we refer to such a choice of
\nu
as outward pointing. Also, we denote by
h
the metric in
\Sigma
induced by
g
. We define the null outward and inward pointing vector fields of
\Sigma
by

\ell^{\pm}=\xi\pm\nu.

In what follows, consider the null second fundamental form of
\Sigma\subset M
with respect to
\ell^{\pm}
given by

\displaystyle\chi_{\pm}(X,Y):=\bar{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{X}\ell^{\pm},Y)=(K\pm A)(X,Y),

where
A
is the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
inside
(M,g)
. From this, the null expansions of
\Sigma
are given by

\theta_{\pm}=\operatorname{tr}_{h}\chi_{\pm}=\operatorname{tr}_{h}K\pm H,

where
H
is the mean curvature of
\Sigma
in
(M,g)
. The surface
\Sigma
is trapped if both
\theta_{+}<0
and
\theta_{-}<0
, outer trapped if
\theta_{+}<0
, and marginally outer trapped (MOTS) if
\theta_{+}=0
. A MOTS
\Sigma
is just a minimal surface in
M
when
M
is a time-symmetric, that is,
K=0
.

Let
\Sigma_{t}
denotes the one parameter family of surfaces created by deforming a MOTS
\Sigma
an amount
t\psi
in the outward normal direction, where
\psi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function. More precisely,

\Sigma_{t}=\{\exp_{x}(t\psi(x)\nu_{x});\,x\in\Sigma\},

where
\exp
is the exponential map of
(M,g)
. The stability of
\Sigma
is studied by investigating
the change of
\theta_{+}
along these deformations of
\Sigma
. A computation as in
[
1
]
shows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{+}}{\partial t}\bigg|_{t=0}=L\psi.

Here, the differential operator

(6)

L\psi=-\Delta_{h}\psi+2h(Z,\nabla_{h}\psi)+\left(\frac{\operatorname{Sc}_{h}}{2}-\operatorname{G}(\ell^{+},\xi)-\frac{|\chi_{+}|^{2}}{2}+\operatorname{div}_{h}Z-|Z|^{2}\right)\psi,

is the MOTS stability operator, where
\operatorname{Sc}_{h}
is the scalar curvature with respect to metric
g
,
Z
is the vector field on
\Sigma
metrically dual to the one-form
K(\nu,\cdot)
,
and
\operatorname{G}
is the Einstein tensor given by

\operatorname{G}=\Ric_{\bar{g}}-\frac{\operatorname{Sc}_{\bar{g}}}{2}\bar{g}.

The operator
L
is not self-adjoint in general and its principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(L)
(defined as the eigenvalue with smallest real part) is real. Moreover, the associated eigenfunction
\psi_{1}
,
L\psi_{1}=\lambda_{1}\psi_{1}
, is unique up to a scale, and it can be chosen to be strictly positive; see
[
1
]
and the references therein.
We remember that
\Sigma
is
stable
if
\lambda_{1}(L)\geq 0
and
\Sigma
is
unstable
if
\lambda_{1}(L)<0
.

Now, we consider the symmetrized operator
L_{s}:C^{\infty}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
given by

L_{s}\psi=-\Delta_{h}\psi+b\psi,

where
b=\Big(\frac{\operatorname{Sc}_{h}}{2}-G(l^{+},\xi)-\frac{|\chi_{+}|^{2}}{2}\Big)
. This operator is obtained by placing
Z=0
in (
6
). We define the principal eigenvalue of the operator
L_{s}
by

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(L_{s})=\inf_{\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla\psi|^{2}+b\psi^{2})d\mu}{\int_{\Sigma}\psi^{2}d\mu}.

The operator
L_{s}
is self-adjoint, its eigenvalues can be written in increasing order, and
\lambda_{1}(L)\geq\lambda_{1}(L_{s})
; see
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
13
]
and the references therein.

Next, we exhibit the notion of stability in the symmetrized sense.

Definition 5
.

A MOTS
\Sigma
is called

•

Stable in the symmetrized sense if
\lambda_{1}(L_{s})\geq 0
.

•

Unstable in the symmetrized sense if
\lambda_{1}(L_{s})<0
.

Recall that the
index
of
\Sigma
in the symmetrized sense is the number of (counted with multiplicity) negative eigenvalues of
L_{s}
. Also, we say that
\Sigma
is degenerate if one of the eigenvalues of
L_{s}
is equal to zero.

Proceeding, the Einstein-Maxwell equations for uncharged matter are

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sc}_{\bar{g}}-\frac{\operatorname{Sc}_{\bar{g}}}{2}\bar{g}+\Lambda\bar{g}

\displaystyle=8\pi(T_{F}+T),

\displaystyle dF

\displaystyle=0,\,\,\operatorname{div}_{\bar{g}}F=0.

Here
(\mathcal{N}^{4},\,\bar{g})
is a time-oriented spacetime,
F
is a
2
-form on
\mathcal{N}
,
T_{F}
is the energy-momentum tensor of the electromagnetic field given by

T_{F}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\Big(F\circ F-\frac{1}{4}|F|^{2}_{\bar{g}}\bar{g}\Big)

with
(F\circ F)_{ij}=\bar{g}^{kl}F_{ik}F_{jl}
, and
T
is a symmetric covariant
2
-tensor field on
\mathcal{N}
, which represents the energy-momentum tensor of the non-electromagnetic matter.

A Cauchy data for these equations (without a magnetic field) is a tuple

(M^{3},\,g,\,K,\,\mu,\,J),

where
(M^{3},\,g)
is an oriented Riemannian
3
-manifold in
\mathcal{N}
such that

\mu:=T(\xi,\xi),\,\,\,\,J:=T(.,\xi)

are the energy density and the momentum density of the non-electromagnetic fields, respectively. Moreover, the data satisfy the constraint equations

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sc}_{g}+(\operatorname{tr}_{g}K)^{2}-|K|_{g}^{2}-2\Lambda

\displaystyle=2|E|^{2}+16\pi\mu,

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}_{g}(K-(\operatorname{tr}_{g}K)_{g})

\displaystyle=8\pi J,

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}_{g}E

\displaystyle=0.

In this context, we say that it holds the dominant energy condition for the non-electromagnetic fields if
[
6
, Section 2]

T(v,\,v)\geq 0,

for all future directed causal vectors. As a consequence of this condition, we obtain
\mu\geq|J|_{g}

[
9
, Section 8.3.4]
. Thus, assuming
(M,\,g,\,K)
is maximal, that is,
\operatorname{tr}_{g}K=0
, we get

\operatorname{Sc}_{g}\geq 2\Lambda+2|E|^{2}.

As was mentioned in the introduction, this setting will be used in Theorem
4
.

3.
Proof of the Main Results

In light of the discussion done in the introduction, we assume Reissner-Nordström-de Sitter spacetime
(\mathcal{N},\,\mathfrak{g})
with the cosmological constant, the mass, and the charge satisfying

(7)

0<m^{2}<\frac{1}{18\Lambda}[1+12\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda+(1-4\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda)^{\frac{3}{2}}].

Also,
\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda\leq\frac{1}{4}
; see
[
3
,
5
]
for more details. We denote
r_{--}
,
r_{-}
,
r_{+}
,
r_{c}
;
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
,
r_{3}
and
\hat{r}_{1}
,
\hat{r}_{2}
to be the roots of

\displaystyle f(r)=-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{4}+r^{2}-2mr+\mathcal{Q}^{2}

f^{\prime}(r)
and
f^{\prime\prime}(r)
, respectively. The roots of
f(r),

f^{\prime}(r)
and
f^{\prime\prime}(r)
have the following behavior

r_{--}<r_{1}<\hat{r}_{1}<0<r_{-}<r_{2}<r_{+}<r_{3}<r_{c}

and
\hat{r}_{2}\in(r_{2},r_{3})
.
In fact, we already know that the roots of
f(r)
have the following behavior

r_{--}<0<r_{-}<r_{+}<r_{c}.

Consequently,

r_{--}<r_{1}<0<r_{-}<r_{2}<r_{+}<r_{3}<r_{c},

by using Vieta’s formulas and Rolle’s theorem.
Proceeding,

\hat{r}_{1}=-\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\Lambda}}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\hat{r}_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\Lambda}}.

Further, using Rolle’s theorem again

r_{--}<r_{1}<\hat{r}_{1}<0<r_{-}<r_{2}<r_{+}<r_{3}<r_{c}

and
\hat{r}_{2}\in(r_{2},r_{3}).
We also note that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rc}f^{\prime}(r)&\mbox{is strictly decreasing in}\,\,\,\Big(-\infty,-\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\Lambda}}\Big)\cup\Big(\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\Lambda}},+\infty\Big)\\
f^{\prime}(r)&\mbox{ is strictly increasing in}\,\,\,\Big(-\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\Lambda}},\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\Lambda}}\Big).\end{array}\right.

In equation (
2
), we saw that the metrics
h
and
g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
are conformal. Then

\Delta_{h}\psi=\frac{1}{r_{0}^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi

and

\operatorname{Sc}_{h}=\frac{2}{r_{0}^{2}}.

Since the Reissner-Nordström-de Sitter spacetime is stationary and satisfies the null energy condition, we have
\chi_{+}=0
. In addition,

\operatorname{G}(\ell^{+},\xi)=\Lambda+\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{r_{0}^{4}}.

From this, the symmetrized operator
L_{s}:C^{\infty}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
follows

\displaystyle L_{s}\psi=-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}_{r_{0}}^{2}}\psi+\Big(\frac{1}{r_{0}^{2}}-\Lambda-\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{r_{0}^{4}}\Big)\psi,

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r_{0}}}
stands for the Laplacian operator of the round sphere with radius
r_{0}
.

The previous analysis lead us to the prove our first result.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

To begin with,

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r_{0}}}=r_{0}^{-2}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{1}}

and

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r_{0}}}\psi=\bar{\lambda}\psi\Leftrightarrow r_{0}^{-2}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}_{1}^{2}}\psi=-\bar{\lambda}\psi\Leftrightarrow r_{0}^{-2}k(k+1)\psi=\bar{\lambda}\psi,

where
\hat{\lambda}_{k+1}=k(k+1)
is the spectrum of
-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}_{1}^{2}}
and
\bar{\lambda}_{k+1}=r_{0}^{-2}k(k+1)
is the spectrum of
-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r_{0}}}
. From this we have

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r_{0}}}\psi+q\psi=\lambda\psi\Leftrightarrow-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r_{0}}}\psi=(\lambda-q)\psi=\bar{\lambda}\psi

and the spectrum of
L_{s}(0)
is given by

(9)

\lambda_{k+1}(L_{s}(0))=r_{0}^{-2}k(k+1)+\frac{1}{r_{0}^{2}}-\Lambda-\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{r_{0}^{4}}.

Proceeding, we get

\lambda_{1}(L_{s}(0))=\frac{1}{r_{0}^{4}}(-\Lambda r_{0}^{4}+r_{0}^{2}-\mathcal{Q}^{2}).

Taking
x=r_{0}^{2}
, we obtain

u(x)=-\Lambda x^{2}+x-\mathcal{Q}^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rc}u(x)<0&\mbox{if}\,\,\,x\in\Big(0,\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)\cup\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda},+\infty\Big)\\
u(x)>0&\mbox{ if}\,\,\,x\in\Big(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda},\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big).\end{array}\right.

From this

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rc}\lambda_{1}(L_{s}(0))<0&\mbox{if}\,\,\,r_{0}\in\Big(0,\Big(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\cup\Big(\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},+\infty\Big)\\
\lambda_{1}(L_{s}(0))>0&\mbox{ if}\,\,\,r_{0}\in\Big(\Big(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big).\end{array}\right.

We know that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}\Big(\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)=2\Big[\frac{1}{3}\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}(2-\sqrt{1-4\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda})-m\Big].

Using (
7
) and the fact that

\frac{1}{18\Lambda}[1+12\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda+(1-4\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda)^{\frac{3}{2}}]=\Big[\frac{1}{3}\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}(2-\sqrt{1-4\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda})\Big]^{2},

we achieve

\displaystyle f^{\prime}\Big(\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)>0.

Since

r_{3},\,\,\,\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\,\,\,r_{c}\in\Big(\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\Lambda}},+\infty\Big)

with
r_{3}<r_{c}
, and
f^{\prime}(r)
is strictly decreasing in
\Big(\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\Lambda}},+\infty\Big)
. Then,

\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<r_{3}<r_{c}.

Consequently,

\lambda_{1}(L_{s}(0))=\frac{1}{r_{0}^{4}}(-\Lambda r_{0}^{4}+r_{0}^{2}-\mathcal{Q}^{2})<0

when
r_{0}=r_{c}
. Therefore, by the perturbation theory for the eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}(L_{s}(a))<0

for a small
a>0
; see
[
11
]
.

At the same time, we deduce that

\lambda_{2}(L_{s}(0))=\frac{1}{r_{0}^{4}}(-\Lambda r_{0}^{4}+3r_{0}^{2}-\mathcal{Q}^{2})

from (
9
). If we assume
x=r_{0}^{2}
, we get

v(x)=-\Lambda x^{2}+3x-\mathcal{Q}^{2}.

So,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rc}v(x)<0&\mbox{if}\,\,\,x\in\Big(0,\frac{3-\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)\cup\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda},+\infty\Big)\\
v(x)>0&\mbox{ if}\,\,\,x\in\Big(\frac{3-\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda},\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big),\end{array}\right.

and we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rc}\lambda_{2}(L_{s}(0))<0&\mbox{if}\,\,\,r_{0}\in\Big(0,\Big(\frac{3-\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\cup\Big(\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},+\infty\Big)\\
\lambda_{2}(L_{s}(0))>0&\mbox{ if}\,\,\,r_{0}\in\Big(\Big(\frac{3-\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big).\end{array}\right.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle f\Big(\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}})(5-\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}})+12\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{24\Lambda}\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2m\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using that

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{1+12\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}+(1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}{18\Lambda}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{(1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}})(5-\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}})+12\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{24\Lambda}\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

and (
7
), we have

f\Big(\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)>0.

Also,

\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\mathcal{Q}^{2}\Lambda}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\Big(\Big(\frac{3-\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

In another direction,

\displaystyle f\Big(\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{2}\Big[1-\frac{\Lambda}{3}\Big(\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2m\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{Q}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{3}-2m\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This combined with (
3
) gives

f\Big(\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)<0.

From the above discussion, there exist a root of
f(r)
in

I:=\Big(\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

Since

r_{2}<r_{+}<r_{3}<r_{c},

\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<r_{3}<r_{c},

and
\hat{r}_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\Lambda}}\in(r_{2},r_{3})
. This implies
r_{+}\in I
or
r_{c}\in I
or
r_{+},r_{c}\in I
. If only
r_{+}\in I
, we have at least two roots bigger than
r_{+}
for
f(r)
. In fact, taking into account that

f\Big(\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)<0,

so there is
\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\bar{r}
such that
f(\bar{r})>0
, otherwise
r_{c}
will be a local maximum point and
f^{\prime}(r_{c})=0
which leads to a contradiction with the fact that the roots of
f(r)
and
f^{\prime}(r)
are distinct. Further,

\lim\limits_{r\to{+\infty}}f(r)=-\infty.

Thereby, there is one root in

\Big(\Big(\frac{3+\sqrt{9-4\Lambda\mathcal{Q}^{2}}}{2\Lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\bar{r}\Big)

and another root in
(\bar{r},+\infty)
which leads to a contradiction with the fact that
f(r)
has four roots and its biggest root is
r_{c}
. Thus,
r_{c}\in I
and

\lambda_{2}(L_{s}(0))=\frac{1}{r_{0}^{4}}(-\Lambda r_{0}^{4}+3r_{0}^{2}-\mathcal{Q}^{2})>0

when
r_{0}=r_{c}
. Using again the perturbation theory for the eigenvalues, we arrive

\lambda_{2}(L_{s}(a))>0

for a small
a>0
. Then, we conclude that

\lambda_{1}(L_{s}(a))<0<\lambda_{2}(L_{s}(a))

and the index of
\Sigma
is one.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4
.

The proof consists in using the Hersch’s trick. Let
\psi_{1}
be the first eigenfunction of the symmetrized operator
given by

L_{s}=-\Delta_{h}+\frac{\operatorname{Sc}_{h}}{2}-G(l^{+},\xi)-\frac{|\chi_{+}|^{2}}{2},

which we can choose to be positive. Let
\Phi
be a conformal map from
\Sigma
to
\mathbb{S}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. So, we can take the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(\Psi\circ\Phi)\psi_{1}\,d\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

where
\Psi
is a Möbius transformation of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Using the fact that
\psi_{1}
is positive, we can choose
\Psi
in such a way that the previous integral vanishes; see
[
12
]
.

The coordinate
(\Psi\circ\Phi)_{i}=\tilde{\psi}_{i}
of
(\Psi\circ\Phi)
is orthogonal to
\psi_{1}
. Since
\Sigma
has index one, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\Big(|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\psi_{i}}|^{2}+\Big(\frac{\operatorname{Sc}_{h}}{2}-G(l^{+},\xi)-\frac{|\chi_{+}|^{2}}{2}\Big)\tilde{\psi}_{i}^{2}\Big)d\mu\geq 0,\,\,i=1,2,3.

Summing these three inequalities, we get

(15)

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Sigma}\Big(|\nabla_{h}(\Psi\circ\Phi)|^{2}+\Big(\frac{\operatorname{Sc}_{h}}{2}-G(l^{+},\xi)-\frac{|\chi_{+}|^{2}}{2}\Big)\Big)d\mu.

By
[
14
]
, we have

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{h}(\Psi\circ\Phi)|^{2}d\mu=8\pi deg(\Psi\circ\Phi)

and

G(l^{+},\xi)\geq\Lambda+|E|^{2},

where we use

\operatorname{Sc}_{g}\geq 2\Lambda+2|E|^{2}

together with Einstein equations.

The estimate (
15
) becomes

(16)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8\pi deg(\Psi\circ\Phi)+\int_{\Sigma}\Big(\frac{\operatorname{Sc}_{h}}{2}-\Lambda-|E|^{2}\Big)d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\pi deg(\Phi)+4\pi-\Lambda|\Sigma|-\int_{\Sigma}|E|^{2}d\mu,

where we use the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem and
g(\Sigma)=0
to obtain (
16
).

By
[
15
]
, we can choose
\Phi
such that

deg(\Phi)\leq 1+\Big[\frac{g(\Sigma)+1}{2}\Big]

and using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in (
16
), we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 12\pi-\Lambda|\Sigma|-\int_{\Sigma}\langle E,\nu\rangle^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 12\pi-\Lambda|\Sigma|-\frac{1}{|\Sigma|}\Big(\int_{\Sigma}\langle E,\nu\rangle d\mu\Big)^{2},

where we used the Hölder’s inequality in the previous estimate. Also, taking into account (
4
),
we achieve

0\leq 12\pi-\Lambda|\Sigma|-\frac{16\pi^{2}\mathcal{Q}(\Sigma)^{2}}{|\Sigma|}.

If equality holds in the above estimate, then we have equality in all previous inequalities. Hence,

\chi_{+}\equiv 0.

At the same time, the equality on the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies
|E|^{2}=\langle E,\nu\rangle^{2}
and
E=c\nu
for some function
c=\langle E,\nu\rangle
. In another direction, the equality on Hölder’s estimate gives us that
\langle E,\nu\rangle
is constant. So,

(\operatorname{Sc}_{g})_{\Sigma}\equiv 2\Lambda+2c^{2}.

∎

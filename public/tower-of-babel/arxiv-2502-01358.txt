Diffusion at Absolute Zero: Langevin Sampling Using Successive Moreau Envelopes
Andreas Habring, Alexander Falk, Thomas Pock

Abstract
In this article we propose a novel method for sampling from Gibbs distributions of the form π ⁡ ( x ) ∝ exp ⁡ ( − U ⁡ ( x ) ) \pi(x)\propto\exp(-U(x)) with a potential U ⁡ ( x ) U(x) . In particular, inspired by diffusion models we propose to consider a sequence ( π t k ) k (\pi^{t_{k}})_{k} of approximations of the target density, for which π t k ≈ π \pi^{t_{k}}\approx\pi for k k small and, on the other hand, π t k \pi^{t_{k}} exhibits favorable properties for sampling for k k large. This sequence is obtained by replacing parts of the potential U U by its Moreau envelopes. Sampling is performed in an Annealed Langevin type procedure, that is, sequentially sampling from π t k \pi^{t_{k}} for decreasing k k , effectively guiding the samples from a simple starting density to the more complex target. In addition to a theoretical analysis we show experimental results supporting the efficacy of the method in terms of increased convergence speed and applicability to multi-modal densities π \pi .

This article is concerned with Langevin sampling from distributions of the form

I-A

Denoising Score Matching and Annealed Langevin

A particular inspiration for the present article has been denoising score matching (DSM) and Annealed Langevin sampling
[
2
]
, which can be classified under the umbrella term
diffusion models

[
1
]
. In particular, in
variance exploding
DSM one considers a sequence of perturbations of the target density
\pi
defined as convolutions with centered Gaussians with different noise levels
0<t_{0}<\dots<t_{n}
,
\pi_{t_{k}}=\pi*\mathcal{N}(0,\sqrt{t_{k}})
. Thus, samples from
\pi_{t_{k}}(x)
can be obtained from samples from
\pi(x)
by adding a Gaussian random variable. For large
t_{k}
, the distribution
\pi_{t_{k}}
becomes progressively well-behaved, namely smooth, everywhere non-zero, and increasingly
Gaussian
. Therefore, the distributions
\pi_{t_{k}}(x)
(respectively, their scores
\nabla\log\pi_{t_{k}}(x)
) are more stable to train and easier to sample from rather than
\pi
(for a detailed explanation, see
[
2
]
). In order to sample from
\pi(x)
, these properties can be exploited by sequentially sampling from
\pi_{t_{k}}
for
k=n,n-1\dots 0
using Langevin sampling for each noise level. This technique is coined
Annealed Langevin
sampling. Figuratively speaking, the sampling procedure starts at a well-behaved distribution and is guided towards an increasingly complex one.

II
Diffusion at Absolute Zero

For a function
G:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the Moreau envelope with parameter
t>0

M_{G}^{t}(x)\coloneqq\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(y)+\frac{1}{2t}\|x-y\|^{2}.

(4)

Moreover, we define the proximal mapping, or simply

4

1

(x,t)

as

which justifies using it as an approximation of

[
19

. Moreover a crucial property of the Moreau envelope is that
\nabla M_{G}^{t}(x)\in\frac{1}{t}(x-{\prox}_{tG}(x))
, at every
x
where
M_{G}^{t}
is differentiable
[
19
, 10.32 Example]
, which yields a practical way of computing the gradient of
M^{t}_{G}
by solving an optimization problem, at least if the prox is unique.

In many applications (cf.
[
9
]
) the potential
U=F+G
can be split

F\equiv 0
and

.

II-A

Consistency of Diffusion at Absolute Zero

The aim of this section is to show that the proposed sampler DAZ is, in fact, consistent in the sense that the sequence of distributions
(\pi^{t})_{t}
is well-defined, Lipschitz continuous, and approximates the target
\pi
as
t\rightarrow 0
. In order to analyze the method theoretically, from now on we impose the following assumptions on
F
and
G
.

Assumption 1
.

1.

F
and
G
are bounded from below and such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp(-U(x))dx<\infty
.

2.

G
is proper, continuous, coercive, and there exists
t_{max}>0
with
{\prox}_{tG}(x)
being single-valued for any
x
and
t<t_{max}
.

3.

There exists
K>0
such that for
\|x\|\geq K
,
\|{\prox}_{tG}(x)\|\leq\|x\|
for any
t\geq 0
.

4.

G
admits a regular subgradient
\partial G
(
[
19
, Definition 8.3]
) and the function
\phi(s)\coloneqq\sup\{\|p\|\;|\;\|x\|\leq s,p\in\partial G(x)\}
satisfies

\int\phi(\|x\|)^{2}e^{-U^{t_{max}}(x)}dx<\infty.

Lemma 1
.

The mapping
t\mapsto{\prox}_{tG}(x)
is continuous on
(0,t_{max})
for any fixed
x
.

Proof.

Let
p_{\tau}={\prox}_{\tau G}(x)
and
t_{n}\rightarrow t\in(0,t_{max})
. By definition of the proximal mapping and boundedness from below of
G
,
(p_{t_{n}})_{n}
is bounded. Therefore, there exists a (not-relabeled) convergent subsequence
p_{t_{n}}\rightarrow\hat{p}
. It holds for arbitrary
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\frac{1}{2t_{n}}\|x-p_{t_{n}}\|^{2}+G(p_{t_{n}})\leq\frac{1}{2t_{n}}\|x-y\|^{2}+G(y).

Taking the limit and noting that
G
is continuous shows that
\hat{p}=p_{t}
by uniqueness of the prox (
1
). Convergence of the original sequence
(p_{t_{n}})_{n}
to
\hat{p}
follows by a standard subsequence argument and the fact that
{\prox}_{\tau G}(x)
is unique.
∎

Lemma 2
.

The function
\tau\mapsto M_{G}^{\tau}(x)
is differentiable on
(0,t_{max})
for any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Denote let
0<s<t<t_{max}
. By definition of the Moreau envelope we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2t}\|x-p_{t}\|^{2}+G(p_{t})-\frac{1}{2s}\|x-p_{t}\|^{2}-G(p_{t})

(5)

\displaystyle\leq M_{G}^{t}(x)-M_{G}^{s}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2t}\|x-p_{s}\|^{2}+G(p_{s})-\frac{1}{2s}\|x-p_{s}\|^{2}-G(p_{s}).

It follows

-\frac{1}{2st}\|x-p_{t}\|^{2}\leq\frac{M_{G}^{t}(x)-M_{G}^{s}(x)}{t-s}\leq-\frac{1}{2st}\|x-p_{s}\|^{2}.

Letting
|s-t|\rightarrow 0
, by
Lemma

1
the result follows and, in particular, we obtain
\partial_{t}M_{G}^{t}(x)=-\frac{1}{2t^{2}}\|x-p_{t}\|^{2}
.
∎

Remark 1
.

This shows – without assuming Lipschitz continuity of
G

[

satisfies the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\partial_{t}M_{G}^{t}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla M_{G}^{t}(x)\|^{2}=0.

.

The function
\tau\rightarrow M_{G}^{\tau}(x)
is Lipschitz continuous on
(0,t_{max})
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, more precisely, there exists
C>0
such that for
0<s,t<t_{max}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

|M_{G}^{t}(x)-M_{G}^{s}(x)|\leq\frac{|s-t|}{2}(\phi(\|x\|)+C)^{2}.

(6)

Proof.

Using
Lemma

2
we find for
0<s<t

\displaystyle|M_{G}^{t}(x)-M_{G}^{s}(x)|\leq\int_{s}^{t}|\partial_{\tau}M_{G}^{\tau}(x)|\;d\tau

(7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\|x-p_{\tau}\|^{2}\;d\tau.

By definition of the Moreau envelope
\frac{1}{\tau}(x-p_{\tau})\in\partial G(p_{\tau})

K>0
, such that for
\|x\|\geq K

\|p_{\tau}\|

The map

The curve

II-B

Relation to Denoising Score Matching

In order to relate the proposed DAZ to DSM, we define the diffusion potential with temperature parameter
T
,
G^{t}_{T}(x)
as

G^{t}_{T}(x)=-T\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(\frac{-G(y)-\frac{1}{2t}\|x-y\|^{2}}{T}\right)dy\right)

(8)

which corresponds to a soft-minimum instead of the strict minimum in (
4

8

\mathcal{N}(0,tT)

(

) is precisely the potential for variance exploding diffusion
[
1
]
with target distribution

. Thus, the proposed method can be understood as the zero temperature limit of diffusion with potential
G^{t}_{T}
:

Lemma 3
.

DAZ is obtained as the zero-temperature limit of regular diffusion. That is,
G^{t}_{T}(x)\rightarrow M_{G}^{t}(x)
, as
T\rightarrow 0^{+}
for any
x
and, if
\nabla G
is locally Lipschitz continuous, the convergence is uniformly on compact sets.

Proof.

The result is an extension of the convergence
\|\;\cdot\;\|_{p}\rightarrow\|\;\cdot\;\|_{\infty}
as
p\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

Numerical Experiments

Since verifying convergence of sampling algorithms is challenging in high dimensions we begin with two small-scale examples for one-dimensional distributions for which error metrics on the space of probability distributions can be estimated with high accuracy.

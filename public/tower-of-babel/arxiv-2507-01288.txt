Existence of wave operators for Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation in two space dimensions
Jun-ichi Segata

Abstract
. In this paper, we study long time behavior of solution to the two dimensional Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation in the framework of the final state problem. We construct a small global solution to the Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation which scatters to a given free solution. From this result, we have the existence of wave operators for the Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation. The proof is based on the space-time resonance method developed by Gustafson-Nakanishi-Tsai [ 10 ] and Germain-Masmoudi-Shatah [ 6 , 7 ] etc.

Introduction

In this paper, we study long time behavior of solution to
the Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation in two dimensions:

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u+\partial_{x}\partial_{y}^{2}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2}),\qquad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},

where

u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is an unknown
function.
Equation (
1.1
) in the three dimensional case was
derived by Zakharov-Kuznetsov
[
22
]
to describe
unidirectional wave propagation in a magnetized plasma.
Equation (
1.1
) in the two dimensional case was
derived by Laedke-Spatschek
[
15
]
from the basic
hydrodynamical equations.
Lannes-Linares-Saut
[
16
]
gave the rigorous justification
of (
1.1
) from the Euler-Poisson system for a uniformly
magnetized media.

Equation (
1.1
) has the conservation of mass

(1.2)

\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}=\|u(0)\|_{L_{x}^{2}},

and the conservation of energy

(1.3)

\displaystyle E[u](t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\{(\partial_{x}u)^{2}+(\partial_{y}u)^{2}\right\}dxdy-\frac{1}{6}\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u^{3}dxdy=E[u](0).

The main purpose of this paper is to study the asymptotic behavior
in time of solution to (
1.1
). We first review the related results on the well-posedness and
scattering.
For the three dimensional case, Linares-Saut
[
18
]

proved the time local well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

with
s>9/8
. Ribaud-Vento
[
21
]

has shown the local well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

with
s>1
by using the local smoothing effect and the maximal
function estimates for the solution to the linearized equation of (
1.1
).
Recently, Herr-Kinoshita
[
12
]
proved the local well-posedness
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with full scaling sub-critical range

s>-1/2
by the Fourier restriction norm method
(see also
[
12
]
for the well-posedness results of (
1.1
)
for the high dimensions).
For the two dimensional case, Faminskii
[
4
]

proved the global well-posedness in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by
applying the contraction mapping principle for the corresponding
integral equation with an aid of the local smoothing effect
for the linearized equation.
Grünrock-Herr
[
9
]
and Molinet-Pilod
[
19
]

independently extended this result to
s>1/2

by using the Fourier restriction norm method.
Furthermore, Kinoshita
[
14
]
proved the local
well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
s>-1/4

by refining the Fourier restriction norm method.
Especially, from his result and the conservation of mass
(
1.2
), we have the global well-posedness of
(
1.1
) in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

To summarize the related results on the scattering, we
consider the generalized Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation

(1.4)

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x_{1}}\Delta u=\partial_{x_{1}}(u^{k}),\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d
,
k\geq 2
are integers,
and
\Delta
is the Laplacian with respect to
x
.
For the high dimensional case, Herr-Kinoshita
[
11
]

proved the small data scattering
for (
1.4
) with
d\geq 5
and
k=2
in the scaling critical Sobolev space.
Furthermore, they proved the scattering for (
1.4
) with
d=4
and
k=2

when the initial data is small and radial in the last
(d-1)
variables.
For the two dimensional case, Farah-Linares-Pastor
[
5
]

have shown the small data scattering for (
1.4
) with
d=2

and
k\geq 4
. Very recently, Anjolras
[
1
]
proved
the small data scattering for the two dimensional
modified Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation
(i.e., (
1.4
) with
d=2
and
k=3
)
by using the space-time resonance method developed by
Gustafson-Nakanishi-Tsai
[
10
]
and
Germain-Masmoudi-Shatah
[
6
,
7
]
etc.
Furthermore, by using the Fourier restriction norm method,
Kinoshita-Correia
1
1

1

work in preparation.
obtained
the small data scattering for the two dimensional
modified Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation under the situation that
the initial and the asymptotic states belong to the same class.

To study the scattering problem for (
1.1
) in two dimensions
is more difficult compared to (
1.4
) with
k>1+2/d
due
to slow decay of the solution to the linearized equation
and low power of the nonlinearity. Furthermore,
from the point of view of the linear scattering theory,
for the two dimensional case, the quadratic
nonlinearity in (
1.1
) belongs to borderline between
the short and long range cases. Therefore it is non-trivial
to obtain long time behavior of solution to (
1.1
)
in two space dimensions.
In this paper, we consider the scattering problem for
(
1.1
) in the framework of the final state problem.
More precisely, we prove the existence of small global
solutions to (
1.1
) which scatters to a given free solution.

Following
[
2
,
9
]
, to study (
1.1
)
we first reduce (
1.1
) into the equation which is
symmetric with respect to
x
and
y
. Let

x^{\prime}:=2^{-2/3}x+2^{-2/3}\cdot 3^{1/2}y
,
y^{\prime}:=2^{-2/3}x-2^{-2/3}\cdot 3^{1/2}y

and let
v(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}):=2^{-2/3}u(x,y)
. By a simple calculation, we see that
if
u
is a solution to (
1.1
), then

v
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+(\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{3}+\partial_{y^{\prime}}^{3})v=(\partial_{x^{\prime}}+\partial_{y^{\prime}})(v^{2}).

Henceforth, we consider the scattering
problem for

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+(\partial_{x_{1}}^{3}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{3})v=(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})(v^{2}),\qquad(t,x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

To state the our main theorem, we introduce
several notation. We define the norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}

by

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle\partial_{x_{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{W_{x}^{3,1}}+\|\langle x\rangle\partial_{x_{1}}^{-1}f\|_{H_{x}^{3}}+\|\langle x\rangle\partial_{x_{2}}^{-1}f\|_{H_{x}^{3}}

\displaystyle+\|\langle x\rangle\partial_{x_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{-2}f\|_{H_{x}^{3}}+\|\langle x\rangle\partial_{x_{1}}^{-2}\partial_{x_{2}}f\|_{H_{x}^{3}}+\|\partial_{x_{1}}^{-2}f\|_{H_{x}^{3}}+\|\partial_{x_{2}}^{-2}f\|_{H_{x}^{3}},

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
and
\langle x\rangle=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}
.
Let
V(t):=e^{-t(\partial_{x_{1}}^{3}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{3})}

be a unitary group generated by

-\partial_{x_{1}}^{3}-\partial_{x_{2}}^{3}
.
Then we have the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that if

v_{\infty}
satisfies
\|v_{\infty}\|_{X}\leq\varepsilon
,
then there exists a unique global solution

v\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
to (
1.5
) satisfying

(1.7)

\displaystyle\|v(t)-V(t)v_{\infty}\|_{H_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon t^{-\alpha}

for any
t>0
, where
\alpha>2/3
.
Similar result holds for
t<0
.

From Theorem
1.1
, we have that for sufficiently small

\varepsilon>0

the wave operator
B_{\varepsilon}\ni v_{\infty}\mapsto v(0)\in H_{x}^{1}

is well-defined, where
B_{\varepsilon}=\{f\in X\ ;\ \|f\|_{X}\leq\varepsilon\}
.

We now give the outline of the proof of
Theorem
1.1
.
For given final data
v_{\infty}
,
we introduce a new unknown function

\displaystyle w(t,x):=v(t,x)-v_{1}(t,x)-v_{2}(t,x),

where
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are given by

(1.8)

\displaystyle v_{1}(t,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[V(t)v_{\infty}](x),

(1.9)

\displaystyle v_{2}(t,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})\int_{t}^{\infty}V(t-\tau)[v_{1}(\tau)^{2}]d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})\int_{t}^{\infty}V(t-\tau)[(V(\tau)v_{\infty})^{2}]d\tau.

Let us derive the evolution equation for
w
.
Let
{{\mathcal{L}}}=\partial_{t}+\partial_{x_{1}}^{3}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{3}
.
Since
{{\mathcal{L}}}v_{1}=0
and

{{\mathcal{L}}}v_{2}=(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})(v_{1}^{2})
,
we have

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{L}}}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{L}}}v-{{\mathcal{L}}}v_{1}-{{\mathcal{L}}}v_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{L}}}v-(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})(v_{1}^{2}).

If
v
satisfies (
1.5
), then

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{L}}}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})(v^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})\{(w+v_{1}+v_{2})^{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})\{w^{2}+2(v_{1}+v_{2})w+v_{1}^{2}+2v_{1}v_{2}+v_{2}^{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})(w^{2})+2(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})\{(v_{1}+v_{2})w\}

\displaystyle+(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})(v_{1}^{2}+2v_{1}v_{2}+v_{2}^{2}).

Hence we see that
w
satisfies

(1.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w+(\partial_{x_{1}}^{3}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{3})w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})(w^{2})+2(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})\left\{(v_{1}+v_{2})w\right\}

\displaystyle+(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})(2v_{1}v_{2}+v_{2}^{2}).

To show Theorem
1.1
, we prove the existence
of solution
w
to (
1.10
) with
w\to 0
in
H^{2}

as
t\to\infty
. We first note that

w
and
v_{1}
can be easily estimated by
the energy and Strichartz estimates
(see Lemma
2.1
, below).
The main difficulty to prove Theorem
1.1

lies on estimate for
v_{2}
. More precisely, we need

L^{2}
estimate for the bilinear oscillatory integral

(1.11)

\displaystyle v_{2}=-(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})\int_{t}^{\infty}V(t-\tau)\left[(V(\tau)v_{\infty})^{2}\right]d\tau.

Since the solution to the linearized equation of (
1.5
)
decays like
O(t^{-1})
in
L^{\infty}
, the Hölder
inequality only yields that integrand of (
1.11
) is

O(t^{-1})
in
L^{2}
which is insufficient because this is not
integrable in time variable. To derive
L^{2}

estimates for (
1.11
), we employ so called
space-time resonance method which is developed by

[
6
,
7
,
10
]
etc.
More precisely, to evaluate (
1.11
), we crucially use
the “null structure” of the nonlinear term
which can be represented as the algebraic identity
(
3.18
) below, and
integration by parts both in space and time variables.
In this step, we use the boundedness theorem by Coifman-Meyer

[
3
]
for a bilinear multiplier operator.
Once we obtain
L^{2}
estimate for
v_{2}
,
we have an existence of solution to (
1.10
)
by the compactness argument.

Note that the space-time resonance method
is now widely used to study usual/modified scattering
for various nonlinear hyperbolic and dispersive PDEs in the framework of
the initial value problem, see
[
1
,
6
,
7
,
10
]

for instance. In this paper we use this approach
to study the scattering problem of (
1.1
)
in the framework of the final state problem.
We believe that for the small initial data, the
corresponding solution to (
1.1
) scatters
to the free solution. Proving this fact is an interesting open question.

We introduce several notations and function spaces
which are used throughout this paper.
For
f\in{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\hat{f}(\xi)

denotes the Fourier transform of
f
. Let

\langle\xi\rangle=\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}+1}
. The differential operator

\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}=(1-\Delta)^{s/2}
denotes
the Bessel potential of order

.
For

is defined
as follows:

\displaystyle L^{q}(t,\infty;L_{x}^{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{u\in{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{1+2});\|u\|_{L^{q}(t,\infty;L_{x}^{r})}<\infty\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(t,\infty;L_{x}^{r})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\|u(\tau)\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\right\|_{L_{\tau}^{q}(t,\infty)}.

We will use the inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces

\displaystyle W^{s,p}=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2});\|f\|_{W^{s,p}}=\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}f\|_{L^{p}}<\infty\},

where
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
We denote
H^{s}=W^{s,2}
.
We denote
A\lesssim B
if there exists a constant
C>0

such that
A\leq CB
holds and
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B\lesssim A
.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we
give the decay and Strichartz estimates for the

). In Section 3, we derive

Linear Dispersive Estimates

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}w+(\partial_{x_{1}}^{3}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{3})w=0,\qquad\quad(t,x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

(i) By the unitary property of
V(t)
, we have

(2.5)

\|V(t)f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}=\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

On the other hand,

(2.6)

\displaystyle[V(t)f](x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix_{1}\xi_{1}+ix_{2}\xi_{2}+it(\xi_{1}^{3}+\xi_{2}^{3})}\widehat{f}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K(t,x_{1}-x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}-x_{2}^{\prime})f(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})dx_{1}^{\prime}dx_{2}^{\prime},

where

\displaystyle K(t,x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix_{1}\xi_{1}+ix_{2}\xi_{2}+it(\xi_{1}^{3}+\xi_{2}^{3})}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}.

Note

\displaystyle K(t,x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix_{1}\xi_{1}+it\xi_{1}^{3}}d\xi_{1}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix_{2}\xi_{2}+it\xi_{2}^{3}}d\xi_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}A(t,x_{1})A(t,x_{2}),

where the function

is given by

\displaystyle A(t,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{iy\xi+it\xi^{3}}d\xi=t^{-\frac{1}{3}}Ai(t^{-\frac{1}{3}}y),\qquad t,y\in\mathbb{R},

and

.
By the well-known property of the Airy function

(see

for instance), we have

\displaystyle|K(t,x_{1},x_{2})|\lesssim t^{-\frac{2}{3}}.

\displaystyle\|V(t)f\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

we have

Substituting this into (

), we have

\displaystyle\||\partial_{x_{1}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\partial_{x_{2}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}V(t)f\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-1}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{1}}.

By the interpolation between (
2.5
) and (

we have (
2.3
).

(ii) The inequality (

3.
Bilinear Dispersive Estimates

In this section we derive
L^{2}
estimate for
v_{2}

defined by (
1.9
) which is key to prove
Theorem
1.1
. We show the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
t>0
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\|(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})\int_{t}^{\infty}V(t-\tau)\left[(V(\tau)f)(V(\tau)g)\right]d\tau\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-1}\|f\|_{Z}\|g\|_{Z},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle\partial_{x_{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{L_{x}^{1}}+\|\langle x\rangle\partial_{x_{1}}^{-1}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\langle x\rangle\partial_{x_{2}}^{-1}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\langle x\rangle\partial_{x_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{-2}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\langle x\rangle\partial_{x_{1}}^{-2}\partial_{x_{2}}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial_{x_{1}}^{-2}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial_{x_{2}}^{-2}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

To show Lemma

Coifman-Meyer theorem
[
3
]
for the multilinear
multiplier operators. The following is a bilinear version
of the Coifman-Meyer theorem.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
m(\zeta,\sigma)
be a
C^{\infty}
function on

(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\backslash\{(0,0)\}
,
and
T_{m}
be a bilinear multiplier operator defined by

\displaystyle T_{m}[f,g](x)=(2\pi)^{-2d}\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix(\zeta+\sigma)}m(\zeta,\sigma)\widehat{f}(\zeta)\widehat{g}(\sigma)d\zeta d\sigma.

Assume that
m
satisfies the Hörmander-Mihlin condition:
for any multi-indices
\beta\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{+}^{2d}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle|\partial_{\zeta,\sigma}^{\beta}m(\zeta,\sigma)|\leq C_{\beta}(|\zeta|^{2}+|\sigma|^{2})^{-\frac{|\beta|}{2}}.

Then for any
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
1<r<1

satisfying
1/r=1/p+1/q
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|T_{m}[f,g]\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\|g\|_{L_{x}^{q}}.

Remark 1
.

By the Plancherel theorem and change of variables

\zeta=\xi-\eta
,
\sigma=\eta
,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}m(\xi,\eta)\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{g}(\eta)d\eta\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|{{\mathcal{F}}}^{-1}_{\xi\mapsto x}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}m(\xi,\eta)\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{g}(\eta)d\eta\right]\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\xi}m(\xi,\eta)\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{g}(\eta)d\xi d\eta\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix(\zeta+\sigma)}m(\zeta+\sigma,\sigma)\widehat{f}(\zeta)\widehat{g}(\sigma)d\zeta d\sigma\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

Hence if
\widetilde{m}(\zeta,\sigma):=m(\zeta+\sigma,\sigma)

satisfies the Hörmander-Mihlin condition (

),
then we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}m(\xi,\eta)\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{g}(\eta)d\eta\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\|g\|_{L_{x}^{q}},

where

and

.
In the proof of Lemma

Proof of Lemma

Classical energy method implies

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{+\infty}(\|w(\tau)\|_{W_{x}^{1,\infty}}+\|v_{1}(\tau)\|_{W_{x}^{3,\infty}}+\|v_{2}(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{3}})\|w(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{+\infty}(\|v_{1}(\tau)\|_{W_{x}^{3,\infty}}\|v_{2}(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{3}}+\|v_{2}(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{3}}^{2})\|w(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}d\tau.

By the Hölder inequality and Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|w(\tau)\|_{L_{\tau}^{3}(t,\infty;W_{x}^{1,\infty})}\|w(\tau)\|_{L_{\tau}^{3}(t,\infty;H_{x}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{t}^{+\infty}\tau^{-1}\|w(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}^{2}d\tau+\varepsilon^{3}\int_{t}^{+\infty}\tau^{-2}\|w(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-3\alpha+\frac{2}{3}}\left(\sup_{t\in[T,\infty)}t^{\alpha}\|w(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(\sup_{t\in[T,\infty)}t^{\alpha}\|w(\tau)\|_{L_{\tau}^{3}(t,\infty;W_{x}^{1,\infty})}\right)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon t^{-2\alpha}\left(\sup_{t\in[T,\infty)}t^{\alpha}\|w(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}\right)^{2}+\varepsilon^{3}t^{-\alpha-1}\left(\sup_{t\in[T,\infty)}t^{\alpha}\|w(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-3\alpha+\frac{2}{3}}\|w\|_{X_{T}}^{3}+\varepsilon t^{-2\alpha}\|w\|_{X_{T}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{3}t^{-\alpha-1}\|w\|_{X_{T}},

where the implicit constants are independent of
\lambda
and
\nu
.
Hence

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[T,\infty)}t^{\alpha}\|w(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}\lesssim T^{-\alpha+\frac{2}{3}}\|w\|_{X_{T}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|w\|_{X_{T}}+\varepsilon^{3}T^{\alpha-1}.

Next we derive the estimates for
w

in
L^{3}(t,\infty;W_{x}^{1,\infty})
.
Since
w
satisfies

\displaystyle w(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})\int_{t}^{\infty}V(t-\tau)\left[w(\tau)^{2}\right]d\tau

\displaystyle-2(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})\int_{t}^{\infty}V(t-\tau)\left[(v_{1}(\tau)+v_{2}(\tau))w(\tau)\right]d\tau

\displaystyle-(\partial_{x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}})\int_{t}^{\infty}V(t-\tau)\left[2v_{1}(\tau)v_{2}(\tau)+v_{2}(\tau)^{2}\right]d\tau,

applying the Strichartz estimates (Lemma
2.1
(ii)),
we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|w(\tau)\|_{L_{\tau}^{3}(t,\infty;W_{x}^{1,\infty})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|w(\tau)^{2}\|_{L_{\tau}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t,\infty;H_{x}^{2,1})}+\left\|(v_{1}(\tau)+v_{2}(\tau))w(\tau)\right\|_{L_{\tau}^{1}(t,\infty;H_{x}^{2})}

\displaystyle+\|2v_{1}(\tau)v_{2}(\tau)+v_{2}(\tau)^{2}\|_{L_{\tau}^{1}(t,\infty;H_{x}^{2})}.

By the Hölder inequality,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|w(\tau)^{2}\|_{L_{\tau}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t,\infty;W_{x}^{2,1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\|w(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}^{2}\|_{L_{\tau}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t,\infty)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{t\in[T,\infty)}t^{\alpha}\|w(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}\right)^{2}\|\tau^{-2\alpha}\|_{L_{\tau}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t,\infty)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-2\alpha+\frac{2}{3}}\left(\sup_{t\in[T,\infty)}t^{\alpha}\|w(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-2\alpha+\frac{2}{3}}\|w\|_{X_{T}}^{2},

where
\alpha>2/3
.
By the Hölder inequality and Lemma
3.3
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|(v_{1}(\tau)+v_{2}(\tau))w(\tau)\|_{L^{1}(t,\infty;H_{x}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|(\|v_{1}(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{2,\infty}}+\|v_{2}(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{2}})\|w(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}\right\|_{L_{\tau}^{1}(t,\infty)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\sup_{t\in[T,\infty)}t^{\alpha}\|w(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}\right)\|\tau^{-1-\alpha}\|_{L_{\tau}^{1}(t,\infty)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon t^{-\alpha}\left(\sup_{t\in[T,\infty)}t^{\alpha}\|w(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon t^{-\alpha}\|w\|_{X_{T}},

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|2v_{1}(\tau)v_{2}(\tau)+v_{2}(\tau)^{2}\|_{L^{1}(t,\infty;H_{x}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\|v_{1}(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{2,\infty}}\|v_{2}(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}+\|v_{2}(\tau)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}^{2}\right\|_{L_{\tau}^{1}(t,\infty)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{3}\|\tau^{-2}\|_{L_{\tau}^{1}(t,\infty)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{3}t^{-1}.

Substituting (
4.5
), (
4.6
), (
4.7
) into (
4.4
), we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[T,\infty)}t^{\alpha}\|w(\tau)\|_{L_{\tau}^{3}(t,\infty;W_{x}^{1,\infty})}\lesssim T^{-\alpha+\frac{2}{3}}\|w\|_{X_{T}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|w\|_{X_{T}}+\varepsilon^{3}T^{\alpha-1}.

where the implicit constants are independent of

\nu
and
\lambda
.

By (
4.3
) and (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle\|w\|_{X_{T}}\lesssim T^{-\alpha+\frac{2}{3}}\|w\|_{X_{T}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|w\|_{X_{T}}+\varepsilon^{2}T^{\alpha-1}.

Hence, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
T>0

which are independent of
\lambda
and
\nu
such that
for any
0<\lambda<1
and
0<\nu<1
,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|w\|_{X_{T}}=\sup_{t\geq T}t^{\alpha}(\|w(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}+\|w(\tau)\|_{L_{\tau}^{3}(t,\infty;W_{x}^{1,\infty})})\leq 2\epsilon,

where
2/3<\alpha<1
.
By combining a priori estimate (
4.9
) with
the standard compactness argument
(see
[
20
, Section 3]
for instance), we find that
there exists a unique solution

v\in C([T,\infty);H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
to (
1.5
)
which satisfies
\|w\|_{X_{T}}=\|v-v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X_{T}}<2\varepsilon
.
By conservation of the energy (
1.3
),
we see
v\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
.
Furthermore, from the above inequality
and Lemma
3.3
, we see

\displaystyle\|v(t)-V(t)v_{\infty}\|_{H_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}+\|v_{2}(t)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon t^{-\alpha}+\varepsilon^{2}t^{-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon t^{-\alpha}.

This completes
the proof of Theorem
1.1
. ∎

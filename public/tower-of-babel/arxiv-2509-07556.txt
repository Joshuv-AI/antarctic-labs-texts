Smoothed Shifted Convolutions of Generalised Divisor Functions
Cheuk Fung (Joshua) Lau

Abstract
. We prove an asymptotic formula for the smoothed shifted convolution of the generalised divisor function d k ​ ( n ) d_{k}(n) and the divisor function d ⁡ ( n ) d(n) , with a power-saving error term independent of k k . In particular, when k k is large, this is an improvement on Topacogullari (2018).

1.
Introduction

Prime numbers are of great interest in number theory, and we may use the von Mangoldt function
\Lambda
to study patterns of prime numbers. For example, to study the number of twin primes less than or equal to
x
, it suffices to estimate
\sum_{n\leq x}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+2)
. One idea for estimating this quantity is to use the following decomposition of
Linnik and Schuur, 1963

\Lambda(n)=\log n\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}\widetilde{d}_{k}(n),

where
\widetilde{d}_{k}(n)=\#\{n=n_{1}\cdots n_{k}:n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}>1\}
. Therefore, if one could handle all sums of the form
\sum_{n\leq x}d_{k}(n)d_{j}(n+h)
, then we could estimate the number of twin primes. Heuristically, since
we expect
\sum_{n\leq x}d_{k}(n)
to grow like
x(\log x)^{k-1}
, one might expect
\sum_{n\leq x}d_{k}(n)d_{j}(n+h)
to behave like
x(\log x)^{k+j-2}
when
h\neq 0
. The case
j=k=2
is known as the binary additive divisor problem, which has been studied by many authors. It is known that for
h\in\mathbb{Z}
non-zero and
|h|\ll x^{\frac{2}{3}}
,

\sum_{n\leq x}d(n)d(n+h)=xP_{2,h}(\log x)+O(x^{\frac{2}{3}+\varepsilon}),

where
P_{2,h}(t)
is a quadratic polynomial depending on
h
. This can be found in
Motohashi, 1994
, where a more detailed review of this problem can be found as well. For
k=3
,
Topacogullari, 2016
proved for
h\in\mathbb{Z}
non-zero and
|h|\ll x^{\frac{2}{3}}
,

\sum_{n\leq x}d_{3}(n)d(n+h)=xP_{3,h}(\log x)+O(x^{\frac{8}{9}+\varepsilon}),

where
P_{3,h}(t)
is a cubic polynomial depending on
h
. In the general case
k\geq 4
, the first result with a main term of this kind was obtained by
Motohashi, 1980
using the dispersion method, namely

\sum_{n\leq x}d_{k}(n)d(n+1)=xP_{k}(\log x)+O_{k}(x(\log\log x)^{c_{k}}(\log x)^{-1}),

where
c_{k}
is a constant depending only on
k
and
P_{k}(t)
is a polynomial of degree
k
. Using spectral methods,
Drappeau, 2017
obtained a power-saving error term and proved that there exists
\delta>0
such that for
h\in\mathbb{N}
and
h\ll x^{\delta}
, we have

\sum_{n\leq x}d_{k}(n)d(n+h)=xP_{k,h}(\log x)+O_{k}(x^{1-\frac{\delta}{k}}),

where
P_{k,h}(t)
is a polynomial of degree
k
depending on
h
. This result was improved by
Topacogullari, 2018
, who proved that for
h
non-zero and
|h|\ll x^{\frac{15}{19}}
,

\sum_{n\leq x}d_{k}(n)d(n+h)=xP_{k,h}(\log x)+O_{k,\varepsilon}(x^{1-\frac{4}{15k-9}+\varepsilon}+x^{\frac{56}{57}+\varepsilon}),

where
P_{k,h}(t)
is a polynomial of degree
k
depending on
h
. If we consider a smoothed version of this problem,
Topacogullari, 2018
proved that for
w:[1/2,1]\to[0,\infty)
a smooth and compactly supported function, if
h\in\mathbb{Z}
is non-zero and
|h|\ll x^{\frac{15}{19}}
, we have

\sum_{n}w\left(\frac{n}{x}\right)d_{k}(n)d(n+h)=xP_{k,h,w}(\log x)+O_{k,w,\varepsilon}(x^{1-\frac{1}{3k-2}+\varepsilon}+x^{\frac{37}{38}+\frac{\theta}{19}+\varepsilon}),

where
P_{k,h,w}(t)
is a polynomial of degree
k
depending on
h
and
w
. The above power saving depends on
k
, and the exponent worsens as
k
increases. In this paper, we obtain an asymptotic where the power saving is uniform in
k
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
w:[1/2,1]\to[0,\infty)
be smooth and compactly supported. Let
h
be a non-zero integer such that
|h|\ll x^{\frac{25}{28}-\eta}
for some
\eta>0
. Then,

\sum_{n}w\left(\frac{n}{x}\right)d_{k}(n)d(n+h)=xP_{x,h,w}(\log x)+O_{k,w,\varepsilon}(x^{1-\frac{7}{128}\eta+\varepsilon}),

where
P_{k,h,w}(t)
is a polynomial of degree
k
depending on
h
and
w
.

For
k
large, this improves on
Topacogullari, 2018
. Theorem
1.1
is a corollary of the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
w:[1/2,1]\to[0,\infty)
be smooth and compactly supported,
h\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
sufficiently large in terms of
\varepsilon
. Then for
0\leq\delta\leq\frac{1}{16}
and
|h|\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n}w\left(\frac{n}{x}\right)

\displaystyle d_{k}(n)d(n+h)=\ xP_{k,h,w}(\log x)

\displaystyle+O_{k,w,\varepsilon,\delta}\left(x^{1-\delta+2\delta\theta+\varepsilon}\left(1+\frac{|h|^{\frac{1}{4}}}{x^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\delta}}\right)+x^{1-\delta+\frac{\theta}{3}+\frac{2\delta}{3}\theta+\varepsilon}\left(1+\frac{|h|^{\frac{\theta}{2}}}{x^{\frac{\theta}{6}+\frac{4\delta}{3}\theta}}\right)\right),

where
P_{k,h,w}
is a polynomial of degree
k
depending only on
h
and
w
, and the implied constant depends on
k,w,\varepsilon,\delta
.

We remark that a fixed power saving for the sharp cutoff problem is currently out of reach, since it remains unknown whether an asymptotic formula with a fixed power saving holds for
\sum_{n\leq x}d_{k}(n)
.

2.
Outline

In this section, we outline the main ideas and set
h=1
for simplicity. The first few steps are similar to the treatment in
Topacogullari, 2018
. We first write

\sum_{n}w\left(\frac{n}{x}\right)d_{k}(n)d(n+1)=\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}}w\left(\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}{x}\right)d(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}+1),

and so it suffices to estimate

(2.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\\
a_{i}\asymp A_{i}\end{subarray}}w\left(\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}{x}\right)d(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}+1),

where
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
are supported dyadically on
a_{i}\asymp A_{i}
. If there is one large variable, or a product of two variables is large, then (
2.1
) can be estimated using the Voronoi formula by splitting the summation into
\ \mathrm{mod}\ \prod_{i}a_{i}
. Otherwise, some product of the variables
a_{i}
must be of ’medium’ size, say
a_{1}\cdots a_{r}
. In this case, let

\Phi_{r}(b):=\sum_{a_{i}\asymp A_{i}\forall i\leq r}d(a_{1}\cdots a_{r}b+1),

and write
\widetilde{\Phi}_{r}(b)
as the expected main term (using the Voronoi formula). Therefore, (
2.1
) becomes

\sum_{a_{i}\asymp A_{i}\forall i>r}\widetilde{\Phi}_{r}(a_{r+1}\cdots a_{k})-\sum_{a_{i}\asymp A_{i}\forall i>r}(\widetilde{\Phi}(a_{r+1}\cdots a_{k})-\Phi(a_{r+1}\cdots a_{k})).

The first term is the main term and can be computed easily, and to upper bound the second term we use Cauchy-Schwarz and it suffices to bound

\sum_{b\asymp B}(\widetilde{\Phi}_{r}(b)-\Phi_{r}(b))^{2}=\sum_{b\asymp B}\widetilde{\Phi}_{r}(b)^{2}-2\sum_{b\asymp B}\widetilde{\Phi}_{r}(b)\Phi_{r}(b)+\sum_{b\asymp B}\Phi_{r}(b)^{2},

where
B=A_{r+1}\cdots A_{k}
. It is straightforward to estimate the first two sums, while if we open the square in the last sum it suffices to estimate sums of the form

(2.2)

\sum_{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{r_{1}n}{x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{r_{2}n}{x}\right)d(r_{1}n+1)d(r_{2}n+1),

where
r_{1},r_{2}
suitably sized with
r_{1}\neq r_{2}
. In fact, before applying Cauchy-Schwarz we can group square factors together, so it suffices to estimate (
2.2
) for squarefree
r_{1},r_{2}
.

To do this, we use Theorem
5.2
, which is Theorem 10.1 of
Grimmelt and Merikoski, 2024
. This result was proven by spectral methods, and it counts the solutions to determinant equations
ad-bc=h
twisted by periodic weights. The resulting error term consists of data concerning the ranges of the variables
a,c,d
, as well as a quantity
\mathcal{K}
that depends on the periodic weight and its underlying geometry.

To outline our strategy, we focus on the particular case
r_{1},r_{2}
coprime, and assume the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture. We can rewrite (
2.2
) as

(2.3)

\sum_{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ad=r_{1}n+1\\
bc=r_{2}n+1\end{subarray}}w_{1}\left(\frac{ad-1}{x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{bc-1}{x}\right).

Rewriting the constraints as determinant equations, note
ad=r_{1}n+1
and
bc=r_{2}n+1
together imply
r_{2}ad-r_{1}bc=r_{2}-r_{1}
. Also, since
(r_{1},r_{2})=1
,
r_{2}ad-r_{1}bc=r_{2}-r_{1}
implies both
ad=r_{1}n+1
and
bc=r_{2}n+1
. Therefore (
2.3
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{r_{2}ad-r_{1}bc=r_{2}-r_{1}}w_{1}\left(\frac{ad-1}{x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{bc-1}{x}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{ad-bc=r_{2}-r_{1}}w_{1}\left(\frac{ad-r_{2}}{r_{2}x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{bc-r_{1}}{r_{1}x}\right)\mathds{1}_{r_{2}\mid a}\mathds{1}_{r_{1}\mid c}

and let
\alpha(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix})=\mathds{1}_{r_{2}\mid a}\mathds{1}_{r_{1}\mid c}
. Then, we dyadically split the variables into
a\asymp A
,
c\asymp C
,
d\asymp D
and without loss of generality assume
r_{1}^{-1}C\ll D\ll r_{2}^{-1}A\ll B
. To treat the very skewed ranges
A>r_{2}C
or
D>C
, we apply Poisson summation.

In other ranges, we may apply Theorem
5.2
with
\Gamma=\Gamma_{2}(r_{2},r_{1})
. Then, it is straightforward to compute the main term, so we focus on the error term, in particular
\mathcal{K}
. To bound

\sum_{0\leq|c|\leq\frac{6C}{D}}\left|\sum_{\tau\in\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\alpha(\tau)\overline{\alpha(\tau\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix})}\right|,

we use a description of
\Gamma_{2}(r_{2},r_{1})\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with projective lines to get

\sum_{0\leq|c|\leq\frac{6C}{D}}\left|\sum_{\tau\in\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\alpha(\tau)\overline{\alpha(\tau\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix})}\right|\ll\frac{C}{r_{1}r_{2}D}+1\ll 1,

and similarly for the corresponding sum over
b
. Combining these bounds, we get
\mathcal{K}\ll 1
. Putting these together, one gets an asymptotic for (
2.2
) with error term
\ll\sqrt{r_{2}x}
. Combining the above and assuming the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture, a sketch of what we get is

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\\
a_{i}\asymp A_{i}\end{subarray}}w\left(\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}{x}\right)d(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}+1)=\mathrm{MT}+R,

where MT is the main term which may be handled straightforwardly, and
R
is the error term with the upper bounds

(2.4)

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{A_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}},

(2.5)

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{A_{1}A_{2}}\left(1+\frac{A_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\left(1+\frac{A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}A_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

(2.6)

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{1+\varepsilon}}{A^{\frac{1}{2}}}+A^{\frac{3}{4}}x^{\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon},

where
A
is an arbitrary product of factors
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
. As mentioned above, we use the three bounds depending on the sizes of the factors
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
. Without loss of generality, assume
A_{1}\geq\cdots\geq A_{k}
, and for
\delta>0
we let the boundary values to be

X_{1}=x^{\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{3}\delta},\quad X_{2}=x^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta},\quad X_{3}=x^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta},\quad X_{4}=x^{2\delta}.

If
A_{1}\gg X_{1}
, we use (
2.4
) to get
R\ll x^{1-\delta+\varepsilon}
. If
A_{1}A_{2}\gg X_{2}
, then for
\delta\leq 1/2
we use (
2.5
) to get

R\ll x^{1-\delta+\varepsilon}\left(1+{x^{-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\delta}}\right)\ll x^{1-\delta+\varepsilon}.

At last, it can be shown that the only remaining case is
X_{4}\ll\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}\ll X_{3}
for some non-empty index set
I\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
. Letting
A=\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}
, using (
2.6
) we get

R\ll\frac{x^{1+\varepsilon}}{X_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+X_{3}^{\frac{3}{4}}x^{\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon}\ll x^{1-\delta+\varepsilon}.

Compared to
Topacogullari, 2018
our approach differs in two aspects: First, the above application of Theorem
5.2
replaces his more classical use of sums of Kloosterman sums. Second, we used a more efficient glueing of variables with Cauchy-Schwarz, as described. The second change alone would allow us to obtain a variant of Theorem
1.1
with fixed, albeit worse, power saving.

5.
A Certain Divisor Problem

We first prove the following result on a certain divisor sum.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be squarefree, and
h\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}

(h,r_{1}r_{2})=1

[1/2,1]

\displaystyle\sum_{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{r_{1}n}{x}\right)

\displaystyle+O_{\varepsilon}\left(x^{\frac{1}{2}+O(\eta)}r_{0}r_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\operatorname{gcd}(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1},r_{0}^{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left(\frac{|h(r_{2}-r_{1})|}{r_{0}}\right)^{\theta}+\frac{x^{\theta}}{r_{1}^{\theta}}\right)\right),

We isolate a lemma from the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
h\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
B,C\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Define
r_{0}=\operatorname{gcd}(r_{1},r_{2})
and
\widetilde{r}_{i}=r_{i}/r_{0}
for
i=1,2
. Suppose
r_{1},r_{2}
are squarefree. Let
\alpha_{0}:\operatorname{M}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{C}
be given by

\alpha_{0}(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix})=\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{2}\mid a}\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{1}\mid c}.

Then, for
\Gamma=\Gamma_{2}(r_{2},r_{1})
,
\alpha_{0}
is left
\Gamma
-automorphic, and that

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq|b|\leq B}\left|\sum_{\tau\in\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\alpha_{0}\left(\tau\right)\overline{\alpha_{0}\left(\tau\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\ll r_{0}^{2}B,

\displaystyle\sum_{0<|c|\leq C}\left|\sum_{\tau\in\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\alpha_{0}\left(\tau\right)\overline{\alpha_{0}\left(\tau\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\ll\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{\widetilde{r}_{1}\widetilde{r}_{2}}C,

and for all
\sigma\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\tau\in\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\alpha_{0}(\tau)\overline{\alpha_{0}(\tau\sigma)}\right|\ll r_{0}^{2}.

Proof.

To prove
\alpha
is left
\Gamma
-automorphic, let
g=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&r_{2}b^{\prime}\\
r_{1}c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma
, then
(a^{\prime},r_{1}r_{2})=(d^{\prime},r_{1}r_{2})=1
, so

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix})=1

\displaystyle\iff\widetilde{r}_{2}\mid a,\ \widetilde{r}_{1}\mid c

\displaystyle\iff\widetilde{r}_{2}\mid a^{\prime}a+r_{0}\widetilde{r}_{2}b^{\prime}c,\ \widetilde{r}_{1}\mid r_{0}\widetilde{r}_{1}ac^{\prime}+cd^{\prime}

\displaystyle\iff\alpha_{0}(g\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix})=1.

We consider the sum over
c
first. By the Chinese Remainder Theorem,

\displaystyle\sum_{0<|c|\leq C}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\alpha_{0}\left(\tau\right)\overline{\alpha_{0}\left(\tau\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix}\right)}\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<|c|\leq C}

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\nmid r_{0},p\mid\widetilde{r}_{1}\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\sum_{\left[\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\right]\in\Gamma_{0}(p)\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\mathds{1}_{p\mid c^{\prime}}\mathds{1}_{p\mid cd^{\prime}}\Bigg)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\nmid r_{0},p\mid\widetilde{r}_{2}\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\sum_{\left[\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\right]\in\Gamma_{0}(p)^{T}\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\mathds{1}_{p\mid a^{\prime}}\mathds{1}_{p\mid cb^{\prime}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\hskip 6.49994pt\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid r_{0}\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\sum_{\left[\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\right]\in\Gamma_{2}(p,p)\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}1\Bigg)

\displaystyle=:\sum_{0<|c|\leq C}

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}\Pi_{2}\Pi_{3}.

To bound
\Pi_{1}
note
p\nmid d^{\prime}
, so
p\mid c
and
\Pi_{1}\leq\prod_{p\mid\widetilde{r}_{1}}\mathds{1}_{p\mid c}=\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{1}\mid c}
. Similarly,
\Pi_{2}\leq\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{2}\mid c}
. Also, we have
\Pi_{3}\leq r_{0}^{2}
. Putting these together, we get the required bound.

The sum over
b
is similar but simpler, so we omit it. For the last bound, we again split into three products

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\alpha_{0}(\tau)\alpha_{0}(\tau\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\tau\in\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\alpha_{0}(\tau)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid\widetilde{r}_{1}\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\sum_{\left[\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\right]\in\Gamma_{0}(p)\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\mathds{1}_{p\mid c^{\prime}}\Bigg)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid\widetilde{r}_{2}\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\sum_{\left[\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\right]\in\Gamma_{0}(p)^{T}\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\mathds{1}_{p\mid a^{\prime}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.49995pt\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid r_{0}\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\sum_{\left[\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\right]\in\Gamma_{2}(p,p)\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}1\Bigg)

\displaystyle=:\Pi_{1}^{\prime}\Pi_{2}^{\prime}\Pi_{3}^{\prime}.

Clearly,
\Pi_{1}^{\prime},\Pi_{2}^{\prime}\leq 1
while
\Pi_{3}^{\prime}\ll r_{0}^{2}
, which is the required bound.
∎

We use Lemma
5.4
to prove the following.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
h\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
L\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Define
r_{0}=\operatorname{gcd}(r_{1},r_{2})
and
\widetilde{r}_{i}=r_{i}/r_{0}
for
i=1,2
. Suppose
r_{1},r_{2}
are squarefree and
\operatorname{gcd}(h,r_{1}r_{2})=1
. Define
k=\operatorname{gcd}(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1},r_{1}r_{2}^{\infty})
and
r=r_{0}/\operatorname{gcd}(r_{0},k)
. Let
\alpha:\operatorname{M}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{C}
be given by

\alpha(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix})=\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{2}\mid a}\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{1}\mid c}\mathds{1}_{r_{2}\mid ad-h\widetilde{r}_{2}},

and define

\mathrm{M}_{2,h,k}(\mathbb{Z}):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{M}_{2,hk}(\mathbb{Z}):\operatorname{gcd}(a,c,k)=\operatorname{gcd}(b,d,k)=1\right\}.

Then, for
\Gamma=\Gamma_{2}(r_{2},r_{1})
,
T_{1,k}=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{M}_{2,1,k}(\mathbb{Z})
, and
T=\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\\
|a|+|b|L+|c|/L+|d|\leq 10\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in T_{1,k}\\
\sigma_{1}^{-1}g\sigma_{2}=:\sigma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in T\end{subarray}}\alpha(\tau\sigma)\overline{\alpha(\tau\sigma g)}\right|\ll\frac{kr_{0}^{2}}{L}+\frac{k^{2}r_{0}^{2}L}{\widetilde{r}_{1}\widetilde{r}_{2}}+k^{2}r_{0}^{2+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the argument in p.64-66 of
Grimmelt and Merikoski, 2024
. Note that

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&fr\\
0&k\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a&afr+bk\\
c&cfr+dk\end{pmatrix},

so for any
\tau=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&fr\\
0&k\end{pmatrix}
, we have

\alpha(\tau\sigma)\leq\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{2}\mid a}\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{1}\mid c}=:\alpha_{0}(\tau).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\\
|a|+|b|L+|c|/L+|d|\leq 10\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in T_{1,k}\\
\sigma_{1}^{-1}g\sigma_{2}=:\sigma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in T\end{subarray}}\alpha(\tau\sigma)\overline{\alpha(\tau\sigma g)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}(\mathbb{Z})}\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\sigma)\alpha_{0}(\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}1&f_{j}r\\
0&k\end{pmatrix}\\
\sigma_{2}^{-1}\sigma\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&b_{0}\\
c_{0}&d_{0}\end{pmatrix}\\
|a_{0}|+|b_{0}|L+|c_{0}|/L+|d_{0}|\leq 10\end{subarray}}1.

To bound this quantity, we split into three cases. First, if
c=0
then
ad=1
implies
a=d=\pm 1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}(\mathbb{Z})\\
c=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\sigma)\alpha_{0}(\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}1&f_{j}r\\
0&k\end{pmatrix}\\
\sigma_{2}^{-1}\sigma\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&b_{0}\\
c_{0}&d_{0}\end{pmatrix}\\
|a_{0}|+|b_{0}|L+|c_{0}|/L+|d_{0}|\leq 10\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{b}\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\begin{pmatrix}\pm 1&b\\
0&\pm 1\end{pmatrix})\alpha_{0}(\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f_{1},f_{2}\leq k\\
|f_{1}r-f_{2}r\pm kb|\leq 10/L\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll k\sum_{b}\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\begin{pmatrix}\pm 1&b\\
0&\pm 1\end{pmatrix})\alpha_{0}(\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\leq k\\
|fr\pm kb|\leq 10/L\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll k\sum_{|b_{0}|\leq 10/L}\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\begin{pmatrix}\pm 1&b_{0}\\
0&\pm 1\end{pmatrix})\alpha_{0}(\tau)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{kr_{0}^{2}}{L},

where we substituted
b_{0}=fr\pm kb
since
\operatorname{gcd}(k,r)=1
, and we used Lemma
5.4
in the last step. The second case is
c\neq 0
and
b_{0}=0
. Note

\sigma_{2}^{-1}\sigma\sigma_{1}=\frac{1}{k}\begin{pmatrix}*&f_{1}ra-f_{2}rd+kb-cf_{1}f_{2}r^{2}/k\\
*&*\end{pmatrix},

so
b_{0}=0
implies
r\mid b
and
k\mid c
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}(\mathbb{Z})\\
c\neq 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\sigma)\alpha_{0}(\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}1&f_{j}r\\
0&k\end{pmatrix}\\
\sigma_{2}^{-1}\sigma\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&b_{0}\\
c_{0}&d_{0}\end{pmatrix}\\
|a_{0}|+|b_{0}|L+|c_{0}|/L+|d_{0}|\leq 10\\
b_{0}=0\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}(\mathbb{Z})\\
0<|c|\leq 10kL\\
r\mid b,\ k\mid c\end{subarray}}\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\sigma)\alpha_{0}(\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f_{1},f_{2}\leq k\\
|a-cf_{2}r/k|\leq 10\\
|d+cf_{1}/k|\leq 10\\
b_{0}=0\end{subarray}}1.

Since
k\mid c
, we have
\alpha_{0}(\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix})=\alpha_{0}(\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c/k&0\end{pmatrix})
and
\alpha_{0}(\tau\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix})=\alpha_{0}(\tau\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix})
, so the sum is

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<|c|\leq 10kL\\
k\mid c\end{subarray}}\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix})\alpha_{0}(\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b,d\\
ad-bc=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f_{1},f_{2}\leq k\\
|a-cf_{2}r/k|\leq 10\\
|d+cf_{1}/k|\leq 10\\
b_{0}=0\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{0<|c|\leq 10L}\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix})\alpha_{0}(\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b,d\\
ad-bck=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f_{1},f_{2}\leq k\\
|a-cf_{2}r|\leq 10\\
|d+cf_{1}|\leq 10\\
f_{2}rd-f_{1}ra+cf_{1}f_{2}r^{2}=kb\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{0<|c|\leq 10L}\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix})\alpha_{0}(\tau)\sum_{f_{1},f_{2}\leq k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,d\\
|a-cf_{2}r|\leq 10\\
|d+cf_{1}|\leq 10\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll k^{2}\sum_{0<|c|\leq 10L}\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix})\alpha_{0}(\tau)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{k^{2}r_{0}^{2}L}{\widetilde{r}_{1}\widetilde{r}_{2}},

where we used Lemma
5.4
in the last step. Finally, for the last case we use the trivial bound. For any
\sigma\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,

\left|\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\sigma)\alpha_{0}(\tau)\right|\ll r_{0}^{2}

by Lemma
5.4
, and so

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}(\mathbb{Z})\\
c\neq 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\tau\in T}\alpha_{0}(\tau\sigma)\alpha_{0}(\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}1&f_{j}r\\
0&k\end{pmatrix}\\
\sigma_{2}^{-1}\sigma\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&b_{0}\\
c_{0}&d_{0}\end{pmatrix}\\
|a_{0}|+|b_{0}|L+|c_{0}|/L+|d_{0}|\leq 10\\
b_{0}\neq 0\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll r_{0}^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
c_{1}&d_{1}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{M}_{2,k}(\mathbb{Z})\\
c\neq 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma_{2}\in T_{1,k}\\
\sigma_{2}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
c_{1}&d_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&b_{0}\\
c_{0}&d_{0}\end{pmatrix}\\
|a_{0}|+|b_{0}|L+|c_{0}|/L+|d_{0}|\leq 10\\
b_{0}\neq 0\end{subarray}}1.

Note

\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&b_{0}\\
c_{0}&d_{0}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{k}\begin{pmatrix}k&-f_{2}r\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
c_{1}&d_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{k}\begin{pmatrix}ka_{1}-f_{2}rc_{1}&kb_{1}-f_{2}rd_{1}\\
c_{1}&d_{1}\end{pmatrix},

and let the last matrix be
\frac{1}{k}\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}&b_{2}\\
c_{2}&d_{2}\end{pmatrix}
. Then, summing over
f_{2}
and grouping together variables, the above sum is

\displaystyle\ll r_{0}^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{2}d_{2}-b_{2}c_{2}=k^{2}\\
|a_{2}|/k+|b_{2}|L/k+|c_{2}|/(kL)+|d_{2}|/k\leq 10\\
b_{2}c_{2}\neq 0\end{subarray}}1\ll r_{0}^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{2},d_{2}\ll k\\
a_{2}d_{2}\neq k^{2}\end{subarray}}\tau(a_{2}d_{2}-k^{2})\ll k^{2}r_{0}^{2+\varepsilon}.

Grouping these estimates together, we are done.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Splitting the divisor function, we get

\sum_{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{r_{1}n}{x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{r_{2}n}{x}\right)d(r_{1}n+h)d(r_{2}n+h)=\sum_{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ad=r_{1}n+h\\
bc=r_{2}n+h\end{subarray}}w_{1}\left(\frac{ad-h}{x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{bc-h}{x}\right).

For convenience, let
r_{0}=(r_{1},r_{2})
and
\widetilde{r}_{i}=r_{i}/r_{0}
for
i=1,2
. Note

\begin{cases}ad&=r_{1}n+h\\
bc&=r_{2}n+h\end{cases}\implies\widetilde{r}_{2}ad-\widetilde{r}_{1}bc=h(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1}).

We wish to recover
n
from the right hand side along with another condition. Since
(\widetilde{r}_{1},\widetilde{r}_{2})=1
,
\widetilde{r}_{1}\mid\widetilde{r}_{2}(ad-f_{1})
implies
\widetilde{r}_{1}\mid(ad-f_{1})
. If we have
r_{0}\mid ad-f_{1}
, then we can choose

n=\frac{ad-f_{1}}{r_{0}\cdot\widetilde{r}_{1}}=\frac{ad-f_{1}}{r_{1}},

since
(r_{0},\widetilde{r}_{1})=1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ad=r_{1}n+h\\
bc=r_{2}n+h\end{subarray}}w_{1}\left(\frac{ad-h}{x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{bc-h}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{r}_{2}ad-\widetilde{r}_{1}bc=h(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1})\\
r_{0}\mid ad-h\end{subarray}}w_{1}\left(\frac{ad-h}{x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{bc-h}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{ad-bc=h(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1})}w_{1}\left(\frac{ad-h\widetilde{r}_{2}}{\widetilde{r}_{2}x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{bc-h\widetilde{r}_{1}}{\widetilde{r}_{1}x}\right)\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{2}\mid a}\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{1}\mid c}\mathds{1}_{r_{2}\mid ad-h\widetilde{r}_{2}}

\displaystyle=:\sum_{ad-bc=h(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1})}w_{1}\left(\frac{ad-h\widetilde{r}_{2}}{\widetilde{r}_{2}x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{bc-h\widetilde{r}_{1}}{\widetilde{r}_{1}x}\right)\alpha(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}).

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a fixed smooth function supported on
[1,2]
which satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\frac{\,\textup{d}x}{x}=1.

Inserting this into our sum, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{ad-bc=h(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1})}w_{1}\left(\frac{ad-h\widetilde{r}_{2}}{\widetilde{r}_{2}x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{bc-h\widetilde{r}_{1}}{\widetilde{r}_{1}x}\right)\alpha(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{ad-bc=h(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1})}w_{1}\left(\frac{ad-h\widetilde{r}_{2}}{\widetilde{r}_{2}x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{bc-h\widetilde{r}_{1}}{\widetilde{r}_{1}x}\right)\psi\left(\frac{a}{A}\right)\psi\left(\frac{c}{C}\right)\psi\left(\frac{d}{D}\right)\alpha(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix})\frac{\mathrm{d}A\mathrm{d}C\mathrm{d}D}{ACD},

and let
S(A,C,D)
be the inner sum, and denote
B:=(\widetilde{r}_{1}x+h\widetilde{r}_{1})/C
. Since the weight functions
w_{1},w_{2}
imply
r_{1}\asymp r_{2}
and we assumed
h\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}
, by swapping the variables we may assume

\widetilde{r}_{1}^{-1}C\ll D\ll\widetilde{r}_{2}^{-1}A\ll B.

If
A>r_{2}Cx^{\eta}
or
D>r_{0}Cx^{\eta}
, we can use Poisson summation to get the correct main term with a negligible error term. Indeed, we have the following congruence requirements on
a
:

ad\equiv h(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1})\ (\mathrm{mod}\ c),\quad a\equiv\widetilde{r}_{2}\bar{d}h\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \frac{r_{2}}{(r_{0},d)}),

where
\bar{d}
is the multiplicative inverse of
d
mod
\frac{r_{0}}{(r_{0},d)}
, and while for
d
we have

\frac{a}{\widetilde{r}_{2}}d\equiv h\pmod{r_{0}},\quad ad\equiv h(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1})\pmod{c}

Therefore, it remains to consider the complementary range

1\ll\frac{A}{C}\ll r_{2}x^{\eta},\quad\widetilde{r}_{1}^{-1}\ll\frac{D}{C}\ll r_{0}x^{\eta}.

Our goal is to apply Theorem
5.2
. However, in the theorem statement the conditions
\operatorname{gcd}(a,c,k)=\operatorname{gcd}(b,d,k)=1
are assumed, where
k=\operatorname{gcd}(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1},r_{1}r_{2}^{\infty})
. Observe that
\operatorname{gcd}(k,\widetilde{r}_{1}\widetilde{r}_{2})=1
, and let
k_{ac}=\operatorname{gcd}(a,c,k)
and
k_{bd}=\operatorname{gcd}(b,d,k)
. Then we can rewrite our original sum as

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\mid k}\sum_{ad-bc=h(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1})}w_{1}\left(\frac{ad-h\widetilde{r}_{2}}{\widetilde{r}_{2}x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{bc-h\widetilde{r}_{1}}{\widetilde{r}_{1}x}\right)\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{2}\mid a}\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{1}\mid c}\mathds{1}_{r_{2}\mid ad-h\widetilde{r}_{2}}\mathds{1}_{k_{ac}=\gamma_{1},k_{bd}=\gamma_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\mid k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ad-bc=\frac{h(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1})}{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}\\
(a,c,k)=(b,d,k)=1\end{subarray}}w_{1}\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}ad-h\widetilde{r}_{2}}{\widetilde{r}_{2}x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}bc-h\widetilde{r}_{1}}{\widetilde{r}_{1}x}\right)\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{2}\mid a}\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{1}\mid c}\mathds{1}_{ad\equiv\gamma_{3}h\widetilde{r}_{2}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \frac{r_{0}}{(r_{0},\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})}\cdot\widetilde{r}_{2}},

where
\gamma_{3}
is the multiplicative inverse of
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}/(r_{0},\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})
modulo
r_{0}/(r_{0},\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})
. This form satisfies the conditions of Theorem
5.2
and we may apply the theorem to the inner sum. Since the outer sum has only
\ll r_{0}^{\varepsilon}
many terms, for simplicity we focus on estimating

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ad-bc=h(\widetilde{r}_{2}-\widetilde{r}_{1})\\
(a,c,k)=(b,d,k)=1\end{subarray}}w_{1}\left(\frac{ad-h\widetilde{r}_{2}}{\widetilde{r}_{2}x}\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{bc-h\widetilde{r}_{1}}{\widetilde{r}_{1}x}\right)\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{2}\mid a}\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{r}_{1}\mid c}\mathds{1}_{r_{2}\mid ad-h\widetilde{r}_{2}},

and the treatment for the general case is entirely analogous. Now apply Theorem

Theorem 5.6

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{r_{1}n}{x}\right)

Proof.

As in the proof of Theroem

, we may arrange the sum to be of the form

and

for

and

Lemma 6.1

\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}}w\left(\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}{x}\right)v_{1}(a_{1})\cdots v_{k}(a_{k})d(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}+h)=M_{v_{1}}+R_{v_{1}},

where

(a,d)=1\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{an}{d}\right),

and the following bounds for the error term

for

(6.1)

\displaystyle R_{v_{1}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{A_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}},

(6.2)

\displaystyle R_{v_{1}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}}{A_{1}A_{2}}\left(1+\frac{(A_{1}A_{2})^{2\theta}}{x^{\theta}}\right)\left(1+\frac{A_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\left(1+|h|^{\frac{1}{4}}\frac{A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}A_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle R_{v_{1}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{1+\varepsilon}}{A^{\frac{1}{2}}}+A^{\frac{3}{4}}x^{\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon}\left(|h|^{\frac{\theta}{2}}A^{\frac{\theta}{2}}+\frac{x^{\frac{\theta}{2}}}{A^{\frac{\theta}{2}}}\right),

Proof.

The first two bounds are from
Topacogullari, 2018
. Let

\Psi_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}}=\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}}w\left(\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}}{x}\right)v_{1}(a_{1})\cdots v_{k}(a_{k})d(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}+h).

Our treatment of the third bound is a modification of
Topacogullari, 2018
. First we rename variables such that
I=\{1,2,\ldots,|I|\}

\Psi_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}}=\sum_{b}\delta_{I}(b)\Phi_{I}(b),

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{I}(b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{i},\,i\in I}d\left(b\prod_{i\in I}a_{i}+h\right)w\left(\frac{b}{x}\prod_{i\in I}a_{i}\right)\prod_{i\in I}v_{i}(a_{i}),

\displaystyle\delta_{I}(b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{|I|+1},\ldots,a_{k}\\
b=a_{|I|+1}\cdots a_{k}\end{subarray}}v_{|I|+1}(a_{|I|+1})\cdots v_{k}(a_{k}).

Let

be a smooth function compactly supported on

\displaystyle\Psi_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}}=\sum_{j}\sum_{b}\delta_{I}(b)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\\

\prod_{i\in I}a_{i}^{\prime}\text{ squarefree}\end{subarray}}d\left(bc^{2}\prod_{i\in I}a_{i}^{\prime}+h\right)w\left(\frac{bc^{2}}{x}\prod_{i\in I}a_{i}^{\prime}\right)v_{j}^{\prime}(a_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{|I|}^{\prime})v_{j}^{sq}(c^{2}),

for some function

, with support contained in
\prod_{i\in I}[\widetilde{A}_{j,i},2\widetilde{A}_{j,i}]

\displaystyle\Psi_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\sum_{b,c}\delta_{I}(b)v_{j}^{sq}(c^{2})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{i}^{\prime},\ i\in I\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\sum_{f}\delta_{j}^{\prime}(f)\Phi_{j}^{\prime}(f),

and let

\displaystyle=M_{0}^{\prime}+O(x^{\varepsilon}\widetilde{A}_{j}^{2}),

(6.5)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=M_{0}^{\prime}+O\left(x^{1+\varepsilon}+x^{\frac{1}{3}+\varepsilon}\widetilde{A}_{j}^{2}\right),

(6.6)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=M_{0}^{\prime}+O\left(x^{1+\varepsilon}+\widetilde{A}_{j}^{\frac{5}{2}}x^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\left(|h|^{\theta}\widetilde{A}_{j}^{\theta}+\frac{x^{\theta}}{\widetilde{A}_{j}^{\theta}}\right)\right),

where

To use Lemma

, we prove that any factorisation into
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
can be partitioned into three admissible cases.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in[0,1]
satisfies
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}=1
and
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{k}
. Then, for any
\delta\leq\frac{1}{16}
, at least one of the following holds:

(A).

\alpha_{1}\geq\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{3}\delta
.

(B).

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}+\delta
.

(C).

There exists an non-empty index set
I\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
such that
2\delta\leq\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\leq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta
.

Proof.

Suppose (A) and (C) do not hold. Then,
\alpha_{1}<\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{3}\delta
and

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta\quad\text{ or }\quad\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}<2\delta.

If
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}<2\delta
, then
\alpha_{1}<2\delta
, and so
\alpha_{i}<2\delta
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,k
. Since
\delta<\frac{1}{16}
, we have
\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta\right)-2\delta>2\delta.
Since
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}=1>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta
, we get a contradiction since there exists
1\leq J\leq k
such that

2\delta\leq\sum_{1\leq j\leq J}\alpha_{j}\leq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta.

Therefore
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta
. Note also
\alpha_{2}
does not satisfy (C). If
\alpha_{2}<2\delta
, then
\alpha_{3},\ldots,\alpha_{k}<2\delta
. Since

\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}=1-\alpha_{1}>\frac{2}{3}-\frac{2}{3}\delta>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta

and
(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta)-2\delta>2\delta
, again we have a contradiction since there exists
2\leq J\leq k
such that

2\delta\leq\sum_{2\leq j\leq J}\alpha_{j}\leq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta.

Thus we must have
\alpha_{2}>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta
, so
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta
, so
\alpha_{3}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}<\frac{1}{3}+\frac{8}{3}\delta
. If
\alpha_{1}\leq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3}\delta
and
\alpha_{2}\leq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{5}{6}\delta
, then
\alpha_{3}\leq\alpha_{2}
implies
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}\leq 1-2\delta
, so
\alpha_{4}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}\geq 2\delta
. By failure of (C), we must have
\alpha_{4}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta
. But then this implies
\alpha_{3}<(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{8}{3}\delta)-(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta)=4\delta
, so
\alpha_{3}<2\delta
by failure of (C). Therefore,
\alpha_{4},\ldots,\alpha_{k}<2\delta
but we get a contradiction as before since
\alpha_{4}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta.
Thus we must have
\alpha_{1}>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3}\delta
or
\alpha_{2}>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{5}{6}\delta
. We have two cases.

•

Case 1:
\alpha_{1}>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3}\delta
. Then, we are done since

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}>\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3}\delta\right)+\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta\right)=\frac{2}{3}-\frac{5}{3}\delta\geq\frac{1}{2}+\delta

for
\delta\geq\frac{1}{16}
.

•

Case 2:
\alpha_{2}>\frac{1}{3}-\frac{5}{6}\delta
. Then,
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}
, so we are done since

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}>2\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{5}{6}\delta\right)=\frac{2}{3}-\frac{5}{3}\delta\geq\frac{1}{2}+\delta

for
\delta\geq\frac{1}{16}
.

Therefore, at least one of (A), (B) or (C) must hold, and we are done.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We first treat the main term. Here, our method diverges from
Topacogullari, 2018
since their argument is not sufficient for large
k
. Let
v:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a smooth function compactly supported on
[1,2]
, such that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}v\left(\frac{2^{j}u}{x^{\frac{1}{k}}}\right)=1

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and let
v_{j}(u):=v(2^{j}u/x^{\frac{1}{k}})
. Since there must exist at least one
a_{i}
with
a_{i}\leq x^{\frac{1}{k}}
, using Lemma
6.1
without loss of generality it suffices to compute

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int

\displaystyle w\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\\
d\mid a_{2}\cdots a_{k}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a_{2}\cdots a_{k}}v_{j}\left(\frac{u}{a_{2}\cdots a_{k}}\right)\lambda_{h,d}(u+h)\,\textup{d}u,

Let
g,g^{\prime}:\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be the functions

g(m,h):=\sum_{d\mid m}\frac{c_{d}(h)}{d},\quad g^{\prime}(m,h):=\sum_{d\mid m}\frac{c_{d}(h)\log d}{d},

and it suffices to estimate
\sum_{j}(Q_{1,j}-2\gamma Q_{2,j}-2Q_{3,j})
, where

\displaystyle Q_{1,j}

\displaystyle:=\int v_{j}(u)\sum_{m}d_{k-1}(m)g(m,h)w\left(\frac{um}{x}\right)\log(um+h)\,\textup{d}u,

\displaystyle Q_{2,j}

\displaystyle:=\int v_{j}(u)\sum_{m}d_{k-1}(m)g(m,h)w\left(\frac{um}{x}\right)\,\textup{d}u,

\displaystyle Q_{3,j}

\displaystyle:=\int v_{j}(u)\sum_{m}d_{k-1}(m)g^{\prime}(m,h)w\left(\frac{um}{x}\right)\,\textup{d}u.

We treat
Q_{1,j}
first. Let
W:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be given by

W(\xi):=w\left(\frac{\xi}{x}\right)\log(\xi+h),

and note
W
is a smooth and compactly supported function, with Mellin transform

\widetilde{W}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}w\left(\frac{\xi}{x}\right)\log(\xi+h)\xi^{s-1}\,\textup{d}\xi.

We mention that it is important for the weight function
w
to be smooth, since we require the Mellin transform to decay rapidly. By Mellin inversion, for any
\sigma>1
we have

\displaystyle Q_{1,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int v_{j}(u)\int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty}\widetilde{W}(s)\sum_{m}\frac{d_{k-1}(m)g(m,h)}{u^{s}m^{s}}\,\textup{d}s\,\textup{d}u

\displaystyle=:\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int v_{j}(u)\int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty}u^{-s}\widetilde{W}(s)D_{h}(s)\,\textup{d}s\,\textup{d}u.

Now, the main term of
Q_{1,j}
will be the residue of
D_{h}(s)
at
s=1
, so we wish to use the residue theorem to move the line of integration. We investigate the poles of
D_{h}(s)
by expanding into Euler products. First, from
Schwarz and Spilker, 1994
,
g
is multiplicative and for all
\alpha\geq 1
we have

g(p^{\alpha},h)=\sum_{0\leq k\leq\alpha}\frac{c_{p^{k}}(h)}{p^{k}}.

If
(p,h)=1
, then

c_{p^{k}}(h)=\begin{cases}1,&\quad\text{ for }k=0,\\
-1,&\quad\text{ for }k=1,\\
0,&\quad\text{ for }k\geq 2,\end{cases}

therefore in this case
g(p^{\alpha},h)=1-\frac{1}{p}
, and
g(1,h)=1
. If
p\mid h
, then

c_{p^{k}}(h)=\mu\left(\frac{p^{k}}{(p^{k},h)}\right)\frac{\varphi(p^{k})}{\varphi\left(\frac{p^{k}}{(p^{k},h)}\right)}.

Therefore, for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
we have

\displaystyle D_{h}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\frac{d_{k-1}(m)g(m,h)}{m^{s}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\sum_{\alpha\geq 0}\frac{d_{k-1}(p^{\alpha})g(p^{\alpha},h)}{p^{\alpha s}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\nmid h}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\sum_{\alpha\geq 1}\frac{d_{k-1}(p^{\alpha})}{p^{\alpha s}}\right)\underbrace{\prod_{p\mid h}\sum_{\alpha\geq 0}\frac{d_{k-1}(p^{\alpha})g(p^{\alpha},h)}{p^{\alpha s}}}_{=:E_{h}(s)}

\displaystyle=E_{h}(s)\prod_{p\nmid h}\left(\frac{1}{p}+\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)^{-(k-1)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right).

Here
E_{h}(s)
is a finite product of non-vanishing holomorphic functions, so we focus mainly on the infinite product. Now observe

\displaystyle P_{h}(s):=\zeta(s)^{-(k-1)}D_{h}(s)

\displaystyle=E_{h}(s)\prod_{p\mid h}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)^{k-1}\prod_{p\nmid h}\left(\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)^{k-1}+1-\frac{1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=E_{h}(s)\prod_{p\mid h}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)^{k-1}\prod_{p\nmid h}\left(1-\frac{k-1}{p^{s+1}}+O(p^{-2s-1})\right).

Note
P_{h}(s)
is holomorphic for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1/2
, and so
D_{h}(s)
has a pole of order
k-1
at
s=1
. Using the residue theorem, for
\sigma^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon
we have

Q_{1,j}=\int v_{j}(u)\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}u^{-s}\widetilde{W}(s)D_{h}(s)\,\textup{d}u+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int v_{j}(u)\int_{\sigma^{\prime}-i\infty}^{\sigma^{\prime}+i\infty}u^{-s}\widetilde{W}(s)D_{h}(s)\,\textup{d}s\,\textup{d}u.

Note

u^{-s}\widetilde{W}(s)D_{h}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\xi}w\left(\frac{\xi}{x}\right)\log(\xi+h)\left[\left(\frac{\xi}{u}\right)^{s}D_{h}(s)\right]\,\textup{d}u,

and observe

\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\left(\frac{\xi}{u}\right)^{s}D_{h}(s)=\frac{\xi}{u}P_{k-2}(\log\xi,\log u),

where
P_{k-1}(X,Y)
is a polynomial of degree
k-1
. Thus the main term of
Q_{1,j}
is

\int_{\mathbb{R}}w\left(\frac{\xi}{x}\right)\log(\xi+h)\int\frac{v_{j}(u)}{u}P_{k-2}(\log\xi,\log u)\,\textup{d}u\,\textup{d}\xi.

If we sum over
j
and bring the summation inside, we get the desired main term

\int_{\mathbb{R}}w\left(\frac{\xi}{x}\right)P_{k}(\log x,\log\xi,\log(\xi+h))\,\textup{d}\xi.

It remains to bound the error term. Using decay estimates for
\widetilde{W}(s)
and
|\zeta(\sigma^{\prime}+it)|\ll|t|^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}
for
t\geq 1
, we have

\left|\sum_{j}Q_{1,j}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}w\left(\frac{\xi}{x}\right)P_{k}(\log x,\log\xi,\log(\xi+h))\,\textup{d}\xi\right|\ll x^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2k}+\varepsilon}.

A very similar argument applies to
Q_{2,j}
and
Q_{3,j}
. We omit the argument for
Q_{2,j}
, but we mention key modifications in the argument for
Q_{3,j}
since
g^{\prime}(\cdot,h)
is not multiplicative. For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, let

g_{\beta}(m,h):=\sum_{d\mid m}\frac{c_{d}(h)}{d^{\beta}},

Then,

g^{\prime}(m,h)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}g_{\beta}(m,h)\Biggr|_{\beta=1},

and so for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
, we have

\widetilde{D}_{h}(s):=\sum_{m}\frac{d_{k-1}(m)g^{\prime}(m,h)}{m^{s}}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\left(\sum_{m}\frac{d_{k-1}(m)g_{\beta}(m,h)}{m^{s}}\right)\Biggr|_{\beta=1},

and let
D_{h,\beta}(s)
be the inner sum. As before, we analyse the poles of
D_{h,\beta}(s)
. Analogous to
g
, it can be shown that
g_{\beta}(\cdot,h)
is multiplicative with analogous properties as
g
, in particular

g_{\beta}(1,h)=1,\quad g_{\beta}(p^{\alpha},h)=1-\frac{1}{p^{\beta}}

for
\alpha\geq 1
and
p\nmid h
. Therefore, as before we have

D_{h,\beta}(s)=E_{h,\beta}(s)\prod_{p\nmid h}\left(\frac{1}{p^{\beta}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)^{-(k-1)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\beta}}\right)\right),

where
E_{h,\beta}(s)
is a finite product of non-vanishing holomorphic functions. Taking the logarithmic derivative, we have

\frac{1}{D_{h,\beta}(s)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}D_{h,\beta}(s)\Biggr|_{\beta=1}=\frac{1}{E_{h,\beta}(s)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}E_{h,\beta}(s)\Biggr|_{\beta=1}-\sum_{p\nmid h}\frac{\log p}{p}\cdot\frac{1-\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)^{-(k-1)}}{\frac{1}{p}+\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)^{-(k-1)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}

The right hand side is analytic for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>\frac{1}{2}
, so
\widetilde{D}_{h}(s)
has an order
k-1
pole at
s=1
. The rest of the argument follows analogously to above, contributing a degree
k-1
polynomial. Combining all contributions from
Q_{1,j},Q_{2,j},Q_{3,j}
, we proved the main term is

xP_{k,h,w}(\log x)+O_{w,\varepsilon,k}(x^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2k}+\varepsilon}).

To treat the required error term from Lemma
6.1
, the idea is to dyadically split the sum over
a_{1}\cdots a_{k}\asymp x
to a sum over
a_{i}\asymp A_{i}
with
A_{1}\cdots A_{k}\asymp X
, and we use the partition in Lemma
6.2
along with (
6.1
), (
6.2
), and (
6.3
). We set
A_{1}\geq A_{2}\geq\cdots\geq A_{k}
, and let

X_{1}=x^{\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{3}\delta},\quad X_{2}=x^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta},\quad X_{3}=x^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{3}\delta},\quad X_{4}=x^{2\delta}.

If
A_{1}\gg X_{1}
, then use (
6.1
) to get

R_{v_{1}}\ll x^{1-\delta+\varepsilon}.

If
A_{1}A_{2}\gg X_{2}
, then use (
6.2
) to get

R_{v_{1}}\ll x^{1-\delta+\varepsilon}\left(1+x^{2\theta\delta}\right)\left(1+1\right)\left(1+\frac{|h|^{\frac{1}{4}}}{x^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\delta}}\right)\ll x^{1-\delta+2\delta\theta+\varepsilon}\left(1+\frac{|h|^{\frac{1}{4}}}{x^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\delta}}\right).

Otherwise, there is an non-empty index set
I\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
such that
X_{4}\ll\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}\ll X_{3}
. For convenience let
A=\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}
, so using (
6.3
), we get

\displaystyle R_{v_{1}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x^{1+\varepsilon}}{X_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+X_{3}^{\frac{3}{4}}x^{\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon}\left(|h|^{\frac{\theta}{2}}X_{3}^{\frac{\theta}{2}}+\frac{x^{\frac{\theta}{2}}}{X_{3}^{\frac{\theta}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=x^{1-\delta+\varepsilon}+x^{1-\delta+\varepsilon}(|h|^{\frac{\theta}{2}}x^{\frac{\theta}{6}-\frac{2\delta}{3}\theta}+x^{\frac{\theta}{3}+\frac{2\delta}{3}\theta})

\displaystyle\ll x^{1-\delta+\frac{\theta}{3}+\frac{2\delta}{3}\theta+\varepsilon}\left(1+\frac{|h|^{\frac{\theta}{2}}}{x^{\frac{\theta}{6}+\frac{4\delta}{3}\theta}}\right).

Combining the above bounds, we are done.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
w:[1/2,1]\to[0,\infty)
be a smooth and compactly supported function and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then for
0\leq\delta\leq\frac{1}{16}
we have the following estimates for different ranges of
h\in\mathbb{N}
.

(a)

For
|h|\leq x^{\frac{1}{3}+\frac{8\delta}{3}\theta}
, we have

\sum_{n}w\left(\frac{n}{x}\right)d_{k}(n)d(n\pm h)=xP_{k,h,w}(\log x)+O_{k,w,\delta,\varepsilon}(x^{1-\delta+\frac{1+2\delta}{3}\theta+\varepsilon}).

(b)

For
x^{\frac{1}{3}+\frac{8\delta}{3}\theta}\leq|h|\leq x^{\frac{1-2\delta+\frac{2(1-16\delta)}{3}\theta}{1-2\theta}}
, we have

\sum_{n}w\left(\frac{n}{x}\right)d_{k}(n)d(n\pm h)=xP_{k,h,w}(\log x)+O_{k,w,\delta,\varepsilon}(|h|^{\frac{\theta}{2}}x^{1-\delta+\frac{1-4\delta}{6}\theta+\varepsilon}).

(c)

For
x^{\frac{1-2\delta+\frac{2(1-16\delta)}{3}\theta}{1-2\theta}}\leq|h|\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}
, we have

\sum_{n}w\left(\frac{n}{x}\right)d_{k}(n)d(n\pm h)=xP_{k,h,w}(\log x)+O_{k,w,\delta,\varepsilon}(|h|^{\frac{1}{4}}x^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}+2\delta\theta+\varepsilon}).

We can choose
\delta=1/16
and
\theta\leq 7/64
from
Kim and Sarnak, 2003
. If we have
\theta=0
from the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture, then all three ranges above can be used and are all non-trivial.

.

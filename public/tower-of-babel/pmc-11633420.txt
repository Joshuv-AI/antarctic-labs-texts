Characterization and phylogenetic analysis of the complete chloroplast genome sequence of Dendrobium hainanense (Orchidaceae)
Authors: Lei Zhao, Peizhang Chen, Wenchang Li, Zhe Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, we report the chloroplast (cp) genome of Dendrobium hainanense. The cp genome was 151,367 bp in length; the overall CG content of the genome was 37.46%. The length of the large single-copy (LSC) region was 83,938 bp, and 15,829 bp in a small single-copy (SSC) region, and a pair of inverted repeats (IRs) of 12,900 bp. One hundred and twenty-seven genes were predicted, including 81 protein-coding genes, 38 genes for tRNA, and eight genes for rRNA. The results of phylogenetic tree showed that Dendrobium hainanense had the closest genetic relationship with Dendrobium parciflorum. This study will be help to understand the genetic diversity of Dendrobium.Keywords: Dendrobium hainanense, complete chloroplast genome, next-generation sequencing, Orchidaceae

Introduction

Dendrobium is one of the three largest genera of Orchidaceae with more than 1500 species worldwide (Zhu et al. 2009). It is native of Tropical & Subtropical Asia to Pacific and New Zealand (Jin et al. 2019). Dendrobium has high ornamental value and is widely used in the global flower trade, at the same time, part of them are important materials of traditional Asian medicine, especially in China (Teoh 2016). In order to prevent the over-exploitation of wild Dendrobium species, all Dendrobium species in China are protected plants under legal protection, and are also protected internationally by the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora (CITES). D. hainanense (Rolfe 1896) is distributed in Hainan, Hong Kong, Vietnam, and Thailand which has cylindrical leaves and small white flowers (Figure 1; Chen et al. 2009). It is morphologically similar to Dendrobium parciflorum (Rchb. f. ex Lindl.), which belong to the Sect. Strongyle (Zhu et al. 2009). In this study, the complete chloroplast (cp) genome of D. hainanense was reported in order to provide molecular data for the systematic study in Dendrobium. The aim of this study was to supplement the data on the cp genome of Dendrobium, to analyze the differences between the cp genomes of Dendrobium hainanense and other orchids of Dendrobium, and to try to explain the reasons for the discrepancy.

Materials and methods

The leaf of D. hainanense samples was collected from the Bawangling region of the Hainan Tropical Rainforest National Park, China (19°4′19″N, 109°13′22″E). With the approval of the Bawangling region administration, we collected the newly grown leaves of the plants and ensured they were healthy and intact, free from insects and fungus, then use silica gel desiccant for storage. A voucher specimen was deposited at the Teaching Herbarium (HUFB) of the College of Forestry, Hainan University (Zhang Zhe, 107189517@qq.com, HUFB 2022718).

We used the modified CTAB protocol to extract the complete cp genome of D. hainanense, then sequenced it by next-generation sequencing through the Illumina NovaSeq 6000. Genome sequences were screened out and assembled with GetOrganelle (Jin et al. 2020). The assembled cp genome was annotated using the online annotation software GeSeq (https://chlorobox.mpimp-golm.mpg.de/geseq.html) (Tillich et al. 2017) and CpGAVAS (http://www.herbalgenomics.org/cpgavas/) (Liu et al. 2012) to determine the starting position of the cp genome and the IR region, and annotate the genes on it. The raw data have been submitted to the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) database under the accession number SRR18136623.

To investigate the phylogenetic position of D. hainanense, the complete cp genome sequences of D. hainanense, other 16 Dendrobium species and Goodyera velutina (Maxim 1867), G. procera (Hook 1823) as outgroups were aligned using MAFFT Online version (Katoh and Standley 2013). Maximum-likelihood (ML) tree was constructed by MAGE 11, with 1000 bootstrap replicates (Figure 2; Tamura et al. 2021).

The bootstrap values are shown at the nodes, and the species and GenBank accession number are shown at the end of each branch. The horizontal scale represents genetic distance, with a unit of 0.01. This tree provides a detailed insight into the phylogenetic relationships among Dendrobium species, highlighting the genetic divergence within the genus and facilitating the study of their evolutionary history and genetic relationships. The species were grouped as follows: Sect. Dendrobium includes D. wilsonii (LC490389) (Li et al. 2020), D. huoshanense (LC490378) (Li et al. 2020), D. fanjingshanense (LC490654) (Li et al. 2020), D. moniliforme (LC490387) (Li et al. 2020), D. devonianum (LC490385) (Li et al. 2020), D. aphyllum (NC035322) (Zhitao et al. 2017), D. loddigesii (LC192960) (unpublished), D. lohohense (LC490670) (Li et al. 2020), D. moschatum (NC061572) (Yang et al. 2022), D. fimbriatum (LC193521) (Zhitao et al. 2017), and D. hancockii (LC500592) (Hou et al. 2019); Sect. Formosae includes D. wattii (NC045856) (Wang et al. 2020); Sect. Crumenata includes D. exile (LC193522) (Zhitao et al. 2017); Sect. Aporum includes D. spatella (LC193511) (Zhitao et al. 2017); and Sect. Strongyle includes D. hainanense (OM792978) (highlighted in red) and D. parciflorum (LC193512) (Zhitao et al. 2017). The outgroups were Goodyera velutina (OM314916) (Kim and Kim 2022) and Goodyera procera (NC029363) (unpublished).

To verify the accuracy of the assembly, trimmed raw sequence data were mapped to the assembled cp genome (Supplementary Figure 1). The cis-spliced genes and one trans-spliced gene were verified to be corrected and annotated use CPGview (Supplementary Figure 2).

Results

The complete cp genome sequence of D. hainanense (GenBank accession no. OM792978) was 151,367 bp in length, and including a large single-copy (LSC) region of 83,938 bp in length, two short inverted repeat (IRa and IRb) regions of 25,800 bp in length, and a small single-copy (SSC) region of 15,829 bp. The GC content of the whole cp genome was 37.46%. The cpDNA of D. hainanense consisted of 127 distinct genes, including 81 protein-coding genes, eight genes for ribosomal RNA, and 38 genes for transfer RNA (Figure 3). Ten cis-spliced genes (rps16, atpF, rpoC1, ycf3, clpP, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, and ndhB) were verified to be corrected and annotated with multiple sequence alignment; ndhB and rpl2 were each repeated once (Supplementary Figure 2). In the cp genome of D. hainanense, the rps12 gene exhibits a characteristic split structure, a feature commonly observed in plant cp genomes. As indicated in Figure 3, the rps12 gene consists of two distinct segments located in separate regions of the genome, suggesting trans-splicing – a process in which exons located in different regions are joined together post-transcriptionally to form a functional mRNA.

The phylogenetic analysis showed that all species can be classified into six section, Sect. Dendrobium, Sect. Formosae, Sect. Crumenata, Sect. Aporum, Sect. Strongyle, and Outgroups (Figure 2). D. hainanense is taxonomically positioned within Sect. Strongyle and is closely affiliated with D. parciflorum, as evidenced by their shared branch with a bootstrap support value of 100, signifying a robust phylogenetic relationship. Furthermore, D. hainanense exhibits a significant phylogenetic proximity to D. spatella, demonstrated by their inclusion within the same well-supported clade in the phylogenetic tree, which also possesses a bootstrap support value of 100, thereby underscoring their intimate evolutionary connection.

Discussion and conclusions

The complete cp genome of D. hainanense was sequenced and analyzed. Our results show that the complete cp genome of D. hainanense displays a typical quadripartite structure and shows relative conservation in genome size and gene content (Green 2011). Chloroplast genomes have been widely used to study phylogenetic relationships. Previous phylogenetic studies of the genus Dendrobium have shown that D. hainanense is closely related to Dendrobium parciflorum based on the complete sequences of the cp genome. The phylogenetic relationships of the genus Dendrobium will become clearer as more species are sequenced. This study provides basic information for the distribution, utilization and phylogenetic and evolutionary studies of D. hainanense. Sequencing the complete cp genome of D. hainanense is beneficial for refining the Dendrobium gene database and provides indispensable data for future research on the phylogeny and molecular breeding of D. hainanense.

In conclusion, this study has contributed to cp genome resources for the family Orchidaceae and the genus Dendrobium. These advancements are expected to facilitate future genetic investigations aimed at the conservation and exploitation of D. hainanense. This will play an important role in the identification of genus Dendrobium species and population genetic studies.

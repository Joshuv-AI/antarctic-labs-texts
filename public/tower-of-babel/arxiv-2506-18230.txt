Unveiling the Pitfalls of CPL Parametrization at High Redshifts: A Critical Assessment of the ω 0 ​ ω a ​ CDM \omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM} Model with DESI DR2 BAO Data
Seokcheon Lee

Abstract
We critically assess the ω 0 ​ ω a ​ CDM \omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM} model, employing the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) parametrization ( ω ⁡ ( z ) = ω 0 + ω a ​ z 1 + z \omega(z)=\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}\frac{z}{1+z} ), against Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) measurements from DESI Data Release 2 (DR2). While DESI DR2 BAO observations are broadly consistent with the standard Λ ​ CDM \Lambda\mathrm{CDM} model, yielding a matter density parameter Ω m0 ≈ 0.3 \Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}\approx 0.3 , our Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) fits reveal significant variations in best-fit parameters and degeneracy directions for the ω 0 ​ ω a ​ CDM \omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM} model across different combinations of BAO observables ( D V / r d D_{V}/r_{\mathrm{d}} , D M / r d D_{M}/r_{\mathrm{d}} , D H / r d D_{H}/r_{\mathrm{d}} , and D M / D H D_{M}/D_{H} ). Crucially, when constrained solely by DESI DR2 BAO data, the ω 0 ​ ω a ​ CDM \omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM} model exhibits fundamental inconsistencies in its predictions. Unlike the Λ \Lambda CDM and ω \omega CDM models, which consistently align with observations across all BAO types, ω 0 ​ ω a ​ CDM \omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM} shows strikingly divergent behavior: systematically predicting lower values for r d r_{\mathrm{d}} -dependent observables, and conversely higher values for D M / D H D_{M}/D_{H} . This conflicting behavior, along with observed unphysical parameter compensation at high redshifts (even for D M / D H D_{M}/D_{H} ), is attributed to the inherent limitations of the CPL parametrization, particularly its performance with high-redshift-sensitive data. The model attempts to compensate for poor individual fits by forcing an artificially inflated Ω m0 \Omega_{\mathrm{m0}} and a large negative ω a \omega_{a} , resulting in a best-fit that deviates significantly from a consistent physical representation. Our findings underscore that DESI DR2 BAO data alone struggles to robustly constrain ω 0 ​ ω a ​ CDM \omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM} due to these model-dependent degeneracies and CPL parametrization pitfalls. Specifically, the CPL parametrization’s limitations at high redshifts affect not only r d r_{\mathrm{d}} -dependent observables but also ratio observables like D M / D H D_{M}/D_{H} . We suggest that applying dynamic dark energy parametrizations to individual D V D_{V} , D M D_{M} , and D H D_{H} measurements might offer a more robust approach than relying on ratio observables, particularly for constraining ω a \omega_{a} . Ultimately, our study highlights the necessity for significant caution when employing approximate dark energy parametrizations such as CPL, especially in analyses involving data sensitive to a wide range of redshifts.

II
DESI DR2 BAO Data and the
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
Model

In this study, we utilize BAO measurements from the DESI DR2
DESI:2025zgx
. DESI DR2 provides high-precision BAO measurements at multiple redshifts, serving as crucial standard rulers to probe the expansion history of the Universe. Specifically, we consider the isotropic volume-averaged angular diameter distance and Hubble parameter combination,
D_{V}(z)/r_{\mathrm{d}}
, and the anisotropic measurements of the angular diameter distance,
D_{M}(z)/r_{\mathrm{d}}
, and the Hubble parameter,
D_{H}(z)/r_{\mathrm{d}}
, across various redshift bins. Additionally, we include the
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-independent observable,
D_{M}(z)/D_{H}(z)
. These observables are defined as:

\displaystyle D_{H}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{H(z)}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle D_{M}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{H_{0}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dz^{\prime}}{E(z^{\prime})}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle D_{V}(z)

\displaystyle=\left[D_{M}(z)^{2}\frac{cz}{H(z)}\right]^{1/3}\,,

(3)

where
H(z)
is the Hubble parameter at redshift
z
, and
H_{0}
is the Hubble constant.
r_{\mathrm{d}}
represents the comoving sound horizon at the drag epoch, a scale determined by the physics of the early Universe before recombination. Its explicit form is given by:

r_{\mathrm{d}}=\frac{c}{H_{0}}\int_{0}^{z_{d}}\frac{dz^{\prime}}{E(z^{\prime})\sqrt{3(1+R(z^{\prime}))}}\,,

(4)

where
R(z)=\frac{3\Omega_{b0}}{4\Omega_{\gamma 0}(1+z)}
is the ratio of baryon to photon energy densities, and
z_{d}
is the redshift at the drag epoch. The ratio
D_{M}(z)/D_{H}(z)
provides information primarily on the anisotropic part of the BAO signal, being independent of
r_{\mathrm{d}}
.

Our primary cosmological model for investigation is the flat
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model, which extends the standard
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
model by allowing the dark energy equation of state,
\omega(z)
, to evolve with redshift. This evolution is parametrized using the CPL form
Chevallier:2000qy
;
Linder:2002et

\omega(z)=\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}\frac{z}{1+z}\,.

(5)

In this model, the evolution of the Hubble parameter
H(z)
is given by:

H(z)=H_{0}E(z)=H_{0}\left[\Omega_{\mathrm{r0}}(1+z)^{4}+\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}(1+z)^{3}+(1-\Omega_{\mathrm{r0}}-\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}})\exp\left(3\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1+w(z^{\prime})}{1+z^{\prime}}dz^{\prime}\right)\right]^{1/2}\,.

(6)

For a flat Universe, the density parameters sum to unity at present:
\Omega_{\mathrm{r0}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{de0}}=1
, where
\Omega_{\mathrm{de0}}
is the present-day dark energy density parameter. The parameters constrained in our analysis are the present-day matter density parameter
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}

\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}

Each of the BAO observables provides unique information regarding the expansion history and the properties of dark energy.

D_{H}(z)/r_{\mathrm{d}}

D_{M}(z)/D_{H}(z)

H(z)
and thus to the evolution of dark energy,
\omega(z)
. However, as we will discuss, the application of an approximate parametrization like CPL to these observables, especially at high redshifts, can lead to complex and sometimes unphysical behaviors of the inferred parameters.

III
Methodology: MCMC Parameter Inference

Our cosmological parameter inference is performed using a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach, which allows for the efficient exploration of the multi-dimensional parameter space and provides the full posterior probability distributions for the model parameters. This Bayesian statistical inference method compares theoretical predictions of BAO observables with the observed DESI DR2 data.

The likelihood function,
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p}|\mathbf{d})
, is constructed based on the
\chi^{2}
statistic, assuming a Gaussian likelihood for the BAO measurements:

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p}|\mathbf{d})\propto\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\chi^{2}(\mathbf{p})\right)

(7)

where
\mathbf{p}
represents the set of cosmological parameters (
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}},\omega_{0},\omega_{a}
) and
\mathbf{d}
denotes the DESI DR2 BAO data vector. The
\chi^{2}
is defined as:

\chi^{2}(\mathbf{p})=\sum_{i}\frac{(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\mathrm{th}}(\mathbf{p}))^{2}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}

(8)

Here,
\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\mathrm{obs}}
are the observed BAO values from DESI DR2,
\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\mathrm{th}}(\mathbf{p})
are the theoretical predictions for these observables based on the chosen cosmological model and parameters
\mathbf{p}
, and
\sigma_{i}
are their corresponding uncertainties. For correlated measurements, the
\chi^{2}
definition would involve the full covariance matrix:
\chi^{2}(\mathbf{p})=(\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{th}}(\mathbf{p}))^{T}\mathbf{C}^{-1}(\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{th}}(\mathbf{p}))
, where
\mathbf{C}
is the covariance matrix. We adopt the covariance matrices provided by the DESI collaboration for the respective combinations of observables
Lee:2025j23
.

For our parameter estimation, we impose uniform (flat) prior distributions on the cosmological parameters to avoid biasing the results. The ranges for these priors are chosen to be sufficiently wide to encompass physically plausible values and allow for broad exploration of the parameter space. Specific prior ranges are detailed in our analysis (e.g.,
0.1<\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}<0.6
,
-3.0<\omega_{0}<1.0
,
-5.0<\omega_{a}<2.0
).

VI.2
Interpretation: Inconsistency and CPL Parametrization Pitfalls

The striking and often conflicting behavior of the
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model across different types of DESI DR2 BAO observables (predicting lower values for
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent observables and conversely higher values for
D_{M}/D_{H}
) is a critical finding. This suggests an underlying inconsistency in how the model, particularly with the CPL parametrization, attempts to fit the entire dataset. Our analysis attributes this behavior to the inherent limitations of the CPL parametrization when applied to data sensitive to high redshifts, especially in the context of observables dependent on the sound horizon,
r_{\mathrm{d}}
.

•

CPL Parametrization’s Behavior at High Redshifts:

The CPL parametrization,
\omega(z)=\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}\frac{z}{1+z}
, is widely recognized as an effective approximation for the dark energy equation of state primarily at low to intermediate redshifts. However, at significantly high redshifts (as
z\to\infty
), the term
\frac{z}{1+z}
approaches 1, meaning
w(z)
asymptotes to
\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}
. This linear approximation may not accurately capture the true behavior of dark energy in the very early Universe. As illustrated conceptually in Figure
8
, an
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model with the best-fit parameters obtained from DESI-only analyses (e.g., a large negative
\omega_{a}
) implies a
w(z)
that deviates significantly from
-1
at high redshifts.

•

Impact on
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent Observables (
D_{V}/r_{\mathrm{d}},D_{M}/r_{\mathrm{d}},D_{H}/r_{\mathrm{d}}
):

The sound horizon scale,
r_{\mathrm{d}}
, is a fossil of the early Universe, determined by the physics around the drag epoch (high redshifts,
z_{d}\sim 1020-1060
). Therefore, BAO observables that are explicitly dependent on
r_{\mathrm{d}}
strongly constrain the integrated expansion history up to these early times. When fitting
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent data with the CPL parametrization, a common degeneracy arises where a large negative
\omega_{a}
is favored to compensate for the model’s behavior at high
z
. This large negative
\omega_{a}
implies a dark energy density that decreases more rapidly at high redshifts than that of a cosmological constant. To then maintain consistency with the observed low-redshift expansion and current matter density, the best-fit typically results in a higher present-day matter density,
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
. This is consistent with our MCMC results from Scenario 1 (Section
III.1
), where we found a relatively high
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
(
\approx 0.3594
) and a substantial negative
\omega_{a}
(
\approx-2.1581
).
Consequently, this combination of a larger
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
and a rapidly decreasing effective dark energy density at high
z
for the best-fit
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
parameters leads to systematically lower predicted values for
D_{V}/r_{\mathrm{d}}
,
D_{M}/r_{\mathrm{d}}
, and
D_{H}/r_{\mathrm{d}}
compared to
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
/
\omega\mathrm{CDM}
models and the DESI DR2 observations (Figures
4
,
5
,
6
). This directly explains the observed systematic shift towards smaller values for
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
in these figures.

•

Behavior of
D_{M}/D_{H}
and its Compensation with
\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}\approx 0
:

In contrast to
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent observables,
D_{M}/D_{H}
is a dimensionless ratio that is independent of
r_{\mathrm{d}}
and is sensitive to the shape of the expansion history
H(z)
at the redshift of observation. It more directly probes the evolution of dark energy,
w(z)
, predominantly in the lower-to-intermediate redshift ranges covered by BAO. For the
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model, the best-fit parameters for
D_{M}/D_{H}
alone (Scenario 2, Section
III.2
) can drive
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
towards values where their sum (
\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}
) approaches zero, which corresponds to a near-constant effective dark energy equation of state at high redshifts. As shown in Figure
9
, this effectively makes
w(z)
close to
-1
at high redshifts, allowing the model to fit the
D_{M}/D_{H}
observable well. This compensation leads to a predicted
D_{M}/D_{H}
value that can be larger than
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}
/
\omega\mathrm{CDM}
predictions, aligning with the DESI observations (Figure
7
). This opposite trend for
D_{M}/D_{H}
further highlights the distinct nature of its constraints and how the CPL parametrization attempts to accommodate them.

•

The “Compromise” Best-fit and Model Consistency Issues:

The observation that
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
best-fit parameters derived from DESI DR2 BAO data alone yield systematically conflicting predictions for
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent and
D_{M}/D_{H}
observables (i.e., lower for the former, higher for the latter) is indeed highly suggestive of an underlying physical inconsistency. This situation, particularly evident in the combined fit (Scenario 3, Section
III.3
), demonstrates that the
\chi^{2}
minimization process, when dealing with strong degeneracies and a flexible model like
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
, can find a parameter combination that minimizes the total
\chi^{2}
by effectively compensating for poor fits in one observable set with potentially better fits in another. This leads to a best-fit that is a ‘compromise’ rather than a consistent physical representation of dark energy evolution across all redshift ranges probed by the data. The elevated
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
and large negative
\omega_{a}
values from BAO-only
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
analyses thus appear to be artifacts stemming from the CPL parametrization’s inability to accurately describe dark energy behavior at high redshifts, especially in the absence of strong high-redshift priors (e.g., from CMB) that would anchor the
\omega_{0}-\omega_{a}
degeneracy.

•

The Role of
D_{M}/D_{H}
:

This analysis does not imply that
D_{M}/D_{H}
should be excluded from DESI DR2 data analysis. On the contrary,
D_{M}/D_{H}
provides crucial,
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-independent information about dark energy evolution. The true problem lies in the consistency of the
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model’s best-fit parameters across the entire DESI DR2 BAO dataset when the CPL parametrization is used. If a model is physically robust, its best-fit parameters should consistently explain all relevant observations simultaneously. Our results indicate that the current DESI DR2 BAO-only
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
best-fit does not meet this criterion of consistent prediction across all BAO observables.

VII
Discussion

Our comprehensive analysis of the
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model constrained by DESI DR2 BAO data has revealed several crucial insights into the interplay between dark energy parametrization, observational data, and cosmological parameter inference. This section elaborates on the fundamental limitations of the CPL parametrization, the implications of BAO observable selection, the challenges posed by parameter degeneracies, and the ultimate necessity of combining multiple cosmological probes.

VII.1
Revisiting the Fundamental Limitations of CPL Parametrization

As demonstrated in Section
VI
, the
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model, when constrained solely by DESI DR2 BAO data, exhibits a concerning inconsistency in its predictions for
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent observables (
D_{V}/r_{\mathrm{d}},D_{M}/r_{\mathrm{d}},D_{H}/r_{\mathrm{d}}
) versus the
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-independent ratio observable (
D_{M}/D_{H}
). This paradoxical behavior, where
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent observables systematically predict lower values and
D_{M}/D_{H}
predicts higher values compared to observations, is primarily attributed to the inherent limitations of the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) parametrization at high redshifts.

The CPL form,
w(z)=\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}\frac{z}{1+z}
, is an effective two-parameter linear approximation for
w(z)
. While it performs well at low redshifts, its behavior at very high redshifts (as
z\to\infty
, where
\frac{z}{1+z}\to 1
) causes
w(z)
to asymptote to
\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}
. For typical best-fit parameters obtained from BAO-only analyses (e.g., large negative
\omega_{a}
), this asymptotic value can deviate significantly from
w=-1
. This behavior fundamentally conflicts with the expectation that dark energy’s influence should diminish at high redshifts, allowing the Universe to be dominated by radiation and matter.

When fitting
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent observables, which are intrinsically tied to the physics of the early Universe (e.g., the sound horizon
r_{\mathrm{d}}
formed at
z_{d}\sim 1000
), the CPL parametrization struggles to accurately capture the dark energy evolution at these early times. To compensate for this inadequacy, the
\chi^{2}
minimization process often drives
\omega_{a}
to large negative values. This creates a dark energy density that decreases much more rapidly than that of a cosmological constant at high
z
. To maintain overall consistency with the observed expansion history, this rapid decrease must be compensated by an artificially inflated present-day matter density,
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
. This is precisely what we observed in Scenario 1 (Section
III.1
), where
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
was pushed to values higher than the fiducial
\Lambda
CDM value, accompanied by a substantial negative
\omega_{a}
. Such a parameter combination, while mathematically minimizing
\chi^{2}
, can represent an unphysical ‘compensation’ rather than a true physical description of dark energy.

VII.2
The Importance of BAO Observable Selection

Our analysis further highlights the critical importance of carefully selecting BAO observables when constraining dynamic dark energy models. The
D_{M}/D_{H}
observable, being independent of
r_{\mathrm{d}}
, carries distinct information content compared to
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent observables. As shown in Scenario 2 (Section
III.2
), constraining with
D_{M}/D_{H}
alone leads to a different parameter degeneracy direction and best-fit values, with
\omega_{0}
closer to zero and an even more extreme negative
\omega_{a}
.

While
D_{M}/D_{H}
is a valuable
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-independent probe that is highly sensitive to the shape of
H(z)
and thus to the evolution of
w(z)
, its constraints are primarily driven by the relative evolution of
D_{M}(z)
and
D_{H}(z)
at specific redshifts. For the CPL parametrization, a phenomenon arises where different
(\omega_{0},\omega_{a})
combinations can yield similar values for
w(z)
(and thus
D_{M}/D_{H}
) by forcing
\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}\approx 0
at high redshifts (Figure
9
). This means that even
D_{M}/D_{H}
, despite its
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-independence, is not immune to parameter compensation effects within the CPL framework, potentially masking the true dark energy behavior at high redshifts or leading to broad, unphysical preferred regions in the parameter space.

Given these challenges, we propose that applying dynamic dark energy parametrizations directly to individual
D_{V}
,
D_{M}
, and
D_{H}
measurements, perhaps with some external prior on
r_{\mathrm{d}}
(e.g., from CMB), might be a more robust approach than relying solely on ratios like
D_{M}/D_{H}
or including it in a way that allows it to dominate degeneracies. While
D_{M}/D_{H}
is a powerful discriminator, its specific sensitivity might, in the context of an imperfect parametrization, lead to biased parameter estimations or hide inconsistencies. Using individual distance measures could potentially offer a clearer view of the expansion history without forcing an artificial compensation within the CPL form.

VII.3
Impact of Parameter Degeneracies

Our results, particularly when comparing the corner plots from Scenarios 1, 2, and 3 (Figures
1
,
2
,
3
), clearly illustrate the severe impact of parameter degeneracies in complex dark energy models like
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
. The CPL parametrization introduces significant correlations between
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
,
\omega_{0}
, and
\omega_{a}
. Different types of BAO observables have distinct degeneracy directions in the parameter space. When these are combined, the
\chi^{2}
minimization algorithm seeks a ’compromise’ solution that best fits all data points simultaneously.

This compromise solution, as observed in Scenario 3 (
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}\approx 0.3441
,
\omega_{0}\approx-0.3722
,
\omega_{a}\approx-2.4460
), is an average of conflicting constraints. It does not necessarily represent a physically consistent set of parameters that accurately describe dark energy across all epochs. Instead, it indicates that a single dataset, even one as precise as DESI DR2 BAO, struggles to robustly constrain models with strong parameter correlations and inherent parametrization limitations. The broad uncertainties and the ‘pulled’ best-fit values are direct consequences of these degeneracies.

VII.4
The Necessity of Multi-probe Combination

Ultimately, our findings underscore a critical aspect of modern cosmology: the limitations of constraining flexible dark energy models using a single cosmological probe. While DESI DR2 BAO provides excellent geometric measurements of the expansion history, its parameter constraints on the
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model are significantly affected by the CPL parametrization’s shortcomings at high redshifts and the strong parameter degeneracies inherent in the model.

To break these degeneracies and obtain more robust, consistent, and reliable constraints on dynamic dark energy models like
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
, it is essential to combine BAO data with other complementary cosmological probes. The Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), for instance, provides crucial information about the early Universe, including precise measurements of
\Omega_{b0}h^{2}
,
\Omega_{c0}h^{2}
, and the sound horizon
r_{\mathrm{d}}
itself. By anchoring the high-redshift physics, CMB data can significantly tighten constraints on
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
and drastically reduce the degeneracy between
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
, especially by providing a strong prior on
r_{\mathrm{d}}
. Similarly, Type Ia Supernovae (SNe Ia) provide complementary constraints on the low-redshift expansion history.

Therefore, while our study meticulously analyzes the challenges posed by CPL parametrization and BAO-only constraints, it points to the broader conclusion that a joint analysis with a diverse set of cosmological data (CMB, SNe Ia, other galaxy surveys) is indispensable for truly unveiling the nature of dark energy and disentangling the effects of model parametrization from genuine cosmological signals. This approach will allow for more consistent parameter estimates and a more reliable test of the validity of dynamic dark energy models.

VIII
Conclusion

In this study, we performed a comprehensive analysis of the flat
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
cosmological model using Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) data from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) Data Release 2 (DR2). While DESI DR2 BAO measurements robustly support the standard
\Lambda
CDM model with a matter density parameter
\Omega_{m0}\approx 0.3
, our primary objective was to investigate the constraints on the dark energy equation of state parameters (
\omega_{0}
,
\omega_{a}
) and the matter density parameter (
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
) and to critically assess the consistency of the
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model when constrained solely by BAO data.

Our key findings highlight a significant inconsistency: the
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
model, when fitted with DESI DR2 BAA data alone, yields conflicting predictions for different types of BAO observables. Specifically, the best-fit
\omega_{0}\omega_{a}\mathrm{CDM}
parameters lead to simulated distributions for
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent observables (
D_{V}/r_{\mathrm{d}}
,
D_{M}/r_{\mathrm{d}}
,
D_{H}/r_{\mathrm{d}}
) that are systematically shifted towards smaller values compared to observations. In stark contrast, the simulated distribution for the
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-independent ratio
D_{M}/D_{H}
is systematically shifted towards larger values. This striking divergence points to an underlying issue with the model’s ability to consistently explain all observed BAO features.

We attribute this predictive inconsistency and the resulting potentially unphysical parameter combinations to the fundamental limitations of the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) parametrization (
w(z)=\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}\frac{z}{1+z}
) when applied to data sensitive to high redshifts. The CPL form’s asymptotic behavior at high redshifts, where
w(z)\to\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}
, struggles to accurately represent the dark energy evolution in the early Universe. To fit the
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-dependent data (which is sensitive to early-Universe physics), the MCMC analysis tends to compensate by pushing
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
to higher values and
\omega_{a}
to large negative values. Conversely, the
D_{M}/D_{H}
observable, primarily sensitive to the shape of
H(z)
at lower redshifts, can be fitted by parameter combinations where
\omega_{0}+\omega_{a}\approx 0
, leading to a different degeneracy direction and pulling the overall best-fit towards a ”compromise” solution that does not consistently satisfy all observables. This confirms that the
\chi^{2}
minimization process, in the presence of strong degeneracies and an approximate parametrization, can find a mathematical minimum that lacks physical robustness.

Based on these findings, we re-emphasize a methodological suggestion for future analyses of dynamic dark energy models. While ratios like
D_{M}/D_{H}
offer valuable
r_{\mathrm{d}}
-independent information, the use of individual BAO observables (
D_{V}
,
D_{M}
,
D_{H}
) in conjunction with external priors on
r_{\mathrm{d}}
(e.g., from Cosmic Microwave Background data) might provide a more robust and physically consistent framework for parameter inference. This approach could potentially mitigate the artificial compensation effects observed with the CPL parametrization and lead to a clearer understanding of dark energy’s true evolution.

Looking ahead, our study strongly reinforces the critical need for combining multiple cosmological probes to robustly constrain dynamic dark energy models. Relying solely on BAO data, even from highly precise surveys like DESI DR2, leaves significant parameter degeneracies unaddressed, particularly for flexible parametrizations like CPL. The inclusion of complementary datasets, such as Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) measurements and Type Ia Supernovae (SNe Ia) data, is indispensable. CMB provides crucial early-Universe information that can tightly constrain
r_{\mathrm{d}}
and
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
, effectively breaking many of the degeneracies seen with BAO alone. Such multi-probe analyses will be vital for obtaining more reliable and consistent constraints on the nature of dark energy, moving us closer to understanding this mysterious component of our Universe.

Endoscopic mucosal resection–assisted mucosal flap creation to facilitate endoscopic submucosal dissection for gastric adenoma amid diffuse fundic gland polyposis
Authors: Rinrada Worapongpaiboon, Clement Wu, Kasenee Tiankanon, Saowanee Ngamruengphong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and AimsEndoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) for a gastric lesion in a patient with familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP) with fundic gland polyps is challenging due to dense polyposis, limiting visualization and access during resection.MethodsA 49-year-old woman with FAP and a high-grade dysplastic gastric lesion underwent ESD. Because of dense polyposis obscuring the instrument tip, proximal EMR was performed under saline solution immersion to create a submucosal entry point for the ESD.ResultsThe proximal EMR enabled clear visualization of the submucosal layer and facilitated safe initiation of ESD. After traction-assisted ESD, en bloc resection was achieved without adverse events. The post-ESD defect was successfully closed with endoscopic suturing. Follow-up upper endoscopy at 3 months showed no local recurrence.ConclusionsThis case highlights the technical challenges of performing gastric ESD in patients with FAP and dense fundic gland polyps, in which visualization and access to the submucosal layer are limited. Creating a submucosal entry point with EMR can improve exposure and facilitate safe initiation of ESD. This hybrid approach may serve as a useful strategy for endoscopists facing similar difficult anatomy.

Case

A 49-year-old woman with familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP), who previously underwent total proctocolectomy with ileal pouch–anal anastomosis in 2021, was found to have a 4- × 4-cm gastric adenoma with high-grade dysplasia arising in the setting of dense fundic gland polyps. In this case, a previous endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) attempt at the outside hospital had been aborted because of difficulty extending the mucosal incision and to avoid causing fibrosis. Therefore, we decided to perform upper endoscopy with ESD of this lesion with the EMR-assisted mucosal flap creation technique.

Procedure

The procedure was performed using a GIF-HQ190 upper endoscope (Olympus, Center Valley, Pa, USA) (Video 1, available online at www.videogie.org). A 4- × 4-cm depressed lesion in the distal gastric body was identified (Fig. 1A). Narrow-band imaging demonstrated irregular microsurface and microvascular patterns, raising concern for a dysplastic lesion. Initial marking using a needle-knife (2-mm ORISE ProKnife; Boston Scientific, Marlborough, Mass, USA) was attempted, but the marking dots were not clearly visible because of the thick and dense polypoid background. Therefore, coagulation forceps (Coagrasper; Olympus) were used to mark the margins outside the lesion (Fig. 1B). After submucosal injection with hetastarch mixed with methylene blue, mucosal resection with the needle-knife was attempted, but the tip of the knife was not visible at the level of the mucosa, due to background polyposis (Fig. 2). Moreover, because of the thickness of the polyp and mucosa, the 2-mm knife length could not adequately gain access to the submucosal layer. As direct submucosal dissection was not feasible, proximal EMR was performed under saline solution immersion using a 10-mm Captivator snare (Boston Scientific) in Endocut mode to create an initial submucosal entry point for subsequent dissection (Figs. 3 and 4). EMR was repeated as needed until the submucosal layer was clearly exposed, after which the EMR was extended laterally to create an approximately 3-cm submucosal entry window. Through this EMR-created entry, ESD was initiated using a needle-knife (2-mm ORISE ProKnife) and a TriangleTipKnife (Olympus) (Fig. 5). Traction was applied to optimize exposure and stabilize the dissection plane using the endoscopic traction system (SureTrac traction system; Micro-Tech Endoscopy, Ann Arbor, Mich, USA). Outcomes

En bloc resection was successfully achieved with a total resection time of 101 minutes (Fig. 7). The patient was discharged the same day without immediate adverse events. Histopathology confirmed gastric adenoma with negative lateral and deep margins (R0 resection). Surveillance EGD at 3-month follow-up demonstrated no recurrence (Fig. 8).Figure 7Resected specimen pinned for histopathologic evaluation. Endoscopic resection is widely recognized as an effective and minimally invasive treatment option for premalignant gastric lesions. However, dense gastric polyposis in patients with FAP can make conventional ESD technically difficult, as reported in a multicenter retrospective study showing reduced resection efficacy due to restricted access and limited visualization.1 Several technical modifications have been described to improve resection success, including traction strategies and specialized knives such as the ITknife2 (Olympus) in the previous report.2 In this case, we successfully overcame these barriers using a hybrid technique: proximal EMR was performed first to create a stable submucosal entry point, followed by traction-assisted ESD to achieve controlled submucosal dissection and safe en bloc removal. This approach may be especially useful in selected patients with FAP with neoplastic lesions arising from a densely polypoid gastric background in which standard ESD is challenging.

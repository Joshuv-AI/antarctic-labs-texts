Model Theory of General von Neumann Algebras I: Generalized Ocneanu Ultraproducts
Jananan Arulseelan

Abstract
. This paper collates, presents, and expands upon technology and results obtained as part of the author’s PhD thesis. We generalize work done in the σ \sigma -finite setting by the author, Goldbring, Hart, and Sinclair by producing a language and axiomatization of full left Hilbert algebras. To improve the accessibility of using this axiomatization, we examine the metric structure ultraproduct associated to this axiomatization. In doing so, we generalize results of Ando-Haagerup and Masuda-Tomatsu. This examination leads us to multiple operator-algebraic characterizations of the ultraproduct which closely resemble known characterizations of the Ocneanu ultraproduct. One of these is closely related to the notion of continuous elements of an ultraproduct with respect to an action. In the spirit of results of the author, Goldbring, and Hart, we prove various undecidable universal theory results for von Neumann algebras with unbounded weights. Notably, we prove that the hyperfinite II ∞ factor together with its canonical tracial weight has an undecidable theory. This result was expected by experts, but could not be made precise until now. We also study the strengthenings of the negative solution to CEP that this result implies.

Let
\mathfrak{A}

\xi\mapsto\xi^{\#}
. Assume further that
\mathfrak{A}
is equipped with an inner product
\left\langle\cdot\,,\!\ \cdot\right\rangle
and denote by

Definition 2.1
.

We call
\mathfrak{A}
a
left Hilbert algebra
if the following conditions all hold.

(1)

Left multiplication is continuous. More precisely, for each
a\in\mathfrak{A}
, the linear map
b\mapsto ab
for
b\in\mathfrak{A}
extends to a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{H}
. We denote this extension by
\pi(a)
.

(2)

The representation
\pi
as above is a
∗
-representation. Namely, we assume
\left\langle ab\,,\!\ c\right\rangle=\left\langle b\,,\!\ a^{\#}c\right\rangle
for all
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{A}
.

(3)

The subalgebra
\mathfrak{A}^{2}
, the span of
\{ab\ :\ a,b\in\mathfrak{A}\}
is dense in
\mathfrak{A}
. This ensures that
\pi
is nondegenerate.

(4)

the map
a\mapsto a^{\#}
and therefore its restriction to
\mathfrak{A}^{2}
has closed extension.

Taking the weak closure of
\pi(\mathfrak{A})
give the associated
left von Neumann algebra

{\mathcal{R}}_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{A})
. We occasionally write
S_{0}a:=a^{\#}
.

We say that
\mathfrak{A}
is a
pre-left Hilbert algebra
if it is only assumed to satisfy conditions (1)-(3) above. We note that this is not standard terminology. It is introduced here to aid our exposition. To see why this is a useful definition to make, we direct the reader to the first half of
[
29

. There, pre-left Hilbert algebras are used extensively but are not given a name. One can define a
(pre-)right Hilbert algebra

\mathfrak{B}
by analogy, replacing left multiplication by right multiplication whenever the former appears above. In this case, we denote the involution by
♭
; the induced
∗
-representation by
\pi^{\prime}
; and the associated
right von Neumann algebra
by
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rho}(\mathfrak{B})
. We use the following notions extensively.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a pre-left Hilbert algebra with Hilbert space completion
\mathcal{H}
. We call a vector

a

if

\pi^{\prime}(v)a:=av\qquad\text{for all }a\in\mathfrak{A}

Proposition 2.3

\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{right}}

(1)

linear combinations, and

(2)

{\mathcal{R}}_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime}

Proof.

The proof of

Definition 2.4
.

Let

Definition 2.7
.

We call a pair

Let

We also define the sets:

One can show via a polarization argument (see

Notice that

Let

We associate a full left Hilbert algebra to a weighted von Neumann algebra

\displaystyle=\left\langle\pi^{\prime}(a)\eta_{\Phi}(x_{n}-x)\,,\!\ \pi^{\prime}(a)\eta_{\Phi}(x_{n}-x)\right\rangle

4.
Axiomatization

In this paper, we only ever consider weights
\Phi
such that
\Phi(1)\geq 1
or
\Phi(1)=\infty
. This is to avoid certain issues with ultraproducts becoming trivial. Namely, one might consider
\mathcal{M}_{i}=\mathcal{M}
and take a state
\varphi
on
\mathcal{M}
. Then if we take
\Phi_{i}=\frac{1}{i}\varphi
, we would get that the ultraproduct is
\{0\}
. Our restriction is fine in practice because one could always re-scale the weight. Very rarely in practice does one consider a bounded weight that is not a state, so this is not particularly objectionable.

We introduce the language
{\mathcal{L}}_{\vNa}
for weighted von Neumann algebras, whose symbols include:

(1)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a sort

with bound
2n

n
-bounded, right
n
-bounded elements

of
D_{\Phi}
with right
n
-bounded adjoint. We let

denote the metric symbol on
S_{n}
, whose intended interpretation is the metric induced by
\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}^{\#}
.

(2)

For each

, binary function symbols

and
-_{n}
with domain

and range
S_{2n}
and whose modulus of uniform continuity is
\delta(\epsilon)=\epsilon

S_{n}
.

(3)

For each

with domain

and range
S_{n^{2}}
and whose modulus of uniform continuity is

S_{n}
.

(4)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}

whose modulus of uniform continuity is
\delta(\epsilon)=\epsilon
. The intended interpretation of these symbols is for the adjoint restricted to each sort.

(5)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}

which lies in the sort
S_{n}
. The intended interpretation of these symbols is the element
0
.

(6)

For each

whose domain is

and range is

(7)

For each

and range

(8)

For each

and whose modulus of uniform continuity is

S_{n}
.

(9)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a predicate symbol

on the sort

taking values in

\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
from an element to the set of self-adjoint elements in

. The

Theorem 4.4

The category of models of

Remark 7.5

Our definition of ultraproduct weight does not agree with the one given in

Theorem 7.9

Let

(1)

(x_{i})\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}:=\{(x_{i})\in\ell_{\Phi}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{A}_{i})\ :\ (x_{i})\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}\text{ and }\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(x_{i})\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}\}

Corollary 7.10

An element
(x_{i})^{\bullet}\in\ell^{\infty}_{\Phi}

if and only if
(\Delta_{\Phi_{i}}^{it}x_{i})^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}

First, note by translation invariance of continuous group homomorphisms,

is continuous at

if and only if it is continuous at all

. Thus since
\sigma_{t}^{\Phi_{i}}(x_{i})=\pi(\Delta^{it}x_{i})
, we conclude.
∎

We are now ready to prove the final piece of our main result, handling those
\sigma

Theorem 7.11
.

Let
x=(x_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}
be an element of the Groh-Raynaud ultraproduct
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{GR}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\mathcal{H}_{\Phi_{i}})

for all

(1)

p_{\Phi}(x_{i})^{\bullet}=(x_{i})^{\bullet}p_{\Phi}
so that

(2)

(x_{i})^{\bullet}
is a strong

-continuous point of
(\sigma^{\Phi_{i}}_{t})_{{\mathcal{U}}}
so that

is an element of the generalized Ocneanu ultraproduct.

Proof.

The (1)

and the fact that the modular group is known to be strong
∗
-continuous. In this case, the ultraproduct of the modular automorphism groups acts as the modular automorphism group of the weight induced on the Hilbert algebra ultraproduct. The latter agrees with the ultraproduct weight on the Groh-Raynaud ultraproduct since
p_{\Phi}
is a projection onto the image of the isometry

Now we prove (2)
\implies
(1). Assume
(x_{i})^{\bullet}
is a strong
∗
-continuous point of
(\sigma^{\Phi_{i}}_{t})_{{\mathcal{U}}}
. Then for all
(v_{i})^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
, we have
(\sigma_{t}^{\Phi_{i}}(x_{i})v_{i})^{\bullet}
strong

(x_{i}v_{i})^{\bullet}
as

. So we compute

\displaystyle(\sigma_{t}^{\Phi_{i}}(x_{i})v_{i})^{\bullet}

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{\Phi_{i}}^{it}x_{i}\Delta_{\Phi_{i}}^{-it}v_{i})^{\bullet}

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{\Phi_{i}}^{it}x_{i}(\Delta_{\Phi_{i}}^{-it}v_{i}))^{\bullet}.

We also have that
(v_{i})^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
if and only if
(\Delta^{-it}v_{i})^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
by Theorem
7.9

(\Delta^{it}v_{i})^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
. By a reparameterization we can replace
(\Delta^{it}v_{i})^{\bullet}
with

Thus
(\Delta^{it}x_{i}v_{i})^{\bullet}

(x_{i}v_{i})^{\bullet}

(x_{i})^{\bullet}(v_{i})^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
and

∎

Corollary 7.12
.

The generalized Ocneanu ultraproduct is a von Neumann subalgebra of the Groh-Raynaud ultraproduct that is the image of a faithful normal conditional expectation.

Proof.

By Theorem
7.11
and Proposition
4.4
, the generalized Ocneanu ultraproduct is a von Neumann subalgebra.

By Theorem
6.7
and since the ultraproduct weight is the restriction of the ultraproduct weight on the Groh-Raynaud ultraproduct, the modular automorphism group on the generalized Ocneanu ultraproduct agrees with the modular automorphism group on the Groh-Raynaud ultraproduct. Since continuous elements are invariant under
\sigma^{\Phi^{{\mathcal{U}}}}_{t}
, we have the desired conditional expectation by Takesaki’s Theorem.
∎

Altogether,

Theorem 7.13
.

For any family
(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i})
of weighted von Neumann algebras, the generalized Ocneanu ultraproduct acting on

is spatially isomorphic to the Hilbert algebra ultraproduct of the corresponding family of full Hilbert algebras
(\mathfrak{A}_{i},\mathcal{H}_{\Phi_{i}})

\operatorname{Mod}(T_{\vNa})
.

8.
Applications to Computability and Ultraproduct Embeddings

Having now developed all of the necessary technology to do so, we use this section to study the (un)decidability of the universal theories of weighted von Neumann algebras with unbounded weights. We expand our catalogue of von Neumann algebras with undecidable universal theory. Fittingly, the first entry with unbounded weight is the infinite analogue of the hyperfinite II
1
factor. Our model-theoretic work facilitates results of this kind, which were not possible in earlier formalisms.

Theorem 8.1
.

The universal theory of the hyperfinite II
∞
factor
{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1}
is not computable in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\vNa}
.

Proof.

Let
\sup_{x}\theta(x)
be a universal sentence in the language of tracial von Neumann algebras where again we assume all variables range over the unit ball. Let
P_{1}
denote the set of trace
1
projections in
{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1}
. It can be seen that
P_{1}
is a quantifier-free definable set by the formula

\max\{d(p^{2},p),d(p^{*},p),|\Phi(p)-1|\}.

Note that for every
p\in P_{1}
, the set

p{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1}p:=\{pxp\ :\ x\in{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1}\}

is a copy of
{\mathcal{R}}
and the restriction of the
\Phi
on
{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1}
to this set is the canonical trace on
{\mathcal{R}}
. Furthermore, the map
x\mapsto pxp
sends the unit ball of
{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1}
to the unit ball of the corresponding copy of
{\mathcal{R}}
. Thus, computing the
\sup_{x}\theta(x)
in any one copy of
{\mathcal{R}}
is the same as computing it in any other. So consider the universal sentence in
\mathcal{L}_{\vNa}
given by

\psi=\sup_{p\in P_{1}}\sup_{x}\theta(pxp).

By assumption,
\psi
is computable in
{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1}
. But

\psi^{{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1}}=(\sup_{x}\theta(x))^{{\mathcal{R}}},

contradicting the uncomputability of the universal theory of
{\mathcal{R}}
.
∎

The proof of the above can be made more general to prove the following principle.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau_{0})
be a II
1
factor equipped with its canonical trace. Assume
(\mathcal{M},\tau_{0})
has undecidable universal theory. If
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}\otimes B(\mathcal{H})
and the tracial weight
\tau
is the II
∞
amplification of the trace
\tau_{0}
, then
(\mathcal{N},\tau)
also has undecidable universal theory.

Theorem
8.1
tells us that the universal theory of
{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1}
can compute the Turing degree
\mathbf{0}^{\prime}
. Using the techniques and results from
[

\mathbf{0}^{\prime}
. We will do this by demonstrating a computable presentation of

(see
[

Proposition 8.3

{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1}

Proof.

Take an effective enumeration
A_{k}
of a dense subset of the unit ball of the hyperfinite II
1

\mathcal{R}

for

Lemma 8.11

If
(\mathcal{M},\Phi)
is a weighted von Neumann algebra and

\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}

Proof.

Recall that
\Delta_{\Phi}=R_{\Phi}^{-1}(2-R_{\Phi})

It follows by functional calculus that

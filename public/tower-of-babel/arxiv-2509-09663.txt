Bogoliubov quasi-particles in superconductors are integer-charged particles inapplicable for braiding quantum information
Zhiyu Fan (gbsn樊知宇), Wei Ku (bsmi顧威)

Abstract
We present a rigorous proof that under a number-conserving Hamiltonian, one-body quasi-particles generally possess quantized charge and inertial mass identical to the bare particles. It follows that, Bogoliubov zero modes in the vortex (or on the edge) of superconductors cannot be their own anti-particles capable of braiding quantum information. As such, the heavily pursued Majorana zero mode-based route for quantum computation requires a serious re-consideration. This study further reveals the conceptual challenge in preparing and manipulating braid-able quantum states via physical thermalization or slow external fields. These profound results should reignite the long-standing quest for a number-conserving theory of superconductivity and superfluidity without fictitiously breaking global U(1) symmetry.



Introduction
-
Computation has became an inseparable part of modern society.
As the classical computers approaching their quantum physical limit
Powell 2008
, significant amount of efforts have been made to seek the next-generation routes to push the computation capabilities.
Quantum computation
Preskill 2023
has since become a intensive research field with profound technical the scientific challenges.

One of the promising routes being heavily pursued in the past decades is the use of Bogoliubov zero modes
Caroli et al.
;
Gygi and Schluter
in the vortex core or on the edge of topological superconductors
Nayak et al. 2008
;
Beenakker
.
The theoretical justification is based on the current description of a Bogoliubov quasi-particle

being a coherent superposition of particle and its antiparticle (“hole”), such that its zero mode, having exactly equal mixture of particle and hole, is its own anti-particle, namely a Majorana zero mode

;
Kitaev

It follows that such zero modes possess non-Abelian statistics in two-dimension
Read and Green
;
Ivanov
, suitable for braiding many-body quantum information.
Together with their spatial non-locality that help resist decoherence against local perturbation, these Bogoliubov zero modes thus offers one of the most promising route towards industrializable quantum computation.

The theoretical foundation of this route is, however, intimately tied to the mainstream description of superconductivity via an effective quantum field with spontaneously broken U(1) symmetry, an aspect that has been long
questioned
by masters of physics
Leggett 2001
due to its violation of the profound particle conservation.
Correspondingly, whether Bogoliubov quasi-particles are allowed to be a coherent mixture of electron and hole, as to produce non-Abelian statistics, has recently been questioned as well
Ortiz et al. 2014
;
Wang et al. 2017
;
Knapp et al. 2020
;
Lapa and Levin 2020
;
Lin and Leggett 2022
.
Furthermore, the current effective field description only arrives from tracing out (
N\!>\!2
)-body fluctuation/information.
Therefore, reassembling the resulting one-body zero modes back into a many-body system and utilizing the resulting non-Abelian statistics [equivalent to adding (
N\!>\!2
)-body quantum information
by hand
] is hardly a reliable/legal practice at the conceptual level for many-body quantum information.

Here, we present a rigorous mathematical proof that under a number-conserving Hamiltonian,
generally
one-body quasi-particles possess quantized charge and inertial mass identical to the bare particles.
Therefore, Bogoliubov zero modes, being the one-body quasi-particles in a superconductor, cannot possibly be their own anti-particles.
That is, these zero modes are not Majorana in nature and their statistics follows the standard Abelian fermionic statistics.
Therefore, a direct application of them in braiding many-body quantum information, as currently being heavily pursued in the field, is conceptually unfounded.
Furthermore, this study also reveals a conceptual challenge in preapring and manipulating braidable quantum states via physical thermalization or weak external fields.
These profound results should reignite the long-standing quest for a proper number-conserving theory of superconductivity and superfluidity without fictitiously breaking global U(1) symmetry.

One-body quasi-particles
-

Eigen-particles carry bare charge and inertial mass
-
Having established this correspondence, let’s examine the rigorous implications of number conservation of
H
on eigen-particles.

Theorem 1:

Under a number-conserving
H
, eigen-particles carry exactly
one
particle number of bare particles.

Proof:

For
H
with number conservation, the total number of bare particle,
N_{c}\equiv\sum_{l}c^{\dagger}_{l}c_{l}
, is a constant of motion, namely
[N_{c},H]=0
.
Correspondingly, diagonalization of
H
through
\mathcal{U}
only involves subspace within each fixed
N=\langle N_{c}\rangle

[N_{c},\mathcal{U}]=0

In turn,

\displaystyle N_{c}\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{l}-\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{l}N_{c}

\displaystyle=[N_{c},\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{l}]=[N_{c},\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}c^{\dagger}_{l}\mathcal{U}]=\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}[N_{c},c^{\dagger}_{l}]\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}c^{\dagger}_{l}\mathcal{U}=\tilde{c}_{l}^{\dagger},

(11)

or
N_{c}\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{l}=\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{l}(N_{c}+1)
, indicates that operator
\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{l}
increases the bare particle number
N_{c}
by 1, thus establishing the theorem.

Immediately following this, the electric charge and inertial mass attached to each bare particle are therefore carried by eigen-particles as well.
Essentially, at long time scales, slow dynamics of the systems as observed by low-frequency experimental probes are all about these massive integer-charged eigen-particles under a diagonal
\tilde{H}
in Eq.
7

Proper Bogoliubov quasi-particles
-
Experts in superconductivity might be immediately alerted by the above rigorous conclusion, as it obviously invalidates the well-accepted nature of Bogoliubov quasi-particles
Bogoljubov

\begin{cases}\gamma_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}=u_{k}\textcolor{blue}{c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}}-v_{k}\textcolor{red}{c_{-k\downarrow}}\\

Therefore,

Correspondingly, their well-accepted nature of mixed electron and hole should be regarded as an

A “proper” description of Bogoliubov quasi-particles through eigen-particles (or their reduced counterparts) must adhere to Eq.

As an illustration to bring back some familiarity, let’s adapt in

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{BCS}}=-\frac{V}{\Omega}\sum_{kk^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{k\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{-k\downarrow}c_{-k^{\prime}\downarrow}c_{k^{\prime}\uparrow},

The corresponding eigen-particles would then be dominated by,

\displaystyle\textcolor{blue}{\tilde{c}_{k\sigma}^{\dagger}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k_{1}k_{1}^{\prime}}\tilde{A}^{k\sigma}_{k_{1};k_{1}^{\prime}}\textcolor{blue}{c_{k\sigma}^{\dagger}}c_{k_{1}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-k_{1}\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-k_{1}^{\prime}\downarrow}c_{k_{1}^{\prime}\uparrow}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k_{1}k_{2}k_{1}^{\prime}}\tilde{A}^{k\sigma}_{k_{1}k_{2};k_{1}^{\prime}}c_{k_{1}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-k_{1}\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{k_{2}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-k_{2}\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-k_{1}^{\prime}\downarrow}c_{k_{1}^{\prime}\uparrow}\textcolor{red}{c_{-k\bar{\sigma}}}+\cdots,

Inability to braid quantum information
-
Given the strict number conservation in
H
, it follows that generally electronic eigen-particles, such as the proper Bogoliubov quasi-particles, cannot possibly be their own anti-particles, as intuitively confirmed by the non-self-adjoint form of Eq.
14
.

Theorem 2:

Under a number-conserving
H
, eigen-particles cannot be Majorana particles.

Proof:

Assuming one-body eigen-particles can be their anti-particles,
\tilde{c}_{l}^{\dagger}=\tilde{c}_{l}
,

\tilde{c}_{l}^{\dagger}=[N_{c},\tilde{c}_{l}^{\dagger}]=-[\tilde{c}_{l}^{\dagger},N_{c}]=-[\tilde{c}_{l},N_{c}]=-\tilde{c}_{l}=-\tilde{c}_{l}^{\dagger},

(16)

can only be satisfied by
\tilde{c}_{l}^{\dagger}=0
, thus establishing the theorem.
(Here,
\tilde{c}_{l}=[\tilde{c}_{l},N_{c}]
is implied by Eq.
11
.)

Now, since proper Bogoliubov quasi-particles are in fact massive integer-charged particles, rather than Majorana particles, their statistical properties are simply those of the regular fermionic ones representable by an Abelian algebra.
The theoretical foundation, namely a non-Abelian statistics, for braiding quantum information directly via Bogoliubov zero modes is therefore undoubtedly flawed.

This conclusion can be simply confirmed by the distinction in the size of degenerate subspace.
Under the current Majoranna proposal
Ivanov
, an even number,
L
, of improper Bogoliubov zero modes would form a
2^{L/2}
-fold degenerate many-body subspace for braiding.
In contrast, since occupying zero modes of eigen-particles does not affect the eigen-energy,
L
proper Bogoliubov zero modes instead form a significantly larger
2^{L}
-fold degenerate subspace, obviously contradicting the current braiding proposal.
Such a large entropy should be easily quantified through, for example, heat capacity experiments or properly scaled finite-size computations.

Braid-able states and challenge in their preparation and manipulation
-
Generally, the above discussion rigorously invalidates all possibilities in
directly
braiding one-body quasi-particles in number-conserving systems.
It does not, however, rule out scenario of braiding objects that can be engineered in eigenstates made from extensive superposition of
degenerate
product states of eigen-particles
Moore and Read 1991
.

Nonetheless, the above discussion on eigen-particles still indicates a generic challenge in preparing and manipulating such braid-able states in materials.
Recall that it is the physical
dynamical
processes in
H
that is behind the eigenstates thermalization of real materials.
Similarly, it is the
integer
-quantized electrons that the external fields directly couple to during weak non-destructive external manipulation.
Given integer-quantized eigen-particles’ ability to fully absorb quantum dynamics in
H
, slow physical processes in preparation and manipulation would, by default,
only
reach product states of eigen-particles.

In contrast, states made of extensive superposition of these product states risk imposing
unphysical
coherence beyond the dynamics encoded in
H
.
Therefore, as speculated from the very beginning
Moore and Read 1991
, preparation and manipulation of such braid-able states would be physically challenging to achieve, even at the conceptual level.
In fact, this is exactly analogous to the lack of experimental observation of moment-less ferromagnetic states,
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{\uparrow}+\ket{\downarrow})
, in macroscopic materials, as opposed to the commonly observed product states with total magnetic moment,
\ket{\uparrow}~\equiv\prod_{l}\tilde{c}_{l\uparrow}^{\dagger}\ket{0}
.
Such moment-less states contain extra coherence beyond the physical dynamics in
H
.

To give a trivial illustration on this generic issue of artificial coherence associated with degeneracy in quantum mechanics, consider an array of centimeter spaced lattice of optical traps having a single atom without physically meaningful inter-trap couplings.
In such a spatially decoupled system, an atom in the local ground state of a single trap at position
x
,
a_{x}^{\dagger}
, is already an eigen-particle of the system.
Correspondingly, the system hosts a high level of degeneracy associated with the freedom in
x
.
Obviously, it is physically achievable to prepare and manipulate the trivial product states
a_{x}^{\dagger}\ket{0}
.
In contrast, states made of
artificial
superposition of these degenerate eigenstates,

\tilde{a}_{k}^{\dagger}\equiv\sum_{x}^{M}\tilde{a}_{x}^{\dagger}e^{ikx}/\sqrt{M}
,
would be extremely challenging to prepare or manipulate, given no physical processes in
H
to establish the extra coherence.

Following the above consideration, perhaps a more achievable (but hardly explored) route for engineering braid-able systems is through explicitly breaking number conservation of a system.
This can be achieved by driving the systems with significant amount of charge fluctuation, or by strongly coupling the systems to a well-designed environment with efficient exchange of electrons.

Toward a number-conserving theory of superconductivity
-
As for the superconducting ground state, the above concern on degeneracy-enabled artificial coherence equally applies.
Indeed, while one is mathematically allowed to introduce artificial superposition of (nearly) degenerate ground states of
H-\mu N
from sectors of different particle numbers, the resulting U(1) symmetry broken states have absolutely no dynamical origin from
H
(c.f. Fig.
1
) and thus would not result from regular thermal equilibrium.

All significant implications of number conservation above should remind experts of the long-standing concerns
Leggett 2001
;
Richardson and Sherman 1964
;
Bardeen 1957
;
Nambu
;
Baym and Kadanoff
on the spontaneously broken U(1) symmetry in effective field descriptions of superconductivity.
As clearly discussed around Fig
1
, such description violates the profound number-conservation and must be viewed as a (highly useful) approximation risking unphysical properties, such as failing to automatically uphold the physical sum rule in dynamical transports, or mistaking Bogoliubov zero modes as Majorana ones.
Our study should thus reignite a serious reconsideration of number conservation toward proper U(1) symmetry
respecting
theories of superconductivity.
Particularly, the general eigen-particle representation employed here is likely to offer a convenient representation that explicitly encodes the essential many-body dynamics with strict number conservation
Hegg et al. 2024
.

Conclusion
-
In short, we present a mathematical proof that under a number-conserving Hamiltonian, eigen-particles as the
N
-body representation of the one-body quasi-particles, rigorously contains particle number 1 of bare particles.
They thus possess the same quantized charge and inertial mass of the bare electrons, and can never be their own anti-particles.
This result corrects a long-standing misconception on experimentally observed Bogoliubov quasi-particles being a mixture of electrons and holes, as resulting from approximate effective field theories.
Correspondingly, their zero modes are not Majorana ones and cannot be used for braiding
N
-body quantum information.
Furthermore, this study reveals a conceptual challenge in preapring and manipulating braid-able quantum states via physical thermal equilibrium or slow external fields.
These profound results should reignite a long-standing quest for a number-conserving, U(1) symmetry
respecting
theory of superconductivity.

Appendix A
1. One-body Green’s function represented by eigen-particles

Appendix B
2. Proper and improper Bogoliubov quasi-particles

In this section we first illustrate dominant fluctuation in proper (number-conserving) Bogoliubov quasi-particles in BCS-like theories.
We then demonstrate how adapting an improper (number-non-conserving) mean-field can result in a familiar form of improper Bogoliubov quasi-particles.

Following BCS’s original argument on s-wave pairing, for a local on-site interaction,

-\frac{V}{\Omega}\sum_{kpq}c_{k+q\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{p-q\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{p\downarrow}c_{k\uparrow},

(S3)

we consider only the dynamical effect of pairing fluctuation, namely,

-\frac{V}{\Omega}\sum_{kq}c_{k+q\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-k-q\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-k\downarrow}c_{k\uparrow},

(S4)

as in the BCS effective Hamiltonian, where
\Omega
denotes the system size.

B.1
Proper Bogoliubov quasi-particles

To find the dressed particles that approximately diagonalize the Hamiltonian (as approximate eigen-particles), one can apply a canonical transformation via
\mathcal{U}=e^{A}
, with

A=\frac{1}{\Omega}\sum_{1,2}\text{$\alpha_{12}$}c_{1\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-1\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-2\downarrow}c_{2\uparrow},

(S5)

where the matrix
\alpha
is real and anti-symmetric (
\alpha_{12}=-\alpha_{21}
) and system-size insensitive.
(Further diagonalization for the rest less relevant fluctuation would resulting smaller corrections beyond this discussion.)
The resulting dressed particles (using
c^{\dagger}_{k\uparrow}
as a simple illustration),

\tilde{c}_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}\equiv e^{-A}c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}e^{A}=c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}+[c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger},A]+\frac{1}{2!}[[c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger},A],A]+\cdots,

(S6)

now absorb the dominant fluctuations, including

\displaystyle[c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger},A]

\displaystyle=[c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger},\frac{1}{\Omega}\sum_{1,2}\text{$\alpha_{12}$}c_{1\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-1\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-2\downarrow}c_{2\uparrow}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\Omega}\sum_{1}\alpha_{1k}c_{1\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-1\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-k\downarrow},

and

\displaystyle[[c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger},A],A]

\displaystyle=[-\frac{1}{\Omega}\sum_{1}\alpha_{1k}c_{1\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-1\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-k\downarrow},\frac{1}{\Omega}\sum_{23}\text{$\alpha_{23}$}c_{2\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-2\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-3\downarrow}c_{3\uparrow}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega^{2}}\sum_{13}\alpha_{1k}\alpha_{k3}c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{1\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-1\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-3\downarrow}c_{3\uparrow}+\cdots,

for example.

Focusing on the leading fluctuations, the dressed particles as approximate eigen-particles have well-defined structures,

\displaystyle\textcolor{blue}{\tilde{c}_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=\textcolor{blue}{c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Omega}\sum_{1}c_{1\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-1\downarrow}^{\dagger}(\alpha_{1k}+\cdots)\textcolor{red}{c_{-k\downarrow}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2!}\frac{1}{\Omega^{2}}\sum_{11^{\prime}}\textcolor{blue}{c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}}c_{1\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-1\downarrow}^{\dagger}(\alpha_{1k}\alpha_{1^{\prime}k}+\cdots)c_{-1^{\prime}\downarrow}c_{1^{\prime}\uparrow}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3!}\frac{1}{\Omega^{3}}\sum_{121^{\prime}}c_{1\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-1\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{2\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-2\downarrow}^{\dagger}(\alpha_{1k}\alpha_{2k}\alpha_{1^{\prime}k}+\cdots)c_{-1^{\prime}\downarrow}c_{1^{\prime}\uparrow}\textcolor{red}{c_{-k\downarrow}}+\cdots,

(S7)

showing a clear pattern containing even-order terms that renormalize the electrons, and odd-order terms that fluctuate “into” holes of their time-reversal counterpart.

B.2
Improper Bogoliubov quasi-particles

To illustrate that the standard (improper) Bogoliubov quasi-particles are improperly reduced eigen-particles, let’s take the Jacobi ansatz
\alpha_{1k}=\frac{1}{2}arctan(\frac{V}{\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{1}})

\Delta_{0}=-\frac{V}{\Omega}\sum_{1}\braket{c_{1\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-1\downarrow}^{\dagger}}.

\displaystyle\textcolor{blue}{\tilde{c}_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}}

\tilde{u}_{k}\equiv cos(\phi)\geq\tilde{v}_{k}\equiv sin(\phi)

B.3
An error in previous attempts toward proper Bogoliubov quasi-particles

Recall that standard one-body quasi-particle excitations contain probabilities of both removing and adding a particle to the system, as can be probed separately by photoemission and inverse photoemission experiments.
Therefore, a straightforward patch-up
Blonder et al. 1982
;
Tinkham 2004
;
Lin and Leggett 2022

\gamma^{\dagger}_{k\uparrow}\approx u_{k}c_{k\uparrow}^{\dagger}-v_{k}C^{\dagger}_{k}c_{-k\downarrow},

\epsilon_{k}>E_{F}

However, the same patch-up seems highly questionable in the particle removal processes (
\epsilon_{k}\leq E_{F}

adds
exactly a particle instead.

Indeed, Eq.
S10
and
S11

S7

S11

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}_{k\uparrow}

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}_{k\uparrow}

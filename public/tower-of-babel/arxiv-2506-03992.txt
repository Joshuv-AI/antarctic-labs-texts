\left\|\mathcal{E}f\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\sigma\right)},\ \ \ \ \ \text{for all }q>3.

In order to extend the domain

This completes the proof of Theorem

assuming that Theorem

holds.
∎

\displaystyle\left(\int_{A\left(0,2^{r}\right)}\left(\sum_{s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq s_{3}\text{ and }s_{3}>\frac{r}{1-\delta}}\left|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{s_{1},U_{1}}^{\eta}f_{1}\left(\xi\right)\right|\ \left|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{s_{2},U_{2}}^{\eta}f_{2}\left(\xi\right)\right|\ \left|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{s_{3},U_{3}}^{\eta}f_{3}\left(\xi\right)\right|\right)^{\frac{q}{3}}\ d\xi\right)^{\frac{3}{q}}

\displaystyle C_{\kappa}\sum_{s_{3}>\frac{r}{1-\delta}}s_{3}^{2}2^{-s_{3}}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|f_{3}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim C_{\kappa}r^{2}2^{-\frac{r}{1-\delta}}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|f_{3}\right\|_{L^{p}}.

∎

3.4.

Proof continued.

sufficiently large,

Thus altogether we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{A\left(0,2^{r}\right)}\left(\sum_{s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\frac{r}{1+\delta}\text{ and }s_{3}\leq\frac{r}{1-\delta}}\left|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{s_{1},U_{1}}^{\eta}f_{1}\left(\xi\right)\right|\ \left|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{s_{2},U_{2}}^{\eta}f_{2}\left(\xi\right)\right|\ \left|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{s_{3},U_{3}}^{\eta}f_{3}\left(\xi\right)\right|\right)^{\frac{q}{3}}\ d\xi\right)^{\frac{3}{q}}

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\frac{r}{1+\delta}\text{ and }s_{3}\leq\frac{r}{1-\delta}}\left(\int_{B\left(0,2^{r}\right)}\left(\left|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{s_{1},U_{1}}^{\eta}f_{1}\left(\xi\right)\right|\ \left|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{s_{2},U_{2}}^{\eta}f_{2}\left(\xi\right)\right|\ \left|\mathcal{E}\mathsf{Q}_{s_{3},U_{3}}^{\eta}f_{3}\left(\xi\right)\right|\right)^{\frac{q}{3}}\ d\xi\right)^{\frac{3}{q}}

4.
Appendix: Application to the probabilistic Fourier extension theorem

Here we briefly describe an alternate, and arguably simpler, proof in
dimension
n=3
of the
probabilistic
Fourier extension inequality
obtained in
[
Saw7
]
. However, this alternate proof has little likelihood
of being extended to the Knapp segment at the boundary of allowable
exponents, something that is not out of the question for the proof given in

[
Saw7
]
.

The square function formulation of the probabilistic inequality proved in

[
Saw7
]
is,

(4.1)

\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\mathop{\rm Fourier}}f\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q}\left(B\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)}\ ,

where
\mathcal{S}_{\mathop{\rm Fourier}}
is the
Fourier
square
function defined by

(4.2)

\mathcal{S}_{\mathop{\rm Fourier}}f\equiv\left(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{G}\left[U\right]}\left|\mathcal{E}\mathbf{1}_{U_{0}}\bigtriangleup_{I;\kappa}^{n-1,\eta}f\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Here the extension operator
\mathcal{E}
is defined by

(4.3)

\mathcal{E}f\left(\xi\right)\equiv\int_{B_{2}\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)}e^{-i\Phi\left(x\right)\cdot\xi}f\left(x\right)dx,\ \ \ \ \ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for
f\in L^{p}\left(B_{2}\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)
. Thus
\mathcal{E}f=\mathcal{F}\Phi_{\ast}\left(f\lambda_{2}\right)=\widehat{\Phi_{\ast}\left(f\lambda_{2}\right)}
, where
\Phi_{\ast}\nu

denotes the pushforward of a measure
\nu
under the map
\Phi
, and
\lambda_{2}
is Lebesgue measure in the plane.

Here is the square function analogue of the second part of Theorem
11
.

Definition 14
.

Suppose
n=3
. Let
\varepsilon,\nu>0
,
0<\delta<1
, and
1<q<\infty
.
Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{\mathop{\rm disj}\nu}^{\kappa,\delta,\mathop{\rm square}}\left(\otimes_{3}L^{q}\rightarrow L^{\frac{q}{3}};\varepsilon\right)

the disjoint
smooth Alpert square function
trilinear inequality

\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\mathop{\rm Fourier}}\mathsf{Q}_{U_{1}}^{s_{1}}f_{1}\ \mathcal{S}_{\mathop{\rm Fourier}}\mathsf{Q}_{U_{2}}^{s_{2}}f_{2}\ \mathcal{S}_{\mathop{\rm Fourier}}\mathsf{Q}_{U_{3}}^{s_{3}}f_{3}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{3}}\left(A\left(0,2^{s}\right)\right)}\leq C_{\varepsilon,\nu}2^{\varepsilon s}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(U\right)}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(U\right)}\left\|f_{3}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(U\right)}\ ,

taken over all
s_{k}\in\left(\left(1-\delta\right)s,\left(1+\delta\right)s\right)
, all
f_{k}\in L^{q}\left(U_{k}\right)
, and all
triples
\left(U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}\right)\subset U^{3}
that satisfy the
weak
\nu
-disjoint condition,

\mathop{\rm diam}\left[\Phi\left(U_{k}\right)\right]\approx\mathop{\rm dist}\left[\Phi\left(U_{k}\right),\bigcup_{j:\ j\neq k}\Phi\left(U_{j}\right)\right]\gtrsim\nu,\text{ for }1\leq k\leq 3,

Theorem 15
.

Let
0<\delta<1
and
\kappa>\frac{10}{\delta}
. The
square function inequality (
4.1
) holds in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

if and only if
for every
q>3
there is
\nu>0
such that the
disjoint
smooth Alpert square function
trilinear inequality
\mathcal{A}_{\mathop{\rm disj}\nu}^{\kappa,\delta,\mathop{\rm square}}\left(\otimes_{3}L^{\infty}\rightarrow L^{\frac{q}{3}};\varepsilon\right)

.

This square function variant can be proved by tracing through the proof of
Theorem
11
above and making the obvious modifications for the
square function.

Theorem
15
can be used to give an alternate proof of the
probabilistic analogue of Fourier extension in
[
Saw7
]
. In fact, the
case
n=3
of Proposition 34 in
[
Saw7
]
says that for
q>3
, there is
\varepsilon_{q}>0
such that for every
s\in\mathbb{N}
, and every

\mathbb{E}_{\pm}\left\|\mathcal{E}\left[\left(\pm\mathsf{Q}_{U}^{s}\right)^{\spadesuit}f\right]\right\|_{L^{q}\left(B\left(0,2^{s}\right)\right)}\lesssim 2^{-s\varepsilon_{q}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q}\left(U\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\right)},

.

extension theorem in the case

.

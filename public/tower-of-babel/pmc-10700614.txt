A study of wormhole solutions in f(Q,T) gravity using some viable shape functions
Authors: Rabia Saleem, Shokaib Shahid, Sobia Sadiq, M. Israr Aslam
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
This article examines static spherically symmetric wormhole (WH) solutions in f(Q,T) theory of gravity where Q represents non-metricity (NM) scalar while T is the trace of the stress-energy tensor. An anisotropic fluid configuration is taken into account to fulfill the task along with the linear form of the f(Q,T) model. For two different choices of the redshift function that are φ(r)=constant, φ(r)=(r0r)12 and for the shape functions, b(r)=r0+r0((rr0)γ−1),b(r)=r0log⁡(r+1)log⁡(r0+1), we construct the corresponding WH solutions. We examine the graphical behavior of the chosen shape functions and analyze all necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of WH. We investigate the stability of obtained solutions and analyze how the energy conditions behave. We found that the null energy condition (NEC) is violated in a few regions of spacetime which shows the existence of traversable WHs in these regions. It is concluded that our results are viable for the positive values of theory parameters and within a range 0<γ<1 (the parameter of the shape function).

Introduction

A WH is a hypothetical structure that connects various regions of the same or different cosmos based on a particular non-singular solution to the field equations. The notion of WHs is derived from the Einstein-Rosen bridge [1] which was originally assumed to be just a formal mathematical finding. Wheeler brought attention to the potential use of WHs to connect two places that are far apart [2]. Numerous researchers were intrigued by the WH structure proposed by Morris and Thorne [3] who found various WH models that preserve the WH's throat and can therefore be traversed. The existence of WH geometries depends on the dispersion of exotic matter that restrains the WH throat from collapsing. In maintaining the WH's stability, a key component is the exotic matter whose presence near the WH neck is indicated by violations of weak energy condition (WEC) and NEC [3]. Visser [4] suggested copy-paste strategy to reduce the use of exotic matter, although this method only works with the exotic fluid close to the WH's neck. Hochberg and Visser [5] examined the static as well as dynamical WH solutions. In [6], Kuhfittig proposed another solution that enforces the requirement b′(r)<1 (b(r) indicates the shape function) at the throat of the WH, which allows the region requiring the exotic matter to be made small arbitrarily. The potential for creating WH structures using dark matter/fluids like phantom energy or Chaplygin gas has also been investigated in literature [7].

The interest of researchers in modified theories of gravity has grown in recent years. These theories which express the early as well as late-time acceleration of the cosmos are geometrical extensions of Einstein's general relativity (GR). The advantage of these extended gravity theories is that the energy constraints can be satisfied for the usual matter sector due to corrections made by the additional fields. In [8], Lobo and Oliveira studied WH structures in f(R) gravity (R is the Ricci scalar) and explored the behavior of WHs under different energy constraints using specific shape functions as well as distinct equations of state. Richarte and Simeone [9] investigated thin-shell WHs with spherically symmetric Einstein-Born-Infeld field equations. They showed that the amount of exotic matter on the shell can be lowered in comparison to the Maxwell case for specific values of the Born-Infeld parameter. They also checked the mechanical stability of WH structures while maintaining spherical symmetry under radial perturbations.

Garcia and Lobo [10] developed the exact WH solutions in non-minimal coupled gravity and concluded that the existence of coupling minimizes the violation of NEC at the throat. DeBenedictis and Horvat [11] used f(R) gravity theory to study the existence and properties of WH throats and observed that modified gravity can reduce the amount of anisotropy necessary to maintain a throat. In the arena of f(R,T) gravity, Azizi [12] discussed WH geometries and obtained the shape function using the flaring out condition at the throat. Jamil et al. [13] calculated the exact static WH models in f(T) gravity (T denotes the torsion scalar) and discussed the results with both isotropic and anisotropic pressure.

Rahaman et al. [14] derived WH solutions by considering the Lorentzian distribution and analyzed them graphically. Pavlovic and Sossich [15] studied various WH solutions for different redshift as well as shape functions and demonstrated the WHs' existence without the requirement of exotic matter. Duplessis and Easson [16] presented non-singular black hole as well as traversable WH solutions in quadratic gravity and concluded that exotic solutions are supported by the vacuum of gravitational theory and do not require the violation of NEC. Bronnikov and Galiakhmetov [17] determined a family of static spherical solutions in Einstein-Cartan gravity by considering an electromagnetic field as well as a non-minimally coupled scalar field and found that the developed solutions may describe black holes as well as WHs. In the same gravity, Mehdizadeh and Ziaie [18] investigated the WH structures as well as the energy conditions supporting them. By assuming a linear equation of state (EoS), they introduced asymptotically flat and anti-de Sitter spacetimes that allow for traversable WHs and observed energy conditions. Moradpour et al. [19] investigated the features of traversable WH geometries in the framework of Rastall theory of gravity with flat asymptotes as well as the feasibility of accurate solutions by a source with the baryonic matter state parameter. They demonstrated that WEC might be satisfied by various solutions and discussed how phantom sources could be used to traverse WH geometries. Sharif and Ikram [20] used f(G,T) gravity (G represents the Gauss-Bonnet invariant) to find the static spherical WHs by considering isotropic, anisotropic, and barotropic matter distributions.

Our current investigation's major objective is to consider a different application of f(Q) gravity that relies on a non-minimal coupling between the NM Q and the trace T of the matter energy-momentum tensor. More specifically, we assume that L=f(Q,T) and that the Lagrangian density of the gravitational field is determined by a generic function of both Q and T. The geometric action can be created in a conventional manner from this gravitational Lagrangian. We can get the general field equations characterizing gravitational processes in the presence of geometry-matter coupling by changing the action with regard to the metric tensor. We obtain the fundamental conclusion that the divergence of the matter energy-momentum tensor does not vanish in the current framework for gravitational interaction by taking into account the covariant derivative of the field equations. For three classes of particular models, it is examined how the f(Q,T) hypothesis affects cosmology. The results show that f(Q,T) gravity can offer helpful insights for the explanation of the early and late phases of cosmic development. They describe both accelerating and decelerating evolutionary periods of the universe [21].

Astrophysicists and cosmologists have recently become interested in f(Q) gravity due to the exploration of more cosmological and astrophysical applications. Successful applications of f(Q) formalism include the exploration of modified energy conditions [22] and its impact on the post-Newtonian limit [23]. Without the use of exotic matter components like scalar fields, it has been demonstrated that the f(Q) gravity [24] can explain analytical cosmological solutions describing acceleration of the universe at early and late evolutionary epochs [25]. Holographic dark energy models have been investigated under f(Q) gravity [26]. In this theory, Hassan et al. [27] found realistic and stable WH geometries with two specific shape functions. Banerjee et al. [28] observed the violation of NEC about the WH's throat for static spherically symmetric WH solutions. Hassan et al. [29] analyzed two f(Q) model families along with two distinct shape functions to construct the WH models and discussed the chance of the existence of traversable WHs fulfilling strong energy condition (SEC) and dominant energy condition (DEC) in the vicinity of exotic matter.

In literature, Garattini [30] considered the shape function which is obtained using EoS p=ωρ, to investigate the size of WH as a function of ω. For this purpose, the author used Gaussian trial wave functionals by means of a variational approach. Godani and Samanta [31] used two different shape functions to study the characteristics of WH in F(R) gravity. They checked the validity of energy conditions. Samanta et al. [32] introduced a novel shape function and used it to analyze energy conditions, EoS, and anisotropy parameters in GR, F(R) gravity, and F(R,T) gravity. Tayde et al. [33] analyzed the solutions of traversable WH for linear F(Q,T)=αQ+βT gravity. They checked energy conditions for shape function (b(r)=r0(r0r)η), and used the Israel junction condition to observe the stability of a thin shell around WH.

In this gravity, Parsaei et al. [34] investigated asymptotically flat WH solutions by computing the shape function using various equations of state. They also discussed some of the mathematical/physical characteristics of solutions such as the energy and boundary conditions. By considering three various redshift functions along with anisotropic fluid at the throat, Hassan et al. [35] demonstrated the effects of the generalized uncertainty principle in Casimir WHs. By extending the symmetric teleparallel gravity, Xu et al. [21] created a scenario where gravitational action is dependent on an arbitrary function of NM as well as the trace of the stress-energy tensor. This theory combines descriptions from the quantum realm in the classical theory with gravitational effects via the NM function. The construction of this gravitational theory is analogous to that of f(R,T) theory [36], [37] but NM characterizing the symmetric teleparallel formulation is used in place of the conventional Ricci scalar. The accompanying of Q and T add non-conservation of the stress-energy tensor. Xu et al. [38] examined this theory's cosmic consequences and analyzed a number of cosmological models by imposing certain functional forms f(Q,T). They compared their predictions to those of the conventional ΛCDM model. Various cosmic parameters were obtained by Pradhan and Dixit [39] in the FRW universe. Using the present data sets such as Joint Light Curve Analysis data sets, union 2.1 compilation of SNe Ia data sets, and Hubble data sets, they were able to calculate the best-suited values of model parameters for observational constraints on the model. Tayde et al. [40] observed the violation of NEC for WH geometries in the vicinity of f(Q,T) gravity for a linear EoS and the anisotropy relation and used Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkov (TOV) equation for stability analysis.

In the present work, we investigate static spherically symmetric anisotropic WH structures by considering specific forms of redshift and shape functions in the arena of f(Q,T) gravity. The next section is devoted to discussing the basic formulation of the f(Q,T) theory of gravity as well as the construction of the field equations. In section 3, we examine the physical behavior of WH solutions through the analysis of energy conditions and stability criterion. We also discuss the embedding diagram for both considered shape functions. Final remarks are given in the last section.

Wormhole solutions

In this section, we choose distinct forms of redshift as well as shape functions and discuss the physical characteristics including energy conditions and stability analysis. The physically plausible matter configuration is discussed through the energy constraints, which are categorized into NEC, WEC and SEC as•NEC: ρ+pr≥0, ρ+pt≥0.•WEC: ρ≥0, ρ+pr≥0, ρ+pt≥0.•SEC: ρ+pr≥0, ρ+pt≥0, ρ+pr+2pt≥0. The NEC is important in GR because its violation reveals the appearance of exotic matter at WHs's throat. As a result, the graphical behavior of NEC is frequently analyzed for WH structures in GR. Additionally, positive energy density is necessary for a realistic matter configuration which sustains the WH geometries. To determine the stability of WH structures, the TOV equation plays a vital role. For the considered WH metric as well as matter distribution, TOV equation turns out to be [41](21)dprdr+Φ′(ρ+pr)−2r(pt−pr)=0, where Fh (hydrostatic force), Fg (gravitational force) and Fa (anisotropic force) are defined as(22)Fh=−dprdr,Fg=−Φ′(ρ+pr),Fa=2r(pt−pr). For the WHs to be remained in equilibrium position, these forces must satisfy the condition, sum of all forces must be zero Fh+Fg+Fa=0.

Concluding remarks

In the same spacetime manifold, the WHs, which theoretically are thought to exist, can connect two distinct spatial regions separated by a spacelike interval. Due to the advancements in gravitational wave astronomy, it is possible to foresee speculatively a framework in which signs of the WH might be discovered. The stable as well as traversable WH solutions in GR have a recognized pathology where the energy constraints are not fulfilled. In the context of classical GR, it is challenging to investigate standard model extensions without the use of exotic matter. In the present era, the analysis of WH structures in modified gravitational theories is significant because it is possible to construct stable models in these theories without the violation of energy constraints.

Using f(Q,T) theory of gravity is a novel technique as it may supply new insights into astrophysical objects such as black holes and WHs. As it is possible to construct stable solutions in these theories without violating energy conditions, the current analysis of WHs in modified theories of gravity is significant. The novel method of applying the f(Q,T) theory of gravity has the potential to offer fresh perspectives on astrophysical phenomena. According to this theory, the energy-momentum tensor T serves as a link between the gravitational influence and the NM function Q and manifestations from the quantum field. As earlier Tangphati et al. [43] discussed the traversable WH in f(R) massive gravity. Using different shape functions and for the different values of model parameter α. Shamir et al. [44] investigated the solutions of traversable WH in the context of f(R,G) gravity. They examined the solutions for isotropic and anisotropic fluids. Sahoo et al. [45] gave the solution for traversable WH in the traceless f(R,T) gravity with an EoS pr=ωρ.

Zubair et al. [46] also discussed the existence of WHs in f(R,T) gravity, they constructed the WH solutions with the nonexotic matter. Furthermore, Konoplya and Zhidenko [47] obtained WH solutions in the Einstein-Dirac-Maxwell theory. They computed normalizable numerical solutions and found required peculiar behavior at the throat. Also, WH geometries are discussed by Banerjee et al. [28] in the context of f(Q) gravity, they also checked the nature of energy conditions. In this work, we have constructed anisotropic WH solutions in the vicinity of f(Q,T) gravity. For this purpose, the field equations are constructed by taking a linear functional form of f(Q,T) as f(Q,T)=αQ+βT. We have considered two different forms of Φ(r), i.e., constant and variable redshift functions, and for both cases, the shape function is chosen in two distinct forms (b(r)=r0+r0((rr0)γ−1)andb(r)=r0ln⁡(r+1)ln⁡(r0+1)) [5], resulting four different WH solutions. The viability of determined solutions is examined by analyzing the behavior of energy conditions as well as the equilibrium criterion.

For this analysis, we need the values of free parameters (α,β, and γ) appearing in the field equations. It is noted that the results are viable only for 0<γ<1 while α and β can be chosen arbitrarily. For the evaluation of energy conditions involving the parameter γ, we have taken 0<γ<0.5, α=0.5 and β=0.15. It is found that all the energy constraints are fulfilled. For the solutions involving only model parameters α and β, three different sets of values of these parameters, i.e., α=0.5, β=0.15; α=0.4, β=0.3 and α=0.6, β=0.4 are taken into account. Also, these values of parameters lead to the validity of energy conditions. Furthermore, we have analyzed the equilibrium criterion via the TOV equation for all constructed solutions. It is demonstrated that our solutions fulfill the equilibrium criterion, which ensures the stability of WHs.

Here, we also observe that if we put β=0, then our results are reduced to f(Q) gravity, and the results of f(Q) gravity are compatible with the results of f(Q,T) gravity. And for α=0, we get solutions in f(T) gravity, where density and pressure components vanish. It is deduced that the determined solutions are feasible as all the necessary conditions for the viability of WH structures are fulfilled.

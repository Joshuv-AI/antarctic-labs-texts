On the boundedness of some real line arrangements of type at most one
Marek Janasz

Abstract
In this note, we show that real line arrangements of type at most one, admitting only intersection points of multiplicity at most five, satisfy certain boundedness properties. In particular, we prove that a free real arrangement of d d lines with intersection multiplicities bounded by 5 5 can have at most 522 522 lines and consequently there exist only finitely many combinatorial types of such arrangements.



1
Introduction

The study of line arrangements in the real and complex projective plane lies at the intersection
of combinatorics, algebraic geometry, and singularity theory. Over the past decades, considerable
attention has been devoted to understanding how algebraic properties of arrangements, such as
freeness or plus-one generated, interact with their combinatorial structure, in particular with the
distribution and multiplicities of intersection points.

A central notion in this area is that of a
free plane curve
, introduced by K. Saito
[
10
]
in a general setting and
later developed extensively for hyperplane arrangements. The freeness can be characterized algebraically via the module of Jacobian syzygies or, equivalently,
via the splitting type of the sheaf of logarithmic vector fields
[
4
]
. Free arrangements enjoy
remarkable structural properties, yet their existence is strongly constrained by combinatorial and geometric conditions. One of the fundamental topics in the subject is to determine whether certain classes of arrangements are
bounded
, that is, whether there exists an absolute
upper bound on the number of lines once suitable restrictions on the intersection multiplicities
are imposed.

For real line arrangements, the situation is particularly subtle due to the presence of
additional geometric inequalities, such as Melchior’s or Shnurnikov’s inequality
[
8
,
12
]
, which have no direct complex
counterpart. These inequalities often lead to strong restrictions on the possible combinatorics
of real arrangements and have been successfully used to prove boundedness results in several
contexts. In particular, boundedness results are known for real free line arrangements with
double, triple and quadruple points
[
9
]
.

More recently, the class of
plus-one generated
curves has emerged as a natural
generalization of free and nearly free curves. Introduced and systematically studied by Abe in the setting of hyperplane arrangements
[
1
]
, and later by Dimca and Sticlaru
[
5
]
in the context of plane curves, these objects form the next level in the hierarchy of plane curves defined by the complexity of their Jacobian syzygies. From the perspective of line arrangements, this class provides a natural testing ground for investigating how far boundedness phenomena extend beyond the free case.

The purpose of the present paper is to establish new boundedness results for real line
arrangements of type at most one under explicit constraints on the multiplicities of intersection
points. Our first main result concerns real free line arrangements whose intersection points have
multiplicity at most five. We prove that such arrangements are bounded, and more precisely, that
they consist of at most
522
lines. The proof combines algebraic constraints coming from
freeness, refined combinatorial counting arguments, and classical inequalities for real line
arrangements.

Our second main result addresses real plus-one generated line arrangements admitting only double,
triple and quadruple intersection points. In this setting, we prove a substantially stronger
bound, showing that the number of lines is at most
47
. This demonstrates that boundedness
persists beyond the class of free arrangements and highlights the rigidity imposed by the
plus-one generated condition when combined with low intersection multiplicities.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we recall the necessary background on Jacobian
syzygies, Milnor algebras, and the notions of freeness, plus-one generation, and the type of a
plane curve. Section 3 is devoted to real free line arrangements with points of multiplicity at
most five, where we first establish combinatorial bounds on high-multiplicity intersection
points and then prove the main boundedness theorem. In Section 4 we treat the case of real
plus-one generated line arrangements with intersection multiplicities at most four and obtain
the corresponding boundedness result.

2
Preliminaries

We follow the notation introduced in
[
4
]
.
Let us denote by
S:=\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
the homogeneous coordinate ring of

\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be a reduced plane curve of degree
d

defined by a homogeneous polynomial
f\in S_{d}
.
We denote by
J_{f}
the Jacobian ideal of
f
which is the ideal generated by the all partials, i.e.,

J_{f}=\langle f_{x},f_{y},f_{z}\rangle.

We consider the graded
S
-module of Jacobian syzygies of
f
, defined by

AR(f)=\{(a,b,c)\in S^{3}:af_{x}+bf_{y}+cf_{z}=0\}.

The minimal degree of a non-trivial Jacobian relation of
f
is defined as

{\rm mdr}(f):=\min\{r\geq 0:AR(f)_{r}\neq 0\}.

The Milnor algebra associated with
f
is defined as

M(f):=S/J_{f}.

Definition 2.1
.

A reduced plane curve
C
is called an
m
-syzygy curve
if its Milnor
algebra
M(f)
admits a minimal graded free resolution of the form

0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m-2}S(-e_{i})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}S(1-d-d_{i})\longrightarrow S^{3}(1-d)\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow M(f)\longrightarrow 0,

where the integers satisfy

e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\ldots\leq e_{m-2}\quad\text{and}\quad 1\leq d_{1}\leq\ldots\leq d_{m}.

The ordered
m
-tuple
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
is called the set of
exponents
of

C
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
2
, Definition 1.1]
)
.

Let
C=\{f=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be a reduced curve of degree
d
, and let
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
be the two smallest degrees of a minimal system of generators of the module of Jacobian syzygies
AR(f)
.
We say that
C
has
type

t(C)
, where the positive integer
t(C)
is defined as

t(C)=d_{1}+d_{2}+1-d.

The following definitions come from
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Definition 2.3
.

A reduced plane curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
is said to be
free
with the exponents
(d_{1},d_{2})
if and only if
t(C)=0
.

Definition 2.4
.

A reduced plane curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
is said to be
plus-one generated
with the exponents
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
if and only if
t(C)=1
.

In order to decide whether a given curve either is free or plus-one generated, we can use the following crucial results.

Theorem 2.5

(du Plessis – Wall,
[
7
]
)
.

Let
C=\{f=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be a reduced curve of degree
d
and let
d_{1}={\rm mdr}(f)
. Let us denote the total Tjurina number of
C
by
\tau(C)
.
Then the following two cases hold.

a)

If
d_{1}<d/2
, then
\tau(C)\leq\tau_{max}(d,d_{1})=(d-1)(d-d_{1}-1)+d_{1}^{2}
and the equality holds if and only if the curve
C
is free.

b)

If
d/2\leq d_{1}\leq d-1
, then

\tau(C)\leq\tau_{max}(d,d_{1})
,
where, in this case, we set

\tau_{max}(d,d_{1})=(d-1)(d-d_{1}-1)+d_{1}^{2}-\binom{2d_{1}-d+2}{2}.

Proposition 2.6

(Dimca – Sticlaru,
[
5
]
)
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be a reduced plane curve defined by
f=0
of degree
d\geq 3
, admitting exactly three minimal syzygies with exponents
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
.
Then
C
is plus-one generated if and only if its total Tjurina number satisfies

\tau(C)=(d-1)^{2}-d_{1}(d-d_{1}-1)-(d_{3}-d_{2}+1).

3
The boundedness of real free line arrangements with points of multiplicity at most five

We will use the following inequality that comes from
[
12
]
. For a line arrangement
\mathcal{L}
we denote by
t_{k}=t_{k}(\mathcal{L})
the number of
k
-fold intersection points in
\mathcal{L}
.

Theorem 3.1

(Shnurnikov)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
be an arrangement of
d
lines such that
t_{d}=t_{d-1}=t_{d-2}=0
. Then the following inequality holds:

t_{2}+\frac{3}{2}t_{3}\;\geq\;8+\sum_{r\geq 4}\frac{4r-15}{2}\,t_{r}.

We start with a general result of independent interest.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{\mathcal{L}}
be an arrangement of
d
lines in
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Fix an integer
k\geq 4
and assume that all intersection points have multiplicity at most
k
,
i.e.
t_{i}(\mathcal{L})=0
for all
i\geq k+1
. Then the number of
k
–fold
intersection points
t_{k}
satisfies the following bounds.

(1) Case
d\geq k+3
.
One has

t_{k}\;\leq\;\frac{d(d-1)-16}{k^{2}+3k-15}.

(2) General case.
One has

t_{k}\;\leq\;\frac{d}{k}\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{k-1}\right\rfloor.

In particular, for every
d\geq 2
we have

t_{k}\;\leq\;\max\!\left\{\frac{d}{k}\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{k-1}\right\rfloor,\;\left\lfloor\frac{d(d-1)-16}{k^{2}+3k-15}\right\rfloor\right\},

where the second term makes sense only when
d\geq k+3
.

Proof.

Counting unordered pairs of distinct lines in two ways yields

\binom{d}{2}\;=\;\sum_{r\geq 2}\binom{r}{2}\,t_{r}.

(1)

Under the assumption
t_{r}=0
for
r\geq k+1
, identity (
1
) becomes

\binom{d}{2}\;=\;t_{2}+\sum_{r=3}^{k}\binom{r}{2}\,t_{r},

(2)

hence

t_{2}\;=\;\binom{d}{2}-\sum_{r=3}^{k}\binom{r}{2}\,t_{r}.

(3)

The case
d\geq k+3
.

Then the maximal multiplicity satisfies
m(\mathcal{L})\leq k\leq d-3
, so we may apply Shnurnikov’s inequality

t_{2}+\frac{3}{2}t_{3}\;\geq\;8+\sum_{r\geq 4}\frac{4r-15}{2}\,t_{r}.

(4)

Since
t_{r}=0
for
r\geq k+1
, (
4
) reduces to

t_{2}+\frac{3}{2}t_{3}\;\geq\;8+\sum_{r=4}^{k}\frac{4r-15}{2}\,t_{r}.

(5)

Substituting (
3
) into (
5
) gives

\binom{d}{2}-\sum_{r=3}^{k}\binom{r}{2}\,t_{r}+\frac{3}{2}t_{3}\;\geq\;8+\sum_{r=4}^{k}\frac{4r-15}{2}\,t_{r},

hence, after rearranging,

\binom{d}{2}-8\;\geq\;\frac{3}{2}t_{3}+\sum_{r=4}^{k}\left(\binom{r}{2}+\frac{4r-15}{2}\right)t_{r}.

(6)

All terms on the right-hand side are nonnegative. In particular,

\binom{d}{2}-8\;\geq\;\left(\binom{k}{2}+\frac{4k-15}{2}\right)t_{k}.

A direct computation yields

\binom{k}{2}+\frac{4k-15}{2}=\frac{k(k-1)}{2}+\frac{4k-15}{2}=\frac{k^{2}+3k-15}{2}.

Therefore,

t_{k}\;\leq\;\frac{2\bigl(\binom{d}{2}-8\bigr)}{k^{2}+3k-15}=\frac{d(d-1)-16}{k^{2}+3k-15},

which proves (1).

General bound.

Fix a line arrangement
\mathcal{L}
. For every line
\ell
in
\mathcal{L}
, the following obvious count holds

(d-1)=\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell}(r-1)t_{r,\ell},

where
t_{r,\ell}
denotes the number of
r
-fold points located on the line
\ell
.
The above formulate tells us that each
k
–fold point from
{\rm Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell
costs
k-1
intersections (since
k-1
other lines meet
\mathcal{L}
at that point). Hence the number of
k
–fold points in
{\rm Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell
is at most
\lfloor(d-1)/(k-1)\rfloor
.
Counting incidences between lines and
k
–fold points gives us

k\,t_{k}\;\leq\;d\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{k-1}\right\rfloor,

and thus

t_{k}\;\leq\;\frac{d}{k}\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{k-1}\right\rfloor,

which proves (2). The final unified estimate follows immediately.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

For
k=5
, part (1) specializes to

t_{5}\leq\frac{d(d-1)-16}{25}.

For the proof of the next result, which is our main contribution in the present paper, we need to recall Melchior’s inequality
[
8
]
.

Theorem 3.4

(Melchior)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
be an arrangement of
d\geq 3
lines such that
t_{d}=0
. Then

t_{2}\geq 3+t_{4}+2t_{5}+3t_{6}+\ldots+(d-4)t_{d-1}.

(7)

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
be an arrangement of
d\geq 8
lines such that
t_{d}=0
. Assume that
\mathcal{L}
is free. Then
d\leq 522
.

Proof.

We assumed that
\mathcal{L}
is free which implies that the following equality holds:

d_{1}^{2}-d_{1}(d-1)+(d-1)^{2}=\tau(\mathcal{L})=t_{2}+4t_{3}+9t_{4}+16t_{5},

where
d_{1}={\rm mdr}(\mathcal{L})
. After applying the naive combinatorial count we obtain

d_{1}^{2}-d_{1}(d-1)+(t_{2}+2t_{3}+3t_{4}+4t_{5}-d+1)=0.

We compute the discriminant of the above equation and we obtain

\triangle_{d_{1}}:=(d-1)^{2}-4(1-d)-4(t_{2}+2t_{3}+3t_{4}+4t_{5}).

(8)

Since
\mathcal{L}
is free we have
\triangle_{d_{1}}\geq 0
, and this means that

(d-1)^{2}+4d-4\geq 4t_{2}+8t_{3}+12t_{4}+16t_{5}=2t_{2}+2t_{3}-4t_{5}+d(d-1).

Hence

\frac{3}{2}(d-1)+2t_{5}\geq t_{2}+t_{3}.

(9)

Moreover, equation (
8
) gives us yet another non-trivial bound on the intersection points, namely

\frac{1}{4}(d^{2}+2d-3)\geq t_{2}+2t_{3}+3t_{4}+4t_{5}.

(10)

Let us come back the naïve combinatorial count:

\frac{d(d-1)}{2}=(t_{2}+2t_{3}+3t_{4}+4t_{5})+t_{3}+3(t_{4}+2t_{5})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{geq}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{4}(d^{2}+2d-3)+t_{3}+3(t_{4}+2t_{5})\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Melchior}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{4}(d^{2}+2d-3)+t_{3}+3(t_{2}-3)\leq\frac{1}{4}(d^{2}+2d-3)-9+3(t_{2}+t_{3})\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{n5}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{4}(d^{2}+2d-3)-9+\frac{9}{2}(d-1)+6t_{5},

and hence we get

t_{5}\geq\frac{1}{24}(d-3)(d-19).

On the other hand we have an upper-bound of the form

t_{5}\leq\frac{d(d-1)-16}{25},

hence we get

\frac{1}{24}(d-3)(d-19)\leq t_{5}\leq\frac{1}{25}(d(d-1)-16),

which gives us
d\in[8,9,10,\ldots,522],
and this completes the proof.
∎

4
The boundedness of real plus-one generated line arrangements with points of multiplicity at most four

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
be an arrangement of
d\geq 3
lines such that
t_{d}=0
and it admits only double, triple and quadruple intersection points. Assume that
\mathcal{L}
is plus-one generated. Then
d\leq 47
.

Proof.

Our proof goes along similar lines as for Theorem
3.5
. First of all, the assumption that
\mathcal{L}
is plus-one generated implies that

t_{2}+4t_{3}+9t_{4}=d(d-1)-t_{2}-2t_{3}-3t_{4}=(d-1)^{2}-d_{1}(d-d_{1}-1)-h,

where
h:=d_{3}-d_{2}+1\geq 1
.
This leads us to

d_{1}^{2}-d_{1}(d-1)+(t_{2}+2t_{3}+3t_{4}-d+1-h)=0.

The condition that
\mathcal{L}
is plus-one generated implies that the discriminant
\triangle_{d_{1}}\geq 0
, and hence we get

d^{2}+2d-3+4h\geq 4t_{2}+8t_{3}+12t_{4}=2(t_{2}+t_{3})+d(d-1).

Summing up, we reach the following inequality:

t_{2}+t_{3}\leq\frac{3(d-1)}{2}+2h.

(11)

Let us come back to the naïve combinatorial count, we have

d(d-1)=2t_{2}+6t_{3}+12t_{4}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Melchior}}}{{\leq}}14t_{2}+6t_{3}-36\leq 14(t_{2}+t_{3})-36\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eee}}}{{\leq}}21(d-1)+28h-36,

and hence we get

d^{2}-22d+57-28h\leq 0.

This gives us that
d\leq 11+2\sqrt{16+7h}.

Since for line arrangements we have
h\leq d-2
(
[
11
]
, Corollary 3.5) we finally obtain the following inequality

d\leq 11+2\sqrt{2+7d},

which gives us
d\leq 47
, and this completes the proof.
∎

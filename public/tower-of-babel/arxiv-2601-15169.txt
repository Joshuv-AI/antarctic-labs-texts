Assessing Orbital Optimization in Variational and Diffusion Monte Carlo Preprint: SAND2026-16112O
Cody A. Melton, Jaron T. Krogel

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the fidelity of orbital optimization in variational Monte Carlo to improve diffusion Monte Carlo results on correlated magnetic systems, using CrSBr as a model system. We compare the performance of different optimization methods, showing that stochastic reconfiguration is a robust and reliable optimizer. We show that short range Jastrow factors are important for improving diffusion Monte Carlo, regardless of the quality of orbitals. Large active spaces are required to converge the variational energy, but ulitmately orbital optimization produces worse diffusion Monte Carlo energies when compared to standard orbitals from density functional theory. We show that this increased bias is due to larger locality errors from the use of pseudopotentials, while the fixed-node error is actually improved by using orbital optimization. Additionally, for observables other than energy, orbital optimization produces a systematically smaller mixed-estimator bias. Ultimately, we believe orbital optimization provides a reliable method to improve variational and pure fixed-node energies as well as lower mixed-estimator bias.

I
Introduction

A significant challenge in computational condensed matter physics is to accurately predict the properties of real materials.
By far the most popular approach is density functional theory
20
, which has been extremely successful in describing weakly correlated systems.
However, strong correlation and its influence on charge, spin, lattice, and topological degrees of freedom impedes an accurate density functional theory description without empirical guidance from experiment, i.e. using experimental data to help select the “best" functional.
Explicit many-body methods such as quantum Monte Carlo
14
;
29
are well-equipped to capture these subtle effects and provide a more accurate description of the system from first principles, without relying on empirical guidance.
While standard quantum Monte Carlo workflows have been largely successful
38
;
13
;
2
;
49
, the fact that these methods are variational implies that they should be systematically improvable given a particular wave function ansatz.
For example, moving toward increasingly larger multi-determinant wave functions which approach the full configuration interaction limit show significantly improved energetics at both the variational Monte Carlo and diffusion Monte Carlo level, in both molecular and condensed systems
6
;
31
;
37
;
41
.
Although multi-determinant wave functions have been demonstrated for condensed systems, they do not scale favorably with system size and therefore make it difficult for standard thermodynamic extrapolations as well as large defect systems.
Therefore, it remains desirable to obtain the best single determinant wave function which scales favorably to hundreds of atoms.

The largest source of error in diffusion Monte Carlo comes from the fixed-node approximation
4
, which is controlled by the accuracy of the nodal surface of the underlying trial wave function.
Historically, the fixed-node error has been minimized by calculating energetics using a variety of wave functions built from density functional theory orbitals and selecting the set that yields the lowest diffusion Monte Carlo energy.
This can be achieved by calculating a broad range of functionals, or by scanning single parameter functionals such as density functional theory+U or hybrid functional exact exchange mixing fractions
23
;
30
.
While this does provide the lowest diffusion Monte Carlo energy over the orbital sets considered, this approach does not provide a systematic procedure to guide us to the optimal single particle orbital set.
Coupling direct diffusion Monte Carlo into an optimization scheme where the free parameters of the wave function are directly optimized would allow for systematic improvement toward the optimal nodal surface, but in general this is too expensive.

Direct optimization of the wave function at the variational Monte Carlo level is most often used to optimize Jastrow parameters.
However, in principle any parameter in the wave function can be optimized, including those that directly influence the nodal surface.
For example, full optimization of the Jastrow, determinant expansion parameters, orbital rotation parameters, and underlying orbital basis set parameters can all be optimized variationally
44
;
45
.
If we limit ourselves to single determinant wave functions, which is of interest for condensed system where more general ansatze are still prohibitive, we have the freedom to optimize rotations between our occupied and virtual orbitals as well as potentially optimizing the underlying basis functions.
Directly optimizing the orbitals through orbital rotations, which we will denote throughout this paper denote as orbital optimization, is the simplest approach to modifying the nodal surface at the variational Monte Carlo level and potentially yield improved nodal surfaces within diffusion Monte Carlo.
Previously, this has been shown to provide starting point independence from the underlying density functional theory orbital set
46

, however there is evidence that orbital optimization alone may not improve the nodal surface even if the variational Monte Carlo energy is improved
11
.

A class of materials where systematically improvable wave functions in quantum Monte Carlo could have a significant impact is correlated magnetic systems.
For example, current density functional theory functional approximations indicate an “inverse Jacob’s ladder" when predicting the magnetic moments of bulk ferromagnetic transition metal materials
15
, where more advanced functionals are less accurate than the simplest local density approximation.
For 2D materials, where electron correlation can be enhanced by the reduced dimensionality, mean-field descriptions of the electronic structure can also be insufficient.
For example, in CrI
3
different functionals predict a wide range of lattice structures, magnetic moments, and even the physical origin of magnetism
42
.
Given the potential technological impact of magnetic 2D materials, ranging from spintronics to quantum information
50
, many-body methods such as quantum Monte Carlo will become increasingly valuable.

In this work, we apply orbital optimization as an approach to improve the wave function on CrSBr, a semiconducting 2D van der Waals material.
CrSBr has recently garnered a lot of attention due to high critical temperature and air stability at ambient conditions, which makes it an attractive candidate for experiment
51
.
We investigate the fidelity of orbital optimization in variational Monte Carlo and its impact on diffusion Monte Carlo from a variety of fronts for CrSBr.
We consider the influence of different optimization procedures, each of which have slightly different cost functions, and compare its impact to both variational Monte Carlo and diffusion Monte Carlo.
We also consider the coupling between orbital rotation parameters and varying fidelity Jastrow factors.
Another consideration is how many virtual orbitals (active space) are necessary in order to converge the energy at both the variational Monte Carlo and diffusion Monte Carlo level using the Kohn-Sham orbitals we consider here.

Ultimately, we find that orbital optimization carried out at the variational Monte Carlo level is not able to produce the lowest diffusion Monte Carlo energy when compared against scans using density functional theory+U orbitals, even when considering large active spaces for the orbital rotations.
This is not unanticipated since theoretically there is no guarantee that improved variational wave functions within a restricted and approximate ansatz will yield improved diffusion Monte Carlo energies.
Whether or not this is true in practice for correlated materials is an important question to answer.
We show that orbital optimization is marginally improving the nodal surface, and therefore reducing the fixed node error.
However, the diffusion Monte Carlo energies are higher due to an increased magnitude in the locality error, which arise from the use of nonlocal pseudopotentials in the Hamiltonian.
We also note that even though the diffusion Monte Carlo energetics are marginally worse, for observables other than the energy we show that it is still be beneficial to use orbital optimization as the significant improvement at the variational level tends to reduce mixed estimator biases.
This increased accuracy is important because quantities like the electron density, for example, can be used to benchmark, and potentially improve, other theories such as DFT.

II.1
Wavefunction Ansatz

The Slater-Jastrow wave function is the most commonly used ansatz in the quantum Monte Carlo community.
Here we extend the Slater-Jastrow wave function to include orbital optimization through the use of a unitary operator
e^{\hat{\kappa}_{\sigma}}
, namely

\Psi_{T}(\mathbf{R})=e^{-J(\mathbf{R})}\prod_{\sigma}e^{\hat{\kappa}_{\sigma}}\det\left[\phi^{\sigma}_{i}(\mathbf{r}_{j,\sigma})\right],

(1)

where
J(\mathbf{R})
is the Jastrow factor and the antisymmetric part is formed out of Slater determinants of single particle orbitals coming from density functional theory calculations, represented as a matrix
\phi_{i}^{\sigma}(\mathbf{r}_{j,\sigma})
with orbital index
i
, electron index
j
and spin index
\sigma
.
The Jastrow factor can be formally expanded over
n
-body interactions

J=\sum_{i,\sigma}u_{1}(\mathbf{r}_{i,\sigma})+\sum_{i,j,\sigma,\mu}u_{2}(\mathbf{r}_{i,\sigma},\mathbf{r}_{j,\mu})+\sum_{i,j,k}u_{3}(\mathbf{r}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{j},\mathbf{r}_{k})+\ldots

(2)

In
QMCPACK
, the expansion is truncated at
u_{2}
, including short-ranged electron-ion and electron-electron-ion inhomogeneous terms. The Kato cusp conditions
21
are incorporated into the homogeneous part of the two-body function.
We may optionally include some long-ranged Jastrow terms which are important in the smallest simulation cells, which we call
k
-space Jastrows.
A general two-body
k
-space Jastrow
12
is typically written in terms of the structure factor

J^{ab}_{k}=\sum_{\mathbf{G}\neq 0}a_{\mathbf{G}}\rho^{a}_{\mathbf{G}}\rho^{b}_{-\mathbf{G}},

(3)

where
\mathbf{G}
are the standard
\mathbf{G}
-vectors of the reciprocal lattice and
a_{\mathbf{G}}
are the optimizable coefficients.
The density operator for species
a
in reciprocal space is given by

\rho^{a}_{\mathbf{G}}=\sum_{i}^{N_{a}}e^{i\mathbf{G}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{i}}.

(4)

In this work we will consider 1-body
k
-space Jastrows between electrons and ions, and 2-body
k
-space Jastrows between electrons only.
The
\mathbf{G}
-vector cutoff in reciprocal space is always chosen as
G_{c}=\left(6N_{e}\pi^{2}/V\right)^{1/3}
, where
N_{e}
is the number of electrons and
V
is the volume of the simulation cell.

The orbital optimization is accomplished by optimizing the parameters in the unitary operator
e^{\hat{\kappa}_{\sigma}}
, where

\hat{\kappa}_{\sigma}=\sum\limits_{i,j}\kappa^{\sigma}_{ji}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\sigma}\hat{a}_{j,\sigma}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}\hat{a}_{i,\sigma}\right)

(5)

where the
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\sigma},\hat{a}_{i,\sigma}
operators are the creation/annihilation operators for spin orbital
(i,\sigma)

44
;
45
.
The
\kappa^{\sigma}_{ji}
is an anti-hermitian matrix for spin-channel
\sigma
, whose elements are the tunable parameters.
For this work, we only consider rotations the orbital set we obtain from our underlying DFT set of orbitals, i.e. the Kohn-Sham occupied and virtual orbitals.
The variational freedom can be increased by enlarging the active space by adding more virtual orbitals.

II.2
Optimizers

Optimization of the wave function in quantum Monte Carlo methods have historically relied on two major approaches, namely the linear method
47
and stochastic reconfiguration
40
;
7
.
We briefly summarize the differences in the optimization approaches available within QMCPACK.
We also note that throughout this work, all optimizations steps use approximately 5
\times 10^{6}
samples per optimization iteration in order to sufficiently resolve the variational energy at each step of the optimization.

II.2.1
One-Shift Only Optimizer

The One-Shift (one-shift only) optimizer in QMCPACK is based on the linear method
44
;
47
, which is historically the most popular approach used in real space quantum Monte Carlo.
The linear method works by expanding the proposed wave function
|\Psi^{\prime}\rangle
in the space of parameter derivatives around the current wave function
|\Psi_{0}\rangle
,

|\Psi^{\prime}\rangle=|\Psi_{0}\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\Delta\theta_{i}\left|\Psi_{\theta_{i}}\right\rangle,

(6)

where
\Delta\bm{\theta}=\left\{\Delta\theta_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{N_{p}}
is the vector of
N_{p}
variational parameters and
|\Psi_{\theta_{i}}\rangle=\partial_{\theta_{i}}|\Psi_{0}\rangle
are the vector of parameter derivatives of the wave function.
Minimizing the variational energy of the new wave function leads to an equation for the parameter update given by a generalized eigenvalue problem

\bm{H}\cdot\Delta\bm{\theta}=E\bm{S}\cdot\Delta\bm{\theta}

(7)

where
\Delta\theta
comprises a potential parameter update.
The one-shift only optimizer accepts or rejects the parameter update based on the magnitude of the wavefunction ratio found via correlated sampling, with small ratios resulting in rejection.
As this is the most common optimizer used for typical Jastrow optimizations in
QMCPACK
, we will focus only on this variant of the linear method.

II.2.2
Stochastic Reconfiguration

While the one-shift only is based on the linear method, which is a quasi-Newton scheme, another popular optimization method related to gradient descent methods is stochastic reconfiguration
39
;
40
.
A simple formulation of SR updates the wavefunction based on a short time projection:

\displaystyle|\Psi^{\prime}\rangle=e^{-\tau\hat{H}}|\Psi_{0}\rangle\approx(1-\tau\hat{H})|\Psi_{0}\rangle

(8)

Expanding in wave function derivatives (Eq. (
6
)) gives a linear equation for the parameter update

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}\Delta\bm{\theta}=-\tau\mathbf{h}

(9)

This optimizer has the benefit that parameter derivatives of the Hamiltonian do not need to be evaluated, only parameter derivatives of the wave function.
stochastic reconfiguration methods have become popular in the machine learning wave function community since stochastic reconfiguration has favorable scaling with parameters relative to linear methods
10
;
18
;
3
.
In pure stochastic reconfiguration, we directly accept the parameter updates from Eq. (
9
).
As a potential way to accelerate convergence, a correlated sampling line search can be performed
48
.
The cost function minimized along the line search can be chosen as a linear combination of energy and variance.
We refer to this optimizer throughout as stochastic reconfiguration with line search.

II.3
DMC

The details of diffusion Monte Carlo have been covered extensively elsewhere
14
;
29
;
22
, so we briefly describe it here.
The accuracy of diffusion Monte Carlo comes from the imaginary time propagator, where the ground state wave function is projected out from some initial trial wave function in the long imaginary time limit, i.e.
|\Phi_{0}\rangle=\lim\limits_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left[-\tau(\hat{H}-E_{0})\right]|\Phi\rangle
.
To avoid the fermion sign problem, we use the fixed node approximation in which the nodes of the projected wavefunction are constrained to match those of the trial wavefunction.
This approximation is often assumed to be the largest source of bias in DMC calculations.

Since almost all practical calculations utilize pseudopotentials to remove inert core electrons, there is an additional sign problem in the projector since the nonlocal pseudopotential matrix elements can be positive or negative.
The sign problem can be avoided if we simply ignore the part of the Green’s function arising from the nonlocal pseudopotential, known as the locality approximation
32
, but as a result the variational principle is lost.
In the alternative “T-moves”
9
;
8
approach, the variational principle is restored but with added bias due to neglect of the sign-changing terms.
The quality of the Jastrow factors directly impact the magnitude of the locality error, and previous work has shown that the pure fixed node energy can be determined via extrapolations with respect to the variational Monte Carlo and diffusion Monte Carlo energies as the Jastrow factor is varied, thus also providing the magnitude of the locality error
24
.

A typical strategy to find the “best" Slater-Jastrow wavefunction
is to run DMC with a few different functionals, or perform a 1D scan over parameters such as the Hubbard
U
or exact exchange mixing fraction.
This optimization uses the variational diffusion Monte Carlo energy as the cost function, but only explores a fraction of the variational freedom allowed within the Slater-Jastrow ansatz
However, the variational principle also applies to variational Monte Carlo, permitting wave function optimization at significantly lower cost.
If the Jastrow factor is optimized, for example, the nodal surface remains unchanged, but the magnitude of the locality error is impacted.
By optimizing parameters that directly modify the nodal surface alongside the Jastrows in orbital optimization, we impact both the DMC fixed-node and locality errors.
In this work, we are interested in determining the extent to which VMC orbital optimization results in reductions of errors at the diffusion Monte Carlo level, which we explore in detail below.

III.1
CrSBr

In this work we investigate the performance of orbital optimization in variational Monte Carlo and diffusion Monte Carlo in the context of the correlated 2D semiconductor CrSBr.
The ground state magnetic configuration is antiferromagnetic between each layer and ferromagnetic within a layer, with the magnetic moment lying along the
b

The structure, including the magnetic moments, is shown in Fig.

Neutron scattering experiments indicate a saturation moment of

Since we are currently interested in determining whether or not orbital optimization within variational Monte Carlo also improves properties in diffusion Monte Carlo, we limit ourselves to the primitive cell with a single layer and do not consider finite-size effects for simplicity.
This restricts CrSBr to the FM phase, but since we consider independent orbital rotations for the up and down orbitals,
the performance of OO we examine here should also extend to arbitrary collinear magnetic order.
For the density functional theory+U calculations, we consider Hubbard parameters between 0-5 eV starting with an initial magnetic moment of

In order to probe orbital optimization for the general case, we consider a single random

We first consider the impact of different optimization methods in

Next, we assess how increasing the sophistication of Jastrow factors impacts the quality of the orbitally optimized wavefunction at both the VMC and DMC levels.

Following this, we disentangle the impact of OO on DMC locality and fixed node errors to allow a clearer comparison between OO and the traditional “U-scan”. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time a detailed analysis constrasting these two errors has been made in the context of OO.

III.3
Impact of Jastrow factors on orbital optimization

In the previous section, we used a fixed Jastrow factor, namely a short-ranged 1-, 2- and 3- body (electron-electron-ion) Jastrow which is commonly used in standard QMC workflows.
However, the VMC optimized orbitals should change as different functional forms of the Jastrow factor are included in the optimization.
In order to explore this, we carried out a variety of orbital optimization calculations with different Jastrow forms.
We considered the standard short-ranged 1- and 2- body Jastrow (J12), J12 with one-body k-space (J12k1), two-body kspace (J12k2), and a 1- and 2- body kspace (J12k12) terms.
We also considered those combinations for a short-ranged 1-, 2-, and 3- body Jastrow factor (J123).
We initialized all rotation and Jastrow parameters to zero and independently optimized them for a 45 orbital active space.
Here, we compare wavefunctions comprised of fixed PBE orbitals and optimized Jastrow factors with ones where both the orbitals and the various Jastrow factors are optimized.

In all cases, we observe that improving the Jastrow ansatz recovers increasing amounts of variational energy at the VMC level (see Fig.

The one-body k-space terms offer no improvement at the variational level for any set of orbitals, whereas two-body long range correlation significantly lowers the energy.
With fixed orbitals, the two-body long range correlation significantly improves the energy, and even outperforms a three-body short range Jastrow.
By allowing the k-space terms to be optimized alongside orbital rotation parameters there is a systematic improvement over corresponding fixed orbital sets.
Therefore, orbital optimization is able to recover more variational energy with all Jastrow types, although the gain in energy is smaller if long range correlations are included.

A somewhat different picture emerges in the DMC results for the same wave functions (see Fig.

For the orbitally optimized wave functions (blue), the long-ranged k-space terms do not affect the diffusion Monte Carlo energies of the optimized wavefunctions at fixed short-ranged order (i.e. J12 and J123 separately).
However, a significant effect is seen as the sophistication of the short-ranged form is increased: better short-ranged correlation leads to improved diffusion Monte Carlo energies.
This suggests that further improvements to short-ranged Jastrow forms (see e.g. reference
28

The topic bears further investigation in future work by ourselves or others.

Within the limitations of the Jastrow forms we employ, we are unable to produce optimized orbitals with nodal structures matching or exceeding those found with DFT alone.
In the next section, we additionally explore the role of increased active space size on the nodal structure using the J123 Jastrow form, since the inclusion long-range correlation does not impact the diffusion Monte Carlo quality.

It is also interesting to examine how the DMC energy and VMC variance are related, as shown in Figure
7
.
Somewhat counter intuitively, for the Slater determinant with Jastrow form, wave functions with the lowest diffusion Monte Carlo energy are those with the highest variational Monte Carlo variance. This trends holds clearly across all OO wavefunctions, regardless of the active space size or the employed optimization method.
The DFT+U orbitals result in the highest VMC variances and lowest diffusion Monte Carlo energies of all. Interestingly, using the pure SR method consistently gives higher variance than all other optimization approaches, while also giving the lowest diffusion Monte Carlo energies among the orbital optimization wave functions.

As we have already described, we suffer from both fixed node error as well as localization error of the pseudopotential.
It is possible that the orbital optimization procedure has actually improved the fixed-node error but has increased the locality error sufficiently to raise the diffusion Monte Carlo energy overall, vice-versa, or increased both errors.
As the Jastrow functions are systematically improved to approach the true fixed-node wave function, the locality error vanishes and the variational Monte Carlo energy would agree with the diffusion Monte Carlo energy
24
.

Therefore, we can exploit this fact to estimate the pure fixed-node error through extrapolation using multiple Jastrow factors, shown in Fig.

For each orbital set, each point corresponds to a new Jastrow factor, where we randomly perturb each parameter up to a 5% change to generate a new Jastrow.
Then both variational Monte Carlo and diffusion Monte Carlo calculations are performed with each of those new Jastrows with fixed orbitals.
We consider the unoptimized PBE orbitals, as well as various orbital optimization orbital sets with varying active space size and perform linear fits through the data.
The line where
E_{\rm DMC}=E_{\rm VMC}
represents the theoretical condition where there is no locality error and the remaining bias is due to fixed node error.
From this, it is clear that orbital optimization is actually improving the fixed-node bias since we intersect the
E_{\rm VMC}=E_{\rm DMC}
at lower energies.
This is true for all active spaces, although 120 orbitals shows the largest fixed-node bias among the orbital optimization calculations.

The magnitude of the locality error for a given wave function error is given by the diffusion Monte Carlo energy difference from the pure fixed-node energy.
From this, we see that the locality error is systematically increased when using orbital optimization when compared to fixed PBE orbitals.

Taken together, orbital optimization actually improves the fixed-node error by a few mHa over bare DFT orbitals.
In this case, and for a currently unknown reason, it increases the locality error significantly, which reduces the accuracy of the diffusion Monte Carlo energies, at least within the T-moves approximation.

The overall errors relative to standard fixed orbital sets is nearly the same magnitude as the gain in diffusion Monte Carlo energy moving from a two- to three-body Jastrow factor.
Therefore, further improvements to short-ranged Jastrow factors may be necessary in order to fully surpass the fixed PBE orbitals.
We note that the magnitude of the locality error is largely system and pseudopotential dependent, so it is possible that orbital optimization will show robust improvements in some systems.
Additionally, calculations with reduced or zero locality error, such as all-electron or pseudo-Hamiltonian approaches

, may show more clear improvements from orbital optimization alone, although this is left for future investigations by ourselves or others.

III.5
Mixed Estimator Bias

In many cases we are interested in more than just the diffusion Monte Carlo energies and also care about various observables, such as the spin density, magnetization density, one-body reduced density matrix, etc.
In each case, these observables do not commute with the Hamiltonian and therefore have a mixed-estimator bias due to
sampling the mixed-distribution
\Psi_{T}\Psi_{0}
.
A simple estimate of the correct diffusion Monte Carlo estimator is given by the extrapolated estimator approximation, i.e.

\langle\hat{O}\rangle_{\Psi_{0}}\approx\langle\hat{O}\rangle_{est}=2\langle\hat{O}\rangle_{\rm DMC}-\langle\hat{O}\rangle_{\rm VMC}

(10)

Therefore, the mixed-estimator bias can be estimated by

\Delta\hat{O}_{\rm mix}=\langle\hat{O}\rangle_{\rm DMC}-\langle\hat{O}\rangle_{\rm VMC}

(11)

This implies that as the trial wavefunction becomes more accurate, e.g. via a lower energy or variance, then the mixed estimator approaches the true diffusion Monte Carlo estimator, and therefore the mixed-estimator bias will be reduced.

As seen above, we are in a regime where DFT+U trial wave functions give the lowest diffusion Monte Carlo energies, but significantly higher variational Monte Carlo energies.
Therefore, variationally the orbital optimization wavefunctions are better and potentially result in a lower mixed-estimator bias even if there is a small compromise in the diffusion Monte Carlo energy.
As a check, we show the mixed-estimator bias of the electron-electron interaction energy in Figure
9
.
The electron-electron interaction energy is a useful proxy for other non-commuting observables, as it is closely tied to the electron density.
By performing a DFT+U scan, the mixed-estimator bias is roughly constant across all values of U and is around 0.2 Ha/f.u.
By performing orbital optimization, we see a significant reduction in the mixed estimator bias that improves with active space size.
Even though the diffusion Monte Carlo energies are slightly worse in this case using orbital optimization, we see a very clear improvement in the mixed estimator bias that is due largely to the significantly improved variational wave function used in variational Monte Carlo.

IV
Conclusions

In this work we investigated the impact of orbital optimization in the correlated magnetic insulator CrSBr from a variety of angles.
First we considered the performance of various optimizers available within
QMCPACK
.
Although each method has its roots in minimizing the variational energy, we find the optimizers produce qualitatively different wave functions, with pure stochastic reconfiguration giving the lowest variational energy despite a slower convergence rate.
We speculate that the use of correlated sampling biases the other optimizers, but further investigation is required.
We also probe the coupling between Jastrow quality and nodal surfaces by optimizing various long and short range Jastrows simultaneously with the orbital rotations.
At the variational Monte Carlo level, orbital optimization robustly produces lower energies than fixed density functional theory(+U) orbitals for all Jastrow factors.
Within diffusion Monte Carlo, improvements to the short range Jastrows systematically improves the energy for all orbital treatments, while the impact of long-range Jastrows is negligible.
For CrSBr, large active spaces are required to converge the orbital optimization.
For all active spaces considered, orbital optimization monotonically lowers the energy and is systematically lower than the standard density functional theory+U orbitals scan.
Despite the clear variational improvement, diffusion Monte Carlo on orbital optimization wave functions is systematically higher than the standard density functional theory+U scan for all active spaces, with intermediate active spaces being the worst.
Given the large improvement in variational Monte Carlo of almost
\sim
50 mHa, the bias incurred at the diffusion Monte Carlo level is only
\sim
6 mHa.
Interestingly, we observe a strong correlation between the variational Monte Carlo variance and diffusion Monte Carlo energy, with larger variance showing the best diffusion Monte Carlo energies for any orbital treatment.
By performing energy extrapolations using Jastrow factor variations, we show that orbital optimization is generating better nodes than density functional theory+U.
However, there is a systematic increase in the locality error associated with orbital optimization, resulting in an overall higher diffusion Monte Carlo energy.
We also show there is a systematic improvement in mixed-estimator biases by using orbital optimization, which is important for observables other than the energy such as the spin-density or one-body reduced density matrix
36
;
27
.

Further investigations are needed to discern whether variational Monte Carlo improvements of the orbitals increases the diffusion Monte Carlo locality error is general, and to uncover the cause.
While there is no guarantee that directly improving the variational Monte Carlo energy through optimization should also improve the diffusion Monte Carlo energy, this has been shown for more flexible neural network wave functions, without pseudopotentials and locality errors, which are only a few mHa away from the exact energies, i.e. Fig. 3b in reference
34
.
Seemingly if the variational wave function is sufficiently flexible, improved variational Monte Carlo energies do yield to improved diffusion Monte Carlo energies.
Our results suggest the fixed node energy is indeed improving even for simple Slater Jastrow despite higher locality error.
Because of this, we would expect to see improved diffusion Monte Carlo energies for calculations that avoid locality errors, e.g. all-electron or pseudo-Hamiltonian approaches
25
;
5
;
19
.
Regardless, orbital optimization is a promising and scalable approach to improving the variational energy, fixed-node error, and mixed-estimator biases for arbitrary observables at the cost of a small increase in locality error.

Pedro Barbosa, Arturo Fernández-Pérez, Víctor León

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of the Bruce-Roberts number for holomorphic 1-forms relative to complex analytic varieties. Our main result shows that the Bruce-Roberts number of a 1-form ω \omega with respect to a complex analytic hypersurface X X with an isolated singularity can be expressed in terms of the Ebeling–Gusein-Zade index of ω \omega along X X , the Milnor number of ω \omega and the Tjurina number of X X . This result allows us to recover known formulas for the Bruce-Roberts number of a holomorhic function along X X and to establish connections between this number, the radial index, and the local Euler obstruction of ω \omega along X X . Moreover, we present applications to both global and local holomorphic foliations in complex dimension two.

1.
Introduction and statement of the results

Let
(X,0)
denotes the germ of a complex analytic variety at
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, and let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
be the germ of a holomorphic function at
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
. The
Bruce-Roberts number
associated with
f
relative to
(X,0)
was originally introduced by J.W. Bruce and R. M. Roberts in their seminal work
[
6
]
. This number, denoted by
\mu_{BR}(f,X)
, is defined as follows:

\mu_{BR}(f,X)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{{\mathcal{O}_{n}}}{{df(\Theta_{X})}},

where
\mathcal{O}_{n}
represents the local ring of holomorphic functions from
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
to
(\mathbb{C},0)
,
df
stands for the differential of
f
, and
\Theta_{X}
is the
\mathcal{O}_{n}
-submodule of
\Theta_{n}
consisting of holomorphic vector fields on
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
that are tangent to
(X,0)
over their regular points. If
I_{X}\subset\mathcal{O}_{n}
is the ideal of germs of holomorphic functions vanishing on
(X,0)
, then

\Theta_{X}=\{\xi\in\Theta_{n}:\;dh(\xi)\in I_{X},\;\forall\,\,h\in I_{X}\}.

In particular, when
X=\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
df(\Theta_{n})
corresponds to the Jacobian ideal
Jf
of
f
which is generated by the partial derivatives of
f
in
\mathcal{O}_{n}
.
Consequently,
\mu_{BR}(f,\mathbb{C}^{n})
coincides with the classical
Milnor number

\mu_{0}(f)
of
f
. We recall that
\mu_{0}(f)
is defined algebraically as the colength of the Jacobian ideal
Jf
in
\mathcal{O}_{n}
.
Furthermore, if
X
is the germ of a complex analytic subvariety at
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, then
\mu_{BR}(f,X)
is finite if and only if
f
has an isolated singularity over
(X,0)
.
The Bruce-Roberts number for holomorphic functions has been studied by many authors, see for instance
[
1
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
34
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
29
]
, and
[
4
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to extend the definition of the
Bruce-Roberts number
to
holomorphic 1-forms relative to complex analytic varieties
.
Our objective is to establish connections between the Bruce-Roberts number and
other well-known indices of 1-forms, such as the Ebeling–Gusein-Zade index, the radial index, the local Euler obstruction, and the Milnor number (Poincaré-Hopf type index). As a result, we offer a reinterpretation of these indices from the perspective of
Foliation theory
. Furthermore, we present applications to both
Foliations
and
Singularity theory
, with a particular focus on complex dimension two. More precisely, let
\omega
be the germ of a holomorphic 1-form with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, and let
X
be a germ of complex analytic variety with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We define the
Bruce-Roberts number
of the 1-form
\omega
with respect to
X
as

\mu_{BR}(\omega,X):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X})}.

Note that
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)
is finite if and only if
\omega
is a 1-form on
X
admitting (at most) an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In the case of a germ of a complex analytic subvariety
X
, this condition is equivalent to statement that

X
is not
invariant
by
\omega
. We recall that
X
is said to be
invariant
by
\omega
, if
T_{p}X\subset Ker(\omega_{p})
for all regular point
p
on
X
. Note also that,
if
\omega=df
, for some
f\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
, then
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)=\mu_{BR}(f,X)
.

For a germ of holomorphic 1-form
\omega=\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{i=1}A_{i}(x)dx_{i}
with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, we define the
Milnor number
of
\omega
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
as

\mu_{0}(\omega):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\rangle}.

It is worth noting that in the literature, the Milnor number of
\omega
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is sometimes denoted as
\operatorname{ind}(\omega;\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, and referred to as the
Poincaré-Hopf index
of
\omega
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
; see, for instance,
[
18
, Definition 5.2.3]
.

Analogous to the
GSV
-index (Gómez-Mont–Seade–Verjovsky
[
23
]
) for vector fields, Ebeling–Gusein-Zade introduced the notion of the
GSV
-index for a 1-form
\omega
with respect to
X
in
[
25
]
,
[
14
]
. This index is denoted by
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,0)
and is related to the
radial index

\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{rad}}(\omega;X,0)
defined in
[
15
]
, and the
local Euler obstruction

\operatorname{Eu}(\omega;X,0)
defined in
[
16
]
.
For further details, precise definitions, and additional results concerning these indices, see, for instance, Sections
2
and
3
.

With this terminology, we now can formulate our first result:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\omega
be the germ of a holomorphic 1-form with isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Let
X
be the germ of a complex analytic hypersurface with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Assume that
X
is not invariant by
\omega
. Then

\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,0)+\mu_{0}(\omega)-\tau_{0}(X),

where
\tau_{0}(X)
is the Tjurina number of
(X,0)
.

We recall that if
X
is defined by
\phi:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
, the Tjurina number of
X
is given by

\tau_{0}(X)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle\phi,\frac{\partial{\phi}}{\partial{x}_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial{\phi}}{\partial{x}_{n}}\rangle}.

It is important to note that in some cases, determining
\Theta_{X}
explicitly can be difficult. However, Theorem
1
provides a straightforward method for computing
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)
. Furthermore, since both
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,0)
and
\mu_{0}(\omega)
are topological invariants when
(X,0)
is an isolated hypersurface singularity, Theorem
1
implies that
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)
is also a topological invariant under homeomorphisms of
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
that fix
(X,0)
.

As a corollary, we recover one of the main results from Nuño-Ballesteros–Oréfice-Okamoto–Pereira–Tomazella
[
34
, Corollary 4.1]
:

Corollary 1
.

Let
X
be an isolated hypersurface singularity defined by

\phi:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
and let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
has an isolated singularity over
(X,0)
. Then

\mu_{BR}(f,X)=\mu_{0}(f)+\mu_{0}(X)+\mu_{0}(\phi,f)-\tau_{0}(X),

where
\mu_{0}(X)
is the Milnor number of
X
and
\mu_{0}(\phi,f)
is the Milnor number of the isolated complete intersection singularity defined by
(\phi,f)
, in the sense of Hamm
[
26
]
.

By combining Theorem
1
, Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 2
.

Let
\omega
be a germ of a holomorphic 1-form with isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Let
X
be a germ of a complex analytic hypersurface with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Assume that
X
is not invariant by
\omega
. Then

\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)-\mu_{0}(\omega)+\tau_{0}(X)-\mu_{0}(X)-\operatorname{Eu}(\omega;X,0)=(-1)^{n-2}\bar{\chi}(M_{\ell}),

where
M_{\ell}
is the Milnor fiber of the generic linear function
\ell:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
on
X
and
\bar{\chi}(M_{\ell})=\chi(M_{\ell})-1
.

Following the definitions and topics explored in
[
30
]
, we define the
relative Bruce-Roberts
of the 1-form
\omega
with respect to
X
as

\mu_{BR}^{-}(\omega,X):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{X}},

where
I_{X}\subset\mathcal{O}_{n}
is the ideal of germs of holomorphic functions vanishing on
(X,0)
.

A natural question arises regarding the relationship between
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)
and
\mu_{BR}^{-}(\omega,X)
.
The following theorem addresses this question:

Theorem 2
.

Let
\omega
be a germ of a holomorphic 1-form with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Let
X
be a germ of a complex analytic hypersurface with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Assume that
X
is not invariant by
\omega
. Then

\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)=\mu_{0}(\omega)+\mu_{BR}^{-}(\omega,X).

Note that if we set
\omega=df
in the preceding theorem, we recover the formula

\mu_{BR}(f,X)=\mu_{0}(f)+\mu^{-}_{BR}(f,X)

as found in
[
30
, Section 3]
.

Section
6
is dedicated to applying our results to germs of holomorphic foliations on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
.
The first observation is that, for a germ of holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
defined by
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, and for
(X,0)
a germ of a complex analytic curve not invariant by
\mathcal{F}
, we have

(1)

\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,0)={\rm tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0),

where
{\rm tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0)
denotes the
tangency order
of
\mathcal{F}
along
X
defined by Brunella
[
7
, Chapter 2, Section 2]
. Then

\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X)=\mu_{0}(\mathcal{F})+\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0)-\tau_{0}(X),

by Theorem
1
, see for instance Corollary
6.1
. Continuing in Proposition
6.2
, we obtain

\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0)=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}\omega+\mu_{0}(X),

where
\varphi
is a Puiseux parametrization of
X
. Combining this with the equation (
1
) and Proposition
3.1
, we get

\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{rad}}(\omega;X,0)=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}\omega.

This provides a straightforward interpretation of the radial index in dimension two. We also present blow-ups formulas for
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)
and
\mu_{BR}^{-}(\omega,X)
and provide several examples. Additionally, we establish a characterization of when a non-dicritical foliation
\mathcal{F}
is a generalized curve foliation in terms of
\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X)
and
\mu_{BR}^{-}(\mathcal{F},X)
; see Corollary
6.8
. Finally, in Section
7
, we derive a global formula for the sum of the Bruce-Roberts numbers of a global foliation on a compact complex surface
S
, see Theorem
7.1
. As a consequence, we obtain an upper bound to the global Tjurina number of
X
in terms of the characteristic numbers of
S
,
\mathcal{F}
and
X
.

It is worth noting that while we only use indices formulas for 1-forms along a complex analytic hypersurface with an isolated singularity, such formulas also apply to 1-forms along isolated complete intersection singularities (ICIS). In a future paper, we will investigate the Bruce-Roberts number of 1-forms along ICIS. For the case of exact 1-forms, see the recent paper
[
29
]
.

2.
Ebeling–Gusein-Zade index of a holomorphic 1-form

In this section, we present definitions and results related to the index of a holomorphic 1-forms with respect to an isolated complete intersection singularity. As noted in
[
17
, p. 133]
, the concept of considering indices of holomorphic 1-forms was first proposed by V.I. Arnold
[
2
]
.
In this paper, we use the algebraic definition of this index specifically for the case where
X
is a complex hypersurface with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For a more comprehensive account of this theory, the reader can refer to
[
14
]
.

Let
\omega
be a germ of a holomorphic
1
-form, given by
\omega=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}A_{i}(x)dx_{i}
, where
A_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{n+k}
in
(\mathbb{C}^{n+k},0)
. Let
(V,0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+k},0)
be an isolated complete intersection singularity (ICIS), defined by an analytic map
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}):(\mathbb{C}^{n+k},0)\rightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{k},0)
. We set
N=n+k
.

Let
U
be a neighborhood of the origin in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
where all the functions
f_{i}

(i=1,\ldots,k)
and the 1-form
\omega
are defined.
Let
S_{\delta}\subset U
be a sufficiently small sphere around the origin which intersects
V
transversally. Let
K=V\cap S_{\delta}
be the link of
(V,0)
.
The set
\{\omega(z),df_{1}(z),...,df_{k}(z)\}
is a
(k+1)
-frame in the space
dual to
\mathbb{C}^{n+k}
for all
z\in K
. Therefore, one has a map

\Psi=(\omega,df_{1},\ldots,df_{k}):K\to W_{N,k+1},

here
W_{N,k+1}
is the Stiefel manifold of
(k+1)
-frames in the space dual to

\mathbb{C}^{N}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The index
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;V,0)
of the 1-form
\omega
on the ICIS
V

at the origin is the degree of the map

\Psi=(\omega,df_{1},\ldots,df_{k}):K\to W_{N,k+1}.

Note that
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;V,0)
is finite if and only if the 1-forms
\omega,df_{1},\ldots,df_{k}
are linearly independent for all points over
K
.

According to
[
25
]
or
[
18
, Theorem 5.3.35]
, the index
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;V,0)
can also be defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;V,0):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{N},0}}{I},

where
I
is the ideal generated by
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
and the
(k+1)\times(k+1)
-minors of the matrix

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{N}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial x_{N}}\\
A_{1}&\cdots&A_{N}\end{array}\right).

For example, when
k=1,\ n=1
and
N=2
, if
\omega=Adx+Bdy
and
V=X=\{\phi=0\}
, we have that
I
is generated by
\phi
and the
2\times 2
minors of

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}&\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\\
A&B\end{array}\right),

i.e.,
I=\langle\phi,\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}B-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}A\rangle
. Then, we have

(2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,0)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{2},0}}{\langle\phi,\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}B-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}A\rangle}.

Let
X
be an isolated hypersurface singularity defined by

\phi:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
, and let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
be a holomorphic function with an isolated singularity on
(X,0)
. If
\displaystyle\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}dz_{j}
, then we can write

(3)

\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,0)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle\phi,\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{j}}A_{k}-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}A_{j}\rangle_{(j,k)\in\Lambda}}

where
\Lambda=\{(j,k);\;j,k=1,\ldots,n,\;j\neq k\}
.
Moreover, according to Lê-Greuel formula (
[
5
]
or
[
28
]
), we have

(4)

\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(df;X,0)=\mu_{0}(X)+\mu_{0}(\phi,f).

3.
Radial index and the Euler Obstruction

In this section, we give some definitions and known results about the radial index and the local Euler obstruction of a 1-form. For a more detailed account of a general theory of indices of vector fields and 1-forms, we refer the reader to
[
18
]
. We will use these results in the case of complex hypersurfaces with an isolated singularity.

Let
V
be a closed (real) subanalytic variety in a smooth manifold
M
, where
M
is
equipped with a (smooth) Riemannian metric. Let

V=\cup_{i=1}^{q}V_{i}
be a subanalytic
Whitney stratification of
V
(see
[
37
]
for this notion). Let
\omega
be the germ at
p\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
of a (continuous) 1-form on
(V,p)
, i.e. the
restriction to
V
of a 1-form defined in a neighborhood of the point
p
in the ambient
manifold
M
. Let
V=\cup_{i=1}^{q}V_{i}
be a subanalytic Whitney stratification of
V
.
For each
p\in V
, set
V_{(p)}=V_{i}
be the stratum containing
p
.
A point
p\in V
is a singular point of
\omega
if the restriction of

\omega
to the stratum
V_{(p)}
containing

p
vanishes at the point
p
.

Definition 3.1
.

The germ
\omega
of a 1-form at the point
p
is called
radial
if, for all
\epsilon>0

small enough, the 1-form is positive on the outward normals to the boundary of the

\epsilon
-neighborhood of the point
p
.

Let
p\in V_{i}=V_{(p)}
,
\dim V_{(p)}=k
, and let
\eta
be a 1-form defined in a
neighborhood of the point
p
. As above, let
N_{i}
be a normal slice (with respect to the Riemannian metric) of
M
to the stratum
V_{i}
at the point
p
and
h
a diffeomorphism
from a neighborhood of
p
in
M
to the product
U_{i}(p)\times N_{i}
, where
U_{i}(p)
is an

\epsilon
-neighborhood of
p
in
V_{i}
, which is the identity on
U_{i}(p)
.

A 1-form
\eta
is called a
radial extension
of the 1-form
\eta|_{V_{(p)}}

if there
exists such a diffeomorphism
h
which identifies
\eta
with the restriction to
V
of the 1-
form
\pi_{1}^{*}\eta|_{V_{(p)}}+\pi_{2}^{*}\eta^{\operatorname{rad}}_{N_{i}}
, where
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are the projections from a neighborhood of
p
in
M
to
V_{(p)}
and
N_{i}
respectively, and
\eta^{\operatorname{rad}}_{N_{i}}

is a radial 1-form on
N_{i}
.

For a 1-form
\omega
on
(V,p)
with an isolated singular point at the point
p
there
exists a 1-form
\tilde{\omega}
on
V
such that

(1)

\tilde{\omega}
coincides with
\omega
on a neighborhood of the intersection of
V

with the boundary
\partial B_{\epsilon}
of the
\epsilon
-neighborhood around the point
p
;

(2)

the 1-form
\tilde{\omega}
has a finite number of singular points (zeros);

(3)

in a neighborhood of each singular point
q\in V\cap B_{\epsilon}
,
q\in V_{i}
,
the 1-form
\tilde{\omega}
is a radial extension of its restriction to the stratum
V_{i}
.

Definition 3.2
.

The
radial index

\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{rad}}(\omega;V,p)
of the 1-form
\omega
at the point
p
is

\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{rad}}(\omega;V,p)=\sum_{q\in\textsf{Sing}(\tilde{\omega})}\operatorname{ind}(\tilde{\omega}|_{V_{(q)}};V_{(q)},q)

where
\operatorname{ind}(\tilde{\omega}|_{V_{(q)}};V_{(q)},q)
is the usual index of the restriction of the 1-form
\tilde{\omega}
to the stratum
V_{(q)}
, see
[
18
, Definition 5.2.3]
.

The radial index
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{rad}}(\omega;V,p)
is well-defined, see
[
18
, Proposition 5.3.9]
. It follows from the definition that
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{rad}}(\omega;V,p)
satisfies the
law of conservation of number
, and moreover one has a Poincaré-Hopf type theorem
[
18
, Theorem 5.3.10]
: for a compact real subanalytic variety
V
and a 1-form
\omega
with isolated singular points on
V
, we have

\sum_{q\in\textsf{Sing}(\omega)}\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{rad}}(\omega;V,q)=\chi(V),

where
\chi(V)
denotes the
Euler characteristic
of the space (variety)
V
.

For 1-form
\omega
one has the following proposition proved in
[
15
, Proposition 2.8]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For a 1-form
\omega
on an ICIS
(V,0)
we have

\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;V,0)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{rad}}(\omega;V,0)+\mu_{0}(V),

where
\mu_{0}(V)
denotes the Milnor number of
(V,0)
, in the sense of Hamm
[
26
]
.

Now, we define the
local Euler obstruction
of a singular point of a 1-form following
[
18
, Section 5.3.5]
. The initial idea was given by MacPherson
[
31
]
who defined the
Euler obstruction
of a singular point of a complex analytic variety. For a recent reference where the reader can found an explicit definition, we refer
[
8
]
.

We start with the definition of the Nash transformation of a germ of a singular variety. Let
(V,0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{N},0)
be the germ of a purely
n
-dimensional complex analytic variety. We assume that
V
is a representative of
(V,0)
defined in a suitable neighborhood
U
of
0\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Let
G(n,N)
be the Grassmann manifold of
n
-dimensional vector subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Let
V_{reg}
be the non-singular part of
V
. There is a natural map
\sigma:V_{reg}\to U\times G(n,N)
which is defined by
\sigma(z)=(z,T_{z}V_{reg})
. The
Nash transform

\widehat{V}
is the closure of the image
\operatorname{Im}\sigma
of the map
\sigma
in
U\times G(n,N)
. It is a usually singular analytic variety. There is the natural base point map
\nu:\widehat{V}\to V
. Let
\widehat{V}^{\prime}:=\widehat{V}\setminus\nu^{-1}(V\setminus V_{reg})
. Then the restriction
\nu|_{\widehat{V}^{\prime}}
maps
\widehat{V}^{\prime}
biholomorphically to
V_{reg}
.

The
Nash bundle

\widehat{T}
over
\widehat{V}
is the pullback of the tautological bundle on the
Grassmann manifold
G(n,N)
under the natural projection map

\widehat{V}\to G(n,N)
. It
is a vector bundle of rank
n
. There is a natural lifting of the Nash transformation to
a bundle map from the Nash bundle

to the restriction of the tangent bundle

of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
to

\widehat{T}
and

over the regular part

Let

at the origin. Let

Definition 3.3

The

\operatorname{Eu}(\omega;V,0)
of the 1-form

is the obstruction to extend the non-zero section

from the pre-image of a neighborhood of the sphere

to the pre-image of its interior. More precisely, its value (as an element of the cohomology group

[(\nu^{-1}(V\cap B_{\epsilon}),\nu^{-1}(V\cap S_{\epsilon}))]

The Euler obstruction of a 1-form can be considered as an index. In particular, it satisfies the law of conservation of number (just as the radial index). Moreover, on a smooth variety the Euler obstruction and the radial index coincide.
We set
\bar{\chi}(Z):=\chi(Z)-1
and call it the

(modulo a point) Euler characteristic of the topological space

(though, strictly speaking, this name is only correct for a non-empty space

We have the following result from

Proposition 3.2

Let

\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{rad}}(\omega;V,0)-\operatorname{Eu}(\omega;V,0)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{rad}}(d\ell;V,0)=(-1)^{n-1}\bar{\chi}(M_{\ell}),

on

Proof of Theorem

First, we state a technical lemma
inspired by

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},g,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
, where
f_{i}
and
g
are relatively prime, for any

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},gp_{1},\ldots,gp_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},g\rangle}.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\rangle}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}}{\langle r_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,r_{n}^{\prime}\rangle},

with
\mathcal{O}=\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\rangle}
and
r_{i}^{\prime}=r_{i}+\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\rangle
, for any

and any
r_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
. The proof is completed using the following exact sequence:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\frac{\mathcal{O}}{\langle p_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,p_{n}^{\prime}\rangle}\xrightarrow{\ \sigma\ }\ \frac{\mathcal{O}}{\langle g^{\prime}p_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,g^{\prime}p_{n}^{\prime}\rangle}\ \xrightarrow{\ \delta\ }\frac{\mathcal{O}}{\langle g^{\prime}\rangle}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\sigma(z^{\prime}+\langle p_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,p_{n}^{\prime}\rangle)=g^{\prime}z^{\prime}+\langle g^{\prime}p_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,g^{\prime}p_{n}^{\prime}\rangle
and
\delta(z^{\prime}+\langle g^{\prime}p_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,g^{\prime}p_{n}^{\prime}\rangle)=z^{\prime}+\langle g^{\prime}\rangle
for any

.

Remark 4.1
.

We consider
\Theta_{X}^{T}
the
\mathcal{O}_{n}
-submodule of
\Theta_{X}
generated by the trivial vector fields, that is,
\Theta_{X}^{T}
is generated by

\phi\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\;\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{j}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}-\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},\;\;\mbox{with }i,j,k=1,\ldots,n;\;k\neq j,

where
\phi:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
is the defining equation of
(X,0)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The following sequence of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces is exact

0\longrightarrow\dfrac{\Theta_{X}}{\Theta_{X}^{T}}{\overset{\cdot\omega}{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})}{\overset{\beta}{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X})}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\beta
is induced by the inclusion
\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\subset\omega(\Theta_{X})
, and
\cdot\omega
is the evaluation map.

Proof.

It is clear that the sequence is well-defined. Let us now prove that it is an exact sequence.
Suppose
f\in\operatorname{Im}(\omega)
. Then, there exists
\eta\in\Theta_{X}
such that
f=\omega(\eta+\Theta_{X}^{T})=\omega(\eta)+\omega(\Theta^{T}_{X})
. Hence,

\beta(f)=\beta(\omega(\eta)+\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T}))=\beta(\omega(\eta))+\omega(\Theta_{X}).

Since
\eta\in\Theta_{X}
, we have
\omega(\eta)\in\omega(\Theta_{X})
, and thus,
\beta(\omega(\eta))=\omega(\eta)\in\omega(\Theta_{X})
. Consequently, it follows that
f\in\operatorname{Ker}(\beta)
. On the other hand, consider
g\in\operatorname{Ker}(\beta)
. Then,
g=f+\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})
, where
f\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
and
\beta(g)\in\omega(\Theta_{X})
. This implies

f+\omega(\Theta_{X})=\beta(f+\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T}))=\beta(g)\in\omega(\Theta_{X}),

therefore,
f\in\omega(\Theta_{X})
, i.e.,
f=\omega(\xi)
, where
\xi\in\Theta_{X}
. Thus, we have
f+\omega(\Theta_{X})=\omega(\xi+\Theta_{X}^{T})
, and therefore
g\in\operatorname{Im}(\omega)
.
Consequently,
\operatorname{Ker}(\beta)=\operatorname{Im}(\omega)
. This establishes the exactness of the sequence and verifies the validity of the lemma.

5.
Relative Bruce-Roberts number for 1-forms

Let
\omega
be a germ of a holomorphic 1-form with isolated singularity at
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
and let
X
be a complex analytic germ with isolated singularity at
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
. We define the
relative Bruce-Roberts
of the 1-form
\omega
with respect to
X
as

\mu_{BR}^{-}(\omega,X)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{X}}.

To establish the relationship between
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)
and
\mu_{BR}^{-}(\omega,X)
, we state the following Lemma which is analogous to
[
30
, Lemma 2.2]
and whose proof follows the same approach. Given a matrix
A
with entries in a ring
R
, we denote by
I_{k}(A)
the ideal in
R
generated the
k\times k
minors of
A
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\omega=\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{i=1}A_{i}dx_{i}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
and let
g\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
be such that
\dim V((\omega,dg))=1
and
V(\omega)=\{0\}
(here
(\omega):=\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\rangle
and
(\omega,dg)
is the
ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{n}
generated by the maximal minors of the matrix of
\left(A_{i},\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}\right)
). Consider the following matrices

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}A_{1}&\cdots&A_{n}\\
\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{n}}\end{array}\right),\quad A^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mu&A_{1}&\cdots&A_{n}\\
\lambda&\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{n}}\end{array}\right),

where
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
. Let
M,M^{\prime}
be the submodules of
\mathcal{O}^{2}_{n}
generated by the columns of
A,A^{\prime}
respectively. If
I_{2}(A)=I_{2}(A^{\prime})
then
M=M^{\prime}
.

We can now prove a theorem similar to
[
30
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\omega=\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{i=1}A_{i}(x)dx_{i}
be a germ of a holomorphic 1-form with isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, where
A_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
. Let
X=\{\phi=0\}
be a germ of a complex analytic hypersurface with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Assume that
X
is not invariant by
\omega
. Then

(i)

\dfrac{\Theta_{X}}{\Theta_{X}^{T}}\cong\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{X}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{X}}
;

(ii)

\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{X}=(\omega)\cdot I_{X}
;

(iii)

\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{(\omega)}\cong\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{X}}{\omega(\Theta_{X})}
,

where
(\omega):=\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\rangle
and
I_{X}=\langle\phi\rangle
.

Proof.

(i)

The homomorphism
\Psi:\Theta_{X}\to\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{X}
defined by
\Psi(\xi)=\omega(\xi)
induces the isomorphism

\overline{\Psi}:\dfrac{\Theta_{X}}{\Theta_{X}^{T}}\to\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{X}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{X}}.

In fact, it is enough to show that
\operatorname{Ker}(\overline{\Psi})=\Theta^{T}_{X}
. Let
\xi+\Theta_{X}^{T}\in\operatorname{Ker}(\overline{\Psi})
then
\omega(\xi)\in\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{X}
, that is, there exist
\eta\in\Theta^{T}_{X}
and
\mu,\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
, such that

\omega(\xi-\eta)=\mu\phi\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;d\phi(\xi-\eta)=\lambda\phi,

then

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mu\phi\\
\lambda\phi\end{array}\right)\in\left\langle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{i}\\
\\
\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}\end{array}\right):\;\;i=1,\ldots,n\right\rangle

and

I_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mu\phi&A_{1}&\cdots&A_{n}\\
&&&\\
\lambda\phi&\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{n}}\end{array}\right)=I_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}A_{1}&\cdots&A_{n}\\
&&\\
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{n}}\end{array}\right)=:(\omega,d\phi).

Therefore

\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}\mu&A_{i}\\
&\\
\lambda&\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}\end{array}\right|\phi\in(\omega,d\phi)

and since
\phi
is regular in
\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{(\omega,d\phi)}
then

\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}\mu&A_{i}\\
&\\
\lambda&\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}\end{array}\right|\in(\omega,d\phi),\;i=1,\ldots,n.

By Lemma
5.1
,
\lambda\in J\phi
and using
[

\xi\in\Theta^{T}_{X}
. Hence,

(ii)

Let

, then there exist
\eta\in\Theta_{X}
and
\mu,\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
such that

\xi=\omega(\eta)=\mu\phi\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;d\phi(\eta)=\lambda\phi.

Using the same argument as the proof of item (i), we obtain

. Hence,
\xi\in(\omega)\cap I_{X}
. Conversely, let

then there exist

Then, by the previous isomorphism and item (ii), we obtain

6.
The Bruce-Roberts number for foliations on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)

In this section, we study the Bruce-Roberts number for germs of holomorphic foliations on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
. A holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
is defined by a holomorphic 1-form

\omega=A(x,y)dx+B(x,y)dy,

or by its dual vector field

(9)

v=-B(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial{x}}+A(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial{y}},

where
A,B\in{\mathbb{C}}\{x,y\}
are relatively prime. The
Milnor number
\mu_{0}(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is defined as the Milnor number of the 1-form
\omega=A(x,y)dx+B(x,y)dy
, i.e.,

\mu_{0}(\mathcal{F})=\mu_{0}(\omega)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle A,B\rangle}.

Let
f(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]
. We say that
C:f(x,y)=0
is
invariant
by
\mathcal{F}
if

\omega\wedge df=(f\cdot h)dx\wedge dy,

for some
h\in\mathbb{C}[[x,y]]
. If
C
is irreducible, then we will say that
C
is a
separatrix
of
\mathcal{F}
. The separatrix
C
is analytical if
f
is convergent. We denote by
Sep_{0}(\mathcal{F})
the set of all separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
. When
Sep_{0}(\mathcal{F})
is a finite set, we will say that the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is
non-dicritical
. Otherwise, we will say that
\mathcal{F}
is
dicritical
.

Let
X
be a curve not invariant by
\mathcal{F}
.
Following
[
7
, Chapter 2, Section 2]
, we can consider the
tangency order of
\mathcal{F}
to
X
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\phi,v(\phi)\rangle},

where
\{\phi=0\}
is the local (reduced) equation of
X
around
0
, and
v
is a local holomorphic vector field generating
\mathcal{F}
around
0
.

In dimension two, Theorem
1
can be established as the following corollary:

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of a singular foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, and let
X
be a germ of a reduced curve at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
. Assume that
X
is not invariant by
\mathcal{F}
.
Then

(10)

\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X)=\mu_{0}(\mathcal{F})+\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0)-\tau_{0}(X).

Proof.

Consider the foliation
\mathcal{F}
defined by the 1-form
\omega=A\,dx+B\,dy
, where
A,B\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
. Let
X
be the complex analytic curve defined by the function
\phi:(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
.
From equation (
2
) it follows that

\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,0)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\left\langle\phi,B\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}-A\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right\rangle}.

The vector field
v
generating
\mathcal{F}
is given by
v=-B\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+A\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
. Therefore, by definition,

\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle\phi,v(\phi)\rangle}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\left\langle\phi,-B\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}+A\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right\rangle}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,0).

Hence,

\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X)=\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0)+\mu_{0}(\mathcal{F})-\tau_{0}(X),

by Theorem
1
.
∎

To proceed, we present some illustrative examples:

Example 6.1
.

Consider the curve
X
given by
X=\{\phi=y^{p}-x^{q}=0\}
, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation defined by the
1
-form
\omega=\lambda xdy+ydx
, with
\lambda\neq-\displaystyle\frac{p}{q}
. Then, by

, we have:

\displaystyle\Theta_{X}=\left\langle qy\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+px\frac{\partial}{\partial x},py^{p-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+qx^{q-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right\rangle.

Note that
X
is not invariant by

, since

\displaystyle\omega\wedge d\phi=(\lambda xdy+ydx)\wedge(py^{p-1}dy-qx^{q-1}dx)=(py^{p}+\lambda qx^{q})\ dx\wedge dy

and

,
\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0)=pq
, and

\displaystyle\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\omega(\Theta_{X})}=p+q,

which indeed satisfies equation (
10

Example 6.2

Consider the foliation

and the foliation

\displaystyle\eta=(2y^{2}+x^{3})dx-2xydy.

The foliations

[

X
from Example

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of a singular foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, and let
X
be a germ of a reduced curve at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
defined by
\phi:(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\rightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
. Suppose that
X
is irreducible and not invariant by
\mathcal{F}
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0)=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}\omega+\mu_{0}(X),

where
\varphi
is a Puiseux parametrization of
X
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is defined by the vector field
v=-B(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial{x}}+A(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial{y}}
.
Then, it follows from the definition the tangency order that

\displaystyle\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0)=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}(A\phi_{y}-B\phi_{x}),

where
\varphi(t)=(x(t),y(t))
is a Puiseux parametrization of
X
.
Without lost of generality, we can suppose that
y(t)\neq 0
. Since

\phi(x(t),y(t))=0
, we obtain

x^{\prime}(t)\ \phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))+y^{\prime}(t)\ \phi_{y}(x(t),y(t))=0\Rightarrow\phi_{y}(x(t),y(t))=-\frac{x^{\prime}(t)\phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))}{y^{\prime}(t)}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(A\phi_{y}-B\phi_{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A\phi_{y}-B\phi_{x})(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(x(t),y(t))\phi_{y}(x(t),y(t))-B(x(t),y(t))\phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(x(t),y(t))\left(-\frac{x^{\prime}(t)\phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))}{y^{\prime}(t)}\right)-B(x(t),y(t))\phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))}{y^{\prime}(t)}\Big(x^{\prime}(t)A(x(t),y(t))+y^{\prime}(t)B(x(t),y(t))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))}{y^{\prime}(t)}[\varphi^{*}\omega](t)\ =\frac{-\varphi^{*}(\phi_{x})(t)\cdot\varphi^{*}\omega(t)}{y^{\prime}(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\varphi^{*}(\phi_{x}\cdot\omega)(t)}{y^{\prime}(t)}.

Thus, we get

(11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0)&=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}(A\phi_{y}-B\phi_{x})\\
&=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ (A\phi_{y}-B\phi_{x})(x(t),y(t))\\
&=\text{ord}_{t=0}-\frac{\varphi^{*}(\phi_{x}\cdot\omega)(t)}{y^{\prime}(t)}\\
&=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}(\phi_{x}\cdot\omega)-\text{ord}_{t=0}\ y^{\prime}(t)\\
&=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}(\phi_{x}\cdot\omega)-\text{ord}_{t=0}\ y(t)+1\\
&=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}\phi_{x}+\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}\omega-\text{ord}_{t=0}\ y(t)+1.\end{split}

Now, from
[
20
, Proposition 2.1, item 3]
, we obtain

(12)

\mu_{0}(X)=i_{0}(\phi,\mathcal{P}_{l}(\phi))-i_{0}(\phi,l)+1,

where
\mathcal{P}_{l}(\phi)=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\frac{\partial l}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\frac{\partial l}{\partial x}
is the polar of
X
with respect to
l\in\mathbb{C}[x,y],
assuming that
l
does not divide
f
. Let
l=ay-bx
. Computing the items on the right side of (
12
), we have

\displaystyle i_{0}(\phi,\mathcal{P}_{l}(\phi))

\displaystyle=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}(a\phi_{x}+b\phi_{y})

\displaystyle=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ (a\phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))+b\phi_{y}(x(t),y(t)))

\displaystyle=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \left(a\phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))-\frac{b\phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))x^{\prime}(t)}{y^{\prime}(t)}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \left(\frac{a\phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))y^{\prime}(t)-b\phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))x^{\prime}(t)}{y^{\prime}(t)}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \left(\frac{\phi_{x}(x(t),y(t))}{y^{\prime}(t)}(ay^{\prime}(t)-bx^{\prime}(t))\right)

\displaystyle=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}\phi_{x}+\text{ord}_{t=0}\ (ay^{\prime}(t)-bx^{\prime}(t))-\text{ord}_{t=0}\ y^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}\phi_{x}+\min\{\text{ord}_{t=0}\ y(t),\text{ord}_{t=0}\ x(t)\}-\text{ord}_{t=0}\ y(t),

and

\displaystyle i_{0}(\phi,l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}(ay-bx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{ord}_{t=0}\ (ay(t)-bx(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{\text{ord}_{t=0}\ y(t),\text{ord}_{t=0}\ x(t)\}.

Finally, by rewriting (
11
) using that and (
12
), we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,0)

\displaystyle=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}\omega+\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}\phi_{x}-\text{ord}_{t=0}\ y(t)+1

\displaystyle=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}\omega+i_{0}(\phi,\mathcal{P}_{l}(\phi))-i_{0}(\phi,l)+1

\displaystyle=\text{ord}_{t=0}\ \varphi^{*}\omega+\mu_{0}(X).

∎

6.1.
The Bruce-Roberts number of a foliation and blow-ups

When we deal with a simple blow-up
\pi
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, we say that
\pi
is
dicritical
with respect to a holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, if the exceptional divisor
\pi^{-1}(0)
is not
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
-invariant. Otherwise,
\pi
is called
non-dicritical
.

Let
X
be a germ of a reduced curve at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
. We set

\Omega_{X}:=\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{C}^{2},0}/I_{X}\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{C}^{2},0}+\mathcal{O}_{2}dI_{X}

the holomorphic 1-forms on
(X,0)
.

On the following proposition, we show how the Bruce-Roberts number in dimension
2
behaves under a blow-up. We denote by
\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,p)
the Bruce-Roberts number of
\mathcal{F}
along
X
around a neighborhood of the point
p
(in Corollary
6.1
, for example, it is defined around
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
).

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of a singular holomorphic foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, let
X
be a germ of an irreducible reduced curve and let
\pi:\tilde{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\rightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
be the blow-up at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
. Assume that
X
is not invariant by
\mathcal{F}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}:=\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}
, and
q\in\pi^{-1}(0)\cap\tilde{X}
, where
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
and
\tilde{X}
are the strict transforms of
\mathcal{F}
and
X
respectively. Denote by
m
the multiplicity of
X
in its Puiseux parametrization, and denote by
\nu
the algebraic multiplicity of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then, we have the following statements:

(a)

If
\pi
is non-dicritical, then

\displaystyle\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,0)

\displaystyle=\mu_{BR}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{X},q)+\nu^{2}-\nu-1+\nu m

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\pi^{-1}(0)\\
p\neq q\end{subarray}}\mu_{p}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}})+\frac{m(m-1)}{2}-\mathcal{D};

(b)

If
\pi
is dicritical, then

\displaystyle\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,0)

\displaystyle=\mu_{BR}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{X},q)+\nu^{2}+\nu-1+(\nu+1)m

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\pi^{-1}(0)\\
p\neq q\end{subarray}}\mu_{p}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}})+\frac{m(m-1)}{2}-\mathcal{D}.

We write
\mathcal{D}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{*}\Omega_{\tilde{X}}}{\sigma^{*}\Omega_{X}}
, where
\tilde{\sigma}:(\overline{X},0)\rightarrow(\tilde{X},0)
is the normalization, and
\sigma=\pi\circ\tilde{\sigma}
.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be the
1
-form defining
\mathcal{F}
and let
\tilde{\omega}
be the
1
-form defining
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
. Applying Proposition
6.2
to the Corollary
6.1
, we have

(13)

\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,0)=\mu_{0}(\mathcal{F})+\operatorname{ord}_{t=0}\varphi^{*}\omega+\mu_{0}(X)-\tau_{0}(X)

and similarly,

\displaystyle\mu_{BR}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{X},q)=\mu_{q}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}})+\operatorname{ord}_{t=q}\tilde{\varphi}^{*}\tilde{\omega}+\mu_{q}(\tilde{X})-\tau_{q}(\tilde{X}),

where
\varphi
and
\tilde{\varphi}
are the Puiseux parametrization of
X
and
\tilde{X}
, respectively.

The proof is obtained using results that show how the indexes on the right side of equation (
13
) change through a blow-up. From
[
10
, Proposition 4.13]
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(\mathcal{F})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\nu^{2}-(\nu+1)+\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\pi^{-1}(0)}\mu_{p}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}})&\text{if}\ \pi\ \text{is non-dicritical;}\\
(\nu+1)^{2}-(\nu+2)+\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\pi^{-1}(0)}\mu_{p}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}})&\text{if}\ \pi\ \text{is dicritical.}\end{array}\right.

and, from
[
39
, p. 4]
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(X)-\mu_{q}(\tilde{X})=m(m-1)\ \text{and}\ \tau_{0}(X)-\tau_{q}(\tilde{X})=\displaystyle\frac{m(m-1)}{2}+\mathcal{D}.

Now, from
[
19
, Section 2]
, evaluating the
1
-form that defines
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
in
\tilde{\varphi}
and taking orders, we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{t=0}\varphi^{*}\omega=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\nu m+\operatorname{ord}_{t=q}\tilde{\varphi}^{*}\tilde{\omega}&\text{if}\ \pi\ \text{is non-dicritical;}\\
(\nu+1)m+\operatorname{ord}_{t=q}\tilde{\varphi}^{*}\tilde{\omega}&\text{if}\ \pi\ \text{is dicritical.}\end{array}\right.

Substituting the above formulas into (
13
), we conclude the proof of the Proposition.
∎

q\in\pi^{-1}(0)\cap\tilde{X}
, where
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
and
\tilde{X}
are the strict transforms of
\mathcal{F}
and
X
respectively. Denote by
m
the multiplicity of
X
in its Puiseux parametrization, and denote by
\nu
the algebraic multiplicity of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then, we have the following statements:

(a)

If
\pi
is non-dicritical, then

\displaystyle\mu_{BR}^{-}(\mathcal{F},X,0)

\displaystyle=\mu_{BR}^{-}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{X},q)+\nu m+\frac{m(m-1)}{2}-\mathcal{D};

(b)

If
\pi
is dicritical, then

\displaystyle\mu_{BR}^{-}(\mathcal{F},X,0)

\displaystyle=\mu_{BR}^{-}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{X},q)+(\nu+1)m+\frac{m(m-1)}{2}-\mathcal{D}.

We write
\mu_{BR}^{-}(\mathcal{F},X,p)
to denote the relative Bruce-Roberts number around the point
p
. Moreover, we have
\mathcal{D}=\dim\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{*}\Omega_{\tilde{X}}}{\sigma^{*}\Omega_{X}}
, where
\tilde{\sigma}:(\overline{X},0)\rightarrow(\tilde{X},0)
is the normalization, and
\sigma=\pi\circ\tilde{\sigma}
.

Proof.

The proof follows similarly, utilizing the results from the proof of Proposition
6.3
together with Corollary
6.4
.
∎

6.3.
Generalized curve foliations and the Bruce-Roberts numbers

We say that
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a
reduced
singularity for
\mathcal{F}
if the linear part
\text{D}v(0)
of the vector field
v
in (
9
) is non-zero and has eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
fitting in one of the cases:

(i)

\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\neq 0
and
\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\not\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
(
non-degenerate
);

(ii)

\lambda_{1}\neq 0
and
\lambda_{2}\neq 0
(
saddle-node singularity
).

In the case
(i)
, there is a system of coordinates
(x,y)
in which
\mathcal{F}
is defined by the equation

(15)

\omega=x(\lambda_{1}+a(x,y))dy-y(\lambda_{2}+b(x,y))dx,

where
a(x,y),b(x,y)\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x,y]]
are non-units, so that
Sep_{0}(\mathcal{F})
is formed by two
transversal analytic branches given by
\{x=0\}
and
\{y=0\}
. In the case
(ii)
, up to a formal change of coordinates, the saddle-node singularity is given by a 1-form of the type

(16)

\omega=x^{k+1}dy-y(1+\lambda x^{k})dx,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
are invariants after formal changes of coordinates (see
[
32
, Proposition 4.3]
).
The curve
\{x=0\}
is an analytic separatrix, called
strong
separatrix, whereas
\{y=0\}
corresponds to a possibly formal separatrix, called
weak
separatrix. The integer
k+1>1
is called
tangency index
of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to the weak separatrix.

According to
[
9
, p. 144]
, a
generalized curve foliation
is a foliation
\mathcal{F}
defined by a vector field
v
whose reduction of singularities (
[
38
]
) admits only non-degenerate singularities with non-vanishing eigenvalues. Assuming that
\mathcal{F}
is non-dicritical at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and denoting
C=Sep_{0}(\mathcal{F})
the union of the separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
, Camacho-Lins Neto-Sad proved the following theorem:

Theorem 6.7
.

[
9
, Theorem 4]

Given a germ of a non-dicritical holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
one has
\mu_{0}(\mathcal{F})\geq\mu_{0}(C)
, and the equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is a generalized curve foliation.

As a consequence of these results, we can establish a new characterization of non-dicritical generalized curve foliations:

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of a non-dicritical holomorphic foliation at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Let
X
be a germ of a reduced curve at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and
C=Sep_{0}(\mathcal{F})=\{f=0\}
be a reduced equation of
Sep_{0}(\mathcal{F})
.
Assume that
X
is not invariant by
\mathcal{F}
. Then

\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X)-\mu_{BR}(f,X)\geq\mu^{-}_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X)-\mu^{-}_{BR}(f,X),

and the equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is a generalized curve foliation.

Proof.

The proof follows from Corollary
6.4
and Theorem
6.7
apply to
\mathcal{F}
and
f
respectively, since

\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X)-\mu^{-}_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X)=\mu_{0}(\mathcal{F})\geq\mu_{0}(C)=\mu_{BR}(f,X)-\mu^{-}_{BR}(f,X).

∎

7.
Applications to global foliations

Let
S
be a compact complex surface and let
\{U_{j}\}_{j\in I}
be an open covering of
S
. A
holomorphic foliation

\mathcal{F}
on
S
can be described by a collection of holomorphic 1-forms
\omega_{j}\in\Omega^{1}_{S}(U_{j})
with isolated zeros such that

\omega_{i}=g_{ij}\omega_{j}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{on}\,\,\,U_{i}\cap U_{j},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,g_{ij}\in\mathcal{O}^{*}_{S}(U_{i}\cap U_{j}).

The
singular set

\textsf{Sing}(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
is the finite subset of
S
defined by

\textsf{Sing}(\mathcal{F})\cap U_{j}=\text{zeros of}\,\,\,\omega_{j},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall j\in I.

A point
q\not\in\textsf{Sing}(\mathcal{F})
is said to be
regular
. The cocycle
\{g_{ij}\}
defines a line bundle
N_{\mathcal{F}}
on
S
, called
the normal bundle
of
\mathcal{F}
. The dual
N^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}
is called
conormal bundle
of
\mathcal{F}
. The foliation gives rise to a global holomorphic section of
N_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes T^{*}S
, with finite zero set and modulo multiplication by
\mathcal{O}^{*}_{S}(S)
, and to an exact sequence

0\to N^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\to T^{*}S\to\mathcal{I}_{Z}\cdot T^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\to 0

where
T^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a line bundle on
S
, called
canonical bundle
of
\mathcal{F}
, and
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
is an ideal sheaf supported on
\textsf{Sing}(\mathcal{F})
. The dual
T_{\mathcal{F}}
of
T^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}
, is called
the tangent bundle
of
\mathcal{F}
.
These line bundles are related to each other via the canonical bundle
K_{S}
of
S
:

K_{S}=T^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes N^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}.

Now, we can state the following theorem:

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a holomorphic foliation on a compact complex surface
S
, and let
X\subset S
be a compact curve, none of whose components are invariant by
\mathcal{F}
. Then

\sum_{p\in\textsf{Sing}(\mathcal{F})\cap X}\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,p)=T_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot T_{\mathcal{F}}+T_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot K_{S}+c_{2}(S)+N_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot X-\chi(X)-\tau(X),

where
\chi(X)=-K_{S}\cdot X-X\cdot X
is the virtual Euler characteristic of
X
, and
\displaystyle\tau(X)=\sum_{p\in X}\tau_{p}(X)
is the global Tjurina number of
X
.

Proof.

For each
p\in\textsf{Sing}(\mathcal{F})\cap X
, we have

(17)

\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,p)=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})+\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,p)-\tau_{p}(X).

by Corollary
6.1
. On the other hand, according to
[
7
, Propositions 2.1 and 2.2]
we get

\sum_{p\in\textsf{Sing}(\mathcal{F})}\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})=T_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot T_{\mathcal{F}}+T_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot K_{S}+c_{2}(S)

and

\sum_{p\in\textsf{Sing}(\mathcal{F})\cap X}\operatorname{tang}(\mathcal{F},X,p)=N_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot X-\chi(X).

We complete the proof by summig over
p\in\textsf{Sing}(\mathcal{F})\cap X
and substituting the above formulas into equation (
17
).
∎

We have the following corollary.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a holomorphic foliation on a compact complex surface
S
, and let
X\subset S
be a compact curve, none of whose components are invariant by
\mathcal{F}
. Then

\tau(X)\leq T_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot T_{\mathcal{F}}+T_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot K_{S}+c_{2}(S)+N_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot X-\chi(X).

Proof.

Since
\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,p)\geq 0
for all
p\in\textsf{Sing}(\mathcal{F})\cap X
, we obtain an upper bound for the global Tjurina number of
X
:

\tau(X)\leq T_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot T_{\mathcal{F}}+T_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot K_{S}+c_{2}(S)+N_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot X-\chi(X),

by Theorem
7.1
.
∎

In the particular case,
S=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
,
\mathcal{F}
a foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
of degree
\deg(\mathcal{F})=d
, and
X\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
an algebraic curve of
\deg(X)=r
, we get

\tau(X)\leq d^{2}+d+1+r(d+r-1).

In
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
, a upper bound for
\tau(X)
is derived under the assumption that there exists a holomorphic foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{2}

that leaves
X
invariant. In contrast, in Corollary
7.2
, the bound for the global Tjurina number of
X
is established using a holomorphic foliation that does not leave

X
invariant.

On the other hand, since

\sum_{p\in\textsf{Sing}(\mathcal{F})}\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})=T_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot T_{\mathcal{F}}+T_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot K_{S}+c_{2}(S)

by
[
7
, Proposition 2.1]
, we have the following corollary as a consequence of Corollary
6.4
.

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a holomorphic foliation on a compact complex surface
S
, and let
X\subset S
be a compact curve, none of whose components are invariant by
\mathcal{F}
. Then

\sum_{p\in\textsf{Sing}(\mathcal{F})\cap X}\left[\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,p)-\mu_{BR}^{-}(\mathcal{F},X,p)\right]=T_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot T_{\mathcal{F}}+T_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot K_{S}+c_{2}(S).

1.
Introduction

Solving Diophantine equations has always been a central theme in the study of mathematics, since the ancient Greeks. In the context of modern algebraic geometry, the problem is integrated into the study of Chow groups of varieties defined over number fields. Recall that for a smooth projective variety
X
over a field
F
and an integer
r\geqslant 0
, the Chow group of codimension
r
cycles of
X
, denoted by
\CH^{r}(X)
, is defined as the abelian group generated by codimension
r
points of
X
modulo rational equivalences. For every prime number
\ell
different from the characteristic of
F
, we have the cycle class map

\mathrm{cl}_{\ell}\colon\CH^{r}(X)\to\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r))

to the
\ell
-adic étale cohomology of
X
, through which we wish to turn the study of
\CH^{r}(X)
to that of the cohomology. The pro-finite étale covering
X_{\overline{F}{}}\to X
induces a finite decreasing filtration
\mathrm{F}^{\bullet}\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r))
on
\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r))
via the Hoschchild–Serre spectral sequence, satisfying that

\frac{\mathrm{F}^{i}\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r))}{\mathrm{F}^{i+1}\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r))}=\mathrm{H}^{i}(F,\mathrm{H}^{2r-i}(X_{\overline{F}{}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r))).

By taking the preimage under
\mathrm{cl}_{\ell}
, it induces a decreasing filtration
\mathrm{F}^{\bullet}\CH^{r}(X)
on
\CH^{r}(X)
, which
a priori
depends on
\ell
and is not necessarily exhaustive. When
F
is a number field, Beilinson and Bloch made the following remarkable predictions in the 1980s (see
[
2
]
), commonly known as the
Beilinson–Bloch conjecture
:

(1)

\mathrm{F}^{\bullet}\CH^{r}(X)
does not depend on
\ell
and
\mathrm{F}^{2}\CH^{r}(X)=\CH^{r}(X)_{\mathrm{tor}}
;
1
1

1

It is conjectured that
\mathrm{F}^{2}\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r))=0
when
F
is a number field
[
13
]
.

(2)

the induced map
(\CH^{r}(X)/\mathrm{F}^{1}\CH^{r}(X))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(F,\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X_{\overline{F}{}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r)))
is an isomorphism;

(3)

the induced map
\mathrm{F}^{1}\CH^{r}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\mathrm{H}^{2r-1}(X_{\overline{F}{}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r)))
is injective, whose image coincides with the Bloch–Kato Selmer group
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F,\mathrm{H}^{2r-1}(X_{\overline{F}{}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r)))
(see Remark
1.1
).

Note that (2) is nothing but the Tate conjecture (
[
27
]
*Conjecture 1 over number fields). The three predictions together imply that
\CH^{r}(X)
has finite rank, which reveals a fundamental difference between geometry over number fields and that over general fields. For example, it is a well-known theorem of Mumford that
\CH^{2}(X)
could have uncountable rank when
F=\mathbb{C}
and
X
is in a certain class of surfaces.

From now on, we assume that
F
is a number field. The Beilinson–Bloch conjecture motivates us to study
\mathrm{H}^{0}(F,\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X_{\overline{F}{}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r)))
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F,\mathrm{H}^{2r-1}(X_{\overline{F}{}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r)))
. For the former one, its dimension can be computed through the order of poles of certain
L
-functions. More precisely, it is expected that

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\mathrm{H}^{0}(F,\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X_{\overline{F}{}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r)))=-\ord_{s=1}L(s,\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X_{\overline{F}{}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r))),

which is equivalent to
[
27
]
*Conjecture 2 under Tate’s Conjecture 1. For the latter one, the Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture relates its dimension to the order of zeros of certain
L
-functions. More precisely, it is expected that

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F,\mathrm{H}^{2r-1}(X_{\overline{F}{}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r)))=\ord_{s=0}L(s,\mathrm{H}^{2r-1}(X_{\overline{F}{}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(r))).

The above conjectural formula can be made toward more general motives, rather than the full cohomology of varieties.

In this note, we sketch the proof of our recent result on bounding Bloch–Kato Selmer groups of the so-called Rankin–Selberg motives, focusing on the case of the tensor product of symmetric power of elliptic curves with analytic rank zero.

We start from a brief recall of the Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture for more general motives, which generalizes the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. Let
F
be a number field contained in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}{}
–
the
algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
in
\mathbb{C}
. Consider a motive
\mathrm{V}
over
F
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
, for which we may understand as a compatible system
\{\mathrm{V}_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
of Galois representations of
F
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
“of geometric origin”. Then the Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture
[
5
]
predicts that

\ord_{s=0}L(s,\mathrm{V})=\dim\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F,\mathrm{V}_{\ell})-\dim\mathrm{H}^{0}(F,\mathrm{V}_{\ell})

for every prime number
\ell
. Here,
L(s,\mathrm{V})
denotes the
L
-function of
\mathrm{V}
, or rather
\mathrm{V}_{\ell}
for every
\ell
, which is conjectured to have a meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
and satisfy a functional equation relating
L(-s,\mathrm{V}^{\vee}(1))
; and
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F,\mathrm{V}_{\ell})
denotes the Bloch–Kato Selmer group of
\mathrm{V}_{\ell}
(see the remark right below), as a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-vector space.

Remark 1.1
.

We recall that
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F,\mathrm{V}_{\ell})
is the subspace of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\mathrm{V}_{\ell})
consisting of classes
c
such that for every finite place
v
of
F
, its localization
\mathrm{loc}_{\ell}(c)
belongs to a certain subspace
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F_{v},\mathrm{V}_{\ell})
of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{v},\mathrm{V}_{\ell})
. When
v\nmid\ell
,
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F_{v},\mathrm{V}_{\ell})=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{unr}}(F_{v},\mathrm{V}_{\ell})
consists of the unramified classes. When
v\mid\ell
and
\mathrm{V}_{\ell}
is crystalline at
v
,
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F_{v},\mathrm{V}_{\ell})
parameterizes crystalline extensions of
\mathrm{V}_{\ell}
by
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
at
v
; in general,
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F_{v},\mathrm{V}_{\ell})\coloneqq\Ker\left(\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{v},\mathrm{V}_{\ell})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{v},\mathrm{V}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{cris}})\right)
.

When
\mathrm{V}_{\ell}=\mathrm{V}_{\ell}(A)
is the rational
\ell
-adic Tate module of an abelian variety
A
over
F
,
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F_{v},\mathrm{V}_{\ell})
is canonically isomorphic to
\left(\varprojlim_{m}\Sel_{\ell^{m}}(A)\right)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, where
\Sel_{M}(A)
denotes the
M
-Selmer group of
A
over
F
for every positive integer
M
.

Fix a positive integer
n
and a pair of rational elliptic curves
A=(A_{0},A_{1})
. Let
n_{0}
and
n_{1}
be the unique even and odd members in
\{n,n+1\}
, respectively. Recall that for every prime number
\ell
and
\alpha\in\{0,1\}
, we have the
\ell
-adic Tate module
\mathrm{T}_{\ell}(A_{\alpha})=\varprojlim_{m}A_{\alpha}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}{})[\ell^{m}]
of
A_{\alpha}
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}{}/\mathbb{Q})]
-module. Put

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{A}_{0,\ell}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(\Sym_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}^{n_{0}-1}\mathrm{T}_{\ell}(A_{0})\right)(1-r_{0}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{A}_{1,\ell}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(\Sym_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}^{n_{1}-1}\mathrm{T}_{\ell}(A_{1})\right)(-r_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathrm{T}^{A}_{0,\ell}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\mathrm{T}^{A}_{1,\ell}.

Then
\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}{}/\mathbb{Q})]
-module of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-rank
n(n+1)
, equipped with a canonical polarization
\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\simeq(\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell})^{\vee}(1)
. Put
\mathrm{V}^{A}_{\ell}\coloneqq\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. Then we obtain a canonically polarized motive
\mathrm{V}^{A}\coloneqq\{\mathrm{V}^{A}_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, and its base change
\mathrm{V}^{A}_{F}
to
F
. It is clear that
\mathrm{H}^{0}(F,\mathrm{V}^{A}_{\ell})=0
for every prime number
\ell
. In particular, when
n=1
, the Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture recovers (part of) the BSD conjecture.

The following theorem is a combination of
[

]
*Theorem 1.1.1 and

]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that

(a)

F
is a solvable CM field;

(b)

[F:\mathbb{Q}]>2
;

(c)

A_{0}
and
A_{1}
are not geometrically isogenous to each other;

(d)

neither
A_{0}
nor
A_{1}
has complex multiplication over

If the
L
-value

does not vanish, then
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F,\mathrm{V}^{A}_{\ell})
vanishes for all but finitely many prime numbers
\ell
.

In comparison, we mention that Tate’s both Conjecture 1 and 2 in
[
27
]
hold when
F
is totally real or imaginary CM and
X=A_{0}^{m_{0}}\times A_{1}^{m_{1}}
for arbitrary elliptic curves
A_{0},A_{1}
over
F
and integers
m_{0}\geqslant 0,m_{1}\geqslant 1
. This is essentially an application of recent theorems on the Hodge conjecture and the Mumford–Tate conjecture for product of low dimensional abelian varieties, potential automorphy theorems and Shahidi’s theorem on the non-vanishing of Rankin–Selberg

]

Remark 1.3
.

We briefly recall the main history (which is by no means complete) of results of the kind in Theorem
1.2
(in particular, the case of central critical value).

(1)

When
n=1
and
F=\mathbb{Q}
, the result in Theorem
1.2
was obtained by the groundbreaking work of Kolyvagin
[
14
]
, who invented the technique known as the Euler system; the proof uses the Gross–Zagier formula
[
11
]
to pass to the rank
1

(2)

In
[
3
]
, Bertolini and Darmon created another method to produce cohomology classes to bound Selmer groups via level raising, which was later streamlined by Howard
[
12
]
known as the bipartite Euler system; it can be used to give an alternative proof of Kolyvagin’s result (under a mild restriction) without using the Gross–Zagier formula.

(3)

In
[
19
]
, one of us proved the result in Theorem
1.2
when
n=2
and
F=\mathbb{Q}
(under some mild restrictions).

(4)

In
[
8
]
, Darmon and Rotger proved a similar result in the spirit of Theorem
1.2
for the motive
\{\mathrm{V}_{\ell}(A)\otimes\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\}_{\ell}
, where
A
is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
are two two-dimensional Artin representations of
\mathbb{Q}
satisfying certain conditions.

(5)

In
[
20
]
, one of us proved a similar result in the spirit of Theorem
1.2
for the motive
\{(\otimes\Ind^{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{V}_{\ell}(A))(-1)\}_{\ell}
, where
F
is a totally real cubic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and
A
is an elliptic curve over
F
satisfying certain mild conditions. Here,
\otimes\Ind
denotes the tensor induction.

We remark that the cohomology classes used to bound Selmer groups in (3), (5), and
[
17
]
are all in the spirit of Bertolini–Darmon.

Remark 1.4

2.
Vanishing of Selmer groups

In this section, we present the Galois-theoretical argument for how one can show the vanishing of Selmer groups, following the original idea of Kolyvagin
[
14
]
.

We start by introducing a set of restrictions on the prime
\ell
. Define the following polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[u,v]
:

\displaystyle L_{0}(u,v)

\displaystyle=1,\quad L_{1}(u,v)=v^{2}-2u,

\displaystyle L_{j}(u,v)

\displaystyle=v^{2j}-\sum_{k=1}^{j}\binom{2j}{k}u^{k}L_{j-k}(u,v),

\displaystyle L(u,v)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{r_{1}}\left((L_{2j}(u,v)+u^{2j+1}+u^{2j-1})\prod_{i=0}^{r_{0}}(u^{2r_{0}-2j}L_{2j}(u,v)-u^{2r_{0}+2i}-u^{2r_{0}-2i})\right).

We introduce the first set of restrictions on the prime number
\ell
for the coefficients.

(L1):

The prime
\ell
does not belong to
\Sigma^{A}
.

(L2):

There exists
(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
\ell\nmid L(u,v)
.

(L3):

The image of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}{}/\widetilde{F})
in
\GL_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}(\mathrm{T}_{\ell}(A_{0}))\times\GL_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}(\mathrm{T}_{\ell}(A_{1}))
consists of all pairs
(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})
with
\det\gamma_{0}=\det\gamma_{1}
, where
\widetilde{F}
denotes the normal closure of
F
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}{}
.

It is elementary that (L1) and (L2) exclude only finitely many
\ell
. By Serre’s theorem
[
26
]
*Théorème 6, (L3) also excludes only finitely many
\ell
under Theorem
1.2
(c,d). Condition (L3) says that the image is the largest possible one; while (L2) says that the largest possible image is indeed large enough to have level-raising primes modulo
\ell
(defined below).

Now we take a prime number
\ell
satisfying (L1–3).

Definition 2.1
.

For every positive integer
m
, a prime number
p
is a
level-raising prime modulo
\ell^{m}
(with respect to the data
(F,A,n)
) if it satisfies

(P1)

p
is odd, is inert in
F^{-}
and splits completely in
H^{+}
.

(P2)

p
does not belong to
\Sigma^{A}
.

(P3)

a_{p}(A_{0})^{2}\equiv(p+1)^{2}\mod\ell^{m}
.

(P4)

\ell\nmid p\prod_{i=1}^{n_{0}}(1-(-p)^{i})
.

(P5)

\ell\nmid L(p,a_{p}(A_{1}))
.

Remark 2.2
.

In Definition
2.1
, (P4) is equivalent to that
\ell
does not divide the cardinality of the finite unitary group of rank
n_{0}
with respect to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
; (P5) is to make sure that the tensor product Galois module
\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
does not have “extra level raising congruence”. See Lemma
2.3
below for their implications.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
p
is level-raising prime modulo
\ell^{m}
. Then

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{unr}}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m}),\quad\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})

are both free
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m}
-modules of rank
1
.

Recall that
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},-)
is defined as the quotient
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},-)/\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{unr}}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},-)
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Definition
2.1
. We leave it to readers as an exercise.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

A
system of Selmer annihilators
for

consists for every integer
m>m_{\ell}
(for some nonnegative integer

depending on

) and all but finitely many level-raising prime

, an element

(a)

\mathrm{loc}_{v}(c_{m,p})\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{v},\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})
is crystalline (that is, the reduction of an element in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{v},\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell})
whose image in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{v},\mathrm{V}^{A}_{\ell})
parameterizes a crystalline extension) for every place

of

above
\ell
;

(b)

the image of
\mathrm{loc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(c_{m,p})
in
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})

of

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
F
and
A
satisfy (a–d) in Theorem
1.2
(and the simplifying assumption that
F
contains an imaginary quadratic field). Then
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F,\mathrm{V}^{A}_{\ell})=0
for every prime number
\ell
satisfying (L1–3) such that a system of Selmer annihilators for
\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}
exists.

Once we have this proposition, the remaining task for the proof of Theorem
1.2
is to show that, as long as
L(0,\mathrm{V}^{A}_{F})\neq 0
, a system of Selmer annihilators for
\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}
exists for all but finitely many

.

Proof.

First, we realize that for every place
v
of
F
above
\Sigma^{A}
,
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{v},\mathrm{V}^{A}_{\ell})
vanishes since
\mathrm{V}^{A}_{\ell}
satisfies the “purity of monodromy filtration”, which implies that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{v},\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell})
is a finite
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module. Thus, there exists a positive integer
m^{\prime}
such that
\prod_{v\mid\Sigma^{A}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{v},\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell})
is annihilated by
\ell^{m^{\prime}}
.

We prove the proposition by contradiction. Suppose that
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F,\mathrm{V}^{A}_{\ell})\neq 0
. Take
m\coloneqq m_{\ell}+m^{\prime}+1
. By (L2,L3) and the Chebotarev density theorem, there are infinitely many level-raising primes modulo
\ell^{m}
. Thus, we can take such a prime
p
for which the element
c_{m,p}
(simply written as
c_{m}
in the proof below) in Definition
2.4
exists.

By (L3),
\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}
is residually absolutely irreducible. By
[
19
]
*Lemma 5.8, one can find an element
s\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F,\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell})
whose image
s_{m}
in
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(F,\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})
satisfies

(a’)

\mathrm{loc}_{v}(s_{m})
is crystalline for every place
v
of
F
above
\ell
;

(b’)

\mathrm{loc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(s_{m})
is a generator of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{unr}}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})
;

(c’)

\mathrm{loc}_{v}(s_{m})
is unramified for every nonarchimedean place
v\neq\mathfrak{p}
of
F
not above
\Sigma^{A}\cup\{\ell\}
.

For every prime
v
of
F
, we have the local Tate pairing

\langle\;,\;\rangle_{v}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{v},\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})\times\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{v},\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(F_{v},\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m}(1))=\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m}.

Since both
s_{m}
and
c_{m}
belong to
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})
, we have
\sum_{v}\langle s_{m},c_{m}\rangle_{v}=0
. On the other hand, we have
\ell^{m^{\prime}}\langle s_{m},c_{m}\rangle_{v}=0
for
v
above
\Sigma^{A}
;
\langle s_{m},c_{m}\rangle_{v}=0
for
v
above
\ell
by (a) and (a’); and
\langle s_{m},c_{m}\rangle_{v}=0
for
v\neq\mathfrak{p}
of
F
not above
\Sigma^{A}\cup\{\ell\}
by (c) and (c’). As a consequence,
\ell^{m^{\prime}}\langle s_{m},c_{m}\rangle_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
. Since
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{p}}
induces a perfect pairing between
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{unr}}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathrm{T}^{A}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})

7.
Interlude: potential map and singular quotient

In this section, we explain the motivation of the map
\nabla^{0}
constructed in the previous section. The underlying idea is the so-called
potential map
used to compute
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sing}}
under a certain favorable situation for general varieties over local fields, which was developed by one of us in
[
20
]
. Again, let
n=2r
be a positive even integer.

Let
K
be a henselian discrete valuation field with residue field
\kappa
and a separable closure
\overline{K}
, such that the characteristic of
\kappa
is different from
\ell
. Let
\Lambda
be either a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-ring that is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module or the quotient field of such a ring, which will serve as the ring of coefficients. We consider a proper strictly semistable scheme
X
over
O_{K}
of pure relative dimension
n-1
. In particular, the special fiber
X_{\kappa}\coloneqq X\otimes_{O_{K}}\kappa
is a strict normal crossing divisor of
X
. Suppose that
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\}
is the set of irreducible components of
X_{\kappa}
. For
p\geqslant 0
, put

X^{(p)}_{\kappa}\coloneqq\coprod_{I\subset\{1,\dots,m\},|I|=p+1}\bigcap_{i\in I}X_{i}.

Then
X^{(p)}_{\kappa}
is a finite disjoint union of smooth proper
\kappa
-schemes of codimension
p
in
X_{\kappa}
. We have the pullback map

\delta_{p}^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{q}(X^{(p)}_{\overline{\kappa}},\Lambda(j))\to\mathrm{H}^{q}(X^{(p+1)}_{\overline{\kappa}},\Lambda(j))

and the pushforward (Gysin) map

\delta_{p*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{q}(X^{(p)}_{\overline{\kappa}},\Lambda(j))\to\mathrm{H}^{q+2}(X^{(p-1)}_{\overline{\kappa}},\Lambda(j+1))

for every integer
j
(see
[
20
]
*§2.1 for more details). These maps satisfy the formula

\displaystyle\delta_{p-1}^{*}\circ\delta_{p*}+\delta_{p+1*}\circ\delta_{p}^{*}=0.

We have the weight spectral sequence
\mathrm{E}^{p,q}_{s}
attached to
X
with coefficients
\Lambda(r)
abutting to
\mathrm{H}^{p+q}(X_{\overline{K}},\Lambda(r))
, whose first page is given by

\mathrm{E}^{p,q}_{1}=\bigoplus_{i\geqslant\max(0,-p)}\mathrm{H}^{q-2i}(X_{\overline{\kappa}}^{(p+2i)},\Lambda(r-i)).

We also have the monodromy map

\mu^{p,q}_{s}\colon\mathrm{E}^{p-1,q+1}_{s}\to\mathrm{E}^{p+1,q-1}_{s}(-1)

induced by the natural one on the first page.

Put

\mathrm{B}^{r}(X)\coloneqq\Ker\left(\delta_{0}^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X_{\overline{\kappa}}^{(0)},\Lambda(r))\to\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X_{\overline{\kappa}}^{(1)},\Lambda(r))\right),

and dually

\mathrm{B}_{r}(X)\coloneqq\coker\left(\delta_{1*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{2(r-1)}(X_{\overline{\kappa}}^{(1)},\Lambda(r-1))\to\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X_{\overline{\kappa}}^{(0)},\Lambda(r))\right).

As a submodule of
\mathrm{E}^{0,2r}_{1}
,
\mathrm{B}^{r}(X)
is contained in
\Ker\mathrm{d}^{0,2r}_{1}
. Thus, it makes sense to put

\mathrm{B}^{r}(X)^{0}\coloneqq\Ker\left(\mathrm{B}^{r}(X)\to\mathrm{E}^{0,2r}_{2}\right),

and dually the quotient
\mathrm{B}_{r}(X)_{0}
of
\mathrm{B}_{r}(X)
. It is not hard to see that the identity map of
\mathrm{H}^{2r}(X_{\overline{\kappa}}^{(0)},\Lambda(r))
induces a map

\mathrm{B}_{r}(X)_{0}\to\mathrm{B}^{r}(X)^{0}.

Finally, denote by
\mathrm{A}_{r}(X)_{0}
and
\mathrm{A}^{r}(X)^{0}
the maximal
\Gal(\overline{\kappa}/\kappa)
-invariant submodule and quotient module of
\mathrm{B}_{r}(X)_{0}
and
\mathrm{B}^{r}(X)^{0}
, respectively. We define the
potential map
to be the composite map

\Delta\colon\mathrm{A}_{r}(X)_{0}\to\mathrm{B}_{r}(X)_{0}\to\mathrm{B}^{r}(X)^{0}\to\mathrm{A}^{r}(X)^{0}.

Retrospective Review of Suspected Melanoma Diagnoses Made by a Single-Centre Teledermoscopy Service in New Zealand
Authors: Savani Kannangara, Amanda Oakley
Published: 2026

Introduction

Early diagnosis of melanoma improves outcomes [1]. Diagnostic accuracy of teledermoscopy is comparable to in-person dermatology [2]. We have previously described a publicly funded, nurse-led teledermoscopy service provided by MoleMap New Zealand for patients referred from primary care with a lesion of concern [3]. Patient and lesion metadata accompanies standardised location, macroscopic, and dermatoscopic images captured by trained experienced nurses using dedicated equipment such as Nikon D300 and DermLite MoleCam. Using deidentified data in consented patients, we evaluated patient characteristics of confirmed and unconfirmed melanomas diagnosed by teledermoscopy (Health New Zealand CASU 4487P).

Case Presentation

We included lesions diagnosed by a teledermatologist from January 2010 to September 2023 as definite, probable, or possible melanoma, and excluded those without histopathology.

Of 825 lesions in 659 patients, 410 were histologically-confirmed melanomas or melanomas in situ (‘melanomas’) (Figure 1A) and 415 were not melanomas (Figure 1B). The ratio of MIS to melanoma was 2.06 (2.8:1.3), indicating underdiagnosis is unlikely. We considered that 561 lesions were appropriately excised: melanomas/melanomas in situ (73%), nevi with moderate–severe atypia (24%), and keratinocyte/other cancers (3%) with a ratio of 2.1 (Figure 1C). Teledermoscopy diagnosis of ‘melanoma’ or ‘probable melanoma’ (N=395) had a higher histological confirmation rate (70%) and median Breslow thickness (0.89 mm) than ‘possible melanoma’ (N=430) (31%, 0.5 mm).

Patient characteristics for melanomas (363 patients) and not-melanomas (320) were similar for median age (67 and 64 respectively), male majority (56%, 53%), European ethnicity (99%, 90%), and Fitzpatrick skin type 2 (65%, 59%). Lesion locations were comparable with 43% on the torso, 30% on the head, and 27% on the extremities.

Our teledermoscopy service had a 70% positive predictive value (PPV) for melanoma and 80% for appropriately excised lesions. The number needed to treat (NNT) was 2.01 for melanoma (N=410) and 1.47 for lesions requiring excision (N=561).

Conclusions

We have reported high diagnostic accuracy comparable to similar studies [4]. We did not find that characteristics of patients in this cohort with melanoma differed from those with other final diagnoses.

Stratifying lesions as “probable” vs. “possible” melanoma might help prioritize high-risk cases, as we have confirmed “probable” cases were more likely to be confirmed and had more advanced disease.

Diagnostic accuracy in teledermoscopy varies. Our results relate to consistently high image quality and the expertise of our teledermatologists and may not be achievable in other centers [4]. We concluded that melanocytic naevi with moderate–severe atypia were correctly excised because of the known variations in the histopathological interpretation of melanocytic lesions [5].

Melanoma can be accurately diagnosed given high quality images and experienced teledermoscopists, resulting in a high PPV and low NNT. The nurse-led clinic model is timely, scalable, and valuable where in-person dermatology access is limited.

Intralesional Vitamin D in Dermatology: An Evidence-Based Review of Utility
Authors: Subuhi Kaul, Piyush Kumar, Anupam Das
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Vitamin D has been used in a gamut of medical conditions, including dermatology. Intralesional Vitamin D, however, is not a common therapeutic option in dermatological disorders. This review is an attempt to summarise the evidence available for using intralesional vitamin D in warts, molluscum, keloids, and vitiligo.

Introduction

Vitamin D is a steroid hormone that plays a major role in cellular differentiation, proliferation, and immunoregulation that renders it essential for human health.[12] In dermatology, vitamin D has been employed in several forms: topical, oral, intramuscular, and intralesional.[3456] Intralesional vitamin D3 (ILVitD) has been utilised in infectious and non-infectious dermatological conditions including warts, molluscum, and keloids.[56789] Historically, it has been used in lupus vulgaris before effective antitubercular therapy became available.[10] Although evidence for its effectiveness in the treatment of verrucae is present, knowledge of its utility in other conditions is limited.[5] Thus, we review the available literature for utility of ILVitD in dermatology.

Methods

We searched PubMed using the keywords “Vitamin D” AND “intralesional” without any limits. A search for additional articles was done on Google Scholar. Clinical trials, systematic reviews, and high-quality reviews that evaluated ILVitD use in dermatology were included.

Mechanism of action

Although the exact mechanism is uncertain, various mechanisms of action have been proposed to explain the efficacy of ILVitD (1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D). Vitamin D has been hypothesised to regulate epidermal proliferation and enhance cytokine release which explains its antiviral action in warts.[511] This action is mediated via vitamin D receptor (VDR).[7] Additionally, stimulation of Toll-like Receptor (TLR)-mediated macrophage activation leads to upregulation of vitamin D receptors and vitamin D hydroxylase, which induces the production of antimicrobial peptides.[12] This is supported by the increased expression of LL37, a cathelicidin-derived AMP, demonstrated after ILVitD administration.[13] In keloids, it slows fibrosis by inhibiting TGF-beta-mediated fibroblast extracellular matrix production.[7] Studies demonstrated decreased serum vitamin D levels in patients with alopecia areata (AA).[1415] Also, hair follicles in affected areas have reduced VDR expression and this was associated with increased inflammation on histopathological examination.[14] Importantly, a decrease in efficacy of vitamin D is noted with cigarette smoking and advanced age.[16]

Utility of intralesional vitamin D in dermatology

Warts (Level of evidence 1)

Trials have compared vitamin D3 with placebo, destructive modalities, and intralesional immunotherapies.[2511171819] A recent metanalysis reported that ILVitD was more effective than placebo with about 60% patients achieving complete resolution. However, when compared with intralesional purified protein derivative (PPD), vitamin D was less effective.[5] Studies vary in the type of warts treated, concentration utilised, volume injected, and interval between and total sessions. Procedure

The concentration of cholecalciferol (vitamin D3) used varies from 100,000 IU/mL to 300,000 IU/mL.[1820] An insulin syringe is used to inject either the largest wart or multiple warts (up to 5 warts per session).[18202122] Most investigators inject about 0.2–0.6 mL of ILVitD per wart.[21113202123] To minimise pain, local application of ice packs, topical anesthesia, or intralesional lidocaine is used prior to intradermal injection.[182021] The treatments are repeated at intervals of 2 to 4 weeks until clearance or up to a maximum of 3 or 4 treatments.[222324]

Results

One trial compared ILVitD injection with cryotherapy in adult patients with extragenital warts and found a statistically significant higher complete clearance rate with vitamin D.[17] Two randomised trials compared ILVitD with intralesional PPD with contrasting results.[1122] Elsayed Ghaly et al. reported complete response in 70% in the ILVitD cohort compared to 50% with PPD,[22] whereas Abou-Taleb et al.[11] noted higher clearance with PPD. Another non-randomised trial showed comparable results with vitamin D and PPD.[25] Intralesional Candida antigen has been compared with vitamin D in three trials. The results were inconsistent; Razik et al. found Candida antigen to be more effective, Abdelaal et al. reported comparable efficacy of both drugs, and Fathy et al. found vitamin D to be more effective.[202124] This difference may be because the wart types differed: Razik et al. included multiple recalcitrant common warts, whereas the others included plantar warts.[202124] Intralesional 2% Zinc sulfate and ILVitD were compared in plantar warts and were comparable.[2326] Measles mumps rubella vaccine (MMR) had higher clearance rates of 80%–87% compared with 67%–77% with ILVitD.[2728] Recently, the efficacy of ILVitD was compared with that of photodynamic therapy (PDT) in recalcitrant palmoplantar warts with comparable response rates of 89% and 86% with vitamin D and PDT, respectively.[18]

Molluscum contagiosum (Level of evidence 5)

Sonthalia et al.[9] used 0.2 mL of ILVitD in two patients with multiple recalcitrant lesions (total two sessions) with complete resolution and no recurrence at the 6-month follow-up. Bajaj et al.[29] used ILVitD in an adult immunocompetent patient with extensive genital MC and reported “significant resolution” of lesions with a single session. The authors injected only 3 lesions (with 0.1 to 0.2 mLs; total amount injected was 0.5 mLs) and noted resolution of the majority of the lesions in 3 weeks. Both authors reported minimal adverse effects. Either pre-procedure lidocaine or non-steroidal pain killers were used post procedure for pain.[929] Considering the clinical response and negligible adverse effects, ILVitD appears promising.

Alopecia areata (no evidence at present)

Reports of vitamin D deficiency in AA prompted exploration of ILVitD as a therapeutic option in AA. Although there are no published studies on the use of ILVitD in AA, the focus of this review, there is an ongoing trial (NCT04660786) to assess the same.[30]

Keloids (level of evidence 3)

Although there are many therapeutic options for keloids, each option comes with differential efficacy and side effects. Mamdouh et al.[7] studied ILVitD in keloids. Forty patients were treated with 0.2 ml (200,000 IU) ILVitD, per 1 cm lesion, once weekly for 3–4 sessions. A statistically significant improvement was noted in terms of Vancouver Scar Scale and ultrasonic keloid scar thickness after treatment. Transient post-procedure adverse effects included pain, redness, swelling, and tenderness and were noted in 52.5% of the study participants. The authors concluded that ILVitD was an effective and safe therapeutic modality for keloids.[7] In another study by Radmehr et al.,[8] intralesional injection of vitamin D3 combined with cryotherapy significantly increased the treatment response and 57% of the lesions in the intervention group were completely resolved after 4 sessions.

Vitiligo (level of evidence 3)

One comparative clinical trial studied intralesional platelet-rich plasma (PRP) and vitamin D in stable non-segmental vitiligo.[31] However, results were not encouraging; PRP injection (with or without vitamin D) had better results compared to vitamin D injections alone.[30] Despite encouraging reports of topical vitamin D analogue in vitiligo, the evidence for ILVitD injections is poor and its use is best avoided. Adverse effects

ILVitD is associated with minor transient peri-procedural side effects. Most patients experience pain at the injection site.[1118] Other common post-procedure changes were erythema and edema.[11] Some studies reported a transient vasovagal response that resolved spontaneously.[202328] The vasovagal responses were described as dyspnea and coughing that resolved in 1 to 2 minutes.[23] Hyperpigmentation was reported in about 10% patients in one study.[22] Importantly, there was no change in serum calcium from baseline after use of ILVitD.[22]

Conclusion

ILVitD has been found to be safe and effective in warts with minimal adverse effects. Given the limited evidence of its utility in keloids and molluscum contagiosum, it can be considered as a possible treatment option if conventional agents have failed. Further controlled clinical trials are required to determine its efficacy in these conditions. Additionally, an ongoing clinical trial of ILVitD will help shed light on its utility as a steroid sparing agent in AA.

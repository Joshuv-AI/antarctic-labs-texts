Heterogeneous Model Alignment in Digital Twin
Faima Abbasi, Jean-Sébastien Sottet, Cedric Pruski

Abstract
Digital twin (DT) technology integrates heterogeneous data and models, along with semantic technologies to create multi-layered digital representation of physical systems. DTs enable monitoring, simulation, prediction, and optimization to enhance decision making and operational efficiency. A key challenge in multi-layered, model-driven DTs is aligning heterogeneous models across abstraction layers, which can lead to semantic mismatches, inconsistencies, and synchronization issues. Existing methods, relying on static mappings and manual updates, are often inflexible, error-prone, and risk compromising data integrity. To address these limitations, we present a heterogeneous model alignment approach for multi-layered, model-driven DTs. The framework incorporates a flexibility mechanism that allows metamodels to adapt and interconnect seamlessly while maintaining semantic coherence across abstraction layers. It integrates: (i) adaptive conformance mechanisms that link metamodels with evolving models and (ii) a large language model (LLM) validated alignment process that grounds metamodels in domain knowledge, ensuring structural fidelity and conceptual consistency throughout the DT lifecycle. This approach automates semantic correspondences discovery, minimizes manual mapping, and enhances scalability across diverse model types. We illustrate the approach using air quality use case and validate its performance using different test cases from Ontology Alignment Evaluation Initiative ( OAEI ) tracks.

2
Related Work

We group related work into (i) DT model alignment and (ii) heterogeneous model alignment, summarizing key approaches, gaps, and the motivation for our study. Here, heterogeneous refers to differences between DT layers (see Fig.
1
), not differences between metamodels (e.g., product, process, service).

DT Model Alignment:
Existing research only explores model-model alignment in DTs, and less emphasis is laid on model-metamodel and metamodel-ontology alignment. Model alignment focuses on matching and linking of equivalent elements ensuring consistency of models across abstraction layers. Su et al.
[
31
]
propose a three-layer knowledge graph architecture: (i) a concept layer organizing key information, (ii) a model layer aligning digital and physical parameters, and (iii) a decision layer combining models with real-time data for decision support. Their architecture facilitates model alignment by structuring key information into a knowledge network, ensuring consistency across layers. Validated in aero-engine blade production, it integrates multi-source data, improves prediction and anomaly detection, strengthening process control and quality management. Woitsch et al.
[
34
]
presents a model-driven approach to integrate data across the entire product lifecycle, from design to service. Their methodology emphasizes the alignment of models, such as product, process, and service models, by establishing consistent interfaces and transformation rules, thereby ensuring semantic coherence and interoperability. This alignment process facilitates the seamless integration of these models, enabling real-time data exchange and synchronization between the physical and digital counterparts. The approach uses DT technology, ontological framework, and model transformations to enable dynamic updates, feedback loops, and accurate integration of evolving physical entities, supporting improved decision-making and lifecycle optimization. Franco et al.
[
9
]
propose a five layer human DT architecture, i.e., (i) physical, (ii) data, (iii) model, (iv) integration, and (v) interaction. By employing a modular approach, their approach facilitates the seamless alignment of various models, ensuring consistency and coherence across different system components. This model-model alignment is achieved through standardized interfaces and protocols, allowing for the synchronization of data and behaviors across the system. Gil et al.
[
12
]
extend DT coupling via co-simulation and semantic lifting to ensure structural integrity, enabling model alignment and matching through reusable simulation units and standardized configuration files. Zheng et al.
[
35
]
present a tri-model CPS architecture (digital, computational, graph-based) to simulate the physical behavior of products in smart manufacturing. The approach enables model-to-model matching and seamless data flow across layers, using a digital thread framework for real-time consistency, enhancing DT interoperability and adaptability in dynamic manufacturing.

Heterogeneous Model Alignment:

This approach is rarely explored in a multi-layered, model-driven DTs. Heterogeneous model alignment spans across abstraction levels, i.e., model–metamodel and metamodel–ontology, ensuring model conformity and structural consistency. Study in
[
19
]
addresses the challenge of maintaining behavioral consistency across diverse modeling languages and tools in MDE. The author proposes a methodology that integrates heterogeneous behavioral models by aligning their respective behavioral metamodels. This alignment is achieved by defining inter-model relations that carry behavioral meaning, enabling the conversion of models and their relations into a unified behavioral formalism. Subsequently, They analyzed global behavioural consistency by model-checking techniques. Metamodel-ontology alignment grounds metamodels in domain ontologies to enable semantic interoperability, reasoning, and integration of evolving real-world knowledge. Study in
[
26
]
uses
“linked open terms”
to align a public bus transport ontology with the transmodel metamodel. It achieves metamodel-ontology alignment by mapping transmodel UML concepts and component ontologies to ontology modules, covering agencies, routes, and journeys, ensuring semantic consistency and interoperability while addressing challenges from transmodel complexity. Study in
[
18
]
presents the concept of
lifting metamodel to ontology
. Process employed by authors makes implicit concepts in the metamodel explicit, enabling semantic interoperability across modeling languages and facilitating the integration of heterogeneous models. By providing a formal, ontology-based representation of metamodels, the approach supports consistent reasoning, improved conceptual matching, and more robust model integration in complex systems.

Existing DT research mainly aligns models with other models, while model-to-metamodel and metamodel-to-ontology alignment is rarely addressed. Current methods are domain-specific and manual, limiting scalability and making real-time, fully semantic, cross-layer integration challenging.

4.1
JavaScript Modelling Framework (JSMF)

JSMF is a dynamic platform, inspired by
Eclipse Modeling Framework (EMF)
that relaxes strict metamodel conformance through partial adherence, dynamic typing, and modular evolution. Its features, i.e., proto-metamodels, on-the-fly customization, and posterior metamodel inference, enable models to evolve from exploratory to validated forms. This adaptability directly supports model-driven, multi-layered DT, where diverse models at different abstraction/modelling levels must be matched and aligned. By allowing gradual convergence from loose to strict structures, JSMF facilitates iterative matching and alignment across DT layers, i.e., data, model and metamodel, ensuring consistency and supporting DT lifecycle.

4.2
Semantic & Structure-aware Metamodel Ontology Matching (SSM-OM)

This subsection details heterogeneous model matching and alignment within model-driven, multi-layered DT, achieved by semantically and structurally aligning a source metamodel with a domain-specific ontology to ensure conceptual consistency and interoperability with established knowledge frameworks.
We propose a hybrid method, inspired from
[
14
]
, and combine graph-based embeddings
[
23
,
5
]
, lexical similarity
[
10
]
, and reasoning with LLMs
[
7
]
to align a source metamodel with a target ontology (e.g.,
Brick Ontology
5
5

5

https://brickschema.org/
). This approach leverages structural constructs from graphs and linguistic insights from embeddings
[
2
,
3
]
, enhanced by LLMs’ deep contextual understanding, to identify semantically equivalent or related concepts across different knowledge domains
[
14
]
. We describe our approach in following steps:

1.

Graph Construction:
We transform a domain-specific metamodel (in
JSON
), extracted from JSMF and constituting
Listing
2
into a structured source
RDF
graph, i.e.,
\mathcal{G\textsubscript{S}}
, representing each class as
rdfs:Class
with human-readable
rdfs:label
and
rdfs:description
(can be missing in classical metamodel). We capture
rdf:property
as class attributes, either as primitives with literal ranges or as references linking classes. This transformation yields the source triple set
\mathcal{T}_{s}
as defined in
Eq
1
:

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{s}={}&\{(c,\mathsf{rdf:type},\mathsf{rdfs:Class})\mid c\in\text{Metamodel Classes}\}\\
&\quad\cup\{(p,\mathsf{rdfs:domain},c),(p,\mathsf{rdfs:range},r)\}\end{split}

(1)

(p,rdfs:domain,c)
indicates that class
c
that has property

p
, whereas
(p,rdfs:range,r)
defines its value type, either reference (class) or primitive (datatype).
This structure enables semantic alignment, and a corresponding triple set
\mathcal{T}_{t}
, is generated for the target ontology as depicted in
Eq
2
.

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{t}={}&\{(c,\mathsf{rdf:type},\mathsf{rdfs:Class})\mid c\in\text{Ontology Classes}\}\\
&\quad\cup\{(p,\mathsf{rdfs:domain},c),(p,\mathsf{rdfs:range},r)\}\end{split}

(2)

The target ontology graph
\mathcal{G\textsubscript{T}}
is built from a domain ontology (e.g.,
Brick
,
SAREF
) in
OWL/RDFS
, capturing class hierarchies (
rdfs:subClassOf
), properties (
rdf:type
,
rdfs:domain/range
), and inter-class relationships through object properties.

2.

Contextual Expansion:
Semantic context for each class
c
is captured via
N-hop
neighborhood expansion using a
breadth first search (BFS)
style traversal, producing a localized subgraph for both the source metamodel and target ontology.
Eq
3
depicts how this context is computed:

\begin{split}\textbf{Context}_{c}^{(h)}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{h}\{(p\textsubscript{i},o\textsubscript{i}))\mid(c\textsubscript{i},p\textsubscript{i},o\textsubscript{i})\in\mathcal{G}\}\end{split}

(3)

For any
RDF
graph
\mathcal{G}
,
c
i
is a class at hop
i
,
p
i
is a predicate (semantic relation, i.e., object, datatype, or ontology-level, linking classes or literals) and
o
i
is an object (class or datatype). Aggregating predicate-object pairs up to
h
hops, defines
\textbf{Context}_{c}^{(h)}
, representing the local semantic structure of class
c
. Each class’s local structure is described in natural language for LLM understanding.

3.

Top-k Candidates:
We propose a
multi-feature matching
approach combining lexical cues, structural features,
sBERT
6
6

6

https://www.sbert.net/docs/sentence_transformer/pretrained_models.html
embeddings, and similarity metrics (jaro–winkler, jaccard) to identify
top-k
semantically related classes and trace evolution between a metamodel and ontology. We extract semantic and structural features from the source and target to support ontology matching.

(a)

Semantic Embeddings:
For each class
c
in
RDF
graph
\mathcal{G}
, a textual description aggregating its label, comments, superclass, and property-value pairs is created to generate two dense
sBERT
embeddings, i.e., a label embedding (
\mathcal{E}\textsubscript{Label}
-lexical semantics) and a rich-context embedding (
\mathcal{E}\textsubscript{rich}
-full semantic context) using
Eq
4
.

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}(c)=\alpha\cdot\mathcal{E}\textsubscript{Label}(\texttt{sBERT}(\texttt{label(c)}))\\
+(1-\alpha)\cdot\mathcal{E}\textsubscript{Rich}(\texttt{sBERT}(\texttt{rich(c)})\end{split}

(4)

rich(c)
=
label(c)
+
comment(c)
+
subClassOf(c)
+
property(c)
and it captures a class’s full semantic context. A unified embedding
\mathcal{V}(c)
combines label and rich-context embeddings through a trade-off
\alpha\in[0,1]
. For each source metamodel and target ontology class (
c
s
,
c
t
), we measure the semantic proximity in vector space using
Eq
5
.

\begin{split}\textbf{semantic-score}&\rightarrow\textbf{sim}(c\textsubscript{s},c\textsubscript{t})\\
&=1-\cos(\mathcal{V}(c\textsubscript{s}),\mathcal{V}(c\textsubscript{t}))\end{split}

(5)

To improve alignment, a lexical boost using the jaro–winkler metric adjusts embedding similarity when class labels are highly similar, overriding the score if similarity exceeds a threshold
\theta
, depicted in
Eq
6
.

\begin{split}\textbf{IF JW (L\textsubscript{s}, L\textsubscript{t})}>\theta&\rightarrow\textbf{semantic-score}\\
&=\textbf{max}(\textbf{semantic-score},0.95)\end{split}

(6)

This rule preserves near-identical lexical matches (
\theta=0.9
), enhancing early candidate filtering, recall, and robust identification of semantically related concepts.

(b)

Structural Features:
To capture structural features, we compute jaccard similarity over each class’s
RDF
property set
(p rdfs:domain c)
(
Eq.
7
), providing a lightweight structural signal that complements embeddings and lexical similarity for improved alignment.

\begin{split}P(c)=\{local\_name(p)\mid domain(p)=c\}\end{split}

(7)

local_name(p)
represents the property’s simplified label, capturing the class’s structural signature. Jaccard similarity between two classes (
c
s
,
c
t
), their jaccard similarity over property set is computed in
Eq
8
:

\textbf{structural\_signal}\rightarrow\textbf{Jaccard}(c_{s},c_{t})=\frac{\left|P(c_{s})\cap P(c_{t})\right|}{\left|P(c_{s})\cup P(c_{t})\right|}

(8)

Jaccard similarity over
RDF
property sets measures shared versus unique properties (0 if both sets are empty), capturing structural semantics to improve alignment by: (i) disambiguating classes with similar labels, (ii) revealing functional alignment across differing names, and (iii) enhancing robustness in sparse or lightweight models.

We use a soft fusion strategy, combining semantic embeddings (Eq.
5
and
6
) and structural features (Eq.
8
) into a tunable hybrid score for each source-target class pair (Eq.
9
) to enhance alignment precision and recall, which can used to track semantic and structural changes in DT lifecycle.

\begin{split}\textbf{score}(c\textsubscript{s},c\textsubscript{t})&=\beta\cdot\textbf{semantic\_score}\\
&\quad+(1-\beta)\cdot\textbf{structural\_signal}\\
\textbf{score}(c\textsubscript{s},c\textsubscript{t})&=\beta\cdot\textbf{sim}(c\textsubscript{s},c\textsubscript{t})+\\
&\quad(1-\beta)\cdot\textbf{Jaccard}(c_{s},c_{t})\end{split}

(9)

With
\beta\in[0,1]
, balancing semantic and structural signals, we compute a similarity matrix,
S

\in

\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, where
n
is number of source metamodel classes and
m
is number of target ontology classes.

5
Evaluation

This section evaluates the
SSM-OM
proof-of-concept on
OAEI
benchmark tracks, reporting key metrics to assess its generalization and robustness across diverse alignment scenarios. In information retrieval, the effectiveness of matching tasks is evaluated by comparing predicted outcomes against a ground truth. Precision measures the accuracy of retrieved results, while recall assesses their completeness. Together, these metrics provide a balanced view of system performance, capturing both relevance and coverage. When applying these metrics to ontology matching, the system-generated correspondences are referred to as the
Alignment (A)
, while the expert-validated outcomes are denoted as the
Reference (R)
. Consequently, precision (
\frac{|\texttt{A}\cap\texttt{R}|}{|\texttt{A}|}
), recall (
\frac{|\texttt{A}\cap\texttt{R}|}{|\texttt{R}|}
) and F1-score (
\frac{2\,|\texttt{A}\cap\texttt{R}|}{|\texttt{A}|+|\texttt{R}|}
) are used to evaluate the the performance of ontology matching tasks.

7
Conclusion

We present a flexible, semantics and structure-aware approach for designing and aligning multi-layered DTs, supporting rigid and relaxed conformance using two-phase process combining JSMF-based adaptation with ontology-driven, LLM-validated alignments. Using a case-based generalization approach, its effectiveness was demonstrated on an air quality use case and validated on
OAEI
tracks. Future work will explore managing semantic drift in multi-layered DTs.

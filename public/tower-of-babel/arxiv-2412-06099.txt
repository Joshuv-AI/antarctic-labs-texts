Abstract
. Software engineers frequently grapple with the challenge of accessing disparate documentation and telemetry data, including TroubleShooting Guides (TSGs), incident reports, code repositories, and various internal tools developed by multiple stakeholders. To enhance the efficiency of on-call engineers (OCEs) and streamline their daily workflows, we introduced DECO—a comprehensive framework for developing, deploying, and managing copilots tailored to improve productivity in engineering routines. This paper details the design and implementation of the DECO framework, emphasizing its innovative NL2SearchQuery functionality and a lightweight agentic framework. These features support efficient and customized retrieval-augmented-generation (RAG) algorithms that not only extract relevant information from diverse sources but also select the most pertinent skills in response to user queries. This enables the addressing of complex technical questions and provides seamless, automated access to internal resources. Additionally, DECO incorporates a robust mechanism for converting unstructured incident logs into user-friendly, structured guides, effectively bridging the documentation gap. Since its launch in September 2023, DECO has demonstrated its effectiveness through widespread adoption, enabling tens of thousands of interactions and engaging hundreds of monthly active users (MAU).

Introduction

The daily activities of software developers encompass a broad spectrum of tasks that extend beyond mere development activities such as coding, debugging, and testing
(
Meyer et al. 2019
)
. Predominantly, engineers allocate the majority of their efforts to ancillary tasks including code review, documentation, and on-call responsibilities
(
Gonçalves et al. 2011
)
. These duties necessitate extensive manual searches across disparate resources—ranging from product documentation and troubleshooting guides to telemetry data, system logs, code repositories, and team reports. Moreover, engineers invest considerable effort into navigating internal tools that are essential for their daily operations during the arduous task of managing such varied information sources. The challenge is compounded when critical institutional knowledge is isolated or hoarded by senior engineers, resulting in significant knowledge gaps when these key individuals are absent or depart from the organization.

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs), such as GPT
(
Achiam et al. 2023
)
, LLaMA
(
Touvron et al. 2023
)
, and Gemini
(
DeepMind 2024
)
, have emerged as powerful tools in this context. These models offer significant potential to enhance the engineers’ efficiency by automating mundane tasks, providing swift access to relevant information, and bridging knowledge gaps. The deployment of such AI tools not only facilitates more efficient workflow but also ensures centralized access to a diverse array of resources, thereby streamlining the decision-making process for software development teams.

Introduction to DECO—a framework for developing, deploying, and managing copilots

In this paper, we introduce DECO, a framework for developing, deploying, and managing copilot applications designed to support the continuous evolution of a software development team’s knowledge base while simplifying access to various internal tools. Copilots configured with DECO streamline daily tasks, improve incident management, and provide engineers with a unified access point to a wide range of internal resources and tools. DECO focuses on four key areas: (1) a
Generalized Development Platform
, enabling contributors to easily create and deploy new copilots; (2)
Skills/Agent Integration
, allowing teams to add new capabilities that incorporate additional knowledge sources or expose skills for accessing internal tools through a chat interface; (3)
Fine-Tuned Retrieval Algorithms
, designed for efficiency and accuracy in production environments; and (4)
Streamlined End-to-End Deployment and Lifecycle Management
, offering a self-hosting model with fully automated deployment pipelines, continuous monitoring, and testing capabilities to support debugging and ongoing improvements. Additionally, DECO supports an extensible development platform that enables integration with internal tools—such as debugging utilities and monitoring systems—frequently used in the daily tasks of engineers.

DECO introduces the following innovations:

Generalized Framework.

Traditional development of copilot applications typically involves iterative adjustments and prompt tuning through trial and error to attain satisfactory performance. In contrast, DECO introduces an “autonomous mode” that facilitates self-onboarding for tenants, adopting a “one-size-fits-all” approach posing a higher level of development sophistication.

Knowledge Gap.

Recognizing the challenges of maintaining high-quality documentation and its critical role in providing accurate context to large language models (LLMs), DECO implements a robust mechanism to transform large volumes of incident logs into user-friendly, structured guides, enriching the knowledge base.

Response Quality.
To enhance the quality of the copilot’s responses, we implemented several key improvements:

DECO employs an agentic framework for skill and agent selection, integrating advanced retrieval algorithms to optimize both accuracy and functionality.

The most common issues with the copilot are not due to hallucinations (see Section
8.2
, where only 0.1% of responses are identified as having grounding issues), but rather document relevancy problems. This challenge becomes particularly pronounced when managing a large inventory of skills and documentation segments. To manage diverse functionalities and enhance response accuracy, DECO employs an agentic framework to select the appropriate agent and corresponding skills for addressing user queries. Compared to using a single planner to choose from a larger set of skills, this sequential selection approach significantly improves skill selection accuracy.
Additionally, we develop an an innovative “NL2SearchQuery” method to enhance document retrieval from documentation sites, Incident Management (IcM) databases, and other text-based sources.
For code search, we introduce the Language-Agnostic RA (LA-RAG), which preprocesses large code repositories containing multiple languages while preserving code component relationships.

DECO incorporates a comprehensive evaluation system alongside telemetry data collection pipelines to facilitate online monitoring and debugging.

Additionally, specialized evaluators are implemented to verify the accuracy of individual modules within the copilot workflow and identify opportunities for improvement.
Hosted on Azure, DECO features a comprehensive telemetry infrastructure that not only tracks copilot response quality but also supports efficient logging for rapid issue triaging.

2.
Background

The primary objective of DECO is to provide a framework for setting up copilots that offer engineers a time-efficient, interactive experience for managing daily tasks and on-call responsibilities. DECO effectively bridges gaps between product telemetry, documentation, on-call duties, code development resources, and team-specific toolkits.

2.1.
Data Sources

By default, DECO incorporates resources from (1) internal documentation repositories, such as troubleshooting guides; (2) historical incident databases; (3) code repositories; and (4) internal Stack Overflow. Teams can also add additional skills to integrate more sources as needed.

Documentation Site

Microsoft maintains an internal documentation site that serves as a central hub for critical information, including engineering documentation and troubleshooting guides relevant to various products and services.

IcM

Incident data, including information such as title, summary, source, and owning team, is stored in a dedicated database
(
Chen et al. 2020
)
. This database also captures all communications between engineers, including investigation notes. These records serve not only as a comprehensive documentation resource for incident triaging but also provide invaluable guidance for broader engineering practices.

Code Base

For questions related to code, we process the original code repository to create indexes that facilitate easy retrieval of relevant functions, classes, statements, and other code elements.

Stack Overflow

Similar to IcM databases, Stack Overflow serves as an invaluable resource for Microsoft engineers, offering a wealth of community-driven knowledge and problem-solving techniques. Discussions from these forums are extracted and summarized into reports for future reference.

Common Infrastructure

DECO provides a unified data preprocessing infrastructure that standardizes ingestion, transformation, and indexing across all pipelines. This common infrastructure includes the following modules, each of which can be customized upon instantiation:

•

Data Ingestion

•

•

4.4.
Code Processor

Modern software codebases are inherently multi-language, often integrating diverse programming paradigms and a variety of file formats. Beyond source code, typical codebases include configuration files such as YAML, JSON, and XML, which play a critical role in defining system behavior but remain outside the main compilation process.
This heterogeneity poses significant challenges for compiler-based indexing tools attempting to comprehensively analyze and query codebases.

Abstract Syntax Tree (AST)-based indexing methods, such as those leveraging Tree-Sitter
(
Brunsfeld et al. 2022
)
, primarily focus on the syntax of individual programming languages while neglecting other essential components like configuration files, external dependencies, and runtime interactions. As a result, these methods often miss crucial relationships that span across language boundaries. Additionally, because compilation or interpretation usually occurs independently for each language (e.g., Python and C# components are processed separately), traditional indexing techniques struggle to capture the holistic behavior of multi-language systems.
Additionally, conventional document chunking and vector-based retrieval approaches frequently struggle to preserve the intricate dependencies and relationships within a codebase. Even worse, these methods risk fragmenting single code segment into multiple chunks, disrupting the continuity of processed documentation and impairing the model’s ability to accurately answer complex user queries.

To address these limitations, DECO introduces the
Language-Agnostic RAG (LA-RAG)
, which leverages LLMs’ multi-language capabilities without relying on compilation. Unlike in-memory Copilot solutions that process only open files within an Integrated Development Environment (IDE), our offline indexing mechanism enables comprehensive search across large-scale codebases containing thousands of files and millions of lines of code spanning multiple languages by structuring the codebase as a loosely coupled reference graph. For the online retrieval, our system is able to accurately determine code construct usage, ensuring context-aware retrieval beyond immediate source files.

The preprocessing pipeline is developed using the same infrastructure as discussed in Section
4
. Similarly as the TSG Processor, the Code Processor access the code based from Git repositories, and tentatively splits long code files into fix-size smaller chunks. After generating code chunks, we apply the following metadata enrichment and embedding steps:

•

LLM Rechunking
: To prevent functions or classes from being split across chunks, we concatenate each chunk with its five preceding and succeeding chunks, then use an LLM to extract complete code segments.

•

Component Relationships
: We use LLM to identify one-hop related components and attach their corresponding code chunks, improving contextual understanding.

•

Description Augmentation
: The same LLM call generates a title, a concise description, references used within the code chunk, and summaries of referenced functions or classes to enhance searchability.

•

Embedding Generation
: We generate embeddings for code chunks and associated metadata, creating a structured search index for efficient retrieval across multiple fields that is used in the online phase (see Section
6
).

Typically, each copilot indexes approximately
300
to
20,000
code chunks for optimized search and retrieval.

5.
Copilot Backend

The backend REST API is implemented as a stateless API, requiring all necessary information, such as the full chat history as input. This design abstracts away much of the engineering overhead related to authentication and state/memory management, which is handled by our bot frontend (discussed in Section
7
). The backend orchestration is created to maximize re-usability across different copilots and allow easy customization of incorporate different skills/configuration settings from different teams.

5.1.
Design Principles

The DECO’s backend orchestration is guided by the following core principles:

Multi-turn interactions
:
The future of generative AI platforms lies in multi-agent, multi-turn copilots
(
Wu et al. 2023
)
. This pattern is emerging across various existing copilots, from OpenAI’s models iteratively correcting their own generated code during sandbox execution to multi-step data-gathering workflows for complex tasks
(
Zheng et al. 2025
)
.

In DECO, all iterative interactions are managed through the frontend, ensuring full transparency and user control. Past interactions are retained in history, allowing users to provide additional inputs when necessary. Unlike platforms that orchestrate multiple agents behind a single API call (see Figure
4(a)
), DECO does not require custom handling mechanisms for multi-turn interactions but only execute one agent in one invocation of the backend (see Figure
4(b)
), which offers better control compared to existing free-chat approaches, such as AutoGen
(
Wu et al. 2023
)
.

Simplicity and Maintainability

To facilitate ease of development, debugging, and extensibility, DECO adopts a modular and interpretable workflow based on a stepwise “tool-use scheme”. Execution follows a structured model where each agent sequentially invokes a subset of skills, actively interacting with the frontend.
This modular execution allows engineers to inspect intermediate states, log agent outputs, and isolate failures efficiently. Since agents are loosely coupled, individual components can be updated, replaced, or extended without affecting the overall pipeline. Additionally, each execution round generates transparent logs, making it easier to trace and debug incorrect responses.

Low-Latency Execution

DECO ensures real-time responsiveness by streaming intermediate outputs from agents as they execute. Unlike traditional batched execution, where users must wait for all skills to complete, our framework delivers partial responses incrementally (see Figure
4(b)
).
This approach enhances usability by allowing users to refine their input based on intermediate results, ensuring a dynamic and interactive experience. Furthermore, since each step displays output progressively, users can cancel an interaction at any time without losing already provided information.

Scalability and Efficient Planning

Flat planners, which evaluate all skills simultaneously, become inefficient as the number of available skills grows. As the skill set expands, a flat planner must consider an exponentially increasing number of possible combinations, leading to computational inefficiencies and suboptimal execution sequences.
Prior research, such as fine-tuning-based approaches
(
Patil et al. 2023
)
, has attempted to mitigate this by training LLMs to generate skill execution plans. However, fine-tuning requires extensive labeled data, making it both costly and impractical in dynamic environments where new skills are frequently introduced.

To overcome these limitations, DECO employs a hierarchical agentic planning approach, dynamically selecting and sequencing agents based on the user query. Instead of treating skills as isolated decision points, the system:

•

First determines the relevant
agent
(e.g., troubleshooting, database analysis, code retrieval).

•

Then, based on the selected agent, determines the appropriate
skills
to invoke.

This hierarchical approach reduces complexity, improves efficiency, and maintains flexibility for integrating new skills, ensuring DECO remains adaptable as capabilities evolve.

5.3.
Agents and Skills

Agent

To enhance DECO’s modularity, we introduce the concept of “agents”, enabling onboarding teams to seamlessly integrate with various skills or even existing copilots through APIs. Each agent is defined by (1) a detailed description of applicable scenarios; (2) a set of skills the agent can invoke; (3) (optional) an LLM prompt that the agent uses at the end of execution to generate a response, combining all skill outputs as input context; and (4) (optional) a hint for the next agent to be invoked. Based on the setting of (3), the agent can either invoke an additional LLM call or directly return the skill output as the agent output for this invocation round.

For example, the
Kusto Agent
includes the skills
kql.generator
and
kql.executor
; the
Root Cause Analysis Agent
uses the
RCA.copilot
skill; the
Code Agent
utilizes the
get_code
skill; and the
QA Agent
employs the skills
get_tsg
,
get_icm
, etc.

Skill

Each skill extends from a common abstract class and is defined by (1) a detailed description of its functionalities; (2) a specification of its input arguments, such as a rephrased user question, which need to be extracted from the skill input; (3) the execution code (in Python), which may involve API calls, LLM interactions, or any other execution processes; and (4) (optional) skill dependencies, which impacts the execution order, ensuring that required outputs from preceding skills are available as inputs.

For example, the
get_tsg
skill requires a “rephrased user intent” as an argument and retrieves relevant documentation via the documentation search API. Its output consists of a list of TSG chunks, which can later be leveraged by the agent’s final LLM call to generate a response. The
kql.executor
skill can execute Kusto queries on behalf of users.

5.4.
Manager

The Termination Decision Maker and the Planner execute in parallel during each workflow invocation round.

The
Termination Decision Maker
collects the chat history (i.e., the user’s question and responses from previously executed agents) and makes a termination decision using an LLM call. The prompt for this call includes detailed termination conditions and examples.

The
Planner
loads all available agents and compiles their names, descriptions, and hints for next agent into an agent selection prompt. An LLM generates the sequence of agents to be invoked in the meta-plan. In each round of workflow invocation, the Planner updates the meta-plan and selects the next agent for execution. In our current implementation, most user queries are resolved with fewer than three agents, and the Planner’s decisions are highly accurate.

5.5.
Executor

Once the Planner selects an agent for execution, the
Executor
performs the following steps to “execute” the corresponding agent:
Step 1
: Selects the skills to execute from the agent’s available skill set and extract arguments;
Step 2
: Generates a skill execution plan based on defined skill dependencies (if any);
Step 3
: Executes the skill execution plan and collects outputs from all invoked skills; and
Step 4
(Optional): Constructs the prompt based on the agent’s definition and makes a final LLM call to generate the agent’s response.

In Step 1, we compile descriptions of all available skills for the selected agent, along with their required input arguments, into the skill selector’s prompt. Few-shot examples are included to help guide the LLM in selecting the appropriate skills based on various user inputs. The output of this LLM call includes both the list of skills to invoke and the corresponding input arguments for each skill.
For the skill orchestration LLM call, we use the Semantic Kernel
(
Microsoft 2024g
)
sequential planner, integrated with Azure OpenAI function calling
(
Microsoft 2024e
)
to restrict the output format. In Step 1, the LLM determines only the set of skills to execute. Step 2 then applies a deterministic optimization mechanism to decide the execution order and parallelism, accounting for dependency constraints. If no constraints exist, all skills are executed in parallel.
For certain agents, some skills are capable of directly generating user responses and terminate this round of invocation.

6.1.
NL2SearchQuery

In the preprocessing pipeline, the processed data often includes several important search fields. For example, “
resolve date
” for IcMs, “
title
” and “
content
" for TSGs, and “
reference
” or “
description
” for code. Using an LLM, we can infer optimal matching criteria or additional filters based on user input to improve the retrieval results. For instance, queries such as “all the code
related
to the class SOSScheduler” or “show me
recent
incidents
similar to
Incident XXX” can be formulated as different search queries.

Since all fields are indexed, similar to a SQL query, we can search for relevant information by matching against different fields and applying different filters/predicates to pinpoint the most relevant documents. This insight informs the design of our
NL2SearchQuery
algorithm, as illustrated in Figure
6
. Given a user query, we leverage an LLM call to extract key arguments for retrieval and construct a complete search query which consists of selected search fields, time ranges, or any other filtering criteria predefined for each retrieval skill. Multiple search queries can be executed in parallel, and the combined result set can be re-ranked using different ranking strategies to produce the final output.
The following sections provide a detailed discussion of the retrieval strategies for IcM, TSG, and code respectively.

6.2.
IcM Retrieval

The
get_icm
skill, essential for generating curated incident reports, is frequently used to respond to user inquiries about how-to guides and to search for similar incidents, thereby offering additional mitigation strategies. We’ve noticed that user queries invoking this skill typically highlight specific incident details, such as failure mechanisms or server names, which align with the mitigation steps or extractable properties of incidents (discussed in Section
4
).
For this task, the following arguments are extracted to form the search query:

•

User Intent
: Reformulates the user query into concise search keywords, considering not only the original question but also the full chat history and previous intermediate outputs to create precise search terms. This is used as the search predicate for the matching.

•

Search Field
: Specifies the field to search within, such as “
summary
”, “
title
”, “
property
”, or “
mitigation
”. For example, if the user asks for similar incidents to a given one, “
title
” is often a suitable option. If the query includes specific properties like server name, “
property
” is used.

•

Search Method
: Options include hybrid search, semantic search, simple search, or vector search
(
Microsoft 2024f
)

Re-ranking.

The initial search query typically returns a large set of incidents, for example, 20. During the re-ranking step, we calculate scores for each incident to determine the top
K

The re-ranking score for a document
d

(1)

\displaystyle P(d)=\alpha\cdot\text{IS}+\beta\cdot\text{TS}+\gamma\cdot\text{SS}

where
IS
represents the information score, which evaluates the quality of the IcM summary based on data quality metrics like token length, pre-computed during the preprocessing pipeline (see Section
4

TS

SS
, the source score, verifies if the retrieved data aligns with the current context, assigning a value of 1 for matches (e.g., matching team or monitor ID in property fields) and 0 otherwise.
These scores are normalized and combined to re-rank the retrieved incidents, focusing on factors like information richness, timeliness, and source relevance. This process is detailed in Algorithm

6.3.
TSG Retrieval

The
get_tsg
function is a critical component of our backend flow. Traditional retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approaches typically use similarity-based searches, which compare user query embeddings with document chunk embeddings or employ term-frequency techniques
(
Robertson et al. 2009
)
. However, empirical analyses show that these methods often underperform with highly technical documents, as embeddings do not capture the subtle distinctions within technical terms effectively. Although recent advancements in supervised retrieval models, such as RAFT
(
Zhang et al. 2024b
)
, AR2
(
Zhang et al. 2022
)
, and ARG
(
Sachan et al. 2023
)
, have improved retrieval accuracy, they necessitate the development and fine-tuning of deep neural networks (DNNs). These approaches are impractical for our self-hosting framework in DECO, as they require specialized machine learning expertise and additional infrastructure for training and hosting ML models, which may not be readily available to different teams.

To address these challenges, we developed a hybrid search algorithm, leveraging an advanced preprocessing pipeline detailed in Section
4
, eliminating the need for additional ML training while enhancing both retrieval accuracy and computational efficiency. Building on the NL2SearchQuery pipeline, we introduce multiple search queries that run in parallel, we extract the following arguments for the search query:

•

Rephrased Query
: User queries are rephrased by the planner to generate search arguments. Searches are conducted using both the original user query and the generated search arguments. For search query involving incidents, we generate additional “search hint” based on the incident summary, similar to HyDE
(
Zhao et al. 2023
;
Gao et al. 2022
)
.

•

Search Field
: Both “
title
” and “
contents
”.

•

Search Method
:
Multi-Field Hybrid Search
based on reciprocal rank fusion (RRF)
(
Cormack et al. 2009
;
Microsoft Corporation 2024
)
is used to combine the similarity score from all the search fields.

We also introduce several additional techniques to enhance retrieval results.
A new
Document Filtering
step refines search results by analyzing the similarity scores of top-ranked documents during the re-ranking stage. If the highest-ranked document(s) show a significant margin over the following results, only these top documents are retained. This approach leverages the observation that a small set of highly relevant TSGs is often sufficient to resolve issues, reducing the inclusion of overly detailed or irrelevant documents and saving tokens for the final LLM calls (see Section
8.1
).

Additionally, during preprocessing, teams provide concise
Repository Descriptions
for each document source. These descriptions are included with the retrieved documents to enhance the LLM’s contextual understanding, improving the quality of the final LLM call within the agent.

Future enhancements include incorporating advanced retrieval algorithms like HyDE and Reverse HyDE
(
Gao et al. 2022
;
Zhao et al. 2023
)
, generating enriched matching fields, and exploring the integration of graph structures into the index
(
Anonymous 2024a
)
and leveraging feedback from historical conversations to dynamically refine search rankings
(
Anonymous 2024b
)
.

8.
Evaluation

The evaluation system in DECO is designed to support the continuous improvement of the framework by (1) helping to triage the reasoning behind low-quality responses and (2) evaluating the performance of individual components to guide further development and optimization. The evaluation process is divided into two categories: offline evaluation and online evaluation.

8.1.
Offline Evaluation

To support ongoing development of the copilot and evaluate changes such as prompt modifications and updates to retrieval algorithms or skill selection mechanisms, we developed an evaluation framework that allows testing in hypothetical scenarios without disrupting the service. The offline evaluation focuses on assessing the quality of copilot responses after code changes.

We observed that LLM evaluation often differs significantly from human judgments, especially those made by domain experts, in terms of relevance and usefulness.
Using a sample of user ratings, we explored the feasibility of employing an LLM to rate copilot responses on a scale from 1 to 5 stars, aiming at simulating human judgment. When comparing LLM-generated ratings with human ratings across 54 questions, we found a correlation of only 0.35 (see Figure
9
), indicating substantial discrepancies between LLM and human evaluations. Manual reviews revealed that many responses deemed inaccurate by domain experts were perceived as plausible by non-experts due to subtle errors that only specialists could detect. This highlights the limitations of using LLMs as evaluators for assessing copilot responses, as discussed in
(
Liu et al. 2023
)
. As a result, we prioritize using question-answer pairs from real user conversations with user ratings as benchmarks for evaluating copilot responses whenever feasible.

Evaluation Framework.

We built an flexible offline evaluation framework (see Figure
8
) for all the evaluation/test suites. The framework connects to the telemetry database using managed identity, allowing for the download of extensive real user queries, bot outputs, and user feedback. A curated set of highly user-rated question-answer pairs serves as the “golden answer” in the evaluation process. The framework features a runner that uses a configuration file to define which evaluators to include in the evaluation pipeline. Each
evaluator
, inheriting from a base class, implements three main functions:

•

Data Generator
: Prepares input data from telemetry or predefined sources.

•

Execution Function
: Runs the generated input data through specific components of the chat flow.

•

Evaluation Function
: Produces evaluation metrics for each processed input.

•

Report Generator
: Aggregates results from individual samples into a detailed dataset-level report, offering insights into chat flow performance.

Planner Evaluator

To evaluate the planner’s accuracy, we designed a series of questions intended to trigger different skills. For each question, a “golden answer” is provided, specifying the correct skills that should be invoked. The evaluation process for this module consists of the following steps:

•

Executing the planner module for each question.

•

Retrieving the list of skills invoked by the planner.

•

Comparing the invoked skills with the golden list. Precision and recall are calculated by comparing the set of selected skills with the golden set. Additionally, for each user question, we compute a Boolean metric named
coverage
, which indicates whether all required skills from the golden list are included in the triggered skills.

Whenever new skills/agents are added, we conduct experiments with this evaluator to ensure that the current planner can accurately select the skill when appropriate. If not, we fine-tune the skill’s description, the planner’s prompt, or the few-shot examples, to reach the optimal performance.

TSG Evaluator

The TSG Evaluator is designed to assess the accuracy of documentation chunks retrieved by the TSG retrieval process that are subsequently referenced in the copilot’s responses, i.e., by the
get_tsg
skill. The first “golden” set of TSG documentation is compiled from real user interactions, focusing only on responses rated four or five stars based on the “reference list" at the end of the copilot’s response, which includes clickable URLs to the cited documents as directed by the prompt.
The second “golden” set is generated by the LLM, which creates synthetic questions for each documentation chunk, allowing us to identify the corresponding TSG documentation that answers those questions, similar to the self-supervised learning
(
Wang and Castro Fernandez 2023
)
.

The evaluation process involves the following steps:

•

Extracting the golden TSG list from conversations with highly rated responses or synthetic data, denoted as
G
.

•

Running the
get_tsg
module for each query to produce a new set of documents, referred to as
Q
. This step yields deterministic results without LLM calls. Optionally, the entire workflow can be executed to capture the final reference list generated by the LLM, denoted as
Q^{*}
.

•

Calculating precision and recall metrics by comparing
G
and
Q^{*}
:

(2)

\displaystyle\text{Precision}=\frac{|G\cap Q^{*}|}{|Q^{*}|},\quad\text{Recall}=\frac{|G\cap Q^{*}|}{|G|}.

•

Assessing coverage by comparing
G
with
Q
to evaluate how well the retrieved documents (
Q
) “cover” the golden set (
G
):

(3)

\displaystyle\text{Coverage}=\frac{|G\cap Q|}{|G|}.

The results show that hybrid search (“HB-*”) significantly improves recall compared to other methods. Document filtering effectively reduces context length without compromising recall. For incident-related questions, where the raw query is combined with the incident ticket summary, search results are often suboptimal. However, rephrasing—similar to the HyDE process—enables the generation of optimized search text based on the ticket summary, improving document retrieval. Since generating ticket summaries involves an LLM call, the rephrasing process can be parallelized, avoiding additional latency on the critical path.

Incident Retrieval Evaluator

To assess IcM similarity, we developed a detailed evaluation metric using LLMs by constructing a comprehensive prompt that compares various aspects of incident summaries (e.g., title, mitigation steps, and recorded root cause). For each pair of IcM summaries, we generate a similarity score of “Low”, “Medium”, or “High”. This allows us to evaluate the similarity between the summary of an incident in a user input that it and the summaries of retrieved incidents based on any search for similar incidents.

The evaluation process involves the following steps:

•

Collecting incident IDs referred to in real user queries.

•

Extracting the summary of each incident.

•

Running our
get_icm
module to extract corresponding historic similar incident summaries for each input.

•

Assessing the similarity between the input incident and the retrieved incidents.

We executed the evaluator against 100 sampled user questions.
We compare these results against the baseline “Similar Incident API” developed by the IcM portal. As shown in Figure

Similarity Evaluator

To maintain consistency in response quality following code modifications, we rerun the copilot to generate new responses and benchmark them against historically high-rated answers. Specifically, we used LLM to assess the similarity between two answers to the same question, using a 1-5 star rating scale. Each newly generated response aims for a similarity rating of at least 4.0 when compared to prior highly-rated answers.

In contrast, DECO adopts a lightweight agentic framework optimized for production-scale deployments, decoupling backend and frontend interactions. This design improves scalability and adaptability, particularly as the number of available skills grows in enterprise environments.

10.
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced DECO, a framework for end-to-end management and deployment of copilots tailored to software engineering tasks. DECO integrates preprocessing pipelines with tightly connected front-end and back-end modules to support multi-turn interactions with users.
Leveraging historical incident tickets, DECO bridges knowledge gaps by enriching documentation with insights from past engineering practices. Its hierarchical skill selection with agentic design dynamically selects the most relevant context and tools, using advanced retrieval algorithms to surface pertinent incidents, documents, and code snippets.
A continuous evaluation pipeline monitors retrieval performance and response quality. Deployed across Microsoft, DECO now serves over 500 Monthly Active Users (MAUs), reducing time spent on incident management and improving engineering workflows.
Future work will focus on refining retrieval algorithms, improving document relevance through user feedback, and enhancing memory management to handle longer chat histories more efficiently.

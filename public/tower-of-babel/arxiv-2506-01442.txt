Agentic Episodic Control
Xidong Yang, Wenhao Li, Junjie Sheng, Chuyun Shen, Yun Hua, Xiangfeng Wang

Abstract
Reinforcement learning (RL) has driven breakthroughs in AI, from game-play to scientific discovery and AI alignment. However, its broader applicability remains limited by challenges such as low data efficiency and poor generalizability. Recent advances suggest that large language models, with their rich world knowledge and reasoning capabilities, could complement RL by enabling semantic state modeling and task-agnostic planning. In this work, we propose the Agentic Episodic Control (AEC), a novel architecture that integrates RL with LLMs to enhance decision-making. The AEC can leverage a large language model (LLM) to map the observations into language-grounded embeddings, which further can be stored in an episodic memory for rapid retrieval of high-value experiences. Simultaneously, a World-Graph working memory module is utilized to capture structured environmental dynamics in order to enhance relational reasoning. Furthermore, a lightweight critical state detector dynamically arbitrates between the episodic memory recall and the world-model-guided exploration. On the whole, by combining the trial-and-error learning scheme with LLM-derived semantic priors, the proposed AEC can improve both data efficiency and generalizability in reinforcement learning. In experiments on BabyAI-Text benchmark tasks, AEC demonstrates substantial improvements over existing baselines, especially on complex and generalization tasks like FindObj, where it outperforms the best baseline by up to 76%. The proposed AEC framework bridges the strengths of numeric reinforcement learning and symbolic reasoning, which provides a pathway toward more adaptable and sample-efficient agents.

Introduction

Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a cornerstone of modern AI, driving many of its most significant breakthroughs through trial and error.
RL has driven landmark achievements in game-playing, with systems like AlphaGo and AlphaZero mastering Go, Chess, and Shogi purely from self-play
silver2016mastering
;
silver2017mastering
.
Beyond games, RL has been successfully applied to robotics
loppenberg2024dynamic
;
ju2022transferring
, embodied agents
gupta2021embodied
, algorithm discovery
fawzi2022discovering
;
mankowitz2023faster
, and scientific applications
tunyasuvunakool2021highly
.
Furthermore, RL is recently central to aligning large language models (LLMs) with human intent, as seen in recent reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) approaches
ouyang2022training
;
trung2024reft
;
shao2024deepseekmath
.
These advances underscore the influence of RL across domains—spanning decision-making, reasoning, and human-AI alignment.

Despite its impressive achievements, RL still falls short of human-level learning in two key respects, i.e.,
data efficiency

schwarzer2021pretraining
;
ball2023efficient
and
generalization

korkmaz2024survey
;
ghosh2021generalization
.
While humans can learn from sparse experiences and transfer knowledge across domains, RL agents usually require millions of interactions to converge and struggle to adapt to novel or compositional tasks.
These limitations reflect a deeper deficiency: the absence of cognitive mechanisms critical to human learning, including symbolic abstraction
binder2011neurobiology
, structured memory
EICHENBAUM2004109
, and selective retrieval of relevant experiences
kuhl2009strategic
.
Crucially, these limitations also highlight a fundamental gap: whereas humans ground their decisions in symbolic language and prior knowledge, traditional RL agents operate on low-level state-action mappings with no semantic abstraction.

To address these limitations, researchers have proposed mechanisms such as Neural Episodic Control (NEC)
pritzel2017neural
, Differentiable Neural Dictionary (DND)
ritter2018been
, and episodic reinforcement learning frameworks
lengyel2008hippocampal
.
These approaches typically incorporate external memory modules to enhance sampling efficiency.
However, they always rely on shallow state representations (e.g., raw observations or low-dimensional embeddings) and lack mechanisms for strategic, semantic-level retrieval of experiences
ritter2018been
;
hung2019optimizing
.
Even advanced methods that integrate meta-learning with episodic memory are typically confined to specific task structures, making it challenging to generalize to more complex scenarios with long-term dependencies
mishra2018simple
;
wang2018prefrontal
.
In contrast, human episodic memory is supported by high-fidelity, semantically structured neural representations during encoding, and is shaped by interactions with prior knowledge and context
xue2018neural
;
ramey2022episodic
. These interactions enable humans to form more distinctive and durable memory traces, facilitating generalization and inference beyond surface-level similarity.

Cognitive neuroscience suggests that human learning emerges from the coordination of multiple systems:
semantic memory
for general world knowledge
rogers2004structure
,
episodic memory
for context-specific recall
tulving2002episodic
, and
working memory
for real-time reasoning and relational representation
miller2018working
.
These systems enable humans to interpret novel environments, recall useful past experiences, and plan actions in a goal-directed manner.
For instance, when faced with a new instruction, a person can decompose it using prior knowledge, retrieve similar situations, and reason over possible outcomes—all without repeated trial-and-error.

Recent advances in LLMs offer an opportunity to integrate similar capabilities into RL agents.
Trained on massive text corpora, LLMs encode rich semantic knowledge and support high-level reasoning, such as logical inference
patel2024multi
, commonsense reasoning
wang2023gemini
, and abstract problem solving
imani2023mathprompter
.
Functionally, they resemble the brain’s semantic memory system, allowing agents to interpret environmental states in structured, symbolic terms.

This motivates a shift from traditional RL architectures toward cognitively inspired frameworks that integrate language-driven understanding with structured memory control.
Such a design emulates the human learning loop: understanding the world semantically, maintaining structured memory of entities and events, and making decisions based on both present context and relevant past experience.
We introduce
Agentic Episodic Control (AEC)
—a framework that operationalizes this principle by integrating the LLM-based semantic encoder, dual-memory systems (episodic and graph-structured working memory), and a critical-state recognition mechanism that dynamically switches between exploration and memory recall.
Besides, AEC bridges symbolic reasoning and efficient decision-making by combining an LLM-based encoder for semantic abstraction, a graph-structured working memory for relational reasoning, and a critical-state recognizer that selectively triggers episodic recall when it matters most.

Our contributions are three-fold:

- We proposed LLM
-
grounded state encoding for episodic control, coupling deep semantic understanding with episodic memory, for the first time leveraging linguistic priors to break the limitations of traditional episodic-memory RL.

- We present a novel dual
-
memory decision architecture.
Based on the critical state recognition mechanism, the agent combines long-term episodic memory with graph-based working memory, enabling it to both recall past high-reward scenarios and reason about immediately perceived structures, thereby achieving remarkable performance in non-stationary tasks.

- We evaluate our method on BabyAI-Text. Our method significantly outperforms strong baselines in both sample efficiency and generalization. In addition, we conduct a preliminary exploration of cross-task memory transfer, suggesting the potential for reusable semantic representations.

Related Work

2.1
Neural Episodic Control

Episodic control refers to capturing and retaining the most influential experiences during the learning process to guide an agent’s decision-making. Model-free episodic control (MFEC)
blundell2016model
employs episodic memory buffers that hold experience tuples, compressing states via Gaussian random projection or variational auto-encoders. NEC
pritzel2017neural
introduced a differentiable neural dictionary that stores past experiences, with convolutional networks used for state encoding. Subsequent studies modify parameterised models through episodic memory
lin2018episodic
;
hansen2018fast
;
chen2022deep
;
liang2024episodic
;
lee2019sample
. Recently, research has shifted to state representation in neural episodic control. Episodic Reinforcement Learning with Associative Memory (ERLAM)
zhu2020episodic
associates related trajectories to form policies with efficient reasoning and propagates new values through a graph built over memory items. Neural Episodic Control with State Abstraction (NECSA)
li2023neural
performs state abstraction on grid-based environments to exploit topological structures and enhance policy learning and generalization. However, human episodic memory encodes experiences with prior knowledge and semantic detail
xue2018neural
;
ramey2022episodic
. We therefore introduce an LLM-based semantic encoder, aligned with this human characteristic to enhance decision-making.

2.2
LLMs for Reinforcement Learning

Effectively enhancing reinforcement learning with large language models is a promising research area. Several approaches have been explored to leverage LLMs in this field. One method is based on a hierarchical framework, where LLMs are used to decompose complex tasks and generate high-level plans, which are then executed by low-level controllers
peng2024hypothesis
;
shukla2023lgts
. For example, Hypothesis, Verification, and Induction framework (HYVIN)
peng2024hypothesis
first employs a large language model to propose subgoal hypotheses. Once these hypotheses are validated, general skills are learned under the guidance of these validated subgoals. Another approach utilizes LLMs to design reward functions, simplifying the typically labor-intensive process of reward function formulation
kwon2023reward
;
wu2023read
;
li2024auto
;
bhambri2024efficient
. For instance, read and reward framework
wu2023read
significantly improves the learning efficiency and performance of RL agents in Atari games by reading human-written game manuals, extracting key information, and generating auxiliary rewards. Auto MC-Reward
li2024auto
leverages large language models to automatically design dense reward functions, where the reward function is an executable Python function that uses predefined observation inputs to construct the reward. Some studies directly use LLMs as behavioral policies, training them via RL to interact with the environment
yan2023ask
;
shi2023unleashing
;
li2022pre
;
carta2023grounding
;
tan2024twosome
. For example, Grounded Language Models (GALM)
carta2023grounding
and Twosome
tan2024twosome
apply LLMs to text-based games. Our work differs from these approaches by leveraging the semantic understanding of LLMs to enhance episodic memory encoding, while dynamically coordinating with graph-structured working memory for real-time reasoning. This tight integration enables more human-like generalization and sample efficiency compared to existing LLM-RL hybrids.

2.3
LLM-based State Embedding

Recent advances demonstrate the versatile capabilities of large language models in enriching state representations across diverse applications
da2024prompt
;
shek2024lancar
;
chen2023llm
;
wang2024llm
;
benara2024crafting
. Language for context-aware robot locomotion (LANCAR)
shek2024lancar
employs an LLM-based contextual information translation module to interpret high-level, ambiguous human-language contextual information from the environment into contextual embeddings accessible to RL agents. LLM-State
chen2023llm
leveraged the inherent contextual understanding and historical action reasoning capabilities of LLMs to provide continuous expansion and updates of object properties. Question-answering embeddings (QA
-
Emb)
benara2024crafting
is a method that leverages large language models to answer a series of yes/no questions for generating interpretable state embeddings. Each response is mapped to a binary feature, ensuring that the embedding dimensions correspond directly to human-interpretable semantics. Our method integrates LLM-based semantic encoding directly into the agent’s core memory architecture, enabling deep semantic grounding of both episodic memory storage and retrieval processes.

The AEC Framework

Figure 1:
The overview of Agentic Episodic Control framework. In the Agentic Episodic Control framework, raw states are processed in parallel by an episodic memory state embedding module (b) to produce commonsense-grounded state embeddings and by a World-Graph working memory module (c) to build a structured working memory of entities and relations. An episodic control module (d), equipped with a critical-state recognizer, then uses these representations to decide whether to exploit episodic memory—retrieving rapid value estimates from past experiences at critical states—or to continue exploring under world-graph guidance. The chosen actions interact with the environment.

We introduce
Agentic Episodic Control
, an efficient episodic
-
control framework (Figure
1
) that couples three complementary modules within a single control loop. First, an LLM-based semantic encoder endows the agent with semantic features rich in commonsense knowledge that ground raw states in commonsense abstractions. In parallel, a World
-
Graph working memory organizes the environment’s entities, attributes, and their evolving relations, enabling on
-
the
-
fly relational reasoning. Over both representations, a LLM-based critical
-
state recognizer monitors task progress; when it detects a configuration that may significantly affect expected returns, it queries the episodic memory for rapid value estimates derived from analogous past experiences, while in less pivotal situations the agent continues to explore under the guidance of the World
-
Graph alone. By uniting deep semantic perception, structured relational context, and selective episodic recall, AEC overcomes the representational limits of prior episodic
-
memory RL and delivers stronger sample efficiency and generalization across changing tasks.

3.1
Episodic memory with LLM-based semantic encoder

Similar to prior episodic control reinforcement learning approaches, we maintain an episodic memory for each action in the action space. The episodic memory resembles a dictionary structure where the key is the state embedding
\phi(s)
and the value is the historical maximum return for each state-action pair:

\mathcal{M}={(\phi(s),\max\hat{Q}(s,a))},

(1)

where
\mathcal{M}
denotes the episodic memory,
\phi(s)
is the state embedding,
s
represents the state,
a
is the action, and
\hat{Q}(s,a)
is the maximum observed return for action
a
in state
s
. However, previous methods employed either random projections or variational autoencoders to generate state embeddings. State embeddings stored in episodic memory were solely based on the current state
s_{t}
, containing limited information and impairing the correlation between states.

To incorporate commonsense knowledge into state embeddings and better utilize information across different states in the state space, we propose an LLM-based semantic encoder that generates more informative state representations. Here, we formalize the LLM-based semantic encoder as
F
and denote the input to the encoder
F
as
p(s)
, which comprises three components:

p(s)=(\text{Environment description},\text{Raw states [s]},\text{Task instruction}).

(2)

The LLM-based semantic encoder processes this input to generate the encoded state embedding:

\phi(s)=F(p(S)).

(3)

For a comprehensive description of our prompting strategy, please refer to Appendix.

At each timestep
t
, the agent observes the current state
s_{t}
from the environment and stores the corresponding state embedding
\phi(s_{t})
, the taken action
a_{t}
, and the received reward
r_{t}
into an episodic data buffer. Only after the episode ends is
\hat{Q}
computed based on the collected data, and the episodic memory is then updated accordingly (Figure
2
). We employ exact matching through
k
-nearest neighbors (KNN) search to identify similar memories, as our encoding process naturally clusters semantically similar states. For novel state-action pairs, we add new entries to the memory table with their corresponding keys and values. For existing pairs, we update their values by taking the maximum between the stored value and the future return obtained in the current episode.

3.2
World-Graph working memory

Cognitive neuroscience suggests that human learning emerges from the coordination of multiple systems
rogers2004structure
;
tulving2002episodic
.
Integrating multiple memory systems can significantly improve an agent’s learning efficiency and generalization. Fortunato et al.
fortunato2019generalization
demonstrated that an RL agent combining a working memory with an external episodic memory outperforms agents with a single memory system, improving generalization and overall performance. Building on these insights, we propose a world-graph working memory module to model the agent’s spatial understanding of the external world. This module represents the environment as a spatiotemporal graph structure
G_{t}=(V_{t},E_{t},X_{t})
that evolves over time. Here, nodes
V_{t}
represent spatial locations explored by the agent, edges
E_{t}
encode reachability relationships between locations as
(u,r,v)
triplets (where
u,v\in V_{t}
are neighboring spatial nodes and
r
denotes movement direction or path attributes), and node features
X_{t}=\{x_{v}|v\in V_{t}\}
integrate environmental characteristics of each location (e.g., observed entity distributions) through feature vectors
x_{v}
. This spatiotemporal representation enables the agent to maintain both spatial topological structure and regional environmental features, providing structured support for path planning and decision-making in complex scenarios.

When the agent receives state
s_{t}
at timestep
t
, the system takes the previously constructed spatial graph
G_{t-1}
as contextual input and processes along with historical interactions and the current state through the LLM. The model begins by identifying the agent’s current spatial position and generating potential graph updates. The graph structure evolves by incorporating new spatial locations as nodes when the agent explores previously unvisited areas, while simultaneously updating the environmental features of known locations. The system also establishes new directional edges to represent discovered paths between positions or modifies existing connections to reflect changes in reachability. This dynamic updating process results in
G_{t}
, which captures the agent’s current spatiotemporal understanding of the explorable environment. Through this approach, the graph continuously adapts to reflect the agent’s expanding knowledge of the spatial environment while supporting effective navigation and decision-making.

3.3
Critical State Based Exploration and Exploitation

While the World-Graph working memory provides real-time spatial representation and the episodic memory stores valuable past experiences, a key challenge emerges: how to effectively balance immediate exploration with the exploitation of accumulated knowledge.

Our solution is a critical state recognition module (powered by an LLM) that dynamically governs this balance. The agent operates in an exploration mode during non-critical states, using the World-Graph working memory to guide its search through the environment without invoking long-term memory queries. Once the agent encounters a critical state, however, it switches to an exploitation mode that taps into episodic memory. A critical state in our context is a pivotal or landmark state that is likely to influence long-horizon outcomes – for example, discovering a key or reaching a locked door. In essence, the agent explores using only working memory until a critical juncture is reached, at which point it utilizes episodic memory to inform its next decisions. This mechanism ensures that historical experience is injected precisely at important decision points, improving long-term planning without distracting the agent during trivial steps. The identification of critical states can be represented by the following formula:

C_{t}=\Psi(\phi(s_{t})),

(4)

where
C_{t}
indicates whether the current state is critical (taking values in
\{\text{Non-critical},\text{Critical}\}
),
\phi(s_{t})
represents the embedding of the current state at time
t
, and
\Psi
denotes the critical state recognition module.

If
\Psi
labels the state as critical, the agent will trigger an episodic memory lookup. The agent queries the episodic memory with the key
\phi(s_{t})
. As described in
3.1
, we perform an exact
k
-
nearest
-
neighbour (KNN) lookup over stored keys; all entries whose encoded state lies within the
k
-
neighbour set are returned. Each match provides a value estimate
\hat{Q}(s_{t},a)
for a candidate action
a
. The agent simply selects the action whose retrieved Q
-
value is maximal:

a_{t}=\arg\max_{a}\left\{\hat{Q}(s_{t},a)\mid(\phi(s_{t}),a)\in\mathcal{M}\right\}.

(5)

Otherwise, the agent continues with standard exploration. The agent receives the current state
s_{t}
and the up
-
to
-
date World
-
Graph working memory
G_{t}
and chooses its next action by reasoning over these two sources. In practice, the policy
\pi_{\text{WG}}(a|s_{t},G_{t})
is implemented by a lightweight decision LLM that attends to entities and relations in
G_{t}
, thereby encouraging structured exploration without the overhead of long
-
term recall.

4
Experiments

4.1
Experimental Setup

Environment.

We evaluate the proposed
Agentic Episodic Control (AEC)
architecture on the BabyAI-Text benchmark
carta2023grounding
, a textual variant of the BabyAI environment
chevalier2018babyai
that reformulates grid-based instruction-following tasks into a purely text-based format. In this environment, the agent receives a natural language instruction along with a textual description of its local, egocentric observation—capturing a forward-facing view that spans up to seven tiles. This design simulates partial observability and tests the agent’s ability to reason over limited, language-grounded sensory input.

We adopt BabyAI-Text as our evaluation platform because it provides a controlled yet challenging testbed for studying grounded language understanding, semantic decision-making, and generalization in RL agents. The textual abstraction removes reliance on visual perception and allows us to isolate the benefits of language-driven episodic memory and structured reasoning.

Our experiments span four representative tasks, each targeting distinct agent capabilities.
GoToLocal
requires navigating to a specified object in a room with eight distractors, assessing spatial grounding and distractor resistance.
PickupLocal
builds on this by requiring the agent to retrieve the correct object, emphasizing precise object identification and action grounding.
UnlockLocal
involves multi-step planning: the agent must infer the need for a key, locate the correct one based on the door’s color, and perform a toggle action to unlock the door. Lastly,
FindObj
evaluates exploration and generalization, requiring the agent to explore across six rooms to locate a target object.

Following the protocol in
carta2023grounding
, we also test the agent’s ability to generalize to unseen instructions and object types.
We refer to the standard setting as
No change
, and the version containing novel, unseen target objects as
New object
.

Baselines.

We compare AEC against a range of baselines, covering both reinforcement learning and large language model–augmented approaches.

DRRN

he2016deep
serves as a standard RL baseline with competitive performance in interactive text environments
wang2022scienceworld
.

EM-DRRN
extends DRRN with an episodic memory module, following the design of EMDQN
lin2018episodic
, to improve sample efficiency.

NECSA-DRRN
incorporates NECSA
li2023neural
into DRRN, discretizing the state-action space into a grid-like structure for efficient memory-based value estimation.

GLAM

carta2023grounding
fine-tunes a large language model with PPO to act as a policy directly in textual environments, serving as a strong LLM-based baseline.

Main results

4.3
Further analysis

Memory Transfer.
We investigate whether AEC’s episodic memory enables cross-task generalization, similar to how humans reuse past experiences. To this end, we conducted a transfer experiment where the agent attempts to solve a task using episodic memories retrieved from a different task. Specifically, we evaluated performance on the GotoLocal and PickupLocal tasks under three memory settings: raw states from the same task; memory encoded from the same task; memory encoded from a different task. These two tasks were selected due to their inherent structural similarity, as both involve identifying and interacting with a target object in a distractor-filled single-room environment. As shown in Table
2
, reusing memory from a related task leads to notable improvements compared to raw states. For example, using PickupLocal-derived memory to solve GotoLocal yields a success rate of 0.75, substantially higher than the 0.49 obtained from raw states. Similarly, applying GotoLocal memory to PickupLocal improves performance from 0.30 to 0.39. Interestingly, we observe an asymmetry in transferability: experiences learned from the more complex PickupLocal task benefit GotoLocal more than the reverse. This suggests that solving more demanding tasks may yield richer, more generalizable memory representations, providing stronger priors when applied to simpler tasks. While preliminary, these results indicate the potential for cross-task memory reuse in AEC and open avenues for future work on compositional generalization.

4.4
Ablation studies

We conducted an ablation study across all four BabyAI-Text tasks, focusing on the effects of the LLM-based semantic encoder and the graph-structured working memory. As shown in Figure
5
, removing either module leads to a consistent performance drop across all tasks, highlighting their complementary roles. Without the LLM state encoder, AEC suffers significant degradation—especially on tasks requiring semantic understanding and generalization. For instance, in GoToLocal, success drops from 0.84 to 0.49. This underscores the importance of semantic abstraction in grounding observations and enabling object-centric reasoning. Similarly, removing the working memory module also reduces performance, though with varying severity.

Interestingly, the performance drop is more task-dependent: while working memory plays a smaller role in GoToLocal and PickupLocal, it is crucial for FindObj, suggesting its role in supporting effective exploration. Together, these results confirm that both the LLM encoder and the working memory are essential for AEC’s strong performance, enabling it to generalize semantically while reasoning over extended horizons.

5
Conclusion and Limitations

In conclusion, AEC effectively integrates LLM-based semantic understanding with structured memory to significantly improve sample efficiency and generalization in RL tasks. It outperforms strong baselines, especially in complex scenarios requiring long-term reasoning. However, this performance gain comes at the cost of increased computational overhead, making AEC less time-efficient than traditional RL methods. Future work may focus on optimizing inference speed and adapting the framework to domains with limited language grounding.

Integrated deep learning model for multi-label retinal disease diagnosis
Authors: Mahmood A. Mahmood, Khalaf Alsalem, Murtada K. Elbashir, Sameh Abd El-Ghany, A. A. Abd El-Aziz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The multi-label retinal disease classification is a difficult one since fundus images might comprise many co-occurring abnormalities, extreme in relation to the class, as well as significant lesion appearance variation. In order to deal with this issue, this paper suggests a combined deep learning architecture to diagnose retinal diseases automatically with the help of the MuReD dataset, which includes 2208 fundus images that were gathered at ARIA, STARE, and RFMiD and reformulated with 19 retinal disease labels, excluding the non-diagnostic category of OTHERS. The proposed model is based on a hybrid convolutional structure that integrates two complementary branches of feature extraction and implements a multi-stage preprocessing pipeline consisting of contrast enhancement, luminance correction, noise reduction, and retinal masking. Fusion and optimization of extracted feature representations are performed to perform multi-label prediction with binary supervision of label-wise predictions as sigmoid outputs. In order to assess the value of architecture and preprocessing design, ablation experiments were performed on a great scale by using single-backbone baselines, hybrid configurations, and preprocessing-specific configurations. According to the results, the highest F1-micro = 0.5484, PR-AUC micro = 0.5696, and ROC-AUC micro = 0.9349 were observed in the best variant of the hybrid, and the best PR-AUC micro = 0.5821 and ROC-AUC micro = 0.9369 were observed in another hybrid variant. Other tests on bootstrap confidence interval, calibration evaluation, Monte Carlo dropout, and repeated multi-seed experiments also indicated the strength of the proposed model. These results reveal that feature fusion with built-in deep learning is an effective solution to multi-label retinal disease diagnosis and has a potential real-world application to computer-aided screening and triage in ophthalmology.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-54982-3.

Introduction

Retinal diseases are considered to cause more visual impairment and blindness in the world, with some of the most clinically significant diseases being diabetic retinopathy (DR), age-related macular degeneration (AMD), and glaucoma. The fact that most of the retinal abnormalities develop over time, with some of them being asymptomatic at the initial stages, necessitates prompt identification to avert permanent visual impairment and enhance the outcome of the treatment. Ophthalmologists usually perform the process of retinal fundus image assessment manually in routine practice, which is expensive in time, observer-dependent, and, in large screening programs, challenging to scale. The rising cases of diabetes, aging, and other lifestyle-related risk factors have thus generated an imminent demand for effective automated systems that can aid in early diagnosis of retinal disease1.

The analysis of medical images by deep learning has become one of the most potent technologies due to its capability to learn hierarchical feature representations of images in an automatic fashion. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have demonstrated good results in the field of ophthalmology, as the system is capable of identifying retinal pathologies by looking at fundus photos and tends to enhance the efficiency of the diagnosis, as well as to decrease the reliance on features that are handcrafted. EfficientNetB0 is an appealing CNN architecture in modern CNNs since it provides a good balance between accuracy and computational efficiency, and DenseNet169 is known to have dense connectivity, good gradient flow, and effective reuse of features. Nevertheless, it is still hard to obtain high retinal disease classification performance due to the fact that fundus images usually have a significant amount of variation in illumination, contrast, lesion size, disease degree, and quality of acquisition2,3.

Even though the previous literature has demonstrated promising outcomes in the case of automated retinal disease detection, one of the gaps that still exists is the possibility of having vigorous multi-label retinal disease classification schemes. A significant amount of the literature has concentrated on single-disease or single-label tasks, in which a single dominant diagnosis is given to each image. These formulations are not entirely based on clinical reality, as there can be a number of abnormalities present in a single retinal image. Moreover, most previous studies use a single CNN backbone, which can restrict the feature variety and can decrease robustness in cases of image differences across datasets or clinical conditions. Sensitivity of class imbalance, poor quality images, and inadequate feature fusion strategies have been reported in other studies and can limit generalization and diagnostic reliability of ophthalmic applications in the real world4,5.

The main problem of multi-label retinal disease analysis is the necessity to learn discriminative representations that can be used in the conditions of clinics. These conditions are long-tailed distributions of classes, label co-occurrence, subtle appearance of lesions, background artifacts, and non-homogeneous imaging appearance. Single-stream architecture cannot be used to simultaneously get all the global contextual information and fine-grained pathology details. In addition, low-light, randomized contrast, non-retinal background locations may decrease the visibility of diagnostically important structures and have a negative impact on downstream classification. Thus, it is apparent that automated systems involving complementary feature extractors and specific preprocessing are required with a need to be computationally efficient to be applicable in optical practice in ophthalmology6,7.

In this regard, the purpose of the current research is to create and test a powerful deep learning system of multilabel classification of retinal diseases based on the MuReD data. The central topic of the paper is the research question: Do you think two complementary CNN backbones, EfficientNetB0 and DenseNet169, along with retina-centered preprocessing, can enhance the detection of various comorbid retinal conditions? It is anticipated that EfficientNetB0 will allow efficient extraction of global and semantic features, whereas DenseNet169 will bring about dense and fine-grained feature propagation. Their combination is intended to yield a richer feature representation than either backbone on its own and thus overcomes an inherent weakness of prior single-model methods.

In order to reinforce discriminative learning, this model uses a preprocessing pipeline that is grounded on contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE), gamma correction, and retinal masking. The goal of such operations is to improve diagnostically relevant retinal structures, minimize illumination bias, and minimize interference by the background prior to feature extraction. Besides, the model has been developed in a computationally feasible environment with pretrained ImageNet weights, controlled input resolution, and lightweight fine-tuning, which is more appropriate in translation into clinical workflows than too complicated or computationally intensive versions.

Literature review

Medical imaging received a breakthrough advancement from deep learning technology because it enabled automatic diagnostic tools with exceptional precision. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have gained immense popularity for disease classification purposes in radiology as well as pathology and ophthalmology fields. These models acquire hierarchical features from medical images, which makes handcrafted features and manual interpretation unnecessary. Research has shown that diagnostic tasks like DR and glaucoma perform equally as well or better using CNN-based models than human experts8.

A significant number of scientific investigations have been dedicated to implementing deep learning methods for diagnosing retinal diseases. The deep learning system created by Gulshan et al.9 used InceptionV3 to detect DR while maintaining exceptional detection abilities. Retinal disease classification with promising results was achieved using a CNN-based architecture, according to Pratt et al.10. A single CNN architecture usage in these models creates generalization limitations for diverse datasets. The proposed solution for overcoming these limitations involves creating hybrid models that combine various deep learning architectures.

In addition to CNN-based retinal disease classifiers, several studies have also specifically addressed glaucoma detection with fundus and OCT modalities. An artificial intelligence-based intelligent system of early glaucoma detection using OCT images showed that sensitive features in early stages of the disease could be enhanced by the combination of domain-relevant features with automatic decision models11. Similarly, a three-step system of automatic classification of glaucoma based on retinal fundus images displayed high attention to sequencing preprocessing, feature extraction, and classification can offset quality differences in clinical images12. Prior analytic research on machine learning algorithms and introductory surveys of AI trends in ophthalmology have also highlighted the fact that classical feature-based pipelines are competitive in a limited (or imbalanced) dataset, particularly where descriptors like histogram of oriented gradients (HOG) are used with ANN classifiers to detect glaucoma and comparative analyses of textural and intensity features13–16. All of this literature points to the fact that both the classic ML model with engineered features and the newer deep CNNs can be applied to glaucoma, and they are what drive us to adopt a multi-source, multi-label retinal dataset and explore hybrid deep architectures that can be applied across heterogeneous retinal pathologies.

The combination of accuracy improvement and lower computational need has made EfficientNet develop into an impactful deep learning architecture. EfficientNet emerged with Tan and Le17 by achieving superior results compared to standard CNN models while needing fewer parameters. The medical imaging field employs EfficientNet as a system for lung disease classification, skin lesion detection, and ophthalmic disease recognition. Due to its lightweight framework, it suits deployment in running clinical applications in real-time.

The medical image classification process benefits from DenseNet, which stands among the most popular deep learning models. The dense connectivity of DenseNet has been designed to pass input information to every preceding layer. The efficient flow of gradients combined with feature reuse leads to better learning efficiency. DenseNet obtains superior results in fundus image classification operations while beating conventional CNN approaches, according to research findings18.

Researchers have examined deep learning hybrids, which unite different CNN structures to improve their operational effectiveness. The combination of ResNet and InceptionNet models in DR classification produced improved accuracy, according to Mikram et al.19. Multi-class retinal disease classification benefited from the VGG16-HCCNN, according to Latha and Priya.20. The research indicates how hybrid model combinations enable better diagnostic results.

Further analysis of literature reveals that the primary drawback of the previous retinal image analysis research is not the lack of deep learning, but the limited scope in which most of the models were tested. Various high-performing systems were designed to address single disease screening tasks, particularly diabetic retinopathy or glaucoma, whereby the diagnostic task is far more limited and does not represent the multi-pathology context of regular ophthalmic practice. Although CNN-based approaches were used with good results, most of them were built on one backbone network, which can either learn global semantic structure or fine local texture, but not both equally. Moreover, some of the past works indicated promising accuracy but did not comprehensively cover such clinically significant problems as label co-occurrence, long-tailed class imbalance, variable illumination, and background artifacts in fundus images. The weaknesses were reduced in hybrid and preprocessing-based techniques, but the literature reveals few attempts at integrating complementary feature extractors, retina-centered enhancement, and multi-label learning into a single model. This means that not only does the remaining research problem include the need to enhance classification accuracy but also to create models that are resistant to heterogeneous retina appearances, can identify multiple diseases simultaneously, and are feasible in real clinical screening or decision-support applications.

More recent research has further broadened the use of artificial intelligence in ophthalmic image and multi-label medical image classifications. Kaya suggested a retina image processing approach to localize the optic disc using the cuckoo search algorithm and structural similarity index and demonstrated that an optimized localized approach could enhance the identification of diagnostically relevant retinal structures prior to subsequent analysis21. Most recently, in ophthalmic deep learning research, Jafarbeglou et al. in22, trained a deep learning model to diagnose inherited retinal diseases, a trend that points to the increasing use of data-driven systems in assisting complex retinal diagnoses in targets other than diabetic retinopathy screening. Furthermore, Yang et al. in23 designed CG-TRAN, a new multi-label retinal disease classification model with partially known pathologies, which is particularly applicable due to the gap of pathology annotation and the reality of the inability to perform multi-label retinal prediction with perfect supervision. In addition to ophthalmology-specific models, recent architecture designers, including CONV-FAN-POX, have demonstrated that time–frequency-inspired deep learning models can also enhance the ability of representation learning in medical image analysis, implying that more elaborate feature modeling schemes can also be useful to the retinal disease classification frameworks24.

Collectively, these new studies affirm that the present state of research is shifting to more powerful, label-conscious, and high-quality diagnostic systems; nonetheless, there remain gaps of clinically applicable multi-label retinal classification models incorporating complementary CNN backbones with robust generalization capabilities.

The process of improving retinal fundus images through preprocessing becomes essential before they can be classified with deep learning approaches. The application of contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE) and Gaussian filtering together with retinal vessel segmentation improves both image contrast while removing noise. Multiple investigations show that preceding processing methods enhance deep learning system performance by improving the detectability of visible pathological components25.

Materials and methods

The proposed model uses several critical elements together with defined techniques for implementation. The model undergoes multiple enhancement and preprocessing techniques, which combine Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) and Gaussian Filtering to improve visual consistency and quality of the images. The effective extraction of visual patterns depends on two proven deep learning models, namely EfficientNetB0 and DenseNet169. Specialized fusion layers join features for improving classification output. A specialized retina segmentation technique functionally separates important region information that is essential for the target operational task. This section demonstrates the methods and processes of the included techniques that operate as part of the methodology.

Contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization

The image preprocessing method known as Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) serves as an advanced technology to enhance local contrast as well as improve fine details visibility, particularly in medical images. The traditional histogram equalization redistributes pixel intensities throughout the image for contrast improvement, yet it causes noise increase, particularly in uniform image areas. Local contrast enhancement through CLAHE occurs when histogram equalization operates on adaptive image tiles instead of whole-scale image application26. CLAHE adds a contrast-limiting operation to the algorithm, which uses predefined threshold values during histogram redistribution through clipping before enhancing the image quality27. The application of CLAHE for medical imaging enables the systematic enhancement of delicate features such as retinal lesions along with vascular abnormalities28. The successful detection of retinal diseases by deep learning models heavily relies on adequate image contrast enhancement. The application of CLAHE in preprocessing enhances structural visibility of vital examination regions, including blood vessels, the optic disk, and the macula, since they represent vital diagnostic information. The research demonstrates that CLAHE outperforms global contrast approaches for improving image visibility, particularly when illuminations are not even29. The method not only maintains edges but also enhances contrast, which makes it appropriate for segmentation and detection operations. CLAHE performs its contrast enhancement exclusively on the luminance channel after color transformations to LAB or HSV color spaces, which ensures both color preservation and contrast enhancement. To improve perceptual brightness, our proposed model first applies CLAHE to the L-channel of LAB color space and then performs gamma correction. Lalitha Surya Kumari30 shows that blending these two methods produces enhanced performance outcomes in deep learning systems. The CLAHE preprocessing step enhances data input quality by promoting better generalization capabilities of neural networks that classify multi-label retinal diseases.

Gaussian filtering

The image preprocessing method Gaussian filtering reduces both noise and detail in images through kernel convolution with Gaussian distributions to perform image smoothing. The filter functions as a linear low-pass mechanism that assigns greater weights to center pixels that decrease gradually toward surrounding pixels according to Gaussian distribution terminology31. The spatial weighting method gently blurs images without distorting their main structures and edges as long as users choose suitable kernel size settings. The Gaussian filter proves valuable within medical image analysis because it weakens high-frequency noise patterns, which might interrupt feature detection or classification procedures32. The model’s diagnostic function provides disease classification together with quantitative analysis of severity through image segmentation and quantitative lesion evaluation, The model provides diagnostic information and severity values, which assist doctors in clinical decision-making and remote disease assessment33. The preparation of images for edge detection segmentation and deep learning-based classification strongly depends on Gaussian filtering since noise suppression maintains model stability. The technique assists in establishing uniform image intensity patterns that lead to improved contrast stability between different samples. During image preprocessing that combines CLAHE and Gaussian filtering, the technique enhances detection of significant structures through noise suppression34. Simplified learning becomes possible for deep neural networks because of this method, which results in enhanced performance. Using Gaussian filtering after CLAHE enhances the processed image and achieves smoothness, which helps minimize artifacts and creates uniform input quality. The process of balancing noise reduction with detail preservation requires specific adjustments of kernel size and sigma values. The application of this step is critical for retinal images since they deal with inconsistent lighting and imaging conditions. The application of Gaussian filtering forms a stable platform that enables higher-quality deep learning features while preserving image structure yet suppressing unwanted noise.

Implementation details for retina-focused preprocessing and augmentation

All the preprocessing components had been chosen to overcome a different image-quality limitation in MuReD. Local contrast and enhancing the appearance of fine structures in the retina, like lesion boundaries, vessel images, and optic disc edges, were improved using CLAHE. Gamma correction was used to reestablish the overall luminance and bring back to the diagnostic level of interest the darker areas of the retina. Morphologically refined adaptive thresholding was used to produce retina-oriented masking that helps to reduce non-retinal background and camera border artifacts. A regularization strategy of data augmentation was then employed to enhance geometric and photometric variability. The reason behind these steps was to enhance visual consistency as well as feature extractability prior to learning hybrid features.

EfficientNetB0

The foundational model of the EfficientNet family serves as an efficient but highly effective convolutional neural network architecture. The network uses a new methodology for compound scaling to achieve uniform alterations of network depth and width alongside resolution with predefined coefficients that maximize accuracy alongside operational efficiency. The compound scaling method of EfficientNetB0 starts with a base network derived through neural architecture search followed by transformation to achieve a compromise between system performance quality and computational requirements. The model does better than conventional architecture such as ResNet and VGG when measuring accuracy-per-parameter efficiency and speed, which makes it appropriate for resource-limited systems17. Shankar et al.35, medical image analysis benefits from the use of EfficientNetB0 because it merges deep spatial feature extraction with minimal computational requirements. The model implements inverted bottleneck MBConv blocks together with swish activation to both enhance representation strength and speed up convergence. The proposed approach adopts EfficientNetB0 as a frozen feature extractor because its pre-training on ImageNet allows it to extract high-level abstract features from retinal images. The network applies global average pooling to features from which it extracts semantic information after dimensionality reduction. EfficientNetB0 accelerates model convergence while improving both generalization performance and reducing overfitting when it operates with data augmentation and contrast enhancement methods. The transfer learning model demonstrates strong robustness, which enables it to process small datasets effectively, including the MuReD dataset, without extensive training. Its economical design serves as an outstanding main component for hybrid systems that merge various feature streams together. The EfficientNetB0 backbone model delivers an effective base for performing high-performing analysis of retinal diseases.

DenseNet169

The deep convolutional neural network DenseNet169 functions among Densely Connected Convolutional Networks (DenseNets), which the researchers Huang et al.36 originally proposed. In DenseNet architecture there exists dense connectivity that delivers previous layer feature maps to subsequent layers, which both encourages feature sharing and propagation throughout the network. Improved gradient flow occurs during training and simultaneously creates compact features that lead to lower parameter usage than classical CNNs with matching accuracy levels. DenseNet169 consists of connected dense blocks and transition layers, which use convolution and pooling for down sampling operations. The combination of depth and efficiency in this network structure makes it optimal for medical image analysis that requires detailed classification alongside preserving image details, such as detecting retinal diseases36,37. DenseNet169 operates as a secondary backbone to obtain sophisticated hierarchical features that enhance the features extracted by EfficientNetB0 in the proposed model. Two information processing streams enable the model to create multiple classification representations that result in improved performance. DenseNet169 receives ImageNet-based pretraining and enables weight freezing for transfer learning on MuReD data to prevent inappropriate overfitting. The model applies global average pooling to its final convolutional results before connecting them to EfficientNetB0 output data. The combination adds value during discriminative learning as it merges spatial information from dense neural networks with semantic knowledge from high-level features. DenseNet169 demonstrates exceptional value in medical imaging tasks due to its ability to detect textures and preserve structures, which are vital for identifying medical lesions and subtle imaging patterns. The model architecture allows deep information processing and prevents gradient disappearance problems, which enables efficient and scalable operation. Hybrid classical model robustness stems heavily from the effective relationship that exists between DenseNet169 and EfficientNetB0.

The pairing of EfficientNetB0 and DenseNet169 as the pair to be used in the proposed model was not accidental. The low-weight, high-accuracy-per-parameter backbone was EfficientNetB0 to obtain global, high-level semantic features on fundus images, and the second stream was DenseNet169 to focus on dense feature reuse and fine-grained textual patterns, which are diagnostically significant in retinal diseases. We used a preliminary experiment on the MuReD dataset to test a few viable dual-backbone models against a single-backbone baseline. We discovered that EfficientNetB0-DenseNet169 was the most suitable trade-off between feature diversity and overfitting control on this relatively small and multi-label dataset: EfficientNetB0 sacrificed less computational cost and stabilized the training. Simultaneously, the dense connectivity of DenseNet169 made minority/rare labels more sensitive.

Fusion layer

Deep learning architecture depends on its fusion layer to unite multiple neural network backbones for improving model discrimination power. A fusion layer joins the extracted features by EfficientNetB0 and DenseNet169 into a combined output. Both spatial and semantic features integrate through output pooling from global average calculation of the networks via concatenation operations. The model enhances its ability to understand input images because it combines distinctive features that provide an improved comprehensive view, which works best for multi-label retinal disease classification. Zhou et al.38, feature fusion techniques are commonly used in medical image analysis because they enhance lesion detection and disease classification accuracy. After feature extraction phase completion, the fusion layer integrates both extracted features and sends them to fully connected layers for decision-making at the highest level. The dual feature combination strategy enables the model to benefit both from detailed patterns extracted by DenseNet169 and general abstracted features obtained through EfficientNetB0. Model fusion enables mutual compensation between two separate models by allowing them to share information that reinforces their different strengths and fills in each other’s weaknesses. Valanarasu et al.39, multi-source learning benefits from this technique for reducing overfitting while increasing generalization when employing transfer learning. The fusion technique demonstrated success during this work, which demonstrates its power at enhancing medical deep learning platforms for future applications in hybrid systems.

Retina segmentation technique

The implementation of retinal segmentation before image processing stages served to improve the classification quality for multiple eye disease diagnoses by eliminating non-relevant background information. The method starts with grayscale conversion and adaptive thresholding, where the Gaussian method separates the retina foreground from background elements. The adaptive Gaussian thresholding method defines unique threshold values for image sections to handle the lighting irregularities that commonly occur in medical images31. The threshold output continues the refinement process by using morphological closing together with an elliptical kernel to link separate regions and complete small holes found inside the retinal area. The technique identifies the biggest contour before using it to create a binary mask for applying to the original image to separate the retina region. The retinal tissue retains primary focus during processing while all additional objects, such as fundus camera edges together with patient identifiers, become excluded. The model achieves better accuracy by focusing on clinically important sections after the retina receives segmentation treatment. Study results demonstrate that preprocessing methods achieve effective outcomes in diabetic retinopathy and age-related macular degeneration research40. Before resizing the target input dimensions, the segmented images undergo normalization procedures to ensure batch training consistency across the process. The segmentation pipeline operates with light computational requirements to allow processing of large-scale datasets. The model benefits from both these methods when it comes to maintaining generalizability and downstream performance robustness.

Proposed model

The proposed hybrid deep learning system in the classification of the multi-labeled retinal disease works on this pipeline as it is described in Fig. 1. MuReD fundus images are 384 × 384x3, resized, and processed with CLAHE, gamma, and retina masking to enhance the visibility of diagnostic features and reduce background noise. Two pretrained CNN backbones, EfficientNetB0 and DenseNet169, are then run in parallel on the enhanced image so that global and fine-grained retinal features are obtained jointly.Fig. 1Flow diagram of the proposed hybrid multi-label retinal disease classification model.

Flow diagram of the proposed hybrid multi-label retinal disease classification model.

Figure 1 is supposed to be viewed as a sequential-to-parallel processing pipeline. First, every input fundus image undergoes preprocessing to resize the image and optimize local contrast, equilumination, and non-retinal background to enhance the quality of the image. Second, one and the same preprocessed image is submitted to EfficientNetB0 and DenseNet169 concurrently to enable both global structural and fine lesion-level details to be learned. Third, the final convolutional representation of each branch is transformed into a small feature representation via global average pooling to give a 1280-dimensional embedding of EfficientNetB0 and a 1664-dimensional embedding of DenseNet169. Fourth, these vectors are combined into a fused descriptor of dimensions 2944 and projected by a dense layer of 1024 units with regular dropout. Lastly, the sigmoid output layer produces 19 independent probabilities that represent the retinal disease labels so that the model can make multiple diagnoses on a single image.

After each CNN branch runs a Global Average Pooling 2D layer, it produces two separate feature vectors containing preserved important features. The model combines these vectors into one input for its fully connected dense layer, which utilizes 1024 ReLU neurons to learn sophisticated patterns associated with the diverse features. An overfitting prevention measure comes from the implementation of a 0.5 rate dropout layer. The last step of the model features a sigmoid-activated layer for multi-label classification that allows simultaneous classification of multiple classes. The combined design capitalizes on EfficientNetB0’s strengths together with DenseNet169’s capabilities, which enhances the complete performance quality of the model.

Individual images are encoded with a 19-dimensional target vector, and the final dense layer was a sigmoid layer, meaning that the network could make multiple retinal condition predictions on the same fundus image. Binary cross-entropy (BCE) was used as training loss due to its ability to break down the loss per label and is the standard loss used in multi-label training of medical images; it is also the match to the architecture of Table 1. Because the labels are imbalanced, we calculated the frequency of labels and weighed the BCE loss in such a way that label errors on the labels in the minority are over-represented in the gradient, whereas errors on the labels in the majority are under-represented in the gradient. Sigmoid + BCE, label and sample-level metric reporting, and class-balanced weighting, together, meant that multi-label classification was addressed properly and class imbalance did not cause the model to overfit itself and only learn the majority retinal conditions.

Proposed model architecture: an overview

The developed model is a two-backbone hybrid convolutional neural network that was designed to classify retinal diseases with more than one label with unlabeled fundus images. The design of it is inspired by the fact that the retinal abnormalities can manifest with various spatial scales and visual features, such as global abnormalities of anatomy and minor local lesions. In order to approximate these heterogeneous patterns in a more efficient way, the model uses two separate backbones of pretrained convolutional networks, i.e., EfficientNetB0 and DenseNet169, to process each preprocessed retinal image in parallel. This parallel design is intended to increase the scale-efficient global representation capability of EfficientNetB0 with the densely connected fine-detail feature propagation of DenseNet169, resulting in a richer and more discriminative representation than either of its two backbones.

The resultant preprocessed image is subsequently provided to the two branches of CNN. A high-level convolutional representation is obtained in each branch and is transformed into a compact feature vector with 2D global average pooling. A feature-level concatenation is then done to these two feature vectors, which are then fed into a fully connected classification head. The last layer employs the sigmoid activation to individually predict all labels of retinal disease and thus will be able to truly classify multiple labels on the same fundus image.

The proposed architecture can be defined mathematically as the dual-backbone multi-label classification model where a processed retinal image is sent through two parallel convolutional feature extractors, the extracted features are condensed into compact embeddings by global average pooling, and finally fused by concatenation, followed by the prediction of the disease probabilities by a dense classification head. This expression explains why the model holds together complementary global and fine-grained retinal representations and still maintains the independence needed to make multi-label predictions.

Dual-backbone feature extraction

The first branch of the feature extractors is EfficientNetB0. EfficientNetB0 creates a final convolutional feature map of size (12 × 12 × 1280) on an input image of size (384 × 384 × 3). This is then reduced to a 1280-dimensional feature vector by a global average pooling 2D operation. This vector is a summary of the highest semantic activations of the whole retinal image and can be described as a compact global representation of disease appearance patterns. Since EfficientNetB0 utilizes the concept of scaling of compounds, it can be especially useful in the context of learning efficient multi-scale representations while controlling the complexity of the model.

The second one is the DenseNet169 with an input size of the same size, DenseNet169 can generate an end feature map of about 12 × 12 × 1664. This is a 1664-dimensional feature vector after Global Average Pooling 2D. DenseNet169 is especially recommended in the analysis of the retina due to its dense connection whereby feature usage is reused across layers where fine-grained texture data and low-level lesion patterns can be maintained throughout the more profound representations. Such behavior can be beneficial in the classification of retinal diseases to conserve small markers, including small exudates, alterations in vessels, local opacities, and subtle pathology textures that can be easily compromised by single-stream architectures.

Fusion mechanism of feature hybridization

The key contribution of the proposed model lies in the combination of the two backbone outputs in the feature level. Following the global pooling, the EfficientNetB0 branch gives a 1280-dimensional vector, and the DenseNet169 branch offers a 1664-dimensional vector. The result of concatenating these two vectors is a fused feature representation of the dimension (1280 + 1664 = 2944). In this way, the model does explicit fusion at the feature level instead of probability averaging, late decision fusion, and weighted voting. The design retains all the discriminative content captured by each branch and is free to learn interactions between both global and local retinal descriptors by the downstream classifier.

After the concatenation, the resulting 2944-dimensional vector is then sent to a fully connected dense layer, which has 1024 units and ReLU activation. It is a projection and the interaction-learning layer, which converts the raw concatenated representation to a task-specific discriminative embedding. Overfitting is then minimized by a dropout layer with a rate of 0.5 that randomly deactivates half of the hidden units on training. The reason behind this is that the fused representation is high-dimensional, and the data has an imbalance in classes with the co-occurrence of diseases. Following dropout, the last classification layer has a 19-output node that corresponds to each label of each disease on the retina. The model can label an individual image with several labels as a single output node is activated, and this feature is crucial to the dataset of the MuReD.

Multi-label prediction and classification head

The dataset consists of a series of retinal images, each of which is represented as a 19-dimensional binary target variable, with each of the variables denoting the absence or presence of a particular retinal disease. This results in a single-label, 19 independent probabilities in each of the final sigmoid layers. The multi-label classification can be well formulated using this formulation as retinal diseases can be present in the same image and cannot be discussed as mutually exclusive. The binary cross-entropy is used to train the classifier and uses the loss calculated separately at each output node and sums the result between labels.

The weighted binary cross-entropy loss is employed to reduce the influence of label imbalance used in the training process. The percentage weights are calculated based on the training distribution such that the minority classes get more weight and the majority classes gain less weight. This approach raises the gradient contribution of low-represented diseases and yields a lower likelihood of the model to predict towards common diseases. By so doing, the loss function supplements the hybrid architecture by making the fused features not only optimized to the typical retinal disorders but also to rare pathological patterns that would have been otherwise ignored.

Experimental setup

In this paper, model generalization was evaluated by an independent data split that is offered in the MuReD dataset as opposed to k-fold cross-validation. The training subset was used to train the model, and a held-out validation/test subset was tested to ensure that it was applied to other images that were not used to directly fit the parameters. To mitigate the risk of seed-specific outcomes even more, the key hybrid combination was replicated with several random seeds, and the confidence intervals of the key measures of evaluation were calculated. Thus, the results reported demonstrate generalization in an independent testing condition with repeated runs, though at this point they are not cross-validated or externally multi-center validated. This must be kept in mind in reading robustness, and further research and analysis in the future should further evaluate stratified cross-validation and external clinical cohorts across various imaging settings.

All experiments were implemented using TensorFlow and Keras. Training and inference were performed on the Kaggle platform with NVIDIA Tesla P100 GPU acceleration. Local development and preprocessing preparation were carried out on a 64-bit × 64-based system with an AMD Ryzen 7 7730U CPU (2.00 GHz) and 16 GB RAM. The GPU-enabled environment supported efficient execution of the proposed hybrid model, including repeated multi-seed training, calibration analysis, and uncertainty estimation.

Code availability

The code that has been developed to implement the proposed multi-label retinal disease classification framework is available on (https://github.com/KOkab2020/Retinal-Disease-Diagnosis). It contains all the experimental pipelines used in this study, from loading the datasets to matching image paths to retina-focused preprocessing via CLAHE, gamma correction, Gaussian filtering, and retinal masking to data augmentation; building the hybrid EfficientNetB0–DenseNet169 model; training with weighted binary cross-entropy; optimizing the threshold; analysis of the calibration; uncertainty estimation with Monte Carlo dropout; bootstrap confidence interval calculation; and repeated multi-seed evaluation. It was implemented in Python, using TensorFlow/Keras, OpenCV, Augmentations, NumPy, Pandas, scikit-learn, and Matplotlib. In addition to label imbalance, MuReD also indicates clinically realistic co-occurrence of labels, whereby multiple pathological conditions can be present in the same image. This property makes the model more difficult to classify, as the model is required to identify multiple co-occurring abnormalities rather than merely one dominating one. Thus, the task described in the proposed model gives the following formulation: multi-label prediction where the outputs are given by the sigmoid, and the supervision is label-wise and binary.

MuReD is particularly suitable for evaluating the multi-label retinal disease classification models, as its structure reflects several issues that are also critical in the real ophthalmic diagnosis. To begin with, it consists of 19 clinically meaningful retinal disease category categories as opposed to a simplified binary or single-label setting, which enables the proposed model to be evaluated in a more realistic diagnostic context. Second, the dataset contains true label co-occurrence, and this means that the same fundus image can have multiple abnormalities on the same image; this is very similar to the normal clinical interpretation in which ophthalmologists often see overlapping pathological features as opposed to a single disease. Third, the distribution of classes is very uneven, and both common and unusual disorders are represented in the data, which makes MuReD a powerful tool for testing whether a deep learning system can remain sensitive to minority pathologies without collapsing to the majority classes. Finally, since MuReD is reconstituted with a variety of sources of public retinal imaging, this causes diversity in the appearance of the image, lesion presentation, and acquisition conditions, which is helpful in the construction of robust deep learning models that can more readily learn to induce clinically relevant retinal patterns rather than overfit to a specific visual distribution. It is due to these reasons that the foundation of the development and evaluation of robust ophthalmic multi-label classification systems is difficult and clinically relevant with the help of MuReD.

Ablation study

The ablation study compares five requested variants to isolate the effect of the backbone choice, feature fusion, retina segmentation, and class-weighting strategy; efficientNetB0 only, denseNet169 only, hybrid, hybrid no seg which keeps the hybrid fusion but removes retina segmentation to quantify the impact of segmentation-guided preprocessing, and hybrid no weights, which keeps the hybrid architecture but disables class weighting, training with plain binary cross-entropy (BCE) to assess the effect of imbalance handling. The preprocessing pipeline should therefore be interpreted as a staged enhancement strategy in which contrast enhancement improves local visibility, luminance correction reduces illumination bias, and retinal masking minimizes background-induced attention leakage.

Based on the ranking of PR-AUC micro, there is a slight difference in the pattern. Raw gave the highest PR-AUC of 0.5261, then clahe + gamma (0.5166), clahe (0.5152), and finally clahe + gamma + seg (0.5131). This implies that the raw images retained loose ranking data that can be utilized in precision-recall behavior, despite the fact that they were not the best thresholded classification results. That is, the raw setting can still more or less effectively segregate positive and negative cases in the probability space, but on thresholding it is not as effective as the improved variants. The fact that the lower PR-AUC of the full processing (0.4635) indicates that the full pipeline could be filtering or downplaying some helpful discriminating details.

All settings have large values of ROC-AUC micro-values, in the range of 0.9110–0.9268, indicating that all preprocessing variants have good overall separability of positive and negative labels. The top ROC-AUC was 0.9268 (CLAHE), which was closely followed by 0.9258 (CLAHE + gamma) and 0.9251 (raw). This implies that CLAHE stands on its own as the most effective ranking discrimination with only a substantial contribution to ROC behavior by gamma correction or segmentation. Yet, even in weaker F1 scenarios, ROC-AUC stays the same; that is why the idea of prioritizing quality alone does not suffice to ensure the enhanced prediction level without thresholding with the help of multi-labels.

The best ECE after was obtained with full preprocessing (0.0132); that is, the probabilities predicted were the most calibrated ones, and 0.0141 and 0.0146 were also reasonable. The same is reflected in the Brier score, where raw (0.0397) has the lowest Brier score and thus the best overall probabilistic accuracy but the worst Brier score even though it provided its best ECE. This difference suggests that not every calibration and discrimination improve simultaneously: a setting can give more calibrated confidence estimates and, at the same time, decrease the classification accuracy.

More insight is given through the uncertainty measures. There was the lowest MC variance mean in clahe + gamma + gaussian (0.002348) and raw (0.002398), indicating that the predictions are slightly more stable in stochastic inference. Predictive entropy mean was minimal with full_preprocessing (0.173260) being more confident with its output compared to the raw (0.18469) and clahe + gamma + seg (0.183728), which were more uncertain. Nonetheless, lower entropy does not imply a better prediction, full preprocessing is the weaker in F1 even though it has the lowest entropy and could be indicating overconfident yet less accurate predictions.

Generally, Table 4 indicates that the best balance in final classification performance is achieved with CLAHE-based enhancement with gamma correction and segmentation because the performance of CLAHE + gamma + seg is the highest in terms of F1-micro. Alone, as well as when combined with gamma correction, CLAHE is also a strong and consistent method when measured by the various discrimination metrics. Conversely, the complete preprocessing pipeline seems to be excessively aggressive on this dataset, enhancing the calibration of confidence but lessening the classification quality. Consequently, the conclusion of the ablation study can be that preprocessing needs to be selective and not maximal and that contrast enhancement together with luminance correction and target segmentation are more useful than all preprocessing steps used simultaneously.

In terms of thresholded multi-label classification, hybrid no segment is the strongest due to the fact that it gives the highest F1-micro and has a higher confidence interval than the weaker ones. This implies that the fused backbone retained more information, which could be helpful in diagnosis in this dataset, than the version that was segmented and that diagnostically useful information was removed by retinal masking. The same can be stated about the DenseNet that yielded more performance in F1-micro compared to the full hybrid model despite having fewer parameters. It means that DenseNet169 is a powerful standalone feature extractor of the MuReD, and potentially, it is more likely to capture retinal texture and lesion continuity in this context than EfficientNetB0.

Another significant trend is indicated in the PR-AUC micro results. Hybrid without weights (0.5821) have the highest PR-AUC, followed by hybrid without segment (0.5696) and DenseNet (0.5333). This implies that the unweighted hybrid model has a very good rank of probability space of the unweighted positive samples, though the thresholded F1-micro is slightly smaller compared to the no-segmentation version. Practically, this implies that class weighting affected the end decision tradeoff between precision and recall, yet the elimination of weighting enhanced probability ranking. These variants are once again followed by the full hybrid model, whereas EfficientNet is once again way behind, which strengthens the notion that it is the weakest architecture in this dataset.

The higher variants of the ROC-AUC micro values include EfficientNet, with the lowest values of 0.9369 and 0.9231 among the more effective models. The highest ROC-AUC is that of the hybrid without weights (0.9369); meanwhile, the segment is the hybrid without weights (0.9349) and DenseNet (0.9292). These findings demonstrate that the majority of variants can separate positive and negative labels fairly well, although the separation is not always reflected in the maximum F1-micro following thresholding. This is why the full hybrid model has a high ROC-AUC but lower F1-micro: it can sort classes well; however, its final binary choices are not as equally distributed as the more successful ones.

The interpretation is further refined through the calibration measures. EfficientNet scores the lowest ECE after (0.0027), and then the hybrid without segment (0.0097) and the full hybrid and DenseNet have slightly higher values of ECE. This could indicate at a first look that EfficientNet is the most calibrated, but this should not be perceived to mean total superiority, as its classification performance is by far the lowest. A model that is being well calibrated may be poor in discrimination. The Brier score offers a more balanced probabilistic perspective, with the best value being hybrid without weights (0.0367), close behind being hybrid without segment (0.0375). This helps to conclude that these two variants give the most useful estimations of probability in general.

There is also a meaningful difference in the uncertainty statistics. The lowest MC variance mean (0.0015) and the lowest predictive entropy mean (0.1562) belongs to the hybrid without weights, which means that the predictions are the most stable and confident among any other variants. DenseNet and hybrid without segment also have a low uncertainty, but the full hybrid is slightly more uncertain. Conversely, EfficientNet exhibits the largest MC variance, the largest predictive entropy, and the largest mutual information, meaning that not only are its predictions less accurate, but it is also more uncertain when doing stochastic inference. This is in line with its low F1, PR-AUC, and ROC-AUC, which validates the fact that it is the least reliable configuration when operating independently.

Another trade-off is indicated by model complexity and efficiency. The three hybrid-based models have a total of 19.7 million parameters each and are the largest models in the comparison. DenseNet has fewer than 14.4 million parameters, and EfficientNet is the smallest with 5.38 million parameters but also has the fastest inference time (0.0101 s/image). Nevertheless, predictive performance is extremely costly due to its pace advantage. The more powerful models include DenseNet, which is a viable tradeoff of accuracy and efficiency, and Hybrid-No-Segment, which is optimal in F1-micro with a slight increase in latency.

Overall, Table 5 demonstrates that the hybrid model without segmentation and class weighting is the most successful in the final multi-label classification, whereas the hybrid model without segmentation and class weighting is the most successful in PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, Brier score, and minimum uncertainty. DenseNet169 is sufficiently strong as a standalone baseline and also performs better than the entire hybrid model, but EfficientNetB0 itself cannot do this job. Thus, the ablation study indicates that the efficiency of the proposed model can be promoted not only by the feature fusion but also by the interaction between preprocessing and imbalance handling and the dataset. Segmentation, in this instance, seems to be eliminating some useful information, and the weighting of classes only partially enhances thresholded classification, whereas DenseNet adds more robust representations of the retina than EfficientNet in the MuReD context.

The ablation study confirms that the hybrid feature-fusion strategy provides important benefits over single-backbone modeling, particularly when compared with EfficientNetB0 alone. The full hybrid model positively affected F1-micro (0.2092 to 0.4915), PR-AUC micro (0.1804 to 0.5150), and ROC-AUC micro (0.7563 to 0.9231) when compared to EfficientNetB0, where it is evident that the fused representation extracts significantly more retinal information than the lightweight backbone. What is more important is that variants of the hybrid-based models showed the highest performance in the study with the highest F1-micro (0.5484) and the PR-AUC micro (0.5821) and the ROC-AUC micro (0.9369) and the minimum Brier score (0.0367). These findings indicate that EfficientNetB0 and DenseNet169 are complementary learners and that EfficientNetB0 and DenseNet169 are efficient encoders of global features and texture-sensitive and lesion-aware features, respectively. However, DenseNet169 on its own was already a competitor and slightly higher than the entire hybrid model in F1-micro, which means that the advantage of feature fusion is conditional on the presence of additional preprocessing and optimization environments. Thus, the findings are in line with the original idea of the hybrid model as being better in its optimized forms but not as an unconditional enhancement irrespective of the setting.

A further elaboration of the ablation results indicates that the proposed model does not just depend on the process of backbone selection but also the interaction of the preprocessing and the imbalance processing. The single-backbone baseline of the DenseNet169-only branch was significantly higher than that of EfficientNetB0, which demonstrates that dense feature reuse is particularly helpful in maintaining delicate retinal lesion patterns in MuReD. Meanwhile, the hybrid variants also indicate that the complementary representations are complementary, but the most successful configuration was not necessarily the most processed one. Specifically, the highest F1-micro was recorded in the hybrid model with no segmentation, and the highest ROC-AUC and PR-AUC scores were obtained with the hybrid model with no class weighting. The trend indicates that segmentation-based masking can enhance selectivity to the retinal areas but can also eliminate peripheral contextual information in individual samples, but class weighting is better at minority label sensitivity but can be characterized by minor changes in score calibration and ranking behavior. Thus, the performance discussion cannot be narrowed down to one measure, but it can be concluded that there is a trade-off between threshold-based classification quality, ranking quality, calibration, and uncertainty, which is especially relevant in multi-label retinal diagnosis.

Comparative analysis of state-of-the-art retinal disease diagnosis models.

A second important trend revealed by Table 8 is that methodological differences strongly affect the fairness of direct comparison. One example is the fuzzy rule-based and stochastic gradient boosting decision-support system suggested by Orlova49, which employs clinical, electrophysiological, and tabular signs as opposed to fundus images. Though the 58% improvement in MAUC/MMCC reported between baseline and the multimodal clinical data indicates the utility of multimodal clinical data, this architecture cannot be directly compared to CNN-based fundus classification models. On the same note, Jafarbeglou et al.22 have managed to perform an excellent diagnosis of inherited retinal disease with multi-input retinal imaging, although their task is disease-specific and not intended to do a large-scale multi-label retinal disorder classification. The CONV-FAN-POX by Tulunay and Kaya is also systematically interesting, as it demonstrates that time–frequency-based CNN design is worthwhile, though this is not a retinal study and is not supposed to be a direct competitor to a benchmark.

In general, the most relevant studies that are directly comparative are those that were assessed on retinal fundus information in a multi-disease context, particularly the work associated with MuReD. A custom multi-layer CNN applied to RFMiD by Sengar et al.53 achieved 82.13% validation accuracy and 76.04% testing accuracy, which indicates that simple CNN models might not perform well when there is a higher level of disease diversity and label ambiguity. Ejaz et al.54 tested various CNN depth versions on both RFMiD and RFMiD 2.0 with results of testing accuracy up to 89.59 again under a four-class formulation. Yang et al.23, in their turn, suggested CG-Tran on MuReD and provided mAP = 69.9% as the most conceptually pertinent article in this table due to the usage of the same 19-label dataset. It has especially been important due to the fact that same-dataset comparisons are more significant than cross-dataset accuracy comparisons in cases where there is a significant difference in label spaces and pathology distributions.

The presented EfficientNetB0 + DenseNet169 hybrid model takes a special place. Instead of being optimized in a simplified multi-class environment, it was tested on the more challenging MuReD benchmark with 19 labels of retinal diseases, clinically realistic multi-pathology co-occurrence, and heterogeneous source data based on the ARIA, STARE, and RFMiD sources. Optimal architectural variants obtained F1-micro = 0.5484, ROC-AUC micro = 0.9369, and PR-AUC micro = 0.5821, and the averaged multi-seed results obtained F1-micro = 0.4603, ROC-AUC micro = 0.9136, and PR-AUC micro = 0.4758. These findings suggest that the model offered is competitive in a significantly more challenging diagnostic context, compared to most of the past studies, which are reported in the table. Notably, the model is not provided as a high-quality classifier only, but as a more clinically relevant multi-label architecture that integrates complementary backbone representations, retina-centered preprocessing, and reliability-focused evaluation.

The inclusion of the limitation column in Table 8 is particularly helpful since it helps to understand that it is not only raw numerical superiority that can be the most effective indicator of methodological value. Other models are more accurate in that they report fewer classes; alternate imaging modalities are used, like OCT, or structured non-image clinical data is used. Conversely, the current model tolerates a more challenging problem formulation and hence must be considered in terms of its breadth of diagnosis and not based on a one-to-one comparison of percentage values only. In this sense, the proposed model has empirically and methodologically valuable contributions: empirically, through high MuReD performance in the context of discrimination measures; and methodologically, through the fact that hybrid feature fusion and imbalance-considering optimization is a viable and competitive strategy in the diagnosis of complex multi-label retinal diseases.

Despite the performance of the proposed multi-label retinal disease classification model, it can be admitted that a number of restrictions are to be considered. To begin with, the research was carried out on one benchmark dataset split, which might not be a complete reflection of the heterogeneity of real-world ophthalmic cohorts, imaging equipment, acquisition procedures, and annotation procedures; thus, the possible bias of the dataset might have affected the findings. Second, although preprocessing, robustness analysis, and uncertainty estimation are included, model output can also be influenced by variations in image quality, including blur, underexposure, noise, artifacts, and unscanned retinal areas, which are typical of real-world screening space. Third, it is unclear how the model will be applied to unobservable clinical sites, where variations in patient demographics, disease rates, and camera equipment, as well as organizational processes, can change the results on post-implementation. Practically speaking, the given paradigm must be seen as a decision-support tool, not as a substitute for ophthalmologists. It may be incorporated into the workflow of ophthalmologists as a front-end screening/triage system that automatically raises the red flag on suspicious fundus pictures, high-risk/uncertain cases to be reviewed by specialists, and can be used to support tele-ophthalmology services in resource-limited environments. Predicted probabilities and uncertainty estimates, as well as visual explanations in these situations, might assist clinicians in understanding when the model prediction can be relied upon and when it is still necessary to conduct a manual check. The emphasis of future work, therefore, should be on the external multi-center validation, prospective evaluation in the actual screening pathways, and closer integration with the image-quality assessment and referral decision systems in order to enhance the clinical preparedness.

Conclusion

In this paper, a hybrid deep learning architecture of multiple-label retinal disease recognition by using EfficientNetB0 and DenseNet169, with a parallel feature-fusion structure and trained on the MuReD dataset, was introduced. The proposed model was aimed at dealing with a number of clinically significant issues such as the heterogeneous appearance of the retina, co-occurring disease labels, class imbalance, and image quality variability. Through combining retina-based preprocessing, dual-backbone-based feature extraction, feature-level fusion, and imbalance-conscious learning, the model intended to offer a stronger and more practically significant substitute for traditional single-backbone retina classification models.

The results of the experiments proved that the given design is efficient and competitive in the context of the MuReD multi-label. The single-backbone DenseNet169 demonstrated high performance, but the single-backbone EfficientNetB0 portrayed significantly poorer results, which may suggest that fine-grained feature reuse is specifically significant to this dataset. The hybrid variants also demonstrated that the feature fusion can be helpful but not necessarily the well-processed variant. Specifically, the hybrid model with no segmentation obtained the best F1-micro, whereas the hybrid model with no class weighting created the best ROC-AUC micro and PR-AUC micro scores of the variants under test. These results show that performance cannot be viewed in just one summary measure, but instead be viewed in several perspective views, which consists of threshold-based classification quality, ranking quality, calibration, and predictive stability. It was also demonstrated by the preprocessing ablation that individual enhancement strategies were more effective than a scatter application of all preprocessing stages which confirmed the fact that preprocessing needs to be tailored to the visual features of the dataset.

Calibration and uncertainty analysis were also used in the study to enhance the validity of the proposed model. The consistency of predictions and their credibility were measured by bootstrap-based confidence intervals, repeated multi-seed experiments, expected calibration error, Brier score, and Monte Carlo dropout-based uncertainty estimates. The multi-seed analysis revealed a consistent performance on various random initializations, whereas the uncertainty results offered a further safety-oriented protection by giving one an idea of the cases that might need further scrutiny. These extensions enhance the methodological rigor of the research and add clinical relevance of the proposed model to raw classification accuracy.

Although these are encouraging results, several limitations must be mentioned. The experiments were performed on a publicly accessible benchmark dataset, and even though it can be said that MuReD is clinically useful due to its multi-label format and its heterogeneous nature, it might not be completely representative of the entire diversity of real clinical populations, devices, acquisition conditions, and annotation practices. Severe blurs, low illumination, noise, artifacts, or previously unknown imaging patterns may still affect performance. Moreover, direct comparison with previous studies on retinal disease must be viewed with caution, as most of the published approaches focus on fewer classes, single-disease tasks, or less complex data than MuReD. Hence, numerical dominance in a simpler context does not always translate into better performance when a more realistic situation with multi-label diagnosis is considered.

The proposed model can be viewed as a decision support system instead of a self-diagnostic one. It has possible applications in screening, triage, and tele-ophthalmology processes, in which it may assist in identifying suspicious fundus images, prioritizing high-risk or uncertain cases, and assisting in making referral choices. Significantly, however, more pragmatic implementation would need external multi-center validation, accommodation to site-specific imaging guidelines and disease frequency, and more extensive integration with image-quality measurement, uncertainty-directed review, and image-explanation instruments aimed at clinicians. Future research ought to then incorporate external validation, prospective workflow testing, and domain adaptation and enhance robustness, trust, and readiness to be applied in the real clinical setting by developing stronger lesion-sensitive interpretability.

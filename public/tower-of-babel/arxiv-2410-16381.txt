Is leptogenesis during gravitational reheating flavourful?
Basabendu Barman, Arghyajit Datta, and Md Riajul Haque

Abstract
We examine the impact of charged lepton Yukawa equilibration on leptogenesis during gravitational reheating. During the post-inflationary era, the inflaton field is assumed to oscillate around the minimum of a monomial potential, leading to the gravitational production of Standard Model (SM) particles, constituting the radiation bath. The heavy right-handed neutrinos (RHN), responsible for generating baryon asymmetry via leptogenesis, are also produced through graviton-mediated scattering of the homogeneous inflaton field and thermal bath, as well as from the the inverse decay of the bath particles. By considering both minimal and non-minimal gravitational contributions to SM, we demonstrate that flavour effects can be safely neglected in the minimal reheating scenario. However, with large non-minimal coupling, these effects may become important, depending on the choice of the RHN mass. We identify the corresponding viable parameter space that satisfies the observed baryon asymmetry in each case.

4
Conclusion

Gravitational interactions are irrefutable, and hence, gravity-mediated production of (beyond) the Standard Model (SM) particles is unavoidable. In this work, we therefore consider a scenario where the Universe is heated up at the end of reheating via gravitational production of the radiation bath. During the same time, we consider the production of heavy right-handed neutrinos (RHN), which further undergo CP-violating out-of-equilibrium decay to produce the observed baryon asymmetry via leptogenesis. These RHNs can be sourced either from the scattering of the inflaton condensate and SM particles in the thermal bath, mediated by graviton or inverse decay process from the thermal bath. However, the gravitational production from the thermal bath is always subdominant compared to the production from the inflation. Once the inflation gets over, the inflaton
\phi
is assumed to oscillate in a monomial potential
\phi^{k}
. Depending on the choice of
k
, RHN mass, and the non-minimal coupling strength, it is possible that the charged lepton Yukawa interaction(s) equilibrates during reheating (typically
\tau
-flavour). We notice, for the minimal gravitational reheating case, the charged lepton Yukawa interactions remain out of equilibrium during the period of reheating. It is, therefore, safe to consider unflavoured leptogenesis in this situation. For non-minimal gravitational reheating, we found from Fig.
4
that the
\tau
-lepton Yukawa may equilibrate, depending on the strength of the non-minimal coupling
\xi
and the RHN mass, and in that case, the flavour effects must be taken into account (as depicted in Fig.

Appendix A
Details of Casas-Ibarra parametrization

As the neutral component of the SM Higgs doublet acquires a VEV leading to the spontaneous breaking of the SM gauge symmetry, neutrinos in the SM obtain a Dirac mass that can be written as

\displaystyle m_{D}=\frac{y_{\nu}}{\sqrt{2}}\,\langle H\rangle.

(49)

The Dirac mass
m_{D}
together with the RHN bare mass
M_{N}
, can explain the nonzero light neutrino masses with the help of Type-I seesaw
[
15
,
16
,
119
]
. The light-neutrino masses can be expressed as,

\displaystyle m_{\nu}\simeq-m_{D}\,M^{-1}\,m_{D}^{T}\,.

(50)

The mass eigenvalues and mixing are then obtained by diagonalising the light-neutrino mass matrix as

\displaystyle D_{m}=\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}m_{\nu}\mathcal{U}^{*}

(51)

with
D_{m}={\rm dia}\,(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})
consisting of the mass eigenvalues and
\mathcal{U}
being the Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata matrix
[
113
]
10
10

10

The charged lepton mass matrix is considered to be diagonal.
. In order to obtain a complex structure of the Yukawa coupling which is essential from the perspective of leptogenesis, we use the well-known Casas-Ibarra (CI) parametrization
[
112
]
. Using this one can write the Yukawa coupling
y_{\nu}
as,

\displaystyle y_{\nu}=-i\frac{\sqrt{2}}{v}\mathcal{U}D_{\sqrt{m}}\mathcal{R}^{T}D_{\sqrt{M}},

(52)

where
\mathcal{R}
is a complex orthogonal matrix
\mathcal{R}^{T}\mathcal{R}=I
, which we choose as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\cos{z}&\sin{z}\\
0&-\sin{z}&\cos{z}\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(53)

where
z=\text{Re}[\theta]+i\,\text{Im}[\theta]
is a complex angle. The above structure of
\mathcal{R}
can be justified by considering two RHNs or considering the third RHN
N_{3}
to be very heavy and effectively decoupled from the bath. In such a scenario our neutrino Yukawa matrix becomes of dimension
3\times 3
. Such a scenario also predicts the lightest active neutrino to be exactly massless. The diagonal light neutrino mass matrix
m_{\nu}^{d}
is calculable using the best fit values of solar and atmospheric mass obtained from the latest neutrino oscillation data
[
113
]
. Now, the elements of Yukawa coupling matrix
y_{N}
for a specific value of

\displaystyle y_{\nu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-0.0002+0.0076i&0.0696+0.00453i&0\\

-0.0142-0.0255i&-0.241+0.151i&0\end{array}\right)\,,

which satisfies the light neutrino mass, as well as produces desired CP asymmetry in the visible sector, as we discuss below.

Appendix B
Non-minimal gravitational production

Here we derive the production rate of RHNs that stems from the non-minimal gravitational interaction. We begin with the action in the Jordan fame
[
54
]
,

\mathcal{S}_{J}=\int\,d^{4}x\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\left[-\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{2}\,\Omega^{2}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{h}+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N_{i}}\right]\,,

(57)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}=\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial^{\mu}\phi-V(\phi)

(58)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{h}=\partial_{\mu}h\,\partial^{\mu}h^{\dagger}-V(hh^{\dagger})

(59)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{N_{i}}=\frac{i}{2}\,\overline{\mathcal{N}_{i}}\,\overleftrightarrow{\not{\nabla}}\,\mathcal{N}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\,M_{i}\,\overline{(\mathcal{N})^{c}}_{i}\,\mathcal{N}_{i}+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{yuk}}

(60)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{yuk}}=-\left(y_{\nu}\right)_{i}\,\overline{\mathbb{L}}\,h\,\mathcal{N}_{i}\,,

(61)

where
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathbb{L}
are the RHN and SM lepton doublet fields in Jordan frame. It is then possible to perform a conformal transformation from the Jordan frame to the Einstein frame,

g_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^{2}\,\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(62)

with
\Omega^{2}\equiv\left(1+\xi_{\phi}\,\phi^{2}/M_{P}^{2}+\xi_{h}\,|h|^{2}/M_{P}^{2}\right)
being the conformal factor. The action in Einstein frame then becomes,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{E}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Biggl[-\frac{M_{P}^{2}\,\mathcal{R}}{2}+\frac{K^{ab}}{2}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}S_{a}\,\partial_{\nu}S_{b}+\frac{i}{2\,\Omega^{3}}\,\overline{N_{i}}\,\overleftrightarrow{\not{\nabla}}\,N_{i}-\frac{1}{\Omega^{4}}\,\left(\frac{M_{i}}{2}\,\overline{N_{i}^{c}}\,N_{i}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{yuk}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{3i}{4\Omega^{4}}\,\overline{N_{i}}\,\left(\overleftrightarrow{\not{\partial}}\,\Omega\right)\,N_{i}-\frac{1}{\Omega^{4}}\,\left(V_{\phi}+V_{h}\right)\Biggr]\,,

(63)

where

\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\rightarrow\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{\Omega^{4}}

(64)

\tilde{\not{\nabla}}\rightarrow\Omega\not{\nabla}-\frac{3}{2}\Omega^{2}(\not{\partial}\Omega)\,,

(65)

and the indices
\{a,\,b\}\in\{\phi,\,h\}
. Following the field redefinitions:
L\rightarrow\Omega^{3/2}L
,
N_{i}\rightarrow\Omega^{3/2}N_{i}
and
\overline{N_{i}}\rightarrow\Omega^{3/2}\overline{N_{i}}
,
we recover the action with canonical kinetic term for the RHNs as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{E}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Biggl[-\frac{M_{P}^{2}\,\mathcal{R}}{2}+\frac{K^{ab}}{2}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}S_{a}\,\partial_{\nu}S_{b}-\frac{1}{\Omega^{4}}\,\left(V_{\phi}+V_{h}\right)+\frac{i}{2}\,\overline{N_{i}}\,\overleftrightarrow{\not{\nabla}}\,N_{i}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\,\Omega}\,M_{i}\,\overline{N_{i}^{c}}\,N_{i}+\frac{1}{\Omega}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{yuk}}\Biggr]\,,

(66)

where

K^{ab}=6\,\frac{\partial\log\Omega}{\partial S_{a}}\frac{\partial\log\Omega}{\partial S_{b}}+\frac{\delta^{ab}}{\Omega^{2}}\,.

(67)

The kinetic terms for the RHNs can be expressed in an effective form

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\;=\;-\sigma_{hN_{i}}^{\xi}\,|h|^{2}\,\overline{N_{i}^{c}}N_{i}-\sigma_{\phi N_{i}}^{\xi}\,\phi^{2}\,\overline{N_{i}^{c}}N_{i}\,,

(68)

with

\sigma_{\phi N_{i}}^{\xi}=\frac{M_{i}}{2M_{P}^{2}}\,\xi_{\phi}

(69)

\sigma_{hN_{i}}^{\xi}=\frac{M_{i}}{2M_{P}^{2}}\,\xi_{h}\,.

(70)

These non-minimal interactions provide additional production channels for the RHNs:
\phi\phi\to N_{i}\,N_{i},\,hh\to N_{i}\,N_{i}
. Then, for the thermal production of RHNs, the production rate becomes,

\displaystyle R^{T,\xi}_{N_{i}}\simeq N_{h}\,\frac{\zeta(3)^{2}\,\xi_{h}^{2}}{32\,\pi^{5}}\,\frac{M_{i}^{2}\,T^{6}}{M_{P}^{4}}\,,

(71)

where
\zeta(x)
is the Riemann-zeta function. For the inflaton scattering process
\phi\phi\rightarrow N_{i}\,N_{i}
, on the other hand, we find

\displaystyle R^{\phi,\xi}_{N_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M_{i}^{2}\xi_{\phi}^{2}\phi_{0}^{4}\omega^{2}}{32\pi M_{P}^{4}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(2n)^{2}|\mathcal{Q}^{(2)}_{2n}|^{2}\times\sqrt{1-\frac{4M_{i}^{2}}{E_{2n}^{2}}}\,,

(72)

where we define
\phi_{0}=\left(\frac{\rho_{\phi}}{\lambda\,M_{P}^{4-k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}
and
{\cal Q}^{(2)}_{n}
by

\phi^{2}(t)=\phi_{0}^{2}(t)\cdot\mathcal{Q}^{2}(t)=\phi_{0}^{2}(t)\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\,{\cal Q}^{(2)}_{n}e^{-in\omega t}\,.

(73)

For small-field approximation (
\xi_{\phi}\ll 1
),
R^{\phi,\xi}_{N_{i}}
is sub-dominant compared to Eq. (
35
). Comparing with the minimal production rate in Eq. (
37
), we find, the non-minimal thermal production dominates if

\displaystyle\xi\gtrsim 5.36\,\frac{T}{M_{1}}\,,

(74)

implying for
\xi\gtrsim 5.3
, the non-minimal contribution from thermal bath becomes important for
T\simeq M_{1}
. Similarly, comparing Eq. (
35
) and (
71
), we see, for

\displaystyle\xi\gtrsim 3.6\,\left(\frac{\rho_{\phi}}{m_{\phi}\,T^{3}}\right)\,,

(75)

the non-minimal thermal contribution dominates over minimal non-thermal contribution. Now, since
\rho_{\phi}\gg m_{\phi}\,T^{3}
for the parameter space of our interest, in this case, the non-minimal coupling needs to be
\gtrsim\mathcal{O}(10^{10})
in order to have
R^{T,\xi}_{N_{i}}\gtrsim R_{N}^{\phi}

A Parareal exponential integrator finite element method for linear parabolic equations
Jianguo Huang, Yuejin Xu

Abstract
In this paper, for solving a class of linear parabolic equations in rectangular domains, we have proposed an efficient Parareal exponential integrator finite element method. The proposed method first uses the finite element approximation with continuous multilinear rectangular basis function for spatial discretization, and then takes the Runge-Kutta approach accompanied with Parareal framework for time integration of the resulting semi-discrete system to produce parallel-in-time numerical solution. Under certain regularity assumptions, fully-discrete error estimates in L 2 L^{2} -norm are derived for the proposed schemes with random interpolation nodes. Moreover, a fast solver can be provided based on tensor product spectral decomposition and fast Fourier transform (FFT), since the mass and coefficient matrices of the proposed method can be simultaneously diagonalized with an orthogonal matrix. A series of numerical experiments in various dimensions are also presented to validate the theoretical results and demonstrate the excellent performance of the proposed method.

Introduction

In this paper, we are intended to study a fast solution of the following linear parabolic equation:

\left\{\begin{split}&u_{t}=\mathcal{D}\Delta u+f(t),\quad\bm{x}\in\Omega,\quad t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+T,\\
&u(t_{0}):=u(t_{0},\bm{x})=u_{0},\quad\bm{x}\in\Omega,\end{split}\right.

In this paper, we mainly discuss the problem above, which is associated with Dirichlet boundary conditions. The theoretical conclusion can also be extended to equations with periodic boundary conditions after minor modification.

In the past few decades, many researchers are devoted to developing fast solver for parabolic equation like (

In recent years, with the development of Parareal method, many scholars have proposed some modified Parareal algorithm mainly in two aspects, decreasing the iteration times to speedup the convergence rate and improving the parallel efficiency for each iteration. Many researchers have tried to decrease the iteration times from different aspects, for instance, implicit time-integration schemes and Newton-like correction are used to accelerate the simulations in

As far as we know, for most numerical methods of PDEs, the spatial variables are often discretized by finite difference methods

The innovation of this paper is the development of parallel numerical method with rigorous global error estimates for general linear parabolic equations, which is proved to be unconditionally stable within finite iterations.

2
The EIFE-linear method and PEIFE-linear method

In this section, for solving (
1.1
) with Dirichlet boundary conditions, we first propose the exponential integrator finite element method (EIFE-linear) for linear parabolic equations. Later, we apply Parareal framework to accelerate the simulation of EIFE-linear method and then develop the PEIFE-linear method.

First of all, we define some standard notations for later requirement. Given a bounded domain
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

and a non-negative integer

, denote the standard Sobolev spaces on
G
by
H^{s}(G)

with norm
\|\cdot\|_{s,G}
and semi-norm
|\cdot|_{s,G}
, and denote the
{L}^{2}
-inner product on
G
by
(\cdot,\,\cdot)_{G}
.
H_{0}^{s}(G)
denotes the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(G)
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{s,G}
. We omit the subscript if
G=\Omega
, when there is no confusion caused. For any non-negative integer
\ell
,
\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(G)
stands for the set of all polynomials on
G
with the total degree at most
\ell
. Moreover, given two quantities
a
and
b
, “
a\lesssim{b}
" abbreviates “
a\leq{C}b
", where the generic positive constant

C
is independent of the mesh size;
a\eqsim b
is equivalent to
a\lesssim b\lesssim a
.

Now, we are ready to consider the problem (
1.1
) with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition and an initial configuration
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, that is

\left\{\begin{split}&u_{t}=\mathcal{D}\Delta u+f(t),\quad\bm{x}\in\Omega,\quad t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+T,\\
&u(t_{0},\bm{x})=u_{0}(\bm{x}),\quad\bm{x}\in\Omega,\\
&u(t,\bm{x})=0,\quad\bm{x}\in\partial\Omega,\quad t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+T,\end{split}\right.

(2.1)

where
0<T<\infty
is the total duration time.

2.1
Semi-discretization in space by finite element approximation

For simplicity, we denote
u(t)(\bm{x})=u(t,\bm{x})
,
\bm{x}\in\Omega
in the following presentation. Suppose that
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a rectangular domain and is devided uniformly with the mesh size
h_{i}>0
along
x_{i}
direction. Associated with one-dimensional piecewise linear finite element space
V_{h_{i}}^{i}
for direction
x_{i}
, we construct the finite element space
V_{h}
, which is a subspace of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and can be denoted as

\begin{split}V_{h}:=&{\rm span}\{{\phi_{1}^{i_{1}}}(x_{1})\cdots\phi_{d}^{i_{d}}(x_{d}):\ 1\leq i_{1}\leq N_{1},\cdots,1\leq i_{d}\leq N_{d}\},\end{split}

(2.2)

where
h=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq d}h_{i}
,
\phi_{i}^{j}(x_{i})
is the j-th basis function of
V_{h}^{i}
,
N_{i}
is the number of uniform subdomains of direction
x_{i}
. For the forthcoming error analysis, we assume the partition
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is quasi-uniform, i.e.,
h\eqsim h_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq d
.

Using the variational framework, the above problem (
2.1
) can be converted to find
u_{h}\in V_{h}
, which satisfies that for each
v_{h}\in V_{h}
,

\left\{\begin{split}&(u_{h,t},v_{h})+a(u_{h},v_{h})=(f(t),v_{h}),\quad t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+T,\\
&u_{h}(t_{0})=P_{h}u_{0},\end{split}\right.

(2.3)

where the bilinear operator
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric and defined by

a(w,v)=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{D}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v\,{\rm d}\bm{x},\quad w,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

(2.4)

and
P_{h}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow V_{h}
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection operator and is stable with respect to
L^{2}
-norm.

According to the inverse inequality for finite elements and Riesz representation theorem, we can derive that there exists abounded linear operator
L_{h}:V_{h}\to V_{h}
such that

a(w_{h},v_{h})=(L_{h}w_{h},v_{h}),\quad\forall w_{h},\ v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(2.5)

Then the problem (
2.3
) can be reformulated as

\left\{\begin{split}&u_{h,t}+L_{h}u_{h}=P_{h}f,\quad\bm{x}\in\Omega,\quad t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+T,\\
&u_{h}(t_{0})=P_{h}u_{0},\quad\bm{x}\in\Omega.\end{split}\right.

(2.6)

Until now, we have derived the semi-discrete scheme for (
2.1
).

2.2
Linear exponential integrator finite element method

As for the temporal discretization, we divide the time interval
[t_{0},t_{0}+T]
into
N_{T}>0
subintervals which are denoted as

\tau_{n+1}=\tau_{n}+\Delta\tau_{n},\quad n=0,\cdots,N_{T}-1,\quad\mbox{and }\Delta\tau=\max\limits_{0\leq n\leq N_{T}-1}\Delta\tau_{n}.

Denote
\{e^{-tL_{h}}\}_{t\geq 0}
as the semigroup on
V_{h}
with the infinitesimal generator
(-L_{h})
. Hence, by the Duhamel formula, the solution
u_{h}
to the problem (
2.6
) can be further expressed as

u_{h}(\tau_{n+1})=e^{-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h}}u_{h}(\tau_{n})+\int_{0}^{\Delta\tau_{n}}e^{-(\Delta\tau_{n}-\sigma)L_{h}}P_{h}f(\tau_{n}+\sigma)\,{\rm d}\sigma.

(2.7)

For computing the numerical solution in (
2.7
) efficiently, we apply linear exponential Runge-Kutta method
[
16
]
to compute the integral and obtain the approximate solution. Define
u_{h}^{n}
as the numerical solution obtained at time
t_{n}
after temporal and spatial discretization. Then a fully-discrete scheme for solution of the linear parabolic problem (
2.1
) can be expressed as follows,

u_{h}^{n+1}=e^{-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h}}u_{h}^{n}+\Delta\tau_{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}(-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h})P_{h}f(\tau_{n}+c_{i}\Delta\tau_{n}),\quad n=0,\cdots,N_{T}-1,

(2.8)

where the interpolation nodes
c_{1},\cdots,c_{s}
are
s
different nodes selected in
[0,1]
and the weights are:

b_{i}(-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h})=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\Delta\tau_{n}(1-\theta)L_{h}}l_{i}(\theta)\,{\rm d}\theta,

(2.9)

and
l_{i}(\theta)
are the familiar Lagrange interpolation polynomials

l_{i}(\theta):=\prod\limits_{m=1,m\neq i}^{s}\frac{\theta-c_{m}}{c_{i}-c_{m}},\quad i=1,\cdots s.

Until now, we have proposed a fully-discrete numerical method called the exponential integrator finite element method (abbreviated as EIFE-linear) for linear parabolic equation (
1.1
). Furthermore, since the semi-discrete scheme (
2.6
) can be regarded as an ODE system, it’s worthy to note that EIFE-linear method can also be implemented in the parallel-in-time pattern.

2.3
Exponential integrator finite element method in parallel-in-time pattern

Next, we design a parallel EIFE-linear method, which is combined with Parareal method and achieve significant speedup. Before applying Parareal algorithm to compute the fully-discrete scheme (
2.8

[t_{0},t_{0}+T]

. It’s obvious that
T_{n}=t_{nM}
for
n=0,\cdots,N-1
.
\mathcal{G}
is the less precise but cheaper scheme chosen to coarse grids while
\mathcal{F}
is the more precise one chosen to fine grids. Taking

as the starting point, denote
\mathcal{G}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1}),\mathcal{H}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})
as the results computed in the time interval
[T_{n},T_{n+1}]
by less precise method
\mathcal{G}
and accurate method
\mathcal{H}
respectively.
\mathcal{F}(v;t_{l},t_{l+1})
is the result calculated by more precise method
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{M}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})
is obtained by applying successively
M

\mathcal{F}
in the subinterval
[T_{n},T_{n+1}]
. To illustrate the algorithm better, define
U_{h}^{n,(k)}
as the numerical result computed at time
T_{n}
after
k

\ominus
, and the parallel parts by symbol
\oplus
.

Since the coarse propagator is applied to the coarse grids with larger time step, it’s naturally to require coarse propagator
\mathcal{G}
to be unconditionally stable. In the following section, we will prove that EIFE-linear method is unconditionally stable for linear problems (Section
3

\mathcal{F}
propagator step by step with the small time step

. Therefore, in order to balance the need of computational cost and numerical accuracy, we choose a less precise EIFE-linear scheme with

while a more precise one with

stages as the fine propagator
\mathcal{F}
(

), that is,

\left\{\begin{split}\mathcal{G}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})&=e^{-\Delta T_{n}L_{h}}v+\Delta T_{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}b_{i}(-\Delta T_{n}L_{h})P_{h}f(T_{n}+c_{i}\Delta T_{n}),\\
\mathcal{F}(v;t_{l},t_{l+1})&=e^{-\Delta t_{l}L_{h}}v+\Delta t_{l}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}b_{i}(-\Delta t_{l}L_{h})P_{h}f(t_{l}+c_{i}\Delta t_{l}).\end{split}\right.

3
Convergence analysis of EIFE-linear method

In this section, we first focus on developing the error bound of EIFE-linear method for solving the problem (
1.1
) with homogenous Dirichlet boundary conditions. It is straightforward to extend the arguments to the same problem with nonhomogenous Dirichlet boundary conditions. For the error between the exact solution
u(t)
and the numerical solution
\big\{u_{h}^{n}\big\}
measured in the
L^{2}
-norm, we have by the triangle inequality

\big\|u(\tau_{n})-u_{h}^{n}\big\|_{0}\leq\|u(\tau_{n})-u_{h}(\tau_{n})\|_{0}+\big\|u_{h}(\tau_{n})-u_{h}^{n}\big\|_{0}.

(3.1)

Thus, we will estimate
\|u(\tau_{n})-u_{h}(\tau_{n})\|_{0}
and
\big\|u_{h}(\tau_{n})-u_{h}^{n}\big\|_{0}
respectively.

Assumption 3.1
.

The exact solution
u(t)
satisfies the following regularity conditions:

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+T}\|u(t)\|_{2,\Omega}\lesssim 1,

(3.2a)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+T}\|u_{t}(t)\|_{2,\Omega}\lesssim 1,

(3.2b)

where the hidden constants may depend on
t_{0}
and
T
.

Assumption 3.2
.

The function
f(t)
is sufficiently smooth with respect to
t
, i.e., for a positive integer
r
, it holds

\sum\limits_{\alpha\leq r}\Big|f^{(\alpha)}(t)\Big|\lesssim 1,\quad\forall t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+T].

(3.3)

First of all, the following result readily comes from Theorem 1.2 in
[
33
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u_{h}(t)
and
u(t)
be the solutions of (
2.6
) and (
2.1
). Assume that
u(t)
fulfills Assumption
3.1
. Then the error bound becomes

\|u_{h}(t)-u(t)\|_{0}\lesssim\|P_{h}u_{0}-u_{0}\|_{0}+h^{2}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{t}\|_{2}\,{\rm d}\sigma\Big),\quad t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+T.

(3.4)

A direct consequence of Lemma
3.1
is

\|u(\tau_{n})-u_{h}(\tau_{n})\|_{0}\lesssim h^{2},\quad\forall n=0,\cdots,N_{T},

(3.5)

where the hidden constant is independent of
h
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that the function
f
satisfies Assumption
3.2
and the exact solution
u(t)
satisfies the regularity condition (
3.2a
) and (
3.2b
) in Assumption
3.1
. Assume that
\{u_{h}^{n}\}
are numerical solution at time
\tau_{n}
by applying EIFE-linear method sequentially with
s
different interpolation nodes
c_{1},\cdots,c_{s}
in
[0,1]
. Then the numerical error bound becomes

\big\|u_{h}(\tau_{n})-u_{h}^{n}\big\|_{0}\lesssim(\Delta\tau)^{s},

(3.6)

where the hidden constant is independent of
h
and
\Delta\tau
.

Proof.

Since all interpolation nodes
c_{1},\cdots,c_{s}
are fixed, according to the definition of
b_{i}(-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h})
in (
2.9
) and (
4.5
) in Lemma
4.1
, we can immediately obtain that

\begin{split}\|b_{i}(-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h})\|_{0}&=\Big\|\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\Delta\tau_{n}(1-\theta)L_{h}}\prod\limits_{m=1,m\neq i}\frac{\theta-c_{m}}{c_{i}-c_{m}}\,{\rm d}\theta\Big\|_{0}\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{1}\Big\|e^{-\Delta\tau_{n}(1-\theta)L_{h}}\Big\|_{0}\Big|\prod\limits_{m=1,m\neq i}\frac{\theta-c_{m}}{c_{i}-c_{m}}\Big|\,{\rm d}\theta\\
&\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\prod\limits_{m=1,m\neq i}\frac{\theta-c_{m}}{c_{i}-c_{m}}\Big|\,{\rm d}\theta\lesssim 1.\end{split}

Therefore,
\|b_{i}(-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h})\|_{0}
are uniformly bounded for
\Delta\tau_{n}>0,i=1,\cdots,s
.

Next, define

\psi_{j}(-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h})=\varphi_{j}(-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h})-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}(-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h})\frac{c_{i}^{j-1}}{(j-1)!},

where

\varphi_{j}(-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h})=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\Delta\tau_{n}(1-\theta)L_{h}}\frac{\theta^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}\,{\rm d}\theta,\quad j\geq 1.

Then according to the Lagrange interpolation formula, we find that, for
1\leq j\leq s
,

\int_{0}^{1}e^{-(1-\theta)\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h}}\frac{\theta^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}\,{\rm d}\theta=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-\Delta\tau_{n}(1-\theta)L_{h}}}{(j-1)!}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\prod_{m=1,m\neq i}^{s}\frac{\theta-c_{m}}{c_{i}-c_{m}}c_{i}^{j-1}\,{\rm d}\theta.

Therefore,

\varphi_{j}(-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}(-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h})\frac{c_{i}^{j-1}}{(j-1)!},

which implies that

\psi_{j}(-\Delta\tau_{n}L_{h})=0,\quad 1\leq j\leq s.

Since
f
fulfills Assumption
3.2
and
P_{h}
is a stable projection operator with respect to
L^{2}
-norm, we can derive that
(P_{h}f)^{(s)}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
also holds. Hence, according to the preceding arguments and Lemma
4.1
, we know the assumptions of Theorem 2.7 in
[
16
]
are held, so we can obtain

\|u_{h}(\tau_{n})-u_{h}^{n}\|_{0}\lesssim\Delta\tau^{s}

in view of this theorem.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

It’s worth noting that no restriction on the time step size
\Delta\tau
is imposed in Theorem
3.1
, which implies that the proposed EIFE-linear method is stable with large time stepping and appropriate to be applied to coarse grids. Moreover, the temporal accuracy of EIFE-linear method can reach to any order when the number of interpolation nodes is sufficiently large.

4
Convergence analysis of PEIFE-linear method for linear problems

Since PEIFE-linear method can be regarded as a modified EIFE-linear method with significant speedup, it’s crucial to show that computing PEIFE-linear method in distribution can be as accurate as computing EIFE-linear method sequentially. In this section, we turn to discuss the error analysis of PEIFE-linear method for a linear parabolic problem with the homogenous Dirichlet boundary condition, i.e., the problem (
2.3
). The conclusion can be extended to the equations with nonhomogenous Dirichlet boundary conditions with minor revision. The corresponding notations are same as that defined in Section
2
.

In the following analysis, we always denote
v(t)(\bm{x})=v(t,\bm{x})
and
w(t)(\bm{x})=w(t,\bm{x})
for
\bm{x}\in\Omega
for brevity. Also, we assume the time intervals are uniform, i.e.,
\Delta t=\Delta t_{0}=\cdots=\Delta t_{NM-1}=\frac{T}{NM}
and
\Delta T=\Delta T_{0}=\cdots=\Delta T_{N-1}=\frac{T}{N}
. We also represent
u_{h}(t)
as the solution of the semi-discrete (in space) problem (
2.6
) (or (
2.7
)), and
\big\{U_{h}^{n,(k)}\big\}
as the numerical solution produced by the PEIFE-linear method (see Algorithm
1
). Following ideas about analyzing Parareal method in
[
12
,
2
]
, we can first suppose the chosen coarse propagator
\mathcal{G}
and fine propagator
\mathcal{F}
satisfy the following assumptions.

Assumption 4.1
.

Suppose
V_{h}
is the finite element space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{0}
, and

1.

Fine propagator
\mathcal{F}
fulfills the following regularity condition:

\begin{split}\max\limits_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\|\mathcal{F}^{M}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{H}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})\|_{0}&\lesssim(\Delta t)^{q+1},\end{split}

(4.1)

where
\Delta t=\max\{\Delta t_{l}|l=0,\cdots,NM-1\}
and the hidden constant is positive and independent of
\Delta t
and
v
.

2.

Coarse propagator
\mathcal{G}
fulfills the following regularity condition:

\begin{split}\max\limits_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\|\mathcal{G}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{H}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})\|_{0}&\lesssim(\Delta T)^{p+1},\end{split}

(4.2)

where
\Delta T=\max\{\Delta T_{n}|n=0,\cdots,N-1\}
and the hidden constant is positive and independent of
\Delta T
and
v
.

3.

Coarse propagator
\mathcal{G}
can satisfy the following Lipschitz conditions:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{G}(w;T_{n},T_{n+1})\|_{0}-\|v-w\|_{0}\lesssim\Delta T\|v-w\|_{0},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{G}(w;T_{n},T_{n+1})+\mathcal{H}(w;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{H}(\xi;T_{n},T_{n+1})\|_{0}

\displaystyle-\|v-\xi\|_{0}\lesssim\Delta T\|v-\xi\|_{0}+(\Delta T)^{p+1}\|w-\xi\|_{0},

(4.4)

where the hidden constants are positive and independent of
\Delta T,v,w
and
\xi
.

We present below a lemma related to the semigroup
\{e^{-tL_{h}}\}_{t\geq 0}
with the infinitesimal generator
(-L_{h})
given by (
2.5
), which has been illustrated in Lemma 3.1 in
[
20
]
and is important to the forthcoming numerical analysis.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
V_{h}
be the finite element space given in (
2.2
), which is equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{0}
. Then there holds

\|e^{-\tau L_{h}}\|_{0}+\|\tau^{\gamma}L_{h}^{\gamma}e^{-\tau L_{h}}\|_{0}\lesssim 1,\quad\forall\tau\geq 0,h>0,

(4.5)

where
\gamma>0
is any given parameter, and the hidden constant is positive and independent of
h,\tau
but may depend on
\gamma
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
f(t)
fulfills Assumption
3.2
with some positive integer
q
. Assume that EIFE-linear method is chosen to fine grids with
q
different interpolation nodes
c_{1},\cdots,c_{q}\in[0,1]
. If
v(t)
is sufficiently smooth with respect to
t
, then

\begin{split}\max\limits_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\|\mathcal{F}^{M}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{H}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})\|_{0}&\lesssim\Delta T(\Delta t)^{q},\end{split}

is fulfilled and the hidden constant is independent of
\Delta T,\Delta t,v
, which means that EIFE-linear method is proper to be applied to fine grids.

Proof.

For any
1\leq n\leq N
, based on the Parareal-EIFE method proposed in Algorithm
1
, we can derive by recurrence that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{M}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{H}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M-1}e^{-\Delta t(M-1-j)L_{h}}\Delta t\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}b_{i}(-\Delta tL_{h})P_{h}f(T_{n}+(j+c_{i})\Delta t)-\int_{0}^{\Delta T}e^{-(\Delta T-\theta)L_{h}}P_{h}f(T_{n}+\theta)\,{\rm d}\theta.

(4.6)

Concerning about the regularity condition, since
t_{nM}=T_{n}
and the definition of
b_{i}(-\tau_{n}L_{h})
in (
2.9
), according to the Lagrangian interpolation theorem, we can derive

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}^{M}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{H}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})\|_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M-1}e^{-\Delta t(M-1-j)L_{h}}\Delta t\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}P_{h}f(T_{n}+(j+c_{i})\Delta t)\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\Delta t(1-\theta)L_{h}}l_{i}(\theta)\,{\rm d}\theta

\displaystyle-\Delta t\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M-1}e^{-(M-j-1)\Delta tL_{h}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\Delta t(1-\eta)L_{h}}P_{h}f(T_{n}+(j+\eta)\Delta t)\,{\rm d}\eta\Big\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Delta t\sup\limits_{0\leq j\leq M-1}\|e^{-(M-j-1)\Delta tL_{h}}\|_{0}\cdot\sup\limits_{0\leq\eta\leq 1}\|e^{-\Delta t(1-\eta)L_{h}}\|_{0}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M-1}\int_{0}^{\Delta t}\frac{M_{q}}{q!}w_{q}(\theta)\,{\rm d}\theta,

(4.7)

where
M_{q}=\sup\limits_{t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+T}|P_{h}f^{(q)}(t)|
and
w_{q}(\theta)=\Big|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{q}(\theta-c_{i})\Big|
is the Lagrange polynomial of order
q
. According to (
4.5
) in Lemma
4.1
, by direct manipulation, we can easily reformulate (
4.7
) to

\|\mathcal{F}^{M}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{H}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})\|_{0}\lesssim\Delta T(\Delta t)^{q}.

In conclusion, EIFE-linear method can fulfill the corresponding regularity condition, then we can know that EIFE-linear is proper to be applied to fine grids.

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
f(t)
fulfills Assumption
3.2
for some positive integer
p
for linear problem. Suppose that EIFE method is unconditionally stable and chosen to be applied to coarse grids with
p
different interpolation nodes
c_{1},\cdots,c_{p}\in[0,1]
. If
v(t)
,
w(t)
,
\xi(t)
are sufficiently smooth with respect to
t
, then

\begin{split}&\max\limits_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\|\mathcal{G}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{H}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})\|_{0}\lesssim(\Delta T)^{p+1},\\
&\|\mathcal{G}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{G}(w;T_{n},T_{n+1})\|_{0}-\|v-w\|_{0}\lesssim\Delta T\|v-w\|_{0},\\
&\|\mathcal{G}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{G}(w;T_{n},T_{n+1})+\mathcal{H}(w;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{H}(\xi;T_{n},T_{n+1})\|_{0}\\
&-\|v-\xi\|_{0}\lesssim\Delta T\|v-\xi\|_{0}+(\Delta T)^{p+1}\|w-\xi\|_{0}\end{split}

are fulfilled and the hidden constants are all independent of
\Delta T,v,w,\xi
, which means that EIFE-linear method is proper to be applied to coarse grids.

Proof.

Following the similar arguments for deriving Theorem
4.1
, we can easily derive that the coarse propagator
\mathcal{G}
can fulfill the regularity condition when fixing
M=1
. So the corresponding details will be omitted.

Considering about the Lipschitz continuous condition (
4.3
), by using (
4.5
) in Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{G}(w;T_{n},T_{n+1})\|_{0}-\|v-w\|_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|e^{-\Delta TL_{h}}v+\Delta T\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}b_{i}(-\Delta TL_{h})P_{h}f(T_{n}+c_{i}\Delta T)-e^{-\Delta TL_{h}}w

\displaystyle-\Delta T\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}b_{i}(-\Delta TL_{h})P_{h}f(T_{n}+c_{i}\Delta T)\Big\|_{0}-\|v-w\|_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\|e^{-\Delta TL_{h}}\|_{0}-1)\|v-w\|_{0}.

(4.8)

As for the estimation of
\|e^{-\Delta TL_{h}}\|_{0}-1
, since
L_{h}
is symmetric and positive definite, according to Taylor expansion,

\begin{split}\|e^{-\Delta T_{n}L_{h}}\|_{0}-1&=\sup\limits_{\lambda\in\lambda(L_{h})}e^{-\Delta T\lambda}-1\\
&\leq e^{-\Delta T\alpha}-1\lesssim\Delta T.\end{split}

Therefore, the estimation of (
4.8
) becomes

\|\mathcal{G}(v;T_{n},T_{n+1})-\mathcal{G}(w;T_{n},T_{n+1})\|_{0}-\|v-w\|_{0}\lesssim\Delta T\|v-w\|_{0},

(4.9)

which implies that EIFE-linear method fulfills the Lipchitz continuous condition (
4.3
).

In the same way, the Lipschitz continuous condition (
4.4
) can also be easily derived. Therefore, we can conclude that EIFE-linear is proper to be applied to coarse grids.

∎

Remark 4.1
.

When choosing EIFE-linear scheme to both coarse grids and fine grids, it’s essential to balance the computational cost and numerical accuracy simultaneously. Generally speaking, we always select a less precise EIFE scheme as the coarse propagator
\mathcal{G}
while a more precise one as the fine propagator
\mathcal{F}
, which implies that the value of
p
is usually less than
q
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that conditions in Assumption
4.1
are all fulfilled. Then the error bound of Parareal algorithm can reach to the accuracy of applying
\mathcal{F}
to the fine grids, which implies that the numerical solution
\big\{U_{h}^{n,(k)}\big\}
produced by (
2.10
) satisfies

\big\|U_{h}^{n,(k)}-u_{h}(T_{n})\big\|_{0}\lesssim(\Delta t)^{q}

when
k\geq N
and the hidden constant is independent of
h
,
\Delta t
and
\Delta T
.

Proof.

Step 1: According to format of Parareal algorithm (see Algorithm
1
), on the one hand, when
k\geq 1
,

\begin{split}U_{h}^{n,(k)}-u_{h}(T_{n})&=\mathcal{G}(U_{h}^{n-1,(k)};T_{n-1},T_{n})+\mathcal{F}^{M}(U_{h}^{n-1,(k-1)};T_{n-1},T_{n})-\mathcal{G}(U_{h}^{n-1,(k-1)};T_{n-1},T_{n})\\
&-\mathcal{H}(u_{h}(T_{n-1});T_{n-1},T_{n})=:{\rm I}_{1}+{\rm I}_{2},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}{\rm I}_{1}&:=\mathcal{F}^{M}(U_{h}^{n-1,(k-1)};T_{n-1},T_{n})-\mathcal{H}(U_{h}^{n-1,(k-1)};T_{n-1},T_{n}),\\
{\rm I}_{2}&:=\mathcal{G}(U_{h}^{n-1,(k)};T_{n-1},T_{n})-\mathcal{G}(U_{h}^{n-1,(k-1)};T_{n-1},T_{n})+\mathcal{H}(U_{h}^{n-1,(k-1)};T_{n-1},T_{n})-\mathcal{H}(u_{h}(T_{n-1});T_{n-1},T_{n}).\end{split}

Then owing to the conditions (
4.1
) and (
4.4
) are fulfilled, we can derive that

\begin{split}&\big\|U_{h}^{n,(k)}-u_{h}(T_{n})\big\|_{0}-\big\|U_{h}^{n-1,(k)}-u_{h}(T_{n-1})\big\|_{0}\\
\lesssim&(\Delta T)^{p+1}\big\|U_{h}^{n-1,(k-1)}-u_{h}(T_{n-1})\big\|_{0}+\Delta T\big\|U_{h}^{n-1,(k)}-u_{h}(T_{n-1})\big\|_{0}+\Delta T(\Delta t)^{q},\quad k\geq 1.\end{split}

(4.10)

On the other hand, when
k=0
, then

\begin{split}U_{h}^{n,(0)}-u_{h}(T_{n})=&\mathcal{G}(U_{h}^{n-1,(0)};T_{n-1},T_{n})-\mathcal{H}(u_{h}(T_{n-1});T_{n-1},T_{n})\\
=&\Big(\mathcal{G}(U_{h}^{n-1,(0)};T_{n-1},T_{n})-\mathcal{G}(u_{h}(T_{n-1});T_{n-1},T_{n})\Big)\\
&+\Big(\mathcal{G}(u_{h}(T_{n-1});T_{n-1},T_{n})-\mathcal{H}(u_{h}(T_{n-1});T_{n-1},T_{n})\Big).\end{split}

Therefore, noting the conditions (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we can derive that

\begin{split}\big\|U_{h}^{n,(0)}-u_{h}(T_{n})\big\|_{0}-\big\|U_{h}^{n-1,(0)}-u_{h}(T_{n-1})\big\|_{0}\lesssim\Delta T\big\|U_{h}^{n-1,(0)}-u_{h}(T_{n-1})\big\|_{0}+(\Delta T)^{p+1}.\end{split}

(4.11)

Define

\begin{split}\alpha-1\eqsim\Delta T,\quad\beta\eqsim(\Delta T)^{p+1},\quad\gamma\eqsim\Delta T(\Delta t)^{q},\quad\delta\eqsim(\Delta T)^{p+1},\end{split}

where the hidden constants are positive and independent of
\Delta t,\Delta T
. Denote
\tilde{e}_{n}^{(k)}=\big\|U_{h}^{n,(k)}-u_{h}(T_{n})\big\|_{0}
, then (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) can be reformulated as:

\left\{\begin{split}\tilde{e}_{n}^{(k)}&\leq\alpha\tilde{e}_{n-1}^{(k)}+\beta\tilde{e}_{n-1}^{(k-1)}+\gamma,\\
\tilde{e}_{n}^{(0)}&\leq\alpha\tilde{e}_{n-1}^{(0)}+\delta.\end{split}\right.

(4.12)

Step 2: Let
e_{n}^{(k)}
as intermediate variables for comparison, which satisfy

\left\{\begin{split}e_{n}^{(k)}&=\alpha e_{n-1}^{(k)}+\beta e_{n-1}^{(k-1)}+\gamma,\\
e_{n}^{(0)}&=\alpha e_{n-1}^{(0)}+\delta.\end{split}\right.

(4.13)

Define
\rho^{(k)}(\tau)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}e_{n}^{(k)}\tau^{n}
with
\tau
sufficiently small. Since
e_{0}^{(k)}=\tilde{e}_{0}^{(k)}=0
, (
4.13
) can be converted to

\left\{\begin{split}\rho^{(k)}(\tau)&=\alpha\tau\rho^{(k)}(\tau)+\beta\tau\rho^{(k-1)}(\tau)+\frac{\gamma\tau}{1-\tau},\\
\rho^{(0)}(\tau)&=\alpha\tau\rho^{(0)}(\tau)+\frac{\delta\tau}{1-\tau}.\end{split}\right.

After simple manipulations, we can obtain that

\left\{\begin{split}&\rho^{(0)}(\tau)=\frac{\delta\tau}{(1-\alpha\tau)(1-\tau)},\\
&\rho^{(k)}(\tau)=\Big(\frac{\beta\tau}{1-\alpha\tau}\Big)^{k}\rho^{(0)}(\tau)+\Big(1-\Big(\frac{\beta\tau}{1-\alpha\tau}\Big)^{k}\Big)\frac{\gamma\tau}{(1-\tau)(1-(\alpha+\beta)\tau)}.\end{split}\right.

Since
\alpha>1
,
\alpha+\beta>1
and
\tau>0
is sufficiently small, after direct manipulation, we can derive that

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma\tau}{(1-\tau)(1-(\alpha+\beta)\tau)}\Big(1-\Big(\frac{\beta\tau}{1-\alpha\tau}\Big)^{k}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma\tau}{(1-(\alpha+\beta)\tau)^{2}},

(4.14a)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\beta\tau}{1-\alpha\tau}\Big)^{k}\rho^{(0)}(\tau)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta^{k}\delta\tau^{k+1}}{(1-\alpha\tau)^{k+2}}.

(4.14b)

Following the equality (
4.14a
)-(
4.14b
), we can derive that

\begin{split}\rho^{(k)}(\tau)\leq\frac{\beta^{k}\delta\tau^{k+1}}{(1-\alpha\tau)^{k+2}}+\frac{\gamma\tau}{(1-(\alpha+\beta)\tau)^{2}}.\end{split}

(4.15)

According to Taylor expansion, then (
4.15
) is transformed as

\begin{split}\rho^{(k)}(\tau)&=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}e_{n}^{(k)}\tau^{n}\leq\beta^{k}\delta\tau^{k+1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}C_{k+1+j}^{j}\alpha^{j}\tau^{j}+\gamma\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}C_{1+j}^{j}(\alpha+\beta)^{j}\tau^{j+1}.\end{split}

Therefore, if
k\geq n
, we can obtain that

e_{n}^{(k)}\leq C_{n}^{n-1}(\alpha+\beta)^{n-1}\gamma.

It’s obvious that
\tilde{e}_{n}^{(k)}\leq e_{n}^{(k)}
, thus
\tilde{e}_{n}^{(k)}\leq C_{n}^{n-1}(\alpha+\beta)^{n-1}\gamma
.

Especially, if choosing
n=N
, which is
k\geq N
, then

\begin{split}\tilde{e}_{n}^{(k)}&\leq C_{n}^{n-1}(\alpha+\beta)^{n-1}\gamma\\
&\lesssim n(1+C\Delta T)^{n-1}\Delta T(\Delta t)^{q}\\
&\lesssim(\Delta t)^{q}.\end{split}

Therefore, when
N
is fixed, the error bound of coarse grids can achieve the accuracy of fine grids in finite steps.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

In reality, for solving large-scale problems, Parareal method can achieve great accuracy when the iteration times is much smaller than the number of processors, i.e.
k\ll N
. Moreover, the number of processors can not be too large, since the cost of communication and sequential correction will increase with the number of processors.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that the function
f
satisfies Assumption
3.2
and the exact solution
u(t)
satisfies the regularity condition (
3.2a
) and (
3.2b
) in Assumption
3.1
. Assume that EIFE-linear method is chosen to both coarse grids and fine grids with
p
and
q
different interpolation nodes in
[0,1]
respectively (
p\leq q,q\geq 1
). Then the numerical solution
\{U_{h}^{n,(k)}\}
produced by PEIFE-linear method satisfies

\big\|u(T_{n})-U_{h}^{n,(k)}\big\|_{0}\lesssim(\Delta t)^{q}+h^{2},

(4.16)

for
k\geq N
, and the hidden constant is independent of
h
and
\Delta t
.

With the help of triangle inequality, the above conclusion can be immediately obtained by combining the results of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
4.3
.

Remark 4.3
.

We can reformulate the model problem (
1.1

6
Conclusions

For solving a class of linear parabolic equations taking the form (
1.1
) in regular domains, we have mainly proposed the PEIFE-linear method, which is a modified EIFE-linear method in the parallel-in-time pattern with significant speedup. The proposed PEIFE-linear method is obtained by using finite element method for spatial discretization and then linear exponential Runge-Kutta approximation accompanied with Parareal method for temporal integration. The implementation of PEIFE-linear method allows FFT-based fast calculation techniques and tensor product spectral decomposition. We have derived the explicit error estimates in
L^{2}
-norm for the PEIFE-linear method after finite iterations. Some numerical examples are also presented to demonstrate the accuracy and high efficiency of the proposed method. Rigorous error analysis of the PEIFE-linear method in
H^{1}
or
L^{\infty}
norm and for the model problem still remains to be explored.

Abstract
. We first obtain a simpler proof of the main results in [IEOT, 93 (2021), 17], which characterized the bounded and compact differences C u , φ − C v , ψ C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi} of two weighted composition operators acting from A α p ​ ( 𝔻 ) A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D}) to A α q ​ ( 𝔻 ) A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D}) . Then we get some characterizations for the difference of weighted composition operator belonging to Schatten class. Moreover, compact difference of a weighted composition operator and a unweighted composition operator on Hardy space is also studied. It shows a rigidity, i.e. C u , φ − C ψ C_{u,\varphi}-C_{\psi} is compact on H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) H^{2}(\mathbb{D}) if and only if both C u ′ , φ : H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) → A 1 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) C_{u^{\prime},\varphi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{D}) and C u ​ φ ′ , φ − C ψ ′ , ψ : A 1 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) → A 1 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) C_{u\varphi^{\prime},\varphi}-C_{\psi^{\prime},\psi}:A_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{D}) are compact, which generalize a result in [JFA, 278 (2020), 108401].

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
be the open unit disc in the complex plane
\mathbb{D}
and
H(\mathbb{D})
be the space of all analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. For
\alpha>-1
and
0<p<\infty
, the standard weighted Bergman space
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is defined by

A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})=\left\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(z)<\infty\right\}.

Here
dA_{\alpha}(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)
, and
dA=\frac{1}{\pi}dxdy
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{D}
. It is known that
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is a Banach space for
1\leq p<\infty
. In particular,
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is a Hilbert space with the following inner product

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}dA_{\alpha}(z),\quad f,g\in A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

The Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is defined by

H^{p}(\mathbb{D})=\left\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}=\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta<\infty\right\}.

When
p=2
, Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
can be viewed as the limiting space of
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
as
\alpha\to-1^{+}
. And the well-known Littlewood-Paley Identity asserts that the
H^{2}
-norm can be converted to an area integral:

(1.1)

\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\simeq|f(0)|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})dA(z).

The theory of analytic function spaces and operators acting on them, especially weighted composition operators, has witnessed an explosive growth in the past several decades. Denoted by
S(\mathbb{D})
the set of all analytic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
. Given
u\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
\varphi\in S(\mathbb{D})
, the weighted composition operators
C_{u,\varphi}
on
H(\mathbb{D})
is defined by

C_{u,\varphi}f=u\cdot f\circ\varphi.

It is known that weighted composition operators are closely related to the isometries on Hardy and weighted Bergman spaces, see for example
[
13
,
14
]
. When
u=1
, it reduces to the composition operator
C_{\varphi}
. One can refer to standard references
[
9
]
and
[
23
]
for various aspects on the theory of composition operators and refer to
[
10
,
11
]
for the study of weighted composition operators on weighted Bergman spaces.

In the course of research on the topological structure of the space of all composition operators, which was initiated by Berkson
[
2
]
and then by Shapiro and Sundberg
[
24
]
in more details, problems of characterizing the path components and compact differences of two composition operators attracted broad interest. On Bergman spaces, the effort to characterize compact difference of two composition operators was initiated by Moorhouse
[
19
]
on
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and then by Saukko
[
21
,
22
]
from
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
to
A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
. By using Joint-Carleson measures, Koo and Wang
[
15
]
studied the bounded and compact difference
C_{\varphi}-C_{\psi}
on
A_{\alpha}^{p}
over the unit ball. For more results about the difference of composition operators on various settings, see
[
7
,
8
,
26
]
and references therein.

Acharyya and Wu
[
1
]
first considered compact differences of two weighted composition operators
C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}
between weighted Bergman spaces with the weights
u,v
satisfying a certain growth condition. Recently, Choe et al.
[
4
]
obtained complete characterizations for bounded and compact difference
C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}
on
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
under an extra condition, i.e.
u,v\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
. And then, they
[
5
]
extended their results to operators acting from
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
to
A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
under a condition that
u,v\in A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
.

To state the main results in
[
5
]
, we first introduce several notations. For
\varphi,\psi\in S(\mathbb{D})
, put

\rho(z)=d(\varphi(z),\psi(z)),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where
d
denotes the pseudo-hyperbolic distance on
\mathbb{D}
, see Section 2.1. Given a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
and
\varphi\in S(\mathbb{D})
, the pull-back measure
\mu\circ\varphi^{-1}
in defined by

(\mu\circ\varphi^{-1})(E)=\mu(\varphi^{-1}(E))

for any Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{D}
. For
u,v\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
\varphi,\psi\in S(\mathbb{D})
and
\beta>0
, we now define several pull-back measures as follows:

\omega_{\varphi,u}^{\alpha,q}:=(|\rho u|^{q}dA_{\alpha})\circ\varphi^{-1},\quad\omega_{\psi,v}^{\alpha,q}:=(|\rho v|^{q}dA_{\alpha})\circ\psi^{-1},

\sigma_{\varphi,u,v}^{\alpha,q,\beta}:=[(1-\rho)^{\beta}|u-v|^{q}dA_{\alpha}]\circ\varphi^{-1},\quad\sigma_{\psi,u,v}^{\alpha,q,\beta}:=[(1-\rho)^{\beta}|u-v|^{q}dA_{\alpha}]\circ\psi^{-1},

The results in
[
5
]
are described in terms of
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measures, see Section 2.3.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
5
]

Let
\alpha>-1
,
0<p\leq q<\infty
and
\beta>\frac{q}{p}(\alpha+2)
. Suppose
u,v\in A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
and
\varphi,\psi\in S(\mathbb{D})
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}:A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
is bounded (compact, resp.).

(ii)

\omega_{\varphi,u}^{\alpha,q}
,
\omega_{\psi,v}^{\alpha,q}
,
\sigma_{\varphi,u,v}^{\alpha,q,\beta}
and
\sigma_{\psi,u,v}^{\alpha,q,\beta}
are (compact, resp.)
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measures.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
5
]

Let
\alpha>-1
,
0<q<p<\infty
and
\beta>\frac{q}{p}(\alpha+1+\max\{1,p\})
. Suppose
u,v\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and
\varphi,\psi\in S(\mathbb{D})
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}:A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
is bounded;

(ii)

C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}:A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
is compact;

(iii)

\omega_{\varphi,u}^{\alpha,q}
,
\omega_{\psi,v}^{\alpha,q}
,
\sigma_{\varphi,u,v}^{\alpha,q,\beta}
and
\sigma_{\psi,u,v}^{\alpha,q,\beta}
are
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measures.

In order to prove
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
in Theorem
1.1
and
(i)\Rightarrow(iii)
in Theorem
1.2
, which are the hardest steps, several technical lemmas were applied in
[
5
]
. See also
[
4
]
for the case
p=q
. To the best of our knowledge, most of the studies of the differences of weighted composition operators rely on these lemmas in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
, see for example
[
16
,
20
]
. Our first aim in this paper is to construct a new method and give a simpler proof for
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
in Theorem
1.1
and
(i)\Rightarrow(iii)
in Theorem
1.2
. Moreover, the condition
u,v\in A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
is not needed in our proof. And the proof does not rely on those technical lemmas. Quite recently, Choe et al.
[
3
]
extended their results to the ball setting and removed the
L^{q}
-condition of the weights
u,v
. We point out that our method here is different from
[
3
]
.

In addition to the bounded and compact differences of (weighted) composition operators, Choe et al.
[
6
]
characterized the Hilbert-Schmidt property of the difference
C_{\varphi}-C_{\psi}
on
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and showed that
C_{\varphi}
and
C_{\psi}
are in the same path component in the space of composition operators under the Hilbert-Schmidt norm if and only if
C_{\varphi}-C_{\psi}
is Hilbert-Schmidt. This result was then extended to the ball setting by Zhang and Zhou
[
27
]
. In
[
1
]
, Acharyya and Wu first characterized Hilbert-Schmidt difference of two weighted composition operators acting from Bergman space or Hardy space to an
L^{2}(\mu)
space. For
0<p<\infty
, we recall that a compact operator
T
acting on a separated Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
belongs to the Schatten class
S_{p}(\mathcal{H})
if its sequence of singular numbers belongs to the sequence space
l^{p}
(the singular numbers are the square roots of the eigenvalues of the positive operator
T^{*}T
).
T
is said to be Hilbert-Schmidt if
T\in S_{2}(\mathcal{H})
. One can refer to
[
29
, Chapter 1]
for some details about Schatten classes. As far as we know, there are few papers studying the Schatten-
p
class differences of weighted composition operators. So our second aim in this paper is to give some characterizations about Schatten-
p
class differences.

For a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
and
r>0
, denote
M_{r}(\mu)
be the weighted averaging function of
\mu
, see Section 2.4. Let

d\lambda(z)=\frac{dA(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}

be the M
{\rm\ddot{o}}
bius invariant measure on
\mathbb{D}
. Our main result states as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
\beta\geq 2(\alpha+2)
. Suppose
u,v\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
\varphi,\psi\in S(\mathbb{D})
. If
M_{r}(\omega_{\varphi,u}^{\alpha,2})
,
M_{r}(\omega_{\psi,v}^{\alpha,2})
,
M_{r}(\sigma_{\varphi,u,v}^{\alpha,2,\beta})
, and
M_{r}(\sigma_{\psi,u,v}^{\alpha,2,\beta})
belong to
L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)
for some (or any)
r>0
,
then
C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}\in S_{p}(A_{\alpha}^{2})
. Moreover, when
p\geq 2
, the above condition is also nesessary for
C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}\in S_{p}(A_{\alpha}^{2})
.

By (
1.1
), we know that
f\in H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
if and only if
f^{\prime}\in A_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Choe et al.
[
4
]
showed that
C_{\varphi}-C_{\psi}
is compact on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
if and only
C_{\varphi^{\prime},\varphi}-C_{\psi^{\prime},\psi}
is compact on
A_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, and obtained a compact criterion for the difference of two composition operators acting on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Inspired by this, we get a characterization for the compact difference of a weighted composition operator and a unweighted composition operator, i.e.
C_{u,\varphi}-C_{\psi}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
u\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
\varphi,\psi\in S(\mathbb{D})
.
C_{u,\varphi}-C_{\psi}
is compact on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if both
C_{u^{\prime},\varphi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
C_{u\varphi^{\prime},\varphi}-C_{\psi^{\prime},\psi}:A_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
are compact.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we collect some preliminary facts and auxiliary lemmas that will be used later. Section 3 is devoted to the new proof of
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
in Theorem
1.1
and
(i)\Rightarrow(iii)
in Theorem
1.2
. The proof of Theorem
1.3
is also presented in Section 3. Moreover, we obtain a Schatten-
p
class characterization for the difference
C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}
via Reproducing Kernel Thesis. In Section 4, we prove Theorem 1.4 and characterize the linear sums of two composition operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Throughout the paper we use the same letter
C
to denote positive constants which may vary at different occurrences but do not depend on the essential argument. For non-negative quantities
A
and
B
, we write
A\lesssim B
(or equivalent
B\gtrsim A
) if there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
.
A\simeq B
means both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some basic facts and present some auxiliary lemmas which will be used in the sequel.

2.1.
Pseudo-hyperbolic Distance

For any
z\in\mathbb{D}
, let
\varphi_{z}(w)=\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{z}w}
be the involutive automorphism that exchanges
0
and
z
. The pseudo-hyperbolic distance between
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
is given by

d(z,w)=\left|\varphi_{z}(w)\right|.

It is easy to check that

(2.1)

1-d^{2}(z,w)=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})(1-|w|^{2})}{|1-\overline{z}w|^{2}}.

Write
E(z,s)=\{w\in\mathbb{D}:d(z,w)<s\}
for the pseudo-hyperbolic disk with center
z\in\mathbb{D}
and radius
s\in(0,1)
.

Given
s\in(0,1)
, it is well-known that

(2.2)

A_{\alpha}(E(z,s))\simeq(1-|z|^{2})^{2+\alpha},

and

(2.3)

1-|z|^{2}\simeq 1-|w|^{2}\simeq|1-\overline{z}w|

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
w\in E(z,s)
. Moreover,

(2.4)

|1-\overline{a}z|\simeq|1-\overline{a}w|

for all
a,z
and
w
in
\mathbb{D}
with
d(z,w)<s
. Here, all the constants suppressed in these estimates depend only on
s
.

The Bergman metric between
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
is given by

\beta(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+d(z,w)}{1-d(z,w)}.

Write
D(z,r)=\{w\in\mathbb{D}:\beta(z,w)<r\}
for the Bergman metric disk centered at
z
with radius
r>0
. Clearly,
E(z,s)=B(z,r)
for
s=\tanh(r)
.

The following lemma, which is to be used in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, can be found in
[
15
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
0<p<\infty
and
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
. Then there is a constant
C=C(\alpha,p,r_{1},r_{2})
such that

|f(z)-f(w)|^{p}\leq C\frac{d^{p}(z,w)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}\int_{D(z,r_{2})}|f(\zeta)|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(\zeta)

for any
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
with
\beta(z,w)<r_{1}

A sequence
\{a_{j}\}
in
\mathbb{D}
is called an
r
-lattice in the Bergman metric if the following conditions hold:

(i)

\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}D(a_{j},r)=\mathbb{D}
,

(ii)

\{D(a_{j},r/2)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
are pairwise disjoint.

For any
r>0
, the existence of
r
-lattice in the Bergman metric is ensured by
[
29
, Lemma 4.8]
. And according to
[
29
, Lemma 4.7]
, with hypotheses (i) and (ii), it is easy to check that

(iii)

there exists a positive integer
N_{0}
such that every point in
\mathbb{D}
belongs to at most
N_{0}
of the sets
D(a_{j},4r)
.

2.2.
Test Functions

Recall the following pointwise estimate for derivatives of functions in
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, see for example
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
0<p<\infty
,
r>0
and
n
be a non-negative integer. There exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,p,r,n)>0
such that

|f^{(n)}(z)|^{p}\leq\frac{C}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2+\alpha+np}}\int_{D(z,r)}|f(w)|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(w)

for all
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Note from Lemma
2.2
that each point evaluation of
n
th order:
f\mapsto f^{(n)}(z)
, is a continuous linear functional on
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Thus, when
p=2
, by the Riesz representation theorem in Hilbert space theory, to each
z\in\mathbb{D}
corresponds a unique function
K_{z}^{[n]}\in A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
such that

(2.5)

f^{(n)}(z)=\langle f,K_{z}^{[n]}\rangle,\quad\forall f\in A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

K_{z}^{[n]}
is called the reproducing kernel function in
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
at
z
of order
n
, whose explicit formula is known as

K_{z}(w)=K_{z}^{[0]}(w)=\frac{1}{(1-\overline{z}w)^{2+\alpha}},\quad w\in\mathbb{D},

and
K_{z}^{[n]}(w)=\frac{\partial^{n}K_{z}}{\partial\overline{z}^{n}}(w)
. It is easy to check that

(2.6)

\|K_{z}^{[n]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}^{2}=(K_{z}^{[n]})^{(n)}(z)\simeq\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2+\alpha+2n}}.

Let
N,i
be non-negative integers such that
N>\frac{2+\alpha}{p}
. For any
a\in\mathbb{D}
, let

F_{a,N}^{[i]}(z)=\frac{z^{i}}{(1-\overline{a}z)^{N+i}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

According to
[
29
, Lemma 4.26]
and Forelli-Rudin’s estimate, see
[
28
, Theorem 1.12]
, we have

\|F_{a,N}^{[i]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\simeq(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{2+\alpha}{p}-N-i}\quad{\rm and}\quad\|F_{a,N}^{[i]}\|_{H^{p}}\simeq(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}-N-i}.

Constants suppressed above are independent of
a
. We set

f_{a,N,p}^{[i]}(z)=(1-|a|^{2})^{N+i-\frac{2+\alpha}{p}}F_{a,N}^{[i]}(z)\quad{\rm and}\quad g_{a,N,p}^{[i]}(z)=(1-|a|^{2})^{N+i-\frac{1}{p}}F_{a,N}^{[i]}(z).

Then
\|f_{a,N,p}^{[i]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\simeq 1
,
\|g_{a,N,p}^{[i]}(z)\|_{H^{p}}\simeq 1
, and both
f_{a,N,p}^{[i]}
and
g_{a,N,p}^{[i]}
converge to 0 uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
as
|a|\to 1^{-}
.

Besides, we have the following result, which derives from
[
29
, Theorem 4.33]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{a_{j}\}
be an
r
-lattice in the Bergman metric. Suppose
\alpha>-1
,
0<p<\infty
and
N,i
be non-negative integers with
N>\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{p}\right\}+\frac{1+\alpha}{p}
. For any
\{c_{j}\}\in l^{p}
, let

H_{N,p}^{[i]}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{N+i-\frac{2+\alpha}{p}}z^{i}}{(1-\overline{a}_{j}z)^{N+i}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then
H_{N,p}^{[i]}\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and
\|H_{N,p}^{[i]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\lesssim\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{l^{p}}
.

2.3.
Compact operator and Schatten-
p
class

Given two complete metrizable topological vector spaces
X
and
Y
, a bounded linear operator
T:X\to Y
is said to be compact if the image of each bounded sequence in
X
has a convergent subsequence in
Y
.

The following compactness criterion derives from
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
, see also
[
9
, Proposition 3.11]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
0<p,q<\infty
. Let
T
be a linear combination of weighted composition operators and assume
T:A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
is bounded. Then
T:A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
is compact if and only if
\|Tf_{n}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{q}}\to 0
for any bounded sequence
\{f_{n}\}
in
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
that converges to 0 uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
.

For any
z\in\mathbb{D}
, let
k_{z}^{[n]}=K_{z}^{[n]}/\|K_{z}^{[n]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}
. Suppose
T
is a bounded linear operator on
A_{\alpha}^{2}
, we define a function

\widetilde{T}_{[n]}(z)=\langle Tk_{z}^{[n]},k_{z}^{[n]}\rangle,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

\widetilde{T}_{[n]}
is called the
n
th-Berezin transform of
T
. When
n=0
, it reduces to the classical Berezin transform.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
T
is a positive operator on
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. If
T
belongs to the trace class, then
\widetilde{T}_{[n]}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)
and
\|T\|_{S_{1}}\gtrsim\|\widetilde{T}_{[n]}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)}
.

Proof.

Fix an orthonormal basis
\{e_{j}\}
for
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Since
T
is positive, it belongs to the trace class if and only if

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle Te_{j},e_{j}\rangle<\infty.

On the other hand, let
S=\sqrt{T}
. By Fubini’s Theorem, the reproducing formula (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we have,

\begin{split}\|T\|_{S_{1}}&=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle Te_{j},e_{j}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|Se_{j}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}^{2}\\
&\gtrsim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|(Se_{j})^{(n)}(z)\right|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n}dA_{\alpha}(z)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle Se_{j},K_{z}^{[n]}\rangle^{2}\right)(1-|z|^{2})^{2n}dA_{\alpha}(z)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle e_{j},SK_{z}^{[n]}\rangle^{2}\right)(1-|z|^{2})^{2n}dA_{\alpha}(z)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\|SK_{z}^{[n]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n}dA_{\alpha}(z)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\langle TK_{z}^{[n]},K_{z}^{[n]}\rangle(1-|z|^{2})^{2n}dA_{\alpha}(z)\\
&\simeq\int_{\mathbb{D}}\langle Tk_{z}^{[n]},k_{z}^{[n]}\rangle d\lambda(z).\end{split}

Therefore,
\int_{\mathbb{D}}\widetilde{T}_{[n]}(z)d\lambda(z)<\infty
. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
T
is a positive operator on
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
1\leq p<\infty
. If
T\in S_{p}(A_{\alpha}^{2})
, then
\widetilde{T}_{[n]}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)
and
\|T\|_{S_{p}}\gtrsim\|\widetilde{T}_{[n]}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)}
.

Proof.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, by
[
29
, Proposition 1.31]
, we have

(\widetilde{T^{p}})_{[n]}(z)\geq\left(\widetilde{T}_{[n]}(z)\right)^{p},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Since
T
is positive,
T\in S_{p}
implies that
T^{p}
is in the trace class. So it follows from Lemma
2.5
that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\widetilde{T}_{[n]}(z)\right)^{p}d\lambda(z)\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}}(\widetilde{T^{p}})_{[n]}(z)d\lambda(z)\lesssim\|T^{p}\|_{S_{1}}=\|T\|_{S_{p}}<\infty.

This completes the proof.
∎

2.4.
Carleson Measure

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
. For
\alpha>-1
and
0<p\leq q<\infty
, we say that
\mu
is a
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}d\mu(z)\right)^{1/q}\leq C\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}

for all
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
. That is,
\mu
is a
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure if the embedding
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})\subset L^{q}(\mathbb{D},d\mu)
is continuous. If, in addition, the embedding
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})\subset L^{q}(\mathbb{D},d\mu)
is compact, then
\mu
is called a compact
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure. Note that
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measures are finite measures.

For
r>0
and
t>0
, we put

M_{r,t}(\mu)(z)=\frac{\mu(D(z,r))}{(1-|z|^{2})^{t(2+\alpha)}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}

for the weighted averaging function of
\mu
. When
t=1
, we put
M_{r}(\mu):=M_{r,1}(\mu)
for simplicity.

The geometric characterizations for
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measures have been known for some time, see
[
18
]
and we state the results as follows.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
and
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
.

(i)

If
0<p\leq q<\infty
, then
\mu
is a
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure if and only if

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}M_{r,\frac{q}{p}}(\mu)(z)<\infty

for some (or any)
r>0
, and
\mu
is a compact
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure if and only if

\lim_{|z|\to 1^{-}}M_{r,\frac{q}{p}}(\mu)(z)=0

for some (or any)
r>0
.

(ii)

If
0<q<p<\infty
, then
\mu
is a
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure if and only if
\mu
is a compact
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure if and only if

M_{r}(\mu)\in L^{\frac{p}{p-q}}(\mathbb{D},dA_{\alpha})

for some (or any)
r>0
.

When
p=q
, we just call
\mu
is a (compact) Bergman-Carleson measure for simplicity, since the characterizations for
(p,p)
-Bergman Carleson measures are independent of
p
.

Let
\mu
be a Bergman-Carleson measure and
r>0
. By Lemma
2.2
and Fubini’s Theorem, it is easy to see that

(2.7)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}d\mu(z)\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(w)|^{p}M_{r}(\mu)(w)dA_{\alpha}(w)

for
0<p<\infty
and any
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
.

Given a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
, the Toeplitz operator
T_{\mu}
acting on
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is the densely defined integral operator

T_{\mu}f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f(w)}{(1-z\overline{w})^{2+\alpha}}d\mu(w),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Clearly,
T_{\mu}
is well defined on
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, the space of all bounded analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
.

It is known that
T_{\mu}
is bounded (compact, resp.) on
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if
\mu
is a (compact, resp.) Bergman Carleson measure. And for
0<p<\infty
, according to
[
29
, Theorem 7.18]
, we know that
T_{\mu}
is in
S_{p}(A_{\alpha}^{2})
if and only if

M_{r}(\mu)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)

for some (or any)
r>0
and
\|T_{\mu}\|_{S_{p}}\simeq\|M_{r}(\mu)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose
\mu
is a compact Bergman-Carleson measure and
R>r>0
. Let
d\nu=M_{r}(\mu)dA_{\alpha}
. If
M_{R}(\mu)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)
, then
T_{\nu}
is in
S_{p}(A_{\alpha}^{2})
and
\|T_{\nu}\|_{S_{p}}\lesssim\|M_{R}(\mu)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)}
.

Proof.

By (
2.3
), it is easy to see that
M_{R-r}(\nu)\lesssim M_{R}(\mu)
. So
M_{R-r}(\nu)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)
, and then
T_{\nu}\in S_{p}(A_{\alpha}^{2})
.
∎

3.
C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}
between Bergman spaces

In this section, we present a new proof of
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
in Theorem
1.1
and
(i)\Rightarrow(iii)
in Theorem
1.2
. And then we present the proof of Theorem
1.3
, which characterize the Schatten class property of

.

Proof of
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
in Theorem 1.1.

Assume
p\leq q
and
C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}
is bounded from
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
to
A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
. Then

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})f_{a,N,p}^{[i]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{q}}<\infty,\quad i=0,1.

Fix
r>0
. By (
2.3
), we get

\begin{split}&\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})f_{a,N,p}^{[0]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{q}}^{q}\\
&\quad\geq\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(a,r))}\left|\frac{u(z)}{(1-\overline{a}\varphi(z))^{N}}-\frac{v(z)}{(1-\overline{a}\psi(z))^{N}}\right|^{q}(1-|a|^{2})^{(N-\frac{2+\alpha}{p})q}dA_{\alpha}(z)\\
&\quad\gtrsim\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(a,r))}\left|u(z)-v(z)\left(\frac{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right)^{N}\right|^{q}\frac{1}{(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{q}{p}(2+\alpha)}}dA_{\alpha}(z),\end{split}

and similarly,

\begin{split}&\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})f_{a,N,p}^{[1]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{q}}\\
&\quad\gtrsim\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(a,r))}\left|u(z)\varphi(z)-v(z)\psi(z)\left(\frac{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right)^{N+1}\right|^{q}\frac{1}{(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{q}{p}(2+\alpha)}}dA_{\alpha}(z).\end{split}

Therefore,

(3.1)

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(a,r))}\left|u(z)-v(z)\left(\frac{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right)^{N}\right|^{q}dA_{\alpha}(z)}{(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{q}{p}(2+\alpha)}}<\infty,

and

(3.2)

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(a,r))}\left|u(z)\varphi(z)-v(z)\psi(z)\left(\frac{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right)^{N+1}\right|^{q}dA_{\alpha}(z)}{(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{q}{p}(2+\alpha)}}<\infty.

Since
\left|\frac{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right|\leq 1
when
\varphi(z)\in D(a,r)
by (
2.3
), it follows from (
3.1
) that

(3.3)

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(a,r))}\left|u(z)\psi(z)\frac{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}-v(z)\psi(z)\left(\frac{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right)^{N+1}\right|^{q}dA_{\alpha}(z)}{(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{q}{p}(2+\alpha)}}<\infty.

Adding (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), and by the triangle inequality, we obtain

(3.4)

\begin{split}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(a,r))}|u(z)|^{q}\left|\frac{\varphi(z)-\psi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right|^{q}dA_{\alpha}(z)}{(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{q}{p}(2+\alpha)}}<\infty.\end{split}

Note that
|1-\overline{a}\psi(z)|\simeq|1-\overline{\varphi(z)}\psi(z)|
when
\varphi(z)\in D(a,r)
by (
2.4
). It follows from (
3.4
) that

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(a,r))}|u(z)|^{q}\rho(z)^{q}dA_{\alpha}(z)}{(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{q}{p}(2+\alpha)}}<\infty.

This shows that
\omega_{\varphi,u}^{\alpha,q}
is a
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure by Lemma
2.7
. Similarly,
\omega_{\psi,v}^{\alpha,q}
is also a
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure.

On the other hand, when
\varphi(z)\in D(a,r)
, we have

(3.5)

\begin{split}\begin{split}&|u(z)-v(z)|\left|\frac{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right|^{N}\\
&\quad\leq\left|u(z)-v(z)\left(\frac{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right)^{N}\right|+|u(z)|\left|1-\left(\frac{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right)^{N}\right|\\
&\quad\lesssim\left|u(z)-v(z)\left(\frac{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right)^{N}\right|+|u(z)|\left|\frac{\varphi(z)-\psi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right|.\end{split}\end{split}

And by (
2.1
), (
2.3
), (
2.4
), we have

(3.6)

\left|\frac{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right|\gtrsim\frac{(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})(1-|\psi(z)|^{2})}{|1-\overline{\varphi(z)}\psi(z)|^{2}}=1-\rho^{2}(z)

for
z\in\varphi^{-1}(D(a,r))
. So we combine (
3.1
) and (
3.4
) to obtain

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(a,r))}|u(z)-v(z)|^{q}(1-\rho(z))^{qN}dA_{\alpha}(z)}{(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{q}{p}(2+\alpha)}}<\infty.

This means that
\sigma_{\varphi,u,v}^{\alpha,q,\beta}
is a
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure for
\beta\geq qN>\frac{q}{p}(2+\alpha)
by Lemma
2.7
. Similarly,
\sigma_{\psi,u,v}^{\alpha,q,\beta}
is also a
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure.

Finally, the proof for the compactness part is just a modification. By Lemma
2.4
, one only needs to write “
\lim_{|a|\to 1}
” instead of “
\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}
” and write “
\to 0
” instead of “
<\infty
”, respectively. We omit the routine details.
∎

According to the proof of
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
in Theorem 1.1, we conclude the following corollary, which coincides with
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
0<p\leq q<\infty
and
N>\frac{2+\alpha}{p}
.
Suppose
u,v\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
\varphi,\psi\in S(\mathbb{D})
. Then

(i)

C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}:A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
is bounded if and only if

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})f_{a,N,p}^{[i]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{q}}<\infty,\quad i\in\mathbb{N}.

(ii)

C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}:A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
is compact if and only if

\lim_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})f_{a,N,p}^{[i]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{q}}=0,\quad i\in\mathbb{N}.

Now we proceed to the proof of
(i)\Rightarrow(iii)
in Theorem
1.2
. Before that, we recall some basic facts about the Khinchine’s inequality, which is an important tool in complex and functional analysis. An introduction to this topic can be found in Appendix of
[
12
]
.

Let
\{r_{j}(t)\}
denote the sequence of Rademacher functions defined by

r_{0}(t)=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if}\ 0\leq t-[t]<\frac{1}{2}\\
-1,&\mbox{if}\ \frac{1}{2}\leq t-[t]<1,\end{cases}

where
[t]
denotes the largest integer not greater than
t
and
r_{k}(t)=r_{0}(2^{k}t)
for
k=1,2,\cdots
. If
0<p<\infty
, then Khinchine’s inequality states that

\left(\sum_{j}|c_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\simeq\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{j}c_{j}r_{j}(t)\right|^{p}dt.

for complex sequence
\{c_{j}\}
.

Proor of
(i)\Rightarrow(iii)
in Theorem 1.2.

Assume
0<q<p<\infty
and
C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}:A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
is bounded. Fix
r>0
, let
\{a_{j}\}
be an
r
-lattice in the Bergman metric and
\{c_{j}\}\in l^{p}
. For
N>\max\{1,\frac{1}{p}\}+\frac{\alpha+1}{p}
, we use the test functions
H_{N,p}^{[i]}
defined in Lemma
2.3
to obtain

\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})H_{N,p}^{[i]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{q}}\lesssim\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{l^{p}},\quad i=0,1.

For
i=0
, we obtain

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}\left[\frac{u(z)}{(1-\overline{a}_{j}\varphi(z))^{N}}-\frac{v(z)}{(1-\overline{a}_{j}\psi(z))^{N}}\right](1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{N-\frac{2+\alpha}{p}}\right|^{q}dA_{\alpha}(z)\\
&\quad\lesssim\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{l^{p}}^{q}.\end{split}

In (
3.7
), we replace
c_{j}
by
r_{j}(t)c_{j}
, so that the right hand side does not change. Then we integrate both sides with respect to
t
from
0
to 1 to obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}r_{j}(t)\left[\frac{u(z)}{(1-\overline{a}_{j}\varphi(z))^{N}}-\frac{v(z)}{(1-\overline{a}_{j}\psi(z))^{N}}\right](1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{N-\frac{2+\alpha}{p}}\right|^{q}dA_{\alpha}(z)dt\\
&\quad\lesssim\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{l^{p}}^{q}.\end{split}

By Khinchine’s inequality and Fubini’s Theorem, we get

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|c_{j}|^{2}\left|\frac{u(z)}{(1-\overline{a}_{j}\varphi(z))^{N}}-\frac{v(z)}{(1-\overline{a}_{j}\psi(z))^{N}}\right|^{2}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2(N-\frac{2+\alpha}{p})}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}dA_{\alpha}(z)\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}r_{j}(t)\left[\frac{u(z)}{(1-\overline{a}_{j}\varphi(z))^{N}}-\frac{v(z)}{(1-\overline{a}_{j}\psi(z))^{N}}\right](1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{N-\frac{2+\alpha}{p}}\right|^{q}dtdA_{\alpha}(z)\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}r_{j}(t)\left[\frac{u(z)}{(1-\overline{a}_{j}\varphi(z))^{N}}-\frac{v(z)}{(1-\overline{a}_{j}\psi(z))^{N}}\right](1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{N-\frac{2+\alpha}{p}}\right|^{q}dA_{\alpha}(z)dt\\
&\lesssim\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{l^{p}}^{q}.\end{split}

By (
2.3
),
\chi_{D(a_{j},2r)}(\varphi(z))\lesssim\frac{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}_{j}\varphi(z)|}
. It follows that

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|c_{j}|^{2}\chi_{D(a_{j},2r)}(\varphi(z))\left|u(z)-v(z)\left(\frac{1-\overline{a}_{j}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}_{j}\psi(z)}\right)^{N}\right|^{2}\frac{1}{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{\frac{2}{p}(2+\alpha)}}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}dA_{\alpha}(z)\\
&\quad\lesssim\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{l^{p}}^{q}.\end{split}

Recall that there is a positive integer
N_{0}
such that each point

belongs to at most
N_{0}
of the disks

. Applying Minkowski’s inequality if

and H

(3.8)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(a_{j},2r))}\left|u(z)-v(z)\left(\frac{1-\overline{a}_{j}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}_{j}\psi(z)}\right)^{N}\right|^{q}dA_{\alpha}(z)}{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-q}}A_{\alpha}(D(a_{j},2r))<\infty.

Adding (

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(w,r))}|u(z)|^{q}\rho(z)^{q}dA_{\alpha}(z)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-q}}dA_{\alpha}(w)\\

This shows that

is a

-Bergman Carleson measure by Lemma

. Similarly,
\omega_{\psi,v}^{\alpha,q}
is also a
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure.

On the other hand, when
\varphi(z)\in D(a_{j},2r)
, by (

\begin{split}|u(z)-v(z)|(1-\rho(z))^{N}&\lesssim|u(z)-v(z)|\left|\frac{1-\overline{a}_{j}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}_{j}\psi(z)}\right|^{N}\\

So we combine (

3.11
) to obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(a_{j},2r))}|u(z)-v(z)|^{q}(1-\rho(z))^{qN}dA_{\alpha}(z)}{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-q}}A_{\alpha}(D(a_{j},2r))<\infty.

Then through a similar argument as (
3.12
), we get

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(w,r))}|u(z)-v(z)|^{q}(1-\rho(z))^{qN}dA_{\alpha}(z)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-q}}dA_{\alpha}(w)<\infty.

This implies that
\sigma_{\varphi,u,v}^{\alpha,q,\beta}
is a
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure for
\beta\geq qN>\frac{q}{p}(\alpha+1+\max\{1,p\})
by Lemma
2.7
. Similarly,
\sigma_{\psi,u,v}^{\alpha,q,\beta}
is a also
(p,q)
-Bergman Carleson measure. The proof is now complete.
∎

According to the proof of
(i)\Rightarrow(iii)
in Theorem 1.2, we conclude the following corollary.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
0<q<p<\infty
and
N>\max\{1,\frac{1}{p}\}+\frac{1+\alpha}{p}
. Suppose
u,v\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
\varphi,\psi\in S(\mathbb{D})
. Then
C_{\varphi}-C_{\psi}:A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{D})
is bounded if and only if it is compact, if and only if

\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})H_{N,p}^{[i]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{q}}\lesssim\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{l^{p}},\quad i\in\mathbb{N}

for any
\{c_{j}\}\in l^{p}
.

Finally, we present the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem 1.3.

Fix
r>0
, denote
G_{r}=\{z:\beta(z,w)<r\}
. For any
f\in A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, we have

\begin{split}\left\langle(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})^{*}(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})f,f\right\rangle&=\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}^{2}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{D}\backslash G_{r}}+\int_{G_{r}}|u\cdot f\circ\varphi-v\cdot f\circ\psi|^{2}dA_{\alpha}\\
&:=I(f)+II(f).\end{split}

Now we estimate each integral separately. For the integral
I(f)
, note that
\rho(z)\geq\tanh r
for
z\in D\backslash G_{r}
. So we obtain by (
2.7
)

(3.13)

\begin{split}I(f)&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}\backslash G_{r}}\left(|u\cdot f\circ\varphi|^{2}+|v\cdot f\circ\psi|^{2}\right)dA_{\alpha}\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(|f\circ\varphi|^{2}|u\rho|^{2}+|f\circ\psi|^{2}|v\rho|^{2}\right)dA_{\alpha}\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f|^{2}\left[M_{2r}(\omega_{\varphi,u}^{\alpha,2})+M_{2r}(\omega_{\psi,v}^{\alpha,2})\right]dA_{\alpha}.\end{split}

Meanwhile, clearly,

\begin{split}II(f)&\lesssim\int_{G_{r}}|u-v|^{2}(|f\circ\varphi|^{2}+|f\circ\psi|^{2})+(|u|^{2}+|v|^{2})|f\circ\varphi-f\circ\psi|^{2}dA_{\alpha}\\
&:=II_{1}(f)+II_{2}(f).\end{split}

For the integral
II_{1}(f)
, note that
1-\rho(z)\geq 1-\tanh(r)
for
z\in G_{r}
, so we obtain by (
2.7
)

(3.14)

\begin{split}II_{1}(f)&\lesssim\int_{G_{r}}|u-v|^{2}(1-\rho)^{\beta}(|f\circ\varphi|^{2}+|f\circ\psi|^{2})dA_{\alpha}\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f|^{p}\left[M_{2r}(\sigma_{\varphi,u,v}^{\alpha,2,\beta})+M_{2r}(\sigma_{\psi,u,v}^{\alpha,2,\beta})\right]dA_{\alpha}.\end{split}

For the integral
II_{2}(f)
, applying Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

|f(\varphi(z))-f(\psi(z))|^{2}\lesssim\frac{\rho(z)^{2}}{(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}\int_{D(\varphi(z),2r)}|f(w)|^{2}dA_{\alpha}(w),

and

|f(\varphi(z))-f(\psi(z))|^{2}\lesssim\frac{\rho(z)^{2}}{(1-|\psi(z)|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}\int_{D(\psi(z),2r)}|f(w)|^{2}dA_{\alpha}(w)

for
z\in G_{r}
. So we use Fubini’s Theorem to obtain

(3.15)

\begin{split}II_{2}(f)&\lesssim\int_{G_{r}}\frac{|u(z)|^{2}\rho(z)^{2}}{(1-|\varphi(z)|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}\left(\int_{D(\varphi(z),2r)}|f(w)|^{2}dA_{\alpha}(w)\right)dA_{\alpha}(z)\\
&\quad+\int_{G_{r}}\frac{|v(z)|^{2}\rho(z)^{2}}{(1-|\psi(z)|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}\left(\int_{D(\psi(z),2r)}|f(w)|^{2}dA_{\alpha}(w)\right)dA_{\alpha}(z)\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f|^{2}\left[M_{2r}(\omega_{\varphi,u}^{\alpha,2})+M_{2r}(\omega_{\psi,v}^{\alpha,2})\right]dA_{\alpha}.\end{split}

Let

d\mu=\left(M_{2r}(\omega_{\varphi,u}^{\alpha,2})+M_{2r}(\omega_{\psi,v}^{\alpha,2})+M_{2r}(\sigma_{\varphi,u,v}^{\alpha,2,\beta})+M_{2r}(\sigma_{\psi,u,v}^{\alpha,2,\beta})\right)dA_{\alpha}.

Combining (
3.13
), (
3.14
) and (
3.15
), we obtain

(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})^{*}(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})\lesssim T_{\mu}.

By Lemma
2.8
, the assumption implies that
T_{\mu}\in S_{\frac{p}{2}}(A_{\alpha}^{2})
. It follows that
(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})^{*}(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})\in S_{\frac{p}{2}}(A_{\alpha}^{2})
, and then
C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}\in S_{p}(A_{\alpha}^{2})
.

Now we assume
C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi}\in S_{p}(A_{\alpha}^{2})
and
p\geq 2
. Then
(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})^{*}(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})\in S_{\frac{p}{2}}(A_{\alpha}^{2})
. And it follows from Lemma
2.5
that the function

z\mapsto\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})k_{z}^{[n]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}^{2}

belongs to
L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)
.

For any
r>0
, by (
2.3
) and (
2.6
), we have

(3.16)

\begin{split}&\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})k_{z}^{[0]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad\gtrsim\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(z,r))}\left|\frac{u(w)}{(1-\overline{z}\varphi(w))^{2+\alpha}}-\frac{v(w)}{(1-\overline{z}\psi(w))^{2+\alpha}}\right|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2+\alpha}dA_{\alpha}(w)\\
&\quad\gtrsim\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(z,r))}\left|u(w)-v(w)\frac{(1-\overline{z}\varphi(w))^{2+\alpha}}{(1-\overline{z}\psi(w))^{2+\alpha}}\right|^{2}dA_{\alpha}(w)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}.\end{split}

Similarly,

(3.17)

\begin{split}&\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})k_{z}^{[1]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad\gtrsim\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(z,r))}\left|u(z)\varphi(w)-v(w)\psi(w)\frac{(1-\overline{z}\varphi(w))^{3+\alpha}}{(1-\overline{z}\psi(w))^{3+\alpha}}\right|^{2}dA_{\alpha}(w)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}.\end{split}

Since
\left|\frac{1-\overline{z}\varphi(w)}{1-\overline{z}\psi(w)}\right|\lesssim 1
when
w\in\varphi^{-1}(D(z,r))
. It follows from (
3.16
) that

(3.18)

\begin{split}&\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})k_{z}^{[0]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad\gtrsim\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(z,r))}\left|u(w)\psi(w)\frac{1-\overline{z}\varphi(w)}{1-\overline{z}\psi(w)}-v(w)\psi(w)\frac{(1-\overline{z}\varphi(w))^{3+\alpha}}{(1-\overline{z}\psi(w))^{3+\alpha}}\right|^{2}dA_{\alpha}(w)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}.\end{split}

Adding (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), and using (
2.4
), we obtain

(3.19)

\begin{split}&\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})k_{z}^{[0]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}^{2}+\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})k_{z}^{[1]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad\gtrsim\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(z,r))}|u(w)|^{2}\rho(w)^{2}dA_{\alpha}(w)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}=M_{r}(\omega_{\varphi,u}^{\alpha,2})(z).\end{split}

Thus
M_{r}(\omega_{\varphi,u}^{\alpha,2})\in L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)
. Similarly,
M_{r}(\omega_{\psi,v}^{\alpha,2})\in L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)
.

On the other hand, when
\varphi(w)\in D(z,r)
, by (
2.4
), we have

\begin{split}\left|1-\frac{(1-\overline{z}\varphi(w))^{\alpha+2}}{(1-\overline{z}\psi(w))^{\alpha+2}}\right|&\lesssim\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\frac{|1-\overline{z}(t\varphi(w)+(1-t)\psi(w))|^{\alpha+1}}{|1-\overline{z}\psi(w)|^{\alpha+1}}\frac{|\varphi(w)-\psi(w)|}{|1-\overline{z}\psi(w)|}\\
&\lesssim\rho(z,w).\end{split}

It follows that when
\varphi(z)\in D(z,r)
,

\begin{split}&|u(w)-v(w)|(1-\rho(w))^{2+\alpha}\\
&\quad\lesssim|u(w)-v(w)|\left|\frac{1-\overline{z}\varphi(w)}{1-\overline{z}\psi(w)}\right|^{\alpha+2}\\
&\quad\leq\left|u(w)-v(w)\frac{(1-\overline{z}\varphi(w))^{2+\alpha}}{(1-\overline{z}\psi(w))^{2+\alpha}}\right|+|u(w)|\left|1-\frac{(1-\overline{z}\varphi(w))^{2+\alpha}}{(1-\overline{z}\psi(w))^{2+\alpha}}\right|\\
&\quad\lesssim\left|u(w)-v(w)\frac{(1-\overline{z}\varphi(w))^{2+\alpha}}{(1-\overline{z}\psi(w))^{2+\alpha}}\right|+|u(w)|\rho(w).\end{split}

So when
\beta\geq 2(\alpha+2)
we combine (
3.16
) and (

) to obtain

\begin{split}&\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})k_{z}^{[0]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}^{2}+\|(C_{u,\varphi}-C_{v,\psi})k_{z}^{[1]}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad\gtrsim\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(z,r))}|u(w)-v(w)|^{2}(1-\rho(w))^{2(2+\alpha)}dA_{\alpha}(w)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2+\alpha}}\geq M_{r}(\sigma_{\varphi,u,v}^{\alpha,2,\beta})(z).\end{split}

Thus,
M_{r}(\sigma_{\varphi,u,v}^{\alpha,2,\beta})\in L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)
. Similarly,
M_{r}(\sigma_{\psi,u,v}^{\alpha,2,\beta})\in L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)
. The proof is complete.
∎

(4.6)

\begin{split}&\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\overline{a}\psi(z))^{N+3}}\frac{\psi(z)-\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\times\left(2+N\overline{a}\frac{\psi(z)-\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}\right)\right|^{2}(1-|a|^{2})^{2(N+2)-1}dA_{1}(z)=0.\end{split}

Since (
4.6
) also holds replacing
N
by
N+1
, we get

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\overline{a}\psi(z))^{N+4}}\frac{\psi(z)-\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\times\left(2+(N+1)\overline{a}\frac{\psi(z)-\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}\right)\right|^{2}(1-|a|^{2})^{2(N+3)-1}dA_{1}(z)=0.\end{split}

Multiplying the integrand in (
4.6
) by
\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}\psi(z)|^{2}}
and adding it to (
4.7
), by the triangle inequality, we obtain

(4.8)

\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\overline{a}\psi(z))^{N+4}}\right|^{2}\left|\frac{\psi(z)-\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\psi(z)}\right|^{4}(1-|a|^{2})^{2(N+3)-1}dA_{1}(z)=0.

Then we also have by (
4.7
)

(4.9)

\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\overline{a}\psi(z))^{N+4}}\right|^{2}\left|\frac{\psi(z)-\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{a}\varphi(z)}\right|^{2}(1-|a|^{2})^{2(N+3)-1}dA_{1}(z)=0.

Since (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) also hold replacing
N
by
N+2
, combining (
4.9
) with (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), respectively, we obtain

(4.10)

\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\overline{a}\psi(z))^{N+4}}-\frac{u(z)\varphi^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\overline{a}\varphi(z))^{N+4}}\right|^{2}(1-|a|^{2})^{2(N+3)-1}dA_{1}(z)=0,

and

(4.11)

\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{\psi^{\prime}(z)\psi(z)}{(1-\overline{a}\psi(z))^{N+5}}-\frac{u(z)\varphi^{\prime}(z)\varphi(z)}{(1-\overline{a}\varphi(z))^{N+5}}\right|^{2}(1-|a|^{2})^{2(N+4)-1}dA_{1}(z)=0.

By Corollary
3.1
, equations (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) imply that
C_{u\varphi^{\prime},\varphi}-C_{\psi^{\prime},\psi}
is compact on
A_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

On the other hand, combining (
4.10
) and (
4.1
), we get

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{u^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\overline{a}\varphi(z))^{N+3}}\right|^{2}(1-|a|^{2})^{2(N+3)-1}dA_{1}(z)=0.

This shows that
(|u^{\prime}|^{2}dA_{1})\circ\varphi^{-1}
is a compact Hardy-Carleson measure by Equation (1.1) in
[
25
]
. It follows that
C_{u^{\prime},\varphi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\to A_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is compact. The proof is now complete.
∎

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
\varphi,\psi\in S(\mathbb{D})
be of bounded valence. Suppose
C_{\varphi}
and
C_{\psi}
are not compact on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Then
aC_{\varphi}+bC_{\psi}
is compact on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if
a+b=0
and
C_{\varphi}-C_{\psi}
is compact on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

The sufficiency is trivial. We only need to prove the necessity.

Assume
aC_{\varphi}+bC_{\psi}
is compact on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Then
aC_{\varphi^{\prime},\varphi}-bC_{\psi^{\prime},\psi}
is compact on
A_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Let
r>0
and
\beta>3
. By Theorem
1.2
, we obtain

(4.12)

\lim_{|w|\to 1}\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}D(w,r)}\rho(z)^{2}|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA_{1}(z)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{3}}=0,

and

(4.13)

\lim_{|w|\to 1}\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(w,r))}(1-\rho(z))^{\beta}|a\varphi^{\prime}(z)+b\psi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA_{1}(z)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{3}}=0.

Recall
G_{\delta}=\{z:\rho(z)<\delta\}
. By the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 1.3]
, if
\varphi
and
\psi
are of bounded valence, then

(4.14)

\lim_{|w|\to 1}\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(w,r))\cap G_{\delta}}|\varphi^{\prime}(z)-\psi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA_{1}(z)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{3}}=0.

Note that
1-\rho(z)\geq 1-\delta
for
z\in G_{\delta}
. If
a+b\neq 0
, then by (
4.13
) and (
4.14
), we get

\lim_{|w|\to 1}\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(w,r))\cap G_{\delta}}|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA_{1}(z)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{3}}=0.

This, together with (
4.12
), implies that

\lim_{|w|\to 1}\frac{\int_{\varphi^{-1}(D(w,r))}|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA_{1}(z)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{3}}=0.

Then by Lemma
2.7
,
(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}dA_{1})\circ\varphi^{-1}
is a compact Bergman Carleson measure. It follows that
C_{\varphi^{\prime},\varphi}
is compact on
A_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Then
C_{\varphi}
is compact on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, which is a contradiction. So we have
a+b=0
, and
C_{\varphi}-C_{\psi}
is compact on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.
∎

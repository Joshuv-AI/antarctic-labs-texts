Dysregulated KMT2D mutant targeting immunogenic rewiring to overcome epigenetic entropy promotes follicular lymphomagenesis
Authors: Yuqian Zhu, Jingwei Yao, Yuelong Jiang, Qian Lai, Qinwei Chen, Jingxing Zhang, Manman Deng, Zhijuan Lin, Yiming Luo, Zhifeng Li, Long Liu, Zhong Zheng, Jie Zha, Bing Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEpigenetic modifier factors in B cell lymphomas often harbor loss-of-function mutations. Mutations occurring in the histone-lysine methyltransferase 2D (KMT2D) gene are the pivotal chromatin-modifying hallmarks of follicular lymphoma (FL) subsets, however their tumor-promoting roles are unclear.MethodsTargeted sequencing was employed to evaluate a core panel of genes implicated in histone methylation (EZH2, KMT2D), histone acetylation (CREBBP, EP300), and chromatin remodeling (ARID1A, BCL7A) in FL patients. Functional characterization of the KMT2D mutation (KMT2Dmut) was carried out in vitro. RNA sequencing analysis was utilized to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and significantly enriched pathways. The role of aldehyde dehydrogenase 1 family member A1 (ALDH1A1) in mediating the function of the KMT2Dmut was investigated in B-lymphoma cell models using lentivirus. Through transcriptome deconvolution, integrative analyses combining chromatin immunoprecipitation and genome-wide chromatin accessibility were performed in KMT2D-mutant cell lines. Additionally, chromatin immunoprecipitation, immunofluorescence assays, and immunoblotting analyses were conducted.ResultsKMT2D mutations were the most frequent genetic alterations in FL (63%), predominantly consisting of nonsense and frameshift variants. Compared with wild-type, KMT2D-mutant FL exhibited markedly reduced H3K9me3 levels and increased chromatin accessibility at distal regulatory regions. Functionally, KMT2Dmut promoted proliferation, suppressed apoptosis, and arrested cells in G2 phase. In FL, mutant KMT2D suppressed H3K9 methylation, leading to ALDH1A1 upregulation and the emergence of a highly plastic B‑cell state, followed by ATP7A overexpression, which ultimately reshaped the immune microenvironment.ConclusionsKMT2Dmut drives FL progression by relieving H3K9me3-mediated epigenetic modification, activating PDK1/SGK1 signaling and an ALDH1A1-driven stemness program, and upregulating ATP7A to rewire copper homeostasis and redox balance. Our findings delineate a central molecular network through which KMT2Dmut operates in FL and provide a mechanistic rationale for therapeutic targeting of KMT2D.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s40164-026-00829-6.Keywords: Follicular lymphoma, KMT2D, Epigenetic modifier, Histone methyltransferase, Immunosuppressive milieu

Introduction

Follicular lymphoma (FL) is a prototypical subset of the most common B-cell malignancies that originate from germinal center (GC) B cells, and is related to poor clinical outcomes due to the progression into diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) [1]. As the common indolent B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma with epigenetic alterations, FL is characterized by abnormal B-cell clonal expansion. Functional heterogeneity is a hallmark of tumor B cells, which frequently exhibit an intermediate phenotype bridging GC and memory B cells. This phenotypic continuum aligns with the established ontogenic trajectory of FL, wherein BCL2-positive memory B cells undergo repeated cycles of GC re-entry to drive malignant transformation [2]. Alterations in epigenetic modifiers for FL lead to extensive transcriptional changes and are conducive to maintaining the GC B cell states, rather than promoting B cell activation or plasma cell differentiation [3]. The initiation of FL is a concerted action of early tumor microenvironment (TME) remodeling and the acquisition of high plasticity in B-cell populations [4]. How the altered state of B cells in the GC triggers the diverse FL phenotype remains to be elucidated.

Notably, FL sufferers whose disease progresses within 24 months (POD24) have inferior outcomes [5, 6]. In addition to molecular heterogeneity, current genetic alterations of POD24 have also proven the involvement of the TME and specific genes for simplified genetic subtyping may influence clinical outcomes in FL progression [7–10]. Genetic profiling of the epigenetic landscape of chromatin-modifying genes has revealed that loss-of-function mutations in histone-lysine methyltransferase 2D (KMT2D) are selected for early during lymphomagenesis. Conditional Kmt2d ablation in animal models has demonstrated that KMT2D deficiency during B-cell development promotes tumorigenesis and impairs the homeostasis of B cells [11, 12]. Accumulating evidence of multiple mechanisms of immunosuppressive TME, including the aberrant feature of CD8 T cells, T regulatory cells (Tregs), and macrophage polarization toward M2 phenotype, has supported the idea that regulation of tumor-intrinsic signaling is crucial for driving lymphoma development [13, 14]. Particularly, tumor-induced Treg cell trafficking is reported in KMT2D mutation (KMT2Dmut) DLBCL, which induces immunosuppressive TME [15]. However, the mechanisms by which KMT2Dmut impairs epigenetic activity within the immunosuppressive milieu remain unexplored.

In this study, we elucidate the evolutionary relationship between KMT2Dmut signatures and epigenetical reprogramming in a histone methylation-dependent manner. Next, we demonstrate the underlying mechanism by which an evolving niche and inflammatory cytokine perturbation affect cuproptosis regulatory circuitry. Our findings thus provide evidence that aldehyde dehydrogenase 1 family member A1 (ALDH1A1) functions as a new epigenetic therapeutic target in KMT2Dmut-driven FL.

Materials and methods

Patients and samples

A total of 48 subjects confirmed with FL and 31 sufferers confirmed with DLBCL in our department from 2022 to 2025 were included in this study. Pathological evaluation was performed in accordance with the 2016 World Health Organization classification criteria [16]. Among the analyses, based on the Follicular Lymphoma International Prognostic Index (FLIPI and FLIPI2), we identified a high-risk patient population at risk of recurrence or resistance to existing therapies. This research passed the hospital’s ethical review and strictly adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki, ensuring that all participants voluntarily signed informed consent forms after fully understanding the research details.

Targeted gene sequencing

Using the NovaSeq X plus Sequencer (manufactured by Illumina), we screened 363 specific gene variants from 38 patients, excluding 10 pairs of POD24 patients who underwent whole exome sequencing. Gene selection was performed by prioritizing those which: (i) were novel calls based on quality thresholds; (ii) have been prioritized as central to disease development; and (iii) are implicated in cancer progression. We customized PCR primers to identify abnormalities in chromatin modifying genes. Subsequently, PCR products were used for subsequent detection to construct a library, and DNA adapters were used to link and index library fragments. Following the manufacturer’s guidelines, the scattered parts were aggregated and fused into a set of units, thus achieving the construction of the repository.

Cell line and culture

All human B-lymphoma cell lines—RL, DOHH2, SU-DHL-4, SU-DHL-6, Karpas422, and SC-1—were procured from Cobioer Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Jiangsu, China) to maintain experimental consistency and reproducibility. The cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium containing 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (Gibco, CA, USA), 100 µg/ml streptomycin, and 100 IU/ml penicillin (Invitrogen). Cultures were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. Cell line identity and authenticity were verified by short tandem repeat (STR) profiling, and low-passage cells were used to maintain phenotypic stability.

Reagents and antibodies

RSL3 (S8155), zVAD-FMK (S7023), Ferrostatin-1 (S7243), Necrostatin-1 (S8037), Erastin (S7242), and DFOM (S5742) were obtained from Selleckchem (Houston, TX, USA). Cell Signaling Technology (Danvers, MA, USA) provided antibodies against H3K9me3 (#13969), anti-PI3K (#4292), anti-phospho-PI3K (#4228), phospho-PDK1 (#3061), ALDH1A1 (#54135), CDKN1A (#2947), CDKN1B (#3686), and SLC7A11 (#12691). Abcam (Cambridge, UK) provided anti-KMT2D (ab213721), anti-PDK1 (ab202468), anti-ATP7A (ab308524), anti-ATP7B (ab131208), anti-PD-L1 (ab205921), anti-ORF1p (ab230966), and anti-Cleaved Caspase 3 (ab32042) antibodies. Histone-H3 antibody was purchased from (17168, Proteintech, Chicago, IL, USA). Affinity Biosciences provided the anti-GAPDH (T0004), anti-AKT (AF6261), anti-phospho-AKT (AF0016), anti-Bcl-2 (AF6139), anti-Bax (AF0120), and HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. HuaAn Biotechnology (Hangzhou, China) provided anti-NLRP3 (ET1610-93), anti-SGK1 (ET1610-19), anti-ASC (ET1611-62) antibodies.

lentivirus infection assay

We employed a KMT2D ORF clone (NM_003482) and knocked out the endogenous KMT2D gene using sgRNA and Cas9 constructs. The R1252* mutant donor construct, obtained from Genechem, Inc. (Shanghai, China), was subsequently subcloned into a lentiviral vector. Target cells were transfected with the donor plasmid by means of a lipid‑based delivery system. To increase the transient transfection efficiency of lipid‑based reagents, the medium was replaced after a 4‑h incubation and cells were subjected to antibiotic selection based on the donor‑encoded resistance marker. Successfully transfected cells were enriched by downstream drug selection and flow cytometric analysis. To generate Cas9-sgRNA lentivirus, HEK293T cells were co-transfected with the U6-sgRNA-EF1α-Cas9-FLAG-CMV-EGFP-P2A-Puro vector and lentiviral packaging plasmids according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Genechem, Shanghai, China). Viral supernatants were harvested, filtered, and titrated. For transduction, target lymphoma cells (RL and SU-DHL-4) previously transfected with the donor template were infected with the packaged lentivirus at a multiplicity of infection (MOI = 20) in the presence of HitransG A reagent (Genechem) as an infection enhancer. The cells were cultured at 37 °C under 5% CO₂ for 16 h, after which the medium was refreshed with complete medium for an additional 3–4 days. A portion of the cells was then harvested and analyzed by flow cytometry to assess GFP reporter expression. During the clone screening, 4 g/ml of puromycin was applied to select the fluorescence efficiency of GFP, and the majority of cells exhibited GFP positive characteristics. The accuracy of the construct was confirmed through direct sequencing technology, serving to verify the chosen mutant clones. shRNA-ALDH1A1 included specified sequences targeting the ALDH1A1 sequence (5’-GCCAAATCATTCCTTGGAATT-3’; 5’-GCTCTCCACGTGGCATCTTTA-3’; 5’-GGGAGAGTACGGTTTCCATGA-3’). For ATP7A depletion, lentiviral vectors were produced with the following RNAi sequences: ATP7A-RNAi1, 5’-CCATTCATGTACTAGCACTAT-3’; and ATP7A-RNAi2, 5’-GCTCCCTAAACAGTGTTGTTA-3’. The control shRNA provided by GeneChem was used as a silencing negative control.

SA-β-Gal assay

Senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-Gal) activity was evaluated using a commercial kit from Beyotime. After collection and equilibration to room temperature, cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (15 min), followed by three PBS washes. A subsequent overnight incubation in freshly prepared SA-β-Gal staining solution was carried out at 37 °C. Senescence was quantified by counting the blue-stained, SA-β-Gal-positive cells from three random microscopic fields.

Cellular senescence activity assay

The assessment of cellular senescence activity was performed with the Cell Meter™ Cellular Senescence Activity Assay Kit (Red Fluorescence), following the manufacturer’s instructions. The detection principle relies on the enzymatic cleavage of the cell-permeant substrate Xite™ Red beta-D-galactopyranoside by SA-β-Gal. This reaction produces a distinct red fluorescence, which serves as the quantitative measure.

In vitro co-culture system

Cells were co-cultured in a polycarbonate filter membrane with the pore size of 0.4 μm and inserts of 6.5 mm (Corning, Sunnyvale, CA, USA). During this process, two different proportions of cells were used: RLs and PBMCs, with 2 × 105 cells/ml and 1 × 106 cells/ml, were set at a ratio of 1:1 or 1:5, consistent with the previous description. PBMCs were placed in the bottom chamber, while the top chamber contained RL cells that were transfected with KMT2Dwt or KMT2Dmut state, and the entire process lasted for 72 h. All cells were cultured in the RPMI 1640 growth medium, and this formula included heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum, which accounted for 10%, and also incorporated 1% penicillin and streptomycin.

Sphere formation

For the formation of tumor sphere, 5 × 104 RL cells were seeded into 6-well plates with ultra-low adhesion and cultured in RPMI 1640 medium for 12 days. During this period, Gibco™ B-27™ supplement factor, 20 ng/ml EGF, and 20 ng/m bFGF were added into the culture medium. The screening method was used for subsequent processes, tumor spheres with the size of 150 μm or more were selected from the cultivated spheres for further research and application.

ATAC-seq and data analysis

Cells were collected and resuspended to a total of 50 µl cold lysis buffer and end-over-end mixed. The collected cell samples were dispersed again in the transposition reaction mixture. Next, these materials were placed at 37 °C and mixed moderately for 30 min. We extracted and purified the DNA produced by transposition after completing the transposition reaction, and the PCR amplification process was performed to amplify the transposed DNA followed by quantifying the ATAC-seq libraries using qPCR-based technology. The high-throughput sequencing of the libraries was performed on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform, and deepTools was used for the detailed analysis to ensure quality control statistics. The identification of peak calling was accomplished using MACS2 (v.2.2.6), and visualization was obtained using IGV software (v.2.10.0).

Chromatin immunoprecipitation assay

After obtaining the cells, formaldehyde solution was added into the cell sample to ensure that the final solution concentration reached 1%, in order to facilitate the cross-linking process of chromatin after ten minutes at room temperature. Next, we added glycine with a final concentration of 125 mM to terminate the crosslinking reaction process. Splitting chromosomal DNA into fragments ranging in length from 100 to 500 base pairs using ultrasound technology. Next, the immunoprecipitation was performed using protein A and G magnetic beads combined with anti-H3K9me3 antibody (#13969; Cell Signaling Technology). Using Illumina NovaSeq™ 6000 platform, the high-throughput ChIP interaction sequence analysis was performed following the manufacturer’s guidelines.

The predictive nomogram of RNA sequencing data

TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, CA, USA) was used for the extraction of total RNA, and the RNA sequencing analysis was performed on an Illumina NovaSeq™ 6000 provided by LC Bio Technology (Hangzhou, China). The level of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) was processed and interpreted with the tool of R software. The CIBERSORT algorithm was applied to deconvolve the immune cell composition and estimate the proportions of infiltrating immune cell subsets. A nomogram was constructed to integrate the dysregulated gene signatures for predictive pathway enrichment of KMT2D mutant-expressing cells. We performed Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) to define the KMT2D signatures in the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database. The significance threshold was set at a nominal p-value less than 0.05 and a q-value of false discovery rate (FDR) less than 0.25.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

According to the supplier’s guidelines, the EZ-press kit based on a rotating column was used for the extraction of whole cell RNA. The isolated RNA was reverse-transcribed to generate cDNA according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. qRT-PCR was carried using SYBR Green Master Mix reagent (TaKaRa, Dalian, China). The relative levels of target genes were standardized using GAPDH gene as a reference standard and were implemented using a 2−△△Ct analysis strategy. Detailed primer sequence data were listed in Supplementary Table S3.

Transmission electron microscope (TEM) imaging

After being seeded in 6-well plates and pretreated with the indicated factors for 96 h, cells were subjected to collection and fixation with 2.5% glutaraldehyde. TEM imaging was performed by Servicebio (Wuhan, China), and the acquired images were analyzed with ImageJ to quantify mitochondrial and cytoplasmic areas.

Western blotting

Cells were harvested and lysed on ice for 30 min using RIPA buffer (Beyotime, China). The lysates were centrifuged to collect cellular protein extracts. Proteins (25 g per sample) were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. The membranes were blocked for 1 h in 5% non-fat milk prepared in TBST, followed by overnight incubation with the respective primary antibodies at 4 °C. After washing with TBST, the membranes were incubated with appropriate secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL). Equal protein loading was verified by immunoblotting for GAPDH. Data analysis was performed with ImageJ software, and images were cropped for presentation.

Flow cytometry

The following antibodies were employed for cell labeling: BUV737 Mouse Anti-Human CD4 (612748, BD Pharmingen), BV711 Mouse Anti-Human CD25 (563159, BD Pharmingen), BV510 Mouse Anti-Human CD19 (562947, BD Pharmingen), APC-H7 Mouse anti-Human CD45 (560274, BD Pharmingen), PE-Cy7 Mouse Anti-Human CD86 (561128, BD Pharmingen), PerCP-Cy5.5 Mouse Anti-Human CD3 (552852, BD Pharmingen), PE Mouse Anti-Human CD163 (556018, BD Pharmingen), CD11b FITC (561688, BD Pharmingen), Alexa Fluor 647 Mouse anti-Human FoxP3 (560045, BD Pharmingen), BV421 Mouse Anti-Human CD68 (564943, BD Pharmingen), Transcription Factor Buffer Set (562574, BD Pharmingen), Human BD Fc Block (564219, BD Pharmingen) APC anti-human CD19 (392503, Biolegend), APC anti-human IgM (314509, Biolegend), APC anti-human IgD (348221, Biolegend), Rabbit Monoclonal IgG1 Antibody (NBP2-62028, Novus), Goat anti-Rabbit IgG-APC Antibody (abs20032, Absin). Data acquisition was performed using a Cytoflex LX flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter), and subsequent analysis was conducted with FlowJo software.

Cell cycle analysis

Cell cycle distribution was analyzed using a cell cycle staining kit (Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd.) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, transfected cells were washed with cold PBS and fixed by dropwise addition of cold 70% ethanol. After fixation, the cells were pelleted, resuspended in a propidium iodide (PI) staining solution containing 50 µg/mL RNase A and 50 µg/mL PI, and incubated in the dark at 37 °C for 30 min. Finally, the samples were subjected to flow cytometry, and the data were analyzed with FlowJo software.

Apoptosis assay

Cellular apoptosis was assessed in transfected cells using an APC Annexin V Apoptosis Detection Kit with 7-AAD, following the manufacturer’s protocol. The stained cells were subjected to flow cytometry, and the resulting data were analyzed with FlowJo software.

Cell proliferation assay

Cell viability was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 7,000 cells per well in a final volume of 100 µL, with four replicate wells per experimental group. After incubation at 37 °C in a CO₂ atmosphere for 4 days, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well at various time points. The absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Thermo Scientific). Optical density (OD) values were used as an indicator of cell viability.

Data collection and processing

The GSE53820 dataset containing the expression data of 6 cases of DLBCL patients and 4 cases of FL tissues has been downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus Database (GEO) database. The GSE67291 dataset, comprising isolated primary cells harvested directly from mice, was downloaded from the GEO database and includes 5 sh-Kmt2d samples and 4 empty vector controls. The current research follows GEO data access policies and publication guidelines. The R statistical package software EdgeR was used to assess differential expression. Genes with |log2 fold change (FC)| >1.0 and adjusted p < 0.05 were considered as DEGs.

Measurement of GSSG/GSH, lipid ROS and Cu2+ levels

For the detection of GSSG and GSH levels, GSSG/GSH Assay Kit (Beyotime S0053, Shanghai, China) was used. The Cu2+ levels were calculated by Copper (Cu) Colorimetric Assay Kit (Elabscience, E-BC-K300-M). For the assessment of lipid ROS, BODIPY 581/591 C11 reagent (Invitrogen D3861, USA) was used. Assays were performed according to the manual instructions.

Multiplex fluorescent immunohistochemical (mIHC)

Immunostaining was performed on fixed primary biopsy specimens from the FL cohort. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissue sections were treated with 3% H2O2 to block endogenous peroxidase activity, followed by incubation with a 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) blocking buffer to minimize non-specific binding. Slides were then incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C, followed by incubation with corresponding secondary antibodies at room temperature. Multiplex immunofluorescence labeling was executed using a six-color mIHC kit (Absin) strictly adhering to the manufacturer’s recommendations. Whole-slide images were acquired and documented utilizing the Phenochart imaging system (Akoya Biosciences).

Immunofluorescence assay

Cells on glass coverslips were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with rabbit anti-H3K9me3 antibody (1:400 dilution). Subsequently, the samples were incubated with fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibody (1:600 dilution) in the dark. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Six random fields were selected and imaged using a fluorescence microscope. The resulting immunofluorescence images were subjected to in-depth analysis with Image Pro Plus 6.0 software.

Mice

Conditional Kmt2d knockout (Kmt2dfl/fl) mice were generated by Gem Pharmatech, Ltd. using CRISPR-Cas9 technology, with loxP sites inserted upstream of exon 2 and downstream of exon 49. All mice used in this study were 8–12 weeks of age and housed under specific pathogen-free conditions. Both male and female animals were included, and sex was not treated as a biological variable. All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of the Laboratory Animal Center, Xiamen University.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SD from at least two independent experiments. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism. Comparisons between two groups were made using Student’s t-test, while multiple group comparisons were conducted by one-way ANOVA. Survival differences were assessed by Kaplan–Meier analysis with the log-rank test. Significance levels were defined as *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001. Additional methodological details and supporting references are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Results

Integrated analysis of the KMT2D‑related driver mutation landscape in FL

Mutations in chromatin-modifying genes have emerged as an important type of epigenetic alteration and have been determined as driver mutations. In order to determine the epigenetic modifiers in FL that have tumor suppressor functions, we investigated which epigenetic regulatory factors most frequently exhibited genomic alterations associated with the loss-of-function mutations in B-cell lymphomas. As illustrated in the box plots, KMT2D expression did not differ significantly between mutant and wild-type cohorts within The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (p > 0.05; Fig. S1A, S3C). KMT2D mutations were identified in 35% of DLBCLs within the TCGA data set (Fig. S1C). Accordingly, our results in terms of the FL data through targeted sequencing indicated that KMT2D had such genomic alterations in approximately 63% of the FL patients (Fig. 1A, B). Among the FL samples analyzed, the spectrum of KMT2D mutations was dominated by nonsense and frameshift types (Fig. 1B, Fig. S1D). The characteristics of FL patients in this study are shown in Supplementary Table S1. We analyzed the immune cell subpopulations of KMT2Dmut and KMT2Dwt patients and found that there was no difference in the absolute lymphocyte‒monocyte ratio (ALC/AMC) in the peripheral blood at diagnosis. However, the presence of KMT2Dmut was significantly associated with TME components (Fig. 1C). The co-occurrence was observed between KMT2D and EP300 mutations (Fig. 1D, Fig. S1B, Table S2), which associates additional epigenetic mechanisms with FL development.

KMT2Dmut affects transcriptomic signatures of FL-cells

Illumina amplicon sequencing revealed that KMT2D wild type is expressed in RL, SU-DHL-4 and DOHH2 cells (Table S4). In FL, KMT2D mutations predominantly present as nonsense and frameshift mutations within the distal regulatory region upstream of the SET domain. To characterize the role of KMT2D in follicular lymphomagenesis, we generated SU-DHL-4 and RL cell lines stably expressing either the KMT2D (R1252*) mutant or wild-type KMT2D via lentiviral transduction (Fig. 2A). This mutation type was identified in 10 paired early-progression samples at our department (Table S5), and its localization in this distal upstream region suggests a propensity toward loss of KMT2D protein function [10, 11]. Given that KMT2D is one of the most frequently mutated genes in B-cell lymphomas and is often associated with perturbed cell cycle regulation [10], we sought to determine whether KMT2D mutations drive lymphomagenesis by modulating cell survival and turnover. Functional assays revealed that the KMT2D mutant significantly accelerated the proliferation rate of both cell lines compared to WT and negative controls (NC), as evidenced by CCK-8 assays (Fig. 2B). To further investigate whether this growth advantage was coupled with an evasion of programmed cell death, we performed flow cytometry using 7-AAD and Annexin V-APC dual staining. Our results demonstrated that the KMT2D mutation markedly reduced the proportion of early apoptotic cells in both RL and SU-DHL-4 lines (Fig. 2C). Notably, no significant differences in proliferation or apoptosis were observed between the WT-expressing cells and NC groups, suggesting that the observed oncogenic effects are specific to the R1252* truncation.

To identify the critical genes for the transcriptome landscape, we conducted RNA-sequencing analysis of independent RNA samples from three sets of RL cells, each comprising cells with either wild-type or mutant forms of KMT2D. A volcano plot was used to illustrate the characteristics of genetic expression (Fig. 2D). We verified that 1785 DEGs, including 775 downregulated and1010 upregulated genes (P < 0.001). PDK1 (FC = 2.34 and P = 4.90E-143) and SGK1 (FC = 3.14 and P = 6.04E-40) were notably upregulated, whereas CDKN1A (p21Cip1, FC = 0.37, and P = 1.77E-96) was significantly downregulated. These levels were substantiated via qRT‒PCR analysis, as shown in Fig. 2F. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of the RNA-sequencing data revealed that upregulated DEGs were enriched in multiple signaling pathways, particularly cell death and apoptosis pathways (Fig. 2E). The western blotting analyses demonstrated that the KMT2Dmut was associated with the substantial elevation in protein expressions of PDK1 and SGK1 compared with those in the two controls (Fig. 2G). Conversely, the phosphorylated forms of PDK1 and p21Cip1 were decreased. As depicted in Fig. S2, the western blot results further revealed the changes in protein levels, including the decreased protein levels of Bax and cleaved caspase-3 in cells expressing the KMT2D mutant, and the increased expression of Bcl-2. Consistently, in the KMT2Dmut cohort, the phosphorylation status of PI3K and AKT was significantly weakened, suggesting that the level of the downstream targets cooperating with the Bcl-2 family’s regulatory mechanisms upregulated by mutant KMT2D has relevance in FL.

Mutant KMT2D affects H3K9me3 signaling in FL

As per the literature review, the PI3K/PDK1/SGK1 signaling axis regulates histone H3 lysine 9 trimethylation (H3K9me3) [17, 18]. Accordingly, the KMT2Dmut significantly downregulated H3K9me3 expression compared with that in untreated cells (Fig. 3A). As the hallmark of driving force of cellular senescence, H3K9me3 is involved in the formation of heterochromatin [19–21]. The enrichment analysis based on Gene Ontology (GO) showed the biological processes of histone H3K9 demethylation, heterochromatin organization, and cellular senescence signaling (Fig. 3B). Cellular senescence, a stress response in the TME induced by molecular damage, is often bilateral and listed as the hallmark of cancer [22]. Based on the distinct involvement of H3K9me3 in modulating the immunosuppressive milieu [20], it is plausible that the aging program orchestrated by KMT2Dmut is modulated via H3K9me3-dependent signaling pathways. Consistent with our observed phenotypes, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of downregulated DEGs revealed marked enrichment of pathways governing cellular senescence, cell cycle, glutathione metabolism, and apoptosis (Fig. 3C–E). GSEA exhibited that the KMT2Dmut led to enrichment of the reactive oxygen species (ROS) pathway and the G2/M checkpoint (FDR q < 0.25; Fig. 3F). Similarly, our in vitro 5-ethynyl-2’-deoxyuridine (EdU) incorporation assays demonstrated that, compared with the control group, KMT2Dmut markedly increased the percentage of EdU-positive cells and the proportion of S-phase cells, and significantly decreased the proportion of G2/M-phase cells, suggesting that the KMT2D mutant arrested cells at the G2/M phase (Fig. 3G, H).

KMT2Dmut accelerates highly-plastic tumor B cell states

We first characterized KMT2D-associated signatures using GSEA across KEGG pathways and validated these in a GEO B-cell lymphoma cohort. Enrichment analysis revealed markedly elevated scores for multiple oncogenic programs, notably the NOTCH signaling pathway (Fig. S3B). Given the known cooperation between Notch-1 and GATA3 in driving lymphoid lineage commitment [23, 24], we intersected DEGs from our RNA-seq with the GSE53820 dataset [25], identifying SGK1 as a pivotal candidate (Fig. 4A, S3A, C). To further decode the molecular architecture of these enriched signatures, we constructed a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network, which identified a prominent cluster centered on stemness maintenance and metabolic reprogramming (Fig. 4B). This was complemented by the observation of distinct SGK1 splicing perturbations in our POD24-related patient cohort (Table S5).

To investigate whether KMT2D mutations drive this aggressive phenotype through epigenetic remodeling, we performed ATAC-seq in KMT2D-mutant FL cells. This analysis revealed increased chromatin accessibility at the genomic loci of key stemness markers, including ALDH1A1, PROM1, and POU5F1 (Fig. 4C). Motif analysis indicated differential binding of BORIS, CTCF, and hematopoietic-specific transcription factors such as GATA3, PU.1, IRF, and ETS1. Consistent with this, KMT2D-mutant cells exhibited enhanced sphere-forming and proliferative capacities (Fig. 4D). We subsequently conducted chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)-seq for H3K9me3 to detect the effects of mutant KMT2D on the epigenomic landscape and chromatin state in FL. Further analysis revealed that the KMT2D (R1252*) mutant had a certain obvious effect on the ChIP-seq signals of H3K9me3 marks at the transcription start site (TSS) of ALDH1A1 (Fig. 4E). Using shRNA-mediated knockdown, we successfully depleted ALDH1A1 expression (Figs. 4F, S5B, C). The two central executors of senescence-induced growth arrest are p21Cip1 and p16INK4A [26]. Importantly, we found that the qPCR-determined expression of p21Cip1 and p16INK4A was significantly elevated upon ALDH1A1 knockdown in the KMT2D mutant background relative to mutant cells alone (Fig. 4G), indicating that the senescence regulatory network is functionally coupled to ALDH1A1 overexpression in this context.

Mutant KMT2D modulates immunogenic reprogramming through ALDH1A1 dysregulation

A primary mechanism through which senescent cells promote tumorigenic phenotypes is thought to be the secretion of senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) factors, which contribute to the regulation of cellular stemness and plasticity within the aging microenvironment [22, 27]. As illustrated in Fig. 5A, inhibition of ALDH1A1 in KMT2D-mutant FL cells upregulated the senescence marker p21Cip1 along with the SASP components IL-8, IL-1β, and IFNβ. While shRNA-mediated knockdown of ALDH1A1 compromised tumor initiation and proliferation while promoting cellular senescence, these inhibitory effects remained largely consistent regardless of KMT2D mutational status, as evidenced by comparable MFI levels between WT and KMT2D-mutant samples (Fig. 5B, C). During senescence, increased apoptotic stress promotes the release of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) into the cytosol, leading to activation of the cGAS–STING pathway, a key regulator of innate immune responses [28, 29]. To understand how H3K9me3-ALDH1A1 activation prompts this mitochondrial stress, we assessed mtDNA localization. This revealed that depleting ALDH1A1 in KMT2D mutant cells resulted in the cytoplasmic translocation of mtDNA (Fig. 5D, E).

Long interspersed element-1 (LINE1) retrotransposons undergo activation in senescence and age-related degeneration, driving pro-senescence phenotypes [19]. Our findings on the regulation of LINE1 mainly focused on ORF1p, laying a foundation to further study the effects of aging determinants on protein-coding genes. To test this, we examined ChIP-seq data from wild-type and mutant KMT2D-treated FL cells. Notably, mutant KMT2D altered H3K9me3 occupancy on chromosome-enriched transposable elements, with abundant LINE1 retrotransposons and ATP7A binding motif variations identified (Fig. 5F, G). Depletion of ATP7A in the TME is proposed to enhance ferroptosis by degrading SLC7A11 [30]. Quantitative analysis demonstrated that mutant KMT2D promotes ATP7A/B activation and elevates SLC7A11 levels compared to its wild-type counterpart (Fig. 5H, I).

KMT2Dmut‑induced SLC7A11 overexpression depends on ATP7A upregulation

As a tumor suppressor, deletion of KMT2D confers therapeutic vulnerability to glycolytic inhibitors [31], thus we wondered whether the inhibition of intratumoral heterogeneity or metabolic pathways would affect the viability of FL-cells harboring loss-of-function mutations in KMT2D. Inhibitors for different types of cell death, including necrosis, ferroptosis and apoptosis, were applied to aim to restore the growth of FL cell lines treated with the KMT2D mutant. The results revealed that erastin, but not other inhibitors, notably impeded cell growth (Fig. 6A), which suggests that ferroptosis is likely the predominant form of cell death mediated by the KMT2Dmut. As shown in Fig. 6C, the KMT2D mutant induced a reduction in lipid ROS, contrary to the robust oxidative stress observed in the control groups. Consistently, the KMT2Dmut accumulated a large amount of the reduced form of glutathione (GSH) and promoted the antioxidant capacity of FL cell lines (Fig. 6B). As described above, the expression level of SLC7A11 was downregulated in ATP7A-deficient FL cells (Fig. 6D, E, Fig. S55B, C), indicating that KMT2D mutant-induced SLC7A11 overexpression is dependent on ATP7A upregulation.

Cuproptosis-triggered ferroptotic signaling refers to a mitochondrial cell death pathway that is triggered by excessive accumulation of copper, which depends on ROS production and subsequently leads to immunogenic cell death (ICD) [32–35]. The combined treatment of mutant KMT2D and copper resulted in a reduction in copper in the cytoplasm and mitochondria, while copper in the medium increased, suggesting that the KMT2Dmut brings copper from the mitochondria of cells into the medium (Fig. 6F). Furthermore, ultrastructural analyses revealed that in the KMT2D mutant-positive group of cells expressing shRNA-ATP7A, the mitochondrial cristae and swelling of mitochondrial matrix was significantly reduced (Fig. 6G, H). To investigate the necessity of ATP7A upregulation for KMT2Dmut-triggered copper efflux in FL cells, we depleted endogenous ATP7A to assess copper export following mutant KMT2D expression. The findings demonstrated that KMT2Dmut reduced intracellular copper retention by promoting ATP7A overexpression (Fig. 6I). Moreover, cells expressing the KMT2D mutant exhibited significantly elevated levels of immunosuppressive cytokines, such as IL-10 and IL-4, compared to KMT2D wild-type cells. In contrast, the KMT2D mutant substantially reversed the alterations in proinflammatory cytokine expression (Fig. 6J).

Mutant KMT2D remodels the immune microenvironment in FL via ATP7A

As shown in Fig. 7A, co‑culture of PBMCs with RL cells expressing wild‑type or mutant KMT2D identified Tregs and M2 macrophages as the most prominently altered populations. A similar pattern was also observed in the GSE53820 dataset, where the transformation from FL to DLBCL was defined by an increase of Treg cells (Fig. S4A). We then employed the CIBERSORT algorithm to estimate the enrichment scores of specific immune cell populations in RL cells with or without KMT2Dmut. The analysis revealed that most scores, including those for CD8 T cells, Tregs, and M2 macrophages, were significantly elevated in KMT2D-MUT compared to KMT2D-WT samples (Fig. 7B). Box plots provide a detailed visualization of these heightened enrichment scores in the KMT2Dmut RL cells (Fig. 7C). To characterize the in vivo role of Kmt2d in lymphomagenesis, we generated an inducible Kmt2d knockout mouse model driven by Cd19-Cre (Fig. 7D). After confirming efficient Kmt2d deletion, we observed that Kmt2d−/− mice developed modest splenomegaly by week 10, although this did not reach statistical significance (Fig. 7E); this phenotype represented a manifestation of lymphoma characterized by follicular hyperplasia of neoplastic B220 + B lymphocytes. Correspondingly, a pronounced increase in Treg cell recruitment was detected in the spleens of Kmt2d−/− mice (Fig. 7F). GSEA of cell enrichment patterns further confirmed that Kmt2d loss was associated with distinct gene signatures for macrophages and effector memory CD8 T cells relative to controls (Fig. 7H). High PD-L1 expression and its colocalization with the Treg-specific marker FoxP3 in the KMT2Dmut group were further confirmed by mIHC (Figs. 8A-J and 9). The western blotting results also confirmed the aforementioned findings for PD-L1 in KMT2Dmut-shRNA-ATP7A (Fig. 7G), indicating that KMT2Dmut might regulate the immune vulnerability associated with copper-dependent ICD. As revealed by mIHC analysis of lymph nodes, the higher expression of ATP7A and the colocalization of H3K9me3 with ALDH1A1 were significantly associated with KMT2D mutations (Fig. 9). Accordingly, when co-cultured with peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), the KMT2Dmut significantly increased cell growth, suggesting that the viability of B-lymphoma cells was modulated by the TME (Fig. 7I).

Discussion

To date, FL displays marked heterogeneity and eventually develops resistance to standard therapies [36]. As the pathognomonic hallmark of FL, t (14; 18) (q32; q21) chromosomal translocation arises from repair failure during VDJ recombination, leading to deregulated expression of the antiapoptotic BCL2 oncogene [37]. Because the epigenetic machinery was often hijacked by FL-cells through mutations in histone-modifying proteins, many higher-grade FLs with frequent KMT2D mutations progress from low-grade lesions and are associated with mutations in the histone acetyltransferase EP300/CREBBP and the histone methyltransferase EZH2, contributing to the malignant progression of FL [38, 39]. However, the specific contribution of KMT2Dmut to the disease progression of FL remained unclear.

The histone-lysine N-methyltransferase 2 (KMT2) family consists of six members, with KMT2A, KMT2B, KMT2C, and KMT2D representing the predominant isoforms. Recent studies have demonstrated that aberrant expression or mutations of KMT2 genes occur frequently across a broad spectrum of cancers. By disrupting histone methylation, these mutations impair DNA damage repair and dysregulate gene expression, thereby fueling tumorigenesis and disease progression [40]. Conversely, such mutations can heighten genomic instability, which may in turn enhance tumor immunogenicity and sensitize tumors to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) [41]. In the present study, we detected KMT2D mutations in approximately 60% of FL samples. Building upon prior targeted sequencing analyses that identified the SGK1-associated/C4 cluster for molecular subtyping, together with the established B-cell lymphoma TME categories (GC-like, Mesenchymal, Depleted, Inflammatory), we further delineated the functional role of SGK1 in this setting [42–44]. Notably, we further discovered that KMT2Dmut profoundly diminished global H3K9me3 levels in FL cells. Given that KMT2D is canonically an H3K4 methyltransferase with no recognized direct role in H3K9 modification, this mutation-driven loss of H3K9me3 via a non-canonical route is a particularly intriguing observation, although the precise underlying mechanism remains to be fully elucidated. Emerging evidence implicates the PI3K/PDK1/SGK1 signaling axis as a potential mechanistic link, and our transcriptomic data revealed that the KMT2Dmut markedly upregulated PDK1 and SGK1, providing a plausible molecular pathway accounting for H3K9me3 reduction.

Transposable elements (TEs) function as pivotal regulatory elements in developmental processes by embedding transcription factor binding sites within pluripotency networks and by acting as promoters, enhancers, or participants in splicing and genome organization. In FL cells, KMT2Dmut induces a hyper-pluripotent arrest and profoundly reorganizes the TE expression landscape, as evidenced by ChIP-seq data. Within this remodeled landscape, activated TEs exhibit enhancer-like activity, establishing chromatin contacts with the promoter of the adjacent ATP7A gene to directly drive its aberrant overexpression. Our results indicate that the combination of KMT2Dmut and copper treatment leads to mitochondrial copper depletion via ATP7A upregulation, consequently promoting SLC7A11 overexpression. As a consequence of the stress response, ICD triggers a cascade of inflammatory responses, including the release of cytokines and damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), which are crucial for the activation of antigen-presenting cells (APCs) and T-cell responses [45]. ICD induction has been shown to enhance the efficacy of anti-PD-1 therapy [33, 46]. Our finding that KMT2Dmut modulates ATP7A-dependent PD-L1 activation suggests that combining ATP7A-mediated cuproptosis agonists with ICIs may hold greater clinical promise.

Cancer heterogeneity is widely attributed to the interplay of the cancer stem-cell model, clonal evolution theory, and tumor microenvironment properties [47, 48]. ALDH1A1, a stemness marker linked to poor prognosis, contributes to an evolving tumor microenvironment through its response to myeloid-derived suppressor cell (MDSC) expansion [49]. By integrating chromatin epigenomic states with DEGs, we sought to identify stemness-specific genes that are synergistically activated in the KMT2D-mutant group. Our study demonstrates that ALDH1A1 loss in KMT2D-mutant cells correlates with upregulation of the key senescence markers p21Cip1 and IL-8, as well as impaired self-renewal capacity of lymphoma cells. FL pathogenesis is driven by the acquisition of a highly plastic B-cell state coupled with early immune microenvironment remodeling, suggesting that KMT2D mutations likely disrupt the transcriptional programs essential for mature B-cell maintenance, thereby promoting reversion to a plastic or intermediately differentiated phenotype.

While KMT2 family mutations are linked to enhanced ICI benefit and a more immunogenic “hot” tumor phenotype with increased CD8 T cells [50], our CIBERSORT and mIHC analyses paradoxically revealed enriched immunosuppressive cells in KMT2D-mutant FL. This indicates that the suppressive TME directly impairs CD8 T cell cytotoxicity through inhibitory cytokines and contact-dependent mechanisms to facilitate immune evasion [51]. Here, we demonstrate that targeting ALDH1A1 disrupts the “ATP7A-ROS” signaling homeostasis, dismantles the FL stem cell hierarchy, and suppresses disease progression (Fig. 10). Together with the pathogenic FL–niche crosstalk that sustains malignant B-cell survival and immune escape, this axis emerges as both a biomarker and an actionable therapeutic target. However, validation in more physiologically relevant settings remains to be established, and canonical hallmarks of ICD remain to be fully examined, which together limit the immediate translational implications of ALDH1A1 inhibition. Addressing these gaps will be essential to substantiate the H3K9me3–ALDH1A1–ATP7A circuitry as a therapeutic vulnerability in KMT2D‑mutant FL.

Overall, our study reveals an epigenomic landscape shaped by the intrinsic properties of KMT2D mutations, driving the emergence of highly plastic tumor B cell states. We also carved out an evolving niche as potential drivers that could be used as a guideline for potential intervention targets of KMT2Dmut-related disorders in basic research and clinical treatment.

Conclusions

This work expands our understanding of the tumor-intrinsic role of KMT2D mutations beyond their canonical function as a histone methyltransferase in FL. We uncover that mutant KMT2D inhibits H3K9me3 and upregulates ALDH1A1 expression. Our findings highlight the role of KMT2D mutations in promoting highly plastic tumor B cell states that shape TME evolution, as well as in perturbing copper homeostasis and redox balance in FL.

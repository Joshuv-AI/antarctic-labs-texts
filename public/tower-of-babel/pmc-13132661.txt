Atmospheric Pressure Corona Discharge Ionization of n-Heptane (I): Formation of the Molecular Ion M+• and Dehydrogenated Ions [M − 2H]+• and [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5)
Authors: Daisuke Fukuyama, Masahiro Hashimoto, Masaaki Ubukata, Kanako Sekimoto, Mitsuo Takayama
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The formation of M+∙, [M − 2H]+∙, and [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5) ions has been reported by using atmospheric pressure corona discharge (APCD) of n-heptane. The M+∙ and [M − H]+ ions can be produced by high-electric field tunnel ionization (HEFTI) and hydride abstraction reactions, respectively. It has been proposed that the [M − 2H]+∙, [M − 3H]+, and [M − 5H]+ ions are produced by the loss of hydrogen atoms from the [M − H]+ ion. The geometric and electronic configurations of the M+∙, [M − 2H]+∙, and [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5) ions were determined by quantum chemical calculations (QCCs). The product ions resulting from higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) of the M+∙ ion can be characterized by the loss of alkyl radicals and alkanes, while those of the [M − 2H]+∙ ion can be characterized by the loss of alkyl radicals and alkenes. The HCD product ions of the [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5) ions can be fundamentally characterized by the loss of alkenes. The product ions present in the HCD spectra can be supported by the geometric and electronic configurations determined by the QCCs. The fragment ions observed in the APCD mass spectrum are likely attributable to the result of collisional interactions between the M+· ion and the air components such as N2, O2, and Ar, or to the result of the HEF-induced cracking reaction leading to the alkyl fragments.

1. INTRODUCTION

Corona discharge (CD) has been utilized as an ionization device for atmospheric pressure chemical ionization mass spectrometry (APCI MS). The CD for APCI is used in the generation of reagent ions, including RH+ and R+·. The APCI can form protonated molecule [M + H]+, dehydride molecule [M − H]+, and molecular ion M+∙ via proton transfer (1), hydride abstraction reactions (2.1) and (2.2), and charge exchange (CE) (3), respectively, by collisional interactions of analyte molecules M with RH+ such as H3O+ or R+∙ such as O2+·, N2+·, and Ar+·. In addition, the production of other analyte ions with oxidative and adduct ions occurs according to their physicochemical properties and chemical structures.1) According to the recent reports,2,3) atmospheric pressure corona discharge (APCD) results in M+∙ ion via a high-electric-field tunnel ionization (HEFTI) (4) under atmospheric pressure conditions. It is important to note that the APCD under humidified atmosphere conditions leads to the formation of reactive species such as H•, HO•, O•, HO2•, H3O+, and O2+·,4–7) but N2+· and Ar+· ions were not observed due to their relatively high ionization energy.3) The formation of hydrogen and hydroxy radicals (H• and HO•) is particularly significant in this context. This reaction is initiated by the dissociation of water molecules H2O, as depicted in reaction (5). It has been established that the hydrogen radical abstracts hydrogen atoms from the backbone of organic compounds, thereby forming a dehydrogenated molecule [M − H]• (6).8,9)

Although APCI is employed to identify hydrocarbons in extensive MS fields,10–13) the identification of hydrocarbons, particularly n-alkane, is a complex undertaking due to various reaction principles of chemical ionization (CI).1,14) The analyte ions of n-alkane molecules M emerge as a consequence of ion–molecule reactions under atmospheric pressure (AP) ambient conditions involving air components O2, N2, Ar, H2O, and CO2, which yield atmospheric ions O2+·, NO+, H3O+, N2H+, and O2H+.1,13,15) In the APCI study of n-alkane, Bell et al. have reported the formation of dehydrogenated analyte ions such as [M − H]+, [M − 3H]+, and [M − 3H + H2O]+.16) As stated by Hourani and Kuhnert10) and Wentrup et al.,13) the formation of the same analyte ions was reported in APCI of n-alkane. The APCI of n-alkane with N2 reagent gas exclusively generates the molecular ion M+· as a consequence of the CE reaction (3). Conversely, the APCI with O2 reagent gas yields dehydride molecules [M − H]+ rather than M+· ion due to hydride abstraction with the reagent ion O2+· (2.2).12) As previously reported,2,3) APCD has been shown to produce molecular ions M+∙ of benzene, phenol, toluene, aniline, 2,6-xylidine, n-hexane, and butyrophenone. It has been established that APCD functions through the implementation of a strong field strength of 108 V/m within the air components,17,18) and that APCD does not form molecular ions of N2+∙, Ar+∙, and H2O+∙ as the reagent ions R+∙ for CE reaction (3). This fact has led to the proposal that analyte molecular ions M+∙ are formed by HEFTI (4),2,3) also referred to as field ionization (FI). Wentrup et al. have recently employed a high-resolution ion trap mass spectrometer, the Q Exactive Plus Orbitrap (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany)19) to identify analyte ions, including [M − 3H]+ and [M − 3H + H2O]+, as well as [M − H]+ ion of n-heptane and n-decane.13) The resolving power of 70000 with the Orbitrap is capable of determining the atomic compositions of all the ionic species.

The present study focuses on the formation and collisional-induced dissociation (CID) characteristics of odd-electron (OE) ions M+· and [M − 2H]+·, and even-electron (EE) ions [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5), produced from n-heptane under APCD conditions, by using the Orbitrap. The n-heptane is frequently employed as a model compound for evaluating ionization20,21) and fragmentation.22–25) The geometric and electronic configurations involving charge and spin (radical) density distributions of the M+·, [M − 2H]+·, and [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5) ions were determined by quantum chemical calculations (QCCs).

2. EXPERIMENTAL

2.1. Analyte

n-Heptane was purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry (Tokyo, Japan) and had over 98% purity, and was used without further purification.

2.2. MS

Mass spectra were acquired on a Q Exactive Focus quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) equipped with a home-built CD system. The mass resolving power for this instrument was 70000 for m/z 200 at full width at half maximum, and the measurable m/z range was above 50. The analyte was subjected to positive ionization in ambient air with a relative humidity of 50% at 298 K. The corona needle utilized as the point electrode was a stainless-steel pin designed “Bishin” (Shiga, Tokyo, Japan) with a diameter of 200 μm and a length of 20 mm. The tip of the needle has a radius of curvature of ca. 1 μm, and the shape of the tip was adequately approximated by a hyperboloid of revolution. The opposite electrode was the stainless-steel orifice plate of the mass spectrometer. The generation of a positive CD was achieved through the application of a voltage of +3.7 kV between the point-to-plane electrodes, which were separated by a gap of 3 mm. An Eppendorf microtube (1.5 mL) containing an analyte was positioned between the needle and the orifice plate. The vaporized analyte was subsequently diffused into the discharge region. The higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) spectra of the precursor ions produced by APCD were obtained with N2 collision gas and a collision energy of 15 eV. The HCD exhibits an intriguing character,26) which gives rise to the formation of noteworthy immonium ions in peptide ions. The provenance of this character is attributable to the occurrence of energetic collisions with collision gas in the C-trap, which is analogous to high-energy CID experiments of keV order energy.27) The low-energy CID experiment of the M+· ion of n-heptane produced by EI was carried out on a triple-quadrupole mass spectrometer JMS-TQ4000GC UltraQuadTM TQ (JEOL, Tokyo, Japan). The electron energy and ion source temperature in EI were 20 eV and 200°C, respectively. The collision gas and energy were N2 and 10 eV, respectively.

2.3. Calculations

The QCCs presented in this paper were conducted using the Gaussian 16 suite of programs,28) and the initial molecules and ionic structures were generated through visual inspection using the GaussView program 6.0. The structural optimization of all neutral and ionic species in the gas phase was conducted using density functional theory with the M06-2X hybrid functional level of theory29) and 6-311++G(d,p) basis set. The charge and spin density distributions were estimated by means of the Mulliken method, and the enthalpy for neutral and ionic species was calculated using the same functional and basis set described above.

3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

3.1. APCD mass spectrum of n-heptane

The APCD mass spectrum of n-heptane primarily exhibited EE alkyl cations of [CnH2n+1]+ (n = 4, 5, 6) at m/z 57, 71, and 85, as well as an OE alkene cation of [C5H10]+· at m/z 70 (see Fig. 1A). The spectrum also exhibited peaks corresponding to OE ions such as M+· at m/z 100 and [M − 2H]+· at m/z 98, and dehydrogenated EE ions [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5) at m/z 99, 97, and 95. However, the spectrum did not show [M − 4H]+ ion, as shown in Fig. 1B. The fragment ions observed in the mass range at m/z 50–99 can be compared with those observed in previously reported APCI,13) CI,30) EI, FI,31) and photo-ionization (PI)24) mass spectra of n-heptane, as summarized in Table 1. The presence of relatively intense fragment ions at m/z 57, 70, 71, 85, and 99 observed in the APCD spectrum exhibited partial similarity to those observed in APCI, CI, FI, PI, and EI mass spectra. Despite the APCD being similar to the APCI with regard to the [M − 2H]+· and [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5) ions, it differs significantly from the FI, PI, and EI, which generally yield molecular ion M+·. Usmanov et al. reported that using a hollow cathode discharge with low-pressure oxygen and air conditions, the mass spectrum of n-heptane showed dehydrogenated ions of [M − 2H]+, [M − 3H]+, and [M − 5H]+ and fragment ions at m/z 87, 85, 83, 81, 73, 71, 69, 67, 59, 57, and 55.32) However, the spectrum obtained by Usmanov et al. did not show the M+· and [M − H]+ ions. Furthermore, the APCD mass spectrum exhibited peaks corresponding to the so-called [M + 13]+ and [M + 15]+ ions, as well as their water cluster ions at m/z 131 and 133 (see Fig. 1A). These atypical ions have been already reported.10,13,15,16,32,33) The formation mechanisms and CID study of these ions will be reported in a forthcoming publication.

3.2. Proposed mechanisms for producing M+·, [M − 2H]+·, and [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5)

The formation of the OE molecular ion M+· at m/z 100 of n-heptane under APCD conditions can be explained by HEFTI (4), as reported in the previous papers.2,3) As illustrated in Scheme 1, the positive charge and spin (radical) are predominantly localized on central carbon C9, while the spin is distributed more extensively on C2, C6, C14, and C15 atoms. The charge and spin density distributions appear to be valuable tools for the analysis of molecular ion fragmentation.

The hydride abstraction reactions (2.1) or (2.2) and hydrogen abstraction reaction (6) are fundamental reactions for elucidating the formation of analyte-related OE ions M+·, [M − 2H]+·, and EE ions [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5) observed in Fig. 1B. As is well established in the literature on CI and APCI of n-alkanes,14,34) the hydride abstraction, which results in a dehydride molecule [M − H]+, is the most basic reaction, as opposed to proton transfer (1), CE (3), and HEFTI (4). Therefore, it can be proposed that the production of dehydrogenated analyte ions, such as [M − 2H]+· and [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5), is a consequence of a combination of hydride abstraction and hydrogen abstraction reaction (6) from n-heptane under APCD conditions. In the APCD under consideration, the formation of the dehydride molecule [M − H]+ can be explained by an energetically favorable hydride abstraction (7.1), that is, collisional interaction between M and O2+· not M and H3O+ (7.2) due to the enthalpy change. 3.3. HCD spectra of OE ions, M+· and [M − 2H]+·, produced from n-heptane

OE ion M+·: The HCD spectrum of the M+· ion of n-heptane obtained with the Orbitrap revealed product ions at m/z 83, 71, 70, 57, 56, and 55, as shown in Fig. 2. The measured and exact masses, along with their respective elemental compositions, assigned on the peak top are depicted in Fig. 2. The EE product ions at m/z 83, 71, 57, and 55 are the result of the loss of (CH3 + H2), C2H5, C3H7, and (C3H7 + H2) from M+· ion, respectively. These ions are characterized by the loss of alkyl radicals •CnH2n+1 via σ-bond cleavage. The successive loss ions, such as (CH3 + H2) and (C3H7 + H2), may be attributable to the higher-energy character of the HCD, which results in marked immonium ions and a-ions in the HCD spectra of peptides.26) The OE product ions at m/z 70 and 56 are attributable to the loss of alkanes C2H6 and C3H8 from the M+· ion, respectively. The characteristic loss of neutrals in the HCD spectra of the OE and EE precursor ions is summarized in Table 2. To compare the CID pattern in the HCD spectrum with that of the CID spectrum of the M+· ion, the EI and low-energy CID spectra of n-heptane were obtained with the triple-quadrupole mass spectrometer, as shown in Fig. 3. The low-energy CID spectrum exhibited product ions at m/z 99, 98, 85, 71, 70, 57, 56, 55, 43, 42, 41, and 29 (Fig. 3B). The product ions at m/z 98 and 85 are attributable to the loss of H2 and •CH3 from the M+· ion, respectively, which differ from the HCD spectrum. The even-mass product ions at m/z 70, 56, and 42 may be attributable to the loss of alkanes CnH2n+2 from the M+· ion, as is well-documented in the field of EI fragmentation of n-alkanes.35,36) The product ions observed in the HCD spectrum of the M+· ion can be consistently explained by the loss of alkyl radicals •CnH2n+1 and alkanes CnH2n+2 from the M+· ion, as illustrated in Schemes 10 and 11. It has been established that the loss of alkyl radicals and alkanes from n-alkane molecular ions occurs via the mechanisms of σ-bond cleavage and 1,3-hydrogen atom transfer (1,3-HAT), respectively.37) It is noteworthy that the fragmentation processes in Scheme 10 are well consistent with the electronic structure involving charge and spin (radical) density distributions obtained with the QCCs (see Scheme 1). It is noteworthy that preferential fragment ions at m/z 85, 71, 70, 57, 56, and 55, as observed in the APCD mass spectrum (see Fig. 1A), bear a striking resemblance to the product ions observed in the low-energy CID of the M+· ion (see Fig. 3B). This finding suggests that the fragment ions in Fig. 1A are probably the result of collisional interactions between the M+· ion and atmospheric air components (O2, N2, Ar) in the gap region of 3 mm of the APCD system. Another possible process for the preferential fragment ions in Fig. 1A may be due to a cracking reaction of the M+· ion with the HEF of 108 V/m at the tip of the corona needle.2,3) As posited by Zhou et al.,38) the HEF-induced cracking reaction of n-heptane yields alkyl radicals, including •C4H9, •C5H11, and •C6H13, which correspond to the ions at m/z 57, 71, and 85, respectively.

3.4. HCD spectra of the EE ions [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5) produced from n-heptane

Among the four isomeric dehydride molecules [M − H]+, the highest and lowest stable isomers are the C6 and C1 isomers, respectively (see Schemes 2A and 2C). It can be posited that the occurrence of a minor product ion at m/z 55 may be attributable to the loss of hydrogen H2 from the product ion at m/z 57. The results obtained above indicate that the formation of HCD product ions produced from the OE and EE precursor ions of the M+·, [M − 2H]+·, and [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5) can be supported by the geometric and electronic configurations obtained with the QCCs. The sites of charge and spin (radical) density distributions predicted by the QCCs are well consistent with the proposed mechanisms for the formation of the product ions in each schematic illustration. The major fragment ions at m/z 85, 71, 70, 57, 56, and 55 observed in the APCD mass spectrum are coincident with the product ions observed in the low-energy CID spectrum of the M+· ion rather than those observed in the HCD spectra of the M+·, [M − 2H]+·, and [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5) ions.

4. CONCLUSION

It has been documented that analyte ions, including the OE ions M+∙ and [M − 2H]+∙ and the dehydrogenated EE ions [M − H]+, [M − 3H]+, and [M − 5H]+ are produced under APCD conditions. The M+∙ and [M − H]+ ions can be produced by the HEFTI and hydride abstraction reactions, respectively. It is proposed that the [M − 2H]+∙, [M − 3H]+, and [M − 5H]+ ions can be produced by the loss of hydrogen atoms from the dehydride molecule [M − H]+. The HCD product ions of the M+∙ ion can be principally characterized by the loss of alkyl radicals •CnH2n+1 and alkanes CnH2n+2, while those of the [M − 2H]+∙ ion can be principally characterized by the loss of alkyl radicals •CnH2n+1 and alkenes CnH2n. The HCD product ions of the [M − nH]+ (n = 1, 3, 5) ions can be fundamentally characterized by the loss of alkenes CnH2n. The geometric and electronic configurations determined by the QCCs were consistent with the product-ion formation in the HCD spectra. The fragment ions observed in the APCD mass spectrum are likely the result of collisional interactions between the M+· ion and the air components (O2, N2, Ar), or the result of the HEF-induced cracking reaction leading to the neutral alkyl fragments (C4H9, C5H11, C6H13).

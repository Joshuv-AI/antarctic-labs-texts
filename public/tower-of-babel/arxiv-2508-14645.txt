Abstract
. Given a lattice Λ ⊂ ℂ ≃ ℝ 2 \Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2} with associated Weierstrass function ℘ Λ \wp_{\Lambda} , we determine the algebraic curves in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} whose image via ℘ Λ \wp_{\Lambda} is contained in an algebraic curve.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and aim of the article

Let
\exp:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be the function sending
z
to
e^{2\pi iz}
. By the Kronecker–Weber theorem, every abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
is contained in a cyclotomic extension
\mathbb{Q}(\exp(a))
for some
a\in\mathbb{Q}
. Note that rational numbers are the only algebraic numbers
a
such that
\exp(a)
is algebraic, by the Gelfond–Schneider theorem
[
MR14
, Corollary 9.3]
.

Abelian extensions of imaginary quadratic fields are contained in those generated by the
j
-invariant of suitable elliptic curves
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
with complex multiplication, and by values of the associated Weber function - a multiple of the Weierstrass function
\wp_{\Lambda}

[
Lan87
, p. 19]
- at preimages of torsion points of
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda

[
Lan87
, Chapter 10]
. Once again, these numbers are characterized by suitable “bialgebraicity” properties: on the one hand, the only algebraic numbers
\tau\in\mathbb{H}\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
j(\tau)
is algebraic belong to imaginary quadratic fields
[
MR14
, Theorem 15.3]
. On the other hand, let
\Lambda_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
be the
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space generated by
\Lambda
. If the invariants
g_{2}
and
g_{3}
of
\Lambda
are algebraic then, by
[
Mas75
, Theorem V]
, the only numbers
a\in\Lambda_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
such that
\wp_{\Lambda}(a)
is algebraic are preimages of torsion points of
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
.

1.1.1.
Complex bialgebraic geometry

Geometric counterparts of the above transcendence results have been studied. For instance, the work of Ax
[
Ax71
]
implies that, if
W\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is an irreducible algebraic variety that is not contained in a translate of a hyperplane defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, then the functions

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\mapsto\exp(x_{i}),\;\;\;\;1\leq i\leq n,

are algebraically independent over
\mathbb{C}
. More generally, bialgebraic subvarieties of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, i.e., irreducible algebraic subvarieties of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
whose image via the map

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{n}

\displaystyle\rightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\mapsto(\exp(x_{1}),\ldots,\exp(x_{n}))

is algebraic, are translates of linear subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}

[
KUY18
, Example 4.5]
. Similar geometric transcendence results hold true for the
j
-invariant and for Weierstrass elliptic functions, cf.
[
Pil11
, pp. 1787-1789]
, and yield a description of bialgebraic subvarieties of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
(uniformizing a product of modular curves) and
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(uniformizing a product of elliptic curves) respectively; cf.
[
KUY18
, Section 4.1]
.

As explained in
[
Pil11
,
KUY18
]
, such a description of bialgebraic varieties plays an important role in the proof of the André–Oort conjecture and analogues thereof.

1.1.2.
Real geometric transcendence

After identifying
\mathbb{C}
with
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, one can wonder about versions of the previous results in real algebraic geometry. If
n=1
- which, in the real setting, is the first interesting case - the relevant results were proved in
[
Tam23
, Theorem 2.2.4, Theorem 3.4.2]
for the exponential function and the
j
-function. For instance, irreducible algebraic curves
\mathcal{V}
in the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}\subset\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
j(\mathcal{V})
is not Zariski dense in
\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
are geodesics with rational endpoints, or with conjugate real quadratic endpoints.

The goal of this article is to prove an analogous result for Weierstrass elliptic functions.

1.2.
Overview of the main results

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}
be a lattice; we regard the attached Weierstrass function as a function from
\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and denote it by
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
. We will describe irreducible algebraic curves
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{V}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda))
is not Zariski dense in
\mathbb{R}^{2}=\mathbb{A}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. We call such curves weakly bialgebraic for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
, cf. Definition
2.1.1
. The following theorem summarizes our main results; see Theorem
2.1.3
and Proposition
3.1.1
for more precise statements.

1.2.1 Theorem
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and let
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}+\tau\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{C}
. There exist weakly bialgebraic curves for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
if and only if
\tau
belongs to a geodesic with rational endpoints. For such a
\tau
, the following assertions hold true:

-

if
\Lambda
is not a CM lattice then weakly bialgebraic curves for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
are translates of two lines through the origin;

-

if
\Lambda
is a CM lattice then weakly bialgebraic curves for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
are translates of lines through the origin containing a non-zero point of
\Lambda
.

1.2.2.
A group-theoretic interpretation of weakly bialgebraic curves

The fact that
\tau
belongs to a geodesic with rational endpoints is equivalent to the fact that
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}+\tau\mathbb{Z}
is isogenous to a lattice equal to its complex conjugate (cf. Corollary
3.1.2
). If
\Lambda=\overline{\Lambda}
, then
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
is the analytification of an elliptic curve
E_{\Lambda}
with equation
y^{2}=x^{3}+ax+b
with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. The two lines in the statement of the theorem are the coordinate axes in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
; the image in
E_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{C})
of the horizontal axis is contained in
E_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R})
. On the other hand, the image of the vertical axis is contained in the set of fixed points of the anti-holomorphic involution
P\mapsto-\overline{P}
(see
2.8.1
). This is the set of real points of the quadratic twist
\widetilde{E}_{\Lambda}
with equation
-y^{2}=x^{3}+ax+b
, mapping to
E_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{C})
via
(x,y)\mapsto(x,iy)
. In other words, the Zariski closures of the images of the coordinate axes in
E_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{C})
are the sets of real points of the subgroups
E_{\Lambda}\subset\mathrm{Res}_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}(E_{\Lambda}\times_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C})
and
\widetilde{E}_{\Lambda}\subset\mathrm{Res}_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}(E_{\Lambda}\times_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C})
. The other weakly bialgebraic curves are obtained from the coordinate axes via translations or isogenies, cf. section
2.8
.

1.2.3.
Proof strategy

Following the strategy employed in
[
Tam23
]
, we deduce the theorem from the description of complex bialgebraic curves for a suitable product of Weierstrass functions. More precisely, we relate (real) weakly bialgebraic curves for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
to complex bialgebraic curves for
\wp_{\Lambda}\times\wp_{\overline{\Lambda}}
, cf. section
2.3
. Note that complex bialgebraic curves for
\wp_{\Lambda}\times\wp_{\overline{\Lambda}}
which are neither horizontal nor vertical lines exist whenever
\Lambda
is isogenous to
\overline{\Lambda}
, i.e., when
\tau
belongs to a geodesic with rational or conjugate real quadratic endpoints (cf. Proposition
3.1.1
). However, it turns out that only in the first case (certain) complex bialgebraic curves for
\wp_{\Lambda}\times\wp_{\overline{\Lambda}}
give rise to real weakly bialgebraic curves for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
.

1.3.
Notation and conventions

The following notation and conventions will be used throughout the article.

-

Let
V
be a complex vector space, and
R\subset\mathbb{C}
a subring. For
v_{1},v_{2}\in V
we denote by
\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle_{R}
the
R
-submodule of
V
generated by
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
.

-

Let
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}
be two lattices in
\mathbb{C}
. We denote by
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2})
the set of
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\alpha\Lambda_{1}\subset\Lambda_{2}
; it is an additive subgroup of
\mathbb{C}
, free of rank at most two. We say that
\Lambda_{1}
is isogenous to
\Lambda_{2}
if
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2})
is non-zero (equivalently, the complex elliptic curves
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda_{1}
and
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda_{2}
are isogenous). A CM lattice is a lattice
\Lambda
such that
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\Lambda)
has rank two.

-

By a subvariety of an algebraic variety we mean a closed (possibly reducible) subvariety. We often identify complex algebraic varieties with their complex points. We identify
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\mathbb{C}
sending
(x,y)
to
x+iy
.

2.
Real bialgebraic curves for Weierstrass elliptic functions

2.1.
Statement of the main theorem

Fix a lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
with attached Weierstrass
\wp
-function
\wp_{\Lambda}:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
. Consider the function

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}

\displaystyle(x,y)

\displaystyle\mapsto(\mathrm{Re}(\wp_{\Lambda}(x+iy)),\mathrm{Im}(\wp_{\Lambda}(x+iy))).

2.1.1 Definition
.

A non-empty subset
\mathcal{V}\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is called weakly bialgebraic for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
if it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

there exist algebraic subvarieties
V\subset\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
W\subsetneq\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\mathcal{V}=V(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{V}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda))\subset W(\mathbb{R})
;

(2)

the set
\mathcal{V}
cannot be written in the form
V_{1}(\mathbb{R})\cup V_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, where
V_{1},V_{2}\subset\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
are algebraic subvarieties and the inclusions
V_{i}(\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{V}
are proper for
i=1,2
.

2.1.2 Remark
.

(1)

The above definition mirrors
[
Tam23
, Definition 2.2.2, Definition 3.1.2]
. The requirement that
\mathcal{V}
is non-empty was mistakenly not included in
loc.cit.
; it is needed in order for the main results stated in
[
Tam23
]
to be true.

(2)

In what follows, we will mostly work with a fixed lattice
\Lambda
; furthermore, we will never consider the stronger notion of “strongly bialgebraic” sets
[
Tam23
, Definition 2.2.2]
. Therefore, weakly bialgebraic sets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
will often be called just “bialgebraic sets”, and
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
will often be denoted by
\mathcal{P}
.

The following is the statement of our main result.

2.1.3 Theorem
.

Let
\Lambda
be a lattice in
\mathbb{C}
. The following assertions hold true.

(1)

If
\Lambda
is not isogenous to
\overline{\Lambda}
, then the only weakly bialgebraic sets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
are singletons.

(2)

Suppose that
\Lambda
is not CM and
\Lambda
is isogenous to
\overline{\Lambda}
. Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
be such that
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\overline{\Lambda})=\mathbb{Z}\cdot\gamma
.

(a)

If
|\gamma|\not\in\mathbb{Q}
, then the only weakly bialgebraic sets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
are singletons.

(b)

If
|\gamma|\in\mathbb{Q}
, then weakly bialgebraic sets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
are singletons and translates of the lines

L_{1}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid\sqrt{\gamma}(x+iy)\in\mathbb{R}\}\text{ and }L_{2}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid\sqrt{\gamma}(x+iy)\in i\mathbb{R}\}.

(3)

If
\Lambda
is CM, then weakly bialgebraic sets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
are singletons and translates of the lines through the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
containing a non-zero point of
\Lambda
.

2.2.
An Ax–Lindemann–Weierstrass type theorem

Let
\Lambda
be a lattice in
\mathbb{C}
. Our proof rests on a functional transcendence result for the map

\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}=\wp_{\Lambda}\times\wp_{\overline{\Lambda}}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C}).

2.2.1 Definition
.

An irreducible algebraic curve
C\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
is bialgebraic for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
if
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}(C)
is not Zariski dense in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
.

2.2.2 Theorem

(Brownawell–Kubota
[
BK77
]
, Pila
[
Pil11
]
)
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
be a bialgebraic curve for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
. Then
C=W+\sigma
for some
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
and some one-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector subspace
W
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
such that
W\cap(\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda})
generates
W
as an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space. Conversely, every
C=W+\sigma
as above is bialgebraic for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
.

Proof.

A curve
C=W+\sigma
as in the statement projects to a translate of a one-dimensional complex torus inside
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\times\mathbb{C}/\overline{\Lambda}
, hence to an algebraic curve in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
. Conversely, a bialgebraic curve
C
for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
has image in
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\times\mathbb{C}/\overline{\Lambda}
that is not Zariski dense. Therefore,
[
Pil11
, Theorem 9.2]
implies that the coordinate functions on
C
cannot be geodesically independent, in the sense of
[
Pil11
, Definition 1.5(2)]
. It follows that
C
is contained in, hence equal to, a line
W+\sigma
as in the statement.
∎

2.2.3 Remark
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
be an algebraic curve and
U\subset C
a neighborhood of a smooth point, biholomorphic to an open in
\mathbb{C}
, where
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
has no poles. The fact that, if
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}(U)
is contained in an algebraic curve, then
U
is contained in a line, follows from
[
BK77
, Corollary 2]
.

2.3.
Relating real and complex bialgabraic sets

Fix a lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}
.

2.3.1.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bialgebraic set that is not a singleton. Considering sums of squares of polynomials, we can write
\mathcal{V}
as the zero locus of a single polynomial
R\in\mathbb{R}[X,Y]
. By point (2) of Definition
2.1.1
, the set
\mathcal{V}
must be the vanishing locus of some irreducible factor
P
of
R
. Furthermore, since
\mathcal{V}
is infinite, the polynomial
P
is also irreducible in
\mathbb{C}[X,Y]
(otherwise, we could write
P=S\overline{S}
for an irreducible polynomial
S\in\mathbb{C}[X,Y]
that is not a multiple of a real polynomial, and both
S
and
\overline{S}
would vanish on
\mathcal{V}
, contradicting Bézout’s theorem).

2.3.2.
The base change diagram

Consider the maps

\begin{aligned} f\colon&\mathbb{C}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}\\
&(v,w)\mapsto(v+iw,v-iw)\end{aligned}\quad\hskip 14.22636pt\quad\begin{aligned} g\colon&\mathbb{C}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}\\
&(a,b)\mapsto\left(\frac{a+b}{2},\frac{a-b}{2i}\right).\end{aligned}

As
\overline{\wp_{\Lambda}(z)}=\wp_{\overline{\Lambda}}(\overline{z})
, we have the following commutative diagram:

(2.3.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbb{C})^{2}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota\circ g^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}}

The left vertical and the top right horizontal map above are induced by the inclusion
\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}
, and the map
\iota:\mathbb{C}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})^{2}
is given by the inclusion
\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
on each component.

2.3.3 Lemma
.

Let
C_{P}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
be the complex curve with equation
P=0
. The curve
f(C_{P})
is bialgebraic for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{V}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda)
. Since
\mathcal{V}
is bialgebraic, there exists a non-zero polynomial
Q\in\mathbb{R}[X,Y]
such that
\mathcal{P}(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
is contained in the real algebraic curve with equation
Q=0
. Let
C_{Q}
be the complex plane curve with equation
Q=0
, and let
\hat{C}_{Q}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})^{2}
be the Zariski closure of
\iota\circ g^{-1}(C_{Q})
.

Let
q=\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}\circ f
. The commutativity of the diagram implies that
A=C_{P}\cap q^{-1}(\hat{C}_{Q})
contains
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
. Note that, as
\mathcal{V}
is infinite, it contains a smooth point of
C_{P}
, hence
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
contains a subspace homeomorphic to an open interval. It follows that
A
contains an open subset of
C_{P}
. On the other hand, since
P
is irreducible, the curve
C_{P}
satisfies the equivalent conditions of
[
GR84
, Theorem, p. 168]
. As the analytic subset
A\subset C_{P}
is not thin (in the sense of
[
GR84
, p. 132]
), we must have
A=C_{P}
, therefore
q(C_{P})\subset\hat{C}_{Q}
.
∎

The above lemma tells us that a bialgebraic set for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
that is not a singleton gives rise to a curve in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
bialgebraic for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
. In the other direction, we have the following result.

2.3.4 Lemma
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a non-empty subset of the form
\mathcal{V}=V(\mathbb{R})
for some algebraic subvariety
V\subset\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
, satisfying condition (2) in Definition
2.1.1
. If
f(\mathcal{V})
is contained in a bialgebraic curve for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
then
\mathcal{V}
is weakly bialgebraic for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
.

Proof.

By the commutativity of the diagram (
2.3.1
), the image of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{V}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda))
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is contained in an algebraic curve in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, vanishing locus of a non-constant polynomial
R\in\mathbb{C}[X,Y]
. Therefore, the set
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{V}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda))
is contained in the real vanishing locus of
R\overline{R}\in\mathbb{R}[X,Y]
; hence, the set
\mathcal{V}
is bialgebraic.
∎

2.4.
Linear description of weakly bialgebraic subsets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}

The notation of
2.3.1
is in force.

2.4.1.

By Theorem
2.2.2
and Lemma
2.3.3
, we can write
f(C_{P})=W+\sigma
for some
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
and some one-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector subspace
W
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
of the form
\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
, with
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
belonging to
\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}
. In particular, we have

(2.4.1)

f(\mathcal{V})\subset W+\sigma.

Let us write
w_{1}=(w_{11},w_{12})\in\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}
and
w_{2}=(w_{21},w_{22})\in\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}
. Note that, since
\mathcal{V}
is not a singleton, the subspace
W
cannot be equal to
\{0\}\times\mathbb{C}
. Thus, we may write

W=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid y=rx\}

for some
r\in\mathbb{C}
, and we find

(2.4.2)

w_{12}=rw_{11}\text{ and }w_{22}=rw_{21}.

We also note for future use that, since
W
is a one-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space generated by
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
as a real vector space, there exists
t\in\mathbb{C}\smallsetminus\mathbb{R}
such that
w_{1}=tw_{2}
, hence

(2.4.3)

w_{11}=tw_{21}\text{ and }w_{12}=tw_{22}.

2.4.2.

Take two distinct elements
(x,y)
and
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathcal{V}
, and let
(x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime\prime})=(x-x^{\prime},y-y^{\prime})
. By (
2.4.1
) and the definition of
f
we have

x^{\prime\prime}-iy^{\prime\prime}=r(x^{\prime\prime}+iy^{\prime\prime}),\text{ hence }|r|=1.

Write, thanks to Hilbert’s Theorem 90,
r=\frac{\rho}{\overline{\rho}}
with
\rho\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
; we see that
\mathcal{V}
is contained in - and hence equal to - a translate of the line
\rho^{-1}\mathbb{R}
.

2.4.3 Lemma
.

Let
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a line passing through the origin. If
L
is weakly bialgebraic, then all its translates are weakly bialgebraic as well.

Proof.

We have
f(L)\subset W+\sigma
for some
W
and
\sigma
as in (
2.4.1
). Hence, for every
(c_{1},c_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the set
f(L+(c_{1},c_{2}))
is contained in
W+\sigma+(c_{1}+ic_{2},c_{1}-ic_{2})
. The latter is bialgebraic for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
by Theorem
2.2.2
; thus, the set
L+(c_{1},c_{2})
is weakly bialgebraic by Lemma
2.3.4
.
∎

2.5.
Proof of Theorem
2.1.3
when
\Lambda
is not isogenous to
\overline{\Lambda}

Let us prove Theorem
2.1.3
(1). Assume that there is a bialgebraic subset
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
which is not a singleton; we use the notation of
2.3.1
and
2.4.1
. Let
\Lambda_{1}=\langle w_{11},w_{21}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\Lambda_{2}=\langle w_{12},w_{22}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
. We claim that
w_{11}
and
w_{21}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, by (
2.4.2
) the numbers
w_{11}
and
w_{21}
are non-zero. If
c\in\mathbb{R}
is such that
w_{21}=cw_{11}
then (
2.4.3
) yields
w_{21}=ctw_{21}
, hence
ct=1
; this contradicts the fact that
t\not\in\mathbb{R}
.

It follows that
\Lambda_{1}
is a full sublattice of
\Lambda
; since
r\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2}
, the lattice
\Lambda
is isogenous to
\overline{\Lambda}
.

2.6.
Proof of Theorem
2.1.3
when
\Lambda
is isogenous to
\overline{\Lambda}
and not CM

Assume that
\Lambda
is not CM and that
\Lambda
is isogenous to
\overline{\Lambda}
. Choose
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\overline{\Lambda})=\mathbb{Z}\cdot\gamma
. Note that
|\gamma|^{2}\Lambda\subset\Lambda
, therefore
|\gamma|^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bialgebraic subset which is not a singleton, and let
r,W
be as in
2.4.1
.

2.6.1 Lemma
.

In the above situation, we have
\gamma r^{-1}\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda^{\prime}=\langle w_{12},\gamma w_{11}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}\subset\overline{\Lambda}
. If
\gamma r^{-1}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
then (
2.4.2
) implies that
\Lambda^{\prime}=\langle rw_{11},\gamma w_{11}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank two, hence there exists an integer
N\geq 1
such that
N\overline{\Lambda}\subset\Lambda^{\prime}
. On the other hand, equation (
2.4.3
) implies that
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset t\overline{\Lambda}
, therefore we obtain
\frac{N}{t}\overline{\Lambda}\subset\overline{\Lambda}
. But
\overline{\Lambda}
is not a CM lattice, hence we must have
Nt^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, contradicting the fact that
t\not\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

2.6.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.1.3
(2)

With the notation fixed at the beginning of
2.6
, by Lemma
2.6.1
and the fact that
|r|=1
(cf.
2.4.2
) we have
|\gamma|\in\mathbb{Q}
. This proves point
(a)
. Let us now suppose that
a=|\gamma|\in\mathbb{Q}
, and let us prove point
(b)
. As observed above, we also have
|\gamma|^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, hence
|\gamma|\in\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore, note that

(2.6.1)

r=\pm\frac{\gamma}{|\gamma|}.

If in addition
\gamma
belongs to
\mathbb{Q}
then (
2.6.1
) implies that
r=\pm 1
. As observed in
2.4.2
, this means that
\mathcal{V}
is a translate of the
x
-axis or
y
-axis; by Lemma
2.4.3
, it remains to show that the
x
-axis and the
y
-axis are bialgebraic. The image of the
x
-axis (resp. of the
y
-axis) via
f
is contained in the line
W_{1}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid x=y\}
(resp.
W_{2}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid x=-y\}
). By Lemma
2.3.4
, it suffices to show that
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are bialgebraic for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
. Write
\Lambda=\langle\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
, and note that, for
i=1,2
, we have
\gamma{\tau}_{i}\in\Lambda
because
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, the elements
w_{i}=(\gamma\tau_{i},\gamma\tau_{i})
belong to
\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}
; furthermore, we have
W_{1}=\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
. Similarly, letting
w_{i}^{\prime}=(-\gamma\tau_{i},\gamma\tau_{i})\in\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}
for
i=1,2
, we have
W_{2}=\langle w_{1}^{\prime},w_{2}^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
. Therefore, Theorem
2.2.2
implies that
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are bialgebraic for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
.

If
\gamma\not\in\mathbb{Q}
, letting
\Lambda^{\prime}=\sqrt{\gamma}\Lambda
we have
a\Lambda^{\prime}=\sqrt{\overline{\gamma}}\gamma\Lambda\subset\sqrt{\overline{\gamma}}\overline{\Lambda}=\overline{\Lambda^{\prime}}
. Therefore, Theorem
2.1.3
(2)(b) holds true for
\Lambda^{\prime}
. Furthermore, multiplication by
\sqrt{\gamma}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\simeq\mathbb{C}
sends bialgebraic subsets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
to bialgebraic subsets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
. This yields a bijection between the sets of bialgebraic sets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
, sending the lines
L_{1},L_{2}
in the statement of Theorem
2.1.3
(2)(b) to the
x
and
y
axes.
Hence, the statement for
\Lambda
follows from the statement for
\Lambda^{\prime}
.

2.7.
Proof of Theorem
2.1.3
for CM lattices

Let us prove Theorem
2.1.3
(3). Let
\Lambda
be a CM lattice and
K=\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\Lambda)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{C}
; to prove our statement, we can replace
\Lambda
by
\alpha\Lambda
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Without loss of generality, let us work with
\Lambda=\langle 1,\tau\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
\tau
in the upper half-plane; we have
K=\mathbb{Q}(\tau)=\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\overline{\Lambda})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bialgebraic set that is not a singleton. We know from
2.4.2
that
\mathcal{V}
is a translate of a line
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
through the origin with the following property: there exists
r\in\mathbb{C}
of absolute value one such that every
(x,y)\in L
satisfies
x-iy=r(x+iy)
. Furthermore, by (
2.4.2
) we have
r\in K
.

Any line
L
as above intersects
\Lambda
at a non-zero point. Indeed, by Hilbert’s Theorem 90 there exists
\rho\in K^{\times}
such that
r=\frac{\rho}{\overline{\rho}}
, and
L
is the line
\rho^{-1}\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
(cf.
2.4.2
). There exists a non-zero integer
N
such that
N\rho^{-1}\in\Lambda
, therefore
N\rho^{-1}
belongs to
\Lambda\cap L
.

Conversely, if
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a line through the origin containing a non-zero point of
\Lambda
, then there exists
\rho\in K^{\times}
such that
L=\rho^{-1}\mathbb{R}
. Let
r=\frac{\rho}{\overline{\rho}}
and
W=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:y=rx\}
. Reversing the argument in the previous paragraph, we see that
f(L)\subset W
. As
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\overline{\Lambda})\otimes\mathbb{Q}=K
, there is a non-zero integer
N
such that
Nr\in\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\overline{\Lambda})
. Letting
w_{1}=(N,Nr)\in\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}
and
w_{2}=(N\tau,Nr\tau)\in\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}
, we have
W=\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
. Theorem
2.2.2
implies that
W
is bialgebraic for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
, therefore the line
L
is bialgebraic for
\mathcal{P}
by Lemma
2.3.4
, and the same holds true for its translates by Lemma
2.4.3
.

2.7.1 Remark
.

Let
\Lambda
be a lattice. The fact that bialgebraic lines for
\mathcal{P}
must be translates of lines through the origin meeting the lattice at a non-zero point can be proved directly, via purely topological considerations. Indeed, if
L
is a line such that
\Lambda\cap L=\{0\}
then the image of
L
in
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
is dense, hence the same is true for the image of
L\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda)
via
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\simeq\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
. Therefore,
L
and its translates cannot be bialgebraic. Theorem
2.1.3
tells us that in the CM case - and in no other case - this topological constraint is the only obstruction to bialgebraicity of lines.

2.8.
Weakly bialgebraic lines and their images

Let us give a more direct explanation of why the lines in Theorem
2.1.3
are weakly bialgebraic; we will focus on lines passing through the origin. We will first see explicitly why the coordinate axes in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
are weakly bialgebraic if
\Lambda=\overline{\Lambda}
, then argue that the general case can be reduced to the case of
\Lambda=\overline{\Lambda}
replacing
\Lambda
by an isogenous lattice.

2.8.1.
The case of self-conjugate lattices

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}
be a lattice such that
\Lambda=\overline{\Lambda}
. For all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\overline{\wp_{\Lambda}(x)}=\wp_{\overline{\Lambda}}(\overline{x})=\wp_{\Lambda}(x)
.
Thus, the image of
\{y=0\}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda)
through
\mathcal{P}
is contained in
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
. More precisely, this image can be described as follows: the map sending
z
to
[\wp_{\Lambda}(z):\wp^{\prime}_{\Lambda}(z):1]
induces an isomorphism between
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
and an elliptic curve
E_{\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
defined by a Weierstrass equation with real coefficients. Via the quotient map
\mathbb{R}^{2}\simeq\mathbb{C}\rightarrow E_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{C})
, the image of the
x
-axis is a circle in
E_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R})
containing the point at infinity, hence
\mathcal{P}(\{y=0\}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda))
is a closed half-line. For completeness we observe that, if
\Lambda=\langle\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a rectangular lattice with
\tau_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\tau_{2}\in i\mathbb{R}
, then the line
\mathbb{R}\tau_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{2}
projects to another circle in
E_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R})
, and to an interval in
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
via
\mathcal{P}
; cf.
[
Was08
, pp. 285, 286]
.

Finally, since the function
\wp_{\Lambda}
is even, we have
\overline{\wp_{\Lambda}(iy)}=\wp_{\overline{\Lambda}}(-iy)=\wp_{\Lambda}(iy)

for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, the image in
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
of the
y
-axis is contained in the set of points
P\in E_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{C})
such that
\overline{P}=-P
, and the image of
\{x=0\}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda)
via
\mathcal{P}
is contained in
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
.

2.8.2.
The case of Theorem
2.1.3
(2)(b)

Let
\Lambda
be a lattice as in Theorem
2.1.3
(2)(b). As explained in
2.6.2
, to describe weakly bialgebraic subsets we may reduce to the case of
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\overline{\Lambda})=\mathbb{Z}\cdot\gamma
with
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
. In this case, the intersection
\Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda\cap\overline{\Lambda}
is a sublattice of
\Lambda
, and it is self-conjugate. The following lemma implies that weakly bialgebraic sets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
are also weakly bialgebraic for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
.

2.8.3 Lemma
.

Let
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
be two lattices in
\mathbb{C}
. If
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
then weakly bialgebraic sets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
are weakly bialgebraic for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
.

Proof.

The function
\wp_{\Lambda}
is even and
\Lambda^{\prime}
-periodic, so there is a rational function
h
on
\mathbb{C}
such that
\wp_{\Lambda}=h\circ\wp_{\Lambda^{\prime}}:\mathbb{C}\smallsetminus\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Let
\widetilde{h}
be the restriction of scalars from
\mathbb{C}
to
\mathbb{R}
of
h
(viewed as an algebraic function on the complement of its poles); we have
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}=\widetilde{h}\circ\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
. Take
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
; if
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(\mathcal{V}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda^{\prime}))
is contained in the real points of a proper subvariety of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
, then the same is true for
\widetilde{h}\circ\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(\mathcal{V}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda))
.
∎

2.8.4.
The CM case

Let
\Lambda=\langle 1,\tau\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a CM lattice such that
\Lambda=\overline{\Lambda}
(every CM lattice is isogenous to one with this property). Let
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the
x
-axis. As explained in
2.8.1
, the image of
L\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\smallsetminus\Lambda)
via
\mathcal{P}
is contained in
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
. Furthermore, the argument in
2.7
shows that every bialgebraic line through the origin is of the form
\rho^{-1}L
for some non-zero
\rho\in K^{\times}
; up to replacing
\rho
by a non-zero rational multiple, we may assume that
\Lambda\subset\rho\Lambda
. As
\wp_{\Lambda}(\rho^{-1}z)=\rho^{2}\wp_{\rho\Lambda}(z)
, Lemma
2.8.3
shows that the bialgebraicity of
\rho^{-1}L
follows from that of
L
.

3.
A modular criterion for the existence of weakly bialgebraic curves

3.1.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}
be a lattice. The existence of bialgebraic sets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
which are not singletons rests on two main points.

(1)

One needs
\Lambda
to be isogenous to
\overline{\Lambda}
to guarantee the existence of bialgebraic curves for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
which are not horizontal or vertical lines; cf.
2.5
.

(2)

The existence of bialgebraic curves for
\wp_{\Lambda\times\overline{\Lambda}}
as above pulling back to curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
requires the more restrictive condition that there is a non-zero element in
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\overline{\Lambda})
with rational absolute value. This was established using
2.4.2
and Lemma
2.6.1
in the non-CM case; in the CM case, such an element always exists.

Note that
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\overline{\Lambda})
is non-zero, respectively contains a non-zero element with rational absolute value, if and only if the same property is true for
\mathrm{Isog}(\alpha\Lambda,\overline{\alpha\Lambda})
, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Let us therefore restrict to lattices of the form
\langle 1,\tau\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
, with
\tau
belonging to the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
. In this section, we will describe the subsets of
\mathbb{H}
corresponding to lattices satisfying conditions (1) and (2) above, in terms of the special geodesics studied in
[
Tam23
]
.

3.1.1 Proposition
.

Let
\Lambda=\langle 1,\tau\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a lattice with
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
.

(1)

The lattice
\Lambda
is isogenous to
\overline{\Lambda}
if and only if
\tau
belongs to a geodesic with endpoints in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
, or with conjugate real quadratic endpoints.

(2)

There exists a non-zero element in
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\overline{\Lambda})
with rational absolute value if and only if
\tau
belongs to a geodesic with endpoints in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
.

Proof.

(1)

If there is a non-zero element in
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\overline{\Lambda})
then
\tau=\frac{a\overline{\tau}+b}{c\overline{\tau}+d}
for some matrix
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of trace zero and negative determinant. Hence,
\tau
belongs to a geodesic as in point (1) - cf.
[
Tam23
, Remark 3.3.6, Proposition 3.3.7]
. Conversely, by the same remark and proposition, if
\tau
belongs to such a geodesic then
A\overline{\tau}=\tau
for a matrix
A
as above, hence
c\overline{\tau}+d
belongs to
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\overline{\Lambda})
.

(2)

If there is
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
with rational absolute value such that
\gamma\cdot\tau=a\overline{\tau}+b
and
\gamma\cdot 1=c\overline{\tau}+d
, with
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
|\gamma|^{2}
equals the absolute value of the determinant of
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
. If
c=0
, then
\tau=x+iy
belongs to the vertical line
x=\frac{b}{2d}
; otherwise,
\tau
belongs to the circle with center
(\frac{a}{c},0)
and radius
r
, with
r^{2}=\frac{bc+a^{2}}{c^{2}}=\frac{|\det(A)|}{c^{2}}
. Therefore
r
and the intersection points of the circle with the
x
-axis are rational.

Conversely, assume that
\tau
belongs to a geodesic with rational endpoints. If the real part of
\tau
is a rational number
\frac{a}{b}
then
b\Lambda\subset\overline{\Lambda}
. Assume that
\tau
belongs to a semicircle with rational center on the
x
-axis and rational radius; write the equation of such a semicircle in the form
(x^{2}+y^{2})+2\frac{d}{c}x-\frac{b}{c}=0
for some
b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
c\overline{\tau}+d
has rational absolute value and belongs to
\mathrm{Isog}(\Lambda,\overline{\Lambda})
. ∎

3.1.2 Corollary
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}
be a lattice. There are weakly bialgebraic sets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
which are not singletons if and only if
\Lambda
is isogenous to a lattice equal to its complex conjugate.

Proof.

If
\Lambda
is isogenous to a lattice
\Lambda^{\prime}=\overline{\Lambda^{\prime}}
, then there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\alpha\Lambda\subset\Lambda^{\prime}
, hence
N\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\alpha\Lambda
for some integer
N\geq 1
. Therefore,
N\frac{\alpha}{\overline{\alpha}}\Lambda\subset\overline{\Lambda}
; by points (2) and (3) of Theorem
2.1.3
, there are weakly bialgebraic sets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
which are not singletons.

Conversely, suppose that there are weakly bialgebraic sets for
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
that are not singletons. We may assume that
\Lambda
is of the form
\langle 1,\tau\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
. Theorem
2.1.3
and Proposition
3.1.1
imply that
\tau
lies on a geodesic with endpoints in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. Therefore, there exists a matrix
A\in M_{2}(\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}})
with non-zero determinant such that
A\cdot\tau
lies on the imaginary axis; the lattice
\langle 1,A\cdot\tau\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
is equal to its complex conjugate and is isogenous to
\Lambda
.
∎

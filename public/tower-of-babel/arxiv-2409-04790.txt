Introduction

Symmetric designs admitting flag-transitive automorphism groups are of most interest.
In 1987, Kantor
[
34
]
classified flag-transitive symmetric
(v,k,1)
designs known as projective planes. More generally, a celebrated result on the classification of linear spaces was announced in 1990
[
15

[

The approach to the classification of linear spaces starts with a result of Higman and McLaughlin
[
27

, according to which any flag-transitive group of a linear space must act primitively on the points of the space.
Buekenhout, Delandtsheer and Doyen
[
14

using O’Nan-Scott theorem for finite primitive permutation groups proved that
the socle of a flag-transitive group of linear space is either an elementary abelian group (affine type), or a non-abelian simple group (almost simple type), and the classification is proved by analyzing each of these possibilities.
The proof in the affine case is due to Liebeck
[
41

[

A

\mathcal{P}
and every pair of points is incident in
\lambda
blocks. An

\mathcal{D}
is a permutation on
\mathcal{P}
which maps blocks to blocks and preserves the incidence. The
full automorphism

\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{D})
of
\mathcal{D}
is the group consisting of all automorphisms of
\mathcal{D}
. A

of
\mathcal{D}
is a point-block pair

such that
\alpha\in B
. For

G

G
is said to be

\mathcal{P}
.
A
(v,k,\lambda)
-difference
set
D
is a
k
-subset of an additively written group
T
of order
v
such that the list of differences
\Delta D=\{\alpha-\beta\mid\alpha,\beta\in D,\alpha\neq\beta\}

\lambda

T

is called

. If
D
is a
(v,k,\lambda)
-difference set, the

of

, where
\mathcal{B}_{D}=\{D+x\mid x\in T\}

[
8
, Theorem VI.1.6]
. Conversely, every symmetric

design admitting a point-regular automorphism group arises in this way. The automorphisms of

, which are also automorphisms of

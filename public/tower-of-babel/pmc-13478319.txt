Hybrid GA-DQL approach for efficient task mapping of IoT applications in fog computing framework
Authors: Niva Tripathy, Sampa Sahoo, Norah Saleh Alghamdi, Gaurav Dhiman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Fog computing has emerged as a promising paradigm to extend cloud services closer to IoT devices, improving response times & reducing network congestion. However, efficient load balancing in fog computing is essential to maximize performance, reduce costs, ensure energy efficiency, & maintain a high quality of service, ultimately supporting the demands of latency-sensitive & resource-intensive IoT applications. The primary objective of task mapping in computing environments such as cloud, fog, & edge computing is to allocate tasks across available resources in an efficient & effective manner, particularly in fog computing, where resources are distributed & closer to end devices. This paper presents a hybrid approach that integrates Genetic Algorithm (GA) & Deep Q-Learning (DQL) for task mapping in fog computing environments. The objective is to minimize makespan & computational costs while maintaining high resource utilization. Our approach leverages a GA to perform initial task allocation by exploring a broad solution space, thereby enhancing convergence toward optimal scheduling patterns. This solution is refined using DQL, which adapts to dynamic environments by learning from continuous feedback and enabling real-time decision-making. By combining the exploration strengths of GA with the adaptive capabilities of DQL, the proposed method effectively manages task allocation & resource utilization. Experimental results show that our hybrid approach outperforms baseline methods, significantly reducing makespan & operational costs.

Introduction

The IoT has become an integral part of the modern digital ecosystem and has enabled connections among a wide range of physical objects, including sensors, smart appliances, industrial machinery, vehicles, and wearable devices. These interconnected systems generate vast amounts of data and enable many real-time applications, such as smart transportation, healthcare monitoring, smart retail, and smart automation. Nevertheless, IoT devices typically have limited computer processing, storage, and battery power, limiting their ability to handle large volumes of data in the field. Therefore, the services of IoT need to be scaled, which means that it needs efficient systems for processing and managing the data generated by the IoT.

Cloud computing has long been used to overcome these constraints by offering scalability, massive storage capacity, and a centralized focal point for data processing. The IoT devices create intensive tasks that can be offloaded to powerful remote servers using cloud infrastructure to run them efficiently. However, transferring large volumes of IoT data to centralized cloud data centres poses several challenges, including significant network delays, increased bandwidth usage, and potential service delays for latency-sensitive applications. Research has shown that cloud infrastructure alone might not be the best solution to the highly responsive demands of real-time IoT services, including systems of smart transport and healthcare1,2.

To overcome these shortcomings, fog computing, an extension of cloud computing, brings computation, storage, and networking services closer to IoT network edge devices. Fog nodes can consist of a gateway, router, base station, or edge server that performs local data processing and then sends the selected data to the cloud. Network congestion is minimized, response times for services are improved, and system scalability is increased by this distributed architecture. Besides, fog computing enables geographically dispersed IoT devices to be location-aware and support mobility, while enhancing energy conservation and bandwidth utilization3.

Although they have these merits, fog nodes’ computing capabilities are typically lower than those of cloud data centers. With the continued increase in the complexity and scale of IoT applications, the overall management of computing resources across the fog and cloud layers is an important issue. Specifically, task scheduling and resource allocation in fog-cloud settings should be guided by several performance goals, as the parameters to be minimized include response time, energy, load balancing, and efficient resource utilization. Past studies have shown that poor task scheduling can lead to overloaded fogs, high latency, and inefficient resource use in a distributed IoT system4. Thus, the development of smarter scheduling plans for IoT task execution across fog and cloud infrastructures is a topical research problem.

The task scheduling problem in distributed fog-cloud environments is typically modeled as an NP-hard problem, in which several tasks with varying computing needs are assigned to heterogeneous computing resources. Conventional heuristic models are usually not capable of handling dynamic workloads and highly distributed computing environments. The solution to these problems has been the increased use of metaheuristic and intelligent optimization methods by researchers. The use of hybrid optimization strategies that combine evolutionary and swarm-based algorithms has yielded encouraging results in enhancing task scheduling performance in cloud and fog environments5.

In recent years, machine learning and reinforcement learning methodologies have been employed as efficient strategies for addressing sophisticated scheduling issues in distributed computing. Reinforcement learning enables an intelligent agent to learn optimal scheduling policies by interacting with the environment and to adapt to changing workload conditions. Specifically, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) addresses the problem of the complex decision processes and high-dimensional state spaces by combining reinforcement learning with deep neural networks. DRL-based methods have been successfully used for resource allocation, task offloading, and workload scheduling problems in edge and fog networks6,7. DRL can be effective to offload energy-efficient computations in mobile edge computing systems, and recent research has suggested using DRLs to model dynamic task scheduling in edge cloud systems8. Moreover, distributed DRA systems have been developed to optimize application placement and resource allocation on fog and edge systems9.

Even though DRL-based scheduling methods can handle dynamic environments, their performance may be highly dependent on exploration mechanisms and training stability. Conversely, evolutionary optimization methods like Genetic Algorithms (GA) can be useful for searching extensive solution spaces and finding near-optimal solutions to NP-hard problems. Genetic algorithms are biologically inspired by natural selection and heredity and thus allow candidate solutions to be improved through crossover and mutation. These features make GA particularly well suited to solving challenging scheduling and resource allocation problems in cloud-fog computing systems10.

Since evolutionary optimization and reinforcement learning are complementary, combining them may enhance task-scheduling efficiency in fog-cloud settings. Hybrid methods may also leverage genetic algorithms’ ability to conduct global searches to identify potentially useful solutions to the task allocation problem, and employ reinforcement learning to update scheduling decisions effectively in response to system feedback. These hybrid intelligent optimization plans can be used to substantially increase resource utilization, reduce time to complete the task, and improve the overall Quality of Service (QoS) of IoT-enabled fog computing systems.

Even though intelligent task scheduling methods have evolved significantly, there are several weaknesses in current research. Most reinforcement-based learning methods are task-offloading or time-based but exhibit slow convergence and instability when training in a highly dynamic fog world. Likewise, metaheuristic techniques like Genetic Algorithms (GA) offer strong global search but lack adaptive learning of real-time workload variations. Current literature seldom combines evolutionary optimization with deep reinforcement learning to use both global exploration and adaptive learning in the context of fog-cloud scheduling. Thus, it is necessary to have a hybrid intelligent scheduling framework that can effectively search the scheduling solution space and dynamically adapt to system conditions in IoT-based fog computing systems. To improve task-mapping performance by optimizing QoS parameters, we introduced a hybrid method that combines GA and DQL.

Related work

In this section, a systematic literature review is presented on task-mapping methods to optimize multiple QoS parameters and overcome computational and storage constraints. Ghafari and Mansouri11 developed a task scheduling technique for cloud systems that maps workloads to available resources using a grey wolf optimizer. Here, the VM management team assesses resource availability, and tasks are assigned to existing VMs if resources permit; otherwise, new VMs are created. Sindhu and Prakash12 presented the CBTSA algorithm to achieve a symmetric balance among energy efficiency, cost, and task scheduling. CBTSA uses a directed acyclic graph to represent the priority of the workload, and a node was chosen to process the workloads with the highest priority. The authors applied a reinforcement learning algorithm and a Markov decision process to improve decision-making for task mapping.

Faraji-Mehmandar et al.13 provides a Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) solution to minimize response time, cost, and energy consumption. In addition, the works treat resource provisioning as a time-series problem. Linear regression (LR) and autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) models are used to predict future workloads and deploy required resources in response to workload fluctuations. Ali et al.14 proposed an NSGA2-based technique to minimize the total computational time and system cost for task scheduling in heterogeneous fog-cloud computing environments. This approach assumes some knowledge of the submitted tasks to create the scheduling policy, making it unsuitable for dynamic and complex scenarios.

Shahidani et al.15 proposed a Q-learning-based algorithm to reduce task execution latency and balance the load in a fog cloud computing environment. However, this work does not account for inter-task dependencies or the heterogeneity of fog and cloud computing environments. Su et al.16 proposed a cloud-edge collaborative computing offloading model for transmission and computation energy consumption and designed a near-real-time computing offloading algorithm that effectively reduces total energy consumption and achieves performance close to the optimal solution. Researchers in17 proposed a cloud-edge collaborative computing offloading model to achieve good performance while minimizing transmission and computational energy consumption. In18 the authors proposed DDQNL-IST, a game-learning algorithm that combined DDQN and distributed LSTM with intermediate state transition to lower the complexity of offloading computation under time-varying conditions. DDQNL-IST used a distributed LSTM and double-Q learning to improve processing and prediction of time intervals and delays. The devices could exploit information asymmetry to obtain a better game-learning outcome.

In19, a multi-agent deep reinforcement learning algorithm, known as a post-decision state (GPDS), is introduced to mitigate malicious interference in wireless networks. By assessing the communication quality, spectrum availability, and the jammer’s strategy from the post-decision state, mobile users can optimize their transmission power and frequency to increase their SINR and channel throughput. In20, a joint multi-task offloading and resource allocation scheme is suggested for satellite IoT. It involved modeling tasks with dependencies as DAGs and using A-PPO to optimize the offloading strategy. This method aimed to enhance offloading efficiency, potentially improving performance and reducing task completion time.

Wang et al.21 introduced a Deep Reinforcement Learning-based IoT application Scheduling algorithm (DRLIS) to optimize response time and balance system load. The proposed approach identified the challenges of scheduling IoT applications in edge and fog computing environments, emphasizing the need for low latency and efficient resource utilization. The limited computational resources and the dynamic nature of the fog environment increase the model’s complexity. Alsubaei et al.22 note that task scheduling in cloud environments is NP-hard. To address this, they proposed two machine-learning-based strategies: Efficient K-means (E-kmeans) and K-means HEFT (KmeanH), aimed at optimizing performance by minimizing processing time and improving efficiency. Scheduling with the proposed method becomes challenging when IoT tasks depend on one another.

Cheng et al.23 proposed a novel Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)-based preemptive method. This method offers scalability and elasticity in the cloud computing environment and aims to optimize job execution costs while meeting users’ expected response times. Real-time job scheduling using the above method is challenging. Yang et al.24 developed a meta-reinforcement learning method for IoT task scheduling in an edge cloud environment. This will improve the task processing speed, optimizing task latency and device energy consumption. Scalability and computational overhead are the challenges in the proposed method.

Allaoui et al.7 provide a comprehensive review of RL- and DRL-based computation offloading mechanisms, analyzing recent research, classifying techniques, and identifying their advantages and limitations. It highlights the challenges posed by the growing number of IoT devices and the massive volumes of data they generate, and discusses how fog computing can mitigate issues related to bandwidth, processing latency, and security. The paper also analyzes various RL techniques, their objectives, strengths, and weaknesses, and outlines future research directions in this area. Fahimullah et al.25 explore the challenges of managing resources in fog computing environments, which are characterized by their distributed and heterogeneous nature. It provides a comprehensive analysis of the existing literature on machine learning techniques for resource management, including resource provisioning, application placement, scheduling, task offloading, and load balancing. The paper compares strategies, metrics, tools, and techniques used in these studies, identifies research gaps, and suggests future directions to advance resource management in fog computing.

Pandey et al.26 focus on optimizing energy consumption in cloud data centers that process large amounts of data. The authors propose a novel architecture that utilizes a Long Short-Term Memory Deep Q-Network (LSTM-DQN) approach, integrating reinforcement learning techniques to enhance energy efficiency. The paper highlights the limitations of traditional resource allocation methods. It compares the proposed strategy against various Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) variants and reports significant improvements in energy efficiency, task completion time, and overall energy consumption. Zheng et al.27 present a novel approach to workload scheduling in edge computing environments using deep reinforcement learning (DRL), specifically employing the Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithm. The authors address challenges such as unbalanced resource allocation, high user mobility, and intermittent traffic in edge computing scenarios. The proposed DRL-based scheduling aims to balance workloads, reduce service times, and minimize the rate of failed tasks. Simulation results indicate that this approach outperforms existing methods in terms of service time, virtual machine utilization, and task failure rates.

System model

This section discusses the system architecture, problem formulation, and the models used in this work.

System architecture

In this subsection, the proposed task mapping architecture is introduced for a fog computing environment.Fig. 1Proposed Integrated Cloud Fog Architecture.

Proposed Integrated Cloud Fog Architecture.

Figure 1 depicts the proposed Integrated Cloud Fog Architecture. It comprises three layers, i.e., the IoT layer, the fog layer, and the cloud layer. The IoT layer comprises various IoT devices, such as sensors and smart terminals. A large number of tasks are generated by these devices and sent to the next layer, i.e., the fog layer, which provides low-delay task processing. The topmost layer is the cloud layer, which consists of high-computation servers. The fog layer consists of several fog controllers. The fog controller consists of three sub-component schedulers, a DQL model for makespan & cost optimizer, and a load balancer. Tasks received from IoT devices are forwarded to the scheduler in the fog controller. The scheduler will forward the task to the DQL model. In the DQL model, the makespan and cost are optimized using the proper algorithm. The load balancer periodically collects information about available resources to maintain load balance. Figure 2 describes the detailed structure of the fog controller. Within the suggested structure, the Genetic Algorithm will be used to generate an initial population of candidate task-scheduling solutions. These candidate solutions are then used as the starting point for the Deep Q-Learning agent, which continues to fine-tune the task-VM mapping using system feedback to achieve optimal scheduling performance.Fig. 2Proposed Integrated Cloud Fog Architecture.

Proposed Integrated Cloud Fog Architecture.

Problem formulation

Proposed GA-DQL algorithm

This subsection discusses the proposed GA-DQL Algorithm. The proposed hybrid algorithm integrates Genetic Algorithm (GA) principles with Deep Q-Learning (DQL) to optimize task mapping in virtual machine (VM) environments, aiming to maximize fitness while balancing the load. The first step in the suggested hybrid architecture is to generate an initial population of potential task–VM mappings using the Genetic Algorithm (GA). The Deep Q-Learning (DQL) agent’s starting state is provided by these candidate solutions, which also represent possible scheduling setups. Then, using reward feedback to learn the best task allocation policies, the DQL component assesses and improves these solutions. In this manner, the DQL uses adaptive learning to enhance scheduling choices during runtime, while the GA conducts global exploration of the solution space.The hybrid integration of evolutionary optimization and reinforcement learning is responsible for the higher performance of the suggested GA–DQL technique. In order to prevent premature convergence, the GA component conducts global exploration to produce a variety of candidate task scheduling solutions. Then, using feedback from the system, the DQL component learns the best task allocation policies to improve these solutions. When compared to independent DRL-based algorithms, this combination allows the suggested framework to achieve superior scheduling efficiency and resource utilization.

Computational complexity analysis

The GA–DQL scheduling framework consists of two main components: the Genetic Algorithm (GA) and Deep Q-Learning (DQL). During the GA phase, candidate task–VM mappings evolve through selection, crossover, and mutation operations. Let P denote the population size, G the number of generations, T the number of tasks, and V the number of virtual machines. Conclusion

This study introduces a hybrid methodology that integrates Genetic Algorithm (GA) and Deep Q Learning (DQL) to enhance task scheduling within fog computing environments. By utilizing GA for extensive solution exploration and DQL for flexible decision-making, the proposed strategy successfully reduces both makespan and operational costs, addressing the requirements of dynamic and resource-limited fog systems. Experimental findings indicate that this approach surpasses conventional scheduling techniques, resulting in improved resource utilization, quicker response times, and lower computational costs, thereby rendering it highly appropriate for real-time IoT applications. This study presents a strong solution to the challenges associated with fog computing, especially for Internet of Things (IoT) applications that demand low latency and cost-effectiveness. Future research may investigate the incorporation of energy efficiency and reliability factors to further improve system performance, thereby facilitating the advancement of smarter and more resilient fog-based IoT networks.

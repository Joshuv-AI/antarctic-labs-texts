Low Dimensional Homology of the Yang-Baxter Operators Yielding the HOMFLYPT Polynomial
Anthony Christiana, Ben Clingenpeel, Huizheng Guo, Jinseok Oh, Józef H. Przytycki, Xiao Wang, Hongdae Yun

Abstract
. In this paper, we analyze the homology of the Yang-Baxter Operators R ( m ) R_{(m)} yielding the HOMFLYPT polynomial, reducing the computation of the n n -th homology of R ( m ) R_{(m)} for arbitrary m m to the computation of n + 1 n+1 initial conditions. We then produce the explicit formulas for the third and fourth homology.

1.1.
Background on Yang-Baxter homology

1.1.1.
Precubic modules and their homology

Let
k
be a commutative ring with identity (e.g.
k=\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}[y^{2}]
). Recall that
a chain complex
\{C_{n},\partial_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, concisely
(C_{\bullet},\partial_{\bullet})
, is a sequence of
k
-modules (abelian groups for
k=\mathbb{Z}
)
C_{n}

\partial_{n}:C_{n}\to C_{n-1}
such that
\partial_{n}\partial_{n+1}=0
for any
n
. One defines the
n
-th homology of a chain complex as

H_{n}(C_{\bullet})=\ker\partial_{n}/\operatorname{im}\partial_{n+1}
.

In this paper, we recall the homology theory for Yang-Baxter Operators using the language of precubic modules:

Definition 1.2
.

A precubic module
\mathscr{C}
is a collection of
k
-modules
C_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, together with maps called face maps or face operators,

d_{i}^{\epsilon}:C_{n}\to C_{n-1},\penalty\ i=1,...,n,\penalty\ \varepsilon=0,1

such that

d_{i}^{\varepsilon}d_{j}^{\delta}=d_{j-1}^{\delta}d_{i}^{\varepsilon}\mbox{ for }1\leq i<j\leq n.

From such a precubic module, we can construct the the chain complex
(C_{n},\partial_{n})
by setting
\partial_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}(d_{i}^{(0)}-d_{i}^{(1)})
. The chain complex consequently leads to a homology (and cohomology) theory.

In this paper, we use the notation
d^{\ell}
and
d^{r}
(i.e.
\epsilon\in\{l,r\}
) in agreement with the graphical interpretation of our chosen face maps (see Figure
1.1
).
The homology of Yang-Baxter operators, as developed in
[

,
Prz-8
]
is defined as an interesting example of this general construction. In the next subsection, we construct precubic modules yielded by Yang-Baxter operators.

1.1.2.
Yang-Baxter operators

Let us recall the definition of the Yang-Baxter equation and Yang-Baxter operators.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
k
be a commutative ring and
V
be a
k
-module. If a
k

R:

V\otimes V\to V\otimes V

(R\otimes Id_{V})\circ(Id_{V}\otimes R)\circ(R\otimes Id_{V})=(Id_{V}\otimes R)\circ(R\otimes Id_{V})\circ(Id_{V}\otimes R),

then we say

is a pre-Yang-Baxter operator. The equation above is called a Yang-Baxter equation. If, in addition,

is invertible, then we say

is a Yang-Baxter operator.

From now on, we assume

is a commutative ring with identity and

is a free

Definition 1.4

Let

R_{(m)}:V_{(m)}\otimes V_{(m)}\rightarrow V_{(m)}\otimes V_{(m)}

R^{ab}_{cd}=\begin{cases}q&\text{if}\quad d=a=b=c;\\

This means for example that

2.
Duality and ordering

2.1.
Duality and strictly order-preserving maps

Functionally, the homology of
R_{(m)}
is a homology theory of finite ordered sequences. The boundary map
\partial_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}(d_{i}^{l}-d_{i}^{r})
has face maps
d_{i}^{\epsilon}
defined in terms of the Yang-Baxter operator,
R:V\otimes V\to V\otimes V
. In turn, we note that
R(a,b)=\sum_{c,d}R^{a,b}_{c,d}(c,d)
has Boltzmann weights depending
only on the relative ordering of

a,b\in X_{(m)}
. So we expect that maps which preserve the relative order of basis elements should accordingly preserve homology groups.

In this section, we put a finer point on this observation—that the relative ordering of basis elements is important for determining homology. First, we prove that maps between bases which strictly preserve order (including by completely reversing ordering) induce chain map isomorphisms. Then, we prove that weakly order-preserving maps yield chain maps.

The chain complex and homology of Yang-Baxter operators
R_{(m)}
admits a natural involution,
(\tau_{m})_{\#}:C^{m}_{n}\to C^{m}_{n}
and
(\tau_{m})_{*}:H_{n}(R_{(m)})\to H_{n}(R_{(m)})
,
n\geq m
, defined on the level of chain modules
C^{m}_{n}=kX_{(m)}^{n}=(kX_{(m)})^{\otimes n}
. This involution has a nice graphical interpretation; see Figure
1.2
.

We will describe this involution step by step. For fixed

and
X_{(m)}=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{m}\}

\tau_{m}:X_{(m)}\to X_{(m)}
is defined by

In fact, strictly order-preserving maps on bases of equal cardinality can be seen as compositions of the filling/creating gap maps, and therefore induce chain map isomorphisms.

We naturally identify

2.2.
Weakly order-preserving maps are chain maps

In fact, more is true, in that even weakly order-preserving maps
X_{(m)}\to X_{(m^{\prime})}
yield chain maps. Since such maps need not preserve Boltzmann weights, we make essential use of the column-unital condition.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
R_{(m)}
and
R_{(m^{\prime})}
be Yang-Baxter operators as in Definition
1.5
on
kX_{(m)}
and
kX_{(m^{\prime})}
, respectively. Then a weakly order-preserving map
f:X_{(m)}\to X_{(m^{\prime})}
satisfies the condition

R_{(m^{\prime})}\circ(f\otimes f)=(f\otimes f)\circ R_{(m)}.

Proof.

Applying both of the above to
(a,b)
, we get

\displaystyle(R_{(m^{\prime})}\circ(f\otimes f))(a,b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(c,d)}R^{ab}_{cd}\cdot(f(c),f(d))

\displaystyle((f\otimes f)\circ R_{(m)})(a,b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(x,y)}R^{f(a)\,f(b)}_{xy}\cdot(x,y).

The Boltzmann weights are nonzero only if
\{c,d\}=\{a,b\}
for the first sum and
\{x,y\}=\{f(a),f(b)\}
for the second sum. If
f(a)=f(b)
, then the only tuple involved is
(f(a),f(a))
, and so the coefficients of both sums above add to 1 by the column-unital condition. If instead
f(a)\neq f(b)
, then
f
preserves order strictly, and hence preserves the Boltzmann weights; thus, the two sums are again the same.
∎

Any map

The left and right boundary maps can be defined as

\displaystyle=L_{\text{wall}}\circ R\otimes\text{id}^{\otimes(n-2)}\circ\cdots\circ\text{id}^{\otimes(i-2)}\otimes R\otimes\text{id}^{\otimes(n-i)}

\displaystyle d_{i}^{r}

\displaystyle=R_{\text{wall}}\circ\text{id}^{\otimes(n-2)}\otimes R\circ\cdots\circ\text{id}^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes R\otimes\text{id}^{\otimes(n-i-1)}.

where we use

to mean the interaction with the left wall in which the leftmost element of the tensor basis is dropped, and similarly for

3.1.
Filtration by alphabet size

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{\bullet}^{p}/C_{\bullet}^{p-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

where

Proposition 3.1
.

The chain complex associated with this filtration is isomorphic to the associated graded chain complex:

C_{\bullet}^{m}\cong\bigoplus_{p=2}^{m}C_{\bullet}^{p}/C_{\bullet}^{p-1}.

It follows that:

H_{n}(C_{\bullet}^{m})\cong\bigoplus_{p=2}^{m}H_{n}(C_{\bullet}^{p}/C_{\bullet}^{p-1}).

Proof.

Since the inclusion
\alpha:C_{\bullet}^{p-1}\to C_{\bullet}^{p}
in the short exact sequence (
3.1
) comes from the strictly order-preserving map

, the induced map

∎

3.2.
Further reductions by filtrations

In the proof above, we have seen that the short exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

Definition 3.2
.

We define

for

inductively on

. Assuming

to be the quotient chain complex of

by

The short exact sequences of chain modules (

Proof.

The map

It would appear more natural to define the ‘final’ complex to be

Theorem 3.7
.

The chain complex

C_{\bullet}^{m,u}\cong\bigoplus_{j=u+1}^{m}\binom{m-u-1}{j-u-1}C_{\bullet}^{jf}

gives

C_{\bullet}^{m}\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}\binom{m-1}{j-1}C_{\bullet}^{jf}.

Proof.

The proof is essentially contained in Figure

\displaystyle C_{\bullet}^{m,u}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{j=u+1}^{m-1}\binom{m-u-2}{j-u-1}C_{\bullet}^{jf}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=u+2}^{m}\binom{m-u-2}{j-u-2}C_{\bullet}^{jf}

5.
Future work, speculations, and conjectures

Both Yang-Baxter Homology and Khovanov-type homology generalize the Jones and HOMFLYPT polynomials. Naturally, then, a long-term goal is to connect these two theories. Our work in computing the third and fourth homology of the Yang-Baxter Operator leading to the Jones and HOMFLYPT polynomials is just the first step in gaining intuition in this direction. Another possible method of relating these two theories might be to find a geometric realization for Yang-Baxter homology to which we can compare Lipshitz-Sarkar’s geometric realization of Khovanov homology
[
LiSa
]
.

In particular, we formulate the following conjectures about further calculations for the homology of
R_{(m)}
.

Conjecture 5.1

(Free Rank of Yang Baxter)
.

We conjecture that

\frank H_{n}(C_{\bullet}^{mf})=\begin{cases}1&m\leq n+1\\
0&m>n+1.\end{cases}

As a corollary, we would then have that
\frank H_{n}(C_{\bullet}^{m})
is

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m-1}{k-1}\frank H_{n}(C_{\bullet}^{mf})=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\binom{m-1}{k-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{m-1}{k}

when
m\geq n+1
and

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m-1}{k-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\binom{m-1}{k}=2^{m-1}

when
m\leq n+1
.

5.1.
Künneth subchain complex of
(C_{n}^{m},\partial_{n})

There is an intriguing subchain complex of
(C_{n}^{m},\partial_{n})
which is isomorphic to the tensor product of smaller chain complexes (this allows us to use a Künneth formula, as long as we work in a PID; say
\mathbb{Q}[y^{2}]
-ring). We conjecture that this subcomplex splits from
(C_{n}^{m},\partial_{n})
.
This subcomplex, denoted by
(C_{n}^{(m,B,A)},\partial_{n})
is constructed as follows:

Definition 5.4
.

Fix a decomposition of letters
X_{(m)}=A\cup B

and consider a submodule
C_{n}^{(m,A,B)}
of
C_{n}^{m}

generated by sequences in
X^{n}_{(m)}
which start with letters from
A
and end with letters in
B
. More formally:

X_{(n,m,A,B)}=\{(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n})\ |\ \mbox{ there is $i$ such that }a_{1},...,a_{i}\in A;a_{i+1},...,a_{n}\in B\}.

Proposition 5.5
.

If
A\geq B
; that is if
v_{i}\in A
and
v_{j}\in B
then

i\geq j
then

(1)

(C_{n}^{(m,A,B)},\partial_{n})
is a subchain complex of
(C_{n}^{m},\partial_{n})
.

(2)

If
(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n})=(u,w)
where
u\in A^{|u|}
,
w\in B^{|w|}
and
|x|
denotes the length of the word
x
then

\partial_{n}(u,w)=(\partial_{|u|}u,w)+(-1)^{|u|}(u,\partial_{|w|}w).

.

(3)

If
A>B
then
C_{\bullet}^{(m,A,B)}\equiv C_{\bullet}^{|A|}\otimes C_{\bullet}^{|B|},

that is
C_{n}^{(m,A,B)}=\sum_{i+j=n}C_{i}^{|A|}\otimes C_{j}^{|B|}
.

Proof.

(1) and (3) follow from (2). To show (2) we
use the fact than if in
(a_{1},...,a_{n})
we have
a_{i}\geq a_{j}
for some
j
then
d_{j}^{\ell}=d_{j+1}^{\ell}
and

d_{j}^{r}=d_{j+1}^{r}
.
∎

Conjecture 5.6
.

The short exact sequence of chain complexes

0\to C_{\bullet}^{(m,A,B)}\to C_{\bullet}^{m}\to C_{n}^{m}/C_{\bullet}^{(m,A,B)}\to 0,

splits. Thus homology
H_{n}(C_{\bullet}^{m})
splits.

The special cases of the conjecture, when
A=\{v_{m}\}
and

B=X_{(m)}
or
B=X_{(m-1)}

(i)

(ii)

The subcomplex

5.4.
More structure?

We conclude with two further possibilities for studying Yang-Baxter homology. First, we can use the involution
\tau
from Section
2
to introduce
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariant homology of
R_{(m)}
(see e.g.
[
Tu
,
BPS
]
). Second, when constructing the homology of Yang-Baxter operators
R_{(m)}
we naturally start from precubic modules. On the other hand precubic sets have natural geometric realizations (elements of
X_{n}
are the names of
n
-cubes and
d_{i,n}^{\varepsilon}
are gluing instructions). In fact, the homology theory for racks and quandles began with this geometric realization, see Roger Fenn’s paper
[
Fenn
]
. The geometric realization of the
homology of
R_{(m)}
seems to be more difficult, and reminds us of the discovery of the geometric realization of Khovanov homology,
[
LiSa
]
. Furthermore, we plan to analyze degenerate maps
s_{i,n}:C_{n}(R_{(m)})\to C_{n+1}(R_{(m)})
given by
s_{i,n}(a_{1},...,a_{n})=(a_{1},...,a_{i-1},a_{i},a_{i},a_{i+1},...,a_{n})
, and look for an analogue of the (very) weak simplicial modules introduced in
[
Prz-5
,
Prz-8
]
.

Navigating the Heart Rhythm Society meeting: A guide for fellows and early-career electrophysiologists
Authors: Estelle Torbey
Published: 2026

Introduction

With the upcoming annual meeting of the Heart Rhythm Society (HRS), this road map is intended to assist fellows-in-training and early-career electrophysiologists (EPs) in making the most of the meeting. The sections below highlight several components of the program that warrant particular attention.

Define your personal objectives before arrival

It is useful to review the program in advance and select the sessions that align with your personal and clinical interests. Personal interests span from seeking mentorship, exploring job opportunities, to building research collaborations. Late-breaking clinical trials and hands-on workshops can meaningfully enhance your expertise and clinical practice, along with this year’s global session on arrhythmias and sports.

Engage in early-career and fellows-in-training programming

HRS traditionally offers dedicated sessions for fellows and early-career professionals, including career development panels, trivia showdowns, board review sessions, and networking events that can broaden your perspective. There are sessions dealing with practical advice on contract negotiation, academic promotion pathways, work-life integration, and transitioning from fellowship to independent practice. Leave time to meet with prior colleagues or former mentors.

Seek mentorship actively

The HRS sessions are a great venue to meet with preidentified leaders that could serve as mentors, whether in clinical innovation, translational research, education, or leadership. Reach out before the meeting to request a brief coffee meeting. Otherwise, register for the roundtables run by senior leaders. Use these conversations to ask focused questions on leadership skills, pitfalls to avoid, and strategies to avoid burnout. For early-career professionals, discuss how to sustain excellence, how to improve resilience and communication, as well as how industry relationship works. Follow up afterward to maintain the relationship.

Present and promote your work strategically

If you are presenting an abstract, treat it as a launching pad to facilitate more visibility for your work within the electrophysiology community. Prepare a concise 2-minute summary of your work. Invite experts in your area to attend your poster or session. Share your findings confidently, and exchange contact information with those who show interest. This can open the door to further collaborations and invitations to other meetings.

As you present your own work, take time to visit other posters and learn from the research your peers are sharing.

Explore innovation and industry thoughtfully

The exhibit hall showcases emerging technologies, ranging from mapping systems and pulsed field ablation platforms to device technologies and digital health tools. Early-career EPs should attend sessions in the exhibit that not only describe new technologies but also discuss clinical trial designs and opportunities to enroll your patients in large multicenter trials. Ask critical questions about evidence, safety, and real-world applicability. Innovation literacy is becoming increasingly essential in modern EP practice.

Plan the follow-through

The benefits of the HRS meeting do not end at the end of the meeting. Within one week of the meeting, remember to send follow-up emails, share manuscripts discussed, and connect on professional platforms.

Conclusion

The HRS meeting represents a convergence of science, mentorship, and opportunity. The most rewarding aspects of the HRS meeting remain reconnecting with longtime mentors, colleagues, and friends. Beyond science and education, these reunions remind us that electrophysiology is a close-knit community built on shared experiences, collaboration, and mutual support.

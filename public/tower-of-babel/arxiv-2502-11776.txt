Parabolic Dijkgraaf-Witten invariants of links in the 3-sphere
Koki Yanagida

Abstract
We define a new invariant of links in the 3 3 -sphere and call it the parabolic Dijkgraaf-Witten (DW) invariant. This invariant is a generalization of the reduced DW invariant derived by Karuo. In this paper, we compute the invariant of several links over which double branched coverings are homeomorphic to the lens spaces. Moreover, we introduce a procedure for computing partial information of the parabolic DW invariant using only link diagrams.



Keywords

Dijkgraaf–Witten invariant, link invariant

Introduction

For a finite group
G
, the Dijkgraaf-Witten (DW) invariant
[
DW90
]
is a topological invariant of closed 3-manifolds, and is simply defined as an element in the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[H_{3}(G)]
.
Here,
H_{3}(G)
is the group homology of
G
.
Dijkgraaf and Witten established a method of computing the invariant from a triangulation of
M
.
However, when
G
is non-abelian, it is not easy to compute the DW invariant because it is generally difficult to determine the group structure of
H_{3}(G)
explicitly.

There have been several studies on generalizations of the DW invariant applicable to compact oriented
3
-manifolds with boundaries
[
Kim18
,
Wak92
]
.
In one such study, for the case in which
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, Zickert
[
Zic09
]
introduced the relative group homology and defined an invariant of 3-manifolds with boundaries.
Analogously, when
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
for some prime
p
, Karuo
[
Kar21a
,
Kar21b
]
defined a reduction of the DW invariant as an invariant of knots in
S^{3}
.
He suggested a method to compute the reduced DW invariants via ideal triangulations of knot complements; however, to ensure that Karuo’s approach works, many aspects of the method must be confirmed.

In this paper, we focus on the case
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and define the parabolic DW invariant as an invariant of links.
Here,
q
is a power of a prime
p
.
The point is that the parabolic DW invariant is defined as an analogue of the DW invariant of
B_{L}^{p}
, where
B_{L}^{p}
is the
p
-fold cyclic covering space of
S^{3}
branched over a link
L
.
Although the definition of the parabolic DW invariant is relatively simple, it turns out to be a generalization of the above reduced DW invariant defined by Karuo (Proposition
A.3
).
Moreover, we compute the parabolic DW invariants of several links whose double branched covering spaces are homeomorphic to some lens spaces (Theorem
2.3
).
As an application, we compute parabolic DW invariants of the
(2,2m)
-torus link and the
2m
-twist knot for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Appendix
B
contains the computations for
1\leq m\leq 50
. (Tables
2
and
3
).

Moreover, using quandle theory
[
Nos17
]
and techniques relating to branched coverings, we establish a procedure for recovering the reduced DW invariant from the parabolic DW invariant (see Theorem
3.2
and Proposition
3.3
).
This procedure is based on some quandle colorings of a link diagram.
Therefore, compared with Karuo’s computation
[
Kar21a
,
Kar21b
]
via ideal triangulations, our procedure allows easier computations of the reduced DW invariant.
As a corollary, when
7\leq p\leq 37
, we compute the reduced DW invariants of the prime knots with seven or fewer crossings (Table
1
).

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
examines the parabolic DW invariant.
In Section
3
, we introduce a computation of the reduced DW invariant with quandle cocycle invariants and present some examples of this computation.
In Appendix
A
, we present another definition of the parabolic DW invariant and show that it is a generalization of the reduced DW invariant
[
Kar21a
,
Kar21b
]
.
Appendix
B
provides specific values of the parabolic DW invariants for the
(2,2m)
-torus link and the
2m
-twist knot with
1\leq m\leq 50
.

Conventional terminology.

Throughout this paper, we fix a prime
p\in\mathbb{N}
and denote the field of order
q
as
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
q
is a power of
p
.
We do not specify the coefficient group when it is
\mathbb{Z}
.

2.1
Definition of parabolic Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant

Before defining the parabolic DW invariant, we begin by reviewing the DW invariant
[
DW90
]
.
Let
M
be an oriented closed 3-manifold, and let
[M]\in H_{3}(M;\mathbb{Z})
denote the fundamental class of

For a group
G

Fix a classifying map
\iota:M\to B\pi_{1}(M)

For a group homomorphism

(Bf)_{*}\circ\iota_{*}
.
Here,

is a continuous map induced by

.
Note that

is well-defined since
Bf
and
\iota
are unique up to homotopy.
Then, the
DW invariant
of
M

[
DW90
]
is defined to be the formal sum

\mathrm{DW}(M)\coloneqq\sum_{f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(M),G)}1_{\mathbb{Z}}\,f_{*}([M])\in\mathbb{Z}[H_{3}(BG)],

(1)

where

is the group ring of

over

and

is the unit of the group ring.
It is well-known that the group homology of

is isomorphic to the ordinary homology of

As stated in the introduction, it is not easy to generalize the DW invariant to
3
-manifolds with boundaries (see, e.g.,
[

]
).

Next, we define the parabolic DW invariant.
We consider the case where

The representation

\begin{pmatrix}1&*\\

\end{pmatrix}

Write

That is,

\mathrm{Para}(L,q)\coloneqq\{f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L),\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\mid f\text{ is parabolic}\}.

\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L)

Now, consider a parabolic representation

From the definition of the parabolic representation, it follows that the restriction

Let

Remark 2.2
.

1.

A classical result says that

3.

(5)

.

Let

We have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(Z_{1})&=f(Z_{2(q^{3}-q)+1})=f(Z_{4(q^{3}-q)+1})=\cdots=f(Z_{2\lambda+1}),\\

since
|\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=q^{3}-q

Thus, by repeatedly applying Lemma

and

Recalling the proof of Lemma

, which sends

Hence,

Computation from the quandle cocycle invariant

In this section, we describe a procedure for computing a reduction of the parabolic DW invariant (

) in terms of quandle cocycle invariants.
For this, we review the cocycle invariants to compute the reduction (see (
6

In addition, we compute the reduced invariants of several links; see Section

.

Throughout this section, we assume that the prime power
q=p^{d}
is generic and odd, and fix a non-square number
r_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}

3.1
Review of quandle cocycle invariants

First, we review some quandles and colorings. For
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, let
X_{r}
be the quotient of
\mathbb{F}_{q}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{(0,0)\}

the relation
(x,y)\sim(-x,-y)
for any

The order of

Define the binary map

-rc^{2}&1-rcd\\
\end{pmatrix}.

The pair
(X_{r},\lhd)
is sometimes called
the parabolic quandle
(see Example 3.15 in
[
Nos17
]
).
For a link diagram

-coloring

-coloring

and

Next, we briefly review the quandle (co)-homology.

and

3.2
Quandle 3-cocycles from the Bloch group

In the previous section, using qundle
3
-cocycles, we introduced a procedure for evaluating a reduction of the parabolic DW invariant.
While it is generally difficult to find non trivial 3-cocycles, the purpose of this section is to describe a quandle 3-cocycle
\psi
with
A=\check{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
; see Proposition
A.1
.

First, we review another complex and a chain map defined in
[
IK14
]
.
Let
Y
be a set acted on by
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. For example, if
Y
is either
X_{r}
or the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\mathbb{F}_{q}\cup\{\infty\}
, then
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
has a right action on
Y
.
Consider
C_{n}^{\Delta}(Y)
to be the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module generated by
(n+1)
-tuples
(y_{0},y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in Y^{n+1}
, and define the differential map by

\partial_{n}^{\Delta}:C_{n}^{\Delta}(Y)\to C_{n-1}^{\Delta}(Y),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ (y_{0},\dots,y_{n})\mapsto\sum_{t=0}^{n}\ (-1)^{t}(y_{0},\dots,y_{t-1},y_{t+1},\dots,y_{n}).

Let us define
C_{*}^{\Delta}(Y)_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}
to be the quotient complex
C_{*}^{\Delta}(Y)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}\mathbb{Z}.

This quotient has been extensively studied, e.g., in
[
Hut13
, Section 2]
.

Next, we introduce a chain map
\varphi_{*}:C_{*}^{R}(X_{r})\to C_{*}^{\Delta}(X_{r})_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}
as follows.
Let
p_{0}
be
(1,0)\in X_{r}
and let
\varphi_{0}
be the zero map. Furthermore, define
\varphi_{n}
with
n\leq 3
by

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p_{0},a,b)-(p_{0},a\lhd b,b),

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}(r,a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p_{0},r,a,b)-(p_{0},r\lhd a,a,b)-(p_{0},r\lhd b,a\lhd b,b)+(p_{0},(r\lhd a)\lhd b,a\lhd b,b).

Because we do not use any chain maps of higher degree, we omit the definition (see
[
IK14
]
for details).
The map
\varphi_{*}
, which is often called the
Inoue-Kabaya chain map
, induces a homomorphism

H_{3}^{Q}(X_{r})\to H_{3}^{\Delta}(X_{r})_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}
; see
[
IK14
, Theorem 3.4]
.
Thus, if we can find a suitable group
A
and a 3-cocycle
\kappa
of
C_{*}^{\Delta}(X_{r})_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}
,
we obtain a quandle 3-cocycle as the pullback
\varphi_{3}^{*}(\kappa)
.

Let us review the Bloch group of the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the abelian group presented by generators
[x]
with
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\}
subject to the relations

[x]-[y]+[\frac{y}{x}]-[\frac{1-x^{-1}}{1-y^{-1}}]+[\frac{1-x}{1-y}]=0,\ \ \ \ x\neq y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\}.

Let
\tilde{\wedge}^{2}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
denote the quotient of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}

by the subgroup generated by all
a\otimes b+b\otimes a
. It is easy to check
\tilde{\wedge}^{2}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
.
Consider the canonical homomorphism
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\tilde{\wedge}^{2}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
that sends
[x]
to
x\wedge(1-x)
.
The kernel is called
the Bloch group
, and is denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
; this kernel is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/((q+1)/2)
(see, e.g.,
[
Hut13
, Lemma 7.4]
).
If

, an explicit expression of the isomorphism
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cong\mathbb{Z}/((q+1)/2)
can easily be obtained with the help of a computer program; see
[
Kar21b
, Appendix A]
for
p=7,11,13
.
Moreover, let
\check{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the quotient group of
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
given by the following relations:

[x]=[1-\frac{1}{x}]=[\frac{1}{1-x}]=-[\frac{1}{x}]=-[\frac{x-1}{x}]=-[1-x],\hskip 23.49976ptx\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\}.

Consider the projection
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\check{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and denote the image of

.
When

Next, we construct a specific homomorphism

.
For distinct points

of

Because

according to
[

\Phi_{*}:H_{3}^{\Delta}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F_{q})}\to\check{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
In summary, we have the following:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

Appendix A
Another definition of parabolic DW invariant

Section
2
defines the parabolic DW invariant with branched coverings.
Concerning 3-manifolds with boundaries, there are other approaches to defining 3-manifold invariants like the DW invariant using the relative group homology (see
[
Nos20
,
Zic09
,
Wak92
]
). However,
it is not so easy to approach the (relative) fundamental class of 3-manifolds in terms of the relative group homology.

In the knot case,
we can briefly define the DW invariant with the relative group homology.
Let us review the relative group homology for groups
H\subset G
and a commutative ring
A
.
Let
C_{n}^{\rm gr}(G;A)
be the free
A
-module in which the basis is formed by the elements
G^{n},

and define the boundary map
\partial_{n}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\in C_{n-1}^{\mathrm{gr}}(G;A)
by the formula

(g_{2},\dots,g_{n})+\bigl(\sum_{\ 1\leq i\leq n-1}\!\!(-1)^{i}(g_{1},\dots,g_{i-1},g_{i}g_{i+1},g_{i+2},\dots,g_{n})\bigr)+(-1)^{n}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n-1}).

Furthermore, we define the relative complex by the mapping cone.
More precisely,
C_{n}^{\rm gr}(G,H;A)
is defined by
C_{n}^{\rm gr}(G;A)\oplus C_{n-1}^{\rm gr}(H;A)
, and the boundary map is defined by

\partial_{n}\bigl(g_{1},\dots,g_{n},h_{1},\dots,h_{n-1})=\bigl(\partial_{n}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})+(-1)^{n}(h_{1},\dots,h_{n-1}),\ \partial_{n-1}(h_{1},\dots,h_{n-1})\bigr).

Then, the relative homology is called
the relative group homology of
(G,H)
, and is denoted by
H_{n}(G,H;A)
.
If a space pair
Y\subset X
is an Eilenberg-MacLane space pair, the ordinary homology
H_{n}(X,Y;A)
is
known to be isomorphic to
H_{n}(\pi_{1}(X),\pi_{1}(Y);A)
.

For example, consider the case where
X
is a knot complement, and
Y=\partial X
is the torus boundary.
Then, the integral third homology
H_{3}(\pi_{1}(X),\pi_{1}(Y))
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
, and the generator
corresponds with the fundamental class of
[X,\partial X]
.
In addition, consider a meridian
\mathfrak{m}
and a preferred longitude
\mathfrak{l}
in
\pi_{1}(X)
,
and suppose there is a homomorphism
f:\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus K)\to G
such that
\{f(\mathfrak{m}),f(\mathfrak{l})\}\subset H
, where
G
is a group and
H\subset G
is a subgroup.
Then, we can canonically define the pushforward
f_{*}[X,\partial X]\in H_{3}(G,H)
up to sign. Here, the sign depends on the choices of
(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{l})
.

To form a comparison with the parabolic DW invariants, we consider the special case of
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and take
H
as the upper-triangular subgroup.
Note that the group isomorphisms
H\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2
if
q
is odd and
H\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}
if
q
is even.
In particular, as a result of the transfer map (see
[
Bro94
, §3.9]
), we can easily obtain
H_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}\wedge\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
In addition, when
q
is generic, we have the long exact sequence

H_{3}(H)\to H_{3}(G)\to H_{3}(G,H)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\to}}H_{2}(H)\to H_{2}(G)=0\ \ \ \ (\mathrm{exact}).

Because
H_{2}(H)
is annihilated by
q
and
H_{3}(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}/(q^{2}-1)
, we have the decomposition

H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),H)\cong H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\oplus H_{2}(H)\cong\mathbb{Z}/(q^{2}-1)\oplus(\mathbb{F}_{q}\wedge\mathbb{F}_{q}).

(7)

Denote the projection
H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),H;\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}/(q^{2}-1)
by
\mathcal{P}
.
Then, it is natural to consider the following definition:

\mathrm{DW}^{\prime}(K)=\sum_{f\in\mathrm{Para}(L,q)}1_{\mathbb{Z}}\,\mathcal{P}(f_{*}[X,\partial X])\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/(q^{2}-1)].

(8)

Proposition A.1
.

Let
L
be a knot
K
, and suppose that
q
is generic. With an appropriate choice of the projection
\mathcal{P}
, the definitions of the parabolic DW invariants are equal. That is,
\mathrm{DW}_{q}(K)=\mathrm{DW}^{\prime}(K).

Proof.

Let
X
be
S^{3}\setminus K
.
Consider the inclusion
E_{K}^{p}\subset B_{K}^{p}
, which induces the isomorphisms

H_{3}(B_{K}^{p})\cong H_{3}(B_{K}^{p},B_{K}^{p}\setminus E_{K}^{p})\cong H_{3}(E_{K}^{p},\partial E_{K}^{p})

according to the homology long exact sequence and the excision axiom.
Denote the composite of the isomorphism by
\delta
. By definition,
\delta([B_{p}^{K}])=\pm[E_{K}^{p},\partial E_{K}^{p}]
, leading to

\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{res}(f)_{*}([E_{K}^{p},\partial E_{K}^{p}]))=\overline{f}_{*}([B_{p}^{K}])
for some choice of
\mathcal{P}
.
Moreover, let
\mathrm{cov}:E_{K}^{p}\to S^{3}\setminus K
be the cyclic covering.
Then,
\mathrm{cov}_{*}[E_{K}^{p},\partial E_{K}^{p}]=p[X,\partial X]\in H_{3}(X,\partial X;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus, for any parabolic representation
f:\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus K)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,

p\mathcal{P}(f_{*}([X,\partial X]))=\mathcal{P}\circ\mathrm{res}(f)_{*}([E_{K}^{p},\partial E_{K}^{p}])=\bar{f}_{*}([B_{p}^{K}])\in H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})).

Replacing
p\mathcal{P}
by
\mathcal{P}
, we obtain the required equality from the definitions of the invariants.
∎

Remark A.2
.

In (
8
), we omit the information on the summand
\mathbb{F}_{q}\wedge\mathbb{F}_{q}
in (
7
).
However, the following discussion demonstrates that this information is largely determined by the pair
(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{l})
.
Because
\pi_{1}(\partial X)=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{l}
can be regarded as 1-cycles in the group homology
H_{1}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})
, and the cross product
\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{l}
generates
H_{2}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}.

Moreover, from the delta map of the long exact sequence, we can deduce that
H_{3}(X,\partial X)\cong H_{2}(\partial X)=H_{2}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}).

Thus,
\delta(f_{*}[X,\partial X])=\pm f_{*}(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{l})=\pm f_{*}(\mathfrak{m})\times f_{*}(\mathfrak{l})
.
Because the delta map
\delta
can be regarded as the projection
H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{F}_{q}\wedge\mathbb{F}_{q}
in (
7
),

\delta(f_{*}[X,\partial X])
is almost determined by the pair
(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{l})
as required.

A.1
Comparison with reduced DW invariant of Karuo

Next, when
q
is generic, Proposition
A.3
states that our invariants in (
2
) and (
8
) is a lift of
the reduced DW invariant of Karuo
[
Kar21a
,
Kar21b
]
.

First, we briefly review the reduced invariant.
Let
\nu K
be an open tubular neighborhood of
K
in
S^{3}
.
Because
\pi_{1}(\partial(S^{3}\setminus\nu K))=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
,
every essential simple closed curve
C
on
\partial(S^{3}\setminus\nu K)
is presented by
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
, where
a
and
b
are coprime.
Let us call
C

the
(a,b)
-curve
. Let
M_{(a,b)}(K)
be the closed 3-manifold obtained from
S^{3}\setminus\nu K
by the Dehn filling along
C
.
Then, for any parabolic representation
f:\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\nu K)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
the kernel of
f
is non-trivial, because
\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\nu K)
is of infinite order and
|\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|<\infty
.
Thus, we can choose a coprime pair
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
f(C)
is trivial; therefore,
f
induces

f_{a,b}:\pi_{1}(M_{(a,b)}(K))\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
However, the pushforward
f_{*}([M_{(a,b)}(K)])\in H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
depends on the choice of
(a,b)
.

To obtain knot invariants, Karuo considered the Bloch group
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and the Bloch-Wigner map
{\mathrm{BW}}:H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))\to\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
; see
[
Kar21a
, Section 2]
for the definitions.
Recall the groups
\mathcal{B}_{q}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\check{\mathcal{B}}_{q}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and the map
\widehat{\rm BW}
from Section 3.2.
Karuo showed that the quotient
\widehat{\rm BW}\circ f_{*}([M_{(a,b)}(K)])
in
\check{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is independent of the choice of
(a,b)
.
Then,
the reduced DW invariant

\widehat{\rm DW}(K,\mathbb{F}_{q})
of
K
is defined as the sum of the pushforwards
\widehat{\rm BW}\circ f_{*}([M_{(a,b)}(K)])
over all parabolic representations
f
in
\mathbb{Z}[\check{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]
.
Note that while the reduced DW invariant in
[
Kar21a
,
Kar21b
]
is deﬁned as a formal sum of all conjugacy classes of parabolic representations with respect to the general linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, this paper deﬁnes the reduced DW invariant as a formal sum of all parabolic representations.

Using a similar argument as in the proof of Proposition
A.1
, we can show the following.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
L
be a knot
K
.
Suppose
q
is generic. With an appropriate choice of the projection
\mathcal{P}
,

\widehat{\rm BW}(\mathrm{DW}_{q}(K))=\widehat{\rm DW}(K,\mathbb{F}_{q})\in\mathbb{Z}[\check{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]
.
In particular, the reduced DW invariant is a specialization of the parabolic DW invariant
\mathrm{DW}_{q}(K)
in (
2
).

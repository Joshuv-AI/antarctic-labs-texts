Out-of-plane angle resolved second harmonic Hall analysis in perpendicular magnetic anisotropy systems Preprint: APS/123-QED
Akanksha Chouhan, Abhishek Erram, Ashwin A. Tulapurkar

Abstract
Second Harmonic Hall (SHH) measurement is a standard and well accepted technique to estimate spin orbit torque (SOT) efficiencies in perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA) systems. Generally, field sweep and in-plane angle sweep SHH measurements are performed and SOT efficiency calculation is done using effective fields based formalism. In this article, we demonstrate an alternate experimental approach of out-of-plane (OOP) angle resolved SHH measurement in PMA systems for SOT efficiencies estimation. Also, we present an alternate formalism for SOT efficiency extraction, derived by solving LLGS equation in the low frequency limit of magnetic susceptibility. Along with SHH measurements, we also experimentally demonstrate anomalous Hall effect (AHE) based spin-torque ferromagnetic resonance (STFMR) for PMA systems.



I
Introduction

Spintronic memory devices rely on magnetization switching caused by spin orbit torques. Accurate estimation of these torques is necessary for proper design of competitive spintronic memory and logic devices. Various characterization techniques exist for estimation of SOT efficiency out of which spin torque ferromagnetic resonance (STFMR) and Second Harmonic Hall (SHH) are the prominent ones. The STFMR, specifically the anisotropic magnetoresistance (AMR) based STFMR
Liu et al. 2011
;
Tulapurkar et al. 2005
;
Bose et al. 2017
is conducive to in-plane magnetic anisotropy ferromagnet (FM)/heavy metal (HM) bilayer systems. This method does not lend itself to perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA) FM/HM bilayers as AMR is directly related to FM thickness and PMA FM layers are very thin
Wei et al. 2020
. Research on an alternate SMR based STFMR measurement in PMA systems has been reported in ref
Wei et al. 2020
;
He et al. 2018
;
He et al. 2016
. However, the SHH method
Pi et al. 2010
;
Kim et al. 2013
;
Hayashi et al. 2014
;
Garello et al. 2013
;
Pai et al. 2014
;
Yun et al. 2017
remains the mainstay for estimation of SOT efficiency in PMA FM/HM bilayer systems. The prevalent SHH method involves estimation of SOT efficiencies through low range field sweep
Kim et al. 2013
;
Garello et al. 2013
;
Pai et al. 2014
as well as through in-plane angle sweep
Avci et al. 2014
;
Roschewsky et al. 2019
;
Dutta et al. 2021
. Small angle out of plane angle sweep has been attempted in ref
Shirokura and Hai 2021
.

In the SHH measurement technique, a small frequency a.c. conduction current flowing through the heavy metal layer causes modulation in SOT and generated spin current which when injected in PMA ferromagnetic layer induces magnetization oscillations. These magnetization oscillations result in modulation of anomalous Hall effect (AHE) and planar Hall effect (PHE) resistances, which is detectable in the second harmonic hall voltage signal. Thus, from the SHH voltage signal, damping-like (
\xi_{DL}
) and field-like (
\xi_{FL}
) SOT efficiencies can be estimated.

In the SHH measurement technique as Hall voltage is being recorded, input current and recorded voltage are transverse to each other. The field sweep way of recording SHH voltage involves injecting a low frequency a.c. current in x direction and a transverse voltage measurement, once for an external magnetic field sweep in x direction (longitudinal case) and next for field sweep in y direction (transverse case). In the in-plane (azimuthal) angle sweep way of recording SHH voltage, the external magnetic field is rotated in the plane of the sample.

In this article we demonstrate an alternate approach of SHH measurement by an out-of-plane (OOP) (polar) angle sweep of the external magnetic field. A comparison of OOP angle sweep in two PMA systems – Pt/Co and Ta/CoFeB is also presented. Next we approach the problem of extraction of SOT efficiencies in SHH measurement by taking a different route wherein the low frequency limit of magnetic susceptibility is exploited to solve LLGS equation. At last, the results of AHE based STFMR for Pt/Co PMA system are shown.

II.2
Harmonic Hall Analysis: Formalism

The reference frame used in the analysis is shown in the Fig.
1
a. The ferromagnetic film is in x-y plane. The external dc magnetic field is applied at polar angle of
\theta_{H}
and azimuthal angle of
\phi_{H}
. The equilibrium magnetization direction is denoted by polar angle of
\theta
and azimuthal angle of
\phi
.
The free energy density of the FM is taken as:
f=\mu_{0}M_{s}[-\hat{m}.\vec{H_{ext}}-(1/2)H_{\perp}m_{z}^{2}]
, where
\hat{m}
denotes unit vector along magnetization direction,
M_{s}
denotes the saturation magnetization and
H_{\perp}
denotes the effective anisotropy field along z direction. The magnetic field acting on the FM is
\vec{H}=-(1/\mu_{0})(\partial{F}/\partial{\hat{m}})=\vec{H_{ext}}+\vec{H_{ani}}
with
\vec{H_{ani}}=H_{\perp}m_{z}\hat{m}
. The magnetization dynamics including the effect of charge current passing through the heavy metal is given by LLGS equation:

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{m}}{dt}=-\gamma_{0}(\hat{m}\times\vec{H_{eff}})+\alpha(\hat{m}\times\frac{d\hat{m}}{dt})

(1)

\displaystyle+\frac{\hbar}{2q}\frac{\gamma_{0}}{M_{s}\mu_{0}t_{FM}}J_{HM}[\xi_{DL}(\hat{m}\times(\hat{y}\times\hat{m}))-\xi_{FL}(\hat{m}\times\hat{y})]

where
\vec{H_{eff}}=\vec{H_{ext}}+\vec{H_{ani}}+\vec{H_{Oe}}
.
\vec{H_{Oe}}=\hat{y}J_{HM}t_{HM}/2
is the Oersted magnetic field produced by the current. Defining effective magnetic fields produced by the spin current as:

h_{DL}=\frac{\hbar}{2q}\frac{1}{M_{s}\mu_{0}t_{FM}}J_{HM}\xi_{DL}
and
h_{FL}=\frac{\hbar}{2q}\frac{1}{M_{s}\mu_{0}t_{FM}}J_{HM}\xi_{FL}
, the above equation can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{m}}{dt}=-\gamma_{0}(\hat{m}\times\vec{H_{eff}})+\alpha(\hat{m}\times\frac{d\hat{m}}{dt})

(2)

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}[(\hat{m}\times(\vec{h_{DL}}\times\hat{m}))-(\hat{m}\times\vec{h_{FL}})]

where
\vec{h_{DL}}=h_{DL}\hat{y}
and
\vec{h_{FL}}=h_{FL}\hat{y}
.
Note that the above equations are written for sub/FM/HM stack. For sub/HM/FM stack the expressions for
\vec{H_{Oe}}
,
\vec{H_{FL}}
, and
\vec{H_{DL}}
should be multiplied by
-1
.

The equilibrium magnetization direction is taken as x’ axis. i.e.
\hat{x^{\prime}}

=[sin(\theta)cos(\phi),sin(\theta)sin(\phi),cos(\theta)]
.
The y’ axis is chosen to lie in the z-x’ plane, and thus
the y’ and z’ directions are taken as:
\hat{y^{\prime}}=[cos(\theta)cos(\phi),cos(\theta)sin(\phi),-sin(\theta)]
and
\hat{z^{\prime}}=[-sin(\phi),cos(\phi),0]
. Note that since we have considered only uni-axial anisotropy along z-axis,
\phi=\phi_{H}
.
If a small magnetic field and damping-like magnetic field oscillating with angular frequency
\omega
are applied to the FM, the oscillating magnetization in the dash frame can be written as:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\delta m_{x^{\prime}}\\
\delta m_{y^{\prime}}\\
\delta m_{z^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\chi_{11}^{H}&\chi_{12}^{H}\\
0&\chi_{21}^{H}&\chi_{22}^{H}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}h_{x^{\prime}}\\
h_{y^{\prime}}\\
h_{z^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\chi_{11}^{I}&\chi_{12}^{I}\\
0&\chi_{21}^{I}&\chi_{22}^{I}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}h_{DL,x^{\prime}}\\
h_{DL,y^{\prime}}\\
h_{DL,z^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix}

(3)

where
\chi^{H}
denotes the Podar susceptibility calculated in the dash frame, given by:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\chi_{11}^{H}&\chi_{12}^{H}\\
\chi_{21}^{H}&\chi_{22}^{H}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{\gamma}{-\omega^{2}+(1+\alpha^{2})\omega_{1}\omega_{2}-i\alpha\omega(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})}

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}-i\alpha\omega+(1+\alpha^{2})\omega_{1}&-i\omega\\
i\omega&-i\alpha\omega+(1+\alpha^{2})\omega_{2}\end{bmatrix}

where
\gamma=\gamma_{0}/(1+\alpha^{2})
,
H_{0}
denotes the magnitude of the external magnetic field and

\displaystyle\omega_{1}=\gamma[H_{\perp}cos^{2}\theta+H_{0}cos(\theta-\theta_{H})]

(5)

\displaystyle\omega_{2}=\gamma[H_{\perp}cos(2\theta)+H_{0}cos(\theta-\theta_{H})]

(6)

The susceptibility due to the damping-like field is given by:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\chi_{11}^{I}&\chi_{12}^{I}\\
\chi_{21}^{I}&\chi_{22}^{I}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\chi_{12}^{H}&-\chi_{11}^{H}\\
\chi_{22}^{H}&-\chi_{21}^{H}\end{bmatrix}

(7)

The oscillating magnetization in the (x,y,z) frame can be written as:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\delta m_{x}\\
\delta m_{y}\\
\delta m_{z}\end{bmatrix}=R\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\chi_{11}^{H}&\chi_{12}^{H}\\
0&\chi_{21}^{H}&\chi_{22}^{H}\end{bmatrix}R^{T}\begin{bmatrix}h_{x}\\
h_{y}\\
h_{z}\end{bmatrix}

(8)

\displaystyle+R\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\chi_{11}^{I}&\chi_{12}^{I}\\
0&\chi_{21}^{I}&\chi_{22}^{I}\end{bmatrix}R^{T}\begin{bmatrix}h_{DL,x}\\
h_{DL,y}\\
h_{DL,z}\end{bmatrix}

where R is the rotation matrix connecting the two reference frames, given by:

\displaystyle R=\begin{bmatrix}cos\phi\,sin\theta&cos\phi\,cos\theta&-sin\phi\\
sin\phi\,sin\theta&cos\theta\,sin\phi&cos\phi\\
cos\theta&-sin\theta&0\end{bmatrix}

(9)

We can easily obtain expressions for the oscillating magnetization using equations
8
and
9

\displaystyle\delta m_{z}=sin\theta(\chi_{12}^{H}sin\phi-\chi_{11}^{H}cos\theta\,\cos\phi)h_{x}

(10)

\displaystyle-sin\theta(\chi_{12}^{H}cos\phi+\chi_{11}^{H}cos\theta\,sin\phi)h_{y}+\chi_{11}^{H}sin^{2}\theta h_{z}

III
CONCLUSION

In summary, in this article we demonstrated the out-of-plane angle sweep of the magnetic field approach for harmonic Hall measurement. The SOT efficiencies estimated through this method were found to be coherent with the SOT efficiencies obtained through field sweep SHH measurement. The SOT efficiencies were obtained from fitting the data by solving the LLGS equation for PMA systems in the low frequency limit of magnetic susceptibility. The effect of PHE on second harmonic Hall voltage was presented. Substantial value of
\Delta R_{PHE}
is seen to affect the
\xi_{DL}
and
\xi_{FL}
. Along with this, AHE resistance of the PMA system was exploited in the STFMR measurement to estimate the SOT efficiencies.

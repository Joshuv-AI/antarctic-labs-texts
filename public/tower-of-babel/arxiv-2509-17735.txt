Symbol Detection in Inter-Symbol Interference Channels using Expectation Propagation with Channel Shortening
Jannis Clausius, Luca Schmid, Laurent Schmalen, Stephan ten Brink

Abstract
Iterative message passing detection based on EP ( EP ) has demonstrated near-optimum performance in many signal processing and communication scenarios. The method remains feasible even for CIR , where the optimal BCJR ( BCJR ) detector is infeasible. However, significant performance degradation occurs for channels with strong ISI ( ISI ), where the initial LMMSE ( LMMSE ) estimate is inaccurate. We propose an EP -based detector that operates in a transformed signal space obtained by channel shortening. Specifically, instead of the conventional approach that iterates between an LMMSE estimator and a non-linear symbol-wise demapper, the proposed method iterates between a linear channel shortening filter-based estimator and a non-linear BCJR detector with reduced memory compared to the actual channel. Additionally, we propose a deliberate mismatch between the initialized messages and the initialized covariance used in the linear estimator in the first iteration for faster convergence. The proposed approach is evaluated for the well-known Proakis-C ISI channel and for CIR from a wireless measurement campaign. We demonstrate improvements of up to 6 dB 6\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B} at 2 2\text{\,} bits per channel use and an improved performance-complexity trade-off over conventional EP-based detection.

Introduction

EP
(
EP
) is a general framework for approximate Bayesian inference
[
1
]
.
The product of the likelihood and the prior is approximated with an exponential family distribution, usually a Gaussian, and iteratively refined.
The adaptation of
EP
to the communication scenario over
MIMO
(
MIMO
) and
ISI
channels
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
can be interpreted as an iterative message passing algorithm between a
LMMSE
estimator and a symbol-wise demapper.
More generally, messages are exchanged between a
LE
(
LE
) and a
NLE
(
NLE
).
In many cases, the true posterior can be closely approximated and near-optimal performance is achieved.
Moreover,
EP
-based detection remains feasible for long
CIR
where the optimal
BCJR
detector
[
5
]
is infeasible due to computational complexity.
However, in the case of discrete priors and a poor initial estimate of the
LE
,
EP
may fail to well-approximate the true posterior.
Consequently, improvements were proposed, e.g., carefully fine-tuning the hyperparameters
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
, adding neural components
[
9
]
, or passing messages based on a Gaussian mixture model
[
10
]
.

Here, we propose the application of
EP
in a transformed signal space obtained by linear channel shortening
[
11
]
.
The transformation to this signal space enhances the initialization of the
LE
and the
NLE
, which now operate in the transformed space.
The resulting transformed system model introduces memory, albeit less than the original channel.
To account for the memory, the symbol-wise demapper is replaced by a more suitable
NLE
, i.e., a trellis-based
BCJR
detector.
Note that the
BCJR
detector is based on the reduced memory of the transformed system model and does not scale exponentially in complexity with the memory of the original channel.
The memory of the transformed system model and the number of message passing iterations are hyperparameters that enable a fine-granular trade-off between performance and complexity.

Preliminaries

2.1
System Model

We consider a linear system model

\tilde{\mathbf{y}}=\tilde{\mathbf{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}+\tilde{\mathbf{n}},\qquad\tilde{\mathbf{n}}\sim\mathcal{C}{\cal N}(\boldsymbol{0},\tilde{N}_{0}\mathbf{I})

with uniform,
i.i.d.
(
i.i.d.
) transmit symbols
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\in\tilde{\mathcal{X}}^{N}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
from an alphabet
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}
with
|\tilde{\mathcal{X}}|=M
.
For
ISI
channels, the normalized channel matrix
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}\in\mathbb{C}^{(N+L)\times N}
is a lower triangular Toeplitz matrix with memory
L\geq 0
, i.e.,
{H_{i,j}=0}
for
{L<i-j}
.
This is equivalent to a convolution with a static
CIR

\tilde{\mathbf{h}}
of length
L+1
.
The equivalent real-valued system model is

\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{n}

(1)

with the decomposed components

\mathbf{H}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{R}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}})&-\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}})\\
\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}})&\mathcal{R}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}})\end{bmatrix},\;\mathbf{x}=[\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{x})^{\mathrm{T}},\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{x})^{\mathrm{T}}]^{\mathrm{T}}

(2)

and real-valued noise
\mathbf{n}\sim{\cal N}(\boldsymbol{0},N_{0}\mathbf{I})

with
N_{0}=\tilde{N}_{0}/2
and
SNR
(
SNR
)
E_{\mathrm{S}}/N_{0}
.
The task of symbol detection at the receiver can be approached via
MAP
(
MAP
) detection

\hat{x}_{i}=\arg\max_{x_{i}}P(x_{i}|\mathbf{y})\propto\arg\max_{x_{i}}\sum_{\sim\{x_{i}\}}p(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x})\cdot P(\mathbf{x})

(3)

where
{\sim\!\{x_{i}\}}
indicates the set of all
x_{j}
with
{j\neq i}
.
We are interested in the distribution
P(\hat{x}_{i})
that corresponds to the soft output of a detector.
The
SMI
(
SMI
)
{I_{\mathrm{SMI}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{I}};\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{I}})=1/(2N)\sum_{i}I(x_{i},\hat{x}_{i})}
evaluates the quality of the distribution in terms of the information rate.

2.2
BCJR Detector

The
BCJR
algorithm
[
5
]
is an optimal detector for the system model (
1
).
It relies on the forward and backward recursions
[
12
]

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}(s)

\displaystyle=\underset{s^{\prime}}{\max}^{*}\left[\alpha_{l-1}(s^{\prime})+\gamma_{i}(s^{\prime},s)\right],

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}(s)=\log\frac{1}{M},

\displaystyle\beta_{i-1}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\underset{s}{\max}^{*}\left[\beta_{i}(s)+\gamma_{i}(s^{\prime},s)\right],

\displaystyle\beta_{N}(s^{\prime})=\log\frac{1}{M},

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}_{i}(s^{\prime},s)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{l-1}(s^{\prime})+\gamma_{i}(s^{\prime},s)+\beta_{i}(s),

\displaystyle\mathrm{max}^{*}(a,b)

\displaystyle\triangleq\log(\mathrm{e}^{a}+\mathrm{e}^{b}),

where
\gamma_{i}(s^{\prime},s)
is a chosen branch metric from state
s^{\prime}
associated with
[x_{i-L},\dots,x_{i-1}]
to the next state
s
associated with
[x_{i-L+1},\dots,x_{i}]
, and
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}(s^{\prime},s)
is an updated branch metric.
Through these recursions and a final marginalization of
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}(s^{\prime},s)
, the algorithm produces symbol-wise
APP
(
APP
) estimates.
The complexity scales with
\mathcal{O}(M^{L+1})
and thus is only feasible for a small memory
L
.

2.3
Channel Shortening

A channel shortening filter
[
11
,
13
]
is a linear receive filter
\mathbf{W}
that transforms the system model (
1
) into

\mathbf{y}^{\mathbf{F}}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{F}},\quad\text{with}\quad\mathbf{F}\approx\mathbf{W}\mathbf{H},

(4)

where
\mathbf{F}
is a target channel with constrained memory, i.e.,
\nu<L
and
{F_{i,j}=0}
for
{\nu<i-j}
. The observed variable in the signal space defined by
\mathbf{F}
is denoted with
\mathbf{y}^{\mathbf{F}}
and
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{\mathbf{F}}
is an error term.
In the case of
ISI
channels,
\mathbf{F}
is Toeplitz and lower triangular.
The optimal filter, in the
MSE
(
MSE
) sense, is

\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{F}\left(\frac{1}{N_{0}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{T}}.

Subsequently, the new, transformed system model in (
4
) with reduced memory
\nu<L
can be processed by an
NLE
with reduced complexity, i.e., a
BCJR
detector with complexity
\mathcal{O}(M^{\nu+1})
.
If the target channel is memoryless (
\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{I}
), the
LMMSE
filter follows
[
14
]
.

2.4
Expectation Propagation

EP
is a general framework for approximate Bayesian inference
[
1
]
. For our application, it is convenient to view
EP
as a message passing scheme iterating between an
LE
and an
NLE

[
3
,
2
,
15
]
.
The
LE
and the
NLE
are derived by assuming that the prior and the likelihood in (
3
) are fully-factorized Gaussians
t_{\mathrm{A}}(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{i}t_{i}(x_{i})\sim{\cal N}(\mathbf{x}:\mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{A}},\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}}))
and
q_{\mathrm{A}}(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{i}q_{i}(x_{i})\sim{\cal N}(\mathbf{x}:\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathrm{A}},\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}))
, respectively. The subscript
\mathrm{A}
indicates
a priori
knowledge available from the other component.
The resulting estimators are

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{\mathrm{LE}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=p(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x})\cdot t_{\mathrm{A}}(\mathbf{x}),

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{\mathrm{NLE}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=q_{\mathrm{A}}(\mathbf{x})\cdot P(\mathbf{x}).

Subsequently, each estimator projects its respective result onto a fully-factorized Gaussian

\operatorname{Proj}_{i}(p(\mathbf{x}))={\cal N}\Big(\operatorname{E}\Big(\sum_{\sim\{x_{i}\}}p(\mathbf{x})\Big),\operatorname{Var}\Big(\sum_{\sim\{x_{i}\}}p(\mathbf{x})\Big)\Big),\;\forall i

where
\operatorname{E}(\cdot)
is the expectation, and
\operatorname{Var}(\cdot)
is the variance.
The projection consists of two steps.
First, the marginalized
PDF
(
PDF
)/
PMF
(
PMF
)
\sum_{\sim\{x_{i}\}}p(\mathbf{x})
is calculated.
Second, the
KLD
(
KLD
) between the marginalized
PDF
/
PMF
and a fully-factorized Gaussian is minimized using moment matching
[
1
]
.
As a result, only the moments of the messages
q_{i}(x_{i})=\operatorname{Proj}_{i}(\hat{p}_{\mathrm{LE}}(\mathbf{x}))\sim{\cal N}(x_{i}:\mu_{i},\sigma_{i})
and
t_{i}(x_{i})=\operatorname{Proj}_{i}(\hat{P}_{\mathrm{NLE}}(\mathbf{x}))\sim{\cal N}(x_{i}:m_{i},v_{i})
are passed between the
NLE
and
LE
.
The Gaussian projections are further divided by the a priori knowledge of the other estimator to obtain extrinsic messages

q_{\mathrm{A},i}(x_{i})=\frac{q_{i}(x_{i})}{t_{\mathrm{A},i}(x_{i})},\qquad t_{\mathrm{A},i}(x_{i})=\frac{t_{i}(x_{i})}{q_{\mathrm{A},i}(x_{i})}.

These messages can be seen as a priori knowledge and are also referred to as extrinsic information, cavity distribution, or unbiased estimate.
The corresponding moments are

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{A},i}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}_{i}}-\frac{1}{v_{\mathrm{A},i}}\right)^{-1},\qquad\mu_{\mathrm{A},i}=\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{A},i}\left(\frac{\mu_{i}}{\sigma^{2}_{i}}-\frac{m_{\mathrm{A},i}}{v_{\mathrm{A},i}}\right),

\displaystyle v^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A},i}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{1}{v_{i}}-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{A},i}}\bigg)^{-1},\qquad m^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A},i}=v^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A},i}\bigg(\frac{m_{i}}{v_{i}}-\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{A},i}}{\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{A},i}}\bigg).

Finally, convergence is aided by momentum-based updates

\displaystyle v^{(\ell)}_{\mathrm{A},i}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\beta}{v^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A},i}}+\frac{1-\beta}{v^{(\ell-1)}_{\mathrm{A},i}}\right),

\displaystyle m^{(\ell)}_{\mathrm{A},i}=

\displaystyle\left(\beta m^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A},i}+(1-\beta)m^{(\ell-1)}_{\mathrm{A},i}\right),

where
{\beta\in(0,1)}
is the step size,
\ell\in\left[0,N_{\mathrm{It}}-1\right]
indicates the iteration, and
N_{\mathrm{It}}
is the number of iterations.

Choice of the Transformed Signal Space

\tilde{\mathbf{F}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N+\nu\times N}

The real-valued equivalent
\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{2(N+\nu)\times 2N}

{\tilde{F}_{i,j}=0}

{\nu<i-j}

Thus, the optimal

3.2
Non-Linear Estimator

The
NLE
is an
APP
detector operating on the transformed system model
\mathbf{y}^{\mathbf{F}}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{I}}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{\mathbf{F}}
.
However, to apply the trellis-based
BCJR

\tilde{\mathbf{F}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N+\nu\times N}
must be considered.
Thus, we choose the log-domain branch metrics of the trellis by evaluating
q_{i}(x_{i})\sim{\cal N}(\mathbf{x}:\mu_{\mathrm{A},i},\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{\mathrm{A},i}^{2}))
at all
x^{\tilde{\mathbf{F}}}\in\mathcal{X}^{\tilde{\mathbf{F}}}

\gamma_{i}=\frac{\left(\mu_{\mathrm{A},i}-\mathcal{R}(x^{\tilde{\mathbf{F}}})\right)^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{i}}+\frac{\left(\mu_{\mathrm{A},i+N}-\mathcal{I}(x^{\tilde{\mathbf{F}}})\right)^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{i+N}}.

The estimate in the transformed signal space is the

\hat{P}_{\mathrm{NLE}}(x^{\tilde{\mathbf{F}}}_{i})=\operatorname{softmax}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i})
where
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i}
is the branch metric after the forward and backward recursion for all
M^{\nu+1}
possible symbols.
Adequately marginalizing
\hat{P}_{\mathrm{NLE}}(\mathbf{x}^{\tilde{\mathbf{F}}})
yields
\hat{P}_{\mathrm{NLE}}(\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}})
.

3.3
Linear Estimator

The
LE
operates in the transformed signal space
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}}
instead of
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{I}}
.
This means we calculate
{p(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}})t_{\mathrm{A}}(\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}})}
instead of
{p(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{I}})\tilde{t}_{\mathrm{A}}(\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{I}})}
where
\tilde{t}_{\mathrm{A}}(\cdot)
is a fully-factorized Gaussian prior with mean
\mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{A}}
and covariance matrix
{\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{A}}=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{A}})}
.
By using the relationship
{\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{I}}=\mathbf{F}^{-1}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}}}
, we obtain the likelihood
p(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}})\sim{\cal N}(\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{H}\mathbf{F}^{-1}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}},\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I})
.
From the perspective of the receiver, the cascade
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{F}^{-1}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}}
can be interpreted as the transmission of
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}}
over the effective channel
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{F}^{-1}
.
Note that
\mathbf{F}
is not necessarily a square matrix, and the pseudo-inverse
{\left(\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{F}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}}}
is used.
The resulting posterior is again a Gaussian distribution
p(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}})\cdot t_{\mathrm{A}}(\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}})\sim{\cal N}(\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}}:\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})
, with

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{N_{0}}\left(\mathbf{F}^{-1}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{F}^{-1}+\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{A}}^{-1}\right)^{-1},

3.4
Mismatched Initialization

Assuming only prior knowledge about the constellation alphabet
\mathcal{X}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{I}}

i.i.d.
with zero mean and unit energy, then
p(\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}})\sim{\cal N}(\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{F}}:\boldsymbol{0},\mathbf{F}\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}})
.
Since the
EP
framework assumes a fully-factorized prior, the natural choice is
{\mathbf{m}^{(0)}_{\mathrm{A}}=\boldsymbol{0}}
and
{\mathbf{v}^{(0)}_{\mathrm{A}}=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{F}\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}})}
as initialization.
However, using the full covariance matrix in the
LE
can significantly improve performance and simplifies the estimation in the first iteration, as shown below.
Inserting
\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{A}}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}}
as covariance in (
5
) and (
6
) and simplifying leads to

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{F}\left(\frac{1}{N_{0}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{F}\left(\frac{1}{N_{0}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{N_{0}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{y}\right).

Thereby, the inverse
\mathbf{F}^{-1}
vanishes from the equation, avoiding the potentially poor approximation of the pseudo-inverse and leading to improved estimates.
As a consequence, the classical channel shortening filter from
[
11
,
13
]
is recovered.
Still, using the full covariance matrix
\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{A}}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}}
contradicts the parallel
EP
framework, which assumes a diagonal structure.
Therefore, we propose setting
{\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{A}}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}}}
in iteration
{\ell=0}
, but initializing the covariance matrix of the message passing prior as
{\mathbf{v}^{(0)}_{\mathrm{A}}=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{F}\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}})}
, thereby ignoring the slight mismatch.
Note that the mismatch is twofold:
If
\mathbf{F}\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}}
is diagonally dominant, the mismatch effectively vanishes. If
\mathbf{F}\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}}
is not diagonally dominant, incorporating non-diagonal elements initially yields substantial improvements, such that the mismatch becomes beneficial.

5
Conclusion

We proposed an
EP
detector operating in a transformed signal space and evaluated it in terms of performance and complexity.
The algorithm iterates between a channel shortening filter-based estimator and a reduced-complexity
BCJR
detector.
In addition, we propose a deliberate mismatch between the initial variance passed to the
LE
and the covariance matrix used in the
LE
that improves the convergence speed.
Overall, the results demonstrate significant gains, especially at high rates, and a favorable trade-off between performance and complexity.

Analysis of risk factors associated with in-hospital mortality in severe HIV-negative PCP patients: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Qi Yanmeng, Li Yan, Zhu Huadong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWith the widespread use of corticosteroids and immunosuppressants, the incidence of pneumocystis pneumonia in non-human immunodeficiency virus patients (non-HIV PCP) has continued to rise and has led to a high mortality rate. This study analyzed the clinical characteristics of severe HIV-negative PCP patients to explore the risk factors that can predict the risk of death.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on the clinical data of 165 adult patients with severe HIV-negative PCP patients who were hospitalized at Peking Union Medical College Hospital from January 2019 to October 2024. Univariate analysis of variance and chi-square test were used to compare the differences between the death group and the survival group. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was employed to identify the risk factors for mortality; To further explore the efficacy of different treatment strategies, the study subjects were divided into three groups: group1 [Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole (TMP/SMZ) monotherapy)], group2 (TMP/SMZ combined with caspofungin), and group3 (TMP/SMZ combined with clindamycin). Chi-square test was used to analyze the differences among the groups.ResultsCompared with the survivor group, the death group had lower platelet counts and albumin levels, and higher rates of shock, mediastinal emphysema and tracheal intubation with mechanical ventilation (p < 0.05). Multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that albumin was negatively correlated with the outcome of death (OR 0.9; 95% CI 0.83–0.97; p = 0.005), while advanced age, cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection, and aspergillus infection might be positively correlated with the outcome of death, but the correlations were not statistically significant(OR 1.02; 95%, CI 1.00–1.05; p = 0.064)、(OR 1.62; 95%, CI 0.81–3.28; p = 0.178)、(OR 1.50; 95%, CI 0.71–2.21; p = 0.292). For patients receiving invasive mechanical ventilation treatment, multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that connective tissue disease was positively correlated with death (OR 2.88; 95% CI 1.02–8.15; p = 0.046), while albumin was negatively correlated with death (OR 0.88; 95% CI 0.79–0.96; p = 0.016). The number of patients in the three groups according to the treatment plan was 79 (48.5%) in group 1, 35 (21.5%) in group 2, and 45 (27.6%) in group 3. The rates of receiving invasive mechanical ventilation were (54.4% vs 77.1%, 79.6%, p = 0.013), and the mortality rates were (39.2% vs 51.4%, 65.3%, p = 0.041), with statistically significant differences (p < 0.05). This suggests that among severe HIV-negative PCP patients, the group using TMP/SMZ had the largest number of patients, the lowest rate of invasive mechanical ventilation, and the lowest mortality rate. Further analysis of the patients who received invasive mechanical ventilation showed that the mortality rates in group 1, group 2, and group 3 were (69.8% vs. 66.7%, 82.1%, p = 0.491), suggesting that the mortality rate in the group TMP/SMZ combined with caspofungin was lower than the groups TMP/SMZ monotherapy and TMP/SMZ combined with clindamycin, but the difference was not statistically significant (p > 0.05).ConclusionsHypoalbuminemia is a risk factor for death in severe HIV-negative PCP patients, while connective tissue diseases are another risk factor for death who receive invasive mechanical ventilation treatment. TMP/SMZ is the main treatment method for severe HIV-negative PCP patients. However, for patients who received invasive mechanical ventilation, TMP/SMZ combined with caspofungin may have a better therapeutic effect than using TMP/SMZ monotherapy and TMP/SMZ combined with clindamycin. Further research with a larger sample size is needed in the future.

Introduction

Pneumocystis jirovecii is an opportunistic pathogenic fungus that can cause severe and fatal pneumonia, commonly known as Pneumocystis carinii pneumonia (PCP) [1, 2]. With the continuous development of highly effective antiretroviral therapy and the implementation of routine preventive measures, the incidence of PCP among patients infected with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) worldwide has significantly decreased [3, 4]. However, with the widespread clinical application of tumor radiotherapy and chemotherapy, corticosteroids, and immunosuppressants, the susceptible population for PCP has gradually shifted from AIDS patients to non-AIDS patients [5–7]. HIV-negative PCP patients have more severe conditions and higher mortality rates [8–10]. Therefore, identifying the risk factors related to the prognosis of HIV-negative PCP mortality is crucial for improving the treatment process of HIV-negative PCP and reducing mortality.

Previous clinical studies have identified risk factors that affect the prognosis of patients with HIV-negative PCP infections, such as shock, the need for high-dose glucocorticoid therapy, respiratory failure, the requirement for mechanical ventilation, and ARDS, etc. [11–15]. However, these studies have certain limitations in terms of the study population, and there is a lack of relatively systematic prognosis research for HIV-negative PCP in clinical practice, as well as representative predictive indicators.

Currently, the preferred treatment for HIV-negative PCP is TMP/SMZ. For severe HIV-negative PCP patients or those with drug resistance, a second-line combination treatment regimen is often adopted. Common second-line treatment therapies include TMP/SMZ combined with caspofungin, TMP/SMZ combined with clindamycin, and clindamycin combined with primaquine, etc. The efficacy of various treatment therapies varies according to different studies [16, 17].

Therefore, we have detailedly described and analyzed the clinical data of 165 patients with Pneumocystis pneumonia who were HIV-negative. The aim is to identify the risk factors that affect the in-hospital mortality prognosis of HIV-negative PCP patients, and to conduct a comparative analysis of the effects of various treatment plans. This is to provide a theoretical basis for improving the prognosis judgment and treatment level of this disease, thereby optimizing clinical diagnosis and treatment plans.

Methods

Research design and subjects

This study was a retrospective single-center case-control study. We conducted a retrospective analysis of adult patients with severe HIV-negative PCP patients who were hospitalized at Peking Union Medical College Hospital from January 2019 to October 2024. Our inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) negative serum HIV tests; (2) meeting the diagnostic criteria for PCP [18]: ① cough, fever, dyspnea or hypoxemia and other clinical manifestations; ② imaging results consistent with PCP, such as bilateral ground-glass opacities or diffuse interstitial infiltration; ③ detection of Yersinia pseudotuberculosis in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF), sputum, bronchial secretions and lung tissue by silver methylamine staining or polymerase chain reaction (PCR); (3) The criteria for severe PCP diagnosis: oxygen partial pressure in breathing air < 70 mmHg and pulmonary artery-arterial oxygen partial pressure difference (A-aDO2) ≥ 45 mmHg [19].

Patients were excluded if they had incomplete medical records, were younger than 18 years, were pregnant, or had missing data for essential study variables.

The design of this study adhered to the “Helsinki Declaration” and was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital, with Ethics Approval Number K8993

Data collection

The demographic characteristics, underlying diseases, laboratory test results, whether there were other pathogenic microorganisms (bacteria, viruses or fungi) coexisting, respiratory support conditions (whether invasive mechanical ventilation was used), whether there were complications (mediastinal emphysema, shock), prognosis (whether death occurred) were collected. The determination of the outcome of the research subjects was obtained from the medical history. In the medical records, if the patient’s death was clearly recorded, they were classified as the death group. If the patient’s condition improved as recorded in the medical record, they were classified as the survival group.

The treatment plans for the research subjects were also collected. The total study subjects and the subjects receiving invasive mechanical ventilation were divided into three groups: group1 (TMP/SMZ monotherapy), group2 (TMP/SMZ combined with caspofungin), and group3 (TMP/SMZ combined with clindamycin). Invasive mechanical ventilation, shock, and death were used as outcome changes. The physician makes the choice of treatment plan mainly based on the severity of illness, adverse drug reactions, and coexisting conditions. TMP/SMZ is the first-line preferred treatment option for PCP patients. For patients with severe PCP infections, especially those requiring tracheal intubation and ventilator support, combined drug therapy is often necessary. TMP/SMZ monotherapy is not sufficient for treatment. For patients with suspected drug resistance, combined drug therapy is also necessary. Clindamycin is the preferred combined treatment drug. Caspofungin is a type of echinocandin antifungal drug and is mainly used as an auxiliary or rescue treatment in PCP treatment. However, when combined with aspergillus infection, sulfonamide combined with caspofungin is often considered for treatment. Additionally, economic conditions are also a factor that clinicians often take into account.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using R 4.4.2 software for statistical analysis. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and median (interquartile range [IQR]), while one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare quantitative variables, and chi-square test and Fisher’s exact test were used to compare categorical variables. We performed univariate analysis of the risk of death during hospitalization for severe HIV-negative PCP patients and these patients who received invasive mechanical ventilation. Variables with a p value less than 0.2 and those with clinical significance were considered for inclusion in the multivariate logistic regression models. The association between each variable and the in-hospital mortality was analyzed. The goodness-of-fit of the multivariable logistic regression models was evaluated using the Hosmer–Lemeshow test, and multicollinearity among independent variables was assessed by calculating variance inflation factors (VIFs).

Chi-square test and Fisher’s exact test were used to compare the differences among the three groups: group1 (TMP/SMZ monotherapy), group2 (TMP/SMZ combined with caspofungin), and group3 (TMP/SMZ combined with clindamycin), and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

Baseline characteristics

This study collected clinical data of 165 patients with severe PCP. The average age was 59 ± 14 years, with 58.2% being male and 41.8% being female. Connective tissue diseases (CTD) were the most common underlying diseases (n = 96, 58.2%), followed by kidney diseases (n = 15, 9.1%), hematological diseases (n = 12, 7.3%), hematological tumors (n = 12, 7.3%), inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) (n = 3, 1.8%), Cushing’s syndrome (n = 3, 1.8%), and organ transplantation (n = 2, 1.2%). PCP occurred concurrently with other pathogen infections, with bacteremia infections being the most common (n = 114, 69.1%), followed by cytomegalovirus (CMV) (n = 86, 52.1%), Aspergillus (n = 53, 32.1%), corona virus (n = 25, 15.2%), Legionella (n = 6, 3.6%), mycoplasma (n = 4, 2.4%), and tuberculosis (n = 1, 0.6%). Among the 165 patients, 111 (67.3%) received invasive mechanical ventilation, 57 (34.5%) suffered from severe septic shock, and the number of deaths was 83, with a mortality rate of 50.30%.

There were no statistically significant differences in gender, age, and underlying diseases between the death group and the survival group (p > 0.05). Compared with the survival group, the death group had lower platelet counts (155 vs. 204 cells per microliter, p = 0.008), lower albumin levels (29.8 vs. 32.6 g/L, p = 0.005), a higher rate of requiring invasive mechanical ventilation (98.8% vs. 35.4%, p < 0.001), a higher proportion of shock (51.8% vs. 17.1%, p < 0.001), and a higher proportion of mediastinal emphysema (21.7% vs. 6.1%, p = 0.015) (Table 1). Analysis of risk factors for In-hospital mortality in severe HIV-negative PCP patients

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were used to identify the risk factors for in-hospital mortality in severe HIV-negative PCP. Variables with a p value < 0.2 in the univariate regression analysis were included in the multivariate regression analysis. The results showed that albumin (odds ratio [OR] 0.9; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.83 - 0.97; p = 0.005) was negatively correlated with in-hospital mortality. Age, CMV, and Aspergillus were associated with mortality, with ORs of 1.02 (95% CI 1.00 - 1.05; p = 0.064), 1.62 (95% CI 0.81 - 3.28; p = 0.178), and 1.50 (95% CI 0.71 - 2.21; p = 0.292), respectively, but without statistical significance (p > 0.05) (Table 2). Further analysis was conducted on the risk factors for in-hospital mortality in 111 patients who received invasive mechanical ventilation. Comparative analysis of severe HIV-negative PVP patients according to the therapies

Group 1 (TMP/SMZ monotherapy) had 79 cases, accounting for 48.5%; Group 2 (TMP/SMZ combined with caspofungin) had 35 cases, accounting for 21.5%; Group 3 (TMP/SMZ combined with clindamycin) had 49 cases, accounting for 30.1%. Additionally, two patients treated with clindamycin and primaquine could not be classified into the therapy groups. Due to the small number of cases, they were excluded from the analyses involving treatment group comparisons. This suggests that sulfanilamide remains the main treatment option for severe HIV-negative PCP patients at present. The rates of receiving invasive mechanical ventilation in the three groups were 54.4%, 77.1%, and 79.6% respectively (p = 0.013), and the mortality rates were 51.4%, 39.2%, and 65.3% respectively (p = 0.041). The rates of receiving invasive mechanical ventilation and the mortality rates in the Group 3 were the highest, indicating that the patients in Group 3 were in a more critical condition and had the highest risk of death (Table 4). Discussion

The mortality rate of 165 severe HIV-negative PCP patients in this study was 50.30%, which was consistent with the reported mortality rates of 33.3% to 69.3% in previous studies [9, 10, 20–22–24]. Patients in the death group had a higher incidence of CMV, bacterial, and aspergillus infections than those in the survival group, suggesting that the death group often had multiple pathogen infections and treatment often needed to take these pathogens into account. The proportion of patients in the death group who received invasive mechanical ventilation, mediastinal emphysema, and shock was higher, at 98.8% (n = 82), 21.7% (n = 18), and 51.8% (n = 43), respectively. Previous studies have found that the mortality rate of HIV-negative PCP patients with pneumocystis pneumonia who received ventilator-assisted breathing could be as high as 62% to 76% [25, 26], while the mortality rate in this study was 77%, which may be related to ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) [27]. To correct the hypoxic condition, the ventilator parameters are usually increased, which may lead to pneumothorax, mediastinal emphysema, and subcutaneous emphysema, which may explain the poor prognosis [28–30].

This study evaluated the predictive factors for in-hospital mortality in patients with severe HIV-negative PCP and the patients received invasive mechanical ventilation. It was found that the albumin level was negatively correlated with mortality, and hypoalbuminemia was a predictive factor for in-hospital mortality in patients with PCP infection. Albumin is an important protein synthesized by the liver and plays a key role in maintaining plasma osmotic pressure and promoting substance transport. Albumin can reduce inflammatory exudation in the lungs and has antioxidant properties. During oxidative stress or infection, serum albumin levels decrease, and its antioxidant capacity is also impaired, ultimately leading to further tissue damage [31]. Existing literature indicates that a decrease in albumin levels is associated with severe pneumonia [32]. Serum albumin levels are considered a good predictor of morbidity and mortality in critically ill patients [33–35]. Previous studies have also shown that low albumin levels in kidney transplant recipients significantly increase the mortality rate of pneumonia [36, 37]. Additionally, some research has found that albumin levels are associated with poor prognosis in patients with sepsis [38, 39]. After the occurrence of sepsis, the high metabolism of the body leads to negative nitrogen balance, accelerating albumin breakdown. Hypoalbuminemia often indicates poor nutritional status and immune condition of the patient. Therefore, when treating severe HIV-negative PCP patients, albumin levels should be given attention, timely supplementation of albumin, and strengthening of nutritional support. In patients received mechanical ventilation treatment, the underlying disease being a connective tissue disease is another risk factor positively correlated with the death outcome. Analyzing the reasons, patients with connective tissue diseases often have pulmonary complications, including interstitial lung disease and pulmonary hypertension, etc. Mechanical ventilation treatment may increase the risk of hospital infection and ventilator-associated infection. This further aggravates the condition of the original disease and increases the mortality rate.

Previous studies have shown that there are gender differences in the survival rate of patients with PCP [31–34]. Due to the differences in immune system functions between men and women, women have a stronger immune response to pathogens, which helps them better control viral load and viral clearance, while men perform slightly worse in this regard [35]. However, this study did not find such gender differences, and the reason for this might be related to the small sample size. Moreover, this study also found that age, CMV infection, and aspergillus infection are positively correlated with the risk of in-hospital mortality, but it does not have statistical significance, suggesting that they may be predictors of the risk of in-hospital mortality, but larger sample size studies are needed to confirm this.

This study further discusses the clinical therapeutic effects of different treatment regimens. SMX-TM monotherapy is the preferred treatment for pneumocystis pneumonia in patients with PCP. SMX-TMP has the advantages of high efficacy and low cost. Its target is dihydrofolate synthase, and it mainly interferes with folate metabolism to inhibit the growth and reproduction of pneumocystis. The oral bioavailability is high [39, 40]. The pneumocystis cyst contains 1,3-β-D glucan, and the echinocandin class of drugs targeting 1,3-β-D glucan may be the only antifungal drug with therapeutic effect for PCP. Currently, the efficacy of echinocandin drugs in treating PCP remains controversial. In a retrospective clinical study of 80 AIDS patients with PCP in Europe [41], 10 patients received caspofungin as rescue treatment due to intolerance to the first-line treatment. 8 patients were successfully treated, 1 patient died of pneumothorax, and 1 patient died of lymphoma. This suggests that caspofungin rescue treatment is effective. Qi H, et al. analyzed 93 severe HIV-negative PCP patients and concluded that for severe HIV-negative PCP patients with Kaposi’s PCP, the initial combination treatment with caspofungin and TMP/SMZ is superior to using TMP/SMZ alone or as a salvage treatment combination [16]. Another prospective clinical cohort study showed that in AIDS patients with moderate to severe PCP, there were no significant statistical differences in overall mortality, treatment response rate, and adverse reactions after 4 weeks and 12 weeks of treatment among those receiving the TMP-SMX monotherapy regimen, the SMX-TMP + clindamycin combination treatment regimen, or the SMX-TMP + caspofungin combination treatment regimen. This suggests that combining caspofungin or clindamycin with TMP/SMZ did not significantly improve the patient’s outcome [17]. This study found that TMP/SMZ is still the most commonly used treatment regimen for severe HIV-negative PCP infections. However, more critical patients received invasive mechanical ventilation treatment tend to adopt combination treatment regimens, with TMP/SMZ combined with caspofungin and TMP/SMZ combined with clindamycin being the most common combination regimens. This study also found that for patients received invasive mechanical ventilation treatment, the mortality rate was lower for the combination of TMP/SMZ combined with caspofungin compared to the TMP/SMZ monotherapy and the TMP/SMZ combined with clindamycin group, but it was not statistically significant. This suggests that for these patients, the treatment regimen of TMP/SMZ combined with caspofungin may be superior to TMP/SMZ monotherapy and the TMP/SMZ combined with clindamycin, but a larger sample size is needed for verification.

This study has certain limitations. Firstly, the sample size of this study is relatively small, especially for those requiring invasive ventilator-assisted ventilation. Secondly, this study is a retrospective study and cannot provide definite conclusions. Future studies with larger sample sizes and prospective designs are needed to clarify the risk factors for death in HIV-negative PCP. Thirdly, the underlying diseases in this study were mainly connective tissue diseases. The research results may not truly reflect the situation of all high-risk populations for Klebsiella pneumoniae pneumonia. Future studies may need to conduct research on PCP infections in different underlying diseases to clarify the impact of underlying diseases on the risk of PCP death.

This study has certain limitations. Firstly, as a retrospective, single-center study, we have endeavored to include all available cases during the study period. Nevertheless, the sample size is relatively small. It should be noted that the analyses are exploratory and hypothesis-generating in nature, and subgroup comparisons (e.g., mechanically ventilated patients, treatment groups) may be underpowered. The findings should therefore be interpreted cautiously. Secondly, this study is a retrospective study and cannot provide definite conclusions. Future studies with larger sample sizes and prospective designs are needed to clarify the risk factors for death in HIV-negative PCP. Thirdly, the underlying diseases in this study were mainly connective tissue diseases. The research results may not truly reflect the situation of all high-risk populations for Klebsiella pneumoniae pneumonia. Future studies may need to conduct research on PCP infections in different underlying diseases to clarify the impact of underlying diseases on the risk of PCP death. Fourth, residual confounding may exist due to unmeasured variables such as baseline disease severity, immunological status, or co-infection burden. Although coexisting pathogens were recorded, their quantitative and temporal profiles could not be fully captured.

This study found that the mortality rate of severe HIV-negative PCP was relatively high, with a rate of 50.30%. The mortality rate of patients received invasive mechanical ventilation was as high as 73.4%. Low albumin levels were a risk factor for death in patients with severe HIV-negative PCP and these patients who received invasive mechanical ventilation. The underlying disease being connective tissue disease was a risk factor for death in patients received invasive mechanical ventilation. TMP/SMZ is the most commonly used treatment for severe HIV-negative PCP patients, but combination therapy is more common in patients who received invasive mechanical ventilation, TMP/SMZ combined with caspofungin may be more effective than TMP/SMZ monotherapy and the TMP/SMZ combined with clindamycin.

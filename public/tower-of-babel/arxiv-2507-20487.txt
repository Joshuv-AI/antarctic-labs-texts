On the multipoint distribution formulas of the parabolic Airy process
Zhipeng Liu, Aaron Ortiz

Abstract
The parabolic Airy process is the Airy 2 process minus a parabola, initially defined by its finite-dimensional distributions, which are given by a Fredholm determinant formula with the extended Airy kernel. This process is also the one-time spatial marginal of the KPZ fixed point with the narrow wedge initial condition. There are two formulas for the space-time multipoint distribution of the KPZ fixed point with the narrow wedge initial condition obtained by [ JR21 ] and [ Liu22a ] . Especially, the equal-time case of [ Liu22a ] gives a different formula of the multipoint distribution of the parabolic Airy process. In this paper, we present a direct proof that this formula matches the one with the extended Airy kernel. Some byproducts in the proof include several new formulas for the parabolic Airy process, and a generalization of the Andreief’s identity.

\int_{\Sigma^{n}}F(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\det\left[p_{i}(w_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}w_{i}=n!\int_{\Sigma^{n}}F(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}(w_{i})\mathrm{d}w_{i}.

(2.35)

(ii)

Assume
k\leq n
. Let
w^{\prime}_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq k
, be complex numbers on a contour
\Sigma^{\prime}
, and
q
be a function on
\Sigma\times\Sigma^{\prime}
. Then

\int_{\Sigma^{n}}F(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{j=1}^{k}q(w_{j},w^{\prime}_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}w_{i}

(2.36)

is antisymmetric in
w^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,w^{\prime}_{k}
.

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

For (i), one just needs to expand the determinant and use the antisymmetry of
F
. Now consider the second part. For any permutation of
\sigma:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we extend it to a permutation on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
by defining

if
i>k
. Then

\begin{split}\int_{\Sigma^{n}}F(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{j=1}^{k}q(w_{j},w^{\prime}_{\sigma_{j}})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}w_{i}&=\int_{\Sigma^{n}}F(w_{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,w_{\sigma_{n}})\prod_{j=1}^{k}q(w_{\sigma_{j}},w^{\prime}_{\sigma_{j}})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}w_{i}\\
&=\int_{\Sigma^{n}}F(w_{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,w_{\sigma_{n}})\prod_{j=1}^{k}q(w_{j},w^{\prime}_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}w_{i}\\
&=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\int_{\Sigma^{n}}F(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{j=1}^{k}q(w_{j},w^{\prime}_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}w_{i},\end{split}

(2.37)

where we relabeled the
w
-variables in the first step, and applied the antisymmetry of
F
in the last step. This proves the second part of the lemma.
∎

3
Equivalence of two Fredholm determinants

In this section, we further rewrite the Fredholm determinant formula in Proposition
2.1
and show the equivalence between this formula and (
1.1
).

We first conjugate the kernel
\mathbf{K}
and rewrite the Fredholm determinant
\det(\mathrm{I}+\mathbf{K})
to a new Fredholm determinant of an operator defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Recall the definition of the kernel
\mathbf{K}
in (
2.3
). It can be rewritten as

\mathbf{K}(i,z;j,u)=\int_{0}^{\infty}L_{1}(i,z;\lambda)L_{2}(\lambda;j,u)\mathrm{d}\lambda,

(3.1)

where

L_{1}(i,z;\lambda)=\begin{cases}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{f_{1}(z)}{f_{1}(v)e^{\lambda v}}\cdot\frac{1}{z-v},&i=1,\\
\int\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\prod_{\ell=2}^{i}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{\ell}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{f_{1}(z)}{f_{i}(v)e^{\lambda v}}\cdot\frac{\prod_{\ell=2}^{i}F_{\ell}(u_{\ell})}{(z-u_{2})\cdot\prod_{\ell=2}^{i-1}(u_{\ell}-u_{\ell+1})\cdot(u_{i}-v)},&2\leq i\leq m,\end{cases}

(3.2)

and

L_{2}(\lambda;j,u)=e^{\lambda u}.

(3.3)

The integration contours in (
3.2
) are the same as in the definition of
\mathbf{K}
in (
2.3
), but here we will make a specific choice of the contour for notation convenience when we do the contour deformations later. We take the
u
contour to be the same as
\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{L}}
, and the
u_{\ell}
,
2\leq\ell\leq m
, contours to be
\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{in}}
. We also set the
v
-contour to be
\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{R}}
. We will also use the contours
\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{out}}
,
2\leq\ell\leq m
. All these contours are defined the same way as in Section
2.2
. See Figure
2
for an illustration.

One can also view
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
as operators, and denote
\mathbf{L}=L_{2}L_{1}
the product operator with the following kernel, after using the above notations of the contours,

\begin{split}\mathbf{L}(\lambda,\theta)&=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{L}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}L_{2}(\lambda;i,u_{1})L_{1}(i,u_{1};\theta)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{L}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\Gamma_{1,\mathrm{R}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\prod_{\ell=2}^{i}\int_{\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{in}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{\ell}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\cdot\frac{f_{1}(u_{1})e^{\lambda u_{1}}}{f_{i}(v)e^{\theta v}}\frac{\prod_{\ell=2}^{i}F_{\ell}(u_{\ell})}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{i-1}(u_{\ell}-u_{\ell+1})\cdot(u_{i}-v)}.\end{split}

(3.4)

Recall that
\det(\mathrm{I}+\mathbf{K})
is well-defined by its series expansion which is absolutely convergent. Also note that if any of the two Fredholm determinants
\det(\mathrm{I}+AB)
and
\det(\mathrm{I}+BA)
has an absolutely convergent series expansion, then the identity
\det(\mathrm{I}+AB)=\det(\mathrm{I}+BA)
holds and both have absolutely convergent series expansions. Therefore
\det(\mathrm{I}+\mathbf{K})=\det(\mathrm{I}+\mathbf{L})
, where

\det(\mathrm{I}+\mathbf{L})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\det\left[\mathbf{L}(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{i}.

(3.5)

Moreover, the summation is absolutely convergent.

It turns out that the kernel
\mathbf{L}
can be further simplified.

Lemma 3.1
.

We have

\begin{split}&\mathbf{L}(\lambda,\theta)\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{k}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}A_{1}(\lambda;i_{1},\gamma_{1})\cdot\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}B(i_{\ell},\gamma_{\ell};i_{\ell+1},\gamma_{\ell+1})\cdot A_{2}(i_{k},\gamma_{k};\theta)\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\mathrm{d}\gamma_{\ell},\end{split}

(3.6)

and

(3.8)

(3.16)

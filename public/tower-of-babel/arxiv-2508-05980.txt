Rigidity of holomorphic maps between Shilov boundaries of type-I bounded symmetric domains
Yun Gao

Abstract
We introduce the concept of orthogonal structure on complex Grassmannians. Based on this structure, we define the notion of orthogonal mappings. This class of maps generalizes holomorphic maps between the Shilov boundaries of type-I bounded symmetric domains. By analyzing the geometric properties of these orthogonal mappings, we obtain rigidity results for such mappings. As an application, we establish a rigidity result for the holomorphic maps from the Shilov boundary of rank 1 1 type I bounded symmetric domain Ω 1 , s \Omega_{1,s} (i.e. unit spheres) to the Shilov boundary of a higher-rank type-I domain Ω r ′ , s ′ \Omega_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}} , where s ≥ 2 s\geq 2 and 2 ≤ r ′ ≤ s ′ 2\leq r^{\prime}\leq s^{\prime} . More specifically, we show that: (1) such maps are constant when s ′ − r ′ < s − 1 s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<s-1 ; (2) for s − 1 ≤ s ′ − r ′ < 2 ​ s − 2 s-1\leq s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<2s-2 , they reduce to the standard linear embeddings after normalization by applying automorphisms on both sides. Our results are optimal and generalize the well-known optimal bounds for proper mappings between rank 1 1 cases and CR maps between Shilov boundaries of higher-rank type I bounded symmetric domains.

We introduce a novel geometric approach to this problem by defining an orthogonal structure on Grassmannians and studying associated orthogonal mappings between them. This framework, integrating orthogonality with an effective analysis of linear subspaces and projective geometry techniques, yields rigidity results without resorting to heavy machinery. These orthogonal mappings slightly generalize the holomorphic maps between the Shilov boundaries of Type I bounded symmetric domains. Consequently, our approach provides new rigidity results for such maps. Here, by a holomorphic map between the Shilov boundaries, we mean a holomorphic map

and denoted by

\mathbb{P}\mathbb{C}^{r,s}=\mathbb{P}^{r,s}

A positive

-planes in

Consider the Grassmannian variety
G(r,r+s)
, whose points bijectively correspond to the

Definition 1.1
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}(r,s)
be a connected open set containing a null point. A holomorphic map
F:U\to\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
is called

if
F(p)\perp F(q)
for all

such that

. We also simply call

a

from

to

.

In our definition of orthogonal maps, we required the domain U to contain a null

contains at least one pair of orthogonal points. From

2.5
, we shall see that the local orthogonal maps from

to
\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
and the local holomorphic maps between Shilov boundary

We are now ready to state our main theorems. Further explanation and details can be found in Section 3.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
s\geq 2
,
2\leq r^{\prime}\leq s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
and let

be a local orthogonal map from
\mathcal{G}(1,s)
to
\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
.

(1)

If
s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<s-1
, then
F
is a constant map.

(2)

If

, then after composing with suitable automorphisms of
\mathcal{G}(1,s)
and
\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
,

is given by the following

[z_{0},\bf z]\to\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}I&0&I&0&0\\
0&z_{0}&0&\bf z&0\\
\end{array}\right),

where
[z_{0},{\bf z}]=[z_{0},z_{1},\cdots,z_{s}]\in\mathcal{G}(1,s)\cong\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
.

Furthermore, the inequalities governing the rigidity are sharp, as evidenced by the generalized Whitney map. Beyond these bounds, rigidity fails.

As a corollary, we have

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
s\geq 2
,
2\leq r^{\prime}\leq s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{s}
be an open neighborhood of a point in
S(\Omega_{1,s})
.
let
F
be a holomorphic map from
U
to
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}s^{\prime}}
satisfying

F(U\cap S(\Omega_{1,s}))\subset S(\Omega_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})
.

(1)

If
s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<s-1
, then
F
is a constant map.

(2)

If
s-1\leq s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<2s-2
, then after composing with suitable automorphisms of
\Omega_{1,s}
and
\Omega_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
,

F
is given by the following

z\to\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&0&0\\

We note that our proof of Theorem

is centered around the orthogonality preservation property and the dimensional analysis deduced from it. This new perspective enables us to avoid sophisticated computations as in the previous works, and makes our arguments considerably shorter.

2
Orthogonal structure on Grassmannian

In
[
GN
]
, the authors introduce an orthogonal structure in projective space to solve the rigidity problems for proper holomorphic mappings among generalized balls. Building upon this framework, in the current section we will generalize the definition of orthogonal structure to the Grassmannian manifold.

Let
r,s,t\in\mathbb{N}
and
n:=r+s+t>0
. Define the indefinite inner product of signature
(r;s;t)
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
as:

\langle z,w\rangle_{r,s,t}=z_{1}\bar{w}_{1}+\cdots+z_{r}\bar{w}_{r}-z_{r+1}\bar{w}_{r+1}-\cdots-z_{r+s}\bar{w}_{r+s},

where
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
and
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
. We also define the indefinite norm
\|z\|^{2}_{r,s,t}=\langle z,z\rangle_{r,s,t}
. Then, for any
z\in\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
endowed with this inner product, we call it a
positive point
if
\|z\|^{2}_{r,s,t}>0
; a
negative point
if
\|z\|^{2}_{r,s,t}<0
and a
null point
if
\|z\|^{2}_{r,s,t}=0
. Two vectors
z
,
w
are called orthogonal (denote
z\perp w
) if
\langle z,w\rangle_{r,s,t}=0
. The
orthogonal complement
of
z
is defined as

z^{\perp}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}\mid\langle z,w\rangle_{r,s,t}=0\}.

We denote by
\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
the
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with the Hermitian inner product defined above and
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}:=\mathbb{P}\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
. We write
\mathbb{C}^{r,s}
and
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
instead of
\mathbb{C}^{r,s,0}
and
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,0}
.

Consider a complex linear subspace
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
such that the restriction of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r,s}
on
V
has the signature
(a;b;c)
; we refer to
V
as an

(a,b,c)
-subspace
of
\mathbb{C}^{r,s,t}
.

If
a=b=0
,
V
is called a
null space
. Similarly, it is called a
positive space
(resp.
negative space
) if
b=c=0
(resp.
a=c=0
). We will also use the terms
null
k
-plane, positive
k
-plane
, and
negative
k
-plane
when
\dim V=k
.
Obviously, the maximum dimension of null spaces in
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
is
\min\{r,s\}
. Null spaces with the maximal dimension are called the
maximal null spaces
.

Let
r
,
s
be positive integers and
r\leq s
. Consider the Grassmannian variety
G(r,r+s)
, whose closed points are in bijection with

r
-dimensional vector subspaces

V\subset\mathbb{C}^{r+s}
. Fix the standard basis
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r+s})
of
\mathbb{C}^{r+s}
. A linear subspace

V
can be represented as the row space of an
r\times(r+s)
matrix:

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}a_{11}&a_{12}&\cdots&a_{1,r+s}\\
a_{21}&a_{22}&\cdots&a_{2,r+s}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{r1}&a_{r2}&\cdots&a_{r,r+s}\\
\end{array}\right),

where the rows of
A
encode a set of spanning vectors of
V
with respect to the standard basis.
Such
A
is called a
representative matrix
of
V
.

The matrix
A
is not unique: left multiplication by any invertible
r\times r
matrix
X
yields another representative matrix
XA
for the same subspace
V
.
Conversely, if two matrices
A
and
A^{\prime}
represent the same subspace, there exists an invertible
r\times r
matrix

X
such that
A^{\prime}=XA
.

Notations:
For a point
p\in G(r,r+s)
, let
V_{p}\subset\mathbb{C}^{r+s}
denote the
r
-dimensional linear subspace
corresponding to
p
, and let
A_{p}
denote a representative matrix of
V_{p}
. If the row vectors
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r}
of
A_{p}
are pairwise orthogonal with respect to the indefinite Hermitian inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{r,s}
(i.e.
\alpha_{i}\perp\alpha_{j}
in
\mathbb{C}^{r,s}
), then
A_{p}=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r})^{t}
is called a representative matrix of
p
, an
orthogonal representative matrix
for
p
.

By equipping
\mathbb{C}^{r,s}
with an indefinite inner product, we naturally induce an orthogonal structure on the Grassmannian
G(r,r+s)
. This structure categorizes subspaces based on their metric properties:

Definition 2.1
.

Let two points
p,q\in G(r,r+s)
be represented by
r\times(r+s)

matrices
A_{p}
and
A_{q}
. With the signature matrix

I_{r,s}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&-I_{s}\end{pmatrix},

we say
p
is orthogonal to
q
(denoted
p\perp q
) if and only if

A_{p}I_{r,s}A_{q}^{H}=0,

where
A^{H}=\bar{A}^{t}
denotes the Hermitian transpose.

A point
p\in\mathcal{G}(r,s)
is classified as

(1)

null
if

A_{p}I_{r,s}A_{p}^{H}=0\quad

(2)

positive
if
A_{p}I_{r,s}A_{p}^{H}>0\quad

Here,
\mathcal{G}(r,s)
denotes the Grassmannian
G(r,r+s)

endowed with this orthogonal structure.

Obviously, for any two points
p
,
q\in\mathcal{G}(r,s)
,

p\perp q\iff V_{p}\subseteq(V_{q})^{\perp}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r,s},

where
(V_{q})^{\perp}
denotes the orthogonal complement with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r,s}
.

Hence,
if
p\perp q
in
\mathcal{G}(r,s)
, then every vector
\alpha\in V_{p}
is orthogonal to every vector
\beta\in V_{q}
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r,s}
. Furthermore, we immediately observe that
\mathcal{G}(1,s)
coincides with the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
.

Let
M_{r,s}
denote the space of
r\times s
complex matrices. The
type-I irreducible bounded symmetric domain

\Omega_{r,s}\subseteq M_{r,s}\cong\mathbb{C}^{r\times s}
is defined by

\Omega_{r,s}=\{A\in M_{r,s}\mid I_{r}-AA^{H}>0\}.

Here we denote by
>0
the positive definiteness of square matrices.
This domain corresponds bijectively to the set of positive
r
-planes in
\mathbb{C}^{r,s}
, making
\Omega_{r,s}
precisely the set of positive points in
\mathcal{G}(r,s)
. The
Shilov boundary
of
\Omega_{r,s}

S(\Omega_{r,s})=\{A\in M_{r,s}\mid I_{r}=AA^{H}\}

coincides with the set of null points in
\mathcal{G}(r,s)
. Moreover, if
p\in S(\Omega_{r,s})
, then
V_{p}
is a maximal null subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{r,s}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathcal{G}(r,s)
be a connected open set containing a null point. A holomorphic map
F:U\to\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
is called
orthogonal
if
F(p)\perp F(q)
for all
p,q\in U
such that
p\perp q
. We also simply call
F
a
local orthogonal map
from
\mathcal{G}(r,s)
to
\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
.

Remark:
For the sake of simplicity, in our definition we require the domain
U
to contain a null point, i.e. to have a non-empty intersection with
S(\Omega_{r,s})
so that the
orthogonality condition will not become vacuous. (Otherwise, it could happen that there are no orthogonal points in
U
.) With this requirement,
Proposition
2.4
and Proposition
2.5
below will imply that orthogonal maps and holomorphic
maps between Shilov boundaries are the same set of mappings. However, we remark that in principle one can define orthogonal maps more generally without requiring
U\cap S(\Omega_{r,s})\neq\emptyset
(but then we need to assume that
U
contains at least a pair of orthogonal points) and some of our results (e.g. Theorem
1.2
) will still be valid. Thus, we may say that a local orthogonal map is a slightly more general object.

Definition 2.3
.

An orthogonal map
F:U\subseteq\mathcal{G}(r,s)\to\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
is called
null
if
F(U)\subseteq S(\Omega_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})
.

From the definition of orthogonal maps and the properties of the Shilov boundary, we immediately derive the following result.

Proposition 2.4
.

Every local orthogonal map
F:\mathcal{G}(r,s)\to\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
preserves null points.

From the definition, orthogonal maps preserves null points. The following proposition is essentially the converse.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
r,s>0
and
F:U\subset\mathcal{G}(r,s)\to\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
be a holomorphic map, where
U
is an open set containing a null point. If
F
maps null points to null points, there exists an open set
U^{\prime}\subset U
, such that
F:U^{\prime}\to\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
is an orthogonal map.

Proof.

By composing with automorphisms and shrinking
U
if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality that
U
is contained in the open set
U_{1}\in\mathcal{G}(r,s)
, where
U_{1}=\{\mathcal{Z}=[I_{r},Z]\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times{(r+s)}}\}
,
Z=(z_{ij})_{r\times s}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times s}
and
F(U)
lies in the analogous chart
U_{1}^{\prime}=\{\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}=[I_{r^{\prime}},Z^{\prime}]\in\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}\times{(r^{\prime}+s^{\prime})}}\}
.

In these coordinates, null points
\mathcal{Z}
in
\mathcal{G}(r,s)
are characterized by the equation
I-ZZ^{H}=0
. Explicitly,
\mathcal{Z}=[I_{r},Z]\in\mathcal{G}(r,s)
is a null point if and only if

h_{ij}(Z,\bar{Z}):=\sum_{k=1}^{s}z_{ik}\bar{z}_{kj}-\delta_{ij}=0,\quad(1\leq i\leq r,\quad 1\leq j\leq s)

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker function.

Now we can assume that
F=[I_{r^{\prime}},F^{\sharp}]
and
F^{\sharp}=(f_{ij})_{r^{\prime}\times s^{\prime}}
. Define

G_{ij}(Z,\bar{Z}):=\sum_{k=1}^{s^{\prime}}f_{ik}(Z)\overline{f_{kj}(Z)}-\delta_{ij},\quad(1\leq i\leq r^{\prime},\quad 1\leq j\leq s^{\prime}).

Here we use
f(Z)
to denote
f(z_{11},\cdots,z_{rs})
.

Since
F
preserves null points, there exists a connected open set
U^{\prime}\subset U
containing a null point and some real analytic functions
g_{ij}^{kl}(Z,\bar{Z})
such that

G_{ij}(Z,\bar{Z})=\sum_{k=1}^{s^{\prime}}f_{ik}(Z)\overline{f_{kj}(Z)}-\delta_{ij}=\sum_{kl}h_{kl}(Z,\overline{Z})g_{ij}^{kl}(Z,\overline{Z}),

holds on
U^{\prime}
.

By polarization (after further shrinking
U^{\prime}
if necessary), this implies for all
\mathcal{Z}=[I_{r},Z]
,
\mathcal{W}=[I_{r},W]
in
U^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle G_{ij}(Z,\bar{W})=\sum_{k=1}^{s^{\prime}}f_{ik}(Z)\overline{f_{kj}(W)}-\delta_{ij}=\sum_{kl}h_{kl}(Z,\bar{W})g_{ij}^{kl}(Z,\bar{W}).

(1)

For any point
\mathcal{Z}=[I_{r},Z]\in\mathcal{G}(r,s)
, a point
\mathcal{W}=[I_{r},W]
lies in
\mathcal{Z}^{\perp}\cap U^{\prime}
precisely when
h_{kl}(Z,\bar{W})=0
for all
k,l
. By (
1
), this implies
F(\mathcal{W})\in F(\mathcal{Z})^{\perp}
. Hence,
F
preserves orthogonality locally, proving
F|_{U^{\prime}}
is an orthogonal map.

3
Rigidity of local orthogonal maps between Grassmannians

In this section, we will introduce some properties of orthogonal maps between Grassmannian. The following proposition is clear.

Proposition 3.1
.

If two points
p,q\in\mathcal{G}(r,s)
satisfy
p\perp q
and
\dim(V_{p}\cap V_{q})>0
, then every vector
\alpha\in V_{p}\cap V_{q}
is a null vector in
\mathbb{C}^{r,s}
.

Notation: Let
K\subset\mathcal{G}^{r,s}
. We use
V_{K}
to denote the linear span of
\cup_{p\in K}V_{p}
in
\mathbb{C}^{r,s}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
s\geq 2
,
1\leq r^{\prime}\leq s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
F
be a local orthogonal map from
U\subset\mathcal{G}(1,s)=\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
to
\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
. If

\dim(V_{F(H\cap U)})=\dim(V_{F(U)})

for every general hyperplane
H
in
\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
, then
F
is null.

Proof.

For any general hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
, by definition, there exists a point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
such that
p\perp H
.
Since the orthogonal map
F
preserves orthogonality, for any vector
\alpha
in
V_{F(p)}
, we have
\alpha\perp V_{F(q)}

for any
q\in H
. This implies
\alpha\perp\beta
for every
\beta\in V_{F(H)}
.

By the dimension condition
\dim(V_{F(H\cap U)})=\dim(V_{F(U)})
, the inclusion
V_{F(H\cap U)}\subset V_{F(U)}
must be an equality. Hence,
\alpha\perp V_{F(U)}
. In particular,
\alpha\perp\alpha
, yielding
\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle=0
, so
\alpha
is a null vector.

For any general
p\in U
, there exists a hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
orthogonal to
p
. The argument above shows that all vectors
\alpha\subset V_{F(p)}
are null. Thus,
V_{F(p)}
is a null subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
, implying
F(p)\in S(\Omega_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})
. Since
p
is arbitrary,
F(U)\subseteq S(\Omega_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})
, proving
F
is null.

∎

The following proposition helps us convert the rigidity problems of orthogonal maps between Grassmannians to those of generalized balls.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
s\geq 2
,
r^{\prime},s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
with
2\leq r^{\prime}\leq s^{\prime}
, and let
F:U\subset\mathcal{G}(1,s)\to\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
be a local orthogonal map. If
s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<2(s-1)
and
F
is non-null, there exists an
(r^{\prime}-1)
-dimensional null subspace
N\subset\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
such that
N\subset V_{F(p)}
for every point
p\in\mathcal{G}(1,s)
.

Proof.

For a general point
p\in\mathcal{G}(1,s)=\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
, denote
N_{p}=V_{F(p)}\cap V_{F(p^{\perp})}
and
\dim N_{p}=k
. By orthogonality preservation,
V_{F(p)}\perp V_{F(p^{\perp})}
, implying
N_{p}\perp N_{p}
. Thus,
N_{p}
is a null subspace in
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
. Hence
k\leq r^{\prime}
.

Select
s+1
pairwise orthogonal non-null points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{s+1}\in\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
. For
i\neq j
,
V_{F(p_{j})}\subset V_{F(p_{i}^{\perp})}
, yielding

\dim\left(V_{F(p_{i})}\cap\sum_{j\neq i}V_{F(p_{j})}\right)\leq k.

Since
\dim V_{F(p_{i})}=r^{\prime}
, there exist at least
(r^{\prime}-k)
linearly independent vectors
\alpha_{1}^{i},\ldots,\alpha_{r^{\prime}-k}^{i}\subset V_{F(p_{i})}
outside
\sum_{j\neq i}V_{F(p_{j})}
. We verify the linear independence of the full set
\{\alpha_{l}^{i}\}_{1\leq l\leq r^{\prime}-k}^{1\leq i\leq s+1}
.

For
\alpha^{i}=\sum_{l=1}^{r^{\prime}-k}x_{l}^{i}\alpha_{l}^{i}
(
x_{l}^{i}\in\mathbb{C}
), linear independence of
\alpha^{1},\ldots,\alpha^{s+1}
follows from
\alpha^{i}\notin\sum_{j\neq i}V_{F(p_{j})}
. Hence,

\dim\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s+1}V_{F(p_{i})}\right)\geq(s+1)(r^{\prime}-k)+k.

(2)

As
N_{p_{i}}\subset\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
is a
k
-dimensional null subspace, then
N_{p_{i}}^{\perp}
is a
(r^{\prime}-k,s^{\prime}-k,k)
-subspace. For
j\geq 2
,
V_{F(p_{j})}\subset V_{F(p_{1})}^{\perp}\subset N_{p_{1}}^{\perp}
and
V_{F(p_{1})}\perp N_{p_{1}}
, so

\sum_{i=1}^{s+1}V_{F(p_{i})}\subset N_{p_{1}}^{\perp}\quad\text{and}\quad\dim\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s+1}V_{F(p_{i})}\right)\leq r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}-k.

(3)

Combining inequalities (
2
) and (
3
) with
s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<2(s-1)
and
s\geq 2
, we have

(s+1)(r^{\prime}-k)+k\leq r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}-k<2r^{\prime}+2(s-1)-k,

yielding
k>r^{\prime}-2
. On the other hand, if
k=r^{\prime}
, then
F
must be null. By the assumption that
F
is non-null, then
k=r^{\prime}-1
. So
V_{F(p)}
contains an
(r^{\prime}-1)
-dimensional null subspace
N_{p}
for all
p\in\mathcal{G}(1,s)\cong\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
.

To show
N_{p}=N_{q}
for all
p
,
q\in\mathcal{G}(1,s)
, suppose otherwise.

For two points
p\perp a
in
\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
, we have
N_{p}\perp N_{a}
in
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
. Suppose
N_{p}\neq N_{a}
; since
\dim N_{p}=\dim N_{a}=r^{\prime}-1
, it follows that
\dim(N_{p}+N_{a})\geq r^{\prime}
. This makes
M_{pa}:=N_{p}+N_{a}
a maximal null subspace in
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}}
. For any point
b
satisfying
b\perp p
and
b\perp a
, the orthogonality conditions
N_{b}\perp N_{a}
and
N_{b}\perp N_{p}
imply
N_{b}\subset N_{pa}
. Consequently, we deduce that
N_{a}+N_{b}=N_{a}+N_{p}=N_{b}+N_{p}=M_{pa}
.

Now we assume that
s=2
. From the argument above, we know
\dim(N_{a}+N_{b})=r^{\prime}
. Since
F
is non-null,
\dim(V_{F(a)}+V_{F(b)}+V_{F(p)})\geq 3+r^{\prime}
. On the other hand, because
V_{F(a)}
,
V_{F(b)}
and
V_{F(p)}
are all contained in
(N_{a}+N_{b})^{\perp}
, then
\dim(V_{F(a)}+V_{F(b)}+V_{F(p)})\leq s^{\prime}
, which contradicts
s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<2
.

When
s\geq 3
, for any points
p,q\in\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
, there exist two points
a,b
such that
a,b\in p^{\perp}
,
a,b\in q^{\perp}
, and
a\perp b
By the preceding argument,
N_{p},N_{q}\subset M_{ab}
. It follows that either
N_{p}=N_{q}
or
N_{p}+N_{q}=M_{ab}
is a maximal null
(0,0,r^{\prime})
-subspace in
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
. If
N_{p}\neq N_{q}
, then
M_{pq}:=N_{p}+N_{q}
is a maximal null space. Hence
N_{p}\perp N_{q}
for any two points
p,q
.

For any point
c
, since
K_{c}
is an
(r^{\prime}-1)
-dimensional null space in
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
and
N_{c}\perp N_{p},N_{c}\perp K_{q}
, it follows that
N_{c}\subset M_{pq}
.

Now we have proved that there exists a maximal null space
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
such that
N_{p}\subset N
for any
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
under the assumption that
N_{p}\neq N_{q}
for any two points
p
,
q
. Assume that
M^{\perp}=M\oplus K
where
K
is a
(0,s^{\prime}-r^{\prime},0)
-subspace for
M^{\perp}
is a
(0,s^{\prime}-r^{\prime},r^{\prime})
subspace.

Since
N_{p}=V_{F(p)}\cap V_{F(p^{\perp})}
and
N_{p}+N_{q}=M
for any
p,q
, it follows that
V_{F(p)}\perp M
. For
\dim V_{F(p)}=r^{\prime}
and
\dim N_{p}=r^{\prime}-1
, there exists a unique
1
-dimensional linear subspace
L_{p}
such that
V_{F(p)}=L_{p}\oplus N_{p}
for any
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
.

Let
F^{K}
be a holomorphic map from
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
to
G(1,K)\cong\mathbb{P}^{0,s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}}
defined by
F^{H}(p)=L_{p}
. The map
F^{H}
is induced by
F
. Obviously,
L_{p}\perp L_{q}
when
p\perp q
. Hence
F^{H}
is an orthogonal map from
\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
to
\mathbb{P}^{0,s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}}
.

Theorem 3.3 in
[
GN
]
shows that for any
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
, if
r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}\leq 2(r+s)-3
, then any orthogonal map from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
is either null or quasi-linear. On the other hand, it is clear that there is no local quasi-linear or null orthogonal map from
\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
to
\mathbb{P}^{0,s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}}
, which is a contradiction.

So,
N_{p}=N_{q}
for any two points
p
,
q
in
\mathcal{G}(1,s)
. Hence, there exists an
(r^{\prime}-1)
-dimensional null subspace
N\subset\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
such that
N\subset V_{F(p)}
for all
p\in\mathcal{G}(1,s)
.

∎

The following proposition comes from Proposition
3.3
directly.

Proposition 3.4
.

Under the assumption of Proposition
3.3
, there exist a
(0,0,r^{\prime}-1)
-subspace
N
and a
(1,s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}+1,0)
-subspace
K
in
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
such that
N\subset V_{F(p)}\subset K\oplus N=\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
for any
p\in U\subset\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
.

We can find the following rigidity theorem about orthogonal maps between projective spaces in
[
GN
]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

[
GN
]

Let
l
,
l^{\prime}\geq 2
in
\mathbb{N}
and
f
be a local orthogonal map from
\mathbb{P}^{1,l}
to
\mathbb{P}^{1,l^{\prime}}
.

(1)If
l^{\prime}<l
, then
f
is a constant map.

(2)If
l\leq l^{\prime}\leq 2l-2
, then
f
is a linear map or a constant map.

Now we are ready to prove our main theorem.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
s\geq 2
,
2\leq r^{\prime}\leq s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
and
F
be a local orthogonal map from
\mathcal{G}(1,s)
to
\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
.

(1)

If
s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<s-1
, then
F
is a constant map.

(2)

If
s-1\leq s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<2s-2
,
F
is a linear map.
After composing suitable automorphisms of
\mathcal{G}(1,s)
and
\mathcal{G}(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})
,

F
has the explicit form

[z_{0},\bf z]\to\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}I&0&I&0&0\\
0&z_{0}&0&\bf z&0\\
\end{array}\right),

where
[z_{1},{\bf z}]=[z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{s+1}]
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3
, there exists a
(0,0,r^{\prime}-1)
-subspace
N
and a
(1,s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}+1)
-subspace
K
in
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
with
K\perp N
such that
N\subset V_{F(p)}\subset K\oplus N
for any point
p\in U
. After composing with automorphism of
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
, we can assume that the representative matrix of
N
is
[I_{r^{\prime}-1},0,I_{r^{\prime}-1},0]
.

Hence, we can assume that
F(p)=F_{1}(p)\oplus F_{2}(p)
, where
F_{1}:U\subset\mathcal{G}(1,s)\cong\mathbb{P}^{1,s}\to G(1,K)\cong\mathbb{P}^{1,s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}+1}
and
F_{2}:\mathcal{G}(1,s)\cong\mathbb{P}^{1,s}\to G(r^{\prime}-1,N)
. The Grassmannian variety
G(1,K)
is the set of points bijectively corresponding to
1
-dimensional linear subspace in
K
. Then
F_{1}
is a local orthogonal map and
F_{2}
is a constant map.

Therefore, we only need to character the orthogonal map
F_{1}
from
\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
to
\mathbb{P}^{1,s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}+1}
.

(1) When
s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<s-1
,
F_{1}
is a constant map by Theorem
3.5
. Hence,
F
is a constant map.

(2)When
s-1\leq s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<2s-2
,
F_{1}
is a linear embedding from
\mathbb{P}^{1,s}
to
\mathbb{P}^{1,s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}+1}
by Theorem
3.5
.
Hence,
F
is a linear map. In addition, using the orthogonal representative matrix and composing the suitable automorphisms of both sides,
F
can be defined by

[z_{0},\bf z]\to\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}I_{r^{\prime}-1}&0&I_{r^{\prime}-1}&0&0\\
0&z_{0}&0&\bf z&0\\
\end{array}\right),

where
[z_{0},{\bf z}]=[z_{0},z_{1},\cdots,z_{s}]
.

From the relationship between the orthogonal map and the holomorphic map between Type I bounded symmetric domains preserving Shilov boundaries, we can get the following result directly.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
s\geq 2
,
2\leq r^{\prime}\leq s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{s}
be an open neighborhood of a point in
S(\Omega_{1,s})
.
let
F
be a holomorphic map from
U
to
\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}s^{\prime}}
satisfying

F(U\cap S(\Omega_{1,s}))\subset S(\Omega_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})
.

(1)

If
s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<s-1
, then
F
is a constant map.

(2)

If
s-1\leq s^{\prime}-r^{\prime}<2s-2
,
F
is a linear map. After composing with suitable automorphisms of
\Omega_{1,s}
and
\Omega_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
,

F
is given by the following

z\to\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&0&0\\
0&z&0\\
\end{array}\right).

Example :
The following generalized Whitney map
f
from
\Omega_{1,s}
to
\Omega_{r^{\prime},r^{\prime}+2s-1}
is given by

z=[z_{1},\cdots,z_{s}]\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccccc}z_{1}&\cdots&z_{s-1}&z_{1}z_{s}&z_{2}z_{s}&\cdots&z_{s}^{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\cdots&0&0&0&\cdots&0&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&0&0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&1\\
\end{array}\right)

preserving the Shilov boundaries . This example shows the inequalities in Theorem
3.6
and Corollary
3.7
governing the rigidity are sharp.

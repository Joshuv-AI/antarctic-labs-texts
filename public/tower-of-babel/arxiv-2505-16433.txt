Non-perturbative effects in JT gravity from KdV equations
Yasuyuki Hatsuda, Takaki Matsumoto, and Kazumi Okuyama

Abstract
It is well-known that the partition function of the Jackiw-Teitelboim (JT) gravity is obtained by an integral transformation of volumes of moduli spaces for Riemann surfaces, also known as the Weil-Petersson volumes. This fact enables us to compute the perturbative genus expansion of the partition function by solving a KdV-type non-linear partial differential equation. In this work, we find that this KdV equation also admits transseries solutions. We give a systematic algorithm to explicitly construct a one-parameter transseries solution to the KdV equation. Our approach is based on general two-dimensional topological gravity, and the results for the JT gravity are easily obtained as a special case. The results in the leading non-perturbative sector perfectly agree with another independent calculation from topological recursions in random matrices.

In

In this paper, we study the non-perturbative effects

From the Witten-Kontsevich theorem

JT gravity and Weil-Petersson volume

In this work we focus on the partition function of the JT gravity with
n
boundaries. We review a basic framework how to compute it. The partition function has a perturbative expansion in a genus counting parameter
\lambda
as
1
1

1

In some references on the JT gravity, this expansion is called “non-perturbative” because
\lambda\sim O(e^{-1/G_{N}})
, where
G_{N}
is the gravitational constant. In this terminology, what we call non-perturbative corrections here is referred to as “doubly non-perturbative” corrections. We follow the standard terminology in random matrices
[
5
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{\text{JT}}(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})=\sum^{\infty}_{g=0}\lambda^{2g+n-2}Z_{g,n}(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}).\end{split}

(1)

where
Z_{g,n}
is given by a path integral on a hyperbolic surface of genus

with the

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{g,n}(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})=\int^{\infty}_{0}\Biggl(\prod^{n}_{i=1}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{2\pi\beta_{i}}}b_{i}db_{i}\,e^{-\gamma b^{2}_{i}/2\beta_{i}}\Biggr)V_{g,n}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}).\end{split}

(2)

In this equation,
V_{g,n}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
denotes the Weil-Petersson volume of the moduli space of a hyperbolic surface of genus
g
with
n
geodesic boundaries of lengths specified by
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
. We can see that
Z_{g,n}
is obtained by an integral transform of
V_{g,n}
. Let us consider a generating function of
V_{g,n}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{n}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}):=\sum^{\infty}_{g=0}\lambda^{2g+n-2}V_{g,n}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}).\end{split}

(3)

One way to compute
V_{g,n}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
is by using Mirzakhani’s recursion relation
[
10
,
28
]
.

Let
M_{g,n}(\bm{b})
be the moduli space of hyperbolic Riemann surfaces of genus
g
with
n
geodesic boundaries of lengths
\bm{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
. This space is of complex dimension
3g-3+n
and carries a natural symplectic form called the Weil-Petersson form. The Weil-Petersson volume
V_{g,n}(\bm{b})
is the volume of
M_{g,n}(\bm{b})
with respect to the volume form induced by the Weil-Petersson form.

According to Mirzakhani’s theorems
[
10
,
28
]
, the volume
V_{g,n}(\bm{b})
is a symmetric polynomial in
b^{2}_{1},\ldots,b^{2}_{n}
of total degree
3g-3+n
. To give a precise form of this polynomial, let
M_{g,n}
be the moduli space of Riemann surfaces of genus
g
with
n
distinct punctures, and let
\overline{M}_{g,n}
denote its Deligne-Mumford compactification. Let
L_{i}
be a complex line bundle over
\overline{M}_{g,n}
whose fiber at each moduli point is its cotangent space at the
i
-th puncture. We denote by
\psi_{i}
the first Chern class of
L_{i}
. We also need the first Miller-Morita-Mumford class on
\overline{M}_{g,n}
and denote it by
\kappa
. Then
V_{g,n}(\bm{b})
takes the form of

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{g,n}(\bm{b})=\sum_{\sum^{n}_{i=1}k_{i}\leq 3g-3+n}C_{g}(\bm{k})\,\frac{b^{2k_{1}}_{1}}{2^{k_{1}}k_{1}!}\cdots\frac{b^{2k_{n}}_{n}}{2^{k_{n}}k_{n}!}\end{split}

(4)

with coefficients

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{g}(\bm{k})&=\int_{\overline{M}_{g,n}}\psi^{k_{1}}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\psi^{k_{n}}_{n}\wedge\exp 2\pi^{2}\kappa,\end{split}

(5)

where the integral over
\overline{M}_{g,n}
vanishes unless the total degree of the integrated classes equals its dimension
3g-3+n
. The symmetry of
V_{g,n}(\bm{b})
in
b^{2}_{1},\ldots,b^{2}_{n}
follows from the fact that the integral (
5
) is symmetric on the indices
k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}
, because the wedge product between the two-dimensional classes
\psi_{i}
is commutative, and
\overline{M}_{g,n}
is invariant under every permutation of the
n
punctures.

The integral (
5
) is an intersection number of the
\psi
classes and the
\kappa
class, up to an overall factor. The intersection numbers involving both the
\psi
classes and the
\kappa
class are expressible in terms of them involving the
\psi
classes only, such as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\tau_{k_{1}}\cdots\tau_{k_{n}}\rangle_{g}:=\int_{\overline{M}_{g,n}}\psi^{k_{1}}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\psi^{k_{n}}_{n},\end{split}

(6)

which vanishes unless the dimension condition
\sum^{n}_{i=1}k_{i}=3g-3+n
holds. The order of the letters in the notation
\langle\tau_{k_{1}}\cdots\tau_{k_{n}}\rangle_{g}
is irrelevant similar to the symmetry of (
5
). In this notation, we have
[
25
,
26
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{g}(\bm{k})=\langle\tau_{k_{1}}\cdots\tau_{k_{n}}\exp\sum_{k\geq 0}\gamma_{k}\tau_{k}\rangle_{g},\end{split}

(7)

where
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\ldots
are infinitely many constants defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{1}=0,\qquad\gamma_{k}=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k-1)!}\quad(k\geq 2).\end{split}

(8)

The expression (
4
) with the relation (
7
) reduces the study of
V_{g,n}(\bm{b})
to that of the intersection numbers of the
\psi
classes.

Let us close this section by giving a concise expression of
V_{g,n}(\bm{b})
combining (
4
) and (
7
). We introduce the exponential generating function for all possible intersection numbers of the
\psi
classes associated with genus
g
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{g}(\{t_{k}\}):=\langle\exp\sum_{k\geq 0}t_{k}\tau_{k}\rangle_{g},\end{split}

(9)

where
t_{0},t_{1},\ldots
are infinitely many formal variables. We also introduce a differential operator acting on the formal variables by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{V}(b):=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{b^{2k}}{2^{k}k!}\partial_{k},\qquad\partial_{k}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}.\end{split}

(10)

We call this operator the boundary creation operator
[
29
]
. By using these objects, we define

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{g,n}(\bm{b},\{t_{k}\}):=\hat{V}(b_{1})\cdots\hat{V}(b_{n})F_{g}(\{t_{k}\}).\end{split}

(11)

Then by combining it with (
4
) and (
7
), the Weil-Petersson volume is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{g,n}(\bm{b})=V_{g,n}(\bm{b},\{\gamma_{k}\}),\end{split}

(12)

as shown in
[
30
,
31
]
.
We call
V_{g,n}(\bm{b},\{t_{k}\})
the generalized Weil-Petersson volume.
In particular, we note that

\displaystyle V_{g,n}(\{t_{k}\})

\displaystyle:=V_{g,n}(\bm{b}=0,\{t_{k}\})=\partial_{0}^{n}F_{g}(\{t_{k}\}),

(13)

\displaystyle V_{g,n}

\displaystyle:=V_{g,n}(\bm{b}=0)=\partial_{0}^{n}F_{g}(\{t_{k}\})|_{t_{k}=\gamma_{k}}.

(14)

3
Topological gravity and KdV equation

Let us collect the generating function (
9
) for the intersection numbers of the
\psi
classes associated with genus
g
and construct a formal power series in the genus counting parameter
\lambda
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}F(\lambda,\{t_{k}\}):=\sum^{\infty}_{g=0}\lambda^{2g-2}F_{g}(\{t_{k}\}).\end{split}

(15)

This is the “free energy” of two-dimensional topological gravity, a field theoretical interpretation of intersection theory of the
\psi
classes. The formal variables
t_{0},t_{1},\ldots
play the role of coupling constants in this theory and characterize its background.
Recall that results on the JT gravity are obtained by the specialization:

\displaystyle\begin{split}t_{k}=\gamma_{k}.\end{split}

(16)

A remarkable property of two-dimensional topological gravity is that the partition function
e^{F}
is a tau function of the KdV hierarchy. This is the statement of the Witten-Kontsevich theorem
[
23
,
24
]
. In other words, if we define the “specific heat” by
u_{g}=\partial^{2}_{0}F_{g}
and its generating function
2
2

2

As will be seen in the next section, the generating function
u(\lambda,\{t_{k}\})
admits transseries expansions. Strictly speaking, we should distinguish the perturbative generating function
u^{(0)}
of
u_{g}
from the full transseries

\displaystyle\begin{split}u(\lambda,\{t_{k}\})=\sum^{\infty}_{g=0}\lambda^{2g}u_{g}(\{t_{k}\}):=\sum^{\infty}_{g=0}\lambda^{2g}\partial^{2}_{0}F_{g}(\{t_{k}\}),\end{split}

(17)

then we have an infinite set of differential equations

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{k}u=\partial_{0}R_{k+1}[u],\end{split}

(18)

where
R_{k}[u]
denote the Gelfand-Dikii differential polynomials of

. One can determine these polynomials through recursion relations

\displaystyle\begin{split}(2k+1)\partial_{0}R_{k+1}=2u\partial_{0}R_{k}+(\partial_{0}u)R_{k}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\partial^{3}_{0}R_{k},\end{split}

(19)

with an initial condition
R_{0}=1
. For instance,
R_{1}[u]=u
,

, and so on. In particular, the equation for

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{1}u=u\partial_{0}u+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{12}\partial^{3}_{0}u.\end{split}

(20)

A consequence of the Witten-Kontsevich theorem is that the KdV hierarchy with an additional equation called the string equation,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{0}F=\frac{t^{2}_{0}}{2}+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}t_{n+1}\partial_{n}F,\end{split}

(21)

determines

uniquely. This property is the reason why we base our study on topological gravity.

Itzykson and Zuber made an ansatz for solving the infinite set of differential equations (

In terms of

4
Transseries solution

We have seen that the KdV equation (
20
) determines the specific heat
u
of topological gravity as its power series solution in the genus counting parameter
\lambda
. Regarding
\lambda
as a perturbation parameter, the KdV equation in our context is a singular perturbation of non-linear differential equation because
\lambda
multiplies the highest derivative. Therefore we expect that the KdV equation has a more general solution called a transseries solution. This solution is a formal power series in two “independent” parameters:
\lambda
and an exponentially small factor, such as
\exp[O(1/\lambda)]
. The latter amounts to the order of nonperturbative effects contributing to topological gravity.

To find a transseries solution of the KdV equation, we take a one-parameter transseries ansatz depending on a free parameter
\sigma
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}u(\sigma,\lambda,\{t_{k}\}):=\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=0}\sigma^{\ell}u^{(\ell)}(\lambda,\{t_{k}\}),\end{split}

(33)

where
u^{(0)}
is the original perturbative series

\displaystyle\begin{split}u^{(0)}(\lambda,\{t_{k}\})=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g}u_{g}(\{t_{k}\})),\end{split}

(34)

and the other
u^{(\ell)}

(\ell\geq 1)
are exponentially small contributions. We call
u^{(\ell)}
the
\ell
-instanton sector for convenience. The KdV equation has a more general transseries solution, a multi-parameter transseries solution depending on two or more free parameters, but it is beyond the scope of this paper. Plugging (
33
) into (
20
) and collecting the terms of order
\sigma^{\ell}
, we find that
u^{(\ell)}
obeying the following inhomogeneous differential equation,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{1}u^{(\ell)}=\sum^{\ell}_{m=0}u^{(\ell-m)}\partial_{0}u^{(m)}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{12}\partial^{3}_{0}u^{(\ell)}.\end{split}

(35)

We would like to solve these equations recursively.
A typical ansatz for
u^{(\ell)}
is of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}u^{(\ell)}(\lambda,\{t_{k}\})=\lambda^{\ell\beta}\exp\biggl[\frac{\ell A(\{t_{k}\})}{\lambda}\biggr]\phi^{(\ell)}(\lambda,\{t_{k}\}),\end{split}

(36)

where
\beta
is a characteristic exponent,
A
an “instanton action”, and
\phi^{(\ell)}
a formal power series of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi^{(\ell)}(\lambda,\{t_{k}\})=\sum^{\infty}_{h=0}\lambda^{h}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}(\{t_{k}\}).\end{split}

(37)

Note that the equation (
157
) gives no restrictions on
\beta
. We will determine
\beta
so that the transseries solution has a resurgence structure. In this section, we give a precise form of
A
and an algorithm to determine
\phi^{(\ell)}
.

4.1
Instanton action

As a first step, we determine the instanton action
A
. This is done by looking at the
\ell=1
sector:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\biggl(\partial_{1}-u^{(0)}\partial_{0}-\partial_{0}u^{(0)}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{12}\partial^{3}_{0}\biggr)u^{(1)}=0\end{split}

(38)

Substituting the ansatz (
36
) for
u^{(1)}
into this equation,
the leading contribution yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}A+\frac{1}{12}(\partial_{0}A)^{3}=0.\end{split}

(39)

We give a solution to this non-linear equation in a very heuristic way.
First, we define

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff}}(\xi,\{t_{k}\})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2tz^{3}}{3}+2\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}\frac{I_{k}}{(2k+1)!!}z^{2k+1},

(40)

where

and
\xi
is a formal variable. Note that this quantity has already been introduced in
[

]
by constructing the genus expansion of the Baker-Akhiezer function. We will briefly explain why it is just below. We also introduce a “critical value”
\xi_{\ast}=\xi_{\ast}(\{t_{k}\})
by

\displaystyle 0=\partial_{\xi}V_{\text{eff}}(\xi,\{t_{k}\})|_{\xi=\xi_{*}}=-2tz_{\ast}+2\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}\frac{I_{k}}{(2k-1)!!}z^{2k-1}_{\ast},

(41)

where
z_{\ast}=\sqrt{2(\xi_{\ast}-y)}
. It is useful to rewrite it as

\displaystyle t=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{I_{k}}{(2k-1)!!}z_{\ast}^{2k-2}.

(42)

Then (
39

\displaystyle A(\{t_{k}\})=V_{\text{eff}}(\xi_{\ast},\{t_{k}\})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2tz_{*}^{3}}{3}+2\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}\frac{I_{k}}{(2k+1)!!}z_{*}^{2k+1}

(43)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{3}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{k-1}{(2k+1)!!}I_{k}z_{\ast}^{2k+1}.

In fact, we easily find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}A&=\partial_{t}V_{\text{eff}}|_{\xi=\xi_{\ast}}+\partial_{t}\xi_{\ast}\cdot\partial_{\xi}V_{\text{eff}}|_{\xi=\xi_{\ast}}=\partial_{t}V_{\text{eff}}|_{\xi=\xi_{\ast}}=-\frac{2z^{3}_{\ast}}{3},\\
\partial_{0}A&=\partial_{0}V_{\text{eff}}|_{\xi=\xi_{\ast}}+\partial_{0}\xi_{\ast}\cdot\partial_{\xi}V_{\text{eff}}|_{\xi=\xi_{\ast}}=\partial_{0}V_{\text{eff}}|_{\xi=\xi_{\ast}}=2z_{\ast},\end{split}

(44)

due to the criticality of
\xi_{\ast}
.

To see a connection with the Baker-Akhiezer function, we consider a Lax pair of the original KdV equation as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&P=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\partial^{2}_{0}+u^{(0)},\\
&B=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{3}\partial^{3}_{0}+u^{(0)}\partial_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}u^{(0)}.\end{split}

(45)

Then, the KdV equation is equivalent to the Lax equation
[\partial_{1}-B,P]=0
. Now let us consider linear auxiliary equations

\displaystyle P\psi=\xi\psi,

(46)

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\psi=B\psi.

(47)

The first equation is regarded as a stationary Schrödinger-type equation with potential
u^{(0)}
. The eigenvalue
\xi
does not depend on
t_{0}
. Moreover, from the Lax equation, we have
\partial_{1}\xi=0
. Therefore
\xi
is a constant. The second equation describes the “time dependence” of the eigenfunction
\psi=\psi(\lambda,\{t_{k}\};\xi)
.
The eigenfunction
\psi=\psi(\lambda,\{t_{k}\};\xi)
is called the Baker-Akhiezer function.

By using the linear auxiliary system (
46
), we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\biggl(\partial_{1}-u^{(0)}\partial_{0}-\partial_{0}u^{(0)}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{12}\partial^{3}_{0}\biggr)\psi\partial_{0}\psi=0.\end{split}

(48)

This means that
\psi\partial_{0}\psi
is a solution to (
38
).
It is expected that a general solution takes the form of a superposition of
\psi\partial_{0}\psi
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}u^{(1)}\sim\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}d\xi\,\psi\partial_{0}\psi,\end{split}

(49)

where we have rescaled
\psi
so that a weight function over
\xi
becomes unity. This is possible because the equation for
\ell=1
is linear and
\xi
is a constant. (
49
) gives an integral representation of the one-instanton solution. The saddle-point approximation of this integral for the WKB-type ansatz of
\psi
(see also (
128
) and (
134
)) implies the solution (
43
).

In Section
5.1
, we will also see the fact that the solution (
43
) is consistent with the large order behavior of the perturbative solution
u^{(0)}
.

(i)

Baker-Akhiezer function, Christoffel-Darboux kernel and wave function in topological recursion

In this section, we show a relationship between our approach and random matrix approach in
[
22
]
.
Recall that the Baker-Akhiezer function satisfies the Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle(\hbar^{2}\partial_{0}^{2}+u^{(0)})\psi(\xi,t_{0})=\xi\psi(\xi,t_{0}),

(126)

where the potential
u^{(0)}
is the perturbative solution to the KdV equation. Here we use
\hbar
rather than
\lambda
or
g_{s}
. These are related by

\displaystyle\hbar=\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}=2\pi^{3}g_{s}.

(127)

The WKB solution to this Schrödinger equation can be constructed by the standard way in quantum mechanics if the potential
u^{(0)}
is given. We consider the WKB ansatz

\displaystyle\psi=\exp\biggl(\frac{\mathcal{A}}{\hbar}\biggr),

(128)

then
v:=\partial_{0}\mathcal{A}
satisfies the so-called Riccati equation

\displaystyle v^{2}+\hbar\partial_{0}v=\xi-u^{(0)}.

(129)

This can be solved perturbatively in
\hbar
:

\displaystyle v=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{n}v_{n},

(130)

because we know the perturbative expansion of the potential

\displaystyle u^{(0)}=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}(\sqrt{2}\hbar)^{2g}u_{g}.

(131)

At the lowest order, we have

\displaystyle v_{0}^{2}=\xi-u_{0}.

(132)

Therefore there are two branches
v_{0}=\pm\sqrt{\xi-u_{0}}
. These branches of course give two independent solutions to (
126
). Here we focus on the branch
v_{0}=\sqrt{\xi-u_{0}}
. The other is obtained by a simple replacement, as explained in
[
30
]
.

The semi-classical coefficients
v_{n}
are easily obtained by a recursion relation (see Eq. (3.13) in
[
30
]
).
We find

\displaystyle v_{0}=\zeta,\qquad v_{1}=\frac{1}{4t\zeta^{2}},\qquad v_{2}=-\frac{5}{32t^{2}\zeta^{5}}-\frac{1}{t^{3}}\biggl(\frac{I_{2}}{8\zeta^{3}}+\frac{I_{3}}{24\zeta}\biggr)-\frac{I_{2}^{2}}{12t^{4}\zeta},

(133)

where
\zeta=\sqrt{\xi-u_{0}}=z/\sqrt{2}
. Once we know
v_{n}
, the semi-classical expansion of
\mathcal{A}
is obtained by
\partial_{0}\mathcal{A}=v
.
Putting the ansatz

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{n}\mathcal{A}_{n},

(134)

we solve
\partial_{0}\mathcal{A}=v
order by order, and obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3}t\zeta^{3}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n+1}I_{n+1}}{(2n+3)!!}\zeta^{2n+3},

(135)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\log\zeta-\frac{1}{2}\log(4\pi),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{5}{48t\zeta^{3}}-\frac{I_{2}}{24t^{2}\zeta},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{64t^{2}\zeta^{6}}+\frac{1}{t^{3}}\biggl(\frac{I_{2}}{16\zeta^{4}}+\frac{I_{3}}{48\zeta^{2}}\biggr)+\frac{I_{2}^{2}}{24t^{4}\zeta^{2}}.

Note that, as shown in Appendix
E
, the odd order part of
\mathcal{A}
is easily obtained by the even order part of
v
.

We next consider the Christoffel-Darboux kernel, which is related to the BA function by

\displaystyle K(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_{-\infty}^{t_{0}}dx\,\psi(\xi,x)\psi(\eta,x),

(136)

or equivalently

\displaystyle\hbar\partial_{0}K(\xi,\eta)=\psi(\xi,t_{0})\psi(\eta,t_{0}).

(137)

The diagonal component is then given by

\displaystyle\hbar\partial_{0}K(\xi,\xi)=\psi(\xi,t_{0})^{2}.

(138)

We have also another useful expression
[
31
]
:

\displaystyle K(\xi,\eta)=\hbar\frac{\partial_{0}\psi(\xi,t_{0})\psi(\eta,t_{0})-\psi(\xi,t_{0})\partial_{0}\psi(\eta,t_{0})}{\xi-\eta}.

(139)

In this expression, the diagonal part is written as

\displaystyle K(\xi,\xi)=\hbar\bigl(\psi(\xi,t_{0})\partial_{0}\partial_{\xi}\psi(\xi,t_{0})-\partial_{\xi}\psi(\xi,t_{0})\partial_{0}\psi(\xi,t_{0})\bigr).

(140)

Plugging the WKB form (
128
) into it and recalling
\partial_{0}\mathcal{A}=v
, we obtain

\displaystyle K(\xi,\xi)=\partial_{0}\partial_{\xi}\mathcal{A}\exp\biggl(\frac{2\mathcal{A}}{\hbar}\biggr)=\exp\biggl[\frac{1}{\hbar}\biggl(2\mathcal{A}+\hbar\log\partial_{\xi}v\biggr)\biggr].

(141)

This allows us to compute the WKB expansion of
K(\xi,\xi)
systematically:

\displaystyle K(\xi,\xi)=\exp\biggl(\frac{1}{\hbar}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{n}K_{n}\biggr).

(142)

After some computations, we arrive at

\displaystyle K_{0}

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{A}_{0},

(143)

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=-2\log\zeta-\log(4\pi),

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{17}{24t\zeta^{3}}-\frac{I_{2}}{12t^{2}\zeta},

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{13}{16t^{2}\zeta^{6}}+\frac{I_{2}}{2t^{3}\zeta^{4}}+\frac{I_{2}^{2}}{6t^{4}\zeta^{2}}+\frac{I_{3}}{12t^{3}\zeta^{2}}.

We claim that under the parameter identification:
\hbar=2\pi^{3}g_{s}
and
\xi=\pi^{2}x
, a function defined by

\displaystyle\Psi(x;g_{s}):=K(\xi,\xi)|_{y=0,t=1},

(144)

is the wave function appearing in the topological recursion context in
[
22
]
.
In fact, the WKB form of the diagonal CD kernel is given by

\displaystyle K(\xi,\xi)|_{y=0,t=1}=\exp\biggl(\frac{1}{g_{s}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{s}^{n}\mathbb{S}_{n}\biggr)

(145)

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{2}

\displaystyle=2\pi^{3}K_{2}|_{y=0,t=1}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\sqrt{x}}-\frac{17}{12x^{3/2}},

(146)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{3}

\displaystyle=(2\pi^{3})^{2}K_{3}|_{y=0,t=1}=\frac{\pi^{4}}{2x}+\frac{2\pi^{2}}{x^{2}}+\frac{13}{4x^{3}}.

See Eqs. (4.17) and (4.18) in
[
22
]
.
The main advantage of this connection is that we can use the Schrödinger equation for the BA function to construct
\Psi(x;g_{s})
. This route presents us a quite systematic way to obtain the WKB solution.
Once we know the wave function (
144
), one can also compute the one-instanton corrections by using the saddle-point method, explained in
[
22
]
.

Note that this wave function solves the so-called quantum curve, a quantization of the spectral curve of the JT gravity:

\displaystyle\biggl(g_{s}^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-Q(x;g_{s})\biggr)\Psi(x;g_{s})=0,

(147)

where

\displaystyle Q(x;g_{s})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin^{2}(2\pi\sqrt{x})}{4\pi^{2}}+g_{s}\biggl(\frac{\sin(2\pi\sqrt{x})}{\pi\sqrt{x}}-\frac{\cos(2\pi\sqrt{x})}{2\sqrt{x}}\biggr)

(148)

\displaystyle+g_{s}^{2}\biggl(\frac{2}{x^{2}}-\frac{(51+2\pi^{2}x)\sin(2\pi\sqrt{x})}{24\pi x^{5/2}}\biggr)+O(g_{s}^{3}).

It seems that the quantum curve receives an infinite number of quantum corrections, and it is hard to find its closed form.

7
Conclusion

In this paper we have studied non-perturbative corrections in the JT gravity
from the transseries solution to the KdV equation.
We explicitly constructed a one-parameter transseries solution to the KdV equation by a systematic algorithm. Our approach is based on general 2d topological gravity, and the results for the JT gravity are easily obtained as a special case

t_{k}=\gamma_{k}
. The results perfectly agree with another independent calculation from topological recursions in random matrices
[
22
]
.

There are many interesting open questions.
Our formalism is valid for multi-instanton sectors, and it is intriguing to compare it with other methods, particularly the matrix model approach.
In this paper, we have only considered a one-parameter transseries solution of the
KdV equation.
For full generality, we need to consider the
multi-parameter transseries solution.
It would be interesting to study the general structure of such a
multi-parameter transseries solution.

Also, in our approach, the Stokes data in the transseries is not determined. We guessed it by the additional resurgent analysis in subsection
5.1
.
In this sense, the KdV equation itself seems incomplete to determine the full non-perturbative corrections.
The construction of the complete transseries solution is important to see a “non-perturbative completion” of the model.
As discussed in
[
5
]
, the effective potential for the
JT gravity matrix model is not bounded from below, and it seems a problem to define the model non-perturbatively.

Appendix B
Sequence for the leading term of
\ell
-instanton sector

We begin with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi^{(\ell)}_{0}=-\frac{3}{\ell(\ell^{2}-1)z^{2}_{\ast}}\sum^{\ell-1}_{m=1}m\phi^{(\ell-m)}_{0}\phi^{(m)}_{0}.\end{split}

(151)

This equation suggests that
\phi^{(\ell)}_{0}
takes the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi^{(\ell)}_{0}=(-1)^{\ell-1}a_{\ell}\frac{e^{\ell A_{1}}}{z^{2(\ell-1)}_{\ast}}\end{split}

(152)

where
a_{\ell}
is a sequence determined by the recurrence relation

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{\ell}=\frac{3}{\ell(\ell^{2}-1)}\sum^{\ell-1}_{m=1}ma_{\ell-m}a_{m},\end{split}

(153)

with the initial condition
a_{\ell}=1
.

We conjecture that the solution to (
153
) is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{\ell}=\frac{\ell}{2^{2\ell-2}}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\text{if $\ell$ is odd}\\[2.0pt]
2^{\nu_{2}(\ell/2)+b_{\ell/2}-1}&\text{if $\ell$ is even}\end{array}\right.,\end{split}

(154)

where
\nu_{2}(n)
is the 2-adic valuation of an integer
n
, defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nu_{2}(n)=\max\{k=0,1,2,\ldots\mid\text{$2^{k}$ divides $n$}\},\end{split}

(155)

and
b_{n}
is a sequence defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}b_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}-1-\nu_{2}(n/4)&\text{if $n\,\mathrm{mod}\,4=0$}\\[2.0pt]
1&\text{if $n\,\mathrm{mod}\,4=1$}\\[2.0pt]
0&\text{if $n\,\mathrm{mod}\,4=2$}\\[2.0pt]
1&\text{if $n\,\mathrm{mod}\,4=3$}\end{array}\right..\end{split}

(156)

If we compute the sequence
a_{\ell}
using (
153
) in
Mathematica
and compare the results with (
154
), then we observe a perfect match.

Appendix C
Derivation of equation (
74
)

Here we derive (
74
).
The
\ell
-instanton sector is expanded as

\displaystyle u^{(\ell)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{g=0}u_{g}\lambda^{2g}&(\ell=0)\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\lambda^{\ell\beta}e^{\ell A/\lambda}\sum^{\infty}_{h=0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}\lambda^{h}&(\ell\geq 1)\end{array}\right..

Using this, we expand

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{1}u^{(\ell)}=\sum^{\ell}_{m=0}u^{(\ell-m)}\partial_{0}u^{(m)}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{12}\partial^{3}_{0}u^{(\ell)},\end{split}

(157)

and collect terms of order
\lambda^{h-1}
. In the following, we assume that
\ell\geq 2
.

The term in the left-hand side of the equation (
157
) is expanded as

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\ell\beta}e^{-\ell A/\lambda}\partial_{1}u^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{h=0}\lambda^{h-1}\ell\partial_{1}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}+\sum^{\infty}_{h=0}\lambda^{h}\partial_{1}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{h=0}\lambda^{h-1}\ell\partial_{1}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}+\sum^{\infty}_{h=1}\lambda^{h-1}\partial_{1}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-1}.

Next, we write the first term in the right-hand side of (
157
) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum^{\ell}_{m=0}u^{(\ell-m)}\partial_{0}u^{(m)}=u^{(\ell)}\partial_{0}u^{(0)}+u^{(0)}\partial_{0}u^{(\ell)}+\sum^{\ell-1}_{m=1}u^{(\ell-m)}\partial_{0}u^{(m)}.\end{split}

(158)

Each term in (
158
) is expanded as

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\ell\beta}e^{-\ell A/\lambda}u^{(\ell)}\partial_{0}u^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\sum^{\infty}_{g=0}\lambda^{i+2g}\phi^{(\ell)}_{i}\partial_{0}u_{g}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{h=1}\lambda^{h-1}\sum^{[(h-1)/2]}_{g=0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2g-1}\partial_{0}u_{g},

and

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\ell\beta}e^{-\ell A/\lambda}u^{(0)}\partial_{0}u^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum^{\infty}_{g=0}\lambda^{2g}u_{g}\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}(\lambda^{i-1}\ell\partial_{0}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{i}+\lambda^{i}\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum^{\infty}_{h=0}\lambda^{h-1}\ell\partial_{0}A\sum^{[h/2]}_{g=0}u_{g}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2g}+\sum^{\infty}_{h=1}\lambda^{h-1}\sum^{[(h-1)/2]}_{g=0}u_{g}\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2g-1},

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum^{\infty}_{h=0}\lambda^{h-1}\biggl(\ell y\partial_{0}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}+\ell\partial_{0}A\sum^{[h/2]}_{g=1}u_{g}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2g}\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum^{\infty}_{h=1}\lambda^{h-1}\biggl(y\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-1}+\sum^{[(h-1)/2]}_{g=1}u_{g}\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2g-1}\biggr),

and

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\ell\beta}e^{-\ell A/\lambda}\sum^{\ell-1}_{m=1}u^{(\ell-m)}\partial_{0}u^{(m)}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum^{\ell-1}_{m=1}\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\sum^{\infty}_{g=0}(\lambda^{i+g-1}m\partial_{0}A\phi^{(\ell-m)}_{g}\phi^{(m)}_{i}+\lambda^{i+g}\phi^{(\ell-m)}_{g}\partial_{0}\phi^{(m)}_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum^{\infty}_{h=0}\lambda^{h-1}\partial_{0}A\sum^{\ell-1}_{m=1}\sum^{h}_{g=0}m\phi^{(\ell-m)}_{g}\phi^{(m)}_{h-g}+\sum^{\infty}_{h=1}\lambda^{h-1}\sum^{\ell-1}_{m=1}\sum^{h-1}_{g=0}\phi^{(\ell-m)}_{g}\partial_{0}\phi^{(m)}_{h-g-1}.

Finally, the second term in the right-hand side of (
157
) is expanded as

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\ell\beta}e^{-\ell A/\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{12}\partial_{0}^{3}u^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{12}\sum^{\infty}_{h=0}\lambda^{h+2}\biggl[\biggl(\frac{\ell\partial_{0}A}{\lambda}\biggr)^{3}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}+3\frac{\ell\partial_{0}A}{\lambda}\frac{\ell\partial_{0}^{2}A}{\lambda}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}+3\biggl(\frac{\ell\partial_{0}A}{\lambda}\biggr)^{2}\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\ell\partial_{0}^{3}A}{\lambda}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}+3\frac{\ell\partial_{0}^{2}A}{\lambda}\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}+3\frac{\ell\partial_{0}A}{\lambda}\partial_{0}^{2}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}+\partial_{0}^{3}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}\biggr]

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum^{\infty}_{h=0}\lambda^{h-1}\frac{(\ell\partial_{0}A)^{3}}{12}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}+\sum^{\infty}_{h=1}\lambda^{h-1}\biggl(\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}\partial_{0}A\partial_{0}^{2}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-1}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}(\partial_{0}A)^{2}\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-1}\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum^{\infty}_{h=2}\lambda^{h-1}\biggl(\frac{\ell}{12}\partial_{0}^{3}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2}+\frac{\ell}{4}\partial_{0}^{2}A\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2}+\frac{\ell}{4}\partial_{0}A\partial_{0}^{2}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2}\biggr)+\sum^{\infty}_{h=3}\lambda^{h-1}\frac{1}{12}\partial_{0}^{3}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-3}.

Plugging these expansions into (
157
), we obtain

\displaystyle\ell\partial_{1}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}+\partial_{1}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-1}

\displaystyle=\sum^{[(h-1)/2]}_{g=0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2g-1}\partial_{0}u_{g}+\ell y\partial_{0}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}+\ell\partial_{0}A\sum^{[h/2]}_{g=1}u_{g}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2g}

\displaystyle+y\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-1}+\sum^{[(h-1)/2]}_{g=1}u_{g}\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2g-1}

\displaystyle+\partial_{0}A\sum^{\ell-1}_{m=1}\sum^{h}_{g=0}m\phi^{(\ell-m)}_{g}\phi^{(m)}_{h-g}+\sum^{\ell-1}_{m=1}\sum^{h-1}_{g=0}\phi^{(\ell-m)}_{g}\partial_{0}\phi^{(m)}_{h-g-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{(\ell\partial_{0}A)^{3}}{12}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}\partial_{0}A\partial_{0}^{2}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-1}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}(\partial_{0}A)^{2}\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{\ell}{12}\partial_{0}^{3}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2}+\frac{\ell}{4}\partial_{0}^{2}A\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2}+\frac{\ell}{4}\partial_{0}A\partial_{0}^{2}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2}+\frac{1}{12}\partial_{0}^{3}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-3}.

This equation is equivalento to

\displaystyle-\ell\partial_{t}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}-\frac{(\ell\partial_{0}A)^{3}}{12}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h}

\displaystyle=\partial_{0}A\sum^{\ell-1}_{m=1}\sum^{h}_{g=0}m\phi^{(\ell-m)}_{g}\phi^{(m)}_{h-g}+\sum^{\ell-1}_{m=1}\sum^{h-1}_{g=0}\phi^{(\ell-m)}_{g}\partial_{0}\phi^{(m)}_{h-g-1}

\displaystyle+\sum^{[(h-1)/2]}_{g=0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2g-1}\partial_{0}u_{g}+\partial_{t}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-1}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}\partial_{0}A\partial_{0}^{2}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}(\partial_{0}A)^{2}\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-1}+\ell\partial_{0}A\sum^{[h/2]}_{g=1}u_{g}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2g}+\frac{\ell}{12}\partial_{0}^{3}A\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\ell}{4}\partial_{0}^{2}A\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2}+\frac{\ell}{4}\partial_{0}A\partial_{0}^{2}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2}+\sum^{[(h-1)/2]}_{g=1}u_{g}\partial_{0}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-2g-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{12}\partial_{0}^{3}\phi^{(\ell)}_{h-3}.

Appendix D
Formulae for boundary creation operator

In this appendix, we present useful formulas that describe how the boundary creation operator
\hat{V}(b)
acts on several important quantities.

The action of
\hat{V}(b)
on the instanton action
A
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{V}(b)A&=\hat{V}(b)V_{\text{eff}}|_{\xi=\xi_{\ast}}\\
&=2\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{z^{2k+1}_{\ast}}{(2k+1)!!}\mathcal{I}_{k}(b).\end{split}

(159)

In the first equality, we used the condition
\partial_{\xi}V_{\text{eff}}|_{\xi=\xi_{\ast}}=0
. The second equality follows from a straightforward calculation using (
31
), and
\mathcal{I}_{k}(b)
is defined by (
30
). Combining (
159
) and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{0}\mathcal{I}_{n}(b)=\frac{\mathcal{I}_{n+1}(b)}{t},\end{split}

(160)

we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{V}(b)z_{\ast}&=-\frac{\mathcal{I}_{0}(b)}{tz_{\ast}}-\frac{z^{(1)}_{\ast}}{z^{2}_{\ast}}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{2^{n}n!}{(2n)!}z^{2n}_{\ast}\mathcal{I}_{n}(b).\end{split}

(161)

Next, using (
161
) and a commutation relation

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{V}(b)\partial_{t}=\partial_{t}\hat{V}(b)+\frac{\mathcal{I}_{0}(b)}{t}\partial_{0},\end{split}

(162)

we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{V}(b)z^{(1)}_{\ast}&=-\Biggl(\frac{z^{(2)}_{\ast}}{z^{2}_{\ast}}-\frac{2(z^{(1)}_{\ast})^{2}}{z^{3}_{\ast}}\Biggr)\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{2^{n}n!}{(2n)!}z^{2n}_{\ast}\mathcal{I}_{n}(b)-\frac{(z^{(1)}_{\ast})^{2}}{z^{2}_{\ast}}\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{2^{n}n!}{(2n-1)!}z^{2n-1}_{\ast}\mathcal{I}_{n}(b).\end{split}

(163)

Furthermore, incorporating (
159
), (
161
) and (
163
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{V}(b_{1})\hat{V}(b_{2})A=-\frac{2\mathcal{I}_{0}(b_{1})\mathcal{I}_{0}(b_{2})}{tz_{\ast}}-\frac{2z^{(1)}_{\ast}}{z^{2}_{\ast}}\Biggl(\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{2^{n}n!}{(2n)!}z^{2n}_{\ast}\mathcal{I}_{n}(b_{1})\Biggr)\Biggl(\sum^{\infty}_{m=0}\frac{2^{m}m!}{(2m)!}z^{2m}_{\ast}\mathcal{I}_{m}(b_{2})\Biggr).\end{split}

(164)

In particular, for
(y,t)=(0,1)
, the above expressions simplify to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\hat{V}(b)A=\frac{2}{b_{i}}\sinh bz_{\ast},\\
&\hat{V}(b)z_{\ast}=-\frac{1}{z_{\ast}}-\frac{z^{(1)}_{\ast}}{z^{2}_{\ast}}\cosh bz_{\ast},\\
&\hat{V}(b)z^{(1)}_{\ast}=-\Biggl(\frac{z^{(2)}_{\ast}}{z^{2}_{\ast}}-\frac{2(z^{(1)}_{\ast})^{2}}{z^{3}_{\ast}}\Biggr)\cosh bz_{\ast}-\frac{(z^{(1)}_{\ast})^{2}}{z^{2}_{\ast}}b\sinh bz_{\ast},\\
&\hat{V}(b_{1})\hat{V}(b_{2})A=-\frac{2}{z_{\ast}}-\frac{2z^{(1)}_{\ast}}{z^{2}_{\ast}}\cosh b_{1}z_{\ast}\cosh b_{2}z_{\ast}.\end{split}

(165)

Appendix E
Odd part in WKB expansion of Baker-Akhiezer function

We see that the odd order part of
\mathcal{A}
-function in (
128
) is easily computed from the even order part of
v
.
To see it, we divide
v
as

\displaystyle v=v_{\text{even}}+v_{\text{odd}},\qquad v_{\text{even}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{2k}v_{2k},\qquad v_{\text{odd}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{2k+1}v_{2k+1}

(166)

Substituting it into the Riccati equation, we obtain

\displaystyle v_{\text{even}}^{2}+v_{\text{odd}}^{2}+2v_{\text{even}}v_{\text{odd}}+\hbar(\partial_{0}v_{\text{even}}+\partial_{0}v_{\text{odd}})=\xi-u^{(0)}.

(167)

By looking at the even/odd order structure, this equation can be split into the following two equations:

\displaystyle v_{\text{even}}^{2}+v_{\text{odd}}^{2}+\hbar\partial_{0}v_{\text{odd}}

\displaystyle=\xi-u^{(0)},

(168)

\displaystyle 2v_{\text{even}}v_{\text{odd}}+\hbar\partial_{0}v_{\text{even}}

\displaystyle=0.

From the second equation, we get

\displaystyle v_{\text{odd}}=-\frac{\hbar}{2}\frac{\partial_{0}v_{\text{even}}}{v_{\text{even}}}=-\frac{\hbar}{2}\partial_{0}\log v_{\text{even}}.

(169)

Therefore the odd order part of
\mathcal{A}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\text{odd}}=-\frac{\hbar}{2}\log v_{\text{even}}.

(170)

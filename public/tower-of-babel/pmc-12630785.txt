Enhanced non resonant transmission through subwavelength apertures in conducting screens using self dual coats
Authors: Roee Geva, Anton Kharchevskii, Omer Tzidki, Pavel Ginzburg, Raphael Kastner
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Self-dual media (SDM) coatings provide a powerful means to enhance wave transmission through subwavelength apertures in conducting screens. When applied around an aperture, SDM suppresses reflections and directs incident electromagnetic energy exclusively through the opening. Experimental and numerical studies show that a finite, double-sided SDM coating with dimensions of about \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda /2 \times \lambda /2$$\end{document} increases transmitted power by roughly 10 dB compared to an uncoated reference. This effect is non-resonant, enabling broadband operation constrained only by the material design. Unlike earlier methods that mainly improved radiation directivity without altering the transmitted power, this approach significantly boosts power density within the aperture itself. These findings highlight SDM coatings as a versatile strategy for efficient energy transmission through sub-wavelength structures, with potential applications in waveguiding, sensing, phased arrays and energy harvesting technologies.

Introduction

The transmission of electromagnetic waves through subwavelength apertures in conducting screens has been a subject of sustained interest since Bethe’s pioneering model1, which described such apertures in terms of a limited set of electric and magnetic dipole moments. These so-called “Bethe holes” found early application in microwave devices such as directional couplers.

In the late 1990s, Ebbesen and colleagues2 reported the phenomenon of extraordinary optical transmission (EOT) through periodic arrays of subwavelength apertures. This effect was attributed to coupling between incident light and surface plasmon modes supported by the periodic structure, producing transmission resonances at frequencies set by the array periodicity. Subsequent studies demonstrated similar enhancements for single apertures surrounded by corrugations3,4, and comprehensive plasmonic models were developed5,6. Other interpretations invoked leaky-wave excitation7,8, diffraction-order phase matching9, or Fabry–Pérot-type resonances10. In all cases, however, the enhancement mechanism was fundamentally resonant, and therefore narrowband.

An alternative strategy involves embedding resonant inclusions, such as split-ring resonators11, within or near apertures. This approach has since evolved into the field of Mie-resonant metaphotonics (or “Mie-tronics”), which exploits high-permittivity dielectric particles to achieve strong local field enhancement across a wide spectral range12–16. Despite their versatility, these methods remain resonance-based and bandwidth-limited. Similarly, structuring the surface surrounding apertures—through corrugations17 or metamaterial coatings18— has been shown to improve radiation directivity, though the total transmitted power remains comparable to that of an uncoated aperture.

The emergence of metamaterials18–21 has provided new opportunities to engineer reflection and transmission at conducting interfaces. In this work, we propose a fundamentally different approach: enhancing transmission through subwavelength apertures using ultra-thin self-dual medium (SDM) coatings. SDMs are balanced electromagnetic materials with equal relative permittivity and permeability (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\epsilon _r=\mu _r$$\end{document}), resulting in zero reflection under normal incidence. Initially conceived as idealized continuous media, SDMs have since been realized in discretized form using complementary electric and magnetic inclusions22. This property has enabled reflection-free energy guiding in narrow waveguides and the design of wideband radiating elements for phased arrays23.

The ability of SDMs to suppress reflections hinges on artificial magnetic conductors (AMCs), which have been extensively investigated in the forms of metamaterials and metasurfaces9,24–32. While AMCs are generally bandwidth-limited, numerous strategies for multi-band, wideband, and angularly stable designs have been reported33–50. In this study, we employ mushroom-type AMC unit cells28 to realize practical SDM coatings.

In comparison, frequency selective surfaces (FSSs)52 are typically resonant structures designed to shape frequency and angular response across extended metallic surfaces, rather than to enhance transmission through existing apertures. In contrast to resonance-based methods, SDM coatings suppress reflections and funnel incident energy through the aperture in a broadband manner. While practical realizations are limited by the finite bandwidth of artificial magnetic conductors, the underlying mechanism is non-resonant. As shown in this work, a finite, double-sided SDM coating of subwavelength dimensions can achieve an order-of-magnitude increase in transmitted power compared with a bare aperture. This result establishes SDM coatings as a promising pathway for efficient, wideband transmission through subwavelength structures with potential applications in waveguiding, sensing, and energy harvesting.

This mechanism provides the theoretical foundation for the enhanced transmission effects demonstrated in subsequent sections.

Self-dual element design evolution

We begin with an idealized SDM element, illustrated in Fig. 2. In this schematic, the blue and green trapezoidal regions correspond to perfect electric conductor (PEC) and perfect magnetic conductor (PMC) domains, respectively. The central aperture coincides with a pre-existing opening in the conducting screen. This arrangement satisfies the self-duality condition in (1), analogous to the rotationally symmetric case depicted in Fig. 1.

A central challenge in the realization of SDM coatings is the practical implementation of PMC behavior, which is commonly approximated using artificial magnetic conductor (AMC) structures. To address this, we employ arrays of deeply subwavelength mushroom-type unit cells, originally introduced in28 and subsequently developed in the metasurface framework21,55. A polarizability model for the enhancement mechanism

To quantify the mechanism responsible for concentrating and redirecting energy through the subwavelength aperture, we employ a polarizability-based model, analogous to those used in aperture coupling for couplers and resonators53. In this framework, the fringing fields at the aperture edges are represented by equivalent dipoles. For symmetric configurations (e.g., double-sided coatings), the two apertures are modeled by identical dipoles, giving an effective total dipole moment twice that of a single aperture.

For single-sided coatings, symmetry is broken. When illuminated from the uncoated side, the dipole on that side is weaker, reducing transmission relative to the double-sided case. Nonetheless, significant coupling persists depending on the application.

Discussion

We have demonstrated both numerically and experimentally that transmission through a subwavelength aperture in a conducting screen can be significantly enhanced. This mechanism increases the power density, and consequently the dipole moment, inside the aperture relative to a bare screen. Importantly, the transmitted field is nearly omnidirectional, unlike approaches that focus the beam while preserving total transmitted power.

The enhancement is fundamentally non-resonant; however, the effective bandwidth is limited by the implementation of the artificial magnetic conductor (AMC). Rather than enlarging the aperture to achieve directional transmission, the proposed structure maintains the characteristics of a small source while substantially increasing the transmitted power density, producing a broad angular distribution. This behavior is clearly evident in the field distributions of Fig. 18.

Unlike previous studies on electromagnetic funneling using self-dual structures, the boundary conditions along the hole depth in this design do not strictly conform to a self-dual medium (SDM). Nevertheless, the enhancement effect remains robust and pronounced. This is also the first report on fabrication and testing of an SDM.

This phenomenon has potential for diverse applications, including high-efficiency RF couplers and sensitive detection of weak signals in enclosed environments. In particular, phased array radiating elements could benefit from intrinsic impedance matching across wide scan angles and broad bandwidths23.

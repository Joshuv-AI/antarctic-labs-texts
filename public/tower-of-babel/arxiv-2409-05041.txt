Stability and rigidity of 3 3 -Lie algebra morphisms
Jun Jiang, Yunhe Sheng, Geyi Sun

Abstract
. In this paper, first we use the higher derived brackets to construct an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra, whose Maurer-Cartan elements are 3 3 -Lie algebra morphisms. Using the differential in the L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra that govern deformations of the morphism, we give the cohomology of a 3 3 -Lie algebra morphism. Then we study the rigidity and stability of 3 3 -Lie algebra morphisms using the established cohomology theory. In particular, we show that if the first cohomology group is trivial, then the morphism is rigid; if the second cohomology group is trivial, then the morphism is stable. Finally, we study the stability of 3 3 -Lie subalgebras similarly.

1.
Introduction

For an algebraic, or a geometric structure, a meaningful problem is that describe a neighborhood of such structure in its moduli space. Deforming the structure is a useful way to solve this problem. Roughly speaking, a deformation of a structure is a small curve through the original structure in the moduli space. The concept of a formal deformation of an algebraic structure began with the seminal work of Gerstenhaber
[
17
,
18
]
for associative algebras. Nijenhuis and Richardson extended this study to Lie algebras
[
27
,
25
]
. There is a well known slogan, often attributed to Deligne, Drinfeld and Kontsevich: every reasonable deformation theory is controlled by a differential graded Lie algebra, determined up to quasi-isomorphism. This slogan has been made into a rigorous theorem by Lurie and Pridham
[
22
,
28
]
. Rigidity and stability are natural questions need to be considered in studying deformations. As important invariants, the cohomology is a useful tool to characterize deformations. In general, taking the differential of a deformation, we obtain a cocycle. Moreover, rigidity and stability can be characterized in terms of the cohomology groups.

It is also meaningful to study deformations of morphisms of given algebraic structures. Deformations of morphisms of associative algebras were studied in
[
3
,
4
]
, and deformations of morphisms of Lie algebras were studied in
[
8
,
14
,
16
,
26
]
. On the other hand, morphisms between two algebraic structures naturally give rise to subalgebras of the direct sum algebras. Deformations of subalgebras of Lie algebras were study in
[
5
,
29
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to study deformations of 3-Lie algebra morphisms, with special attention to the rigidity and stability problems.
The notion of
3
-Lie algebras, or more generally,
n
-Lie algebras (also called Filippov algebras)
[
13
]
can be seen as a generalization of Lie algebras to higher arities. See the review article
[
10
]
for their applications in mathematical physics. In particular,
n
-Lie algebras are the algebraic structures corresponding to Nambu mechanics and related to Nambu-Poisson structures
[
24
,
31
,
32
]
. Moreover,
3
-Lie algebras also appear in the study of Bagger-Lambert-Gustavsson theory of multiple M2-branes and string theory
[
2
,
7
,
11
]
. Deformations of
3
-Lie algebras were studied in
[
12
,
23
,
33
]
.
In
[
1
]
, Arfa, Ben Fraj and Makhlouf introduced the cohomology complex of
n
-Lie algebra morphisms and studied one-parameter formal deformations of
n
-Lie algebras morphisms.

In this paper, first we use the derived bracket approach to give the controlling algebra of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms. Consequently, the cohomology of a
3
-Lie algebra morphism is introduced using the differential in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra that govern deformations of the morphism. Then we study (geometric) deformations of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms, which is different from formal deformations considered in
[
1
]
. In particular, rigidity and stability of deformations of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms are explored. We show that for a 3-Lie algebra morphism
f:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
, if
H^{1}(f)=0,
then it is rigid; if
H^{2}(f)=0,
then it is stable. Due to the close relation between morphisms and subalgebras, we also study deformations of
3
-Lie subalgebras similarly.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we construct an
L_{\infty}
-algebra via the higher derived brackets, whose Maurer-Cartan elements are
3
-Lie algebra morphisms (Theorem
2.12
). In particular, we give the
L_{\infty}
-algebra that governs deformations of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms using Getzler’s twisted brackets. In Section
3
, we introduce the cohomology of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms using the differential in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra that governs deformations of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms. In Section
4
, (geometric) deformations of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms are studied. We give the conditions when
3
-Lie algebra morphisms are rigid and stable (Theorem
4.8
, Theorem
4.11
). We also give the sufficient condition on a cocycle giving a deformation of a
3
-Lie algebra morphism (Theorem
4.9
). In Section
5
, we study deformations of
3
-Lie subalgebras with special attention to the stability (Theorem
5.5
). We show that deformations of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms give rise to deformations of
3
-Lie subalgebras (Proposition
5.6
), and establish the relation between the corresponding cohomology groups.

2.
Derived brackets and Maurer-Cartan characterization of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms

In this section, first we recall derived brackets which is a very useful way to construct
L_{\infty}
-algebras. Then we construct the controlling algebra of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms using derived brackets.

2.1.
L_{\infty}
-algebras and derived brackets

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}^{k}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space.
The desuspension operator
s^{-1}
changes the grading of
\mathfrak{g}
according to the rule
(s^{-1}\mathfrak{g})^{i}:=\mathfrak{g}^{i+1}
. The degree
-1
map
s^{-1}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,s^{-1}\mathfrak{g}
is defined by sending
v\in\mathfrak{g}
to its copy
s^{-1}v\in s^{-1}\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
20
]

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}^{k}
equipped with a collection
(k\geq 1)
of linear maps
l_{k}:\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
of degree
1
with the property that, for any homogeneous elements
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

(i)

(graded symmetry) for every
\sigma\in S_{n}
,

\displaystyle l_{n}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})=\varepsilon(\sigma)l_{n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}),

(ii)

(generalized Jacobi identity) for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in S(i,n-i)}\varepsilon(\sigma)l_{n-i+1}(l_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})=0,

where
\varepsilon(\sigma)=\varepsilon(\sigma;v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})
is the Koszul sign for a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
and
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\in V
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
15
]

A Maurer-Cartan element of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
is an element
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{0}
satisfying the Maurer-Cartan equation

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k}(\alpha,\cdots,\alpha)=0.

Remark 2.3
.

In general, the Maurer-Cartan equation (
1
) makes sense when the
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a filtered
L_{\infty}
-algebra
[
9
]
. In the following, the
L_{\infty}
-algebra under consideration satisfies
l_{k}=0
for
k
sufficiently big, so the Maurer-Cartan equation makes sense.

Let
\alpha
be a Maurer-Cartan element. Define
l_{k}^{\alpha}:\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}~~(k\geq 1)
by

(2)

l_{k}^{\alpha}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{n!}l_{k+n}(\underbrace{\alpha,\cdots,\alpha}_{n},x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}).

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[
19
]
)

(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}^{\alpha}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, called the twisted
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Now we recall the V-data and derived brackets, which are very powerful tool to construct explicit
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Definition 2.5
.

A
V
-data consists of a quadruple
(L,F,P,\Delta)
, where

\bullet

(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a graded Lie algebra,

\bullet

F
is an abelian graded Lie subalgebra of
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
,

\bullet

P:L\,\rightarrow\,L
is a projection, that is
P\circ P=P
, whose image is
F
and its kernel is a graded Lie subalgebra of
(L,[\cdot,\cdot])
,

\bullet

\Delta
is an element in
\ker(P)^{1}
such that
[\Delta,\Delta]=0
.

Theorem 2.6
.

(
[
34
]
)

Let
(L,F,P,\Delta)
be a
V
-data. Then
(F,\{{l_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra where

\displaystyle l_{k}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})=P\underbrace{[\cdots[[}_{k}\Delta,a_{1}],a_{2}],\cdots,a_{k}],\quad\mbox{for homogeneous}~a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}\in F.

Moreover, there is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
s^{-1}L\oplus F
constructed as following.

Theorem 2.7
.

(
[
34
,
15
]
)

Let
(L,F,P,\Delta)
be a
V
-data. Then the graded vector space
s^{-1}L\oplus F
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra where the nontrivial products, called the higher derived brackets, are given by

\displaystyle l_{1}(s^{-1}f,\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-s^{-1}[\Delta,f],P(f+[\Delta,\theta])),

\displaystyle l_{2}(s^{-1}f,s^{-1}g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{f}s^{-1}[f,g],

\displaystyle l_{k}(s^{-1}f,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P[\cdots[[f,\theta_{1}],\theta_{2}]\cdots,\theta_{k-1}],\quad k\geq 2,

\displaystyle l_{k}(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k-1},\theta_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P[\cdots[[\Delta,\theta_{1}],\theta_{2}]\cdots,\theta_{k}],\quad k\geq 2.

Here
\theta,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k}
are homogeneous elements of
F
and
f,g
are homogeneous elements of
L
.

Moreover, if
L^{\prime}
is a graded Lie subalgebra of
L
that satisfies
[\Delta,L^{\prime}]\subset L^{\prime}
, then
s^{-1}L^{\prime}\oplus F
is an
L_{\infty}
-subalgebra of the above
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(s^{-1}L\oplus F,\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
.

2.2.
Maurer-Cartan characterization of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms

Definition 2.8
.

[
13
]

A
3
-Lie algebra is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
with a linear map
\pi:\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
such that

\pi(x_{1},x_{2},\pi(x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}))=\pi(\pi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),x_{4},x_{5})+\pi(x_{3},\pi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}),x_{5})+\pi(x_{3},x_{4},\pi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{5})),

where
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g},1\leq i\leq 5.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
and
(\mathfrak{h},\mu)
be
3
-Lie algebras. A
3
-Lie algebra morphism is a linear map
f:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
such that

f(\pi(x,y,z))=\mu(f(x),f(y),f(z)),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector space. Consider the following graded vector space

C^{*}_{\text{3-Lie}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n\geq 0}C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n}\wedge\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\quad(n\geq 0).

Theorem 2.10
.

(
[
30
]
)

With the above notations,
(C^{*}_{\text{\rm{3-Lie}}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{R}})
is a graded Lie algebra, where

(3)

\displaystyle[P,Q]_{\mathsf{R}}=P{\circ}Q-(-1)^{pq}Q{\circ}P,\quad\forall~P\in C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),Q\in C^{q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),

and
P{\circ}Q\in C^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

\displaystyle(P{\circ}Q)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q},x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{(k-1)q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}(k-1,q)}(-1)^{\sigma}P\Big(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k-1)},Q\big(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k+q-1)},x_{k+q}\big)\wedge y_{k+q},\mathfrak{X}_{k+q+1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q},x\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{(k-1)q}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}(k-1,q)}(-1)^{\sigma}P\Big(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k-1)},x_{k+q}\wedge Q\big(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(k+q-1)},y_{k+q}\big),\mathfrak{X}_{k+q+1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{p+q},x\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}(p,q)}(-1)^{pq}(-1)^{\sigma}P\Big(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p)},Q\big(\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+1)},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q-1)},\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma(p+q)},x\big)\Big),

for all
\mathfrak{X}_{i}=x_{i}\wedge y_{i}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
,
~i=1,2,\cdots,p+q
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Moreover,
\pi:\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a
3
-Lie algebra on
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
[\pi,\pi]_{\mathsf{R}}=0
, i.e.
\pi
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}_{\text{\rm{3-Lie}}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{R}})
.

In the following, we use derived brackets to construct an
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are
3
-Lie algebra morphisms.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be vector spaces and
\mathcal{G}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
. Denote by
L
the graded vector space

L=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathcal{G}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathcal{G}}_{n}\wedge\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}).

Denote by
\mathcal{G}^{l,k}
the subspace of

\otimes^{n}(\wedge^{2}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}))\wedge(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})

where the numbers of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
are
l
and
k
respectively. Then the vector space
\otimes^{n}(\wedge^{2}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}))\wedge(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})
is
expended into
\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{l+k=2n+1}\mathcal{G}^{l,k}
. Thus we have that

L\cong\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}\Big(\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l+k=2n+1}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{G}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{G}^{l,k},\mathfrak{h})\Big)

and denote this isomorphism by
\mathrm{H}:\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}\Big(\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l+k=2n+1}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{G}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{G}^{l,k},\mathfrak{h})\Big)\,\rightarrow\,L
.

For
P\in\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n}\wedge\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
, we have

(4)

\mathrm{H}(P)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},(x,a))=(0,P(x_{1}\wedge y_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\wedge y_{n},x)),

where
\mathfrak{X}_{i}=(x_{i},a_{i})\wedge(y_{i},b_{i})\in\wedge^{2}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})
.

Moreover, using this isomorphism, we can transfer the graded Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{R}}
on
L
to

\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}\Big(\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l+k=2n+1}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{G}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{G}^{l,k},\mathfrak{h})\Big),

which is also denoted by
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{R}}
, i.e.

[A,B]_{\mathsf{R}}=\mathrm{H}^{-1}([\mathrm{H}(A),\mathrm{H}(B)]_{\mathsf{R}}),\quad\forall A,B\in\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}\Big(\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l+k=2n+1}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{G}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{G}^{l,k},\mathfrak{h})\Big).

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
and
(\mathfrak{h},\mu)
be
3
-Lie algebras. We have a
V
-data
(L,F,P,\Delta)
as follows:

\bullet

the graded Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{R}})
is given by

(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})}_{n}\wedge(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{R}});

\bullet

the abelian graded Lie subalgebra

\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n}\wedge\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})

\bullet

is the projection onto the subspace
F
;

\bullet

\Delta=\pi+\mu
.

Consequently, we obtain an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(F,\{l_{k}\}^{+\infty}_{k=1})

(5)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} l_{1}(a_{1})&=[\pi,a_{1}]_{\mathsf{R}},\\

Proof.

For
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in F
, since

\mathrm{H}([Q_{1},Q_{2}]_{\mathsf{R}})=([\mathrm{H}(Q_{1}),\mathrm{H}(Q_{2})]_{\mathsf{R}})=\mathrm{H}(Q_{1}){\circ}\mathrm{H}(Q_{2})-(-1)^{pq}\mathrm{H}(Q_{2}){\circ}\mathrm{H}(Q_{1})

is an abelian graded Lie subalgebra.

It is obvious that

. Since

and

-Lie algebra structures on
\mathfrak{g}
and

, by Theorem

, we have

[\pi,\pi]_{\mathsf{R}}=[\mu,\mu]_{\mathsf{R}}=0.

Moreover, since

3.
Cohomologies of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms

In this section, we establish the cohomology theory of a
3
-Lie algebra morphism using Getzler’s twisted
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Definition 3.1
.

[
21
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
be a
3
-Lie algebra and
V
be a vector space. A representation of
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
on

is a linear map
\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(V,V)
such that

\displaystyle\rho(x_{1},x_{2})\rho(x_{3},x_{4})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\pi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),x_{4})+\rho(x_{3},\pi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}))+\rho(x_{3},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{2}),

\displaystyle\rho(x_{1},\pi(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x_{3},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{2})-\rho(x_{2},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{3})+\rho(x_{2},x_{3})\rho(x_{1},x_{4}),

where
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
be a
3
-Lie algebra. Then
\mathrm{ad}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is a representation, where

\mathrm{ad}_{x\wedge y}z=\pi(x,y,z),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

It is also called the adjoint representation.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a
3
-Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Consider the following short exact sequence of vector spaces:

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow 0.

Choose a section
s:\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.
p\circ s={\rm{Id}}
. Define a map
\overline{\mathrm{ad}}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
by

\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{u\wedge v}(\overline{x})=\overline{\pi(u,v,s(\overline{x}))},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h},\bar{x}\in\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}.

Proposition 3.3
.

With the above notations, the map
\overline{\mathrm{ad}}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
is independent of the choice of sections and is a representation of
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

For another section
t:\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.
p\circ t={\rm{Id}}
, we have that
\mathrm{Im}(s-t)\in i(\mathfrak{h})
and
\overline{\pi(u,v,s(\overline{x}))}-\overline{\pi(u,v,t(\overline{x}))}=\overline{\pi(u,v,(s-t)(\overline{x}))}=0
. Thus
\overline{\mathrm{ad}}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
is independent of the choice of sections.

For any
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}\in\mathfrak{h}
and
\overline{x}\in\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
, we have that

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{u_{1}\wedge u_{2}}\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{u_{3}\wedge u_{4}}(\overline{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\pi(u_{1},u_{2},s\Big(\overline{\pi(u_{3},u_{4},s(\overline{x}))}\Big))}=\overline{\pi\Big(u_{1},u_{2},\pi(u_{3},u_{4},s(\overline{x}))}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{\pi(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\wedge u_{4}}(\overline{x})+\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{u_{3}\wedge\pi(u_{1},u_{2},u_{4})}(\overline{x})+\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{u_{3}\wedge u_{4}}\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{u_{1}\wedge u_{2}}(\overline{x}).

Similarly, the following equation holds:

\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{u_{1}\wedge\pi(u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})}=\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{u_{3}\wedge u_{4}}\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{u_{1}\wedge u_{2}}-\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{x_{2}\wedge x_{4}}\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{x_{1}\wedge x_{3}}+\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{x_{2}\wedge x_{3}}\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{x_{1}\wedge x_{4}}.

Thus
\overline{\mathrm{ad}}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
is a representation of
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
.
∎

4.
Rigidity and stability of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms

In this section, we study deformations of
3
-Lie algebra morphism. We show that the condition
H^{1}(f)=0
will imply a rigidity theorem, and the condition
H^{2}(f)=0
will imply a stability theorem.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
f
be a
3
-Lie algebra morphism from
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
to
(\mathfrak{h},\mu)
. A deformation of
f
is a smooth one parameter family of morphisms
f_{t}
from
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
to
(\mathfrak{h},\mu)
such that
f_{0}=f
.

Definition 4.2
.

Two deformations
f_{t}
and
f_{t}^{\prime}
of
f
are called equivalent if there exists two smooth family of inner isomorphisms of
3
-Lie algebras
\varphi_{t}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
and
\psi_{t}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
such that
\varphi_{0}={\rm{Id}},\psi_{0}={\rm{Id}}
and

(11)

\varphi_{t}\circ f_{t}^{\prime}=f_{t}\circ\psi_{t}.

Note that if
\psi_{t}
and
\varphi_{t}
are inner automorphisms of
3
-Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
respectively, then there are
\mathcal{U}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{i}\wedge v_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}\wedge\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathcal{X}=\sum_{j=1}^{l}x_{j}\wedge y_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}\wedge\mathfrak{g}
such that

\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\varphi_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu(u_{i},v_{i},\cdot),\quad\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\psi_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\pi(x_{j},y_{j},\cdot).

Let
f_{t}
be a deformation of
f
. Denote
\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}f_{t}
by
\dot{f_{0}}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

With the above notations,
\dot{f_{0}}
is a
1
-cocycle in
C^{1}(f)
. Moreover if
f_{t}
and
f_{t}^{\prime}
are equivalent deformations of
f
, then
[\dot{f_{0}}]=[\dot{f_{0}^{\prime}}]
in
H^{1}(f)
.

Proof.

Since
f_{t}
is a
3
-Lie algebra morphism, for any
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

(12)

\displaystyle\mu(\dot{f_{0}}(x),f(y),f(z))+\mu(f(x),\dot{f_{0}}(y),f(z))+\mu(f(x),f(y),\dot{f_{0}}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\mu(f_{t}(x),f_{t}(y),f_{t}(z))=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}f_{t}(\pi(x,y,z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{f_{0}}(\pi(x,y,z)).

Thus by (
10
) and (
12
), we have
\delta(\dot{f_{0}})=0
.

Assume that
\varphi_{t}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
and
\psi_{t}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
are inner isomorphisms such that
f_{t}
and
f^{\prime}_{t}
are equivalent.
Denote
\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\varphi_{t}
by
\dot{\varphi_{0}}
and
\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\psi_{t}
by
\dot{\psi_{0}}
. Taking derivative of (
11
), and using the fact that
\varphi_{0}={\rm{Id}}
and
\psi_{0}={\rm{Id}}
, we have

\dot{\varphi_{0}}(f(x))+\dot{f_{0}^{\prime}}(x)=\dot{f_{0}}(x)+f(\dot{\psi_{0}}(x)).

Since
\varphi_{t}
and
\psi_{t}
are inner automorphisms of
3
-Lie algebras
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{g}
respectively, it follows that there exist
\mathcal{U}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{i}\wedge v_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}\wedge\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathcal{X}=\sum_{j=1}^{l}y_{j}\wedge z_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}\wedge\mathfrak{g}
such that

\dot{\varphi_{0}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu(u_{i},v_{i},\cdot),\quad\dot{\psi_{0}}=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\pi(y_{j},z_{j},\cdot).

Therefore, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu(u_{i},v_{i},f(x))+\dot{f^{\prime}_{0}}(x)=\dot{f_{0}}(x)+f(\sum_{j=1}^{l}\pi(y_{j},z_{j},x)),

which implies

. Thus
[\dot{f_{0}}]=[\dot{f_{0}^{\prime}}]
in
H^{1}(f)
.
∎

\displaystyle-\alpha(u_{2}\wedge v_{2},\pi(u_{1},v_{1},w)-sp(\pi(u_{1},v_{1},w)))+\alpha(u_{1}\wedge v_{1},\pi(u_{2},v_{2},w)-sp(\pi(u_{2},v_{2},w)))

\displaystyle+p\Big(\pi(u_{1},v_{1},s(\alpha(u_{2}\wedge v_{2},w))-\pi(u_{2},v_{2},s(\alpha(u_{1}\wedge v_{1},w)))

\displaystyle-\pi(v_{2},w,s(\alpha(u_{1}\wedge v_{1},u_{2})))-\pi(w,u_{2},s(\alpha(u_{1}\wedge v_{1},v_{2})))\Big).

Then
\phi
is a vector bundle map.

Define a section
\sigma:M\,\rightarrow\,E
by

\sigma(U)=(U,\Lambda(U)),\quad\forall U\in M,

where
\Lambda(U)\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{3}U,\mathfrak{g}/U)
is given by

(15)

\Lambda(U)(u,v,w)=p(\pi(u,v,w)),\quad\forall U\in M,~u,v,w\in U.

Since
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
is a
3
-Lie algebra, we have that

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}(\Lambda(U))(u_{1}\wedge v_{1},u_{2}\wedge v_{2},w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-p\Big(\pi(\pi(u_{1},v_{1},u_{2})-sp(\pi(u_{1},v_{1},u_{2})),v_{2},w)\Big)-p\Big(\pi(u_{2},\pi(u_{1},v_{1},v_{2})-sp(\pi(u_{1},v_{1},v_{2})),w)\Big)

\displaystyle-p\Big(\pi(u_{2},v_{2},\pi(u_{1},v_{1},w)-sp(\pi(u_{1},v_{1},w)))\Big)+p\Big(\pi(u_{1},v_{1},\pi(u_{2},v_{2},w)-sp(\pi(u_{2},v_{2},w)))\Big)

\displaystyle+p\Big(\pi(u_{1},v_{1},sp(\pi(u_{1},v_{1},w)))\Big)-p\Big(\pi(u_{2},v_{2},sp(\pi(u_{1},v_{1},w)))\Big)

\displaystyle-p\Big(\pi(v_{2},w,sp(\pi(u_{1},v_{1},u_{2})))\Big)-p\Big(\pi(w,u_{2},sp(\pi(u_{1},v_{1},v_{2})))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Thus
\phi(\sigma(U))=(U,0)
, i.e.
\phi\circ\sigma=0
.

Moreover, by (
15
), for
\mathfrak{h}\in M
, we have that
\sigma(\mathfrak{h})=0
if and only if
\mathfrak{h}
is a
3
-Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Denote
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}:E_{\mathfrak{h}}\,\rightarrow\,F_{\mathfrak{h}}
by
\phi(\mathfrak{h},\cdot)
, then
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}
is the coboundary operator of
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},\pi)
with coefficients in the representation
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
. For any
a\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})\cong T_{\mathfrak{h}}M
, there exists
A(t)\in\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
such that
a(u)=p(\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}A(t)u)
, we have

\displaystyle D^{v}(\sigma)_{\mathfrak{h}}(a)(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}A(t)^{*}\Big(\Lambda(A(t)\mathfrak{h})\Big)(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}A(t)^{-1}\Big(\pi(A(t)u,A(t)v,A(t)w)\Big))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\Big(-a(\pi(u,v,w))+\pi(a(u),v,w)+\pi(u,a(v),w)+\pi(u,v,a(w))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial(a)(u,v,w),

where
A(t)^{*}:\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h}_{t},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{t})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
is given by

A(t)^{*}(f_{t})(u)=[A(t)^{-1}\Big(f_{t}(A(t)u)\Big)],\quad\forall~~f_{t}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{t},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}_{t}),u\in\mathfrak{h}.

Since
H^{2}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})=0
, it follows that

T_{\mathfrak{h}}M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D^{v}(\sigma)_{\mathfrak{h}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}E_{\mathfrak{h}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}F_{\mathfrak{h}}

is exact. By Proposition
4.6
, we obtain that the space
H
of
3
-Lie subalgebras is a manifold in a neighborhood of
\mathfrak{h}
, whose dimension is
\mathrm{dim}Z^{1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
.

Moreover, assume
\gamma(t)\in H
, by Proposition
5.2
, we have
\dot{\gamma}(0)\in Z^{1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
. Since
\mathrm{dim}H=\mathrm{dim}Z^{1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
, then it means that
T_{\mathfrak{h}}H=Z^{1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
. Thus any
\alpha\in Z^{1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
gives rise to a deformation of
\mathfrak{h}
.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

(Stability of
3
-Lie subalgebra)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
be a
3
-Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be a
k
-dimensional
3
-Lie subalgebra of
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
. If

, then
\mathfrak{h}
is a stable subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

Let
M=\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
and
E=\coprod\limits_{V\in M}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{3}V,\mathfrak{g}/V),F=\coprod\limits_{V\in M}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}V\otimes\wedge^{2}V\wedge V,\mathfrak{g}/V)
. Then
E
and
F
are vector bundles over
M
whose fibres of

are
\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{3}V,\mathfrak{g}/V)
and
\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}V\otimes\wedge^{2}V\wedge V,\mathfrak{g}/V)

Denote by
\Omega
the space of all

-Lie algebra structures on
\mathfrak{g}
. For any
\varpi\in\Omega
, there is a section
\sigma_{\varpi}\in\Gamma(E)
given by

\sigma_{\varpi}(V)=(V,\Lambda_{\varpi}(V)),\quad\text{where}\quad\Lambda_{\varpi}(V)(u,v,w)=p(\varpi(u,v,w)),\quad\forall u,v,w\in V,

and a bundle map
\phi_{\varpi}:E\,\rightarrow\,F
,

\phi_{\varpi}(V,\alpha)=(V,\tilde{\partial}_{\varpi}(\alpha)),\quad\forall V\in M,\alpha\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{3}V,\mathfrak{g}/V),

where
\tilde{\partial}_{\varpi}:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{3}V,\mathfrak{g}/V)\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}V\otimes\wedge^{2}V\wedge V,\mathfrak{g}/V)
is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}_{\varpi}(\alpha)(u_{1}\wedge v_{1},u_{2}\wedge v_{2},w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha\Big((\varpi(u_{1},v_{1},u_{2})-sp(\varpi(u_{1},v_{1},u_{2})))\wedge v_{2}+u_{2}\wedge(\varpi(u_{1},v_{1},v_{2})-sp(\varpi(u_{1},v_{1},v_{2}))),w\Big)

\displaystyle-\alpha(u_{2}\wedge v_{2},\varpi(u_{1},v_{1},w)-sp(\varpi(u_{1},v_{1},w)))+\alpha(u_{1}\wedge v_{1},\varpi(u_{2},v_{2},w)-sp(\varpi(u_{2},v_{2},w)))

\displaystyle+p\Big(\varpi(u_{1},v_{1},s(\alpha(u_{2}\wedge v_{2},w))-\varpi(u_{2},v_{2},s(\alpha(u_{1}\wedge v_{1},w)))

\displaystyle-\varpi(v_{2},w,s(\alpha(u_{1}\wedge v_{1},u_{2})))-\varpi(w,u_{2},s(\alpha(u_{1}\wedge v_{1},v_{2})))\Big).

Moreover, the map

\Omega\,\rightarrow\,\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(\mathrm{Hom}(E,F)),\quad\varpi\,\rightarrow\,(\sigma_{\varpi},\phi_{\varpi}),

is continuous under the

-topology on the space of sections.

It follows that
\phi_{\varpi}\circ\sigma_{\varpi}(V)=(V,0)
for all
\varpi\in\Omega
. Assume that
\sigma_{\pi}(\mathfrak{h})=(\mathfrak{h},0)
, i.e.

is a

-Lie subalgebra of

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
and
(\mathfrak{h},\mu)
be
3
-Lie algebras. If
f_{t}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
is a deformation of the
3
-Lie algebra morphism
f:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
, then there is a deformation of the
3
-Lie subalgebra
\mathrm{G}_{f}=\{(x,f(x))|\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}\}
in
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
, where the
3
-Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
is given by

[(x,u),(y,v),(z,w)]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}}=\Big(\pi(x,y,z),\mu(u,v,w)\Big),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proof.

Since
f_{t}
is a deformation of

, then
\mathrm{G}_{f_{t}}
is a curve in

in

.

On the cohomology groups level, we have the following proposition, which implies that the rigidity and the stability of
3
-Lie algebra morphisms and subalgebras of
3
-Lie algebras are insistent.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
and
(\mathfrak{h},\mu)
be
3
-Lie algebras,
f:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
be a
3
-Lie algebra morphism. Then
H^{k}(f)\cong H^{k}\Big(\mathrm{G}_{f},(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})/\mathrm{G}_{f}\Big)
for
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

For any
n\geq 1
, define maps

\Xi_{n}:\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}\wedge\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}\Big(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathrm{G}_{f}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathrm{G}_{f}}_{n-1}\wedge\mathrm{G}_{f},(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})/\mathrm{G}_{f}\Big)

by

\displaystyle\Xi_{n}(\alpha)\Big((x_{1},f(x_{1}))\wedge(y_{1},f(y_{1})),\cdots,(x_{n-1},f(x_{n-1})),(x,f(x))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(0,\alpha(x_{1}\wedge y_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\wedge y_{n-1},x))].

Since
\Xi_{n}
are injective and
\mathrm{dim}(C^{n}(f))=\mathrm{dim}(C^{n}(\mathrm{G}_{f},(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})/\mathrm{G}_{f})
, then
\Xi_{n}
are isomorphic for
n\geq 1
.

Moreover, since
\overline{\mathrm{ad}}_{(x,f(x))\wedge(y,f(y))}[(z,u)]=[(0,\mu(f(x),f(y),u-f(z)))]
, we have

\displaystyle\partial(\Xi_{n}(\alpha))\Big((x_{1},f(x_{1}))\wedge(y_{1},f(y_{1})),\cdots,(x_{n},f(x_{n})),(x,f(x))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\Big(0,\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(-1)^{i}\alpha\Big(x_{1}\wedge y_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}\wedge y_{i}},\cdots,x_{j-1}\wedge y_{j-1},\pi(x_{i},y_{i},x_{j})\wedge y_{j}

\displaystyle+x_{j}\wedge\pi(x_{i},y_{i},y_{j}),\cdots,x_{n}\wedge y_{n},x\Big)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}\alpha\Big(x_{1}\wedge y_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}\wedge y_{i}},\cdots,x_{n}\wedge y_{n},\pi(x_{i},y_{i},x)\Big)\Big)]

\displaystyle+[\Big(0,\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\mu(f(x_{i}),f(y_{i}),\alpha(x_{1}\wedge y_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}\wedge y_{i}},\cdots,x_{n}\wedge y_{n},x))

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}\Big(\mu(f(y_{n}),f(x),\alpha(x_{1}\wedge y_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\wedge y_{n-1},x_{n})

\displaystyle+\mu(f(x),f(x_{n}),\theta(x_{1}\wedge y_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\wedge y_{n-1},y_{n}))\Big)\Big)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Xi_{n+1}(\delta(\alpha))\Big((x_{1},f(x_{1}))\wedge(y_{1},f(y_{1})),\cdots,(x_{n},f(x_{n})),(x,f(x))\Big).

Thus
H^{k}(f)\cong H^{k}\Big(\mathrm{G}_{f},(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})/\mathrm{G}_{f}\Big)
for
k\geq 1
.
∎

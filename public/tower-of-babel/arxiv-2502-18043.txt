Microscopic study of halo nuclei through ( p , t ) (p,t) reactions Journal: Physics Letters B
P. Descouvemont

Abstract
We analyze ( p , t ) (p,t) two-neutron transfer reactions in a semi-microscopic model. The overlap integrals of the target nucleus are calculated in a microscopic cluster model. The Resonating Group Method (RGM) assumes a cluster structure of the nucleus, and is well adapted to halo nuclei since the long-range part of the wave function is accurately described. We focus on ( p , t ) (p,t) reactions involving 6 He and 11 Li, which are well known core+n+n halo nuclei. The RGM is based on a nucleon-nucleon interaction, and therefore does not involve any fitting procedure. It also provides overlap integrals of excited states of the core nucleus. We present overlap integrals and spectroscopic factors of 6 He and 11 Li. We compute the He 6 ​ ( p , t ) 4 ​ He {}^{6}{\rm He}(p,t)^{4}{\rm He} and Li 11 ​ ( p , t ) 9 ​ Li {}^{11}{\rm Li}(p,t)^{9}{\rm Li} cross sections at the DWBA, and compare them with experiments. For 11 Li we also determine the Li 11 ​ ( p , t ) 9 ​ Li ∗ \mbox{${}^{11}{\rm Li}(p,t)^{9}{\rm Li}$}^{*} cross section which involves the first excited states of 9 Li. A fair agreement with experiment is obtained, considering that no parameter is adjusted.



1
Introduction

Halo nuclei represent an active field in nuclear physics. Given the low neutron separation energy, these exotic nuclei are close to the driplines, and present unusual properties, such as a large radius
[
1
]
or soft E1 excitations
[
2
]
. The development of radioactive beams has permitted the investigation of halo nuclei through various reaction mechanisms
[
3
]
. Elastic scattering, breakup, and fusion are among the most used techniques
[
4
]
. These experiments,
however, do not provide a direct access to the nuclear spectroscopy. Scattering models, linking the description of the colliding nuclei and the reaction mechanism, are therefore necessary.

The interest of
(p,t)
reactions as a spectroscopic tool is well known
[
5
,
6
]
. This two-neutron transfer process has been shown to provide valuable information on neutron-rich nuclei (see Ref.
[
7
]
and references therein). In
(p,t)
reactions, the target nucleus is seen as a core surrounded by the two transferred neutrons, and
therefore provides an efficient tool to probe the structure of halo nuclei. Two-neutron transfer data have been obtained on light exotic nuclei, such as
6
He
[
8
,
9
]
,
8
He
[
10
]
, or

11
Li
[
11
]
.

Calculations of
(p,t)
cross sections on stable nuclei started long ago (see, for example, Refs.
[
12
,
13
]
).
A detailed description of the literature is presented in Ref.
[
7
]
. A three-body model of the halo nucleus was used in Ref.
[
14
]
for
{}^{6}{\rm He}(p,t)^{4}{\rm He}
and in Ref.
[
15
]
for
{}^{11}{\rm Li}(p,t)^{9}{\rm Li}
. The influence of the transfer reactions on elastic scattering was investigated in Ref.
[
16
]
. In Ref.
[
17
]
, the author discusses the normalization of the
{}^{6}{\rm He}(p,t)^{4}{\rm He}
cross section with shell-model overlap functions.

An aim in the present work is to study
(p,t)
reactions in a semi-microscopic model, where overlap integrals are obtained in the Resonating Group Method (RGM, see Ref.
[
18
]
). In this approach, the Pauli principle is exactly taken into account. Even if a neutron cannot be considered as a genuine cluster, a cluster model is well adapted to describe halo nuclei since the long-range part of the wave function can be accurately described. Another advantage
is that core excited states can be included. The method and its application to several light halo nuclei are presented in Ref.
[
19
]
. In
(p,t)
reactions, the scattering wave functions on the entrance and exit channels are obtained from an optical potential.

The text is organized as follows. First, we briefly describe the RGM, and emphasize the calculation of the overlap integrals. Then we show how to determine the transfer scattering matrices by using the DWBA. Applications to the
{}^{6}{\rm He}(p,t)^{4}{\rm He}
and
{}^{11}{\rm Li}(p,t)^{9}{\rm Li}
reactions are presented.

2
Overlap integrals in the RGM

Our main aim is to investigate
(p,t)
reactions with a microscopic description of the target. To this end, the Hamiltonian of the target nucleus, involving
A
nucleons, is written as

\displaystyle H_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{A}t_{i}+\sum_{i<j}^{A}v_{ij},

(1)

where
t_{i}
is the kinetic energy of nucleon
i
, and
v_{ij}
an effective nucleon-nucleon interaction. It involves a central nuclear term, a spin-orbit component, and the Coulomb interaction. For exotic nuclei, such as
11
Li, the Schrödinger equation associated with Hamiltonian (
1
) does not have an exact solution. We use the Resonating Group Method (RGM)
[
20
,
18
]
which assumes that the nucleons are grouped in clusters. This technique is well adapted to halo nuclei, which present a marked core + neutron structure.

In a three-cluster approach, the
A
-nucleon wave function is therefore (schematically) written as

\displaystyle\Psi_{A}=\sum_{c}\mathcal{A}\Phi^{c}_{A-2}\Phi_{n}\Phi_{n}G_{c}(\boldsymbol{r_{x}},\boldsymbol{r_{y}}),

(2)

where
\mathcal{A}
is the antisymmetrization operator, and
\boldsymbol{r_{x}}
and
\boldsymbol{r_{y}}
are, respectively, the neutron-neutron distance, and the relative coordinate between the core and the two-neutron system. For the sake of clarity we omit the spin and parity, as well as the various angular-momentum couplings. In Eq. (
2
),
\Phi^{c}_{A-2}
and
\Phi_{n}
are the core wave function and a neutron spinor, and
G_{c}(\boldsymbol{r_{x}},\boldsymbol{r_{y}})
is a relative function. The summation runs over different core states, labeled by
c
. Typical examples of halo nuclei are the
6
He and
11
Li nuclei, which involve an
\alpha
and
9
Li core, respectively.

The core wave functions
\Phi^{c}_{A-2}
are defined in the shell model as

\displaystyle\Phi^{c}_{A-2}=\sum_{i}d_{i}^{c}\phi_{i},

(3)

where
\phi_{i}
are Slater determinants involving
A-2
harmonic oscillator orbitals. Coefficients
d_{i}^{c}
are obtained from the diagonalization of the angular-momentum and isospin operators
[
21
]
. The core wave functions (
3
) are therefore characterized by spin, parity and isospin quantum numbers, as expected from the physics of the problem. These indices, however, are omitted for the sake of clarity in this general presentation.

The shell-model description of the
\alpha
particle is very simple (4 nucleons in the 0s shell) and a microscopic cluster description of
6
He has been used by several authors (see, for example, Refs.
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
).
In contrast, a microscopic approach of the
11
Li nucleus is more difficult
[
25
,
26
]
. A
p
-shell description of the
9
Li core involves 90 Slater determinants
\phi_{i}
. The main difficulty arises from angular momentum projection of the total wave function (
2
). This projection involves 7-dimension integrals, which must be performed numerically. The calculation is fast for
6
He, but highly time consuming for heavier systems such as
11
Li.

In practice, we express the target wave functions (
2
) by using the Generator Coordinate Method, where it can be written as a superposition of projected Slater determinants (see, for example, Refs.
[
27
,
18
,
21
]
for details). For three-cluster systems, we adopt the hyperspherical coordinates, where the six coordinates
\boldsymbol{r_{x}}
and
\boldsymbol{r_{y}}
are replaced by the hyperradius, the hyperangle, and the 4 angles associated with
\boldsymbol{r_{x}}
and
\boldsymbol{r_{y}}
. In this approach, the wave function (
2
) is expanded over hyperspherical harmonics involving the hypermoment
K

[
28
,
29
]
.

A significant improvement of the RGM, compared to non-microscopic models, is that excited states of the core are naturally included. It was shown in Refs.
[
26
,
19
]
that core excitations play an important role in the description of
11
Li. The
9
Li core presents a 1/2
-
excited state at low energy (2.69 MeV) and is expected to affect the
11
Li wave function, and therefore the
{}^{11}{\rm Li}(p,t)^{9}{\rm Li}
cross section.

An important quantity in transfer cross sections is the overlap integral
[
30
]
. In a three-body model, the two-neutron overlap integral in channel
c
is defined as

\displaystyle I_{c}(\boldsymbol{r_{x}},\boldsymbol{r_{y}})=\langle\Phi^{c}_{A-2}\Phi_{n}\Phi_{n}|\Psi_{A}\rangle,

(4)

and is different from the relative function
G_{c}(\boldsymbol{r_{x}},\boldsymbol{r_{y}})
owing to the presence of the antisymmetrizer in the total wave function (
2
). The numerical calculation of the overlap integrals is described in Ref.
[
19
]
, where we follow a method developed by Varga and Lovas
[
31
]
. In practice,
I_{c}(\boldsymbol{r_{x}},\boldsymbol{r_{y}})
is expanded in hyperspherical functions and is used as an input in the
(p,t)
cross sections. The calculation of transfer cross sections to excited states of the residual (core) nucleus is possible with the overlap integrals associated with various channels
c
. This effect is important in the
{}^{11}{\rm Li}(p,t)^{9}{\rm Li}
reaction, where the cross section to the
9
Li excited state has been measured
[
11
]
.

From the overlap integral (
4
), we define the radial amplitude as

\displaystyle P_{c}(r_{x},r_{y})=\int|I_{c}(\boldsymbol{r_{x}},\boldsymbol{r_{y}})|^{2}d\Omega_{x}d\Omega_{y},

(5)

which provides the spectroscopic factor in channel
c
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{c}=\int P_{c}(r_{x},r_{y})dr_{x}\,dr_{y}.

(6)

The definition of the spectroscopic factor is well known for two-body systems, but can be extended to three (or more) body systems. This quantity naturally arises in transfer reactions
[
32
,
33
]
.

3
DWBA calculation of
(p,t)
cross sections

We now consider a
(p,t)
reaction on a
A
-nucleon system composed of a core and of two surrounding neutrons. The initial scattering wave function at energy
E_{i}
and in partial wave
J\pi
reads

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}^{JM\pi}(E_{i},\boldsymbol{R})=

\displaystyle\left[[\Psi_{A}\otimes\Phi_{p}]^{S_{i}}\otimes Y_{L_{i}}(\Omega_{R})\right]^{JM}

\displaystyle\times\chi^{J\pi}_{S_{i}L_{i}}(E_{i},R),

(7)

where
\boldsymbol{R}
is the relative coordinate and
L_{i}
the orbital angular momentum. In our approach, the target nucleus is described in the RGM with definition (
2
).

Similarly, in the exit channel, the scattering wave function involving the state
c
of the core nucleus, is defined by

\displaystyle\Psi_{f,c}^{JM\pi}(E_{f},\boldsymbol{R^{\prime}})=

\displaystyle\left[[\Phi^{c}_{A-2}\otimes\Phi_{t}]^{S_{f}}\otimes Y_{L_{f}}(\Omega_{R^{\prime}})\right]^{JM}

\displaystyle\times\chi^{J\pi}_{S_{f}L_{f},c}(E_{f},R^{\prime}),

(8)

where
\boldsymbol{R^{\prime}}
is the relative coordinate between the triton and the residual nucleus, and
E_{f}
the relative energy in the exit channel. Notice that it can be in the ground state, but also in excited states. This property will allow us to study the
\mbox{${}^{11}{\rm Li}(p,t)^{9}{\rm Li}$}^{*}
reaction to the
9
Li first excited state. In Eqs. (
7
,
8
), the relative functions are obtained from optical potentials. The channel spins are denoted as
S_{i}
and
S_{f}
.

From the scattering wave functions, we compute the transfer scattering matrices at the DWBA from

\displaystyle U^{J\pi}_{fc,i}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\Psi^{JM\pi}_{f,c}(E_{f})|V_{tr}|\Psi^{JM\pi}_{i}(E_{i})\rangle,

(9)

where
V_{tr}
is the transition potential. The scattering matrix can be reformulated as

\displaystyle U^{J\pi}_{if}=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int\chi^{J\pi}_{S_{i}L_{i}}(E_{i},R)\,{\mathcal{K}}^{J\pi}_{L_{i}L_{f}}(R,R^{\prime})\,

\displaystyle\times\chi^{J\pi}_{S_{f}L_{f},c}(E_{f},R^{\prime})dRdR^{\prime},

(10)

where the transfer kernel is defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}^{J\pi}_{L_{i}L_{f}}(R,R^{\prime})=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}\langle I^{3}(\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{x},\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}_{y})\,Y_{L_{f}}(\Omega_{R^{\prime}})

\displaystyle|V_{tr}|I_{c}^{A}(\boldsymbol{r}_{x},\boldsymbol{r}_{y})\,Y_{L_{i}}(\Omega_{R})\rangle.

(11)

In this equation,
{\mathcal{J}}
is the Jacobian, and
I^{3}
and
I_{c}^{A}
are the overlap integrals of the triton and of the residual nucleus (
6
He or
11
Li), respectively. We have omitted angular-momentum couplings for the sake of clarity. In Eq. (
11
),
\boldsymbol{r_{x}}
,
\boldsymbol{r_{y}}
,
\boldsymbol{r^{\prime}_{x}}
, and
\boldsymbol{r^{\prime}_{y}}
are expressed as functions of
\boldsymbol{R}
, and
\boldsymbol{R^{\prime}}
, and the integration is performed over
\Omega_{R}
and
\Omega_{R^{\prime}}
.

We closely follow the formalism presented in Ref.
[
15
]
, with two main differences: (1) the overlap integrals are obtained in a microscopic model; (2) core excitations are included, and provide the associated scattering matrices. From the scattering matrices, the transfer cross sections
d\sigma_{t}/d\Omega
can be easily determined.

In the next sections, the RGM description of the
6
He and
11
Li nuclei is given in Ref.
[
19
]
. We adopt the same conditions of the calculations.

7
Conclusion and outlook

This paper explores the structure of halo nuclei, such as
6
He and
11
Li, through
(p,t)
reactions, which offer insights into their spectroscopic properties. We employ a semi-microscopic model using the Resonating Group Method (RGM) to calculate overlap integrals, incorporating the Pauli principle and allowing for core excitations. This approach does not contain any fitting parameter, and is particularly suitable for halo nuclei due to their core + neutron + neutron structure.

The RGM generates microscopic overlap integrals used as input for calculating
(p,t)
cross sections within the Distorted Wave Born Approximation (DWBA). The model is applied to both
{}^{6}{\rm He}(p,t)^{4}{\rm He}
and
{}^{11}{\rm Li}(p,t)^{9}{\rm Li}
reactions. For
6
He, the calculated cross section reasonably matches experimental data, although it overestimates values at small angles. The results demonstrate a low sensitivity to the choice of the
6
He+p
optical potential.

In the case of
11
Li, the model predicts both the ground state and excited state contributions to the cross section. The spectroscopic factors for
11
Li are calculated for various channels, revealing the dominant role of the
9
Li ground state configuration. The
{}^{11}{\rm Li}(p,t)^{9}{\rm Li}
cross section calculations are compared to experimental data, showing reasonable agreement. The inclusion of core excitations and the use of microscopic overlap integrals within the RGM framework offer a valuable tool for investigating the structure of halo nuclei through
(p,t)
reactions.

The dependence of the cross section at
\theta=0^{\circ}
has been analyzed, and suggests that the
{}^{6}{\rm He}(p,t)^{4}{\rm He}
cross section could be larger and at lower energy (
\mbox{$E_{\rm lab}$}\approx 50
MeV). Generally speaking, measurements at other energies would be useful to assess theoretical models.

The present model goes beyond traditional DWBA calculations, where the projectile and residual-nucleus wave functions are described in the simple potential model, neglecting the internal structure. Of course a fully microscopic approach would be desirable, but is currently not feasible, essentially for two reasons:
(i)
we are considering weakly bound halo nuclei and a scattering model should include these properties. A microscopic cluster study of
6
He and of
11
Li is possible
[
26
]
, but is very demanding in terms of computer capabilities. Adding an incident particle means that a double angular-momentum projection is necessary which would still considerably increase the computer times.
(ii)
More important, a reaction such as
11
Li+p
, even at low energies, presents many open channels which cannot be accounted for in a microscopic theory. The use of optical potentials, which simulate the absorption to these open channels, is therefore unavoidable.

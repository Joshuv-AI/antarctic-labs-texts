Laparoscopic Appendectomy in Children: Tunisian Experience
Authors: Hayet Zitouni, Najoua Ben Kraiem, Saloua Ammar, Mahdi Ben Dhaou, Riadh Mhiri
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe purpose of this study is to compare the surgical outcomes of open appendectomy (OA) and laparoscopic appendectomy (LA) for pediatric acute appendicitis in order to guide its future management.Materials and MethodsWe conducted a retrospective study including all children under the age of 14 years, who underwent appendectomy for acute appendicitis, during a period of 6 years (2013-2018). We divided our patients in two groups based on the surgical modality: the laparoscopic and open group. P values of less than .05 were considered statistically significant.ResultsA total of 690 appendectomies were identified: 151 (22%) were performed laparoscopically and 539 (78%) via laparotomy. The demographic data were comparable between the 2 groups. There was no statistical difference regarding the age, sex. However the frequency of obese patients was higher in laparoscopic approach (P < .001).The mean operative time was significantly longer in the Laparoscopic group (77 ± 39, 6 minutes) compared with Open group (57 ± 27 minutes) (P < .001). Prophylactic drain placement was significantly more common in the open appendectomy group (P = .034). Patients undergoing laparoscopic appendectomy experienced a quicker recovery to apyrexia, reduced use of postoperative analgesics, and postoperative antibiotic compared to those undergoing open appendectomy (P < .05). The mean length of hospital stay was respectively 4 ± 1 day in LA group and 4 ± 2 days in the OA group. No significant difference was observed. The results showed no significant difference between the 2 groups regarding the incidence of wound infection, intra abdominal abscess, intestinal obstruction and umbilical hernia (P > .05).There was no death in this study.ConclusionLaparoscopic appendectomy is safe and feasible for the management of appendicitis without significant influence on the complications rate.

Introduction

Acute appendicitis is one of the most common abdominal emergencies in the pediatric population.1,2 First described in 1894 by MacBurney, appendectomy via laparotomy was the gold standard for the treatment of acute appendicitis and remained so for over a century due to its effectiveness.3 The development of laparoscopic surgery, a minimally invasive technique, was initially applied to adult patients but has since become widely used in pediatric cases. In 1983, gynecologist Kurt Semm performed the first laparoscopic appendectomy (LA), and since then, this procedure has gained popularity for the management of appendicitis in children.4,5 this popularity can be explained by the great advantages of LA that are manifested in the shorter length of hospital stay, the better cosmetic result and an earlier oral intake. However, there remains some controversy regarding operative time, postoperative complication rates, and the indications and contraindications for the procedure.6 This study aims to share our experience in laparoscopic appendectomy, to assess its safety in children by comparing the outcomes of laparoscopic and open appendectomy in order to guide the future management of pediatric acute appendicitis.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

A retrospective chart review was performed for all children who were operated for appendicitis in the department of pediatric surgery of Hedi Chaker’s Hospital between January 2013 and December 2018. Patients presenting with a clinical picture of appendicitis, who were found to have appendicitis on ultrasonographic examination (or computed tomography scan), were included. Exclusion criteria included children older than 14 years and concomitant additional surgical procedures to appendectomy (Mikel’s diverticulum, intussusception, tuberculosis).

Patients were retrospectively categorized into two groups based on the surgical approach for appendectomy. The study was conducted with the approval of the ethical committee of University of Sfax Faculty of Medicine (approval number: 8/24, date: 19 April 2024), and all patient’s records were kept confidential in the process.

Surgical Procedure

The choice of the procedure was according to the surgeons’ skills. Laparoscopic appendectomy was performed either by senior surgeon or by a trainee under the supervision of senior. However, almost of open appendectomy were done by trainees.

Conventional Laparoscopic Appendectomy: Appendectomy was performed using an open technique. Three trocars of 5 mm were inserted in umbilicus in the left lower quadrant and in suprapubic portion. The mesoappendix was cut with electrocoagulation. The appendix was tied at the base and then it was removed from the abdomen through the umbilical incision.

Laparoscopic Single Site Appendectomy (SILS)

A 15-20 mm incision through the umbilicus is made. The flexible ring covered by the glove is inserted. This single-port technique uses a 5-mm 0-degree scope. The dissection of the appendix is performed with one or two 43-cm long, 5-mm diameter laparoscopic instruments. After exteriorization of the appendix through the port site, a traditional appendectomy was achieved.

Open Appendectomy

Open appendectomy was performed using a classic Mc Burney’s incision.

Operational Definition

The child Obesity was defined after tracing the body mass index (BMI) in the BMI chart of the centers for disease control and prevention. “Obesity is defined as a BMI at or above 95th percentile for children of same age and sex.” 7

Complicated appendicitis includes perforation of the appendix or abscess formation, and peritonitis.

Hospital stay was defined as the length of stay after appendectomy.

Analgesia: a standard analgesia comprised intravenous administration of paracetamol (15 mg/kg/every 8 hours) was administered systematically on the day of the procedure. The initial dose of paracetamol was administered in the last 30 minutes of surgery. The day after pain intensity was assessed according to the number of times analgesia is used.

Postoperative ileus was defined as a clinical syndrome of abdominal distension with or without vomiting the day after the surgery.

Data Collection

All data concerning age, gender, perioperative findings, conversion, operative time, length of stay, outcomes and complications (intra-abdominal abscess formation, wound infection) were extracted from the patients’ medical records and using telephone survey. Patients and parents provided informed consent before answering a set of questionnaires.

Statistical Analysis

All data were analyzed through SPSS system: the statistical package of social science, version 18 (SPSS Inc.; Chicago, IL, USA). The continuous data such as age, operative time and hospital stay’s duration were analyzed using student’s test after the assessment of normal parametric distribution by Kolmogorov Simonov test. The Chi-square or Ficher exact test was used for the categorical variable such as gender, complications (wound infection, intestinal obstruction). The significance level was set at P ≤ .05.

Results

A total of 763 children were identified retrospectively undergoing appendectomy, however, 73 patients were excluded due to unreachable contacts or concomitant additional surgical procedures to appendectomy (Meckel’s diverticulum, intussusception, tuberculosis …..). As a result, 690 children were included in this study (Figure 1).

Laparoscopic appendectomy (LA) and open appendectomy (OA) were performed respectively on 151 patients (22%) and 539 patients (78%).

For laparoscopic group, 137 patients (90.7%) were treated by single site laparoscopic approach and 14 cases by conventional laparoscopy. Twelve cases (7.9%) were converted to open procedure and were included in the laparoscopic group data. Conversions were performed by single site laparoscopy in 9 cases and by conventional laparoscopy in 3 cases.

Patient’s Characteristics

The demographic data were comparable between the 2 groups. There was no statistical difference regarding the age and sex. Frequency of obese patients was higher in laparoscopic approach (37.1%) than laparotomy (16.7%) (P < .001).

Operative Findings

Simple acute appendicitis was the most common condition in both groups (77.6% in LA vs. 79.7% in OA). There was no difference in the distribution of the various stages of acute appendicitis (P = .56). Table 2 summarizes these findings.

The mean operative time was significantly longer in the LA group (77 ± 39minutes) compared with OA (57 ± 27 minutes), whatever simple or complicated appendicitis (P < .001). Prophylactic drain placement was significantly more used in the open appendectomy group (P = .034). (Table 2)

Post-Operative Findings

The mean length of hospital stay was respectively 4 ± 1 day in LA group and 4 ±2 days in the OA group. No significant difference was observed. The mean postoperative recovery period and time to apyrexia were one day shorter in the laparoscopic group compared to the open group (P < .0001). Additionally, the laparoscopic group had lower postoperative analgesic and antibiotic use (P < .05). However there was no statistical difference regarding the resumption to diet, rate of complications, occurrence of intra abdominal abscesses, wound infection, intestinal obstruction and umbilical hernia. Surgery was required for the treatment of two cases of intra abdominal abscesses one of which was operated on by laparoscopy and two cases of bowel obstruction who had an open appendectomy.

Discussion

The adoption of laparoscopy, particularly in children, continues to be debated regarding its advantages and disadvantages compared to open procedure.8 In recent years, the use of laparoscopic appendectomy in children has significantly increased.9,10 This has been explained by the shorter length of hospital stay, the better cosmetic result and lower morbidity’s rate11-16 even in the treatment of appendicitis in obese children. Although, some surgeons advise caution when performing laparoscopic appendectomy in obese patients, particularly in cases of complicated appendicitis.17 Michailidou’s study of 2812 children, including 619 obese patients, showed that the choice of surgical approach should be based on the surgeon’s expertise rather than the patient’s weight.13 In our study, just the frequency of obese patients was statistically higher in laparoscopic approach (37.1%) than laparotomy (16.7%) (P < .001). Additionally, laparoscopy allows better exploration of the entire abdominal cavity and pelvis, especially in girl, so as not to miss another differential diagnosis or not to cause large incision for negative appendectomy.18-20 In fact despite the development of Appendicitis scoring systems, a negative appendectomy can be observed in 6.38% of cases as reported by sefa sağ, et al21 Taken in account single site laparoscopy and conventional laparoscopy, the operative time was significantly longer in LA group, consistent with others reports.6,22,23 The reasons that may explain this are

Advancements in laparoscopic techniques and instruments, which facilitate quicker localization and dissection of the appendix, have narrowed the gap in operation time between the two procedures.24,25

Concerning the use of drain, it was significantly associated with the open appendectomy group in our study. By analyzing our results regarding the distribution of complicated appendicitis, we noticed that it was similar between both groups. Therefore we can explain this conduct by noting that trainees who performed the majority of open appendectomies, and who were concerned about the post operative complications, tended to use drainage frequently. In a recent meta-analysis, Neogi et al6 did not find any statistical differences in drain use between the laparoscopic group and open group. In any case, the drains do not prevent the need for reoperation but they can provide an early warning if a leak develops.26

In consistency with previous studies, our research found that laparoscopic appendectomy is associated with reduced postoperative pain,22,27,28 shorter recovery periods, and reduced need for postoperative antibiotics and analgesics.6 Nowadays, several authors had highlighted the use of serum interleukin to discern the degree of post-operative stress in children and they found that laparoscopic surgery is associated with lower degree of surgical stress.29-32 The validity of this biomarker was not assessed in this study.

In addition, in this work, the length of hospital stay and incidence of postoperative ileus were similar for both procedures. This similarity is likely due to the careful precautions taken by surgeons when using a new procedure while remembering that this study reports on our first 6 years dealing with laparoscopy.22

Several studies have shown that the length of hospital stay after laparoscopy, even even in simple and complicated appendicitis, is generally shorter. This could be attributed to the smaller size of incisions, lower postoperative discomfort, and quicker recovery associated with the mini invasive surgery.33,34

Among postoperative complication, intra-abdominal abscess formation (IAA) represents the main concern for LA in children.35 Previous reviewers published that LA associated with higher rates of IAA, particularly in cases of complicated appendicitis, potentially due to the spread of germs during pneumoperitoneum or partial appendectomy. Kelly and al,36 showed that the disease severity was the cause behind the IAA formation rather than the surgical approach. In this paper, the complications ratio was indeed a little bit higher for LA (4%) (Versus 3% for OA), but without making any significant difference.

As laparoscopic techniques and instruments continue to improve, and as surgeon experience in laparoscopy increases, the rate of complications is expected to decrease.37

To conclude, our findings strongly supports that laparoscopy is a safe and effective approach for the treatment of pediatric acute appendicitis consistent with others reports. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. The retrospective design of the present study may introduce selection and confounding biases, clinical and biological data for assessing surgical complications and late-stage recovery were lacking. Laparoscopic appendectomy was performed by many surgeons with different level of experience. The significantly higher drainage utilization in the OA group is possibly attributable to selection bias, given that these patients potentially had a more complex course. Finally, we included SILS and conventional laparoscopy but the little size of 3 trocars laparoscopy may not offer adequate comparison between the three surgical procedures and it could influence outcomes.

Conclusion

In the present study, the operative time in the LA group was longer, but the postoperative complication rate was similar. Furthermore, laparoscopy appendectomy was performed on obese children without incident. We believe that enhancing the learning curve may help reduce laparoscopic operative time and occurrence of complications.

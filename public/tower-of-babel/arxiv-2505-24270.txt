Nonlinear PDEs with modulated dispersion IV: normal form approach and unconditional uniqueness
Massimiliano Gubinelli, Guopeng Li, Jiawei Li, and Tadahiro Oh

Abstract
. We study the modulated Korteweg-de Vries equation (KdV) on the circle with a time non-homogeneous modulation acting on the linear dispersion term. By adapting the normal form approach to the modulated setting, we prove sharp unconditional uniqueness of solutions to the modulated KdV in L 2 ​ ( 𝕋 ) L^{2}(\mathbb{T}) if a modulation is sufficiently irregular. For example, this result implies that if the modulation is given by a sample path of a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index 0 < H < 2 5 0<H<\frac{2}{5} , the modulated KdV on the circle is unconditionally well-posed in L 2 ​ ( 𝕋 ) L^{2}(\mathbb{T}) . At a philosophical level, our normal form approach can be viewed as a controlled path approach to nonlinear Young integration , which allows for the construction of solutions to the modulated KdV (and the associated nonlinear Young integral) without assuming any positive (Hölder) regularity in time. As an interesting byproduct of our normal form approach, we obtain an improved Euler approximation scheme as compared to the classical sewing lemma approach. We also establish analogous sharp unconditional uniqueness results for the modulated Benjamin-Ono equation and the modulated derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) with a quadratic nonlinearity. In the appendix, we prove sharp unconditional uniqueness of the cubic modulated NLS on the circle in H 1 6 ​ ( 𝕋 ) H^{\frac{1}{6}}(\mathbb{T}) .

1.2.
Irregularity of the modulation

We recall a particular notion of irregularity of the modulation

Definition 1.1
.

Let

0<\gamma<1

Given

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{w}\|_{\mathcal{W}^{\rho,\gamma}_{T}}:=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{0\leq r<t\leq T}\langle a\rangle^{\rho}\frac{|\Phi^{w}_{t,r}(a)|}{|t-r|^{\gamma}}<\infty,

where

(1.9)

\displaystyle\Phi^{w}_{t,r}(a)=\int_{r}^{t}e^{iaw(t^{\prime})}dt^{\prime}.

We say that

is

(\rho,\gamma)
-irregular
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
if it is

(\rho,\gamma)
-irregular

[0,T]

In
[
13
]
,
the authors proved that a fractional Brownian motion is a
(\rho,\gamma)
-irregular function.

Theorem A
.

Let
\{W_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
be a fractional Brownian motion
of Hurst index

.
Then,
for any
\rho<\frac{1}{2H}
,
there exists
\frac{1}{2}<\gamma<1

such that, with probability one, the sample paths of

W
are
(\rho,\gamma)
-irregular on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Theorem
A

shows that there exist continuous paths which are

-irregular for arbitrarily large

In a later work
[

]

-irregularity is a generic property of Hölder functions of sufficiently low regularity; see

Theorem B
.

Let

Given any

Here, “generic” is to be understood according to the notion of

Theorems

1.4.
Unconditional uniqueness

In this subsection,

Our first theorem is for the modulated KdV (

Theorem 1.4

In this subsection, we sketch the main idea

In the unmodulated setting,

In proving Theorem

Heuristically speaking,
the normal form reduction
converts the issue of having no positive temporal regularity
into a higher order (namely, cubic in this case) nonlinearity

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}_{x}\big(\mathcal{N}^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,r}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})\big)(n)\\
&\quad=-2n\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}\\
n=n_{123}\end{subarray}}\Phi^{w}_{t,r}(\Xi_{\text{\rm KdV}}(n,n_{12},n_{3}))e^{i\Xi_{\text{\rm KdV}}(n_{12},n_{1},n_{2})w(r)}n_{12}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\widehat{f}_{j}(n_{j}),\end{split}

where we used the following short-hand notation:

(1.30)

\displaystyle n_{1\cdots k}=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}.

See Lemma
3.2
below
for the boundedness and nonlinear smoothing property
of the trilinear operator
\mathcal{N}_{t,r}^{\text{\rm KdV}}
.

By substituting (
1.25
)
into
A(t,n)

on the right-hand side of (
1.28
), we have

(1.31)

\displaystyle A(t,n)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}_{x}\big(\mathcal{N}_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\text{\rm KdV}}(\mathbf{u}(t^{\prime}),\mathbf{u}(t^{\prime}),\mathbf{u}(t^{\prime}))\big)(n)dt^{\prime}.

Then, putting
(
1.28
) and (
1.31
) together,
we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(t,n)-\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(0,n)=\mathcal{F}_{x}\big(X^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,0}(\mathbf{u}(0))\big)(n)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}_{x}\big(\mathcal{N}^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\mathbf{u}(t^{\prime}))\big)(n)dt^{\prime},

where we used the short-hand notation (
2.1
).
Namely, we have

(1.32)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{u}(t)=u_{0}+X^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,0}(u_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{N}^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\mathbf{u}(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime}.\end{split}

Hence,
we arrive at the following
normal form equation

for the
modulated KdV (
1.2
):

(1.33)

\displaystyle\begin{split}u(t)&=U^{w}(t)u_{0}+U^{w}(t)X^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,0}(u_{0})+U^{w}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{N}^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,t^{\prime}}(U^{w}(t^{\prime})^{-1}u(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime}.\end{split}

The main point of the normal form reduction
is to transform the original
equation (
1.10
)
to the normal form equation (
1.32
)
which
encodes multilinear dispersive smoothing
(coming from the irregularity of
w
)
in an explicit manner
thanks to the presence of

\Phi^{w}_{t,r}(\Xi_{\text{\rm KdV}})

in
X^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,0}

and
\mathcal{N}^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,t^{\prime}}

(see
(
1.13
)
and
(
1.29
)).
Using the boundedness properties
of
X^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,0}

and
\mathcal{N}^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,t^{\prime}}

(Lemmas
3.1

and
3.2
),
a standard contraction argument
yields
unconditional local
well-posedness
of (
1.32
) (and thus of (
1.33
))
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
,
s\geq 0
,
under the hypothesis of
Theorem
1.4
.
In the proof of
Theorem
1.4
presented
in Section
3
,
we justify the formal computation
in (
1.28
)
and show that
any solution to the modulated KdV (
1.2
)
(namely, satisfying the Duhamel formulation (
1.19
))
in the class
C([0,\tau];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))

is also a solution to the normal form equation (
1.33
),
which
in turn allows us
to show that
a unique solution
to the normal form equation (
1.33
)
is also an unconditionally unique solution to the original modulated KdV (
1.2
).

Remark 1.15
.

Consider the following modulated cubic NLS on
\mathbb{T}
:

(1.36)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u\cdot\partial_{t}w=|u|^{2}u.

The associated resonance function

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Xi_{\text{\rm NLS}}(\bar{n})&=\Xi_{\text{\rm NLS}}(n,n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})=n^{2}-n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}-n_{3}^{2}\\
&=2(n-n_{1})(n-n_{3}),\end{split}

where the last step holds under

\Xi_{\text{\rm NLS}}(\bar{n})

or

) is

(i)
does not apply.

In

58
,
90
]
,
the fourth author with his co-authors

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=|u|^{2}u.

In order to overcome

and also unconditional uniqueness

]

In Appendix

,
we implement a (Poincaré-Dulac) normal form reduction for the modulated cubic NLS (

),

. See also Remark

1.6.
Comparison with
the sewing lemma approach

In this subsection, we
compare the normal form approach
presented in the previous subsection with the sewing lemma approach
to nonlinear Young integrals in
[
23
]
.
For this purpose, we first need to introduce some preliminary notations.

Let
V
be a Banach space and
T>0
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,T}=\big\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in[0,T]^{n}:\ t_{i}>t_{j}\text{ for }i<j\big\}.

We denote by
C_{n,T}V

the space of continuous functions
from
\Delta_{n,T}
to
V
.
We define the coboundary operator

\updelta:C_{n,T}V\to C_{n+1,T}V

as follows;
given
f\in C_{n,T}V

and
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1})\in\Delta_{{n+1},T}
,
we set

\displaystyle(\updelta f)_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{k}f_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{k-1},t_{k+1},\ldots,t_{n+1}}.

For example, for
f\in C_{T}V

and

g\in C_{2,T}V
,
we have

(1.39)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\updelta f)_{t,r}&=f_{t}-f_{r},\\
(\updelta g)_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}&=g_{t_{1},t_{3}}-g_{t_{1},t_{2}}-g_{t_{2},t_{3}}\end{split}

for
(t,r)\in\Delta_{2,T}

and
(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\in\Delta_{3,T}
.
As noted in
[
57
]
,
the sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow C_{1,T}V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\updelta}}{{\longrightarrow}}C_{2,T}V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\updelta}}{{\longrightarrow}}C_{3,T}V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\updelta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots

is exact.
In particular, we have

\updelta\circ\updelta=0
and
if
f\in C_{n,T}V
with
\updelta f=0
,
then there exists a
g\in C_{n-1,T}V

such that
f=\updelta g
;
see, for example,
[
57
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Given
0<\gamma\leq 1
, we denote by
C^{\gamma}_{T}V=C^{\gamma}([0,T];V)
the space of
\gamma
-Hölder continuous functions taking values in
V
, endowed with the seminorm:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{\gamma}_{T}V}=\sup_{(t,r)\in\Delta_{2,T}}\frac{\|(\updelta f)_{t,r}\|_{V}}{|t-r|^{\gamma}}.

We also define

\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}_{T}V=\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}([0,T];V)
via the norm:

(1.40)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}_{T}V}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}V}+\|f\|_{C^{\gamma}_{T}V}.

We also introduce the spaces

C^{\gamma}_{n,T}V
,
n=2,3
,
equipped with the following Hölder-type norms;
for
g\in C_{2,T}V
and
h\in C_{3,T}V
, we set

(1.41)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|g\|_{C^{\gamma}_{2,T}V}&=\sup_{(t,r)\in\Delta_{2,T}}\frac{\|g_{t,r}\|_{V}}{|t-r|^{\gamma}},\\
\|h\|_{C^{\gamma}_{3,T}V}&=\inf_{0<\alpha<\gamma}\sup_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\in\Delta_{3,T}}\frac{\|h_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}\|_{V}}{|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\alpha}|t_{2}-t_{3}|^{\gamma-\alpha}}.\end{split}

Given a Banach space
V
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
we use
\mathrm{Lip}_{k}(V)

to denote the Banach space of
locally Lipschitz maps
f:V\to V

with polynomial growth of order
k

such that

(1.42)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}_{k}(V)}=\sup_{x,y\in V}\frac{\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{V}}{\|x-y\|_{V}\big(1+\|x\|_{V}+\|y\|_{V}\big)^{k-1}}<\infty.

Then,
from (
1.41
) and (
1.42
), we have

(1.43)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{\gamma}_{2,T}\mathrm{Lip}_{k}(V)}=\sup_{(t,r)\in\Delta_{2,T}}\frac{1}{|t-r|^{\gamma}}\sup_{x,y\in V}\frac{\|f_{t,r}(x)-f_{t,r}(y)\|_{V}}{\|x-y\|_{V}\big(1+\|x\|_{V}+\|y\|_{V}\big)^{k-1}}.

Next, we
review the essential idea
on the construction of a nonlinear Young integral
via the sewing lemma (Lemma
1.16
) presented in
[
23
]
.
Fix a driver
X\in C^{\gamma}_{2,T}\mathrm{Lip}_{k}(V)
, where
C^{\gamma}_{2,T}\mathrm{Lip}_{k}(V)

is as in (
1.43
), such that

\displaystyle X_{t,r}(0)=0

for any
(t,r)\in\Delta_{2,T}
,
and

(1.44)

\displaystyle(\updelta X)_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}=0

for any
(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\in\Delta_{3,T}
.
Given

\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];V)
for some
0<\alpha<1
,
the nonlinear Young integral
\mathcal{I}^{X}(\mathbf{u})
of
\mathbf{u}

is a unique function
with
\mathcal{I}^{X}(\mathbf{u})(0)=0

whose increment is given by

(1.45)

\displaystyle\updelta\big(\mathcal{I}^{X}(\mathbf{u})\big)_{t,r}=X_{t,r}(\mathbf{u}_{\bullet}).

Here,
\mathbf{u}_{\bullet}
denotes
the function
\mathbf{u}
evaluated at the variable of integration

\bullet
, satisfying
r\leq\bullet\leq t
.
As it is, the expression on the right-hand side of (
1.45
)
is not well defined.
By replacing
\bullet
by the left endpoint
r
,
we have

(1.46)

\displaystyle X_{t,r}(\mathbf{u}_{\bullet})=X_{t,r}(\mathbf{u}_{r})+R_{t,r}

for some two-parameter process
R=R^{X,\mathbf{u}}
.
Define
\Theta=\Theta^{X,\mathbf{u}}
on
\Delta_{2,T}

by setting

(1.47)

\displaystyle\Theta_{t,r}=X_{t,r}(\mathbf{u}_{r}),\quad(t,r)\in\Delta_{2,T}.

Then,
by applying the coboundary operator
\updelta

defined in (
1.39
)
to (
1.46
)
and noting that the left-hand side of (
1.46
) vanishes
under
\updelta
,
any error term
R
in (
1.46
) (if it exists) satisfies

(1.48)

\displaystyle(\updelta R)_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}=-(\updelta\Theta)_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}=X_{t_{1},t_{2}}(\mathbf{u}_{t_{2}})-X_{t_{1},t_{2}}(\mathbf{u}_{t_{3}})

for any
(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\in\Delta_{3,T}
,
where we used
(
1.44
) at the second equality.

We now recall
the sewing lemma due to the first author
[
54
]
;
see also

[
57
, Proposition 2.3, Corollaries 2.4 and 2.5]

and
[
43
, Lemma 4.2]
.
We set

C^{1+}_{n,T}V=\bigcup_{\gamma>1}C^{\gamma}_{n,T}V
.

Lemma 1.16

(sewing lemma)
.

Let
V
be a Banach space and
fix
T>0
.
Then, there exists a unique linear map
(
called the sewing map
)

\Lambda:C^{1+}_{3,T}V\cap\Ker\updelta|_{C_{3,T}V}\to C^{1+}_{2,T}V
such that

(i)

We have

\updelta\Lambda h=h

for each
h\in C_{3,T}V\cap\Ker\updelta|_{C_{3,T}V}
.

(ii)

For each
\zeta>1
,
the sewing map
\Lambda
is continuous
from
C^{\zeta}_{3,T}V\cap\Ker\updelta|_{C_{3,T}V}
to

C^{\zeta}_{2,T}V
such that

\displaystyle\|\Lambda h\|_{C^{\zeta}_{2,T}V}\leq\frac{1}{2^{\zeta}-2}\|h\|_{C^{\zeta}_{3,T}V}

for any
h\in C^{\zeta}_{3,T}V
.

(iii)

Given any
g\in C_{2,T}V

with

\updelta g\in C^{\zeta}_{3,T}V
,
there exists unique

f\in C([0,T];V)

(
modulo an additive constant
)

such that

\updelta f=(\Id-\Lambda\updelta)g
.
In addition,
we have

\displaystyle(\updelta f)_{t,r}=\lim_{|\Pi([r,t])|\to 0}\sum_{j=0}^{n}g_{t_{j},t_{j+1}}

for any
(t,r)\in\Delta_{2,T}
,
where
the limit is over any partition

\Pi([r,t])

of
[r,t]
:

\Pi([r,t])=\{r=t_{n}<\dots<t_{1}<t_{0}=t\}

whose mesh size

|\Pi([r,t])|=\sup_{j}|t_{j}-t_{j+1}|

tends to
0
.

Since

X\in C^{\gamma}_{2,T}\mathrm{Lip}_{k}(V)

and

\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];V)
,
it follows from (
1.48
) with (
1.43
) and (
1.40
) that

\updelta R=-\updelta\Theta\in C^{\gamma+\alpha}_{3,T}V
,
where
\Theta
is as in (
1.47
); see
[
23
, (3.27)]
.
Hence, if
\gamma+\alpha>1
,
then we can apply the sewing lemma (Lemma
1.16
)
to define an error term
R
by setting

(1.49)

\displaystyle R=-\Lambda\updelta\Theta\in C^{\gamma+\alpha}_{2,T}V.

Then,
putting
(
1.45
),
(
1.46
),
and (
1.49
) together,
we define the nonlinear Young integral
\mathcal{I}^{X}(\mathbf{u})
of
\mathbf{u}

(with respect to the nonlinear Young driver
X
)
to be a unique function
in
\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}([0,T];V)

with
\mathcal{I}^{X}(\mathbf{u})(0)=0

whose increment is given by

(1.50)

\displaystyle\updelta(\mathcal{I}^{X}(\mathbf{u}))=(\Id-\Lambda\updelta)\Theta.

Once (
1.50
) holds,
the nonlinear Young integral
\mathcal{I}^{X}(\mathbf{u})

is given by
the unique limit of
Riemann-Stieltjes type sums:

(1.51)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{X}(\mathbf{u})(t)=\lim_{|\Pi([0,t])|\to 0}\sum^{n}_{j=0}\Theta_{t_{j},t_{j+1}}=\lim_{|\Pi([0,t])|\to 0}\sum^{n}_{j=0}X_{t_{j},t_{j+1}}(\mathbf{u}_{t_{j+1}}),

where
the limit is in the sense of Lemma
1.16
(iii).

We are now ready to compare the normal form approach
with the sewing lemma approach.
Given
\rho>\frac{5}{4}
and
\frac{1}{2}<\gamma<1
,
fix a

(\rho,\gamma)
-irregular function
w
on an interval
[0,T]

and
s\geq 0
as in Theorem
1.4
.
Consider the modulated KdV (
1.2
) on
\mathbb{T}

and write
(
1.11
)
as

(1.52)

\mathbf{u}=u_{0}+\mathcal{I}^{X^{\text{\rm KdV}}}(\mathbf{u}),

Here,

X^{\text{\rm KdV}}

is the bilinear driver defined in (
1.12
).
The main goal is to give a precise meaning
to
the integral term
\mathcal{I}^{X^{\text{\rm KdV}}}(\mathbf{u})
.

\bullet

Sewing lemma approach:

From
Lemma
3.1
,
we have

X\in C^{\gamma}_{2,T}\mathrm{Lip}_{k}(V)
.
Thus,
if
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))

for some
0<\alpha<1
such that
\gamma+\alpha>1
,
then
we can apply the sewing lemma
(Lemma
1.16
)
as described above
to construct
the nonlinear Young integral
\mathcal{I}^{X^{\text{\rm KdV}}}(\mathbf{u})
.
From (
1.50
) with (
1.47
), we have

(1.53)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{X^{\text{\rm KdV}}}(\mathbf{u})(t)-\mathcal{I}^{X^{\text{\rm KdV}}}(\mathbf{u})(r)=X^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,r}(\mathbf{u}(r))-(\Lambda\updelta\Theta)_{t,r}

for any
0\leq r<t\leq T
,
where
\Lambda
is the sewing map.

\bullet

Normal form approach:

Let
\mathbf{u}\in C([0,\tau];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))

be the unique solution to (
1.52
)
constructed in Theorem
1.4
.
Then, by repeating the computations in (
1.28
)
over an time interval
[r,t]
,
we have

(1.54)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}^{X^{\text{\rm KdV}}}(\mathbf{u})(t)-\mathcal{I}^{X^{\text{\rm KdV}}}(\mathbf{u})(r)&=\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}(r)\\
&=X^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,r}(\mathbf{u}(r))+\int_{r}^{t}\mathcal{N}^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\mathbf{u}(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime}\end{split}

for any
0\leq r<t\leq\tau
,
where
\mathcal{N}^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,t^{\prime}}
is as in (
1.29
).
We point out that in obtaining (
1.54
),
we used the fact that
\mathbf{u}
is a solution to (
1.52
).

By comparing (
1.53
) and (
1.54
),
we obtain

(1.55)

\displaystyle(\Lambda\updelta\Theta)_{t,r}=-\int_{r}^{t}\mathcal{N}^{\text{\rm KdV}}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\mathbf{u}(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime}

for any
0\leq r<t\leq\tau
,
where
the left-hand side makes sense
only for
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,\tau];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))

with
\gamma+\alpha>1
,
whereas
the right-hand side makes sense
for
\mathbf{u}\in C([0,\tau];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))
.
Hence, we can view
the right-hand side
of (
1.55
)
as an extension of
the definition of

\Lambda\updelta\Theta
on the left-hand side
to functions of lower temporal regularity
\alpha\geq 0

Let

. We write

We use

In expressing the dependence of a function

, which is standard in probability theory and stochastic analysis.

We use

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle n\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{f}(n)|^{2}.

from

into

.
Given a multilinear operator

we use the following short-hand notation:

(2.1)

\displaystyle S(f)=S(f,\dots,f).

We recall the following basic lemma on a discrete convolution
which follows
from an elementary computation.
See, for example,

[
51
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

\displaystyle\alpha\geq\beta\geq 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\quad\alpha+\beta>1.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=n_{1}+n_{2}}\frac{1}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{\alpha}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle^{\beta-\lambda}}

for any

,
where

and

when

.

into the nearly resonant and highly non-resonant parts,
as explained in Part (i),
is the basis of (a practical application) the normal form method
as seen in
[
58
,
90
]
.
In the modulated setting,
it follows from the identity (
1.27
) with
(
1.8
)
that
the highly
non-resonant contribution
(coming from
|\Xi(\bar{n})|\gtrsim K
,
where
\Xi(\bar{n})
denotes the resonance function of a given equation)
enjoys a gain of
K^{-\rho}
,
while the nearly resonant contribution
benefits from the frequency restriction coming from

|\Xi(\bar{n})|\ll K
.
This viewpoint applies to a general modulated dispersive PDE, not restricted to one-dimensional models.
For example,
there are unconditional uniqueness results

[
71
,
3
]

for higher dimensional (unmodulated dispersive) PDEs
via the normal form method.
We expect that
our normal form approach
with the identity (
1.27
)
applies to
the modulated versions of these
higher dimensional models.

Remark A.7
.

(i)
In the unmodulated setting,
an infinite iteration of normal form reductions
was needed to prove unconditional uniqueness
of the (unmodulated) cubic NLS (
1.38
) in
H^{\frac{1}{6}}(\mathbb{T})
;
see
[
58
]
.
On the other hand, we only needed to
implement one normal form reduction
to prove Theorem
A.1
,
which can be seen as an instance of regularization by noise.

(ii)
The regularity restriction
s\geq\frac{1}{6}

appears in
(
A.18
),
which shows that if we were to stop normal form reductions
within a finite number of steps,
we would not be able to go below
the regularity threshold
s=\frac{1}{6}
.
Hence, as in the case of the unmodulated cubic NLS (
1.38
)
studied in

[
58
,
90
]
,
we expect that an infinite iteration of normal form reductions is needed
to go below
s=\frac{1}{6}
.
We, however, point out
that this comes with a twist due to
the fact that the identity (
1.26
)
is replaced by
(
1.27
) in the modulated setting.
We will address this issue in a forthcoming paper.

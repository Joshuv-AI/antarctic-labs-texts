Abstract
. We provide a general method to study representations of quivers over abstract stable homotopy theories (e.g. arbitrary rings, schemes, dg algebras, or ring spectra) in terms of Auslander-Reiten diagrams. For a finite acyclic quiver Q Q and a stable ∞ \infty -category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , we prove an abstract equivalence of the representations 𝒞 Q \mathcal{C}^{Q} with a certain mesh ∞ \infty -category 𝒞 ℤ ​ Q , mesh \mathcal{C}^{\mathbb{Z}Q,\,\mathrm{mesh}} of representations of the repetitive quiver ℤ ​ Q \mathbb{Z}Q , that we build inductively using abstract reflection functors. This allows to produce, from the symmetries of the Auslander-Reiten quiver, universal autoequivalences of representations 𝒞 Q \mathcal{C}^{Q} in any stable ∞ \infty -category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , which are the elements of the spectral Picard group of Q Q . In particular, we get abstract versions of key functors in classical representation theory —e.g. reflection functors, the Auslander-Reiten translation, the Serre functor, etc. Moreover, for representations of trees this enables us to realize the whole derived Picard group over a field as a factor of the spectral Picard group.

1.
Introduction

One of the best understood examples of triangulated categories is the derived category of representations
\mathsf{D}^{b}(kQ)
, for
Q
a quiver and
k
a field. This is largely due to Happel’s work
[
Hap87
,
Hap88
]
and his use of methods coming from representation theory —mainly
Auslander-Reiten theory

[
ARS97
]
— to find quite explicit combinatorial descriptions of such categories. Encoded in the Auslander-Reiten quiver
\Gamma(\mathsf{D}^{b}(kQ))
of the derived category there is a plethora of information. For instance, it reveals that derived equivalent quivers must have the same unoriented underlying graph. Moreover, many relevant functors can be read from this quiver —reflection functors, Serre functors, suspension, etc.— and as shown by Miyachi and Yekutieli
[
MY01
]
, in some cases one can even recover all symmetries of
\mathsf{D}^{b}(kQ)
(namely the derived Picard group) from those of
\Gamma(\mathsf{D}^{b}(kQ))
.

However, beyond the setting of representations over a field, little is known when considering more general coefficients, such as the integers or arbitrary commutative rings. Our approach to the subject is rather radical but well-founded: it has recently been observed that certain well-known symmetries of categories of representations are actually mere consequences of the stability —in the sense of homotopy— of the categories involved, and so they exist in a much broader generality, often for the corresponding representations in any stable homotopy theory. This paper seeks to contribute to the development of an
abstract representation theory
of quivers in the sense of Groth and Stovicek
[
GŠ16a
,
GŠ16b
,
GŠ18a
,
GŠ18b
]
—see also our
section

3
. In particular, we aim to introduce techniques from Auslander-Reiten theory into the study of categories of representations over abstract stable homotopy theories, ranging from derived categories of arbitrary rings, schemes or dg algebras to the homotopy category of spectra.

There are several approximations to formalizing the abstract notion of a stable homotopy theory. The most classical one of a triangulated category fails badly for our purposes: representations —i.e., functors— in a triangulated category form no longer a triangulated category, and moreover, the lack of essential functoriality properties in such categories obstructs some of the most fundamental constructions in representation theory. We choose to work within the framework of
stable
\infty
-categories

[
Lur17
]
, where a well-behaved notion of homotopy coherent representations is at our disposal. For a quiver
Q
and a stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
, one can simply consider the
\infty
-category of functors
\mathcal{C}^{Q}
which is again stable. When specialized to the derived
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}({R})
—the
\infty
-categorical enhancement of the ordinary derived category of a ring
R
—, one precisely recovers derived representations:

(1.1)

\mathcal{D}({R})^{Q}\simeq\mathcal{D}({RQ})

We should mention that similar statements can be formulated using alternatives to higher category theory. For instance, the foundational work of Groth and Stovicek is written in the language of (stable) derivators. However, the use of certain gluing operations between categories of representations, which play a crucial role in this paper, naturally led us to work in the more flexible setting of
\infty
-categories. In a similar spirit, there are already the works
[
DJW19
,
DJW21
]
.

We study abstract stable
\infty
-categories of representations via
coherent diagrams
of the shape of the
Auslander-Reiten quiver
.
More precisely, for the repetitive quiver
{\mathbb{Z}Q}
(i.e., the component of the Auslander-Reiten quiver containing the indecomposable projectives), we show that there is a natural equivalence of
\infty
-categories

(1.2)

\mathcal{C}^{Q}\xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }\mathcal{C}^{{{\mathbb{Z}Q},\,\mathrm{mesh}}}

with a certain

\infty
-category
of representations of
{\mathbb{Z}Q}
(
5.14
). Along the way, we give a new construction of abstract reflection functors —alternative to the one given in
[
DJW21
]
— via an adequate gluing of a source (
4.10
) or a sink (
4.12
) to the stable
\infty
-category of representations. When iterated, these yield the equivalence (
1.2
). Our result generalizes and refines one of the main results in
[
GŠ16a
]
, which only considers Dynkin quivers of type A.

This new perspective offers immediate advantages. While the quiver
Q
itself may possess only trivial automorphisms,
{\mathbb{Z}Q}
has a ton of non-trivial ones. This allows us to transfer these symmetries encoded in the Auslander-Reiten quiver to the
\infty
-category of representations over
Q

We thus obtain abstract versions of key functors in representation theory: reflection functors, Auslander-Reiten translations, Serre functors, etc. —indeed, we show that when specialized to

In this paper, we formulate Picard groups using a new definition of

\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma_{Q})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathsf{Pic}_{R}(Q).

Preliminaries on (stable)
\infty

2.1.
Basics on
\infty
-categories

This article freely uses the theory of
\infty
-categories
(a.k.a.
quasi-categories
), as developed by Joyal
[
Joy08a
,
Joy08b
]
and Lurie
[
Lur09
,
Lur17
,
Lur25
]
among others. Here we only enumerate the abstract basics of the general theory while fixing some notation. We refer the reader to the just cited references for the foundations; other valuable sources are
[
Cis19
]
and
[
Lan21
]
.

\infty
-categories are a framework for homotopy coherent mathematics, where sets are replaced by
\infty
-groupoids
or
spaces
:

(1)

An
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
consists of objects
x,y,z,...
and for each two of them a mapping space
\mathsf{map}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)\in\mathsf{Spc}
of morphisms composable up to homotopy. An
\infty
-groupoid is an
\infty
-category in which every morphism is invertible.

(2)

To every
\infty
-category one can associate a category
h\mathcal{C}
called its
homotopy category
that forgets the higher homotopical information. It has objects those of
\mathcal{C}
and morphisms
\mathrm{Hom}_{h\mathcal{C}}(x,y)=\pi_{0}(\mathsf{map}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y))
.

(3)

Conversely, through the
nerve
construction, every category
\mathsf{C}
can be seen as an
\infty
-category
N(\mathsf{C})
such that
h(N(\mathsf{C}))\cong\mathsf{C}
.

(4)

Given any two
\infty
-categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, functors between them form again an
\infty
-category
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
.

(5)

Most familiar notions from category theory (adjunctions, (co)limits, Kan extensions, …) extend to a homotopy coherent version in
\infty
-category theory. E.g. a final object
w\in\mathcal{C}
is one such that
\mathsf{map}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,w)
is contractible for all
x
.

(6)

There is an
\infty
-category of spaces (
\infty
-groupoids)
\mathsf{Spc}
which takes in
\infty
-category theory the role of the category of sets in classical category theory. Similarly, there is an
\infty
-category of (small)
\infty
-categories
\mathsf{Cat}_{\infty}
(c.f.
2.3
).

Getting to the concrete model that we use,
\infty
-categories live inside the category of simplicial sets
\mathsf{sSet}
: they are the simplicial sets
\mathcal{C}
in which every horn
\Lambda_{k}^{n}\to\mathcal{C}
can be extended to a simplex
\Delta^{n}\to\mathcal{C}
for
0<k<n
. It is worth noting that (by a non-trivial result of Joyal)
\infty
-groupoids in this model are precisely Kan complexes, i.e. those simplicial sets satisfying the same extension property for
0\leq k\leq n
.

2.2.
Homotopical algebra

A major source of
\infty
-categories of use comes from inverting a class of morphisms in a category (or even
\infty
-category).

We recall that the
localization
of an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
by a class of morphisms
\mathcal{W}
is an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{W}^{-1}\mathcal{C}
and a functor
\gamma:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{W}^{-1}\mathcal{C}
such that:

(1)

\gamma(w)
is invertible in
\mathcal{W}^{-1}\mathcal{C}
for any
w\in\mathcal{W}
;

(2)

for any
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}
, the functor
\gamma^{*}:\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{W}^{-1}\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\to\mathsf{Fun}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
is an equivalence, where
\mathsf{Fun}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
denotes functors sending morphisms in
\mathcal{W}
to invertible ones.

We remark that:

(1)

The
\infty
-categorical localization of (the nerve of) a 1-category is in general not a 1-category, and this is crucial.

(2)

The homotopy category of the localization
h(\mathcal{W}^{-1}\mathcal{C})
is canonically equivalent to the 1-categorical localization
(h\mathcal{W})^{-1}h\mathcal{C}
.

The localization is particularly interesting when the original category possesses some homotopical structure. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a model category (that we assume complete and cocomplete) with class of weak equivalences
\mathcal{W}
. There is
associated
an
\infty
-category

L(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{W}^{-1}N(\mathcal{M})
carrying the homotopy theory of
\mathcal{M}
. In fact, note that
h(L(\mathcal{M}))
is canonically equivalent to the ordinary homotopy category
\mathsf{Ho}(\mathcal{M})
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The following holds for any model category
\mathcal{M}:

(1)

L(\mathcal{M})
has all limits and colimits.

(2)

For any small category
I
, the canonical functor

(2.1.1)

L(\mathsf{Fun}(I,\mathcal{M}))\to\mathsf{Fun}(N(I),L(\mathcal{M}))

is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories, where
\mathsf{Fun}(I,\mathcal{M})
is endowed with the pointwise weak equivalences.

(3)

Under the equivalence (
2.1.1
), homotopy limits and homotopy colimits in
\mathcal{M}
correspond, respectively, to limits and colimits in
L(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

See
[
Cis19
, Proposition 7.7.4, Theorem 7.9.8 and Remark 7.9.10]
.
∎

Example 2.2
.

\mathsf{Spc}
is the
\infty
-category associated to the Kan-Quillen model structure on
\mathsf{sSet}
, that is, the localization of
N(\mathsf{sSet})
by the weak homotopy equivalences.

2.3.
The homotopy theory of
\infty
-categories

A crucial feature of
\infty
-category theory is that the collection of all (small)
\infty
-categories can be organized in an
\infty
-category
\mathsf{Cat}_{\infty}
with all limits and colimits. This is realized by the Joyal model structure
[
Joy08b
]
.

In the homotopy theory of
\infty
-categories it is useful to consider the
interval

J
that is the nerve of the contractible groupoid with objects
\{0,1\}
. Given maps
f,g:X\to Y
in
\mathsf{sSet}
, a
J
-homotopy
between them is a map
h:J\times X\to Y
with
h|_{\{0\}\times X}=f
and
h|_{\{1\}\times X}=g
. We denote by
[X,Y]
the set of
J
-homotopy classes of maps. A
weak categorical equivalence
is a map
f:X\to Y
in
\mathsf{sSet}
such that
f_{*}:[X,\mathcal{C}]\to[Y,\mathcal{C}]
is bijective for any
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
. A functor
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between
\infty
-categories is an
isofibration
if it is an inner fibration and if it has the right lifting property with respect to the inclusion
\{0\}\hookrightarrow J
.

With these definitions, the following can be found in
[
Cis19
, ch. 3]
:

Theorem 2.3

(Joyal)
.

There is a combinatorial model structure on
\mathsf{sSet}
such that:

(1)

Cofibrations are the monomorphisms, weak equivalences are the weak categorical equivalences and fibrations are the so called
Joyal fibrations
.

(2)

Every object is cofibrant and fibrant objects are precisely the
\infty
-categories.

(3)

Weak equivalences between
\infty
-categories are the equivalences of
\infty
-categories and fibrations between
\infty
-categories are the isofibrations.

Definition 2.4
.

\mathsf{Cat}_{\infty}
is the
\infty
-category associated to the Joyal model structure, that is, the localization of
N(\mathsf{sSet})
by the weak categorical equivalences.

Remark 2.5
.

Different but equivalent definitions of
\mathsf{Cat}_{\infty}
can be found in
[
Lur09
]
(as the coherent nerve of a simplicial category) and
[
CN22
]
(explicitly in terms of cocartesian fibrations).

.

Most of the time we can forget about size issues and think of every
\infty
-category as a (small) simplical set. However, one should note that most interesting examples do not live in

\infty
-category

\infty

3.
Abstract representation theory

In this section, we set the framework for a representation theory with coefficients in abstract stable homotopy theories.

This article is mainly concerned with representations of quivers. By a
quiver
we mean a quadruple
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1},s,t)
consisting of a set of vertices
Q_{0}
, a set of arrows
Q_{1}
, and maps
s,t:Q_{1}\to Q_{0}
corresponding to the source and target, respectively. A quiver is called
finite
if both
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
are finite,
acyclic
if it contains no oriented cycles, and a
tree
if it contains no unoriented cycles. Classically, one identifies a quiver
Q
with the small category freely generated by it, and
representations
in a category
\mathcal{C}
are nothing but functors
Q\to\mathcal{C}
. We recall that in the special case that
\mathcal{C}=\mathsf{Mod}\,R
is a category of modules over a ring, the category of representations is (equivalent to) that of modules over the
path algebra

RQ
.

Since the emergence of tilting theory
[
AHHK07
]
, representation theorists have heavily focused on understanding derived categories of representations and their exact equivalences. Originally, results were stated over a field on which they might depend, however, some of them are of such a combinatorial nature that they hold for arbitrary rings, or even abelian categories —this is for example the case of reflection functors, c.f.
section

4
. We focus on this type of results and abstract the derived category of the coefficients. Our claim is that these results are actually formal consequences of stability
[
GŠ18b
]
, and so they should hold over any stable homotopy theory.

3.1.
Homotopy coherent representations

Let
Q
be a quiver and
\mathcal{A}
an abelian category (e.g. modules or sheaves). We study derived representations by forming the derived category
\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A}^{Q})
, which is a localization of the category of complexes
\mathsf{Ch}(\mathcal{A}^{Q})
. Via the canonical equivalence
\mathsf{Ch}(\mathcal{A}^{Q})\simeq\mathsf{Ch}(\mathcal{A})^{Q}
, an object of
\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A}^{Q})
can be identified with a representation of complexes in
\mathcal{A}
. However, an important observation is that such an identification cannot be made on morphisms:

(3.0.1)

\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A}^{Q})\not\simeq\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A})^{Q}.

This is a distinction between (homotopy) coherent and incoherent diagrams of complexes, respectively. More generally, if
\mathcal{T}
is a triangulated category, then
\mathcal{T}^{Q}
is no longer triangulated. Somehow the process of forming functor categories into a triangulated category forgets about the common homotopical behavior occurring in these categories, and this needs to be fixed. We replace
\mathcal{T}
by an
\infty

\mathcal{C}
, so that
\mathcal{T}=h\mathcal{C}
, and consider (homotopy coherent) diagrams of shape
Q
in
\mathcal{C}
.

From now on, we identify a quiver
Q
with the small
\infty
-category freely generated by it, that is, the nerve of the small category freely generated by it.

Notation 3.1

Given a small

3.2.
Stably equivalent shapes

As we already mentioned, one of our main motivations comes from tilting theory and its generalizations. We say that two quivers
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are
derived equivalent
over a field
k
if there is an exact equivalence

\mathsf{D}^{b}(kQ)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{\simeq}}\mathsf{D}^{b}(kQ^{\prime})

between their derived categories. When such an equivalence is of combinatorial nature, it can often lead to derived equivalences of representations over arbitrary abelian categories —these are called
universal derived equivalences
by Ladkani
[
Lad07
,
Lad08
]
. In the same spirit, Groth and Stovicek
[
GŠ18b
]
define stably equivalent shapes in the setting of abstract representation theory.

Definition 3.14
.

Let
K
and
K^{\prime}
be two small
\infty
-categories. A
stable equivalence
between them is a natural equivalence of (ordinary) functors

\varphi:(-)^{K}\simeq(-)^{K^{\prime}}:h\mathsf{CAT}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{ex}}\to h\mathsf{CAT}_{\infty}.

This means a collection of equivalences
\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}^{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}\mathcal{C}^{K^{\prime}}
, one for each stable
\infty
-category

, such that for each exact functor
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
, the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{K^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{K^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}}

homotopy commutes. When it exists, we say
K
and
K^{\prime}
are
stably equivalent
.

Example 3.15
.

For finite acyclic quivers
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
, any equivalence
\mathsf{Sp}^{Q}\simeq\mathsf{Sp}^{Q^{\prime}}
induces a stable equivalence between the two shapes (

).

The interest in finding stably equivalent shapes is double. First and most obvious, they vastly generalize classical derived equivalences, and could provide more conceptual explanations of those. Second, any such equivalence exhibits a potentially interesting
symmetry in abstract stable homotopy theory

and the commutative square
[
GŠ18b
, sec. 10]
, or an application of the abstract representation theory of Dynkin quivers of type

to the construction of higher triangulations
[
GŠ16a
, sec. 13]
.

One could also ask for a stronger version of

\infty

Reflection functors

In this section we give a new construction of abstract BGP reflection functors, that is, an adaptation of Bernstein, Gel’fand and Ponomarev’s reflection functors

4.1.
Classical BGP reflection functors

Let
Q
be a (finite) quiver and
v\in Q_{0}
any vertex. There is a
reflected quiver

\sigma_{v}Q
which is obtained from
Q
by changing the orientation of every arrow adjacent to
v
. This construction is especially relevant when
v
is a
source
(only has outgoing arrows) or a
sink
(only has incoming arrows), and in that situation, BGP reflection functors relate the representation theory of
Q
with that of
\sigma_{v}Q
. They were originally conceived to simplify Gabriel’s proof of the classification of representation-finite hereditary algebras
[
BGP73
]
.

Let us explain the classical construction when
v\in Q_{0}
is a source. Write
Q^{\prime}=\sigma_{v}Q
and consider the categories of representations
\mathsf{rep}_{k}\,Q
and
\mathsf{rep}_{k}\,Q^{\prime}
over a field
k
. The reflection of a representation
M:Q\to\mathsf{mod}\,k
at
v
consists of a representation
S^{-}_{v}M:Q^{\prime}\to\mathsf{mod}\,k
. It is given on vertices by
(S^{-}_{v}M)_{u}=M_{u}
if
u\neq v
and

(S^{-}_{v}M)_{v}=\mathrm{coker}\left(M_{v}\to\textstyle\bigoplus\limits_{v\to w}M_{w}\right).

For an arrow
\alpha:u\to u^{\prime}
in
Q
, we let
(S^{-}_{v}M)_{\alpha}=M_{\alpha}
if
u\neq v
, and if
u=v
, we define
(S^{-}_{v}M)_{\alpha}
as the composition

M_{u^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{inc}}\textstyle\bigoplus\limits_{v\to w}M_{w}\xrightarrow{\ \ }(S^{-}_{v}M)_{v},

where the second arrow is the canonical map to the cokernel. Using the universal property of the cokernel, this easily builds into a functor

S_{v}^{-}:\mathsf{rep}_{k}\,Q\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathsf{rep}_{k}\,Q^{\prime}.

Moreover, there is a dual construction for the sink case, which applied to
v\in Q^{\prime}_{0}
provides another functor

S_{v}^{+}:\mathsf{rep}_{k}\,Q^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathsf{rep}_{k}\,Q.

It can be verified that
S_{v}^{-}
is left adjoint to
S_{v}^{+}
.

The adjunction
S_{v}^{-}:\mathsf{rep}_{k}\,Q\rightleftarrows\mathsf{rep}_{k}\,Q^{\prime}:S_{v}^{+}
closely relates the representation theories of
Q
and its reflected quiver
Q^{\prime}
. For example, it induces a bijection between iso-classes of indecomposable representations of
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
, except for the simple representation
S(v)
which is annihilated by both functors (see e.g.
[
Kra08
]
). The adjunction
S_{v}^{-}\dashv S_{v}^{+}
is
never
an equivalence of categories. However, it follows from Happel’s results
[
Hap88
]
that it induces an equivalence of derived categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}S_{v}^{-}:\mathsf{D}^{b}(kQ)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{D}^{b}(kQ^{\prime}):\mathbb{R}S_{v}^{+}.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\scriptstyle\simeq}

Moreover, later on Ladkani
[
Lad07
]
proved that the same equivalence exists for arbitrary abelian categories
\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A}^{Q})\simeq\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A}^{Q^{\prime}})
. The reason for this phenomenon is explained in the following sections. Namely, while kernel and cokernel do not form equivalences of abelian categories, fiber and cofiber do form them between stable
\infty
-categories (c.f.

5.
Coherent Auslander-Reiten diagrams

Let
k
be a field. To every
k
-linear Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt category
\mathsf{A}
there is associated an important piece of information
\Gamma(\mathsf{A})
called the Auslander-Reiten quiver. It has vertices the iso-classes of indecomposables in
\mathsf{A}
, and for
M,N\in\mathsf{ind}\,\mathsf{A}
, the number of arrows
[M]\to[N]
equals
\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Irr}(M,N)
, where
\mathrm{Irr}=\mathrm{rad}/\mathrm{rad}^{2}
denotes the space of irreducible morphisms (c.f.
[
ASS06
, ch. IV]
). This quiver provides a very detailed (but normally not complete) description of the category
\mathsf{ind}\,\mathsf{A}
.

Recall that a
translation quiver

(\Gamma,\tau)
consists of a locally finite quiver (only a finite number of adjacent arrows to any vertex)
\Gamma
together with an injective map
\tau:\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}\to\Gamma_{0}
defined on a subset
\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}\subset\Gamma_{0}
such that, for any
x\in\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}
and
y\in\Gamma_{0}
, arrows
y\to x
are in bijection with arrows
\tau x\to y
. The map
\tau
is called the
translation
of
\Gamma
, and a
polarization
of
\Gamma
is any choice of an injective map
\mu:\Gamma_{1}^{\prime}\to\Gamma_{1}
with
\Gamma_{1}^{\prime}=\{y\to x\mid x\in\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}\}
sending
y\to x
to an arrow
\tau x\to y
. A
morphism of translation quivers
is a morphism of quivers commuting with
\tau
and
\mu
. Any full subquiver of
\Gamma
consisting of a vertex
x
, its translate
\tau x
and their (common) neighbors is called a
mesh
in
\Gamma
. Finally, a translation quiver is called
stable
if
\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}=\Gamma_{0}
.

(
[
Hap88
]
)

Let

(1)

5.1.
Representations of the Auslander-Reiten quiver

Let
Q
be a finite acyclic quiver and
A=kQ
its path algebra. We explain here an intrinsic way to manipulate the Auslander-Reiten quiver
\Gamma_{Q}=\Gamma(\mathsf{D}^{b}(\mathsf{mod}\,A))
.

From now on we use derived
\infty
-categories, so we can interpret
\mathcal{D}^{b}({kQ})
as a stable
\infty
-category of representations
\mathcal{D}^{b}({k})^{Q}
. Moreover, since

\mathcal{D}^{b}({kQ})
consists of contravariant representations of the indecomposable projectives. Under this identification, the inclusion

induces a restriction functor

\mathrm{res}:\mathcal{D}^{b}({k})^{\Gamma_{Q^{\mathrm{op}}}}\xrightarrow{\ \ \hskip 8.19447pt}\mathcal{D}^{b}({k})^{Q}\simeq\mathcal{D}^{b}({kQ})

which evaluates a representation

at the indecomposable projectives, i.e.
(\mathrm{res}\,Y)_{v}=Y_{P(v)}
. Conversely, there is a way to recover the values of a representation

in terms of the
P(v)

\mathbb{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(P(v),X)\simeq X_{v}
, where
\mathbb{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(-,X):\mathcal{D}^{b}({kQ})\to\mathcal{D}^{b}({k})
is the derived functor of the internal hom-complex. There is a functor

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{b}({k})^{Q}\simeq\mathcal{D}^{b}({kQ})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\ \ \hskip 8.19447pt}\mathcal{D}^{b}({k})^{\Gamma_{Q^{\mathrm{op}}}}

\displaystyle X\quad

\displaystyle\xmapsto{\ \ \hskip 8.19447pt}\ \widetilde{X}=\mathbb{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(-,X)

which is a homotopy right-inverse of the restriction. Indeed, this functor extends a representation
X
of
Q
to a representation
\widetilde{X}
of
\Gamma_{Q^{\mathrm{op}}}
; from taking values in the indecomposable projectives to taking values in every indecomposable of
\mathcal{D}^{b}({kQ})
. We call the extended representations
\widetilde{X}=\mathbb{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(-,X)\in\mathcal{D}^{b}({k})^{\Gamma_{Q^{\mathrm{op}}}}

coherent Auslander-Reiten diagrams
. From these, one can exploit properties of the Auslander-Reiten quiver at the level of representations:

Example 5.4
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{D}^{b}({k})^{Q}\simeq\mathcal{D}^{b}({kQ})
and consider the Auslander-Reiten translation
\tau
in
\mathsf{D}^{b}(kQ)
(i.e. the derived Nakayama functor) and its inverse
\tau^{-}
. There are natural equivalences:

(\tau X)_{v}\simeq\mathbb{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(P(v),\tau X)\simeq\mathbb{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(\tau^{-}P(v),X)\simeq\widetilde{X}(\tau^{-}P(v)).

That is, one can recover the translate of
X
in
\mathsf{D}^{b}(kQ)
from the coherent Auslander-Reiten diagram
\widetilde{X}=\mathbb{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(-,X)
and the translation of
\Gamma_{Q}
.

Coherent Auslander-Reiten diagrams admit the following abstract description:

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{D}^{b}({kQ})
and write
\widetilde{X}=\mathbb{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(-,X)\in\mathcal{D}^{b}({k})^{\Gamma_{Q^{\mathrm{op}}}}

(1)

\Gamma_{Q}
starting in

), then

Follows from the fact that
\widetilde{X}=\mathbb{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(-,X):\mathcal{D}^{b}({kQ})^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{D}^{b}({k})

Let us prove (1), (2) and (3) now. The last one is clear. For (2):

Corollary 6.13

Notation 6.14

For a quiver

\mathrm{Aut}^{0}_{\mathsf{tr},\sigma}(\Gamma_{Q}^{\mathrm{irr}})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\pi_{0}(\mathsf{Aut}(\mathcal{C}^{Q})^{\simeq})

for any
\mathcal{C}
stable
\infty
-category.

Let

be a field and consider the special case

in the group homomorphism (

), that we write

For all

coincides with

on iso-classes of indecomposable objects of

fixing every component, so that we can identify

with an automorphism in

, there is an equivalence

of functors

and evaluate, we get an equivalence

7.
Relation to Picard groups

We employ the group actions constructed in the previous section to contribute to the computation of Picard groups of quivers over ring spectra —in particular, of spectral Picard groups. Our main strategy consists of reducing these computations to the case of coefficients in a field, where we can leverage the results of
[
MY01
]
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{V},\otimes,\mathbb{1})
be a monoidal
\infty
-category. An object

is called
\otimes
-invertible

such that

of

i.e. the group of iso-classes of
\otimes
-invertible objects with multiplication
\otimes
and unit
\mathbb{1}
.

For

, we write
\mathsf{Pic}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)=\mathsf{Pic}({}_{A}\mathsf{BMod}_{A}(\mathcal{C}))
. For an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring
R
, we also shorten
\mathsf{Pic}_{R}(A)=\mathsf{Pic}_{\mathsf{Mod}_{R}}(A)
and
\mathsf{Pic}_{R}(Q)=\mathsf{Pic}_{R}(RQ)

Remark 7.2
.

If
\mathcal{V}
is a monoidal
\infty
-category, then the homotopy category

inherits the structure of a monoidal (1-)category and

.

.

(2)

Proof.

Examples 7.13
.

(1)

If
R
is a non-zero connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring (e.g. the sphere spectrum
\mathbb{S}
, or the Eilenberg-Maclane spectrum of a nonzero commutative ring), then
\mathsf{Pic}_{R}(Q)
contains
\mathrm{Aut}^{0}_{\mathsf{tr},\sigma}(\Gamma_{Q}^{\mathrm{irr}})
as a semidirect factor.

(2)

Let
k
be any field. The characteristic homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\to k
and the unit morphism from the sphere spectrum
\mathbb{S}\to H\mathbb{Z}
induce homomorphisms

\mathsf{Pic}_{\mathsf{Sp}}(Q)\xrightarrow{H\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{S}}-}\mathsf{Pic}_{\mathcal{D}({\mathbb{Z}})}(Q)\xrightarrow{k\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}-}\mathsf{Pic}_{\mathcal{D}({k})}(Q).

If
Q
is a tree, then by
7.12
, this shows both the integral and spectral Picard groups containing
\mathsf{Pic}_{\mathcal{D}({k})}(Q)
as a semidirect factor.

Experience suggests that, due to its universality, the two split epimorphisms above have no kernel. Jointly with Moritz Rahn and Jan Stovicek we plan to prove:

Conjecture 7.14

If
Q

\mathsf{Pic}_{\mathsf{Sp}}(Q)\xrightarrow{H\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{S}}-}\mathsf{Pic}_{\mathcal{D}({\mathbb{Z}})}(Q)\xrightarrow{k\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}-}\mathsf{Pic}_{\mathcal{D}({k})}(Q)

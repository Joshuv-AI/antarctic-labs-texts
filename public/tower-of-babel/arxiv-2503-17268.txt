Dimensional reduction of dynamical systems on graphons
Bisna Mary Eldo, Sarbendu Rakshit, Naoki Masuda

Abstract
Dynamical systems on networks are inherently high-dimensional unless the number of nodes is extremely small. Dimension reduction methods for dynamical systems on networks aim to find a substantially lower-dimensional system that preserves key properties of the original dynamics such as bifurcation structure. A class of such methods proposed in network science research entails finding a one- (or low-) dimensional system that a particular weighted average of the state variables of all nodes in the network approximately obeys. We formulate and mathematically analyze this dimension reduction technique for dynamical systems on dense graphons, or the limiting, infinite-dimensional object of a sequence of graphs with an increasing number of nodes. We first theoretically justify the continuum limit for a nonlinear dynamical system of our interest, and the existence and uniqueness of the solution of graphon dynamical systems. We then derive the reduced one-dimensional system on graphons and prove its convergence properties. Finally, we perform numerical simulations for various graphons and dynamical system models to assess the accuracy of the one-dimensional approximation.



1
Introduction

Many complex systems are dynamical and networked in nature, with applications ranging from modeling and inference of epidemic spreading, collective oscillations and synchronization in biological systems, and cascading failures in infrastructure systems, to climate dynamics. Dynamical systems on networks are by definition high-dimensional unless there are only a small number of nodes, because each node of the network usually carries at least one state variable. A strategy to facilitate understanding such high-dimensional dynamics on networks is dimension reduction. Various dimension reduction techniques have been developed to reduce high-dimensional data in general, aiming to map the given data or system to a lower-dimensional space without losing much information
[
1
,
2
]
. Dimension reduction methods tailored for dynamical systems have also been developed. These techniques aim to approximate dynamical systems in higher-dimensional space by an alternative, more tractable lower-dimensional dynamical systems with an acceptable accuracy
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
. These methods ideally help us extract key properties of the network dynamics such as invariants, modular structure, and bifurcations.

In this article, we consider two closely related dimension reduction techniques for dynamical systems on networks proposed in network science research. One is to approximate the original dynamical system by the dynamics of a single observable that is a weighted average of the state variables of all nodes in the network, proposed by Gao, Barzel, and Barabási
[
8
]
, which we refer to as GBB reduction. The other method is a generalization of the GBB reduction, which is a type of mean-field theory, to the case of general adjacency matrices of the network
[
9
]
, which we refer to as spectral reduction (also called DART
[
10
]
). The spectral reduction uses the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the adjacency matrix to reduce the original dynamical system on networks to a lower-dimensional system in terms of weighted linear combinations of the state variable that are distinct from what the GBB reduction uses.

We mathematically study the GBB and spectral reductions on large networks using graphons. A graphon is a continuum limit of graphs (i.e., networks) as the number of nodes tends to infinity
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
. Graphons are represented by a symmetric measurable function
W:[0,1]\times[0,1]\to[0,1]
, where
W(y,y^{\prime})
dictates the probability that two nodes parameterized by
y
and
y^{\prime}
are adjacent to each other. Graphons are an emerging topic at the intersection of mathematics, statistics, network science, and some other fields. They have been used for modeling power network dynamics
[
15
]
, epidemics spreading
[
16
]
, synchronization of oscillators
[
17
,
18
]
, random walks
[
19
]
, and consensus formation
[
20
]
, for example. The continuum limit of graph sequences provides a framework for the study of dynamical systems on massive graphs, where classical methods would become computationally intractable
[
19
]
. In this article, we focus on the continuum limit of a class of nonlinear dynamical systems and its dimension reduction.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In sections
2
and
3
, we study mathematical properties of graphon dynamical systems such as the existence, uniqueness and the boundedness of the solution. We also prove the convergence of the solution for the finite network to the corresponding continuum limit solution. These parts are mainly extensions of Ref.
[
17
]
, which dealt with a different class of graphon dynamical systems.
In section
4
, we define and theoretically and numerically study the GBB and spectral reductions of graphon dynamical systems.

2
Dynamics on finite networks and graphon dynamical systems

Various complex systems in the real world can be described by dynamical systems on networks
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
. Dynamical systems on networks are necessarily high-dimensional because each node is assigned with one or more dynamical variables and these variables interact via edges of the given network. Even if the dynamics at each node is one-dimensional, the entire dynamical system on the network is
N
-dimensional, where
N
is the number of nodes in the network.

We consider the class of dynamical systems on networks given by

\dfrac{dx_{i}}{dt}=f(x_{i})+\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}A_{ij}G(x_{i},x_{j}),

(1)

where
x_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
represents the dynamical state of the
i
th node at time
t
,
f(x_{i})\in\mathbb{R}
represents the single-node dynamics,
G(x_{i},x_{j})\in\mathbb{R}
describes the interaction between the
i
th and
j
th nodes, and
A_{ij}

(\geq 0)
is the strength of the influence of node
j
on node
i
, corresponding to the weighted adjacency matrix of the network. Equation (
1
) has been used for studying dimension reduction of dynamics on networks in network science research. They are the Gao-Barzel-Barabási (GBB) reduction
[
8
]
and the spectral reduction
[
9
,
10
]
, including their various extensions
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
. Equation (
1
) is also used for studying the interplay between the structure and the dynamics of complex systems
[
30
]
, early warning signals
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
, signal propagation in complex networks
[
34
]
, interplay between network motifs and dynamical stability
[
35
]
, and information flow
[
36
]
. Motivated by these previous studies, we study the continuum limit of this family of dynamical systems.

To state graphon dynamical systems corresponding to (
1
),
we start by introducing graphons. A graphon is a continuum limit of a growing sequence of finite networks (i.e., graphs). The growth of networks means that
N
increases in sequence. In the graphon formalism, the nodes are placed in
I=[0,1]
. In intuitive terms, two nodes with latent variables
y,y^{\prime}\in I
are adjacent by an edge with probability
W(y,y^{\prime})
. Every Cauchy sequence of graphs with an increasing
N
has a limit object under the cut norm, known as graphon
[
11
,
13
,
37
,
19
]
. Precisely speaking, a graphon is a symmetric measurable function
W:[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
.
The norm on
W
is given by the usual
L^{p}
norm and is denoted by
\left\|W\right\|_{p}
. See Refs.
[
11
,
38
,
19
,
17
]
for theoretical frameworks of graphons as a limiting object of dense and sparse graph sequences.

We will prove the convergence of (
1
) and its one-dimensional approximation to the corresponding graphon dynamical systems as
N\to\infty
. To this end, we use step graphons
[
19
]
. Consider a dynamical system on a finite network given by (
1
). The step graphon is a method to formulate an arbitrary finite network by graphons. Consider a uniform partition
\mathcal{P}_{N}=\{I_{N,i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,N}
of
I
, where
I_{N,i}=\left[\frac{i-1}{N},\frac{i}{N}\right)
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, and
I_{N,N}=\left[\frac{N-1}{N},1\right]
. Each element in
\mathcal{P}_{N}
represents a node in a network having
N
nodes. Let us generate the step graphon version of the adjacency matrix
A
, and then (
1
). As in Ref.
[
19
]
, we define a map
\eta:\mathbb{M}_{N}\to\mathbb{W}
, where
\mathbb{M}_{N}
is the space of
N\times N
matrices of which all entries are in
[0,1]

[
14
,
39
,
12
]
, and
\mathbb{W}
is the space of all graphons. The map represents the step graphon and is defined by
\eta(A)(y,y^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{ij}\chi_{I_{N,i}}(y)\chi_{I_{N,j}}(y^{\prime})
, where
A
is the adjacency matrix with entries in
\left[0,1\right]
, and
\chi_{I_{N,i}}
is the indicator function, i.e.,

\chi_{I_{N,i}}(y)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }y\in I_{N,i},\\
0&\text{if }y\not\in I_{N,i}.\end{cases}

(2)

To write (
1
) in terms of the step graphon, we consider any vector
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and define a map
\tilde{\eta}
from
{\bf u}
to a piecewise constant function on
[0,1]
by

\tilde{\eta}({\bf u})(y):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}u_{i}\chi_{I_{N,i}}(y).

(3)

Using (
3
), we obtain

\tilde{\eta}(f(\boldsymbol{x}))(y)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}f(x_{i})\chi_{I_{N,i}}(y),

(4)

where
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})^{\top}
,
f(\boldsymbol{x})=\left(f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{N})\right)^{\top}
, and
⊤
represents the transposition.

For any
y\in[0,1]
, we obtain
y\in I_{N,\ell}
for a particular
\ell
. This implies that
\chi_{I_{N,i}}(y)=1
if
i=\ell
, and
\chi_{I_{N,i}}(y)=0
if
i\neq\ell
. Using this
\ell
, one can rewrite (
4
) as
\tilde{\eta}(f(\boldsymbol{x}))(y)=f(x_{\ell})
, which yields
\tilde{\eta}(f(\boldsymbol{x}))(y)=f(\tilde{\eta}(\boldsymbol{x})(y))
.
Using this relationship and the unique values of
i
and
j
satisfying
y\in I_{N,i}
and
y^{\prime}\in I_{N,j}
for any given
y,y^{\prime}\in[0,1]
, we obtain from (
1
)

\dfrac{\partial\,x(y,t)}{\partial t}=f(x(y,t))+\int_{I}\eta(A)(y,y^{\prime})\,G(x(y,t),x(y^{\prime},t))\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}.

(5)

Note that we use
x
to represent the dynamical state of the nodes in the cases of both finite discrete networks and graphons. This convention should not arise confusion because we use subscripts in the former case (e.g.,
x_{i}
) and an argument taking the value in a unit interval (e.g.,
x(y)
, where
y\in[0,1]
) in the latter case.

Now we introduce a general graphon dynamical system (see e.g. Refs.
[
40
,
41
,
19
]
). We define

\frac{\partial\,x(y,t)}{\partial t}=f(x(y,t))+\int_{0}^{1}W(y,y^{\prime})\,G(x(y,t),x(y^{\prime},t))\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime},

(6)

where
W
in (
6
) is any graphon. Equation (
6
) is different from (
5
), in which
\eta(A)
is confined to be the step graphon induced by an
N\times N
adjacency matrix.

Conversely, the discrete-network variant of the graphon dynamical system derived from (
6
) is given by
(
1
)
[
12
,
19
]
, or equivalently (
5
), where

A_{ij}=(W_{N})_{ij}\equiv N^{2}\int_{I_{N,i}\times I_{N,j}}W(y,y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\in[0,1].

(7)

We have two main mathematical goals with this article. First, we prove that the solution of the discrete dynamical system converges to the graphon dynamical system as
N\to\infty
, i.e., (
5
) combined with (
7
) converges to (
6
) as
N\to\infty
. We achieve this by mainly using the results in a previous study
[
17
]
. Second, we develop graphon versions of the GBB and spectral reduction methods, approximating the solutions of (
6
), and prove the convergence for the GBB reduction. Then, we numerically examine the accuracy of the two dimension reduction methods for graphon dynamical systems.

3
Continuum limit

In this section, we mathematically establish the graphon continuum limit of
(
1
). The formalism and results in this section are largely similar to those in Ref.
[
38
]
, which established such a limit and proved various properties of the limit. Therefore, we explicitly mention how each of our formalisms and proofs are similar to or different from theirs. In Ref.
[
38
]
, sparse networks induced by the graphon are considered. However, we focus on dense networks in this paper. This is because
f
does not generally commute with
\mathbb{E}
, the expectation with respect to the random ensemble of sparse graphs. In addition,
\mathbb{E}[A_{ij}G(x_{i},x_{j})]
, where
A_{ij}
is a random variable, and therefore
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
are as well, is not equal to
\mathbb{E}[A_{ij}]\mathbb{E}[G(x_{i},x_{j})]
in general due to correlation between
A_{ij}
and
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\}
, which may cause difficulty. We assume the following properties
[
38
]
:

Assumption 1

(Lipschitz continuity)
.

First, the self-dynamics
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz continuous function on the closed interval
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
with Lipschitz constant
L_{f}
, i.e.,
|f(x)-f(x^{\prime})|\leq L_{f}|x-x^{\prime}|
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega
.
Second, the coupling function
G:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz continuous function with Lipschitz constant
L_{G}
, i.e.,
\left|G(x_{i},x_{j})-G(x_{i}^{\prime},x_{j}^{\prime})\right|\leq L_{G}\left\|(x_{i},x_{j})-(x_{i}^{\prime},x_{j}^{\prime})\right\|_{2}
for all
(x_{i},x_{j}),~(x_{i}^{\prime},x_{j}^{\prime})\in\Omega\times\Omega
.

Assumption 2

(Positively invariant domain)
.

The dynamical system (
6
) admits an invariant set
S\subset L^{q}(I)
defined by
S=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in L^{q}(I)|x(y,t)\in\Omega~\text{for almost every}~y\in I\}
. That is, if
\boldsymbol{x}(0)\in S
, then
\boldsymbol{x}(t)\in S
for all
t\geq 0
.

Assumption 3

(W-1)
.

Let
W:I^{2}\to I
is a continuous function almost everywhere on
I^{2}
.

3.1
Existence, uniqueness, and boundedness of the solution

Theorem 4

(Existence and uniqueness of the solution)
.

Suppose that
W\in L^{p}(I^{2})
with
p\geq 2
. Then, the initial value problem (IVP) for (
6
) with initial condition
\boldsymbol{x}(0)=\mathbf{g}\in S\subset L^{q}(I),~q=\frac{p}{p-1}
has a unique solution
\boldsymbol{x}\in C^{1}(L^{q}(I);[0,T])
that satisfies
\boldsymbol{x}(\cdot,t)\in S
for all
t\geq 0
and continuously depends on
\mathbf{g}
.

This theorem is an adaptation of Theorem 3.1 in Ref.
[
38
]
; the following proof is similar to theirs. However,
in contrast to their theorem and proof, we do not assume sparsity of the network, and we consider the Lipschitz continuity in a bounded domain.

Since
f
and
G
are Lipschitz on the bounded sets
\Omega
and
\Omega\times\Omega
, respectively, we extend them to globally Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and still denote the them by
f
and
G
for the purpose of the proof. We first solve the extended system in
C(L^{q}(I);[0,\tau])
by the contraction mapping theorem. Assumption
2
implies that, if
\boldsymbol{g}\in S
, then the corresponding solution satisfies
\boldsymbol{x}(\cdot,t)\in S
for all
t\geq 0
. Therefore, the solution of the extended system coincides with the solution of the original system on
S
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
L=\max\{L_{f},L_{G}\}
and
\mathcal{M}=C(L^{q}(I;\mathbb{R});[0,\tau])
. Define
K:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
by

[K\boldsymbol{x}](t)=\mathbf{g}+\int_{0}^{t}\left[f(x(y,s))+\int_{I}W(\cdot,y^{\prime})G(x(y,s),x(y^{\prime},s))\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s.

(8)

We rewrite the IVP (
6
) as a fixed point equation for the mapping
K
as

\boldsymbol{x}=K\,\boldsymbol{x},

(9)

and show that
K
is a contraction on
\mathcal{M}
.
We know that both
I^{2}
and
I
are of finite measure, and that
W
is a measurable function from
I^{2}
to
I
. Therefore, for any
x\in L^{q}(I)
and
W\in L^{p}(I^{2})
, where
p>1
and
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
, we obtain

\left\|x\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}\leq\left\|x\right\|_{L^{p\lor q}(I)}~\mbox{and}~~\left\|W\right\|_{L^{p}(I^{2})}\leq\left\|W\right\|_{L^{p\lor q}(I^{2})},

(10)

where
p\lor q
represents the larger of
p
and
q
.
By letting
L=\max\{{L_{f},L_{G}}\}
and

\left\|K\boldsymbol{x}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}=\max\limits_{t\in[0,\tau]}\,\left\|Kx(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(I)},

(11)

we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|K\boldsymbol{x}_{1}-K\boldsymbol{x}_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{t\in[0,\tau]}\left\|Kx_{1}(t)-Kx_{2}(t)\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\limits_{t\in[0,\tau]}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\left[L\,\lvert x_{1}(\cdot,s)-x_{2}(\cdot,s)\rvert+\int_{I}W(\cdot,y^{\prime})\lvert G(x_{1}(\cdot,s),x_{1}(y^{\prime},s))-G(x_{2}(\cdot,s),x_{2}(y^{\prime},s))\rvert\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\right]\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\max\limits_{t\in[0,\tau]}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\left[\lvert x_{1}(\cdot,s)-x_{2}(\cdot,s)\rvert+\int_{I}W(\cdot,y^{\prime})\left\|\left(x_{1}(\cdot,s),x_{1}(y^{\prime},s)\right)-\left(x_{2}(\cdot,s),x_{2}(y^{\prime},s)\right)\right\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\right]\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\max\limits_{t\in[0,\tau]}\left\{\left\|x_{1}(\cdot,t)-x_{2}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}+\left\|\int_{I}W(\cdot,y^{\prime})\lvert x_{1}(\cdot,t)-x_{2}(\cdot,t)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left\|\int_{I}W(\cdot,y^{\prime})\lvert x_{1}(y^{\prime},t)-x_{2}(y^{\prime},t)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}\right\}.

(12)

We evaluate the last term on the right-hand side of (
12
) as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{I}W(\cdot,y^{\prime})\lvert x_{1}(y^{\prime},t)-x_{2}(y^{\prime},t)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\int_{I}W(\cdot,y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\,\left\|x_{1}(\cdot,t)-x_{2}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{I}W(y^{\prime},\cdot)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\,\left\|x_{1}(\cdot,t)-x_{2}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{I}W(y,\cdot)\mathrm{d}y\,\left\|x_{1}(\cdot,t)-x_{2}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\,\left\|W(y,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{p}(I)}\,\cdot\left\|x_{1}(\cdot,t)-x_{2}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\,\left\|W(y,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{p}(I)}\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}\,\cdot\left\|\,\left\|x_{1}(\cdot,t)-x_{2}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|W\right\|_{L^{p}(I^{2})}\,\cdot\left\|\boldsymbol{x}_{1}(t)-\boldsymbol{x}_{2}(t)\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{x}_{1}(t)-\boldsymbol{x}_{2}(t)\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}

(13)

Similarly, we obtain

\left\|\int_{I}W(\cdot,y^{\prime})\left|x_{1}(\cdot,t)-x_{2}(\cdot,t)\right|\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}\leq\left\|W\right\|_{L^{p}(I^{2})}\,\left\|\boldsymbol{x}_{1}(t)-\boldsymbol{x}_{2}(t)\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{x}_{1}(t)-\boldsymbol{x}_{2}(t)\right\|_{L^{q}(I)}.

(14)

By substituting (
13
) and (
14
) in (
12
), we obtain

\left\|K\boldsymbol{x}_{1}-K\boldsymbol{x}_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}\leq 3L\tau\left\|\boldsymbol{x_{1}}-\boldsymbol{x_{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}.

(15)

By setting
\tau=\dfrac{1}{2L\left(2\left\|W\right\|_{L^{p}(I^{2})}+1\right)}
, we obtain
\left\|K\boldsymbol{x}_{1}-K\boldsymbol{x}_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{x_{1}}-\boldsymbol{x_{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}
. Therefore,
K
is a contraction on
\mathcal{M}
.

Finally, to show that
K(\mathcal{M})\subset\mathcal{M}
, we define
z(y,t):=0
,
\forall(y,t)\in I\times[0,\tau]
. We obtain

\displaystyle\left\|K\boldsymbol{x}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|K\boldsymbol{x}-K\boldsymbol{z}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}+\left\|K\boldsymbol{z}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{z}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}+\left\|K\boldsymbol{z}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{x}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}+\left\|K\boldsymbol{z}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}}.

(16)

By substituting
z(y,t)=c
, where
c\in\Omega
in (
8
), we obtain

[K\boldsymbol{z}](t)=\mathbf{g}+t(f(c)+s(y)G(c,c)),

(17)

where

s(y):=\int_{0}^{1}W(y,y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}.

(18)

Because
0\leq s(y)\leq 1
, it is guaranteed that
K\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathcal{M}}
. Combination of (
16
) and
K\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathcal{M}}
implies that
K\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{M}
, i.e.,
K(\mathcal{M})\subset\mathcal{M}
.

Because
K
is contracting and
K(\mathcal{M})\subset\mathcal{M}
, by the Banach contraction mapping principle, there exists a unique solution for the IVP (
6
),
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{M}\subset C(L^{q}(I);[0,\tau])
.

Finally, since the initial condition satisfies
g(y)\in\Omega
for almost every
y\in I
, we have
\boldsymbol{g}\in S
. By Assumption
2
, the corresponding solution satisfies
\boldsymbol{x}(\cdot,t)\in S
for all
t\geq 0
. Equivalently,
x(y,t)\in\Omega
for almost every
y\in I
and all
t\geq 0
. Therefore, although the proof uses globally Lipschitz extensions of
f
and
G
, the solution remains in
S
. Hence, along this solution, the extended system coincides with the original graphon dynamical system.
∎

Remark 1
.

Let
\Omega=[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
be the bounded interval associated with the invariant domain

for the graphon dynamical system. Suppose that the initial condition satisfies

. Then, the solution obtained in Theorem

is uniformly bounded on every finite time interval. More precisely, for any

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}||x||_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq\max\{|a|,|b|\}.

In particular, the bound is independent of

, provided that the invariant domain

3.2
Convergence

In this section, we prove the convergence of the dynamical system on finite networks induced by a graphon (i.e.,
(
5
) supplied with (

)) to that on the graphon (i.e., (

\{x_{1}(t),\ldots,x_{N}(t)\}
is the solution of (

Lemma 1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{x}_{N}
and
\boldsymbol{x}
be the solution of the IVP (
5
) and (
6
) with the initial condition
\boldsymbol{x}_{N}(0)=\mathbf{g}_{N}=\left(g_{N1},\ldots,g_{NN}\right)

\boldsymbol{x}(0)=\mathbf{g}
, respectively, where
g_{Ni}=N\int_{I_{N,i}}g(y)\mathrm{d}y
and
\mathbf{g}\in L^{\infty}(I)
. We assume that
g(y)\in\Omega

4
Dimension reduction methods

In this section, we derive a one-dimensional reduced system intended to approximate the graphon dynamical system (

The mathematical results in this section establish consistency of the GBB reduction between the finite-network and graphon versions. We also bound the error between the solution of the GBB-reduced system and the corresponding observable of the full graphon dynamical system.

4.1

\displaystyle\leq\left|f\left(\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t))\right)-f\left(x_{\text{eff}}(t)\right)\right|+\left|\beta_{\text{eff}}\right|\cdot\left|G(\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t)),\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t)))-G\left(x_{\text{eff}}(t),x_{\text{eff}}(t)\right)\right|+\left|\text{Res}_{\text{GBB}}(t)\right|.

(62)

Using Assumption
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\text{RHS of }\eqref{eq:thm7-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{f}\left|\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t))-x_{\text{eff}}(t)\right|+\left|\beta_{\text{eff}}\right|L_{G}\left\|(\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t)),\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t)))-(x_{\text{eff}}(t),x_{\text{eff}}(t))\right\|_{2}+\left|\text{Res}_{\text{GBB}}(t)\right|

(63)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{f}\left|\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t))-x_{\text{eff}}(t)\right|+\sqrt{2}\left|\beta_{\text{eff}}\right|L_{G}\left|\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t))-x_{\text{eff}}(t)\right|+\left|\text{Res}_{\text{GBB}}(t)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(L_{f}+\sqrt{2}\left|\beta_{\text{eff}}\right|L_{G}\right)\left|\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t))-x_{\text{eff}}(t)\right|+\left|\text{Res}_{\text{GBB}}(t)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(L_{f}+\sqrt{2}\left|\beta_{\text{eff}}\right|L_{G}\right)\left|e(t)\right|+\left|\text{Res}_{\text{GBB}}(t)\right|.

With
K\equiv L_{f}+\sqrt{2}\left|\beta_{\text{eff}}\right|L_{G}
, we obtain

\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}e(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|\leq K\left|e(t)\right|+\left|\text{Res}_{\text{GBB}}(t)\right|.

(64)

By substituting
\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{\partial\left(\boldsymbol{x}(\cdot,t)\right)}{\partial t}\right)=\mathcal{L}(f(\boldsymbol{x}(t)))+\mathcal{L}\left(\int_{0}^{1}W(\cdot,y^{\prime})G(x(\cdot,t),x(y^{\prime},t))\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\right)
in
(
59
)
and applying Proposition

, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\text{Res}_{\text{GBB}}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathcal{L}(f(\boldsymbol{x}(t)))-f(\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t)))\right|+\left|\mathcal{L}\left(\int_{0}^{1}W(\cdot,y^{\prime})G(x(\cdot,t),x(y^{\prime},t))\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\right)-\beta_{\text{eff}}G\left(\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t)),\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t))\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(L_{f}+2s_{*}L_{G}\right)\sqrt{D_{s}(x)(t)}.

(65)

By substituting (
65
) in (
64
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}e(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq K\left|e(t)\right|+\left(L_{f}+2s_{*}L_{G}\right)\sqrt{D_{s}(x(t))}.

(66)

By applying Gr
\ddot{\text{o}}
nwall’s inequality to (

), we obtain

\left|e(t)\right|\leq e^{Kt}\left|e(0)\right|+\left(L_{f}+2s_{*}L_{G}\right)\int_{0}^{t}e^{K(t-\tau)}\sqrt{D_{s}(x(\tau))}\mathrm{d}\tau,

(67)

namely,

\left|\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t))-x_{\text{eff}}(t)\right|\leq e^{Kt}\left|\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(0))-x_{\text{eff}}(0)\right|+\left(L_{f}+2s_{*}L_{G}\right)\int_{0}^{t}e^{K(t-\tau)}\sqrt{D_{s}(x(\tau))}\mathrm{d}\tau.

(68)

Since

\left|\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t))-x_{\text{eff}}(t)\right|\leq e^{KT}\left|\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(0))-x_{\text{eff}}(0)\right|+\left(L_{f}+2s_{*}L_{G}\right)\int_{0}^{T}e^{K(T-\tau)}\sqrt{D_{s}(x(\tau))}\mathrm{d}\tau.

(69)

∎

Now, we prove that the dynamical variable for the GBB reduction for finite networks,

Theorem 8
.

Let
\boldsymbol{x}_{N}
and
\boldsymbol{x}
be the solution of the IVP (
5
) and (
6
), respectively. Then, for any fixed
T\geq 0,\,\lim_{N\to\infty}\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathcal{L}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}_{N}(t))-\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t))\right|=0
.

Proof.

Using (
39
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{L}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}_{N}(t))-\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t))\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}W_{N}(y,y^{\prime})x_{N}(y,t)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}W_{N}(y,y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}-\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}W(y,y^{\prime})x(y,t)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}W(y,y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left(W_{N}(y,y^{\prime})x_{N}(y,t)-W(y,y^{\prime})x(y,t)\right)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}W(y,y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}\right|.

(70)

We obtain

\displaystyle(\text{Numerator of \eqref{eq:L-convergence-proof-1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left(W_{N}(y,y^{\prime})x_{N}(y,t)-W_{N}(y,y^{\prime})x(y,t)+W_{N}(y,y^{\prime})x(y,t)-W(y,y^{\prime})x(y,t)\right)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\left|W_{N}(y,y^{\prime})\left(x_{N}(y,t)-x(y,t)\right)\right|\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{1}\left|x(y,t)\left(W_{N}(y,y^{\prime})-W(y,y^{\prime})\right)\right|\mathrm{d}y\right]\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left\|W_{N}(\cdot,y^{\prime})\left(x_{N}(\cdot,t)-x(\cdot,t)\right)\right\|_{L^{1}(I)}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}+\int_{0}^{1}\left\|x(\cdot,t)\left(W_{N}(\cdot,y^{\prime})-W(\cdot,y^{\prime})\right)\right\|_{L^{1}(I)}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}.

(71)

By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to (
71
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\text{Right-hand side of \eqref{eq:Lnorm-convergence-proof-1}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left\|W_{N}(\cdot,y^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\left\|x_{N}(\cdot,t)-x(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}+\int_{0}^{1}\left\|x(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\left\|W_{N}(\cdot,y^{\prime})-W(\cdot,y^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|x_{N}(\cdot,t)-x(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|W_{N}(\cdot,y^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}+\left\|x(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|W_{N}(\cdot,y^{\prime})-W(\cdot,y^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|x_{N}(\cdot,t)-x(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\left\|\left\|W_{N}(\cdot,y^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\right\|_{L^{1}(I)}+\left\|x(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\left\|\left\|W_{N}(\cdot,y^{\prime})-W(\cdot,y^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\right\|_{L^{1}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|x_{N}(\cdot,t)-x(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\left\|\left\|W_{N}(\cdot,y^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}+\left\|x(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\left\|\left\|W_{N}(\cdot,y^{\prime})-W(\cdot,y^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|x_{N}(\cdot,t)-x(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\left\|W_{N}\right\|_{L^{2}(I^{2})}+\left\|x(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\left\|W_{N}-W\right\|_{L^{2}(I^{2})}.

(72)

Because
I
is of finite measure,
\left\|x(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}\leq\left\|x(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}
holds true. By applying this inequality, Remark
1
, Theorem
5
, and the condition
\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|W_{N}-W\right\|_{L^{2}(I^{2})}=0
to (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left(W_{N}(y,y^{\prime})x_{N}(y,t)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}-W(y,y^{\prime})x(y,t)\right)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\right|=0.

(73)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathcal{L}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}_{N}(t))-\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{x}(t))\right|=0.

(74)

∎

This result shows that the GBB observable in the finite network converges to the corresponding graphon observable.

[

5
Numerical results

In this section, we numerically analyze the accuracy of the dimension reduction methods on graphon dynamical systems. We use six dynamical systems and six types of graphons for each dynamical system.
A graphon dynamical system, (
6
), is an integro-differential equation. To find their solutions numerically, we use the methods described in Ref.
[
43
]
. We first compute the integral involving the graphon using the Simpson’s rule. Once we compute the integral term, the integro-differential equation reduces to a normal ODE, which we solve by the Runge-Kutta method of degree 4.

W(y,y^{\prime})=(1-p)W_{q}(y,y^{\prime})+p\left[1-W_{q}(y,y^{\prime})\right],

(94)

where
W_{q}
represents the ring graphon given by (
93
), and
0\leq p\leq 0.5
.
We obtain a ring graphon if
p=0
and an ER graphon if
p=0.5
. We set
p=0.1
and
q=1/3.

The power-law graphon (see Fig.
1
(d)) is defined by
W(y,y^{\prime})=\min\{C(yy^{\prime})^{\nu},1\}
, where
0<C<1
and
-0.5<\nu<0
. The present power-law graphon is similar to the model proposed in Refs.
[
55
,
18
]
. The weighted degree of the node for any

is given by (

). Because

\displaystyle s(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{C^{-\frac{1}{\nu}}y^{-1}}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}+\int_{C^{-\frac{1}{\nu}}y^{-1}}^{1}C\left(yy^{\prime}\right)^{-\nu}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

(95)

Because we have assumed that

(96)

as
y\to 0

represents “in proportion to”. Because

. By substituting this in

as

and
\nu=-0.2
.

The modular graphon
[
56
]
models a network with community structure. In networks with community structure,
nodes in the same community are more likely to be adjacent to each other than nodes in different communities are. We consider a modular graphon with two modules (i.e., communities) by dividing
[0,1]

[\gamma,1]
, where
\gamma\in(0,1)

. The modular graphon (see Fig.

(e)) is defined by

W(y,y^{\prime})=\begin{cases}p_{\text{in}}&\text{if }y,y^{\prime}\in[0,\gamma)\text{ or }y,y^{\prime}\in[\gamma,1],\\
p_{\text{out}}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(99)

We set
p_{\text{in}}=0.2
,
p_{\text{out}}=0.01
, and
\gamma=1/3
.

By definition, a bipartite network consists of two subsets of nodes, and the edges exist only between two nodes in the different subsets.
The bipartite graphon
[
51
]

consists of the interval
[0,1]
partitioned into two segments,
[0,\gamma)
and
[\gamma,1]
, corresponding to the two subsets of nodes, where
\gamma\in(0,1)
.
The bipartite graphon (see Fig.

0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(100)

We set

6
Conclusions

Motivated by various studies of dynamics on large networks, we have analyzed a class of nonlinear dynamical systems on infinitely large networks and its dimension reduction using graphons. Much of sections
2
and
3
is inspired by Ref.
[
17
]
, which dealt with a different class of dynamical systems on graphons. We provided different or simpler proofs wherever appropriate and pointed out the correspondence between the Galerkin approximation and the finite-network solution. We focused on dense graphons; developing similar mathematics for sparse graphons
[
38
,
57
]
warrants future work.

Mathematically, we analyzed the accuracy of the GBB reduction by establishing an error bound. In numerical simulations, we used six dynamical system models and six types of graphons. We observed that the GBB and spectral reductions were reasonably accurate for most pairs of dynamics and graphon. The obtained accuracy for the graphon dynamics is comparable with that of these dimension reduction methods for finite networks examined in the literature
[
8
,
9
,
26
,
25
]
. We also numerically found that the performance of the GBB and spectral reductions were similar on the six graphons. This last result is somewhat unexpected because the spectral reduction uses the global information about the network (i.e., the leading eigenvalue and eigenfunction of the graphon) and therefore more sophisticated than the GBB reduction, which only uses the weighted degree of each node. These results may be because the six graphons used in this study are benign, with simple
W
functions having either high symmetry, monotonicity in terms of
y
and
y^{\prime}
, or being blockwise constant. To examine the accuracy of approximation in the case of more complicated graphons is saved for future work.

We did not attempt to mathematically show the convergence of the spectral reduction for finite networks to that for graphons. For the proof, we need the convergence of both eigenvalue and eigenvector of
A

(=W_{N})
to the eigenvalue and eigenfunction, respectively, of
W
.
The convergence of the eigenvalue is known
[
58
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, the convergence of the eigenvector to the graphon eigenfunction is generally not known. The convergence of the eigenvector to eigenfunction was shown in Theorem
3.6
in
[
59
]
. However, certain conditions such as uniform convergence of the graph limit as well as the compact convergence of the Riemann sum to their respective integral, which general graphons
W
may not satisfy, need to be met for this theorem to be applied. Justification of the spectral reduction for graphon dynamical systems warrants future work.

Appendix D
Derivation of the spectral reduction for graphon dynamical systems

We consider a perturbation of the state

from the observable
R
by setting

, where
\left|\Delta x(y,t)\right|\ll 1

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial R}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}a(y)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}x(y,t)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}a(y)f(x(y,t))\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}a(y)\,W(y,y^{\prime})\,G(x(y,t),x(y^{\prime},t))\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}y.

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}a(y)[f(R)+Jf(R)\Delta x(y,t)+\mathcal{O}\left((\Delta x(y,t))^{2}\right)]\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=f(R)+Jf(R)\int_{0}^{1}a(y)\Delta x(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{1}a(y)\mathcal{O}\left((\Delta x(y,t))^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}a(y)[x(y,t)-R]\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}a(y)x(y,t)\mathrm{d}y-\int_{0}^{1}a(y)R\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle R\left[1-\int_{0}^{1}a(y)\mathrm{d}y\right]=0.

(121)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}a(y)\,W(y,y^{\prime})\,G(R+\Delta x(y,t),R+\Delta x(y^{\prime},t))\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle G(R,R)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}a(y)\,W(y,y^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+J_{1}G(R,R)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}a(y)\,W(y,y^{\prime})\,\Delta x(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+J_{2}G(R,R)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}a(y)\,W(y,y^{\prime})\,\Delta x(y^{\prime},t)\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}a(y)\,W(y,y^{\prime})\,\left[\mathcal{O}\left((\Delta x(y,t))^{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left((\Delta x(y^{\prime},t))^{2}\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}y,

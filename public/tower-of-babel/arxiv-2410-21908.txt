Cactus scheme, catalecticant minors, and scheme theoretic equations
Jarosław Buczyński, Hanieh Keneshlou

Abstract
The r r -th cactus variety of a subvariety X X in a projective space generalizes the r r -th secant variety of X X and it is defined using linear spans of finite subschemes of X X of degree r r . One of its original purposes was to study the vanishing sets of catalecticant minors. In this article, we equip the cactus variety with a scheme structure, via “relative linear spans” of families of finite schemes over a potentially non-reduced base. In this way, we are able to study the vanishing scheme of the catalecticant minors. For a sufficiently high degree Veronese variety, we show that r r -th cactus scheme and the zero scheme of appropriate catalecticant minors agree on a dense open subset which is the complement of the ( r − 1 ) (r-1) -th cactus variety (or scheme). This article is the first part of a series. In the follow up, as an application, we can describe the singular locus of (in particular) secant varieties to high degree Veronese varieties in terms of singularities of the Hilbert scheme. We will also generalize the result to high degree Veronese reembeddings of other varieties and schemes.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, we work over an algebraically closed field
{\Bbbk}
of any characteristic.

Let
V
be a
{\Bbbk}
-vector space of dimension
(n+1)
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{\Bbbk}(V)

be the projective space with a homogeneous coordinate ring

S:={\Bbbk}[\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{n}]=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}S^{d}V^{*}
.
Here
S^{d}V^{*}
is the vector space of homogeneous degree
d
polynomials in
S
.
Let
S^{(d)}V
denote the
d
-th symmetric tensor power of
V
,
which we can consider as degree
d
homogeneous part
of the divided powers ring dual to
S
.
The main results of this article concern certain natural subschemes of the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(d)}V)
,
whose homogeneous coodrinate ring is
\bigoplus_{e=0}^{\infty}S^{e}(S^{d}V^{*})
.
In particular, we will consider homogeneous ideals in

\bigoplus_{e=0}^{\infty}S^{e}(S^{d}V^{*})
and
the vector space
S^{d}V^{*}=S^{1}(S^{d}V^{*})
plays a double role in our story: on one hand it is a degree
d
homogeneous part of
S
, and on the other hand, it is the degree
1
part of
\bigoplus_{e=0}^{\infty}S^{e}(S^{d}V^{*})
.

For any
0\leqslant i\leqslant d
the
(i,d-i)
-th catalecticant matrix is a matrix whose entries are elements of
S^{d}V^{*}
interpreted as linear forms on
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(d)}V)
.
More precisely, considering the linear embedding

S^{(d)}V\subset S^{(i)}V\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(d-i)}V\simeq\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{i}V^{*},S^{(d-i)}V)

of symmetric tensors into partially symmetric tensors, the catalecticant matrix of
F\in S^{(d)}V
is the matrix of the linear map
F\colon S^{i}V^{*}\to S^{(d-i)}V
.
Thus, such matrix determines the following map of vector bundles on
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{d}V)
:

\Upsilon^{i,d-i}\colon{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\Bbbk}(S^{(d)}V)}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{i}V^{*}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\Bbbk}(S^{(d)}V)}(1)\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(d-i)}V.

For each
i
and each positive integer
r
the
(r+1)\times(r+1)
minors of the
(i,d-i)
-th catalecticant matrix generate a homogeneous ideal
\Minors_{r}(\Upsilon^{i,d-i})
in the homogeneous coordinate ring of
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(d)}V)
.
The purpose of this article is to study the subscheme of
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(d)}V)
defined by this ideal, which we denote by
\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)
.

It is known that:

•

If
r=1
, then for any
1\leqslant i\leqslant d-1
,
the ideal
\Minors_{1}(\Upsilon^{i,d-i})
is equal to the ideal of Veronese variety

\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)

[
Puc98
]
.

•

If
r=2
and the base field
{\Bbbk}
is
{\mathbb{C}}
, then for any
2\leqslant i\leqslant d-2
,
the ideal
\Minors_{2}(\Upsilon^{i,d-i})
is the ideal of the secant variety to the Veronese variety
\sigma_{2}(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}V))

[
Rai13
]
.

In particular, in the two cases above this ideal does not depend on
i
.

•

For any
r\geqslant 1
, the
r
-th secant variety

\sigma_{r}(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V))
is contained in
\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)
.
In fact, even more, the
r
-th cactus variety

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)
is contained in
\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)
.

•

If
r\leqslant i\leqslant d-r
and
d\geqslant 2r
,
then
{\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)}_{\operatorname{red}}
,
the reduced subscheme
of
\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)
, is equal to the cactus variety

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)

(if
{\Bbbk}={\mathbb{C}}
, then see

[
BB14
, Thm 1.5]
;
for arbitrary
{\Bbbk}
, see

[
BJ17
, Thm 7.8]
).
In this case, the ideal

\Minors_{r}(\Upsilon^{i,d-i})

might potentially depend on
i
,
but the reduced vanishing set is again
the same for any
i
in this range.

The
cactus variety
mentioned in the last two items above is defined as the following closure:

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)=\overline{\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V\\
\length R\leqslant r\end{subarray}}\left\{\text{scheme theoretic linear span of }\nu_{d}(R)\right\}}.

Our goal in this article is to introduce a geometrically meaningful scheme structure on
the cactus variety and compare it to the scheme structure of

\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)
for
r\leqslant i\leqslant d-r
.

1.1
Cactus scheme and catalecticant minors

We define the cactus scheme (Definition
4.3
) using the relative linear spans (Definition
4.1
) of not necessarily flat families of subschemes of
\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)
,
and we denote the resulting scheme as

4

\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)
using spans of only flat families
(also see Definition
4.3

This latter scheme has a nice description as an image under a morphism from a projectivised vector bundle over the Hilbert scheme of
r
points on
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V
,
see Lemma
4.7
.

In the theorem below and throughout the paper, for any locally closed subschemes

and

in the same ambient scheme (for instance,

) by

we mean the difference of the schemes, that is the locally closed subscheme supported on the set

with scheme structure around

identical as the scheme structure of
X
around
x
.
In particular,
X\setminus Y
does not depend on the scheme structure of
Y
.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have the following relations between the locally closed subschemes
of
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(d)}V)
:

(i)

\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)\subset\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{r}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)\subset\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)
for any
r\geqslant 1
,
d\geqslant r
,
r-1\leqslant i\leqslant d-1
.

(ii)

If
r\geqslant 1
,
d\geqslant 2r
,
r\leqslant i\leqslant d-r
,
then

\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)\setminus\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r-1}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)=\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{r}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)\setminus\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{r-1}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)\\
=\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\setminus\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r-1}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V).

The theorem is proven in Section
6
, with its major ingredients stated as Theorems
4.9
,
5.23
, and
6.1
.
It is perhaps worth to explicitly remark about Theorem
1.1

that:

•

The nontrivial inclusions to prove are
\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{r}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)\subset\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)
in
(i)

and
\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\setminus\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r-1}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\subset\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)\setminus\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r-1}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\right)
in
(ii)
.

•

The symmetry

implies

\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)=\Upsilon^{d-i,i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)

and thus we can swap the roles of

and

in the theorem.
In particular, the conclusions of

hold also for

.
In fact, we expect that the same conclusion holds for any

and

•

As a consequence of

,
we see that the scheme structure of

1.2
Structure of
Hilbert scheme of finite Gorenstein schemes and catalecticant minors

Our second result compares the scheme structure of
\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)\setminus\Upsilon^{i,d-i}_{r-1}({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)

to the scheme structure of

.
See
[
Jel17
]

for a comprehensive survey on

,
particularly
[
Jel17

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
r\geqslant 1
,
d\geqslant 2r
,
and
r\leqslant i\leqslant d-r
.
Then there exists a vector bundle

{\mathcal{E}}
over
{{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}^{\operatorname{Gor}}{({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)}

and a closed subset

such that:

•

, where we also construct explicitly the vector bundle

.

We remark that the combinations of Theorems

and

give also the isomorphism:

2.1
Scheme inclusion criterion

By the very basic definitions in set theory,
for a fixed set
W
and two of its subsets
A,B\subset W
,
we have
A\subset B
if and only if
for all
x\in A
we have
x\in B
.
To check if
A=B
is equivalent to check if for all
w\in W
, we have
x\in A
if and only if
x\in B
.
This works well for algebraic varieties, for instance, for subvarieties of affine or projective spaces: to verify if one subvariety is contained in the the other it is enough to check the inclusion on points.
However, on the level of schemes the conditions become more interesting.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a scheme
W

has enough
{\Bbbk}
-points
if
W
is locally Noetherian and for any point
w\in W
, the closure
\overline{\left\{w\right\}}
contains a point
w^{\prime}\simeq\Spec{\Bbbk}
.

For instance, any scheme
W
of finite type over
{\Bbbk}
has enough
{\Bbbk}
-points.
If
w\in W
is a
{\Bbbk}
-point, then the spectrum
\Spec{\mathcal{O}}_{W,w}
of the local ring and its completion have enough
{\Bbbk}
-points.
On the other hand,

{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{{\Bbbk}(t)}=\Spec{\Bbbk}(t)[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]

is Noetherian, but does not have enough
{\Bbbk}
-points.
Although it is not strictly necessary for all our results to restrict to such schemes, some formulations of the statements and the arguments proving some other statements are simpler when working with schemes that have enough
{\Bbbk}
-points.
At the same time, the notion is general enough for our needs.
Indeed, Noetherian or locally Noetherian is a natural assumption, many statements in algebraic geometry
will not work without this assumption.
Projective, affine, quasiprojective, quasiaffine over an algebraically closed field
{\Bbbk}
have enough
{\Bbbk}
-points.
All these are natural assumptions,
and in fact these are all we use in the statements and proofs.
The proofs are easier, as for all local schemes with enough
{\Bbbk}
-points we always have the splitting of the structure ring:

{\Bbbk}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{W,w}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{W,w}/\mathfrak{m}={\Bbbk}
.
thus
{\mathcal{O}}_{W,w}={\Bbbk}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
(as a
{\Bbbk}
-vector space), which is heavily exploited in some arguments,
see for instance statement and proof of Proposition
2.8
or
proof of Lemma
5.11
.
The splitting fails if the base field is
{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{W,w}={\mathbb{R}}[s,t]/(s^{2}+1,t^{2})
, or if
{\Bbbk}={\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{W,w}={\mathbb{C}}(t)[s]/s^{2}
, or analogous examples.
Dealing with these local rings is not a disaster,
but it is a nuisance and adds a bit to some proof.
The following proposition shows that if a scheme has enough
{\Bbbk}
-points, then we do not have to worry about
these local rings.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose
W
is a
{\Bbbk}
-scheme which has enough
{\Bbbk}
-points,
and let
X
and
Y
be two closed subschemes of
W
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

X\subset Y
as schemes,

(ii)

for all finite (over
{\Bbbk}
) local subschemes

R\subset W
if
R\subset X
, then
R\subset Y
,

(iii)

for all finite (over
{\Bbbk}
) local Gorenstein subschemes

R\subset W
if
R\subset X
, then
R\subset Y
.

The equivalence of
(ii)
and
(iii)
can seen as the statement, that every finite local scheme is a (scheme theoretic) union of its Gorenstein subschemes.

Proof.

The implications
(i)

\Longrightarrow

(ii)

\Longrightarrow

(iii)
are immediate.
Thus, assume that
(iii)
holds.

Let
w\in X
be any point and let
x
be a
{\Bbbk}
-point
in
\overline{\left\{w\right\}}
.
Since
X
is closed
x\in X
and taking the Gorenstein scheme
R=\Spec{\Bbbk}
supported at
x
we have
x\in Y
by Condition
(iii)
.
Replacing
W
by an open neighborhood of
x

we can assume that
W
is affine.
Furthermore, we can assume that
W=\Spec{\mathcal{O}}_{W,x}
is local (and Noetherian).

Let
\mathfrak{m}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{W,x}
be the maximal ideal,

I_{X}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{W,x}
be the ideal of
X

and
I_{Y}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{W,x}
be the ideal of
Y
.
We have to show that
I_{Y}\subset I_{X}
.
Suppose on the contrary, that there exists
f\in I_{Y}
, but
f\notin I_{X}
.
Let
k
be the maximal integer such that
f\in\mathfrak{m}^{k-1}+I_{X}
(so that
f\notin\mathfrak{m}^{k}+I_{X}
).
Note that
k
is finite since
{\mathcal{O}}_{W,x}
is Noetherian and
k>0
since
f\in\mathfrak{m}^{0}+I_{X}=(1)
.

Define
I_{0}=\mathfrak{m}^{k}+I_{X}
and construct a finite sequence of ideals
I_{0}\subset I_{1}\subset\dotsb\subset I_{l}
inductively.
Note that the quotient
{\mathcal{O}}_{W,x}/I_{0}
is local and finite
{\Bbbk}
-algebra by our assumption on (local) Noetherianity of
W
and
\mathfrak{m}^{k}\subset I_{0}
.
Therefore the quotient is Artinian.
Thus also
{\mathcal{O}}_{W,x}/I_{j}
is going to be local Artinian for any
j
, since
I_{0}\subset I_{j}
.
If the socle of
{\mathcal{O}}_{W,x}/I_{j}
is
\langle f+I_{j}\rangle
, then set
l=j
and stop the construction.
If not, then there is an element
g\in{\mathcal{O}}_{W,x}\setminus I_{j}

such that
f\notin I_{j}+(g)
.
For instance,
one can pick any
g
that modulo
I_{j}
is a non-zero element of the socle which is not proportional to the class of
f
.
Define
I_{j+1}:=I_{j}+(g)
.
The construction will eventually stop because the algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{W,x}/I_{0}
is Artinian.

Then
\Spec{\mathcal{O}}_{W,x}/I_{l}
is a local Artinian Gorenstein subscheme of
X
.
By
(iii)
it is contained in
Y
, thus
I_{Y}\subset I_{l}
, a contradiction,
since
f\in I_{Y}
, but
f\notin I_{l}
.
∎

Let us remark that this criterion has also an interesting analogue in an invariant setting, when there is a suitable group action involved.
Since it is not directly required in the arguments presented in this article, we leave it out of this paper.

2.2
Finite subschemes as tensors

For every vector space
W
over
{\Bbbk}
of dimension
N+1
, we consider the underlying affine space

{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}^{N+1}
and projective space
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}^{N}
, which we denote by
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W
respectively.
Fix
Q=\Spec A
an affine scheme of finite type over
{\Bbbk}
, so that
A
is a finitely generated
{\Bbbk}
-algebra.
Our main interest is in the case where
Q
is finite and thus also
A
is a finite
{\Bbbk}
-algebra.

Pick a basis
\left\{x_{0},\dotsc,x_{N}\right\}
of
W
and let
\left\{\alpha_{0},\dotsc,\alpha_{N}\right\}
be the dual basis of
W^{*}
. Then

{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)=\Spec\Sym W^{*}=\Spec{\Bbbk}[\alpha_{0},\dotsc,\alpha_{N}],

and we think of
W^{*}=(\Sym W^{*})_{1}
as a subset of
\Sym W^{*}
. Moreover, elements of
W^{*}
, in particular
\alpha_{i}
, are functionals on
W
.
By a slight abuse of notation, if

\Theta\in\Sym W^{*}
we denote

1\otimes\Theta\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym W^{*}
also by
\Theta
and if
T\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W
then by
\Theta(T)
we mean the element of
A
obtained by evaluating the polynomial
\Theta
at
T
.

The set of
{\Bbbk}
-points of
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
is in a natural bijection with
W
.
Unless potentially confusing,
we will denote the vector in
W

and the corresponding
{\Bbbk}
-point in
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)

by the same symbol.
Now we generalize this correspondence to
A
-points.

If
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
is any morphism,
then
F^{*}\colon\Sym W^{*}\to A
is the underlying morphism of algebras.
We define the
corresponding tensor

F_{\otimes}\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W=\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}(W^{*},A)
as
F_{\otimes}:=F^{*}|_{W^{*}}
.
Explicitly in coordinates,

F_{\otimes}=F^{*}(\alpha_{0})\otimes x_{0}+\dotsb+F^{*}(\alpha_{N})\otimes x_{N}.

Conversely, given any tensor
T\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W
, we can define the
corresponding morphism
of schemes
T_{\operatorname{sch}}\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
,
such that
T_{\operatorname{sch}}^{*}\colon\Sym W^{*}\to A
is defined as
T\in\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}(W^{*},A)
on the generators
W^{*}
of
\Sym W^{*}
and then we extend the linear map to an algebra homomorphism.
In coordinates,

\text{if }T=a_{0}\otimes x_{0}+\dotsb+a_{N}\otimes x_{N}\text{ for some }a_{i}\in A,\text{ then }T_{\operatorname{sch}}^{*}(\alpha_{i})=a_{i}.

Clearly, these two operations are inverses of each other:

(F_{\otimes})_{\operatorname{sch}}=F

and
(T_{\operatorname{sch}})_{\otimes}=T
.
Moreover, they satisfy the following natural properties:

Proposition 2.3
.

In the notation of
Q,A,W
as above,
suppose
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
is a morphism of schemes.
Denote by
\overline{F}\subset Q\times_{{\Bbbk}}{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
the graph of
F
.
Then:

(i)

F
is a closed embedding if and only if
F_{\otimes}(W^{*})
generate
A
.

(ii)

If
\Theta\in\Sym W^{*}
,
then
F^{*}\Theta=\Theta(F_{\otimes})\in A
.

(iii)

The ideal of the scheme theoretic image

F(Q)
is

I=\left\{\Theta\in\Sym W^{*}\mid\Theta(F_{\otimes})=0\right\}\subset\Sym W^{*}.

(iv)

The ideal in
A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym W^{*}
of
\overline{F}
is equal to

\left\{\Theta\in A\otimes\Sym W^{*}\mid\Theta(F_{\otimes})=0\right\}\subset\Sym W^{*}.

(v)

If
Q^{\prime}=\Spec A^{\prime}
is another affine scheme of finite type and
G\colon Q^{\prime}\to Q
is a morphism, then

(F\circ G)_{\otimes}=(G^{*}\otimes\id_{W})(F_{\otimes}).

Proof.
All these items are straightforward consequences of definitions. We provide the details for reader’s convenience.

To prove
(i)

note that
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
is a closed embedding if and only if the ring map is surjective, that is
F^{*}(\Sym W^{*})=A
.
The latter equality is equivalent to the claim that for any set of generators of
{\Bbbk}
-algebra
\Sym W^{*}
, the set of images of these generators generates
A
. In particular, the embedding assumption is equivalent to
F_{\otimes}(W^{*})
generating
A
, as claimed.

For
Item
(ii)

we express
\Theta=\sum_{J}k_{J}\alpha^{J}
in coordinates
(here
J
is a multiindex,
k_{J}\in{\Bbbk}
, and only finitely many coefficients
k_{J}

are non-zero):

\Theta(F_{\otimes})=\Theta\left(\textstyle{\sum_{i=0}^{N}}F^{*}\alpha_{i}\otimes x_{i}\right)=\textstyle{\sum_{J}}k_{J}\left(\alpha^{J}(\textstyle{\sum_{i=0}^{N}}F^{*}\alpha_{i}\otimes x_{i})\right)\\
=\textstyle{\sum_{J}k_{J}}\left(\textstyle{\prod_{i\in J}}F^{*}\alpha_{i}\right)=F^{*}\Theta.

Now
(iii)
becomes a direct consequence of
(ii)
, since
I=\ker F^{*}
.

To prove

(iv)
,
let
\overline{I}

denote the ideal of the graph.
By the definition of a graph,
\overline{I}
is
generated by
\alpha_{i}-F^{*}(\alpha_{i})
.
Since
F^{*}(\alpha_{i})=\alpha_{i}(F_{\otimes})
,
the generators can be written as

\left\{\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{i}(F_{\otimes})\mid i\in\left\{0,\dotsc,N\right\}\right\}
.
Now, if
\Theta\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym W^{*}
, then using the generators we replace any variable
\alpha_{i}

by
\alpha_{i}(F_{\otimes})
,
to see that
\Theta\equiv\Theta(F_{\otimes})\mod\overline{I}
.
Since
\Theta(F_{\otimes})\in A
, and
A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym W^{*}/\overline{I}\simeq A
,
it follows that
\Theta\in\overline{I}
if and only if

\Theta\equiv 0\mod\overline{I}
if and only if
\Theta(F_{\otimes})=0
.

Finally, to see that
(v)
holds
note that

(F\circ G)_{\otimes}=\textstyle{\sum_{i=0}^{N}}(G^{*}\circ F^{*}\alpha_{i})\otimes x_{i}=\textstyle{\sum_{i=0}^{N}}(G^{*}\otimes\id_{W})(F^{*}\alpha_{i}\otimes x_{i})\\
=(G^{*}\otimes\id_{W})\left(\textstyle{\sum_{i=0}^{N}}F^{*}\alpha_{i}\otimes x_{i}\right)=(G^{*}\otimes\id_{W})(F_{\otimes}).

∎

For a while we restrict our attention to finite schemes
Q
.
We commence by presenting three simple examples.

Example 2.4
.

Suppose
Q={\Bbbk}[t]/t^{2}
.
Any map
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
is determined uniquely by two vectors
v,v^{\prime}\in W
:

F_{\otimes}=1\otimes v+t\otimes v^{\prime}
, which we will write as
v+tv^{\prime}
for brevity.
The unique point of support of the image of

F
is
v\in{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
.

F
is an embedding if and only if
v^{\prime}\neq 0
.
These two observations are generalized in
Proposition
2.8
(i)
and
(ii)
.
For two morphisms
F,G\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
with
F_{\otimes}=v+tv^{\prime}

and
G_{\otimes}=w+tw^{\prime}

their images
F(Q)
and
G(Q)
are equal if and only if
v=w
and
v^{\prime}=\lambda w^{\prime}
for some
\lambda\in{\Bbbk}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
.

Example 2.5
.

Suppose
Q_{k}={\Bbbk}[t]/t^{k}
.
Any map
F\colon Q_{k}\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
is determined uniquely by
k
vectors
v,v^{\prime},\dotsc,v^{(k-1)}\in W
:

F_{\otimes}=v+tv^{\prime}+\dotsb+t^{k-1}v^{(k-1)}
.
The unique point of support of

F
is
v\in{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
.

F
is an embedding if and only if
v^{\prime}\neq 0

(again, compare to
Proposition
2.8
).
On the other hand, if
v^{\prime}=0
,
then the map
F

factorizes through
Q_{k-1}
, that is
Q_{k}\to Q_{k-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle G}}{{\to}}{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)

with
G_{\otimes}=v+tv^{(2)}+\dotsb+t^{k-2}v^{(k-1)}
.
If
v^{\prime}\neq 0
and
F_{\otimes}=v+tv^{\prime}
(without higher order terms), then the image of
Q_{k}
is contained in the affine line
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
passing through
v
and tangent to
v^{\prime}
and
F(Q_{k})
is the unique subscheme of
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
of length
k
supported at
v
.
More generally, the linear span
of
F(Q_{k})

(the smallest linear subspace
V\subset W
such that
F(Q_{k})\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(V\right)
)
is equal to the linear span of
v,v^{\prime},\dotsc,v^{(k-1)}
.

Example 2.6
.

Let
Q=\Spec{\Bbbk}[s,t]/(s^{2},t^{2})
and suppose
W\simeq{\Bbbk}^{3}
with a basis
\left\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\right\}
.
Consider
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
such that

F_{\otimes}=sx_{1}+tx_{2}+stx_{0}
.
This
F
is an embedding of
Q
,
F(Q)
is supported at
0\in{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
,
and the ideal of
F(Q)
is:

(\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}^{2},\alpha_{2}^{2})
.

If
Q=\Spec A
is a finite scheme over
{\Bbbk}
,
then it is a finite disjoint union
of its connected components

Q=Q_{1}\sqcup\dotsb\sqcup Q_{l}
,
with each
Q_{i}=\Spec A_{i}
finite local scheme,
and
A=A_{1}\times\dotsb\times A_{l}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose
Q
is a finite scheme as above,
and
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
is a morphism.
Set
F_{i}\colon Q_{i}\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
to be the restriction
of
F
to the irreducible component
Q_{i}
.
Then

F_{\otimes}=(F_{1})_{\otimes}+\dotsb+(F_{l})_{\otimes}
.

Proof.

We have
F_{\otimes}\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W
and
as
{\Bbbk}
-vector spaces

A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W=A_{1}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W\oplus\dotsb\oplus A_{l}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W
.
Decomposing
F_{\otimes}
into direct components as above
we obtain the desired expression of
F_{\otimes}
as the sum of
(F_{i})_{\otimes}
’s.
∎

Suppose
Q=\Spec A
is a finite local scheme over
{\Bbbk}
and

\mathfrak{m}\subset A
is the maximal ideal.
Then we have a natural splitting
A={\Bbbk}\cdot 1\oplus\mathfrak{m}
as
{\Bbbk}
-vector spaces.
Thus, each tensor
T\in{}_{A}W

can be uniquely written as

T=T_{{\Bbbk}}+T_{\mathfrak{m}}
with

T_{{\Bbbk}}=(T\modulo\mathfrak{m}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W)\in(A/\mathfrak{m})\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W=W
and
T_{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathfrak{m}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W
.
As we see in Proposition
2.8
below,
T_{{\Bbbk}}
corresponds to the point of support of
T_{\operatorname{sch}}(Q)
and
T_{\mathfrak{m}}
corresponds to how
Q
is mapped into
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
up to translation by
T_{{\Bbbk}}
.
In particular,
T_{\mathfrak{m}}\modulo\mathfrak{m}^{2}
is responsible for the image of the Zariski tangent space of
Q
.

Proposition 2.8
.

With
Q
as above,
let
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
be a morphism.
Then:

(i)

The support of
F(Q)
is
(F_{\otimes})_{{\Bbbk}}
seen as a
{\Bbbk}
point in
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
.

(ii)

F
is an embedding if and only if

((F_{\otimes})_{\mathfrak{m}}\modulo\mathfrak{m}^{2})\in(\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2})\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W\simeq\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}\left((\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2})^{*},W\right)

is injective.

Proof.
Let
Q^{\prime}\simeq\Spec{\Bbbk}\subset Q

be the reduced subscheme of
Q

and let
G\colon Q^{\prime}\to Q
be the embedding morphism.
Then the first claim follows from
Proposition
2.3
(v)
.

An element of
\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}\left((\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2})^{*},W\right)
is injective if and only if the same element seen as
\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}(W^{*},\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2})
is surjective.
Since
\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}=\mathfrak{m}\otimes_{A}(A/\mathfrak{m})
,
by Nakayama’s Lemma,

(F_{\otimes})_{\mathfrak{m}}\modulo\mathfrak{m}^{2}

is a surjective element of
\Hom(W^{*},\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2})

if and only if
(F_{\otimes})_{\mathfrak{m}}(W^{*})\subset\mathfrak{m}
generate
\mathfrak{m}
as an
A
-module.
For any
\alpha\in W^{*}
, we have

(F_{\otimes})_{\mathfrak{m}}(\alpha)=(F_{\otimes}-(F_{\otimes})_{{\Bbbk}})(\alpha)=F_{\otimes}(\alpha)-\alpha((F_{\otimes})_{{\Bbbk}})
,
with the second term in
{\Bbbk}\subset A
.
Thus
(F_{\otimes})_{\mathfrak{m}}(W^{*})\subset F_{\otimes}(W^{*})+{\Bbbk}\subset A
, and vice versa,

F_{\otimes}(W^{*})\subset(F_{\otimes})_{\mathfrak{m}}(W^{*})+{\Bbbk}\subset A
.
In particular,
(F_{\otimes})_{\mathfrak{m}}(W^{*})
generates
A
if and only if
F_{\otimes}(W^{*})
does,
which is equivalent to
F
being an embedding by Proposition
2.3
(i)
.
∎

We want to analyse similarly subschemes of
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W=\Proj(\Sym W^{*})
.
Denote by
\pi\colon{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)\setminus\left\{0\right\}\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W
the standard quotient map.

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose
Q
is finite or local scheme over
{\Bbbk}
, and
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W
is any morphism.
Then there exists a morphism
\hat{F}\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)

such that
F=\pi\circ\hat{F}
.

Proof.

Since
Q
(and thus its image
F(Q)
) is finite (in particular,
0
-dimensional over
{\Bbbk}
) or local,
there exists a hyperplane

\left\{\alpha=0\right\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W

for some
\alpha\in W^{*}

such that
F(Q)\cap\left\{\alpha=0\right\}=\emptyset
.
In other words,

F(Q)\subset\left\{\alpha\neq 0\right\}\simeq{\mathbb{A}}^{N}
.
We can now embed
{\mathbb{A}}^{N}
onto a height
1

hyperplane
Z(\alpha-1)
of
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
,
and
F
factorizes through
Z(\alpha-1)

as claimed.
∎

Thus, by Lemma
2.9

and the construction of the tensor corresponding to
a morphism
Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
,
for every finite scheme
Q
and a morphism
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W

there exists a tensor
\hat{F}_{\otimes}\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W
.
Note that not all tensors in
A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W

determine a morphims
Q\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W

and the tensor is not uniquely determined.
To see this in detail,
write
Q=Q_{1}\sqcup\dotsb\sqcup Q_{l}
as a union of irreducible (and connected) components,
where
Q_{i}=\Spec A_{i}

and
A=A_{1}\times\dotsb\times A_{l}
.
Let
\mathfrak{m}_{i}\subset A
be the maximal ideal in

A
corresponding to the local ring
A_{i}
.
Also let
1_{A_{i}}\in A
denote the image of
1
from
A_{i}

in the natural vector space splitting
A=A_{1}\oplus\dotsb\oplus A_{l}
.
For each
\hat{T}\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W
we write uniquely

\hat{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}(1_{A_{i}}\otimes v_{i}+\hat{T}_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}})

with
v_{i}\in W
and
\hat{T}_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}\in(\mathfrak{m}_{i}\cap A_{i})\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W
.

Proposition 2.10
.

In the setting as above,
\hat{T}\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W
determines a morphism of schemes
T_{\operatorname{sch}}\colon Q\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W
if and only if
v_{i}\neq 0
for all
i
.

∎

The claim of the proposition is intuitive, and straightforward to prove.
We skip the details.

2.3
Gorenstein duality for submodules of free modules

In this section
A
is a
{\Bbbk}
-algebra and
W
is a finite dimensional
{\Bbbk}
-vector space.
We will consider finitely generated free modules over
A
, and to be consistent with the rest of the paper we will view them as
{}_{A}W:=A\otimes_{\Bbbk}W
for some
W
as above.
We also want to study
{}_{A}W

in a company of another free module

{}_{A}W^{*}:=A\otimes_{\Bbbk}W^{*}
arising from the dual vector space
W^{*}=\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}(W,{\Bbbk})
.
We have the natural pairing
\ev\colon{}_{A}W^{*}\times{}_{A}W\to A
: for
\phi\in{}_{A}W^{*}
and
w\in{}_{A}W
let
\ev(\phi,w):=\phi(w)
.
The map
\ev
is
A
-bilinear and
we naturally have
{}_{A}(W^{*})^{*}={}_{A}W
.

Note that
{}_{A}W^{*}
is
not
necessarily the dual module
D({}_{A}W)
to
{}_{A}W
in the sense of
[
Eis95
, §21.1]
,
as
D({}_{A}W)
is not necessarily free.
Instead, the geometric interpretation of
{}_{A}W
and
{}_{A}W^{*}
are the modules corresponding to dual (and trivial) vector bundles on
\Spec A
.
The algebraic interpretation of these “weird” duality
is that
{}_{A}W^{*}=\Hom_{A}(W,A)
,
in contrast to the dual module
D({}_{A}W)
, which is
\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}(W,{\Bbbk})
(for finite local
A
) or, more generally,

\Hom_{A}(W,\omega_{A})
.

Definition 2.11
.

M\subset{}_{A}W
is an
A
-submodule. We define the
perpendicular submodule

of
M
to be

M^{\perp}:=\left\{\phi\in{}_{A}W^{*}\mid\forall_{m\in M}\ \phi(m)=0\right\}.

This is a very natural generalization of the definition for vector spaces over a field,
yet the behavior of
\perp
might be slightly unintuitive,
as the following examples illustrate.
Note that for
A
-submodules
M
and
M^{\prime}
in
{}_{A}W
we always have:

M\subset M^{\perp\perp},\quad\text{ and if }M\subset M^{\prime}\subset{}_{A}W\text{ then }(M^{\prime})^{\perp}\subset M^{\perp}\subset{}_{A}W^{*}.

(2.12)

Example 2.13
.

Suppose
A={\Bbbk}[s,t]/(s^{2},st,t^{2})
and
W={\Bbbk}^{2}
.
Let
M=\left\langle(s,0),(0,t)\right\rangle
.
Then

M^{\perp}=\left\langle(s,0),(t,0),(0,s),(0,t)\right\rangle\subset{}_{A}W^{*}

and also

M^{\perp\perp}=\left\langle(s,0),(t,0),(0,s),(0,t)\right\rangle\subset{}_{A}W.

In particular,
M^{\perp\perp}\neq M
.

Example 2.14
.

Suppose
A={\Bbbk}[[t]]
or
A={\Bbbk}[t]
and
W={\Bbbk}
.
Let
M=(t^{k})\subset{}_{A}W\simeq A

for some
k>0
.
Then

M^{\perp}=0

and

M^{\perp\perp}={}_{A}W.

In particular,
M^{\perp\perp}\neq M
.

In our proofs in Section
5
we will however need a number of cases with
M=M^{\perp\perp}
.
Thus below we present two such desirable situations in Propositions
2.15
and
2.17
.
The first of these cases in a sense corresponds to a section of the trivial vector bundle over
\Spec Q
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Suppose
A
is a local
{\Bbbk}
-algebra with maximal ideal

\mathfrak{m}
,
and that
T\in{}_{A}W
is such that
its residue
(T\modulo\mathfrak{m})\in A/\mathfrak{m}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W\simeq W
is non-zero.
Finally, let
M=T\cdot A\subset{}_{A}W
be the submodule generated by
T
.
Then
M^{\perp\perp}=M
.

Proof.
Let
T_{{\Bbbk}}=(T\modulo\mathfrak{m})\in W
, and pick a complement of the set
\left\{T_{{\Bbbk}}\right\}
to
a basis
\left\{T_{{\Bbbk}},x_{1,{\Bbbk}},\dotsc,x_{N,{\Bbbk}}\right\}
of
W
.
Pick also lifts of
x_{i,{\Bbbk}}
to
x_{i}\in{}_{A}W
such that
(x_{i}\modulo\mathfrak{m})=x_{i,{\Bbbk}}
.
By Nakayama’s Lemma

\left\{T,x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}\right\}
minimally generates
{}_{A}W
.
Since
{}_{A}W
is a free module, any minimal set of generators
(in particular,
\left\{T,x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}\right\}
)
is a basis of

{}_{A}W
.
Let
\left\{\tau,\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{N}\right\}
be the dual basis of
{}_{A}W^{*}=\Hom({}_{A}W,A)
.
Then
M^{\perp}=A\cdot(\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{N})
and immediately,
we get that
M^{\perp\perp}=A\cdot T=M
as claimed.
∎

For the rest of this subsection we focus on finite local Gorenstein
{\Bbbk}
-algebras.
Before the next proposition we phrase a technical lemma,
which is a perhaps standard observation
for which we have not found any reference.

Lemma 2.16
.

Suppose
A
is a finite local Gorenstein
{\Bbbk}
-algebra with a socle
\mathfrak{s}
,
and
\left\{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{r}\right\}
is a finite collection of elements of
A
, at least one of which is non-zero.
Then there exists an element
b\in A

such that

\left\{ba_{1},\dotsc,ba_{r}\right\}\subset\mathfrak{s}
and at least one of
ba_{i}
is non-zero.

Proof.
Denote by
\mathfrak{m}
the maximal ideal of
A
.
Let
d
be the socle degree of
A
,
so that
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{m}^{d}
.
Suppose for some
k
we have
a_{i}\in\mathfrak{m}^{k}

for all
i
, but
a_{i_{0}}\notin\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}

for at least one of the indices
i_{0}
.
If
k=d
, then take
b_{d}=b=1\in A
, and we are done.

So suppose
k<d
. Note that
\mathfrak{m}\cdot a_{i_{0}}
is not identically
0
, as otherwise,
a_{i_{0}}
is annihilated by the maximal ideal, that
a_{i_{0}}\in\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{m}^{d}
, a contradiction with
a_{i_{0}}\notin\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}
.
Pick
b_{k}\in\mathfrak{m}

such that
b_{k}a_{i_{0}}\neq 0
.
Then,
\left\{b_{k}a_{1},\dotsc,b_{k}a_{r}\right\}\subset\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}
and it contains a nonzero element, thus we may start over with a higher value of
k
,
and eventually take
b
equal to the product of all
b_{k}
that appeared in the construction.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the second important case when
M=M^{\perp\perp}
.

Proposition 2.17
.

Suppose
A
is a finite local Gorenstein

{\Bbbk}
-algebra.
Then for any finite-dimensional vector space
W
and an
A
-submodule
M\subset{}_{A}W

we have:

(i)

\Hom_{A}(M,A)={}_{A}W^{*}/M^{\perp}
, and

(ii)

M^{\perp\perp}=M
.

Example
2.14
illustrates that if we skip the finiteness assumption on the algebra
A
, the conclusions of the proposition fail to hold, even if
A
is Gorenstein (or even regular) and local. Moreover, for finite algebras we cannot skip the Gorenstein assuption, see Example
2.13
. On the other hand, the conclusions hold for finite Gorenstein algebras
A
, but we do not need the non-local case, and the proof is slightly simpler for local
A
.

Proof.
By definition of Artinian Gorenstein ring, there exists a (nonunique) isomorphism of
A
-modules
A
and
\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}(A,{\Bbbk})
.
Pick any such isomorphism
A\to\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}(A,{\Bbbk})
and denote by
s^{*}\colon A\to{\Bbbk}
the image of
1\in A
under this isomorphism.
Then, for any
a\in A
the image of
a

under the isomorphism is the map taking

b\mapsto s^{*}(a\cdot b)
.
Note that
s^{*}
takes a non-zero value on any non-zero element of the socle of
A
.

Consider the sequence:

0\to M^{\perp}\to{}_{A}W^{*}\to\Hom_{A}(M,A)\to 0,

where
{}_{A}W^{*}\simeq\Hom_{A}({}_{A}W,A)
(using the isomorphism
A\to\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}(A,{\Bbbk})
as above) and the second map is the restriction map.
We claim that the sequence is exact, which proves the first claim.
The first non-trivial map in the sequence is injective by definition. The second one is surjective by
[
Eis95
, Prop. 21.5(a,b)]
.
Thus, it remains to verify
that for any
\phi\in{}_{A}W^{*}
,
its image in
\Hom_{A}(M,A)
is
0
if and only if
\phi\in M^{\perp}
.
Explicitly,
\phi
is mapped to a morphism that takes
m\to s^{*}(\phi(m))
.
Thus, if
\phi\in M^{\perp}
, then
\phi(m)=0
for all
m\in M
and consequently
\phi
is mapped to
0
.
On the other hand, if
\phi\notin M^{\perp}
, then take
m\in M
such that
\phi(m)\neq 0
.
Pick any
a\in A
such that
a\cdot\phi(m)
is non-zero and contained in the socle of
A
, by Lemma
2.16
.
Then,
a\cdot m\in M
and

s^{*}(\phi(a\cdot m))=s^{*}(a\cdot\phi(m))\neq 0,

Thus,
\phi
is mapped to a non-zero element of
\Hom_{A}(M,A)
, completing the proof of the exactness of the above short sequence.

Note that
\Hom_{A}(M,A)\simeq\Hom_{A}(M,\omega_{A})\simeq\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}(M,{\Bbbk})
by
[
Eis95
, §21.1]
.
Thus
\dim_{{\Bbbk}}\Hom_{A}(M,A)=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}M
and by using the exact sequence twice (for
M
and
M^{\perp}
) it follows that
\dim_{{\Bbbk}}M^{\perp\perp}=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}M
.
Since
M\subset M^{\perp\perp}
and both
W
and
A
are finitely dimensional over
{\Bbbk}
, we must have
M^{\perp\perp}=M
as claimed
∎

In the remainder of this subsection we prove three lemmas
about finite local Gorenstein algebras and finitely generated modules over such rings. These lemmas are important ingredients of the main proofs, but also potentially are of independent interest.
The first lemma is a criterion for a module over such ring to be free (or, equivalently, flat).
The second lemma is also a freeness criterion, but for submodules of a free module.
The setting for the final lemma is more specific, restricted to
A={\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2})
.

Lemma 2.18
.

Suppose
A
is a finite local Gorenstein
{\Bbbk}
-algebra.
Denote by
\mathfrak{m}
the maximal ideal of
A
and by

s
any nonzero element of the (
1
-dimensional) socle of
A
.
Let
M
be a finitely generated
A
-module.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

M
is a free
A
-module,

(ii)

the coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{M}}
on
\Spec A
corresponding to
M
is locally free,

(iii)

\dim_{\Bbbk}(s\cdot M)=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(M/(\mathfrak{m}\cdot M))
.

Proof.
The equivalence of
(i)
and
(ii)

is clear, as
\Spec A
consists of only one point.
Pick a basis of
M/(\mathfrak{m}\cdot M)
together with its lift
\left\{m_{1},\dotsc,m_{r}\right\}
to
M
.
By Nakayama’s lemma
\left\{m_{1},\dotsc,m_{r}\right\}
minimally generate
M
.
Then
\left\{s\cdot m_{1},\dotsc,s\cdot m_{r}\right\}

{\Bbbk}
-linearly spans
s\cdot M
, hence we always have
\dim_{\Bbbk}(s\cdot M)\leqslant\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(M/(\mathfrak{m}\cdot M))
.

Now assume
M
is free as in
(i)
.
Then,

M=A\cdot m_{1}\oplus\dotsb\oplus A\cdot m_{r}

and thus
s\cdot M={\Bbbk}\cdot m_{1}\oplus\dotsb\oplus{\Bbbk}\cdot m_{r}
as claimed.
Conversely, suppose
(i)
fails to hold, that is there is a non-trivial
A
-relation among
\left\{m_{1},\dotsc,m_{r}\right\}
,
say:

a_{1}\cdot m_{1}+\dotsb+a_{r}\cdot m_{r}=0,

with
a_{i}\in A
.
By Lemma
2.16
, for some
b\in A
we get
ba_{i}=c_{i}s
with at least one
c_{i}
non-zero.
Therefore,

0=ba_{1}\cdot m_{1}+\dotsb+ba_{r}\cdot m_{r}=c_{1}(s\cdot m_{1})+\dotsb+c_{r}(s\cdot m_{r}),

obtaining a non-trivial relation between the generators
s\cdot m_{1},\dotsc,s\cdot m_{r}

of
s\cdot M
.
Hence
\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(s\cdot M)<r=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(M/(\mathfrak{m}\cdot M))
and
(iii)

fails to hold, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 2.19
.

Suppose
A
is a finite local Gorenstein

{\Bbbk}
-algebra.
Denote by
\mathfrak{m}
the maximal ideal of
A
and by

s
any nonzero element of the socle of
A
.
Let
M\subset{}_{A}W
be an
A
-submodule.
Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

M
is a free
A
-module,

(ii)

\dim_{\Bbbk}(M\cap s\cdot{}_{A}W)=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}\left(M/(\mathfrak{m}\cdot{}_{A}W\cap M)\right)
,

(iii)

s\cdot M=(M\cap s\cdot{}_{A}W)
.

Proof.
Consider
{}_{A}W/(\mathfrak{m}\cdot{}_{A}W)

and the image of
M\to{}_{A}W/(\mathfrak{m}\cdot{}_{A}W)
.
Pick a basis of the image
\overline{m}_{1},\dotsc,\overline{m}_{r}
, a complement
\overline{w}_{r+1},\dotsc,\overline{w}_{N}

of this independent set
to a basis of

{}_{A}W/(\mathfrak{m}\cdot{}_{A}W)
,
and
their lifts
\left\{m_{1},\dotsc,m_{r}\right\}\subset M

and
\left\{w_{r+1},\dotsc,w_{N}\right\}\subset{}_{A}W
.
Note that
M=A\cdot(m_{1},\dotsc,m_{r})+M^{\prime}
, for some
A
-submodule
M^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{m}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\left\langle w_{r+1},\dotsc,w_{N}\right\rangle
.
It follows that

\left\{s\cdot m_{1},\dotsc,s\cdot m_{r}\right\}

is a
{\Bbbk}
-basis of
s\cdot M
,
in particular,

\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(s\cdot M)=r=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(M/(\mathfrak{m}\cdot{}_{A}W\cap M)
.
Since
s\cdot M\subset(M\cap s\cdot{}_{A}W)

we always have
\dim_{\Bbbk}(M\cap s\cdot{}_{A}W)\geqslant\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(M/(\mathfrak{m}\cdot{}_{A}W\cap M)
and thus
(ii)
is equivalent to
(iii)
.

On the other hand, item
(i)

is equivalent to
M^{\prime}=0
by Lemma
2.18
.
Thus, if
(i)
holds, then
(iii)
holds.

Now assume
(iii)
holds.
If
M^{\prime}\neq 0
, then by Lemma
2.16
applied to the
w_{i}
-coefficients of a non-zero element of
M^{\prime}
, we get a non-zero element of
(M^{\prime}\cap s\cdot{}_{A}W)
, which is not in
s\cdot M
, a contradiction.
Thus,
M^{\prime}=0
and
(i)
holds
as claimed.
∎

Now assume
A={\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2})
. Considering the natural embedding
{\Bbbk}\subset A
,
for an element
\Phi\in{}_{A}W={\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2})\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}W
we can write it (uniquely) as

\Phi=\varphi+t\varphi^{\prime}
with
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\in W
and define
\partial\Phi:=\varphi^{\prime}
.
If
M\subset{}_{A}W
is a submodule,
then we define the following linear subspace:

\partial M:=\left\{\partial\Phi\mid\Phi\in M\right\}\subset W.

Lemma 2.20
.

With
A={\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2})
and notation as above,
for any
A
-submodule
M\subset{}_{A}W

we have the following properties of
\partial M
:

(i)

M/(M\cap tW)\subset\partial M
,

(ii)

Considering
W\subset{}_{A}W
induced by
{\Bbbk}\subset A
we have

\dim_{{\Bbbk}}M=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}\partial M+\dim_{{\Bbbk}}M\cap W
; equivalently,

\codim_{{\Bbbk}}(M\subset{}_{A}W)=\codim_{{\Bbbk}}(\partial M\subset W)+\codim_{{\Bbbk}}(M\cap W\subset W)
.

Proof.
If
\varphi\in M/(M\cap tW)
,
then
\varphi+t\varphi^{\prime}\in M
for some
\varphi^{\prime}\in W

thus
t\cdot(\varphi+t\varphi^{\prime})=t\varphi\in M
and
\varphi=\partial(t\varphi)\in\partial M
, proving
(i)
.

To prove
(ii)
consider the surjective
{\Bbbk}
-linear map

\partial\colon M\to\partial M
, taking
\Phi
to
\partial\Phi
.
The kernel of this map is precisely
M\cap W
,
which proves the desired equality of dimensions.
The equality of codimensions is then straightforward, as
\dim_{{\Bbbk}}({}_{A}W)=2\dim_{{\Bbbk}}W
.
∎

2.4
Relative Macaulay bound and Gotzmann’s persistence

Macaulay bound and Gotzmann’s persistence theorems are standard tools in commutative algebra
and they govern the numerical behavior of
homogeneous ideals in a standard graded polynomial rings.
For a reference, we state in Proposition
2.23

the special cases of these theorems that we will need.
The goal of this section is to conclude some analogous consequences in a simplified relative case,
see Proposition
2.24
. We restrict our attention to the generality required in this article.

Suppose
Q=\Spec A
is a finite local Gorenstein scheme with the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset A
and the socle
\mathfrak{s}\subset A
, which is
1
-dimensional. Assume further that
S
is a
{\Bbbk}
-algebra. The main cases of interest are
S=\Sym V^{*}
for a vector space
V
over
{\Bbbk}
, and then we will often consider the standard grading on
S
and
{}_{A}S:=A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S
: elements of
S^{d}V^{*}
have degree
d
and
A
has degree
0
. However, this assumption is not required in the first two lemmas.

We will consider families of ideals in
S
parameterized by
Q
.
That is, let
I\subset A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S
be an ideal, and construct two
ideals in
S
:

•

I_{{\Bbbk}}=I\modulo\mathfrak{m}\cdot S
(this is the ideal of the special fibre),

•

I_{\mathfrak{s}}=I\cap\mathfrak{s}\cdot S\subset\mathfrak{s}\cdot A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S\simeq S
(we call this one the
ideal of socle fibre
of
I
).

Note that seemingly the latter ideal
I_{\mathfrak{s}}

depends on the choice of isomorphism of
S
modules

\mathfrak{s}\cdot A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S\simeq S
,
but this choice is limited to a multiplication
by an invertible scalar in
{\Bbbk}\simeq\mathfrak{s}
.
Thus, the resulting ideal is in fact uniquely determined.

It is easy to verify that

I_{{\Bbbk}}\subset I_{\mathfrak{s}}
.
Moreover, as illustrated in the following lemma, these two ideals can be used to reduce a general case to the case of the base
{\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2})
, which we will exploit in the proof of Proposition
2.24
.

Lemma 2.21
.

Suppose
S
,
A
,
\mathfrak{s}\subset A
and
I\subset{}_{A}S
are as above and let
A^{\prime}={\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2})
.
Fix any non-zero
s\in\mathfrak{s}
,
and define
\xi\colon A^{\prime}\to A
by
t\mapsto s
.
Denote by the same letter
\xi\colon{}_{A^{\prime}}S\to{}_{A}S
the induced map
\xi\otimes\id_{S}
and let
I^{\prime}=\xi^{-1}(I)
.
Then,

(i)

I^{\prime}_{{\Bbbk}}=I_{{\Bbbk}}
and
I^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}=I_{\mathfrak{s}}
, where
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}=(t)\subset A^{\prime}
is the socle of
A^{\prime}
,
and

(ii)

if
\mathfrak{q}\subset S
is any ideal
and
(I:\mathfrak{q}\cdot{}_{A}S)=I
,
then
(I^{\prime}:\mathfrak{q}\cdot{}_{A^{\prime}}S)=I^{\prime}
.

Proof.
The first item is immediate from the definitions.
For the second item, let

\Phi\in(I^{\prime}:\mathfrak{q}\cdot{}_{A^{\prime}}S)
, so that

\Phi\cdot\mathfrak{q}\subset I^{\prime}
.
Then,
\xi(\Phi\cdot\mathfrak{q})=\xi(\Phi)\cdot\mathfrak{q}\cdot{}_{A}S\subset I
, hence
\xi(\Phi)\in I
,
and by definition of
I^{\prime}
, we must have
\Phi\in I^{\prime}
as claimed.
∎

We also need another lemma about saturated ideals.

Lemma 2.22
.

Suppose
A
,
S
,
{}_{A}S
,
\mathfrak{s}
are as above.
Let
\mathfrak{q}\subset S
be an ideal.
If
I\subset{}_{A}S

is an ideal such that
I=(I:\mathfrak{q}\cdot{}_{A}S)
,
then also
I_{\mathfrak{s}}=(I_{\mathfrak{s}}\colon\mathfrak{q})
.

Proof.

Suppose
\Phi\in(I_{\mathfrak{s}}\colon\mathfrak{q})
,
then
\Phi\cdot\mathfrak{q}\subset I_{\mathfrak{s}}
.
Thus,
\Phi\cdot\mathfrak{s}\cdot\mathfrak{q}\subset I
and therefore
\Phi\cdot\mathfrak{s}\subset I
and
\Phi\in I_{\mathfrak{s}}
, showing that
I_{\mathfrak{s}}
is saturated with respect to
\mathfrak{q}
, as claimed.
∎

In the proposition below and throughout the article we will denote by
\mathfrak{n}\subset A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
the homogeneous ideal generated by
V^{*}
.
Moreover, for an ideal
I
in
\Sym V^{*}
or in
A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
we will write
I^{\sat}
to be the saturation with respect to
\mathfrak{n}
, that is

I^{\sat}:=(I:\mathfrak{n}^{\infty})
.

Proposition 2.23
.

Suppose
I\subset\Sym V^{*}
is a homogeneous ideal
with respect to the standard grading and
let
S=\Sym V^{*}/I
be the graded quotient algebra.
Suppose
i
and
r
are two non-negative integers.
Then:

(i)

(Macaulay’s bound)
If
i\geq r
and
\dim S_{i}\leqslant r
,
then

•

\dim S_{k+1}\leq\dim S_{k}
, and for all
k\geqslant i
, and

•

\dim S_{k}\geqslant\dim S_{i}
for all
r-1\leqslant k\leqslant i
.

(ii)

If
i\geqslant r
,
\dim S_{i}=r
and
I
is generated in degrees at most
i
,
then for all
k\geqslant i
we have:

•

(Gotzmann’s persistence)
\dim S_{k}=r
,

•

(saturation)
I^{\sat}_{k}=I_{k}
,

•

(weak Lefschetz for saturation)
If
I=I^{\sat}
, then multiplication by a general linear form
\alpha\in V^{*}
determines an invertible
{\Bbbk}
-linear map

S_{l}\to S_{l+1}
for any
l\geq r-1
,

•

(weak Lefschetz property) multiplication by a general linear form
\alpha\in V^{*}
determines an invertible
{\Bbbk}
-linear map

S_{k}\to S_{k+1}
.

∎

For more general statements and proofs of Macaulay’s bound and Gotzmann’s persistence, see for instance
[
Gre98
, Thms 3.3, 3.8]
.
The claim about saturation follows from applying
Macaulay’s bound to
I^{\sat}
and using
I\subset I^{\sat}
with equality in very large degrees.
The injectivity in weak Lefschetz properties follows from the saturation property and finiteness of primary decomposition. The surjectivity follows from the dimension count and Macaulay’s bound.

We have the following generalizations and consequences
of Macaulay’s bound and Gotzmann’s Persitence theorems.

Proposition 2.24
.

Fix a positive integer
r
and two degrees
j>i\geqslant r
.
Suppose
I\subset{}_{A}S
is a homogeneous ideal such that
both
({}_{A}S/I)_{i}
and
({}_{A}S/I)_{j}
are flat (hence free)
A
-modules of rank
r
, and let
J=(I)_{\leqslant i}
.
Then,

(i)

({}_{A}S/I)_{k}
is flat of rank
r
for all
i<k<j
and
I
has no minimal generators in degrees
i+1,i+2,\dotsc,j
.

(ii)

({}_{A}S/J)_{k}=({}_{A}S/J^{\sat})_{k}
and they are both flat of rank
r
in all degrees
k\geqslant i
.

(iii)

({}_{A}S/J^{\sat})_{k}
is flat of rank
r
in all degrees
k\geqslant r-1
.

Proof.
Both algebras
S/I_{{\Bbbk}}
and
S/I_{\mathfrak{s}}
satisfy

\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/I_{\bullet})_{k}\begin{cases}\geqslant r&\text{for }r-1\leqslant k\leqslant j\\
\leqslant r&\text{for }i\leqslant k\end{cases}\quad\text{ with }\bullet\in\left\{{\Bbbk},\mathfrak{s}\right\}

by the Macaulay bound in Proposition
2.23
.
In particular, for
i\leqslant k\leqslant j
we have

\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/I_{{\Bbbk}})_{k}=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/I_{\mathfrak{s}})_{k}=r
.
Thus, the flatness of
I
in degrees between
i
and
j

follows from Lemma
2.19
.

Similarly, both algebras
S/J_{{\Bbbk}}
and
S/J_{\mathfrak{s}}
satisfy

\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/J_{\bullet})_{k}\begin{cases}\geqslant r&\text{for }r-1\leqslant k\leqslant i-1\\
=r&\text{for }i\leqslant k\end{cases}.

by Macaulay bound and Gotzmann’s persistence of Proposition
2.23
.
As above, this proves the flatness of
J

in degrees at least
i
.
In degrees
k
between
i
and
j
both
I_{k}
and
J_{k}
are
A
-modules of the same
{\Bbbk}
-dimension
and with
J\subset I
.
Thus,
I_{k}=J_{k}
and therefore
I
has no generators
in these degrees, concluding the proof of
(i)
.

To show the remaining claims, by Lemma
2.21
it is enough to assume that
A={\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2})
.
Since
J\subset J^{\sat}
we must have

\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/(J^{\sat})_{\bullet})_{k}\leqslant\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/J_{\bullet})_{k}

By Lemma
2.22

the ideal
(J^{\sat})_{\mathfrak{s}}
is saturated. As
\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/(J^{\sat})_{\mathfrak{s}})_{k}=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/(J)_{\mathfrak{s}})_{k}=r
for
k\gg 0
and the Hilbert function of a saturated ideal is non-decreasing, we obtain

\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S/(J^{\sat})_{\mathfrak{s}})_{k}=r\text{ for }k\geqslant r-1.

We claim that
((J^{\sat})_{\mathfrak{s}})_{k}=((J^{\sat})_{{\Bbbk}})_{k}
for all
k\geq r-1
.
Indeed, for some
k_{0}
(initially equal to
i
) this is true for all
k\geq k_{0}
, and we always have
((J^{\sat})_{\mathfrak{s}})_{k}\supset((J^{\sat})_{{\Bbbk}})_{k}
.
We argue by a downward induction on
k_{0}
.
If
k_{0}=r-1
, then we are done.
If
k_{0}\geqslant r
,
then pick a general
\alpha\in V^{*}
.
Suppose
\Phi\in S_{k_{0}-1}
is such that

\alpha\Phi\in((J^{\sat})_{{\Bbbk}})_{k_{0}}
.
Thus,

\alpha\Phi+t\xi\in(J^{\sat})_{k_{0}}

for some
\xi\in S_{k_{0}}
.
The multiplication by
\alpha
determines
an isomorphism

\left(S/(J^{\sat})_{\mathfrak{s}}\right)_{k_{0}-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot\alpha}}{{\to}}\left(S/(J^{\sat})_{\mathfrak{s}}\right)_{k_{0}}
by the weak Lefschetz property in Proposition
2.23
.
Thus, let
\Psi\in S_{k_{0}-1}

be such that
\alpha\Psi=\xi\modulo((J^{\sat})_{\mathfrak{s}})_{k_{0}}
.
Therefore, we have

\alpha\Phi+t\alpha\Psi\in(J^{\sat})_{k_{0}}

and by generality of
\alpha
and saturatedness of
J^{\sat}
we must have

\Phi+t\Psi\in(J^{\sat})_{k_{0}-1}
.
Thus,
\Phi\in((J^{\sat})_{{\Bbbk}})_{k_{0}-1}
,
and we may proceed by induction, proving the claim.

∎

Fix a base scheme

over
{\Bbbk}

In this section, our main interest is in the case where

Definition 3.1

3.1

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose
Q
is a base scheme and
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{}_{Q}{\mathcal{S}}

be a homogeneous ideal sheaf.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{I}}
is a linear ideal sheaf,

(ii)

for any open affine subset
U\subset Q
,
the
{\mathcal{I}}(U)\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(U)[\alpha_{0},\dotsc,\alpha_{N}]

is generated in degrees
0
and
1
.

(iii)

for any

{\mathcal{I}}_{q}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{Q,q}[\alpha_{0},\dotsc,\alpha_{N}]

is generated in degrees
0
and
1
.

Proof.

These equivalences are consequences of the triviality of the sheaf of algebras
{}_{Q}{\mathcal{S}}
over
Q
, and also of its grading.

In detail,

(i)

means that there exists an open cover of

such that for each open set
U
in this
cover the ideal
{\mathcal{I}}(U)
is generated in degrees
0
and
1
.
In particular, the implication

(ii)
\Longrightarrow

(i)

is clear.
Also
(i)
\iff
(iii)

holds since each grading of
{\mathcal{I}}
is a coherent sheaf and surjectivity of sheaf map
{\mathcal{I}}_{1}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}}{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}={\mathcal{I}}_{1}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{d-1}W^{*}\to{\mathcal{I}}_{d}
is equivalent to surjectivity of its localisations at each point
[
Sta24
,
Lem. 01AG
]
.

The final implication is a “
[
Har77
]

(i)

holds, and that

is an open affine subset.
It is covered by basic open affine subsets

, such that

is generated in degrees

and
1
.
Fix

.
For any

(after clearing the denominators) we can express

for some non-negative integer

Since

there is a finite combination of the

.
∎

Conjecture 3.6

(Linear ideals are saturated)
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{}_{Q}{\mathcal{S}}
is a linear ideal sheaf.
Let

•

The conjecture is true in the flat case (if

•

Definition 3.7

Corollary 3.25

Their homogeneous ideals are, respectively:

\displaystyle I^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{00}-\alpha_{01},\alpha_{01}-\alpha_{11},\alpha_{02},\alpha_{03},\alpha_{22},\alpha_{23},\alpha_{33}),

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{00},\alpha_{01},\alpha_{02},\alpha_{03},\alpha_{22},\alpha_{23},\alpha_{33})

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{01},\alpha_{11},\alpha_{02}-s\alpha_{00},\alpha_{03}-t\alpha_{00},\alpha_{22}-s^{2}\alpha_{00},\alpha_{23}-st\alpha_{00},\alpha_{33}-t^{2}\alpha_{00}),

and the scheme
{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{7}
we consider is given by the intersection ideal

I=I^{\prime}\cap I^{\prime\prime}\cap I^{\prime\prime\prime}
.
It is straightforward to verify that
{\mathcal{R}}
is a flat linearly independent familia
3
over
Q
.
Its linear span is defined by the following
5
linear equations:

\displaystyle I_{1}=\langle

\displaystyle\alpha_{02}-s(\alpha_{00}-\alpha_{01}),\alpha_{03}-t(\alpha_{00}-\alpha_{01}),\alpha_{22}-s^{2}(\alpha_{00}-\alpha_{01}),

\displaystyle\alpha_{23}-st(\alpha_{00}-\alpha_{01}),\alpha_{33}-t^{2}(\alpha_{00}-\alpha_{01})\rangle.

Moreover,

{\mathcal{R}}\subset\left\{\rk M=1\right\}
where
M
is the following symmetric matrix:

M=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{00}&\alpha_{01}&\alpha_{02}&\alpha_{03}\\
\alpha_{01}&\alpha_{11}&0&0\\
\alpha_{02}&0&\alpha_{22}&\alpha_{23}\\
\alpha_{03}&0&\alpha_{23}&\alpha_{33}\end{pmatrix}

In the notation of Corollary
3.29
,

\pi_{1,M}({\mathcal{R}})
and
\pi_{2,M}({\mathcal{R}})

are not linearly independent.
Yet, despite the assumption of
Corollary
3.29
is not satisfied (even after swapping the roles of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
),
we still have a containment

{\mathbb{P}}\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle\subset\left\{\rk M\leq 3\right\}
.

4
Relative linear span and cactus scheme

In this section, we introduce and discuss the cactus scheme and its “flat” version (Definition
4.3
).
These are defined as unions of relative linear spans (Definition
4.1
) of independent familia
r
’s. We show in Proposition
4.6
that (under suitable assumptions) their reduced subschemes coincide with the cactus varieties known from earlier works. We also show that appropriate minors tend to vanish on the cactus scheme (Theorem
4.8
).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{Q}W
be a familia
r
over a scheme
Q
with enough
{\Bbbk}
-points, given by the homogeneous ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathcal{S}}
.
Suppose that
{\mathcal{R}}\to Q
is linearly independent.
We define
the relative linear span

\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle_{\operatorname{rel}}
of
{\mathcal{R}}
to be the scheme-theoretic image of
\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle
under the projection map

\pi\colon{\mathbb{P}}_{Q}W\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W
, that is

\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle_{\operatorname{rel}}:=\overline{\pi(\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle)}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W.

Similarly, one can define the affine version of relative linear span inside
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)\simeq{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}^{N+1}
, but we will not use it here.

Relative linear spans of flat familia
r
’s without the assumption of linear independence, but only over a reduced base
Q
are discussed in
[
BJ17
, §5.6–5.7]
.
In particular, the definition there is “generic”, component by component.
If the base is not reduced,
then the generic definition would not take into account the non-reduced structure.
In the case of reduced base and linearly independent flat familia
r
, the two definitions agree.
On the other hand, if the familia
r
is not independent,
then one has to deal with pathologies similar to Example
3.15
, which is beyond the scope of this article.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
Q
be a scheme with enough
{\Bbbk}
-points,
and let
{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{Q}W
be a linearly independent familia
r
over
Q
with
\pi\colon{\mathbb{P}}_{Q}W\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W
the natural projection.
Let
Q^{\prime}
be another scheme with enough
{\Bbbk}
-points.
The following are basic properties of relative linear span.

(i)

\pi({\mathcal{R}})\subset\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle_{\operatorname{rel}}

(ii)

If
Q^{\prime}\to Q
is a morphism and
{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}=Q^{\prime}\times_{Q}{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{Q^{\prime}}W
is the pullback,
then
\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}\right\rangle_{\operatorname{rel}}\subset\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle_{\operatorname{rel}}
and if in addition the scheme theoretic image of
Q^{\prime}
under
Q^{\prime}\to Q
is

, then
\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}\right\rangle_{\operatorname{rel}}=\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle_{\operatorname{rel}}
.

Proof.
Item
(i)
follows from the definition,
since
{\mathcal{R}}\subset\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle
,
and thus
\overline{\pi({\mathcal{R}})}\subset\overline{\pi\left(\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle\right)}=\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle_{\operatorname{rel}}
.
Item
(ii)
follows immediately from Corollary
3.24
.
∎

Definition 4.3
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W

be a locally closed subscheme.

The
r
-th cactus scheme
of
X
is defined as

\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{r}\left(X\right):=\overline{\bigcup_{{\mathcal{R}}\to Q}\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle_{\operatorname{rel}}},

where the scheme theoretic union
is over all independent familia
(r^{\prime})
’s
{\mathcal{R}}\to Q
in
X
over schemes
Q
with enough
{\Bbbk}
-points for all
r^{\prime}\leq r
.
Furthermore, we define
the flat
version of
r
-th cactus scheme:

\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(X\right):=\overline{\bigcup_{{\mathcal{R}}\to Q\text{ flat}}\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle_{\operatorname{rel}}},

but here the union is over all independent and flat familia
(r^{\prime})
’s

{\mathcal{R}}\to Q
in
X
over schemes
Q
with enough
{\Bbbk}
-points for all
r^{\prime}\leq r
.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any locally closed subschemes
X,Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(W)
and any integer
r\geqslant 1
,
the following are basic properties
of their cactus schemes.

(i)

\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(X\right)\subset\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{r}\left(X\right)\subset\left\langle X\right\rangle
.

(ii)

If
r=1
, then
\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{1}\left(X\right)=\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{1}\left(X\right)=\overline{X}
.

(iii)

If
Y\subset X
, then

\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(Y\right)\subset\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(X\right)

and

\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{r}\left(Y\right)\subset\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{r}\left(X\right)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

In the definitions of
\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{r}\left(X\right)
and
\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(X\right)

it is enough to use
Q
which are finite local and Gorenstein.

Proof.

The lemma is an immediate consequence of the definitions and
Corollary
3.25
(iii)
.
∎

Suppose
X
contains at least
r
of
{\Bbbk}
-points which are linearly independent
(for example
\dim X>0
and
\dim_{{\Bbbk}}\left\langle X\right\rangle\geq r-1

Then in the definitions of

and

it is enough to consider familia
r

without the necessity of considering familia
(r^{\prime})
’s for

.
Indeed, locally, we can always add some

of the independent
{\Bbbk}
-points multiplied by the local base

to obtain a familia

(r^{\prime})
.
Thus, in the proofs we will often for a simplicity of notation only consider the familia

Moreover, in the rest of the article, we will
frequently assume that the scheme

under consideration imposes independent linear conditions
to its subschemes of length at most
r

•

any finite subscheme

of length at most

is linearly independent,

•

in particular,
any familia

in

The main example of such

is the Veronese variety

for

.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}W

imposes independent conditions to its subschemes of length at most
r
.
Then the reduced subschemes of

\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{r}\left(X\right)
and of

\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(X\right)
are equal to the “cactus variety”
\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right):=\overline{\bigcup_{R\subset X}\left\langle R\right\rangle}
, where the union is over all subschemes
R
of length at most
r
.

Proof.
Any finite subscheme
R\subset X
of length
r^{\prime}
with
r^{\prime}\leq r
produces a “trivial” familia
(r^{\prime})

R\to\Spec{\Bbbk}
over
\Spec{\Bbbk}
in

.
By our assuptions, this familia
(r^{\prime})
is independent and also (trivially) flat, hence its relative linear span (which is equal to
\left\langle R\right\rangle

\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{r}\left(X\right)
and

\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(X\right)
, proving

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)\subset{\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(X\right)}_{\operatorname{red}}\subset{\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{sch}}_{r}\left(X\right)}_{\operatorname{red}}

On the other hand, by Lemma

the cactus scheme is the closure of the union of
\left\langle{\mathcal{R}}\right\rangle_{\operatorname{rel}}
for a familia
r
over
a finite local base

.
If

and the image of

Thus,

∎

By the above proposition, we think of

and

as of two fairly natural (geometrically defined) choices of scheme structure on the cactus variety that was studied previously

]
,
[

]
,
[

.

indicates that in fact

At this moment, we cannot give an interesting example

Suppose

The following theorem is the main reason we propose the two cactus scheme structures.
Similarly to the situation in Corollary

Suppose

Recall the catalecticant map

\Upsilon^{i,d-i}\colon{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(d)}V)}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{i}V^{*}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(d)}V)}(1)\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(d-i)}V

5
Relative apolarity

In this section, we provide a very general setting of relative apolarity developing
the classical apolarity theory
[
Ems78
]
,
and some of its extended versions in
[
Jel18
]
and
[
KK22
]
.
Suppose
V
is a vector space over
{\Bbbk}
and
Q
is a scheme with a morphism
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{P}}S^{(d)}V
.
The main aim of this section is to define and investigate the annihilator of
F
,
which is a homogeneous ideal sheaf in the graded

{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}
-algebra:

{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{i}V^{*}
.
For a purpose of further reference,
we will consider a slightly more general setting.

Throughout this section, for consistency, we assume that
S^{(i)}V=0
for every
i<0
.

5.1
Apolarity action

We define the apolarity pairing:

\lrcorner\colon{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\times\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(i)}V\to\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(i)}V.

Here

\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(i)}V=\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}({\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*},{\Bbbk}),

and the apolarity action is defined by the following formula: for any open subset
U\subset Q

choose
\Theta\in{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(U)\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
and

F\in\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(U)\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(i)}V
, define

(\Theta\lrcorner F)(\Psi)=F(\Theta\cdot\Psi)

for all
\Psi\in{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(U)\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
.
If we choose a basis
\left\{x_{0},\dotsc,x_{n}\right\}
of
V
and its dual basis
\left\{\alpha_{0},\dotsc,\alpha_{n}\right\}
of
V^{*}
, then
we can identify

\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(i)}V
with the divided power series ring
{\Bbbk}_{dp}[[x_{0},\dotsc,x_{n}]]
.
The apolarity action satisfies the following properties :

(i)

For any index
i

\alpha_{i}\lrcorner x_{0}^{(a_{0})}\dotsm x_{n}^{(a_{n})}=\begin{cases}x_{0}^{(a_{0})}\dotsm x_{i}^{(a_{i}-1)}\dotsm x_{n}^{(a_{n})}&\text{if }a_{i}>0\\
0&\text{if }a_{i}=0.\end{cases}

(ii)

The pairing is
{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}
bilinear, that is, for any open subscheme
U\subset Q
,
if
s_{1},s_{2},t_{1},t_{2}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(U)
,
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(U)\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
,
and
G_{1},G_{2}\in\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(U)\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(i)}V
we have:

(s_{1}\Theta_{1}+s_{2}\Theta_{2})\lrcorner(t_{1}G_{1}+t_{2}G_{2})=s_{1}t_{1}\Theta_{1}\lrcorner G_{1}+s_{1}t_{2}\Theta_{1}\lrcorner G_{2}+s_{2}t_{1}\Theta_{2}\lrcorner G_{1}+s_{2}t_{2}\Theta_{2}\lrcorner G_{2}.

(iii)

The apolarity action
is consistent with the algebra structure
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
,
that is for any open subscheme
U\subset Q
,

\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(U)\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
, and
G\in\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(U)\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(i)}V
:

(\Theta_{1}\Theta_{2})\lrcorner G=\Theta_{1}\lrcorner(\Theta_{2}\lrcorner G)=\Theta_{2}\lrcorner(\Theta_{1}\lrcorner G).

These properties determine uniquely the pairing
\lrcorner
on
the polynomial part

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(i)}V

\displaystyle\subset\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(i)}V,\text{ or equivalently,}

\displaystyle{\Bbbk}_{dp}[x_{0},\dotsc,x_{n}]

\displaystyle\subset{\Bbbk}_{dp}[[x_{0},\dotsc,x_{n}]].

For further properties of
\lrcorner
see for instance

[
Jel17
, §3.1, §4.4]
.
In particular,
\lrcorner
behaves well with respect to morphisms of the base scheme:

\text{if }f\colon Q^{\prime}\to Q,\text{ then }f^{*}\otimes\id_{{\Bbbk}_{dp}[[x_{0},\dotsc,x_{n}]]}(\Theta\lrcorner G)=f^{*}\otimes\id_{\Sym V^{*}}(\Theta)\lrcorner f^{*}\otimes\id_{{\Bbbk}_{dp}[[x_{0},\dotsc,x_{n}]]}(G).

(5.1)

Notation 5.2
.

For brevity and clarity of presentation,
we will frequently skip tensorising maps with identity
\otimes\id_{\dots}
, whenever no confusion may arise.
For instance, a shorter version of (
5.1
) is
f^{*}(\Theta\lrcorner G)=f^{*}\Theta\lrcorner f^{*}G
.
Slightly more formally,
whenever we have a morphisms of rings,
modules, or sheaves
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{F}}\to{\mathcal{G}}
,
and
W
is another ring, module, or sheaf,
such that it makes sense to consider

{\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{\bullet}W
and
{\mathcal{G}}\otimes_{\bullet}W
,
then the induced morphism
{\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{\bullet}W\longrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}\otimes_{\bullet}W

is denoted by the same symbol
\varphi
.

Another important property is that the apolarity respects grading in the following sense:

\lrcorner\colon{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{i}V^{*}\times{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(j)}V\to{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(j-i)}V.

In fact, it similarly preserves also non-standard gradings, see Lemma
5.18
.

Example 5.3

Let

5.2
Definition of relative annihilator ideal sheaf

Informally, similarly to the classically studied
non-relative case,
the annihilator of
F
in the relative setting
is defined as all
\Theta
such that
\Theta\lrcorner F=0
.
We commence formalizing the definition, by restricting to an affine case,
then proving it can be glued together to a quasi-coherent sheaf.

Whenever there is no risk of harm for the clarity, we state our definitions in a more general setting than needed just for the purpose of this paper.
We hope this will serve as a reference in further research.

Definition 5.5

(Relative annihilator for affine
Q
)
.

Suppose
Q=\Spec A
is an affine scheme and

F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leq d)}V\right)={\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(\bigoplus_{j=0}^{d}S^{(j)}V\right)
is a morphism.
Then define

\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right):=\left\{\Theta\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\mid\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes}=0\right\}.

We commence with the following semicontinuity observation:

Lemma 5.6

(Semicontinuity of
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
with respect to
Q
)
.

Suppose
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are two affine schemes, and
G\colon Q^{\prime}\to Q
,

F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leq d)}V\right)
are two morphisms.
Denote
F^{\prime}=F\circ G\colon Q^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leq d)}V\right)
.
Then,

G^{*}(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right))\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\right).

Note that by writting
G^{*}(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right))
in the statement of the lemma we exploit
Notation
5.2
.
Similarly, we use this notation in the proof.

Proof.
Let
Q=\Spec A
and
Q^{\prime}=\Spec A^{\prime}
,
and suppose

\Theta\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)\subset A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
.
Then

G^{*}(\Theta)\lrcorner F^{\prime}_{\otimes}=G^{*}(\Theta)\lrcorner(F\circ G)_{\otimes}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by Prop.~\ref{prop_basic_properties_of_tensor_of_a_scheme}\ref{item_composition_in_terms_of_tensors}}}}{{=}}G^{*}(\Theta)\lrcorner(G^{*}F_{\otimes})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{equ_hook_preserved_by_pullback}}}}{{=}}G^{*}(\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes})=G^{*}(0)=0.

Therefore,
G^{*}(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right))\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\right)
as claimed.
∎

The following examples illustrate failures of “continuity” of
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
.

Example 5.7
.

Let
Q_{k}=\Spec{\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{k})
,
and
F_{k,\otimes}=x^{d}+ty^{d}+t^{2}z^{d}
.
Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=(t^{k-1}\beta,t^{k-2}\gamma,\alpha\beta,\alpha\gamma,\beta\gamma,\beta^{d}-t\alpha^{d},\gamma^{d}-t^{2}\alpha^{d},\alpha^{d+1})

\displaystyle\text{ for }k\geqslant 2,

\displaystyle\text{and }\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=(\beta,\gamma,\alpha^{d+1})

\displaystyle\text{ for }k=1.

It is straightforward to see that

\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F_{k}\right)|_{Q_{i}}\subsetneqq\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F_{i}\right)
for
0\leqslant i<k
.
As one example,

\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F_{k}\right)|_{Q_{1}}=(\alpha\beta,\alpha\gamma,\beta\gamma,\beta^{d},\gamma^{d},\alpha^{d+1})\neq\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F_{1}\right)
.

Example 5.8
.

Consider
Q=\Spec{\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2})
and
Q^{\prime}=\Spec{\Bbbk}
with the unique embedding
G\colon Q^{\prime}\to Q
.
Suppose
F_{gen}\in S^{(d)}V
is a general element.
Let

F_{\otimes}:=x_{0}^{(d)}+tF_{gen}\in{\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2})\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(d)}V
.
Pick a homogeneous
\Theta+t\Theta^{\prime}\in{\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2})\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{i}V^{*}
.
Then:

(\Theta+t\Theta^{\prime})\lrcorner(x_{0}^{(d)}+tF_{gen})=\Theta\lrcorner x_{0}^{(d)}+t(\Theta\lrcorner F_{gen}+\Theta^{\prime}\lrcorner x_{0}^{(d)}).

Hence,
\Theta+t\Theta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
if and only if

\Theta\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(x_{0}^{(d)}\right)
and
\Theta\lrcorner F_{gen}=-\Theta^{\prime}\lrcorner x_{0}^{(d)}
.
We always have
-\Theta^{\prime}\lrcorner x_{0}^{(d)}=c\cdot x_{0}^{(d-i)}
for some
c\in{\Bbbk}
,
but if
1\leqslant i\leqslant\frac{d}{2}
then we never have

\Theta\lrcorner F_{gen}\in\left\langle x_{0}^{(d-i)}\right\rangle
(unless
\Theta=0
).
In particular, in low degrees,
we must have strict inclusion

0=\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{i}|_{Q^{\prime}}\subsetneqq\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(x_{0}^{(d)}\right)_{i}\neq 0
.

There are however two types of situations, where the continuity of
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
holds, at least partially.
The first one is the case of open subsets (Proposition
5.9
), and this property will allow us to extend the definition of annihilator to a sheaf of ideals on any (not necessarily affine) scheme
Q
.
The second is the flat case,
when the rank of a catalecticant matrix is constant on
Q
, see Proposition
5.15
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Suppose
Q=\Spec A
is an affine scheme,
s\in A
, and
U\simeq\Spec A_{s}\subset Q
is an open subscheme with
U=Q\setminus\left\{s=0\right\}
.
Denote by
G^{*}\colon A\to A_{s}
the localization map.
Then for any

F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)

we have

\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F|_{U}\right)=A_{s}\cdot G^{*}(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)).

Again, we exploit Notation
5.2
both in the statement and the proof of the proposition.

Proof.
The inclusion

A_{s}\cdot G^{*}(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right))\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F|_{U}\right)

follows from the general semicontinuity,
see Lemma
5.6
.
Thus, to prove the opposite inclusion,
pick any
\Theta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F|_{U}\right)\subset A_{s}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
.
Therefore, there exists an integer
k\geq 0

such that
s^{k}\cdot\Theta^{\prime}
is in the image of

G^{*}\colon A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\to A_{s}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
,
that is there exists
\Theta\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
such that
G^{*}(\Theta)=s^{k}\cdot\Theta^{\prime}
.
Then:

\displaystyle G^{*}(\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{By \eqref{equ_hook_preserved_by_pullback}}}}{{=}}G^{*}(\Theta)\lrcorner(F|_{U})_{\otimes}=s^{k}\cdot\Theta^{\prime}\lrcorner(F|_{U})_{\otimes}=0,\text{that is}

\displaystyle\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes}

\displaystyle\in\ker(G^{*}\colon A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(\leqslant d)}V\to A_{s}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(\leqslant d)}V)

\displaystyle=\left\{t\in A\mid ts^{l}=0\text{ for some }l>0\right\}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(\leqslant d)}V.

Therefore, choosing a basis

\left\{f_{0},\dotsc,f_{N}\right\}
of
S^{(\leqslant d)}V
,
we can write

\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}t_{i}f_{i}

with each
t_{i}\cdot s^{l_{i}}=0
for some
l_{i}>0
.
Let
l=\max(l_{i}\mid i\in\left\{0,\dotsc,N\right\})
.
We have
s^{l}\cdot\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes}=0
,
that is
s^{l}\cdot\Theta\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
.
This proves the claim, as

\Theta^{\prime}=s^{-k-l}\cdot G^{*}(s^{l}\cdot\Theta)\in s^{-k-l}\cdot G^{*}(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)).

∎

Definition 5.10
.

Suppose
Q
is a scheme and

F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)
is a morphism of schemes.
We define

\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}

to be the unique quasi-coherent sheaf of ideals
such that for each open affine subset
U\subset Q
the value of the sheaf

\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)(U)
is the ideal
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F|_{U}\right)
as in Definition
5.5
.
Such ideal sheaf exists by Proposition
5.9
.

Before proceeding we prove one more technical lemma about the annihilator in the case of finite local schemes. This lemma says that despite we do not have the continuity of annihilators in general, we can sometimes read the annihilator of a point from the annihilator of a family.

Lemma 5.11
.

Suppose
Q=\Spec A
is a finite local scheme over
{\Bbbk}
and
F\colon Q\to S^{(\leq d)}V
is a morphism.
Let
F_{{\Bbbk}}\colon\Spec{\Bbbk}\to S^{(\leq d)}V
be the restriction of
F
to the closed reduced point
\Spec{\Bbbk}\in Q
and let
s\in A
be a non-zero element of the socle of
A
.
Then the map of
\Sym V^{*}
-modules

\Sym V^{*}\to A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}

taking
\Phi
to
s\cdot\Phi

maps
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F_{{\Bbbk}}\right)
isomorphically onto
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)\cap s\cdot\Sym V^{*}
.

Proof.
Let
\mathfrak{m}\subset A
be the maximal ideal.
We
write
F_{\otimes}=F_{{\Bbbk}}+F_{\mathfrak{m}}
,
where
F_{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathfrak{m}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
.
Take
\Phi\in\Sym V^{*}
.
We have

s\Phi\lrcorner F_{\otimes}=s(\Phi\lrcorner F_{{\Bbbk}})+\Phi\lrcorner(sF_{\mathfrak{m}})=s(\Phi\lrcorner F_{{\Bbbk}}).

Therefore,
\Phi\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F_{{\Bbbk}}\right)

if and only if
s\Phi\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
as claimed.
∎

5.3
Descending the annihilator to projective space

The goal of this section is to prove that we can almost equally well define the annihilator for morphisms
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(\leqslant d)}V)
.
To commence, we state the following lemma,
whose proof and its consequence are straightforward,
and we skip the details.

Lemma 5.12

(Invariance of annihilator under rescalings)
.

Suppose
Q=\Spec A
is an affine scheme,

F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)

is a morphism, and
s\in A
is any element.
Let
sF\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)
be the morphism, whose corresponding tensor is

s\cdot F_{\otimes}
.
Then
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(sF\right)
.
In particular, if
s
is invertible, then
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(sF\right)=\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
.

Corollary 5.13
.

Suppose
Q
is a scheme
and
F,F^{\prime}\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)

are two morphisms such that:

•

0\in{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)
is not in the image of
F
or of
F^{\prime}
, and

•

the compositions of
F
and
F^{\prime}
with

\pi\colon{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)\setminus\left\{0\right\}\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(\leqslant d)}V)
are equal.

Then
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)=\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\right)
.

Lemma 5.14
.

Suppose

F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)

and set

F^{\prime}\colon\mathbf{G}_{m,{\Bbbk}}\times_{{\Bbbk}}Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)

to be the natural morphism coming from the action
of
\mathbf{G}_{m,{\Bbbk}}
on
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)
by rescaling.
Let
G_{1}
be any section
Q\to\mathbf{G}_{m,{\Bbbk}}\times_{{\Bbbk}}Q
, and
G_{2}
be the projection
\mathbf{G}_{m,{\Bbbk}}\times_{{\Bbbk}}Q\to Q
.
Then
G_{1}^{*}(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\right))
generates
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
and

G_{2}^{*}(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right))
generates
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\right)
.

Proof.
It is enough to check the statement
for affine
Q=\Spec A
.
Then
\mathbf{G}_{m,{\Bbbk}}\times_{{\Bbbk}}Q=\Spec A[t,t^{-1}]
and
G_{2}^{*}\colon A\to A[t,t^{-1}]
is the natural inclusion, while
G_{1}^{*}\colon A[t,t^{-1}]\to A
is identity on
A
and maps
t
to an invertible element of
A
. We have
G_{1}^{*}\circ G_{2}^{*}=\id_{A}
.
Moreover, using Notation
5.2
, it is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{\otimes}

\displaystyle=t\cdot G_{2}^{*}(F_{\otimes})\in A[t,t^{-1}]\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(\leqslant d)}V,\text{ and}

\displaystyle G_{1}^{*}(F^{\prime}_{\otimes})

\displaystyle=G_{1}^{*}(t)\cdot G_{1}^{*}G_{2}^{*}(F_{\otimes})=G_{1}^{*}(t)\cdot F_{\otimes}\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(\leqslant d)}V.

In particular, since both
t\in A[t,t^{-1}]
and

G_{1}^{*}(t)\in A
are invertible, we have:

\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\circ G_{2}\right)=\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\right)\text{ and }\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\circ G_{1}\right)=\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)

by
Lemma
5.12
.
Therefore,
by Lemma
5.6

we must have
G_{2}^{*}(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right))\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\right)
and

G_{1}^{*}(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\right))\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
and to prove the statement it is enough to check that:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\circ G_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\subset(A[t,t^{-1}]\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*})\cdot G_{2}^{*}(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)),\text{ and}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)

\displaystyle\subset G_{1}^{*}(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\right)).

Any
\Theta^{\prime}\in A[t,t^{-1}]\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
can be expressed as

\Theta^{\prime}=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}G_{2}^{*}(\Theta^{\prime}_{i})t^{i}
with

\Theta^{\prime}_{i}\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
with only finitely many
\Theta^{\prime}_{i}\neq 0
.
Then
\Theta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\circ G_{2}\right)
if and only if

G_{2}^{*}\Theta^{\prime}_{i}\lrcorner G_{2}^{*}F_{\otimes}=G_{2}^{*}(\Theta^{\prime}_{i}\lrcorner F_{\otimes})=0
for all
i
,
which is only possible if
\Theta^{\prime}_{i}\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
, proving the first claim above.

Now pick
\Theta\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\circ G_{1}\right)=\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
and consider
G_{2}^{*}\Theta
. By Lemma
5.6
and
Corollary
5.13
we have

G_{2}^{*}\Theta\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\circ G_{2}\right)=\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\right)
.
Thus,

\Theta\lrcorner G_{1}^{*}F^{\prime}_{\otimes}=G_{1}^{*}G_{2}^{*}\Theta\lrcorner G_{1}^{*}F^{\prime}_{\otimes}=G_{1}^{*}(G_{2}^{*}\Theta\lrcorner F^{\prime}_{\otimes})=0

showing the second claim above, and concluding the proof of the lemma.
∎

We denote by
\pi\colon\left({\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right)\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(\leqslant d)}V)
the natural quotient map.

Proposition 5.15
.

Suppose
Q
is a scheme and
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(\leqslant d)}V)
is a morphism.
Then there exists a unique quasicoherent sheaf of ideals
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}

such that for any open subset
U\subset Q

and any lift
\hat{F}_{U}\colon U\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)
such that
\pi\circ\hat{F}_{U}=F|_{U}
, the equality of ideals holds

\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)|_{U}=\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(\hat{F}_{U}\right)
.

Proof.
Define
\hat{Q}:=Q\times_{{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)}\left({\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right)
, which is a
\mathbf{G}_{m,{\Bbbk}}
-bundle over
Q
.
Then, for
\hat{F}\colon\hat{Q}\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)

the ideal sheaf
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(\hat{F}\right)
is
\mathbf{G}_{m,{\Bbbk}}
-invariant
by Corollary
5.13
.
Therefore
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(\hat{F}\right)
descents to an ideal sheaf over the quotient
Q
. This descent is
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
which we are looking for.

If
U\subset Q
lifts to the affine space then it also lifts to
\hat{Q}
and the restriction of the
\mathbf{G}_{m,{\Bbbk}}
-bundle
\hat{Q}\to Q
to
U
is trivial.

\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)|_{U}
agrees with
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(\hat{F}_{U}\right)
by Lemma
5.14
.
Uniqueness of
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
follows from
Corollary
5.13

as locally any two liftings
U\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)
will lead to the same ideal.
∎

Lemma 5.16
.

Suppose
Q=\Spec A
is a local scheme and
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(\leqslant d)}V)
is a morphism.
Then
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{0}=0
.

Proof.
Suppose
a\in A
and
a\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
, so that

a\lrcorner F_{\otimes}=aF_{\otimes}=0
.
But
F_{\otimes}\modulo\mathfrak{m}\neq 0
by Proposition
2.8

and Lemma
2.9
.
This is only possible if
a=0
.
∎

5.4
Equivariant behaviour of apolarity and a graded case

Suppose
\psi\colon\Sym V^{*}\to\Sym V^{*}
is a
{\Bbbk}
-algebra
automorphism,
and denote by
\overline{\psi}
the
{\Bbbk}
-linear dual automorphism:

\overline{\psi}\colon\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}S^{(i)}V\to\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}S^{(i)}V,\text{ with }\overline{\psi}(F)(\Theta)=F(\psi^{-1}(\Theta)),

taking into account that

\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}S^{(i)}V=\Hom_{{\Bbbk}}(\Sym V^{*},{\Bbbk})
.
Since
\psi
preserves the algebra structure of
\Sym V^{*}
, the automorphism
\overline{\psi}
preserves the
\Sym V^{*}
-module structure on
\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}S^{(i)}V
given by
\lrcorner
:

\overline{\psi}(\Theta\lrcorner F)=(\psi(\Theta)\lrcorner\overline{\psi}(F)).

Assume in addition that
\psi
preserves the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{n}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}S^{i}V^{*}
, so that
\psi(\mathfrak{n}^{i})=\mathfrak{n}^{i}
for all
i
.
Since
(\mathfrak{n}^{i})^{\perp}=S^{(\leq i-1)}V
, for such
\psi
the dual map
\overline{\psi}
preserves the space of polynomials
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}S^{(i)}V
. By a slight abuse of notation, we will denote the restricted automorphism of
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}S^{(i)}V
also by
\overline{\psi}
.

We also want to keep exploiting Notation
5.2
. Note that so far we were using it with respect to tensorising on the second factor, skipping for instance
\otimes\id_{\Sym V^{*}}
or
\otimes\id_{S^{(\leqslant d)}V}
. Below we will use it also to skip the identity on the first factor,
\id_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}}\otimes
or
\id_{A}\otimes
, by considering
\psi(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right))
or
\overline{\psi}(F_{\otimes})
and similar.

Lemma 5.17

(Equivariance of annihilator)
.

Suppose
\psi
is a
{\Bbbk}
-algebra automorphism of
\Sym V^{*}
preserving
\mathfrak{n}
.
Let
Q
be a scheme and
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)
a morphism of schemes.
Then,
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(\overline{\psi}\circ F\right)=\psi(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right))
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose
Q=\Spec A
is affine.
Assume
\Theta\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
. Then,

\displaystyle\Theta\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(\overline{\psi}\circ F\right)\iff

\displaystyle\Theta\lrcorner(\overline{\psi}\circ F)_{\otimes}=0\iff\overline{\psi}(\psi^{-1}(\Theta)\lrcorner F_{\otimes})=0\iff

\displaystyle\psi^{-1}(\Theta)\lrcorner F_{\otimes}=0\iff

\displaystyle\psi^{-1}(\Theta)\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)\iff\Theta\in\psi(\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)).

∎

Suppose now that
\Sym V^{*}
is a positively graded (in the sense of
[
HS04
, p. 726]
) by a finitely generated abelian group
H
, and let
H^{*}=\Hom(H,\mathbf{G}_{m,{\Bbbk}})
.
Then the action of
H^{*}
on
\Sym V^{*}
preserves the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{n}
and
H^{*}
acts on
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leqslant d)}V\right)
for each
d
.

Lemma 5.18

(Homogeneity of annihilator of a homogeneous
F
)
.

If
W\subset S^{(\leq d)}V
is a weight space of the action of
H^{*}
, and
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
is a morphism of schemes,
then
\Ann(F)
is an
H
-homogeneous ideal sheaf in
{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}
, where the grading of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}
is trivial.

∎

From now on we will frequently consider the
apolar algebra
of a morphism
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(\leq d)}V\right)
:
let
\operatorname{Apolar}\!\left(F\right):={\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}/\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
.
Thus,
\operatorname{Apolar}\!\left(F\right)
is a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}
-algebras,
which are all finite modules over
{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}
.
In case
Q=\Spec A
is affine, we will simply consider
\operatorname{Apolar}\!\left(F\right)
to be the algerba of global sections.

In particular, the special case
Q=\Spec{\Bbbk}
is vastly and classically explored in the literature, as
\operatorname{Apolar}\!\left(F\right)
is a finite (over
{\Bbbk}
) local Gorenstein algebra and all such algebras arise in this way, see for instance
[
Jel17
, §2.1, 2.3, 3.3–3.5]
(and references therein) for a review of properties of these algebras.
Perhaps the most widely used and known property is that for homogeneous
F
the apolar algebra is self dual, with a shift in gradings.
We show that an analogous duality holds more generally, whenever the base
Q
is itself a finite local Gorenstein scheme.

Lemma 5.19

(Duality)
.

Suppose
W\subset S^{(\leq d)}V
is a weight space of the action of
H^{*}
of degree
D\in H
.
Assume
Q=\Spec A
is a finite local Gorenstein scheme over
{\Bbbk}
,
and
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(W\right)
is a morphism of schemes.
Then for all degrees
I\in H
the following
A
-modules are isomorphic:

\operatorname{Apolar}\!\left(F\right)_{I}=\Hom_{A}\left(\operatorname{Apolar}\!\left(F\right)_{D-I},A\right).

Proof.

Consider the map
A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\to A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(\leq d)}V
given by
\Theta\mapsto\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes}
.
Its kernel is
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
, so that

A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\lrcorner F_{\otimes}=A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}/\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)=\operatorname{Apolar}\!\left(F\right)
(as
A
-modules).
Moreover, the map reverses the grading:

\operatorname{Apolar}\!\left(F\right)_{I}=\left(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\lrcorner F_{\otimes}\right)_{D-I}

(5.20)

Next, we verify that the following containments of
A
-submodules (respectively, of
A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{\leqslant d}V)_{D-I}
and of
A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}(\Sym V^{*})_{D-I}
) hold:

\displaystyle\left(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\lrcorner F_{\otimes}\right)_{D-I}

\displaystyle\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{D-I}^{\perp}\text{ and}

(5.21)

\displaystyle\left(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\lrcorner F_{\otimes}\right)_{D-I}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{D-I}.

Indeed, suppose

G\in\left(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\lrcorner F_{\otimes}\right)_{D-I}
,
that is
G=\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes}
for some homogeneous form

\Theta\in\left(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\right)_{I}
.
Then, for any
\Psi\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{D-I}
,

\Psi(G)=\Psi\lrcorner G=\Psi\lrcorner(\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes})=(\Psi\cdot\Theta)\lrcorner F_{\otimes}=\Theta\lrcorner(\Psi\lrcorner F_{\otimes})=0,

so
G\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{D-I}^{\perp}
as claimed in the first inclusion of (
5.21
).

Now let
\Theta\in\left(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\lrcorner F_{\otimes}\right)_{D-I}^{\perp}
.
Then for all
\Psi\in\left(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\right)_{I}

we have:

0=\Theta(\Psi\lrcorner F_{\otimes})=\Psi(\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes}),

so that
\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes}\in\left(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\right)_{I}^{\perp}
.
Since
\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes}
is in the weight space of degree
I
,
it follows that
\Theta\lrcorner F_{\otimes}=0
, proving the second inclusion of (
5.21
).

Now we combine the first inclusion of
(
5.21
)
with the the perpendicular parts of the second one.
By (
2.12
) we obtain:

\left(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\lrcorner F_{\otimes}\right)_{D-I}\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{D-I}^{\perp}\subset\left(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\lrcorner F_{\otimes}\right)_{D-I}^{\perp\perp}.

To conclude, we must use the hypothesis
on
Q
or, more precisely, on
A
, that they finite local Gorenstein.
We have to use
Proposition
2.17
.
The two containments (
5.21
) imply:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{D-I}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\ref{prop_perp_of_perp_for_Gorenstein}\ref{item_perp_of_perp_for_Gorenstein}}}}{{=}}\left(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}\lrcorner F_{\otimes}\right)_{D-I}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\eqref{equ_grading_reversed}}}}{{=}}\operatorname{Apolar}\!\left(F\right)_{I}.

To conclude, apply Proposition
2.17
to
M=\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{D-I}^{\perp}
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Apolar}\!\left(F\right)_{I}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{D-I}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\ref{prop_perp_of_perp_for_Gorenstein}\ref{item_dual_of_submodule_versus_perp}}}}{{=}}\Hom\left(\bigl(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}/\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)^{\perp\perp}\bigr)_{D-I},A\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\ref{prop_perp_of_perp_for_Gorenstein}\ref{item_perp_of_perp_for_Gorenstein}}}}{{=}}\Hom\left(\bigl(A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}/\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)\bigr)_{D-I},A\right)=\Hom\left(\operatorname{Apolar}\!\left(F\right)_{D-I},A\right).

∎

The proof presented above is perhaps not the most direct one.
Instead, one could exploit from the very beginning the presentation of
A
as a quotient of a polynomial ring by the annihilator of a polynomial in a dual ring. Then present
\operatorname{Apolar}\!\left(F\right)
in the same way, but using polynomial rings with more variables, and with a grading in which some variables have degree
0
.
Then the statement would follow from the classical Gorenstein duality.
Our proof has the advantage that a large part of it holds for any base algebra.
The finite Gorenstein assumption is only required for the implication of Proposition
2.17
.
Even though the following example shows this assumption is necessary to get the conclusion as in the lemma,
we believe that the partial results
(such as inclusions of (
5.21
)) have a potential to be applied in further research.

Example 5.22
.

Suppose
A={\Bbbk}[s,t]/(s^{2},st,t^{2})

and define
F_{\otimes}=sx^{(d)}+ty^{(d)}\in A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(d)}V
for a two dimensional
V
spanned by
x,y
.
Then
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)=(s,t,\alpha\beta,\alpha^{d+1},\beta^{d+1})
.
In particular,
A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym V^{*}/\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)

in degree
0
is isomorphic to
{\Bbbk}
,
while in degree
d
is isomorphic to
{\Bbbk}^{2}
.
Therefore, unlike in the Gorenstein case,
the two gradings fail to be dual to one another.

5.5
Apolarity lemma

We conlude this section with a proof of relative apolarity lemma.
In the classical case, the apolarity lemma is a foundation for many research projects,
such as
[
RS00
]
,

[
BB21
]
,

[
RV17
]
,
[
BT20
]
, to mention just a few.
Informally, apolarity lemma appears whenever one needs set-theoretic membership tests for points of secant or cactus varieties, or recover Waring or cactus decomposition.
The ultimate goal of the relative apolarity lemma is to upgrade the set-theoretic tests and equations to scheme-theoretic ones.

In this article, from now on we will only consider cases homogeneous with respect to the standard grading.
That is, we will consider only maps of schemes
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(d)}V\right)
(without lower degree terms)
or its projective variant
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(d)}V)
.
Some more general versions of classical apolarity lemma have been studied,
see
[
Gał23a
]
and references therein,
and most likely they generalize to the relative setting analogous to
the setting of this paper, but proving it would require to dive into the setting of toric varieties and their Cox rings, which is outside of the applications we have in mind here.

Throughout this subsection
Q
is a scheme that has enough of
{\Bbbk}
-points. Frequently, either in the statements or in the proofs, we will restrict to the local case, so that
Q=\Spec A
for a local
{\Bbbk}
-algebra
A
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residue field

A/\mathfrak{m}={\Bbbk}
.
Recall, that
\nu_{d}
denotes the Veronese map.
Similarly to Notation
5.2
,
by a minor abuse we will denote also by
\nu_{d}
(instead of
\id_{Q}\times\nu_{d}
) the Veronese map
{\mathbb{P}}_{Q}V\to{\mathbb{P}}_{Q}(S^{(d)}V)
.

Theorem 5.23

(Relative apolarity lemma)
.

Suppose
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(d)}V)
and

{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{Q}V
is a family of subschemes.
Let
\overline{F}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{Q}(S^{(d)}V)

be the graph of
F
.
Then

\overline{F}\subset\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}})\right\rangle
if and only if
{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{R}})\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
.

We state two lemmas.
The first one in the classical case is standard, see for instance
[
IK99
, Lem. 2.15]
,
or
[
BB14
, Prop. 3.4(ii)&(iii)]

and the proof in the relative case is no different.
We repeat the argument for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 5.24
.

Suppose
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(d)}V\right)
and
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes\Sym V^{*}
is a homogeneous ideal sheaf.
Then for any integers
i
and
j
such that

i\leqslant j\leqslant d
, if
{\mathcal{I}}_{j}\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{j}
, then
{\mathcal{I}}_{i}\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{i}
.
In particular, if
{\mathcal{I}}_{d}\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{d}
, then
{\mathcal{I}}\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
.

Proof.
The proof relies on the following property:

\text{if }F^{\prime}_{\otimes}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes S^{(i)}V\text{ is such that }S^{i}V^{*}\lrcorner F^{\prime}_{\otimes}\equiv 0,\text{ then }F^{\prime}_{\otimes}=0.

(5.25)

First we prove the latter claim: if
{\mathcal{I}}_{d}\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{d}
, then
{\mathcal{I}}\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
.
Since
{\mathcal{I}}
is homogeneous,
it is enough to check the claim in each degree
k
.
If
k>d
, then
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{k}={\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{k}V^{*}
and there is nothing to check.
If
k=d
, then the assumption and the claim coincide.
If
k<d
then
\Phi\in{\mathcal{I}}_{k}
implies
S^{d-k}V^{*}\cdot\Phi\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{d}\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{d}
.
Hence
S^{d-k}V^{*}\lrcorner(\Phi\lrcorner F_{\otimes})=0
, and by (
5.25
)
\Phi\lrcorner F_{\otimes}=0
, that is
\Phi\in\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
as claimed.

In general, in order to prove the former claim of the lemma, it is enough to assume
{\mathcal{I}}
is generated in degrees up to
j
, since
{\mathcal{I}}_{i}
and
{\mathcal{I}}_{j}
is not affected by the higher degree generators.
Then
{\mathcal{I}}_{d}\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{d}
and we conclude that
{\mathcal{I}}\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)
, in particular
{\mathcal{I}}_{i}\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{i}
.
∎

Lemma 5.26
.

Suppose the notation for
F,\overline{F},{\mathcal{R}}

is as in Theorem
5.23

and that the base scheme
Q=\Spec A
is local.
Then,

\overline{F}\subset\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}})\right\rangle
if and only if

•

I({\mathcal{R}})_{0}=0
, and

•

for all
\Phi\in I({\mathcal{R}})_{d}

we have
\Phi(F_{\otimes})=0
.

Proof.
By definition,
the linear span
\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}})\right\rangle

is the subscheme of
{\mathbb{P}}_{A}(S^{(d)}V)

defined by the ideal generated by

I({\mathcal{R}})_{0}+I(\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}))_{1}\subset A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}\Sym(S^{d}V^{*})
.
Note that
I(\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}))_{1}=I({\mathcal{R}})_{d}\subset S^{d}V^{*}
.
Consider the affine subscheme

{\mathcal{W}}\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{A}\left(S^{(d)}V\right)
defined by the same ideal in
\Sym(S^{d}V)
,
that is the ideal generated by
I({\mathcal{R}})_{0}+I({\mathcal{R}})_{d}
.

Pick the lift of
F
to the affine space,

\hat{F}\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(d)}V\right)
,
that corresponds to the tensor
F_{\otimes}
.
Note that since
Q
is local and the image
\hat{F}(Q)
avoids the origin,
we have
\overline{F}\subset\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}})\right\rangle
if and only if the graph of
\hat{F}
is contained in
{\mathcal{W}}
.
The latter holds if and only if the generators of the ideal of
{\mathcal{W}}
are all in the ideal of the graph of
\hat{F}
.
That is, by Proposition
2.3
(iv)
,
this happens if and only if the equations of

I({\mathcal{R}})_{0}
and of
I({\mathcal{R}})_{d}
at
F_{\otimes}
are zero.
For
I({\mathcal{R}})_{d}
this is precisely the second item. But
I({\mathcal{R}})_{0}\subset A
, and the evaluation does not do anything. So the condition for vanishing evaluations of
I({\mathcal{R}})_{0}
is just
I({\mathcal{R}})_{0}=0
, as claimed in the first item of the lemma, concluding the proof.
∎

5.23
.

First we note,
that since
Q
has enough of
{\Bbbk}
-points,
it is enough to prove the equivalence for local

Indeed, there are four objects involved in the equivalence:
two of them are sheaves, and the other two are schemes.
Replacing

For
\overline{F}\subset\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}})\right\rangle

For
\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}})\right\rangle

By Lemma
5.26

I({\mathcal{R}})\subset\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)

•

I({\mathcal{R}})_{0}=0
, and

•

for all
\Phi\in I({\mathcal{R}})_{d}

\Phi(F_{\otimes})=0
.

Proposition 5.27
.

Suppose
F\colon Q\to{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}(S^{(d)}V)
is a morphism such that its image is contained in
\left\{\rk\Upsilon^{i,d-i}=r\right\}
for some
i
and some
r
.
Then
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{i}
is a locally free sheaf on
Q
.
Moreover, for any morphism of schemes

if we denote by
F^{\prime}
the composition
F\circ G
, then
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F^{\prime}\right)_{i}=G^{*}\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{i}
.

Proof.
To prove the first claim, covering
Q
by sufficiently small open affine subsets, by Proposition
5.9

it suffices to prove it for
Q=\Spec A
affine,
and for
F
such that lifts to a morphism

\hat{F}\colon Q\to{\mathbb{A}}_{{\Bbbk}}\left(S^{(d)}V\right)
.
Then, the map

\cdot\lrcorner F_{\otimes}\colon A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{i}V^{*}\to A\otimes_{{\Bbbk}}S^{(d-i)}V

is a homomorphism of
A

The second claim follows from the first as pulling back short exact sequences of locally free sheaves is exact.
∎

6.1
Catalecticant minors produce flat families

Theorem 6.1
.

Fix any positive integer

Lemma 6.2
.

{\mathcal{R}}\to Q

Proof.
Without loss of generality we may assume

. Suppose

and

.
Note that
\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{i}

is locally free (hence flat) over

are locally free over

By the Macaulay bound from Proposition

\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(\Sym V^{*}/\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{{\Bbbk}})_{k}\leqslant r\text{ and }\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(\Sym V^{*}/\operatorname{Ann}\!\left(F\right)_{\mathfrak{s}})_{k}\leqslant r

Theorem 6.3
.

The following two quasiprojective schemes are isomorphic:

\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}^{\operatorname{univ}})\right\rangle

(A)

(C)

in

.

(D)

If

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{\operatorname{Gor}}:={{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{r}^{\operatorname{Gor}}{({\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V)}
be the Hilbert scheme of Gorenstein subchemes of length
r
.
By construction
[
Jel17
, Def. 4.33]
the Gorenstein Hilbert scheme is an open (not necessarily dense) subscheme

{\mathcal{H}}^{\operatorname{Gor}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
. Consider the map

\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}^{\operatorname{univ}})\right\rangle\to{\mathcal{H}}
.
Each
{\Bbbk}
-point
[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}\setminus{\mathcal{H}}^{\operatorname{Gor}}
corresponds to a finite subscheme
R\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}V
of degree
r
.
By Corollary
3.24

the fibre
\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}^{\operatorname{univ}})\right\rangle_{[R]}
is equal to

\left\langle\nu_{d}(R)\right\rangle
. Considering the restricted map

\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}^{\operatorname{univ}})\right\rangle\setminus Z\to{\mathcal{H}}

we claim that:

•

the map factorises through
{\mathcal{H}}^{\operatorname{Gor}}
,
that is
\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}^{\operatorname{univ}})\right\rangle\setminus Z\to{\mathcal{H}}^{\operatorname{Gor}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
, and

•

the map
\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}^{\operatorname{univ}})\right\rangle\setminus Z\to{\mathcal{H}}^{\operatorname{Gor}}
is surjective.

The first claim (about factorization) is equivalent to equality of the fibres

Z_{[R]}=\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}^{\operatorname{univ}})\right\rangle_{[R]}=\left\langle\nu_{d}(R)\right\rangle
over each
{\Bbbk}
-point
[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}\setminus{\mathcal{H}}^{\operatorname{Gor}}
.
The latter span is covered by the spans of the smaller subschemes
\nu_{d}(R^{\prime})\subsetneqq\nu_{d}(R)
by
[
BB14
, Lem. 2.3]
or

[
BJ17
, Lem. 6.19]
.
Hence
\left\langle\nu_{d}(R)\right\rangle\subset Z_{[R]}
.
Since

Z\subset\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}^{\operatorname{univ}})\right\rangle
,
the other inclusion

Z_{[R]}\subset\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}^{\operatorname{univ}})\right\rangle_{[R]}
is automatic.

The second claim (about surjectivity) is also set theoretic,
hence pick a
{\Bbbk}
-point

[R]\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\operatorname{Gor}}

and consider the fibre

\left\langle\nu_{d}(R)\right\rangle
.
We want to prove that

Z\cap\left(\left\{[R]\right\}\times\left\langle\nu_{d}(R)\right\rangle\right)
is not dense.
By construction of
Z
this intersection is equal to the union of linear spans of smaller schemes,

Z\cap\left(\left\{[R]\right\}\times\left\langle\nu_{d}(R)\right\rangle\right)=\bigcup_{R^{\prime}\subsetneq R,\ \deg R^{\prime}=r-1}{\left\langle\nu_{d}(R^{\prime})\right\rangle}.

There are only finitely many subschemes
R^{\prime}\subsetneq R
of degree
r-1

by
[
BJ17
, Lem. 5.16]
and each span
\left\langle\nu_{d}(R^{\prime})\right\rangle
is of dimension
r-2
.
Thus,
Z\cap\left(\left\{[R]\right\}\times\left\langle\nu_{d}(R)\right\rangle\right)

cannot cover
\left\langle\nu_{d}(R)\right\rangle\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{r-1}
.
That is
Z\cap\left(\left\{[R]\right\}\times\left\langle\nu_{d}(R)\right\rangle\right)
is not dense and there is a
{\Bbbk}
-point in the complement

\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}^{\operatorname{univ}})\right\rangle\setminus Z
that is mapped to
[R]
, as claimed.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be the vector bundle over
{\mathcal{H}}^{\operatorname{Gor}}
such that

\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}^{\operatorname{univ}})\right\rangle|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\operatorname{Gor}}}={\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}({\mathcal{E}})

and
Z^{\operatorname{Gor}}:=Z\cap{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}({\mathcal{E}})
.
By the first item above, we have
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}({\mathcal{E}})\setminus Z^{\operatorname{Gor}}=\left\langle\nu_{d}({\mathcal{R}}^{\operatorname{univ}})\right\rangle\setminus Z
, while the latter is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}V)\right)\setminus\mathfrak{K}^{\operatorname{flat}}_{r-1}\left(\nu_{d}({\mathbb{P}}V)\right)

by Theorem
6.3
.
Moreover,
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}({\mathcal{E}})\setminus Z^{\operatorname{Gor}}\to{\mathcal{H}}^{\operatorname{Gor}}
is surjective by the second item above. These statements complete the proof of the theorem.
∎

When Ebstein Meets Fallot
Authors: Jules Ader, Charlène Bredy, Jean-Luc Pasquié
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCase SummaryA 14-year-old boy was admitted after resuscitated ventricular fibrillation. He had tetralogy of Fallot associated with Ebstein's anomaly, surgically repaired at age 7 without tricuspid intervention. His ECG demonstrates overlapping features of both conditions: high-amplitude right bundle branch block and right ventricular hypertrophy consistent with repaired tetralogy of Fallot, along with broad “Himalayan” P waves, first-degree atrioventricular block, and fragmented QRS complexes reflecting Ebstein-related conduction abnormalities. Clinical Vignette

A 14-year-old boy presented in our institution for resuscitated sudden death with documented ventricular fibrillation. At birth, he was diagnosed with tetralogy of Fallot (ToF) associated with Ebstein's anomaly. At age 7, he underwent complete surgical repair of ToF without surgery of the tricuspid valve. His ECG pattern is of particular interest as it combines criteria seen in both congenital abnormalities (Figure 1).

Discussion

This ECG demonstrates overlapping features of Ebstein's anomaly and surgically repaired ToF. ToF explains the high-amplitude right bundle branch block with right ventricular hypertrophy, which is unusual in Ebstein's anomaly due to the small functional right ventricle (Figure 2). Ebstein's anomaly is responsible of tall and broad P waves (sometimes described as “Himalayan”), first-degree atrioventricular block—unless masked by an associated accessory pathway—Q waves in anterior and inferior leads and a polyphasic QRS complex with late potentials, particularly evident in leads I and aVL, reflecting delayed conduction across atrialized ventricle (Figure 3).1

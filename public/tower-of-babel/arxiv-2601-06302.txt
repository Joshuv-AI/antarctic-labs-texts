I
Introduction

The origin of the baryon asymmetry remains a central puzzle at the intersection of particle physics and cosmology.
Sakharov’s conditions
Sakharov:1967dj
require baryon-number violation, C and CP violation, and departure from equilibrium.
In spontaneous baryogenesis
Cohen:1987vi
;
Cohen:1988kt
, a time-dependent scalar generates
\mu_{B}=\dot{\phi}/\Lambda_{*}
; however, if
\dot{\phi}
oscillates, smooth freeze-out leads to adiabatic cancellation—a point noted qualitatively in Refs.
Dolgov:1996qq
;
Dasgupta:2018qpz
.
Here we sharpen this into a transfer-function bound and then identify a minimal way to evade it: a sign-definite bias tied to irreversible entropy production.
In this sense the baryon asymmetry can be viewed as a fossil record of non-adiabaticity during reheating. We emphasize two points of broad (hep-ph) interest. First, the suppression result can be read as a model-independent filter statement: in linear response, Eq. (
1
) implies that any ”zero-mean” oscillatory source convolved with a smooth freeze-out window is low-pass filtered, with the efficiency controlled by the single dimensionless combination
\omega\tau_{\rm off}
. Second, the entropy-clock construction provides a minimal, sign-definite bias that is tied to irreversibility (
\dot{S}>0
) and therefore naturally evades adiabatic cancellation. The resulting mapping of the observed asymmetry onto the single overlap parameter
\epsilon\Pi_{\rm eff}
yields a compact target for reheating model building and for independent constraints on non-adiabatic dynamics.

II
Framework

A derivative coupling
\mathcal{L}_{\rm SB}=(\partial_{\mu}\phi/\Lambda_{*})J^{\mu}_{B}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\theta\,J^{\mu}_{B}
with
\theta\equiv\phi/\Lambda_{*}

acts as a chemical potential
\mu_{B}(t)=\dot{\theta}(t)
.
The rate equation
\dot{n}_{B}+3Hn_{B}=-\Gamma_{B}(n_{B}-n_{B}^{\rm eq})
with
n_{B}^{\rm eq}=(\chi_{B}/6)\mu_{B}T^{2}
yields

\frac{n_{B}}{s}=K\int dt\,W(t)\frac{\mu_{B}(t)}{T(t)},

(1)

where
s=(2\pi^{2}/45)g_{*}T^{3}
,
K\equiv\chi_{B}T^{3}/(6s)=15\chi_{B}/(4\pi^{2}g_{*})\simeq 0.02
–
0.04
, and
W(t)
is a normalized window around freeze-out.
See Appendix A for a derivation of Eq. (
1
) and Appendix B for the overlap illustration.

\hat{\mu}(t)=\hat{\mu}_{0}A(t)\cos(\omega t+\varphi)

\omega\tau_{\rm off}\gg 1

For a representative smooth envelope
A(t)\propto e^{-t/\tau_{\rm off}}
and a narrow violation window, the overlap integral yields an explicit form factor

F(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}},\qquad x\equiv\omega\tau_{\rm off},

(2)

with
F(x)\to 1
for
x\ll 1
and
F(x)\to x^{-1}

IV
Entropy clock

We propose a source tied to thermodynamic irreversibility:

\theta(t)=\epsilon\ln\!\left[\frac{S(t)}{S_{0}}\right],\quad\mu_{B}=\epsilon\,\frac{d\ln S}{dt},

(3)

where
S\equiv a^{3}s
is the comoving entropy.
During reheating,
\dot{S}>0
by the second law, hence
\mu_{B}
in Eq. (
3
) is sign-definite and provides a DC component that bypasses the oscillatory bound.
The sign of
\epsilon
is a UV input (in our sign convention
\epsilon>0
yields an excess of matter).

V
Dynamical realization

A minimal realization is a heavy scalar
\phi
with derivative coupling
(\partial_{\mu}\phi/\Lambda_{*})J_{B}^{\mu}
and a reheating- or dissipation-dependent minimum
\phi_{\rm eq}(t)
set by energy transfer into radiation.
In the tracking regime
m\gg H
,
\phi\simeq\phi_{\rm eq}(t)
and
\mu_{B}=\dot{\phi}/\Lambda_{*}
is controlled by the same non-adiabatic dynamics that produces comoving entropy.
For example, a generic coupling to the stress-energy trace,
\mathcal{L}\supset(\phi/f)\,T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}
, yields
\phi_{\rm eq}\propto\langle T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}\rangle
during reheating (
w\neq 1/3
).
(Further details are given in Appendix F.)

VI
Overlap formulation

Define
\Pi(t)\equiv d\ln S/d\ln a=(1/H)\,d\ln S/dt
. Equation (
1
) becomes

\frac{n_{B}}{s}=K\epsilon\int dt\,W(t)\,\Pi(t)\,\frac{H(t)}{T(t)}.

(4)

The effective overlap

\Pi_{\rm eff}\equiv\int dt\,W(t)\,\Pi(t)

(5)

vanishes if violation occurs only in adiabatic epochs.
In the slow-variation limit,
(n_{B}/s)\simeq K\epsilon\Pi_{\rm eff}(H/T)|_{T_{\rm ov}}
, giving

\epsilon\Pi_{\rm eff}\frac{T_{\rm ov}}{M_{\rm Pl}}\simeq\frac{(n_{B}/s)_{\rm obs}}{1.66\sqrt{g_{*}}\,K}\approx 3\times 10^{-10},

(6)

where
(n_{B}/s)_{\rm obs}\simeq 8.7\times 10^{-11}

Planck:2018vyg
and
M_{\rm Pl}=1.22\times 10^{19}
GeV.

IX
Distinguishing limit

In gravitational baryogenesis
Davoudiasl:2004gf
,
\mu_{B}\propto\dot{R}
is suppressed in radiation domination and can be parametrically small in the sudden-thermalization idealization.
In contrast, the entropy clock depends on
\dot{S}>0
and can yield a nonzero integrated bias provided the temporal overlap condition
\Pi_{\rm eff}\neq 0
holds.
This difference can be used as a discriminator in reheating scenarios approaching rapid thermalization.

X
Falsifiability

Three testable conditions: (i) non-adiabaticity
\dot{S}>0
during baryogenesis; (ii) temporal overlap
\Pi_{\rm eff}\neq 0
; (iii) an independent constraint on reheating (e.g. from GWs) maps to a constraint on
\epsilon\Pi_{\rm eff}
via Eq. (
6
).
In the Weinberg benchmark, the relevant scale is linked to the observable parameter
\bar{m}_{\nu}
.
Big Bang Nucleosynthesis requires reheating to complete above the MeV scale
Cyburt:2015mya
.

XI
Conclusions

We presented a quantitative way to diagnose when baryogenesis from a time-dependent CPT/CP–odd source is efficient, and when it is inevitably suppressed.
The key result is the transfer-function bound: any zero-mean oscillatory source convolved with a smooth freeze-out window is low-pass filtered, implying adiabatic cancellation whenever the source varies faster than the interaction time scale.
We then proposed an “entropy-clock” realization in which the baryon-number bias is tied to irreversible entropy production, yielding a compact overlap formulation in terms of
\Pi_{\rm eff}
and a single characteristic overlap temperature
T_{\rm ov}
.

For model building, the message is practical: successful baryogenesis requires a genuinely non-adiabatic overlap between the effective source and the active epoch of baryon-number violation.
This criterion is falsifiable via the master constraint in Eq. (
6
) and can be stress-tested in concrete realizations (e.g., the minimal benchmark discussed in the main text), where the role of the overlap and the freeze-out profile can be assessed explicitly.

Appendix A
From the rate equation to the overlap formula

Starting from the baryon-number rate equation

\dot{n}_{B}+3Hn_{B}=-\Gamma_{B}\bigl(n_{B}-n_{B}^{\rm eq}\bigr),\qquad n_{B}^{\rm eq}\equiv\frac{\chi_{B}}{6}\,\mu_{B}T^{2},

(9)

and defining
Y_{B}\equiv n_{B}/s
with
s=(2\pi^{2}/45)g_{*}T^{3}
, one finds in linear response
(valid for
\mu_{B}/T\ll 1
)

\dot{Y}_{B}\simeq-\Gamma_{B}\Bigl(Y_{B}-K\frac{\mu_{B}}{T}\Bigr),\qquad K\equiv\frac{15\chi_{B}}{4\pi^{2}g_{*}}\simeq 0.02\text{--}0.04.

(10)

The formal solution is

Y_{B}(t)=K\int^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\Gamma_{B}(t^{\prime})\,e^{-\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}\Gamma_{B}(u)\,du}\,\frac{\mu_{B}(t^{\prime})}{T(t^{\prime})}.

(11)

Defining the normalized violation window

W(t)\equiv\Gamma_{B}(t)\,e^{-\int_{t}^{\infty}\Gamma_{B}(u)\,du},\qquad\int dt\,W(t)=1,

(12)

one obtains Eq. (1) of the main text:

\frac{n_{B}}{s}=K\int dt\,W(t)\frac{\mu_{B}(t)}{T(t)}.

(13)

Appendix B
Entropy clock and the overlap integral
\Pi_{\rm eff}

Let
S\equiv a^{3}s
be the comoving entropy and define

\Pi(t)\equiv\frac{d\ln S}{d\ln a}=\frac{1}{H}\frac{d\ln S}{dt}.

(14)

For the entropy-clock source
\mu_{B}=\epsilon\,d\ln S/dt=\epsilon\,H\,\Pi
,
Eq. (
13
) becomes

\frac{n_{B}}{s}=K\epsilon\int dt\,W(t)\,\Pi(t)\,\frac{H(t)}{T(t)}.

(15)

When
H/T
varies slowly across the window, one may factor it out at an
“overlap temperature”
T_{\rm ov}
and define

\Pi_{\rm eff}\equiv\int dt\,W(t)\,\Pi(t),

(16)

which is
\mathcal{O}(1)
when the entropy-production episode overlaps freeze-out
and vanishes if baryon violation occurs only during adiabatic epochs (
\dot{S}=0
).

Appendix C
Derivation of the toy-model transfer function

For a source
\mu_{B}(t)=\mu_{0}e^{-t/\tau_{\rm off}}\cos(\omega t)
and a narrow violation window centered at
t=0
,
the overlap integral is proportional to

I(\omega)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,e^{-t/\tau_{\rm off}}e^{i\omega t}=\frac{\tau_{\rm off}}{1-i\omega\tau_{\rm off}}.

(17)

The overall amplitude relevant for the suppression is set by
|I(\omega)|
, which yields the dimensionless low-pass factor

F(x)\equiv\frac{|I(\omega)|}{|I(0)|}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}},\qquad x\equiv\omega\tau_{\rm off}.

(18)

This gives
F(x)\to 1
for
x\ll 1
and
F(x)\to x^{-1}
for
x\gg 1
, capturing the parametric adiabatic suppression.

Asymptotics:

•

x\ll 1
:
F(x)=(1+x^{2})^{-1/2}\simeq 1-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}(x^{4})
(no suppression)

•

x\gg 1
:
F(x)\simeq x^{-1}
(adiabatic cancellation / low-pass suppression)

The transition between the unsuppressed and suppressed regimes occurs at
x\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
; for instance
F(1)=1/\sqrt{2}
and
F(x)\simeq x^{-1}
for
x\gg 1
.

Appendix F
Dynamical realization details

A minimal realization uses a heavy scalar
\phi
with:

1.

Derivative coupling to the baryon current:

\mathcal{L}\supset(\partial_{\mu}\phi/\Lambda_{*})J_{B}^{\mu}

2.

Coupling to the stress-energy trace:

\mathcal{L}\supset(\phi/f)T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}

During reheating with equation of state
w\neq 1/3
, the trace

\langle T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}\rangle=\rho-3p\neq 0
sources
\phi
:

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+m^{2}\phi=\langle T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}\rangle/f.

(22)

In the tracking regime (
m\gg H
),
\phi
adiabatically follows the minimum:

\phi_{\rm eq}\simeq\frac{\langle T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}\rangle}{m^{2}f}.

(23)

Then
\mu_{B}=\dot{\phi}/\Lambda_{*}\propto d\langle T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}\rangle/dt
.

Using the entropy production relation
T\dot{S}=a^{3}\Gamma_{\phi}\rho_{\phi}

for perturbative reheating, one obtains

\mu_{B}=\epsilon\,\frac{d\ln S}{dt},\qquad\epsilon\propto\frac{1}{f\Lambda_{*}}\times\mathcal{O}(1).

(24)

The sign of
\epsilon
is determined by the relative signs of the couplings
and sets whether the mechanism produces matter or antimatter.

Appendix G
Gravitational-wave connection

An early matter-dominated reheating stage (before thermalization) can source
a stochastic gravitational-wave background through:

•

Inflaton oscillations (if anisotropic)

•

Preheating resonances

•

Second-order scalar perturbations

Detection of such a background would constrain the reheating temperature
T_{R}

and duration, thereby mapping to a constraint on
\epsilon\Pi_{\rm eff}

via Eq. (6). This provides a potential cross-check independent of the
baryon asymmetry measurement.

For the Weinberg operator benchmark, the overlap temperature is linked to

\bar{m}_{\nu}
via Eq. (8), making the mechanism testable through the combination
of neutrino mass measurements and cosmological probes of reheating.

Abstract
. This paper investigates the long-time dynamics of a nonlocal epidemic model with free boundaries, where a pathogen with density u ⁡ ( t , x ) u(t,x) and the infected humans with density v ⁡ ( t , x ) v(t,x) evolve according to a reaction-diffusion system with nonlocal diffusion over a one dimensional interval [ g ⁡ ( t ) , h ⁡ ( t ) ] [g(t),h(t)] , which represents the epidemic region expanding through its boundaries x = g ⁡ ( t ) x=g(t) and x = h ⁡ ( t ) x=h(t) , known as free boundaries. Such a model with free boundary conditions based on those of Cao et al. [ 4 ] was considered by several works. Inspired by recent works of Feng et al. [ 19 ] and Long et al. [ 27 ] , we propose a new free boundary condition, where the expansion rate of the epidemic region, determined by h ′ ​ ( t ) h^{\prime}(t) and g ′ ​ ( t ) g^{\prime}(t) , is proportional to a linear combination of the outward flux of the pathogen u u through the range boundary (as in [ 4 ] ) and the weighted total population of infected individuals v v within the region (as in [ 27 ] ). We prove that the system under this new free boundary condition is well-posed, and its long-time dynamical behavior is characterized by a spreading-vanishing dichotomy. Moreover, we obtain sharp criteria for this dichotomy, including a sharp threshold in terms of the initial data ( u 0 , v 0 ) (u_{0},v_{0}) ; and by studying a related eigenvalue problem, we also find a sharp threshold in terms of the diffusion rate, which complements related results in Nguyen and Vo [ 30 ] . This is Part 1 1 of a two part series. In Part 2 2 , we will determine the spreading speed of the model when spreading occurs, and for some typical classes of kernel functions, we will obtain the precise rates of accelerated spreading. Introduction

To better understand the spreading of epidemic diseases, various mathematical models have been developed to capture key features of disease transmission dynamics. Among them, one important class focuses on modeling interactions between human populations and their environment. A classical example is the cholera transmission model proposed by Capasso and Paveri-Fontana
[
8
]
in 1979, which describes the faecal-oral transmission mechanism and takes the form:

u^{\prime}(t)=-au(t)+ev(t),\quad v^{\prime}(t)=-bv(t)+G(u(t)),\quad t>0,

(1.1)

where
a
,
b
, and
e
are positive constants. Here,
u(t)
and
v(t)
represent the average densities of the pathogen and the infected human population at time
t
, respectively. The quantity
1/a
denotes the average lifetime of the pathogen in the environment, and
1/b
is the average infection period of infected individuals. The coefficient
e
reflects the multiplicative effect by which the infected human population increases the density of the pathogen. The function
G(u)
characterizes the infection rate of humans and is assumed to satisfy

(G1):

G\in C^{1}([0,\infty]),G(0)=0,G^{\prime}(z)>0
for all
z\geq 0
;

(G2):

\left(\frac{G(z)}{z}\right)^{\prime}<0
for
z>0
and
\lim_{z\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{G(z)}{z}<\frac{ab}{e}
.

A simple example of such a function is
G(z)=\frac{\alpha z}{1+z^{\lambda}}
with
\alpha\in(0,ab/e),\ \lambda\in(0,1]
.
It was proved in
[
8
]
that the basic reproduction number

\mathcal{R}_{0}:=\frac{eG^{\prime}(0)}{ab}

(1.2)

is a key determinant for the dynamics of (
1.1
) (with positive initial value
(u(0),v(0))
). More specifically, if
\mathcal{R}_{0}\leq 1
, then the epidemic eventually dies out and
(u(t),v(t))\rightarrow(0,0)
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. If
\mathcal{R}_{0}>1
, then the epidemic persists and the solution converges to the unique positive equilibrium
(u^{*},v^{*})
, determined by

\frac{G\left(u^{*}\right)}{u^{*}}=\frac{ab}{e}\quad\text{ and }\quad v^{*}=\frac{a}{e}u^{*}.

(1.3)

Subsequent works
[
5
,
7
,
6
,
9
]
extended model (
1.1
) to include spatial movement over a bounded spatial region and investigated threshold parameters that characterize persistence or extinction. The corresponding Cauchy problem (with the entire Euclidean space as the spatial domain of the system) has been studied in
[
35
,
40
]
, where traveling waves of the system are used to estimate the spreading speed of the epidemic.

During the early phase of an epidemic, disease transmission typically occurs within a limited region. As individuals move, the infected area gradually expands. The boundary of this region is usually called the epidemic’s propagation front. However, the Cauchy problem models in
[
35
,
40
]
are not able to provide the precise location of this front. To overcome this limitation, a diffusion model with free boundaries, inspired by the work of Du and Lin
[
13
]
, has been proposed and investigated in several recent works, which has the following form:

\begin{cases}u_{t}=d_{1}u_{xx}-au+ev,&t>0,x\in(g(t),h(t)),\\
v_{t}=d_{2}v_{xx}-bv+G(u),&t>0,x\in(g(t),h(t)),\\
u(t,x)=v(t,x)=0,&t>0,x\in\{g(t),h(t)\},\\
h^{\prime}(t)=-\mu[u_{x}(t,h(t))+\rho v_{x}(t,h(t))],&t>0,\\
g^{\prime}(t)=-\mu[u_{x}(t,g(t))+\rho v_{x}(t,g(t))],&t>0,\\
h(0)=-g(0)=h_{0},&\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\left[-h_{0},h_{0}\right],\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
h(t)
and
g(t)
are the moving boundaries of the infected region
[g(t),h(t)]
, and
d_{1}>0,d_{2}\geq 0,\mu>0,\rho\geq 0
are constants. The interval
\left[-h_{0},h_{0}\right]
represents the initial population range of the species. The equations for
h^{\prime}(t)
and
g^{\prime}(t)
mean that the expanding rate of the infected region is proportional to the spatial gradient of
u
and
v
at the front, which can be deduced from reasonable biological assumptions as in
[
2
]
.

Problem (
1.4
) was first considered by Ahn et al.
[
1
]
for the special case
d_{2}=\rho=0
. They proved that the long-time dynamical behavior of (
1.4
) exhibits a spreading-vanishing dichotomy: either
(g(t),h(t))
remains within a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}
for all
t>0
, and
(u(t,x),v(t,x))
converges uniformly to
(0,0)
as
t\to\infty
(the vanishing case); or
(g(t),h(t))
tends to
(-\infty,+\infty)
as
t\to\infty
, and
(u(t,x),v(t,x))
converges to a unique positive steady state (the spreading case).
Moreover, when spreading occurs in problem (
1.4
), Zhao et al.
[
38
]
showed that there exists a uniquely determined constant
c_{0}>0
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h(t)}{t}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{-g(t)}{t}=c_{0}.

The model (
1.4
) has been further extended to include nonlocal interaction mechanisms
[
24
]
and time delay effects
[
10
]
under the assumption
d_{2}=\rho=0
, as well as the general case with
d_{2}>0
and
\rho\geq 0

[
32
]
.

Recently, Cao et al.
[
4
]
first studied a Fisher-KPP model with nonlocal diffusion and free boundaries, and since then, such problems have attracted considerable attention, leading to many interesting works (see, for example,
[
11
,
16
,
23
]
). In particular, a nonlocal version of model (
1.4
) has been considered, which takes the following form:

\begin{cases}u_{t}=d_{1}\displaystyle\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}J_{1}(x-y)u(t,y)\mathrm{d}y-d_{1}u-au+ev,&t>0,x\in(g(t),h(t)),\\
v_{t}=d_{2}\displaystyle\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}J_{2}(x-y)v(t,y)\mathrm{d}y-d_{2}v-bv+G(u),&t>0,x\in(g(t),h(t)),\\
u(t,x)=v(t,x)=0,&t>0,x\in\{g(t),h(t)\},\\
h^{\prime}(t)=\displaystyle\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}\displaystyle\int_{h(t)}^{\infty}[\mu J_{1}(x-y)u(t,x)+\mu\rho J_{2}(x-y)v(t,x)]\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x,&t>0,\\
g^{\prime}(t)=-\displaystyle\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{g(t)}[\mu J_{1}(x-y)u(t,x)+\mu\rho J_{2}(x-y)v(t,x)]\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x,&t>0,\\
h(0)=-g(0)=h_{0},u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),&|x|\leq h_{0},\end{cases}

(1.5)

where
x=g(t)
and
x=h(t)
are the moving boundaries to be determined together with
u(t,x)
and
v(t,x)
, which are always assumed to be identically
0
for
x\in\mathbb{R}\backslash[g(t),h(t)]
.

The kernel function
J_{i}(x)~(i=1,2)
is assumed to satisfy the following condition

(J):

~J_{i}\in C\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right),~J_{i}(x)=J_{i}(-x)\geq 0,~J_{i}(0)>0,~\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}J_{i}(x)\mathrm{d}x=1,~i=1,2.

Problem (
1.5
) has the advantage over (
1.4
) in that it may capture the spread of epidemics with long-distance dispersal. The free boundary conditions in (
1.5
) can be biologically explained as in
[
4
]
.

When
d_{2}=\rho=0
, Zhao et al.
[
39
]
proved that the long-time behaviour of (
1.5
), similar to that of (
1.4
), exhibits a spreading-vanishing dichotomy, and Du and Ni
[
15
]
further determined the corresponding spreading speed in the spreading case. A striking difference between (
1.5
) and (
1.4
) was identified in
[
15
]
, showing that the spreading in (
1.5
) may have infinite asymptotic speed (known as accelerated spreading) depending on whether the kernel functions satisfy the following condition:

(J1):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}xJ_{i}(x)\mathrm{d}x<+\infty\mbox{ for $i=1$ or 2}.

More precisely, they proved that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h(t)}{t}=-\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{g(t)}{t}=\begin{cases}c_{0}\in(0,\infty)&\text{ if }\textbf{(J1)}\text{ holds},\\
\infty&\text{ if }\textbf{(J1)}~\text{does not hold}.\end{cases}

Subsequently, for the case
d_{2}>0
and
\rho\geq 0
, Chang and Du
[
3
]
studied the long-time dynamics of problem (
1.5
) and determined the spreading speed of the moving fronts, along with the rate of accelerated spreading when
J_{i}(x)\sim|x|^{-\gamma}
for
|x|\gg 1
and
\gamma\in(1,2]
. When the term
ev
is replaced by a more general function
H(v)
, Nguyen and Vo
[
30
]
examined the impact of diffusion and established a sharp threshold for
d=d_{1}=d_{2}
. Further developments have focused on the incorporation of nonlocal interaction terms, where the term
ev
is replaced by
e\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}K(x-y)v(t,y)\mathrm{d}y
. In such a case, when
d_{2}=\rho=0
, the model was considered in
[
37
,
12
]
, while the more general case
d_{2}>0
,
\rho\geq 0
was studied in
[
33
,
34
,
17
]
. Recently, Li et al
[
26
,
25
]
further investigated a nonlocal modification of
G(u)
, replacing it with
G\left(\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}K(x-y)u(t,y)\mathrm{d}y\right)
.

The entire space version of problem (
1.5
), which does not involve any free boundaries, takes the form

\begin{cases}u_{t}=d_{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}J_{1}(x-y)u(t,y)\mathrm{d}y-d_{1}u-au+ev,&t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
v_{t}=d_{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}J_{2}(x-y)v(t,y)\mathrm{d}y-d_{2}v-bv+G(u),&t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(1.6)

which has been investigated by many authors, and a small sample can be found in
[
15
,
20
,
36
]
and the references therein.

To explain the new free boundary condition to be used in this paper, let us note that if we define

W_{J}(x):=\int_{x}^{+\infty}J(y){d}y,

then the free boundary condition in (
1.5
) can be written equivalently as

\begin{cases}\displaystyle h^{\prime}(t)=\mu\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}\big[u(t,x)W_{J_{1}}(h(t)-x)+\rho v(t,x)W_{J_{2}}(h(t)-x)\big]{d}x,\\
\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)=-\mu\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}\big[u(t,x)W_{J_{1}}(h(t)-x)+\rho v(t,x)W_{J_{2}}(h(t)-x)\big]{d}x.\end{cases}

(1.7)

Recently Feng et al.
[
19
]
revisited the locally diffusive Fisher-KPP free boundary model of Du and Lin
[
13
]
, where the Stefan type free boundary condition
h^{\prime}(t)=-\mu u_{x}(t,h(t))
in
[
13
]
was replaced by

h^{\prime}(t)=\mu\int_{0}^{h(t)}u(t,x)W(h(t)-x)\mathrm{d}x,

(1.8)

and the weight function
W(x)
is sign-changing and resembles those used in bird flight models (see, e.g.,
[
29
]
).
This condition is based on the biological assumption that the range boundary’s movement is determined by the weighted total population within the range, independent of the species’ diffusion strategy. More recently,
Long et al.
[
27
]
applied a similar free boundary condition to the nonlocal diffusion model of Cao et al.
[
4
]
, with
W(x)
nonnegative and locally Lipschitz continuous in
[0,\infty)
,
W(0)>0
, but independent of the dispersal kernel governing the spatial movement of the species. Their work shows that the dynamics of the new model are mostly similar to those of the old model in
[
4
]
, while some new propagation phenomena do occur.

Motivated by these works, in this paper, we consider the following variation of (
1.5
):

\begin{cases}u_{t}=d_{1}\displaystyle\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}J_{1}(x-y)u(t,y)\mathrm{d}y-d_{1}u-au+ev,&t>0,x\in(g(t),h(t)),\\
v_{t}=d_{2}\displaystyle\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}J_{2}(x-y)v(t,y)\mathrm{d}y-d_{2}v-bv+G(u),&t>0,x\in(g(t),h(t)),\\
u(t,x)=v(t,x)=0,&t>0,x\in\{g(t),h(t)\},\\
h^{\prime}(t)=\mu\displaystyle\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}\big[u(t,x)W_{J_{1}}(h(t)-x)+\rho v(t,x)W(h(t)-x)\big]\mathrm{d}x,&t>0,\\
g^{\prime}(t)=-\mu\displaystyle\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}\big[u(t,x)W_{J_{1}}(h(t)-x)+\rho v(t,x)W(h(t)-x)\big]\mathrm{d}x,&t>0,\\
h(0)=-g(0)=h_{0},u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),&|x|\leq h_{0},\end{cases}

(1.9)

where the parameters
d_{1},d_{2},a,b,e,\mu,\rho,h_{0}
are given positive constants, and
W
satisfies

(W):

W\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty))
is nonnegative and locally Lipschitz in
[0,\infty)
with
W(0)>0
.

Throughout this paper, we assume that the kernel function
J_{i}(x)~(i=1,2)
satisfies
(J)
and
W
satisfies
(W)
.
The initial functions
u_{0},v_{0}
satisfy

u_{0},v_{0}\in C([-h_{0},h_{0}]),~u(\pm h_{0})=v(\pm h_{0})=0,~u_{0}(x),v_{0}(x)>0~\text{in}~(-h_{0},h_{0}),

(1.10)

and the function
G(u)
satisfies
(G1)
and
(G2)
. Moreover, we always assume

u(t,x)=0,~v(t,x)=0~\text{for}~t\geq 0,~x\notin(g(t),h(t)).

(1.11)

The new free boundary conditions in (
1.9
) seem reasonable from a biological point of view, where the growth rate of
h(t)
is proportional to a linear combination of

\displaystyle\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}u(t,x)W_{J_{1}}(h(t)-x)\mathrm{d}x\mbox{ and }\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}v(t,x)W(h(t)-x)\mathrm{d}x,

with the former representing the total population of pathogens (e.g., cholera bacteria originating from human waste) that move out of the infected region
[g(t),h(t)]
across the right boundary
h(t)
via environmental media such as water, but unlike the situation of the pathogens, it is plausible that the contribution to the advance of
h(t)
from the infected humans is determined by the total population of infected individuals in the region, as in
[
27
]
, expressed by the second term above,
with a weight function
W(x)
independent of the dispersal kernel
J_{2}(x)
. So in (
1.9
) the expansion rate of
h(t)
is proportional to a linear combination of the outward flux of the pathogen
u
through the range boundary and the weighted total population of infected individuals
v
in the infected region.

2.
Global existence and uniqueness, and some other basic results

2.1.
Global existence and uniqueness

For convenience, we introduce the following notations. For any given
h_{0},T>0

\displaystyle\ \frac{d^{\prime}_{1}}{2e}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}J_{1}(x-y)(\phi_{1}(x)-\phi_{1}(y))^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\frac{d^{\prime}_{2}}{2G^{\prime}(0)}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}J_{2}(x-y)(\phi_{2}(x)-\phi_{2}(y))^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle-\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\left[\left(-\frac{a}{e}+\frac{d^{\prime}_{1}}{e}\left(\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}J_{1}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y-1\right)\right)\phi_{1}^{2}(x)+2\phi_{1}(x)\phi_{2}(x)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}+\frac{d^{\prime}_{2}}{G^{\prime}(0)}\left(\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}J_{2}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y-1\right)\phi_{2}^{2}(x)\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ \lambda_{p}(d_{1}^{\prime},d_{2}^{\prime}),

where we have used the fact that

\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}J_{i}(x-y)\phi_{i}^{2}(x)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x<\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\phi_{i}^{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x,~i=1,2.

This proves the monotonicity.

(ii) Thanks to the properties of
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}J_{i}(x-y)\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}J_{i}(x-y)\frac{\phi_{i}^{2}(x)+\phi_{i}^{2}(y)}{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\phi_{i}^{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x,~i=1,2.

For any
{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\textit{{E}}
and
\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{\textit{{E}}}=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}}{\boldsymbol{\phi}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}}{e}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}J_{1}(x-y)\phi_{1}(x)\phi_{1}(y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+\frac{d_{2}}{G^{\prime}(0)}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}J_{2}(x-y)\phi_{2}(x)\phi_{2}(y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\left[\left(-\frac{a}{e}-\frac{d_{1}}{e}\right)\phi_{1}^{2}(x)+2\phi_{1}(x)\phi_{2}(x)+\left(-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}-\frac{d_{2}}{G^{\prime}(0)}\right)\phi_{2}^{2}(x)\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\left(-\frac{a}{e}\phi^{2}_{1}(x)+2\phi_{1}(x)\phi_{2}(x)-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}\phi^{2}_{2}(x)\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\mathbf{A}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}^{T}dx\leq\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\lambda_{\max}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}^{T}dx=\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\lambda_{\max}\left(\phi_{1}^{2}(x)+\phi_{2}^{2}(x)\right)\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{\max},

where we have used

{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}),\ \mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{a}{e}&1\\
1&-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}\end{pmatrix},\ {\boldsymbol{\phi}}\mathbf{A}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}^{T}\leq\lambda_{\max}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}^{T},

and the maximum eigenvalue of the matrix
\mathbf{A}
is easily calculated to be

\lambda_{\max}=\frac{-\frac{a}{e}-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{a}{e}-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}\right)^{2}+4}}{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle\lambda_{p}(d_{1},d_{2})=-\sup_{\|{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\|_{\textit{{E}}}=1}\langle{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}}{\boldsymbol{\phi}},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\rangle\geq-\lambda_{\max}=\frac{\frac{a}{e}+\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}-\sqrt{\left(\frac{a}{e}-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}\right)^{2}+4}}{2}.

(2.19)

On the other hand,
let

\tilde{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}=(\tilde{\phi_{1}},\tilde{\phi_{2}})=\left(\frac{m}{\sqrt{(L_{2}-L_{1})(m^{2}+n^{2})}},\frac{n}{\sqrt{(L_{2}-L_{1})(m^{2}+n^{2})}}\right)

with

n=2,\ m=-\frac{a}{e}-\frac{d_{1}}{e}-\left(-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}-\frac{d_{2}}{G^{\prime}(0)}\right)+\sqrt{\left[\left(-\frac{a}{e}-\frac{d_{1}}{e}\right)-\left(-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}-\frac{d_{2}}{G^{\prime}(0)}\right)\right]^{2}+4}.

Then clearly,

\|\tilde{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\|_{\textit{{E}}}=1
,
and

\displaystyle\lambda_{p}(d_{1},d_{2})\leq

\displaystyle-\langle{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}},\tilde{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left[\frac{d_{1}}{e}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}J_{1}(x-y)\frac{m^{2}}{(L_{2}-L_{1})(m^{2}+n^{2})}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right.

\displaystyle\left.\ \ \ \ \ \ +\frac{d_{2}}{G^{\prime}(0)}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}J_{2}(x-y)\frac{n^{2}}{(L_{2}-L_{1})(m^{2}+n^{2})}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right]

\displaystyle-\left[\int_{L_{1}}^{L_{2}}\left(\left(-\frac{a}{e}-\frac{d_{1}}{e}\right)\frac{m^{2}}{(L_{2}-L_{1})(m^{2}+n^{2})}+2\frac{mn}{(L_{2}-L_{1})(m^{2}+n^{2})}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\left(-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}-\frac{d_{2}}{G^{\prime}(0)}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{(L_{2}-L_{1})(m^{2}+n^{2})}\right)\mathrm{d}x\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(-\frac{a}{e}-\frac{d_{1}}{e}\right)m^{2}+2mn+\left(-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}-\frac{d_{2}}{G^{\prime}(0)}\right)n^{2}}{m^{2}+n^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(-\frac{a}{e}-\frac{d_{1}}{e}\right)+\left(-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}-\frac{d_{2}}{G^{\prime}(0)}\right)-\sqrt{\left[\left(-\frac{a}{e}-\frac{d_{1}}{e}\right)-\left(-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}-\frac{d_{2}}{G^{\prime}(0)}\right)\right]^{2}+4}}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{a}{e}+\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}+\frac{d_{1}}{e}+\frac{d_{2}}{G^{\prime}(0)}-\sqrt{\left(\frac{a}{e}-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}+\frac{d_{1}}{e}-\frac{d_{2}}{G^{\prime}(0)}\right)^{2}+4}}{2}.

(2.20)

Letting
d_{1},d_{2}\to 0
in
(
2.3
)
we deduce

\limsup_{(d_{1},d_{2})\to(0,0)}\lambda_{p(d_{1},d_{2})}\leq\frac{\frac{a}{e}+\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}-\sqrt{\left(\frac{a}{e}-\frac{b}{G^{\prime}(0)}\right)^{2}+4}}{2}.

Combining this with
(
2.19
)
, we arrive at the desired conclusion (ii).

(iii) The detailed proof of this result can be found in

3.1.

Lemma 3.1
.

If

, then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u\|_{C([g(t),h(t)])}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\|v\|_{C([g(t),h(t)])}=0

and

\lambda_{p}(g_{\infty},h_{\infty})\geq 0,

where
\lambda_{p}(g_{\infty},h_{\infty})
is the principal eigenvalue of (
2.13
) with
[L_{1},L_{2}]=[g_{\infty},h_{\infty}]
.

Proof
.
We first prove that
\lambda_{p}(g_{\infty},h_{\infty})\geq 0
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
\lambda_{p}(g_{\infty},h_{\infty})<0
. Then, by Proposition
2.12
and assumptions
(J)
and
(W)
, there exists a sufficiently large
T_{0}>0
such that

\lambda_{p}(g(T),h(T))<0,\quad|g(T)-g_{\infty}|<\varepsilon_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad|h(T)-h_{\infty}|<\varepsilon_{1},\quad T>T_{0},

where
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,h_{0}/4)
is small enough such that

J_{1}(x)>0\quad\mbox{for }x\in[-4\varepsilon_{1},4\varepsilon_{1}]\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{h_{0}}W(x)\mathrm{d}x>0.

According to Proposition
2.14
, the solution
(u_{1}(t,x),v_{1}(t,x))
of the following problem

\begin{cases}(u_{1})_{t}=d_{1}\displaystyle\int_{g(T)}^{h(T)}J_{1}(x-y)u_{1}(t,y)\mathrm{d}y-d_{1}u_{1}-au_{1}+ev_{1},&t>T,\ x\in[g(T),h(T)],\\
(v_{1})_{t}=d_{2}\displaystyle\int_{g(T)}^{h(T)}J_{2}(x-y)v_{1}(t,y)\mathrm{d}y-d_{2}v_{1}-bv_{1}+G(u_{1}),&t>T,\ x\in[g(T),h(T)],\\
u_{1}(T,x)=u(T,x),\quad v_{1}(T,x)=v(T,x),&x\in[g(T),h(T)],\end{cases}

(3.1)

converges to the unique positive steady state
(\tilde{u}_{1},\tilde{v}_{1})
of (
3.1
) uniformly in
[g(T),h(T)]
as
t\to+\infty
. Moreover, a simple comparison argument gives

u(t,x)\geq u_{1}(t,x),\quad v(t,x)\geq v_{1}(t,x)\quad\text{for}~t>T~\text{and}~x\in[g(T),h(T)].

Thus, there exists
T_{1}>T
such that

u(t,x)\geq\frac{1}{2}\tilde{u}_{1}(x)>0,\quad v(t,x)\geq\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{1}(x)>0\quad\text{for }t>T_{1}\text{ and }x\in[g(T),h(T)].

Now, we denote

\delta_{1}:=\inf_{x\in[-4\varepsilon_{1},4\varepsilon_{1}]}J_{1}(x)>0,\quad\delta_{2}:=\inf_{x\in[g(T),h(T)]}\tilde{u}_{1}(x)>0,\quad\delta_{3}:=\inf_{x\in[g(T),h(T)]}\tilde{v}_{1}(x)>0.

Then for
t>T_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\mu\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}\int_{h(t)}^{+\infty}J_{1}(x-y)u(t,x)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x+\mu\rho\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}v(t,x)W(h(t)-x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\mu\int_{g(T)}^{h(T)}\int_{h(t)}^{h(t)+2\varepsilon_{1}}J_{1}(x-y)u(t,x)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x+\mu\rho\int_{g(T)}^{h(T)}v(t,x)W(h(t)-x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu\int_{g(T)}^{h(T)}\int_{h(t)}^{h(t)+2\varepsilon_{1}}J_{1}(x-y)\tilde{u}_{1}(x)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\mu\rho\int_{g(T)}^{h(T)}\tilde{v}_{1}(x)W(h(t)-x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu\delta_{2}\int_{h(t)-2\varepsilon_{1}}^{h(t)-\varepsilon_{1}}\int_{h(t)}^{h(t)+2\varepsilon_{1}}J_{1}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\mu\rho\delta_{3}\int_{h(t)-h(T)}^{h(t)-g(T)}W(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq\mu\varepsilon^{2}\delta_{1}\delta_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mu\rho\delta_{3}\int_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{h_{0}}W(y)\mathrm{d}y>0,

where we have used the fact
\left[h(t)-2\varepsilon_{1},h(t)-\varepsilon_{1}\right]\subset[g(T),h(T)]
for
t\geq T
.
This implies
h_{\infty}=+\infty
, which contradicts the assumption that
h_{\infty}-g_{\infty}<+{\infty}
. Therefore,
\lambda_{p}(g_{\infty},h_{\infty})\geq 0
.

Next, we show that
(u(t,x),v(t,x))\to(0,0)
uniformly in
[g(t),h(t)]
as
t\to\infty
. Denote by
(u_{2}(t,x),v_{2}(t,x))
the unique solution of

\begin{cases}(u_{2})_{t}=d_{1}\displaystyle\int_{g_{\infty}}^{h_{\infty}}J_{1}(x-y)u_{2}(t,y)\mathrm{d}y-d_{1}u_{2}-au_{2}+ev_{2},&t>0,\,x\in[g_{\infty},h_{\infty}],\\
(v_{2})_{t}=d_{2}\displaystyle\int_{g_{\infty}}^{h_{\infty}}J_{2}(x-y)v_{2}(t,y)\mathrm{d}y-d_{2}v_{2}-bv_{2}+G(u_{2}),&t>0,\,x\in[g_{\infty},h_{\infty}],\\
u_{2}(0,x)=u_{20}(x),\quad v_{2}(0,x)=v_{20}(x),&x\in[g_{\infty},h_{\infty}],\end{cases}

where
u_{20}(x)=u_{0}(x)
,
v_{20}(x)=v_{0}(x)
if
x\in[-h_{0},h_{0}]
and
u_{20}(x)=v_{20}(x)=0
if
|x|>h_{0}
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have

0\leq u(t,x)\leq u_{2}(t,x),\quad 0\leq v(t,x)\leq v_{2}(t,x)\quad\text{for }t>0\text{ and }x\in[g(t),h(t)].

Since
\lambda_{p}(g_{\infty},h_{\infty})\geq 0
, Proposition
2.14
infers that
(u_{2}(t,x),v_{2}(t,x))\rightarrow(0,0)
uniformly for
x\in[g_{\infty},h_{\infty}]
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, and hence

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}(u(t,x),v(t,x))=(0,0)\quad\text{uniformly for }x\in[g(t),h(t)].

This completes the proof. ∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\mathcal{R}_{0}\leq 1
, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|u\|_{C([g(t),h(t)])}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|v\|_{C([g(t),h(t)])}=0.

Proof.
Let
(\bar{u}(t),\bar{v}(t))
be the solution of the following ODE problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &u^{\prime}(t)=-au+ev,&t>0,\\
&v^{\prime}(t)=-bv+G(u),&t>0,\\
&u(0)=\|u_{0}\|_{\infty},v(0)=\|v_{0}\|_{\infty}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.2)

If
\mathcal{R}_{0}=\frac{eG^{\prime}(0)}{ab}<1
, it then follows from
[
8
]
that the solution
(0,0)

\mathcal{R}_{0}=1
, then we define

where
\alpha>0
is a constant to be determined.
Therefore,
V(t)\geq 0
for
t\geq 0
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle V^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle\ \alpha\bar{u}^{\prime}(t)+\bar{v}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \alpha\left[-a\bar{u}+e\bar{v}\right]-b\bar{v}+G(\bar{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha a\bar{u}+\left[\alpha e\bar{v}-b\bar{v}\right]+G(\bar{u}).

; then

Moreover, by the comparison principle (Lemma

\displaystyle=\mu\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}\int_{h(t)}^{+\infty}J_{1}(x-y)u(t,x)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x+\mu\rho\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}v(t,x)W(h(t)-x)\mathrm{d}x

{\rm(i)}\ \ J_{1}(x)>0
in
\mathbb{R}
or
{\rm(ii)}\ \ W(x)>0
in
[0,2L^{*}]
,

then there exists
\bar{\sigma}>0
depending on
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
such that spreading happens for (
1.9
) if
\sigma>\bar{\sigma}
.

Proof
. Arguing indirectly we assume that
h_{\infty}-g_{\infty}<+\infty
for any
\sigma>0
. By Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
2.12
, we know that

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|u\|_{C([g(t),h(t)])}=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|v\|_{C([g(t),h(t)])}=0
and
h_{\infty}-g_{\infty}\leq 2L^{*}
.

If
J_{1}(x)>0
in
\mathbb{R}
, then for any
t>0
and
x\in(g(t),h(t))
,

W_{J_{1}}(h(t)-x)=\int_{h(t)-x}^{\infty}J(z)\mathrm{d}z\geq\int^{\infty}_{2L^{*}}J(z)\mathrm{d}z=:\varrho_{1}>0.

It follows that

\dfrac{1}{\mu}h^{\prime}(t)\geq\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}u(t,x)W_{J_{1}}(h(t)-x)\mathrm{d}x\geq\varrho_{1}\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x.

Hence

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{g(s)}^{h(s)}u(s,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{h_{\infty}-h_{0}}{\mu\varrho_{1}}=:\varrho_{2}.

A straightforward calculation yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}u_{t}(t,x)\mathrm{d}x+h^{\prime}(t)u(t,x)\big|_{(t,h(t))}+g^{\prime}(t)u(t,x)\big|_{(t,g(t))}

\displaystyle=\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}\left[d_{1}\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}J_{1}(x-y)u(t,y)\mathrm{d}y-d_{1}u-au+ev\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d_{1}}{\mu}\left[\mu\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}J_{1}(x-y)u(t,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x-\mu\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\right]-a\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x.

(3.6)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle-[h^{\prime}(t)-g^{\prime}(t)]=

\displaystyle\ \mu\left(\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}J_{1}(x-y)u(t,x)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x-\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle-\mu\rho\left(\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}v(t,x)W(h(t)-x)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}v(t,x)W(x-g(t))\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \mu\left(\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}J_{1}(x-y)u(t,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x-\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\right).

Together with (
3.2
), we see

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\geq-\frac{d_{1}}{\mu}[h^{\prime}(t)-g^{\prime}(t)]-a\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore, we have

\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\geq\sigma\int_{-h_{0}}^{h_{0}}\psi_{1}(x)\mathrm{d}x-2\frac{d_{1}}{\mu}L^{*}-a\int_{0}^{t}\int_{g(s)}^{h(s)}u(s,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s.

Summarizing the above discussions and letting
t\to\infty
we obtain

\sigma\leq\frac{1}{\int_{-h_{0}}^{h_{0}}\psi_{1}(x)\mathrm{d}x}\left(a\varrho_{2}+2\frac{d_{1}}{\mu}L^{*}\right),

which contradicts the unboundedness of
\sigma
. This proves the lemma when (i) holds.

If (ii) holds, namely
W(x)>0
over
[0,2L^{*}]
, then there exists
\tilde{\varrho}_{1}>0
such that
W(x)\geq\tilde{\varrho}_{1}
for
x\in[0,2L^{*}]
. It follows that, for any
t>0
and
x\in(g(t),h(t))
,

\dfrac{1}{\mu}h^{\prime}(t)\geq\rho\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}v(t,x)W(h(t)-x)\mathrm{d}x\geq\rho\tilde{\varrho}_{1}\int_{g(t)}^{h(t)}v(t,x)\mathrm{d}x.

Hence

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{g(s)}^{h(s)}v(s,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{h_{\infty}-h_{0}}{\mu\rho\tilde{\varrho}_{1}}=:\tilde{\varrho}_{2}.

From this we can argue analogously as in case (i) above to deduce a contradiction.
The proof is now complete. ∎

Lemma 3.14

3.3.

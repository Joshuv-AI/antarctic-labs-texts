Relating aqueous to vitreous humor proteomics for identification of reliable biomarkers in proliferative diabetic retinopathy
Authors: Ingeborg Klaassen, Michael WT Tanck, Holly J Duncan, Elif Yildiz, Reinier O Schlingemann, David HW Steel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to identify aqueous humor (AQ) proteins that correlate with vitreous humor (VH) levels in proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR). Using paired VH and AQ samples from 41 patients undergoing vitrectomy for PDR, we measured 92 protein biomarkers via a high-throughput immunoassay. We assessed Spearman correlations between AQ and VH levels for each protein and associations with PDR severity parameters. Thirty-two proteins showed positive AQ-VH correlations. A stepwise selection process, based on detectability, consistency and correlation strength narrowed this down to 21 proteins. An exploratory analysis of associations with disease severity parameters suggested that of these proteins, CXCL-10, IL-8, and MCP-4 were most strongly associated with fibrosis, while GAL-9 and PGF were most strongly associated with the extent of neovascularization, supporting the potential of these proteins as useful biomarkers. In conclusion, 21 AQ proteins show significant correlation with patient-matched VH levels in PDR patients, indicating that relative patient-level differences in AQ reflect relative differences in VH for these proteins. This methodological framework provides a foundation for future studies aiming to identify diagnostic or treatment stratification biomarkers for PDR and related complications such as diabetic macular edema.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-55988-7.Keywords: Vitreous and aqueous proteome, Retina, Vitrectomy, Intravitreal, Liquid biopsies, HumansSubject terms: Biochemistry, Biomarkers, Diseases, Medical research

Introduction

Omics of liquid biopsy by aqueous humor (AQ) sampling has been advocated as a potential biomarker source for retinal disease, particularly in potentially predicting treatment response as part of personalized medicine in people with diabetic macular edema and other retinal conditions treated with intravitreal injections1,2. Although the protein signature of the vitreous humor (VH) would instinctively give the most valuable information about ongoing processes in the retina, taking biopsies from the vitreous is more invasive and normally only possible by vitrectomy. The less invasive nature of AQ sampling makes it a clinically attractive alternative, but this requires rigorous validation. For AQ to be applied clinically, its biomarker profile must be systematically correlated with VH data to confirm it serves as a faithful proxy for the posterior segment environment3.

A number of studies have compared protein levels between aqueous and vitreous humor, most with small sample sizes or by exploring a small panel of proteins, showing good or no correlations depending on the proteins analyzed4–14. While in the past analysis options were limited due to the small AQ sample volume, the development of more sensitive and specific technology platforms have dramatically improved the possibilities15. In a validation study against other multiplex assays, the Olink platform, based on the Proximity Extension Assay (PEA) method, yielded the most reliable and sensitive results from less than 2 µL of VH16. In the largest study of its kind using the Olink platform, Wilson et al.17 analyzed 812 proteins shared between AQ and VH across 58 subjects with various retinal diseases. Their analysis identified a substantial number of proteins with strong and reliable AQ-VH correlations. However, a limitation of this study was that a heterogeneous group of patients was included.

Proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) is a vision-threatening complication where vitrectomy is frequently indicated18–22. Although pre-operative anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) antibodies such as bevacizumab, ranibizumab and aflibercept are used to optimize surgical outcomes23,24, a substantial proportion of patients do not achieve clinically meaningful improvements in visual acuity due to a variety of causes including persistent disease activity with progressive ischemia, recurrent traction and hemorrhage20,25,26.

To better understand the pathobiology and treatment response in PDR, we conducted a comparative proteomic analysis of paired AQ and VH samples. We employed the Olink Immuno-Oncology T96 panel, chosen for its coverage of proteins previously linked to PDR in VH27,28, and its applicability in ocular fluids16, to quantify 92 biomarkers. Our primary objective was to identify which proteins show significant correlation between paired AQ and VH levels within PDR patients, a necessary first condition if AQ is to be considered as a potential surrogate for VH in future research. A secondary objective was to explore associations between AQ protein levels and clinically recognizable disease features (e.g., fibrosis, neovascularization extent). We directly compared protein levels in AQ and VH from the same patients to assess correlation between the two compartments. Finally, we investigated the association between AQ protein levels and a variety of clinically recognizable features. These findings highlight the potential of AQ-based biomarkers for non-invasive diagnosis and prediction of treatment response in PDR.

Results

Patient characteristics

Table 1 summarizes the baseline demographic, systemic, and ophthalmic characteristics of the 41 patients included in the study. The median age was 52.0 years, and 41% of patients were female. Regarding diabetes type, 68% had Type 1 diabetes, while the remaining patients had Type 2 diabetes managed with diet (2%), oral tablets (10%), or insulin (20%). The mean duration of diabetes was 25.8 ± 11.0 years, and the median HbA1c level was 74.7 mmol/mol.

Ophthalmic evaluation showed a median pre-operative visual acuity of 0.8 logMAR, which improved post-operatively to 0.3 logMAR. The primary indication for vitrectomy was macular traction in 27% of patients and vitreous hemorrhage in 73%. On examination, 41% of eyes were classified as having active PDR, while 59% were inactive. Preretinal hemorrhage was present in 32% of eyes. Tractional retinal detachment was absent in 51% of eyes, involved the macula in 15%, and was extra-macular in 34%.

Compared to non-diabetic controls (n = 20), 70 proteins had significantly higher levels in the vitreous of PDR patients. These findings persisted after correction for sex and age, even though unadjusted age was significantly higher in the PDR group (71.5 vs. 52.0 years in PDR, p < 0.001; Supplemental Table 1).

Correlations of protein levels in aqueous and vitreous humor

We first visualized the raw NPX values of all 92 detected proteins across all VH and AQ samples (Fig. 1). Columns are arranged as AQ then VH for each patient to preserve within‑patient pairing. As expected, most proteins showed higher absolute NPX levels in VH than in AQ (darker orange columns in VH samples). However, the heatmap also reveals that a subset of proteins (upper rows) show a reversed pattern (higher NPX in AQ than VH) across many patients. No statistical claims about correlation are drawn from this figure. Instead, we next performed patient‑level correlation analysis to formally test which proteins show concordance between AQ and VH levels across the cohort. Among the 92 proteins, 32 (including MCP-1, IL-8, and PGF) showed a strong correlation (Spearman correlation coefficient (ρ) > 0.600) between AQ and VH levels, all of which remained significant after Bonferroni correction (P = 0.05/92) (Table 2). ANGPT2, VEGFA, and IL-6 were among the 29 moderately correlated proteins (0.400 < ρ < 0.600). The remaining 31 proteins (including IL-10 and TNF) exhibited weak or negative correlations (ρ < 0.400). No association was found between AQ-VH protein correlations and protein length, molecular weight, isoelectric point, net charge, aliphatic index, or Grand Average of Hydropathicity (GRAVY) (Fig. 2).

We also examined the correlation between AQ and VH protein levels using ratios and coefficients of variation (CoV). Proteins with a strong Spearman correlation generally exhibited low inter-subject variation, whereas weakly correlated proteins tended to show high variation (Table 2). However, the aqueous-to-vitreous protein ratios were not associated with either the Spearman correlation coefficients or the percentage of variation.

To explore these differential protein correlations between AQ and VH, we hypothesized the existence of a concentration gradient spanning from the vitreous to the anterior chamber. We calculated the difference between VH and AQ protein levels on a per-patient basis and plotted their median distribution in two groups: proteins with a ρ > 0.6 (green-blue) and those with ρ < 0.6 (pink) (Fig. 3A). Proteins with a strong AQ-VH correlation (ρ > 0.6) showed a distribution centered at positive VH-minus-AQ values, indicating they have higher protein levels in VH than in AQ. In contrast, weakly correlated proteins (ρ < 0.6) displayed a different distribution without this pattern. To further visualize this relationship, we plotted all VH-AQ differences against the Spearman’s ρ values (Fig. 3B). This approach confirmed a positive association, with larger positive VH-AQ differences associated with higher ρ values. To quantify this difference, we compared the median VH-AQ differences and absolute protein levels between groups (Fig. 3C). In all three comparisons shown in Fig. 3C, strongly correlated proteins (ρ > 0.6) showed significantly larger VH-AQ differences and significantly higher protein levels in both compartments (p < 0.0001).

To identify the most suitable biomarker protein candidates in AQ samples, representative of VH levels, we implemented a stepwise selection process based on four key criteria: (1) Detectability - proteins with measurable levels above the limit of detection (LOD) in all AQ samples; (2) Directional gradient - proteins demonstrating higher concentrations in VH than AQ; (3) Correlation strength - AQ-VH Spearman’s ρ > 0.500 (with relaxed stringency for VEGFA to ensure its inclusion); and (4) Consistency - coefficient of variation (CoV) ≤ 20% for NPX levels of AQ: VH ratios. This rigorous selection yielded 21 candidate proteins (Fig. 4A; Table 3, Supplemental Fig. 1).

Comparison with previously published cohort

We used the data of Wilson et al.17. as a validation cohort. We found that the 54 proteins in common with their study showed a good agreement in Spearman’s ρ values between VH and AQ samples, with 45 proteins having a similar direction and magnitude (Supplemental Fig. 2, Supplemental Table 2).

Correlation of protein levels in AQ and VH to clinical variables

Beyond the primary objectives of this study, and as proof of concept, we conducted an exploratory analysis to determine whether the 21 selected proteins could serve as diagnostic or predictive biomarkers, assessing their correlations with clinical variables using covariate-adjusted ordinal logistic regression. Most clinical variables were associated with age (i.e. New vessels elsewhere (NVE), NVE with fibrosis, New vessels at optic disc (NVD), NVD with fibrosis, NV extend, diabetic retinopathy (DR) activity, traction retinal detachment (TRD)). Other associations found were with prior amount PRP (NVE, NVE fibrosis, NV extent), indication for vitrectomy (NVD fibrosis, post-operative visual acuity (VA), TRD), glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1c) (DR activity) or gender (DR activity, pre-retinal hemorrhage, pre-operative VA). No associations were found with diabetes mellitus (DM) type, duration of DM. While others have reported lens status-related protein differences29, only MMP-12 in AQ showed Bonferroni-corrected significant variation between phakic and pseudophakic eyes (P < 0.0001; Fig. 4B). PDCD1 (AQ, P < 0.001) and IFN-γ (VH, P < 0.001) showed compartment-specific differences (Fig. 4B). However, when analyzing AQ: VH ratio or VH-AQ difference, 12 out of the 21 selected proteins showed a nominally significant influence of lens status (p < 0.05) (Supplemental Table 3). Of these 12, four passed the Bonferroni threshold (p < 0.002): CAIX, CXCL9, MCP1 and MCP4. MMP-12 correlated with age (AQ: ρ = 0.517; VH: ρ = 0.547) but not after Bonferroni-correction. Only HGF (ρ = 0.442) and HO-1 (ρ = 0.438) in AQ showed Bonferroni-significant correlations with preoperative VA (p < 0.0005). No other clinically relevant associations were found.

A meaningful biomarker should exhibit a consistent correlation with PDR-related clinical variables in both VH and AQ samples. For clinical practicality, we combined NVD and NVE into a single measure, using the mean of their severity stratifications (0–3) for analysis with ordinal logistic regression. The same approach was applied to NVD-associated fibrosis and NVE-associated fibrosis. In addition, the extent of neovascularization and the level of TRD were analyzed. Proteins in AQ and VH with significant associations (p < 0.05) are presented in Table 4, along with their odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (95% CI). There were six associations Bonferroni significant: CXCL-9 in VH and AQ for fibrosis; CCL3, CXCL-10, IL-8 in VH for fibrosis and GAL-9 for extent of neovascularization. All other associations did not survive multiple testing correction. Several proteins correlated with multiple clinical variables, but some exhibited unique associations: CXCL-10, IL-8 and MCP-4 were specifically linked to fibrosis, whereas GAL-9 and PGF were exclusively associated with the extent of neovascularization. A selection of proteins per variable is presented in box plots over ordinal categories (Fig. 3C). A full list of the OR of the proteins per clinical variable is given in Supplemental Table 4.

As proteins interact in networks rather than in isolation27, we used multiple ordinal logistic regression with the LASSO-method (Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator), to investigate whether multiple proteins in AQ were independently associated with the clinical variables (adjusted for co-variates). When restricted to the 21-protein panel, the model detected CD27 as an independent predictor for neovascularization. For fibrosis and extent of neovascularization, no significant proteins were identified in AQ samples. For TRD, IL-8 and MCP-2 were detected.

Discussion

In the present study, we analyzed paired aqueous and vitreous humor samples from 41 patients undergoing vitrectomy surgery for PDR. This clinical setting provided a unique opportunity to collect both samples simultaneously. As the largest study to date of this nature focused on a single disease stage, the primary aim was to determine whether AQ protein levels reflect those in the VH, a relationship reported inconsistently in the existing literature.

Spearman rank correlation analysis showed that only 32 of the 92 proteins analyzed were strongly correlated (ρ > 0.600) between the two humor types. Good correlations were not dependent on physiochemical properties of these proteins, but rather by their expression levels and antibody-dependent detection limits. We chose the Onco-Immunology T96 Olink panel based on its strong overlap with vitreous proteins implicated in PDR by our prior work and other studies, as well as its prior validation for use with ocular fluids16. While this provided a targeted approach, a recognized limitation is that the panel does not encompass the full spectrum of PDR-relevant proteins and includes some targets with less established roles in the disease.

Our findings suggest that, in general, AQ protein levels cannot by default be regarded as representative of VH levels. However, for the 32 proteins that did show strong correlation, AQ concentrations may serve as a valid reflection of the protein concentrations in vitreous. To further identify clinically useful biomarkers, we applied a stepwise selection approach based on the following criteria: (1) detectability, (2) directional gradient, (3) correlation strength and (4) consistency. Regarding detectability, only proteins with measurable levels above the LOD in 100% of AQ samples were included. Since the LOD is antibody- and platform-dependent, proteins that were potentially present but undetectable due to assay limitations were excluded.

For the directional gradient, a posterior-to-anterior diffusion gradient was assumed, and proteins exhibiting higher concentrations in the VH than in AQ were prioritized. Consistent with this model, proteins with elevated intravitreal levels were reliably detected in the AQ, demonstrating a strong intercompartmental correlation. This phenomenon can be explained by diffusion along a concentration gradient, where proteins passively move from the posterior (vitreous) to the anterior (aqueous) segment, facilitated by aqueous humor dynamics. Aqueous humor is produced by the ciliary processes in the posterior chamber, flows through the pupil into the anterior chamber, and is ultimately drained via the trabecular meshwork and Schlemm’s canal. When intravitreal protein levels are sufficiently high, this slow, passive transport accompanying the flow results in measurable protein concentrations in the aqueous humor. This proposed posterior-to-anterior diffusion is further supported by the lens’s role as a structural barrier. The intact crystalline lens in phakic eyes restricts communication between the two compartments, predicting a positive but potentially attenuated correlation. Conversely, the absence of the lens in aphakic or pseudophakic eyes would be expected to facilitate more direct diffusion, potentially strengthening the observed correlation between vitreous and aqueous protein levels. Our data, which account for lens status, are consistent with this anatomical and physiological framework. Furthermore, these proteins in general also showed the highest correlation strength. To select candidate biomarker proteins, we applied a Spearman correlation threshold (ρ > 0.500) ensuring the inclusion of key mediators like VEGFA, a major player in DR. This broader cutoff added two proteins, GAL-9 (ρ = 0.540) and MCP-2 (ρ = 0.526), compared to the stricter ρ > 0.600 threshold.

Finally, we selected those candidates that were consistent between all patient samples with a coefficient of variation (CoV) ≤ 20% for NPX levels of AQ: VH ratios. To be able to use aqueous taps as a biomarker or diagnostic tool, individual variation in AQ: VH ratios should be minimal to enable accurate assessment of disease stage and treatment decisions. Our study shows that, while overall correlations between aqueous and vitreous protein levels exist, they do not always hold true at the individual level due to inter-individual variation, which limits the ability to infer vitreous levels from a single aqueous tap in all cases. This discrepancy may be influenced by differences in disease state, specific binding properties that retain proteins in the vitreous, or elevated clearance rates, factors that warrant further investigation in future studies.

A number of studies have compared protein levels between aqueous and vitreous humor, most with small sample sizes or by exploring a small panel of proteins. These studies have shown good or no correlations depending on the proteins analyzed4–12, or have simply assessed the presence or absence of proteins in both compartments using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS)13. In another study using LC-MS, of the 323 proteins identified in both the AQ and VH, only 12 (3.96%) were significantly differentially-expressed between the two ocular chamber. In this study ratio’s between VH and AQ were provided, but no correlation analysis was performed14. A study by Ecker et al.11 compared the levels of 34 proteins in AQ and VH in 11 eyes from patients undergoing vitrectomy for a macular hole or retinal detachment. Among the proteins that overlapped with our study, VEGFA and IL-6 showed a similar moderate correlation. In line with our findings, no correlation was observed for IL-10, TNF, and VEGFR2. HO-1 and IL-8 showed no correlation in their study, whereas in our study, a strong correlation was observed. One possible explanation for this discrepancy would be the differences in disease type. Another larger study of Wilson et al.17, including 58 eyes with diverse retinal diseases, performed a large screening of proteins using multiple arrays of the Olink Target 96 platform and found 812 proteins of which 369 showed a Spearman’s ρ higher than 0.600. Furthermore, we found that 45 of 54 proteins in common with their study showed a good agreement in Spearman’s ρ values. Thus, we observed that most correlations between AQ and VH protein levels aligned well with earlier studies, indicating the consistency of these findings.

As an exploratory analysis, we also attempted to correlate protein levels with clinical features. Several correlations between vitreous protein levels and disease parameters have been suggested previously17,27,28, but relatively little is known about aqueous protein levels and their association with disease parameters. If aqueous protein levels can be correlated with disease parameters, and preferably also with disease progression, this would provide a minimally invasive method for determining disease stage or guiding treatment options. In our study, we identified several proteins in the vitreous and/or aqueous humor of which levels correlated with clinical parameters such as neovascularization and fibrosis. Only one protein (CXCL-9 in fibrosis) demonstrated a Bonferroni-significant correlation in aqueous humor. CXCL-9, a CXCR3 ligand, exerts context-dependent pro- or anti-fibrotic effects depending on organ and cell type. For example, it is anti-fibrotic in the liver32. In proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR), vitreous CXCL-9 is significantly elevated compared to non-diabetic controls33. However, no direct evidence shows that CXCL-9 alone drives retinal fibrosis or epiretinal membrane formation in diabetes34–36. Rather, it appears to function within a broader chemokine network mediating endothelial activation and leukocyte trafficking. Any contribution to fibrotic membrane formation in PDR is likely indirect, through chronic inflammation and microvascular injury. Certain proteins showed exclusive associations (CXCL-10, IL-8, and MCP-4 with fibrosis; GAL-9 and PGF with neovascularization extent), while others exhibited multiple associations with clinical variables, potentially reflecting an association with general disease progression (e.g., CXCL-9, TNFRSF12A). Confounding factors, comorbidities, or interdependencies among proteins, which often function in networks, may have influenced these findings, making it challenging to identify independent predictors. For most clinical parameters, we accounted for or excluded confounding effects from variables such as age and panretinal photocoagulation (PRP) extent. Previous studies have suggested that lens status may be a confounding factor29, but we could only confirm this for MMP-12. Furthermore, our current analysis of the 21-protein panel may have missed stronger correlations with other clinical grading scales.

A primary objective of this study was to establish a robust foundation for aqueous humor proteomics by defining a core set of proteins that reliably reflect the vitreous environment in PDR. While we successfully identified this panel, we did not observe strong correlations between these protein levels and cross-sectional clinical features of disease severity. We attribute this lack of association primarily to a fundamental limitation of our study design: the absence of longitudinal data. The clinical course of PDR is defined by dynamic processes, such as regression or recurrence of neovascularization, and the development or resolution of diabetic macular edema, which are not fully captured by a single preoperative snapshot. It is highly plausible that protein levels in the aqueous humor are not static markers of a disease state, but dynamic indicators of disease activity. Therefore, their true predictive power likely lies in how they change over time or in response to therapeutic interventions. Moreover, relying on a single aqueous measurement in isolation, without corresponding vitreous data or longitudinal follow-up, may be premature, as it risks overlooking compartment-specific signals and conflating incidental findings with true disease drivers. Future studies incorporating serial aqueous humor sampling paired with detailed longitudinal clinical tracking are essential to determine whether fluctuations in these specific proteins can predict treatment response, disease progression, or the risk of complications.

A further limitation of our study design is the absence of aqueous humor samples from non-diabetic control patients. While we demonstrate correlations between paired vitreous and aqueous samples within PDR patients, we cannot determine whether the absolute levels of the 21 identified proteins in PDR aqueous deviate from normal. This distinction is important because a protein could show strong VH-AQ correlation in PDR yet still be present at normal absolute levels in aqueous, limiting its utility as a disease biomarker. We direct the reader to Supplementary Table 1, which shows that vitreous levels of the 21 correlated proteins are significantly elevated in PDR compared to control vitreous, supporting their disease relevance. However, vitreous data cannot substitute for aqueous controls. We are actively addressing this limitation by collecting paired aqueous and vitreous samples from non-diabetic control patients (e.g., undergoing vitrectomy for macular hole or epiretinal membrane). These samples will be analyzed in a subsequent study using a larger Olink panel to directly compare PDR aqueous protein levels to normal aqueous levels. We consider validation with normal aqueous controls a critical next step before any clinical application of aqueous-based biomarkers.

While the Olink platform used in this study is well-suited for broad biomarker discovery due to its high multiplexing capacity and sensitivity, it is not designed for routine diagnostic use. Translation of these findings to a clinical diagnostic test would require a different platform, typically an automated immunoassay or a targeted multiplex array compatible with standard clinical laboratory workflows. Any such test would further require rigorous analytical validation (including assessment of accuracy, precision, sensitivity, and specificity) as well as independent clinical validation before implementation in routine care.

Although one of the largest to date, a limitation of our study was the small sample size, which reduced statistical power. Nevertheless, our results corroborate previous findings and may serve as a valuable reference for future comparative studies.

Conclusion

In our study, we demonstrated a strong correlation between vitreous and aqueous protein levels for a selected panel of 21 proteins at baseline in patients with proliferative diabetic retinopathy scheduled for vitrectomy surgery, highlighting the potential of selected proteins in aqueous humor to act as a surrogate for monitoring vitreous composition and disease severity. Notably, many other proteins did not show a correlation between AQ and VH. It is nevertheless conceivable that future treatment decisions may be guided by the variation of specific proteins in aqueous samples, particularly those that most reliably reflect vitreous protein levels.

Among the 21 proteins identified as detectable, directional and having both a strong correlation and a low variation in the AQ: VH ratio, CXCL-10, IL-8, and MCP-4 appeared to be the most strongly associated with fibrosis, whilst GAL-9 and PGF were most strongly associated with the extent of neovascularization. Future research is needed to confirm these findings. Our study provides valuable insights that could contribute to the diagnosis and treatment of PDR. Moreover, the results of this study are also relevant for the identification of appropriate protein biomarkers in diabetic macular edema and may enhance the interpretation of disease mechanisms and treatment choices in these cases.

Materials and methods

Study population

The study followed the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki with approval from the UK National Health Service research ethics committee (South East Coast-Surrey research ethics committee reference 12/LO/0130). Informed consent was obtained from the subjects after explanation of the nature of the study. Consecutive patients undergoing vitrectomy for the complications of PDR by one surgeon from March 2015 to October 2018 were recruited (n = 41). Indications for surgery included vitreous hemorrhage and/or traction retinal detachment (TRD). The procedures for isolation and storage of samples were unchanged during this period. Eyes with previous vitrectomy surgery were excluded. Notably, no patients in the study had received anti-VEGF agents preoperatively. Critically, no patient had required anti-VEGF treatment for diabetic macular edema (DME) in the four months preceding surgery, ensuring that the cohort was specifically defined by PDR activity and not concurrent DME. A control group of non-diabetic patients undergoing surgery for idiopathic macular holes were also recruited (n = 20). The median age (Q1-Q3) of this group was 71.5 (66.5–74.3) years and included 15 (75%) females.

Sample collection

At the beginning of surgery prior to turning on the infusion, paired samples of aqueous and vitreous were taken. For aqueous a clear corneal paracentesis with a 30-gauge needle was performed to extract ~ 0.1 ml. An approximate volume of 0.2–0.5 ml of undiluted mid-vitreous sample was then obtained using a 25-gauge vitrectomy probe with a cut rate of 5000 cpm. The samples were immediately transferred to a − 80 °C freezer and stored. All surgeries were carried out by one surgeon using the Alcon Constellation system (Alcon, Fort Worth, Texas, USA) with wide angle non-contact viewing using a standardized technique. A variety of clinical signs were recorded for each patient either immediately preoperatively or during surgery after vitreous hemorrhage clearance.

Determination of protein levels

Samples were thawed and centrifuged for 15 min at 14,000 g at 4 °C to remove any cellular debris and supernatant was divided in aliquots of 40 µl and stored at − 80 °C until analysis (average storage time is 7 years). Of each patient an aliquot of AQ and VH was shipped on dry ice to our local Olink Service Provider (Arcadia, UMC Utrecht, The Netherlands), randomized over plates and analyzed using the Olink Target 96 platform. Protein levels showed strong correlation with measurements from the orthogonal RayBiotech platform using the same samples (Supplemental Fig. 4), as also employed in our previous study27. A single measurement was performed for each sample using the Immuno-Oncology T96 panel. Protein detection was facilitated through proximity extension assay (PEA) technology, where 92 oligonucleotide-labeled antibody probes sets were selectively bound to their respective target proteins listed in Table 2. Target protein levels were measured on a relative scale and presented as normalized protein expression (NPX), which is an arbitrary unit on a log2 scale. Protein expression levels were normalized using intensity normalization, with the median as the normalization factor. Samples were randomized across plates to ensure comparable median values between plates. For each assay, the plate-specific median was subtracted from all values on that plate, and the overall median across all plates was then added. Normalization in the AQ and VH groups was performed independently from each other. The Olink Quality Control system consists of both internal and external controls. The internal controls are spiked into every sample and are designed to monitor the three main steps of the Olink protocol: immunoreaction, extension, and amplification/detection. The external controls are used to monitor technical variation (sample controls), the limit of detection (LOD) (negative controls) and to normalize each assay to compensate for potential variation between runs and plates (plate controls). All NPX values were subsequently rank-quantile transformed to diminish possible biases by including values under LOD.

Statistical analysis

Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was used to investigate significant correlations between protein concentrations. Moderate correlations were defined as coefficients between 0.400 and 0.599 and strong correlations above 0.600. A Bonferroni corrected p-value of (0.05/92 = 5.43 × 10− 4) was considered to indicate statistically significant correlations between AQ and VH protein levels.

Associations between AQ/VH proteins and clinical variables were examined using ordinal logistic regression with either the AQ or VH protein as independent variable with adjustment for relevant covariates.

The proportional odds assumption was tested using the Brant test. LASSO penalized multiple ordinal logistic regression was used to identify AQ/VH proteins independently associated with the clinical outcomes. The effect of covariates on the clinical outcomes was also tested using ordinal logistic regression. Statistical analyses were performed in SPSS (version 28), R (version 4.3.2) or Graphpad Prism (version 10.2.0).

Heatmap

Expression heatmaps were generated in Heatmapper (http://heatmapper.ca/expression/), a web-based tool supporting datasets of up to 2,500 rows and 300 columns. Clustering was performed on rows (proteins) using Average Linkage (UPGMA), where inter-cluster distance was calculated as the mean of all pairwise distances. Spearman’s Rank Correlation distance, defined as 1 - Spearman’s ρ, was used as the distance measure. This non-parametric, rank-based approach detects monotonic relationships without assuming linearity and reduces sensitivity to outliers.

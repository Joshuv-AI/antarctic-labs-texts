Spectral multipliers on two-step stratified Lie groups with degenerate group structure Thanks: The author gratefully acknowledges the support by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG) through grant MU 761/12-1.
Lars Niedorf

Abstract
. Let L L be a sub-Laplacian on a two-step stratified Lie group G G of topological dimension d d . We prove new L p L^{p} -spectral multiplier estimates under the sharp regularity condition s > d ​ | 1 / p − 1 / 2 | s>d\left|1/p-1/2\right| in settings where the group structure of G G is degenerate, extending previously known results for the non-degenerate case. Our results include variants of the free two-step nilpotent group on three generators and Heisenberg–Reiter groups. The proof combines restriction type estimates with a detailed analysis of the sub-Riemannian geometry of G G . A key novelty of our approach is the use of a refined spectral decomposition into caps on the unit sphere in the center of the Lie group.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Delta
be the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Given a Borel measurable function
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, called
spectral multiplier
, the operator
F(-\Delta)
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined by functional calculus corresponds to a radial Fourier multiplier operator via

\smash{(F(-\Delta)f)^{\wedge}(\xi)=F(|\xi|^{2})\hat{f}(\xi)},\quad f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The Mikhlin–Hörmander theorem
[
12
]
implies that
F(-\Delta)
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
whenever

\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}=\sup_{t>0}{\lVert F(t\,\cdot\,)\eta\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}<\infty\quad\text{for some }s>n/2,

where
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is a generic nonzero cutoff function, and
L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Sobolev space of fractional order
s\geq 0
. The regularity condition
s>n/2
is sharp and cannot be decreased
[
31
]
.
If we ask only for boundedness for fixed
p
, it is now known (see
[
9
,
3
,
30
]
) that for
1<p\leq 2(d+1)/(d+3)
, the operator
F(-\Delta)
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
under the weaker assumption that

\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}<\infty\quad\text{for some }s>d\left|1/p-1/2\right|.

We call such results
p
-specific
spectral multiplier estimates. These estimates, in turn, imply
L^{p}
-boundedness of Bochner–Riesz means
(1+\Delta)_{+}^{\delta}
under the sharp order of regularity
\delta>d\left|1/p-1/2\right|-1/2
.

Theorems of Mikhlin–Hörmander type are known in various settings. Let

(1.1)

L=-\left(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2}\right)

be a left-invariant sub-Laplacian on a stratified Lie group
G
, where
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
are left-invariant vector fields corresponding to a basis of the first layer of a given stratification of
G
(for further details, see
Sections

2
and
3
). Let
G
be equipped with a left-invariant Haar measure, and let
L^{p}(G)
be the associated Lebesgue space. The sub-Laplacian
L
extends to a positive, self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(G)
. Thus, for any spectral multiplier
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, the operator
F(L)
on
L^{2}(G)
can be defined by functional calculus, which is bounded on
L^{2}(G)
if and only if
F\in L^{\infty}
.

For those sub-Laplacians, Christ
[
4
]
and Mauceri–Meda
[
20
]
showed the sufficiency of the threshold
s>Q/2
, where
Q
is the so-called
homogeneous dimension
of the underlying Lie group, which is in general strictly greater than the topological dimension
d
.

However, the threshold
s>Q/2
in
[
4
,
20
]
is not sharp in general. It was improved to
s>d/2
by Müller–Stein
[
23
]
and Hebisch
[
11
]
for sub-Laplacians on the subclass of Heisenberg (-type) groups. Recent extensions of this result include several subclasses within the class of two-step stratified Lie groups
[
16
,
19
]
, Grushin type operators
[
7
,
8
]
, and sub-Laplacians on classes of compact manifolds
[
5
,
1
]
. The threshold
s>d/2
in all these results is sharp
[
14
]
. For general stratified Lie groups it is not known whether the threshold
s>d/2
is always sufficient, and even the two-step case is far from being completely understood.

In sub-elliptic situations, even less is known about the sufficiency of the
p
-dependent threshold
\smash{s>d\left|1/p-1/2\right|}
. A first partial result for Grushin operators was given by Chen and Ouhabaz
[
2
]
, which was later completed in
[
26
]
. The method of
[
26
]
was subsequently adapted to sub-Laplacians on Heisenberg-type groups
[
27
]
and further extended to the broader class of Métivier groups
[
29
]
, a subclass of two-step stratified Lie groups characterized by a specific non-degeneracy condition. However, given that Mikhlin–Hörmander type theorems with sharp regularity condition
s>d/2
are available for subclasses of two-step stratified Lie groups even where this non-degeneracy condition fails
[
15
,
16
,
19
]
, the question arises whether corresponding
p
-specific spectral multiplier estimates also hold for these groups, or at least for certain subclasses.

This article aims to explore the case of sub-Laplacians
\smash{L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2})}
within a particular subclass of two-step stratified Lie groups where the group structure may exhibit degeneracy.

Recall that
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
is a basis of the first layer of a stratification of
G
. If we also fix a basis for the second layer of the chosen stratification, in the associated exponential coordinates, the group law of the Lie group
G
is given by

(x,u)\cdot(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})=\left(x+x^{\prime},u+u^{\prime}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,(\vec{J}x)^{\intercal}x^{\prime}\right),\quad(x,u),(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}},

where
\smash{(\vec{J}x)^{\intercal}x^{\prime}=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{2}}(J_{k}x)^{\intercal}x^{\prime}e_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}
, with
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{d_{2}}\}
denoting the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
, and
J_{1},\dots,J_{d_{2}}
being some skew-symmetric matrices acting on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
. In these exponential coordinates, the left-invariant vector fields
X_{j}
are given by

X_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{d_{2}}(J_{k}x)^{\intercal}e_{j}\,\partial_{u_{k}}.

The most prominent example of a two-step stratified Lie-group is the
Heisenberg group
, where
d_{2}=1
,
d_{1}=2n
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, and
J
is given by

J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}.

The class of Heisenberg groups is contained in the larger classes of Heisenberg type and Métivier groups, which are defined as follows:

Definition
.

Let
J_{\mu}:=\mu_{1}J_{1}+\dots+\mu_{d_{2}}J_{d_{2}}
for
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
.

•

G
is called a
Métivier group
if
J_{\mu}
is invertible for all
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\setminus\{0\}
.

•

G
is called a
Heisenberg type group
if
J_{\mu}
is orthogonal for
|\mu|=1
.

The class of Heisenberg type groups is a strict subclass of the class of Métivier groups. By homogeneity, if
G
is a Heisenberg type group, we have

J_{\mu}^{2}=-|\mu|^{2}\,\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}.

Passing to the larger class of Métivier groups means that we give up this rotation invariant structure. Then, the matrices
J_{\mu}
are no longer orthogonal for
|\mu|=1
and the spectral decomposition of
J_{\mu}
into eigenspaces – which depends on
\mu
– is much more complicated, see also
[
16
, Lemma 5]
. The same applies to the spectral information of the sub-Laplacian
L
which is much harder to control and much more challenging than in the case of Heisenberg type groups.

The class of Lie groups considered in this paper satisfies the following two assumptions, where we allow the group structure to be
degenerate
in the sense that the matrices
J_{\mu}
might have a non-trivial kernel. Recall that the matrices
J_{\mu}
act on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
, which in exponential coordinates is the first layer of the stratification
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
of the Lie algebra of
G
.

Assumption A
.

There exist integers
r_{1},\dots,r_{N}>0
, and an orthogonal decomposition
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}=V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{N}
such that, if
P_{1},\dots,P_{N}
are the corresponding orthogonal projections, then, for all
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\setminus\{0\}
and all
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
,

(1)

J_{\mu}P_{n}=P_{n}J_{\mu}
,

(2)

\smash{J^{2}_{\mu}P_{n}}
has rank
2r_{n}
and exactly one non-zero eigenvalue.

In other words,
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
decomposes into
N
blocks, and the matrices
J_{\mu}
act independently on each block. Within each block,
J_{\mu}^{2}
has exactly one non-zero eigenvalue. This condition was previously considered in
[
19
]
.

Assumption B
.

If
x,x^{\prime}\in\ker J_{\mu^{0}}
for some
\mu^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\setminus\{0\}
, then
(Jx)^{\intercal}x^{\prime}=0
.

In light of
Assumption

A
, this assumption is natural to consider. If
Assumption

A
holds and the matrices
J_{\mu}
restricted to
V_{n}
are have a kernel of dimension at most one, then
Assumption

B
is automatically satisfied. This follows from the skew-symmetry of
J_{\mu}
, which implies that
(J_{\mu}y)^{\intercal}y=0
for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
, and thus
(J_{\mu}x)^{\intercal}x^{\prime}=0
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\ker J_{\mu}
and all
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
if
\dim\ker J_{\mu}\leq 1
.

We shed some more light on Assumptions
A
and
B
in
Section

2
, where we compare it with the aforementioned Heisenberg type and Métivier groups, and give concrete examples of groups that satisfy this assumption. Our examples include a variant of the free two-step nilpotent Lie group on three generators and Heisenberg–Reiter groups, which were previously considered in
[
15
,
19
]
.

It should be emphasized that
Assumption

A
depends a priori on the choice of coordinates on
G
. However, it is actually possible to give a coordinate-independent formulation, see
[
19
]
. We proceed with our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}})
be a sub-Laplacian on a two-step stratified Lie group
G\cong\mathbb{R}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
, where
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
are the left-invariant vector fields associated with a basis of the first layer of a stratification of
G
.
Suppose that
G
satisfies Assumptions
A
and
B
and that additionally

(1.2)

\bar{d}_{1}:=2\left(r_{1}+\dots+r_{N}\right)\geq d_{2}-1

in
Assumption

A
. Let

p_{\bar{d}_{1},d_{2}}:=\frac{\bar{d}_{1}(d_{2}-1)+2d_{2}(d_{2}+1)}{\bar{d}_{1}(d_{2}-1)+3d_{2}^{2}+1}.

Then, if
1\leq p\leq p_{d_{1},d_{2}}
, the following statements hold:

(1)

If
p>1
and if
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded Borel function such that

\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}<\infty\quad\text{for some }s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right),

then the operator
F(L)
is bounded on
L^{p}(G)
, and

\lVert F(L)\rVert_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}\leq C_{p,s}\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}.

(2)

For any
\delta>d\left(1/p-1/2\right)-1/2
, the Bochner–Riesz means
(1-tL)^{\delta}_{+}
,
t\geq 0
, are uniformly bounded on
L^{p}(G)
.

As shown in
[
14
]
, the order of regularity
s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right)
is sharp. The threshold
\delta>d\left(1/p-1/2\right)-1/2
corresponds to the same order of regularity as in the Bochner–Riesz conjecture, see
[
33
]
. It is important to emphasize that
Theorem

1.1
should be interpreted as a result for high-dimensional settings, where
d_{1}
is much larger than
d_{2}
. Specifically,
Assumption

A
implies that the function
\mu\mapsto\dim\ker J_{\mu}
is constant on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\setminus\{0\}
. Therefore, if
r_{0}=\dim\ker J_{\mu}
, then
\bar{d}_{1}=d_{1}-r_{0}
. Even with both Assumptions
A
and
B
, the condition
1.2
is not necessarily satisfied. A counterexample can be found in the Heisenberg–Reiter groups discussed below, see
Example

2.3
.

If
1.2
holds, then
\smash{p_{\bar{d}_{1},d_{2}}\geq 1}
, and
Theorem

1.1
gives results. In fact, note that
\smash{p_{\bar{d}_{1},d_{2}}}
is an increasing function in
\bar{d}_{1}
. Since
\smash{p_{\bar{d}_{1},d_{2}}=1}
in the limiting case
\smash{\bar{d}_{1}=d_{2}-1}
, the first part of
Theorem

1.1
provides results whenever
\smash{\bar{d}_{1}>d_{2}-1}
, while the second part provides results for
\smash{\bar{d}_{1}\geq d_{2}-1}
.

Theorem
1.1
is completely new in the non-Métivier case. However,
Theorem

1.1
also recovers some of the results of
[
29
]
for Métivier groups, although the results of
[
29
]
are stronger in this case. Note that
Assumption

B
is trivially satisfied for Métivier groups, although not every Métivier group necessarily satisfies
Assumption

A
, see
[
16
, Proposition 25]
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Two-step stratified Lie groups

Let
G
be a two-step stratified Lie group. By definition, this means that
G
is a connected, simply connected nilpotent Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
into two non-trivial subspaces
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, called layers, such that

[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]=\mathfrak{g}_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{2}]=0.

We refer to
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
as the
first
and
second
layer of
\mathfrak{g}
, respectively. Let

d_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}\geq 1,\quad d_{2}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad d=\dim\mathfrak{g}.

In exponential coordinates, the group law of
G
is given by

(x,u)(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})=(x+x^{\prime},u+u^{\prime}+\tfrac{1}{2}[x,x^{\prime}]),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},u,u^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}.

We recall some prominent classes of two-step stratified Lie groups. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be an inner product with respect to which the layers
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are orthogonal. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
denote the dual of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. For any
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, let
\omega_{\mu}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be the skew-symmetric bilinear form given by

\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})=\mu([x,x^{\prime}]),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

The group
G
is called a
Métivier group
if
\omega_{\mu}
is non-degenerate for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, which means that
\mathfrak{r}_{\mu}=\{0\}
, where
\mathfrak{r}_{\mu}
is the radical

\mathfrak{r}_{\mu}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}:\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})=0\text{ for all }x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}\}.

The inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
induces a norm on the dual
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
which we denote by
|\cdot|
. Let
J_{\mu}
be the skew-symmetric endomorphism such that

(2.1)

\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})=\langle J_{\mu}x,x^{\prime}\rangle,\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

Given a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
of the first layer
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and a basis
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
of the second layer
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, the group law in the associated coordinates is given by

(x,u)\cdot(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})=\left(x+x^{\prime},u+u^{\prime}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,(\vec{J}x)^{\intercal}x^{\prime}\right),\quad(x,u),(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}},

where
\smash{(\vec{J}x)^{\intercal}x^{\prime}=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{2}}(J_{k}x)^{\intercal}x^{\prime}e_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}
with
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{d_{2}}\}
being the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}

and
J_{k}
is the skew-symmetric matrix acting on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
which corresponds to the endomorphism
\smash{J_{U_{k}^{*}}}
under the chosen basis, with
U_{k}^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
being the dual element to
U_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Moreover, if we identify we matrix
J_{\mu}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
with its representing matrix in the coordinates
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
and write
\mu=\mu_{1}U_{1}+\dots+\mu_{d_{2}}U_{d_{2}}
, then

J_{\mu}=\mu_{1}J_{1}+\dots+\mu_{d_{2}}J_{d_{2}}.

Henceforth, we will usually identify
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
by means of the bases
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
and
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
.

Note that the group
G
is a Métivier group if and only if
J_{\mu}
is invertible for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. It is called a
Heisenberg type group
if the endomorphisms
J_{\mu}
are additionally orthogonal for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
of length 1, which is equivalent to

J_{\mu}^{2}=-|\mu|^{2}\id_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}.

The class of Heisenberg type groups is a strict subclass of that of Métivier groups, an example of a Métivier group that is not of Heisenberg type can be found for instance in
[
22
]
. Note that the dimension
d_{1}
of the second layer of always even if
G
is a Métivier group. If
d_{1}
is even,
d_{2}=1
and

J_{\mu}=\mu\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{d_{1}/2}\\
-I_{d_{1}/2}&0\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\mu\in\mathbb{R},

then
G
is a
Heisenberg group
.

2.2.
Spectral analysis of the matrices
J_{\mu}

Next, we analyze the spectral properties of the family of matrices
J_{\mu}
. Recall that any matrix
J_{\mu}
is skew-symmetric, so the matrix
iJ_{\mu}
on the complexification
\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}
is self-adjoint, which implies that
-J_{\mu}^{2}
is also self-adjoint.

The matrices
J_{\mu}
admit a “simultaneous spectral decomposition” if
\mu
lies in a Zariski open subset
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
. This is made precise in the following statement, which can be found, for example, in
[
16
, Lemma 5]
and
[
29
, Proposition 3.4]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

There exist

•

a non-empty, homogeneous Zariski-open subset
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
,

•

numbers
N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
,
r_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{N})\in\smash{(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{N}}
,

•

a function
\mu\mapsto\mathbf{b}^{\mu}=(b^{\mu}_{1},\dots,b^{\mu}_{N})\in[0,\infty)^{N}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, and

•

functions
\mu\mapsto P_{n}^{\mu}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
with
P_{n}^{\mu}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{1}
,
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
,

such that

(2.2)

-J_{\mu}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}(b_{n}^{\mu})^{2}P_{n}^{\mu}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}.

Moreover, the functions
\mu\mapsto b^{\mu}_{n}
and
\mu\mapsto P_{n}^{\mu}
satisfy the following two properties:

(i)

the functions
\mu\mapsto b^{\mu}_{n}
are homogeneous of degree
1
and continuous on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, real analytic on
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
, and satisfy
b_{n}^{\mu}>0
for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
and
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, and
b_{n}^{\mu}\neq b_{n^{\prime}}^{\mu}
if
n\neq n^{\prime}
for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
and
n,n^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
,

(ii)

the functions
\mu\mapsto P_{n}^{\mu}
are (componentwise) real analytic on
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
, homogeneous of degree
0
, and the maps
P_{n}^{\mu}
are orthogonal projections on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
of rank
2r_{n}
for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
, with pairwise orthogonal ranges.

A few comments about
Proposition

2.1
are in order. In the special case where
G
is a Métivier group, we have
r_{0}=0
since
\ker J_{\mu}=0
for
\mu\neq 0
. If
G
is even a Heisenberg type group, we may choose
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
,
N=1
,
\mathbf{b}^{\mu}=|\mu|
, and the only projection is identity map on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. However, for a general two-step stratified Lie group, it may happen in the above decomposition that
b_{n}^{\mu}=0
or
b_{n}^{\mu}=b_{n^{\prime}}^{\mu}
for
n\neq n^{\prime}
if
\mu
is in the Zariski closed set
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
. This structure is generally difficult to handle, since the functions
\mu\mapsto b^{\mu}_{n}
and
\mu\mapsto P_{n}^{\mu}
may have singularities on the set
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
. For more details on these singularities, we refer the reader to
[
16
]
, and also
[
17
]
.

2.3.
Particular classes of non-Métiver groups

We shed more light on Assumptions
A
and
B
, which define the class of Lie groups considered in this paper.

In
Assumption

A
, we require a decomposition
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{N}
such that the matrices
J_{\mu}
act independently on each subspace
V_{n}
. Moreover,
J_{\mu}^{2}
must admit a unique non-zero eigenvalue. Let
P_{n}
denote the orthogonal projection onto
V_{n}
. Under
Assumption

A
, the spectral decomposition of the matrix
J_{\mu}^{2}P_{n}
, when restricted to
V_{n}
, consists of a single projection, which is the projection onto the unique eigenspace of
J_{\mu}^{2}P_{n}
. Since we assume that the rank of
J_{\mu}^{2}P_{n}
remains constant, the rank of this projection also remains constant as
\mu
varies over
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
.

Comparing this setup with the decomposition in
Proposition

2.1
, satisfying
Assumption

A
means that the decomposition
2.2
extends actually to
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, that is,

(2.3)

-J_{\mu}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}(b_{n}^{\mu})^{2}P_{n}^{\mu}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\},

where
b_{n}^{\mu}>0
for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. However, in
2.3
, the projections
P_{n}^{\mu}
are not necessarily the projections onto the full eigenspace associated with
b_{n}^{\mu}
, since it may happen that
b_{n}^{\mu}=b_{n^{\prime}}^{\mu}
for
n\neq n^{\prime}
. Even though we require that there is a unique eigenvalue on each block
V_{n}
, the eigenvalues for some blocks may coincide.

Assumption
A
is the same as Assumption (A) in
[
19
]
. This assumption plays a crucial role in
[
19
]
, where a Mikhlin–Hörmander type result with sharp order of regularity
s>d/2
is proved. The key to the proof of the main result of
[
19
]
is a weighted Plancherel estimate with a weight that depends only on the second layer
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Specifically, let
L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2})
denote the sub-Laplacian associated with the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
, and let

U=\left(-(U_{1}^{2}+\dots+U_{d_{2}}^{2})\right)^{1/2}=\left(-\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}\right)^{1/2}

be the square root of the negative partial Laplacian on the second layer
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Then, for all functions
F,\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, the (group) convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}
associated with the operator
F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)
satisfies

(2.4)

\int_{G}\left||u|^{\alpha}\,\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(x,u)\right|^{2}\,d(x,u)\lesssim 2^{\ell(2\alpha-d_{2})}\|F\|_{L_{2}^{\alpha}}^{2}

for all
\alpha\geq 0
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
. Here, the operator
F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)
should be thought of as a dyadic piece of a decomposition of the operator
F(L)
.

By a partial Fourier transform, the weight
|u|^{\alpha}
corresponds to taking derivatives of
b^{\mu}_{n}
and
P_{n}^{\mu}
with respect to the variable
\mu
. In fact, proving an estimate such as
2.4
beyond two-step stratified Lie groups where
Assumption

A
is violated and where the functions
\mu\mapsto b^{\mu}_{n}
and
\mu\mapsto P_{n}^{\mu}
may admit singularities is a highly non-trivial problem. Versions of such weighted Plancherel estimates are available under the assumption that one has good control over the derivatives of both
\mu\mapsto b^{\mu}_{n}
and
\mu\mapsto P_{n}^{\mu}
, but beyond that not much is known about the validity of such estimates, see
[
16
]
for more details.

To prove the statement of
Theorem

1.1
,
Assumption

A
alone does not appear sufficient, and we need to additionally require
Assumption

B
, which is satisfied by several notable classes of non-Métivier groups. In
Assumption

B
, we require that
\langle J_{\mu}x,x^{\prime}\rangle=0
for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
and all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
that lie in the kernel of
J_{\mu^{0}}
for some fixed
\mu^{0}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
. This condition is automatically satisfied of the kernel of the matrix
J_{\mu}
is one-dimensional on each subspace
V_{n}
. While many of the arguments in this paper remain valid without
Assumption

B
, this assumption seems to be vital for a crucial step in the proof.

2.4.
Some examples

Finally, we give two examples of classes of two-step stratified Lie groups for which the results of
Theorem

1.1
hold.

Example 2.2

(Copies of
N_{3,2}
glued along their centers)
.

The free
2
-step nilpotent Lie group
N_{3,2}
on
3
generators is the simply connected, connected nilpotent Lie group defined by the relations

[X_{1},X_{2}]=Y_{3},\quad[X_{2},X_{3}]=Y_{1},\quad[X_{3},X_{1}]=Y_{2},

where
X_{j},Y_{j}
,
j\in\{1,2,3\}
is a basis of its Lie algebra. Then, in exponential coordinates,
N_{3,2}
can be identified with
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{y}
, where the group law is given by

(x,y)\cdot(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime}+x\wedge x^{\prime}/2)

and
x\wedge x^{\prime}
denotes the cross product of
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This particular group was previously considered in
[
15
]
. Now, for any
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, let
G_{n}=N_{3,2}
. Let
G
be the the quotient of
G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{2}
given by identifying the centers of the groups
G_{n}
via the identity map, that is,
G
is given by the relations

[X_{n,1},X_{n,2}]=Y_{3},\quad[X_{n,2},X_{n,3}]=Y_{1},\quad[X_{n,3},X_{n,1}]=Y_{2},

where
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and
X_{n,j},Y_{j}
,
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
,
j\in\{1,2,3\}
is a basis of the Lie algebra of
G
. In particular,
G
is a Lie group of topological dimension
3(N+1)
whose center has dimension
d_{2}=3
.

We chose the usual Euclidean inner product on
G=\mathbb{R}^{3N}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. If
\smash{J_{\mu}^{G}}
is defined as in
2.1
, then

J_{\mu}^{G}=\begin{pmatrix}J_{\mu}^{N_{3,2}}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&J_{\mu}^{N_{3,2}}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

where

J_{\mu}^{N_{3,2}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\mu_{3}&\mu_{2}\\
\mu_{3}&0&-\mu_{1}\\
-\mu_{2}&\mu_{1}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}).

If we split
\mathbb{R}^{3N}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\dots\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
and choose
P_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{3N}\to\mathbb{R}^{3N}
as the canonical projection onto the
n
-th component, then
J_{\mu}P_{n}=P_{n}J_{\mu}
for all
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Moreover, note that

\left(J_{\mu}^{N_{3,2}}\right)^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}-\mu_{2}^{2}-\mu_{3}^{2}&\mu_{1}\mu_{2}&\mu_{1}\mu_{3}\\
\mu_{1}\mu_{2}&-\mu_{1}^{2}-\mu_{3}^{2}&\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\\
\mu_{1}\mu_{3}&\mu_{2}\mu_{3}&-\mu_{1}^{2}-\mu_{2}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

For
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, this matrix has a unique nonzero eigenvalue, which is
-|\mu|^{2}
and which is associated with the eigenvectors
(-\mu_{3},0,\mu_{1})
and
(-\mu_{2},\mu_{1},0)
, while its kernel is given by the subspace spanned by
\mu
. Thus, the constructed group satisfies both Assumptions
A
and
B
.

Finally, we check the dimensional conditions of
Theorem

1.1
. If we let
2r_{n}
be the rank of
J_{\mu}^{2}P_{n}
, then
2\left(r_{1}+\dots+r_{N}\right)=2N\geq 2=d_{2}-1
. Moreover,

\displaystyle p_{\bar{d}_{1},d_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{d}_{1}(d_{2}-1)+2d_{2}(d_{2}+1)}{\bar{d}_{1}(d_{2}-1)+3d_{2}^{2}+1}\bigg|_{(\bar{d}_{1},d_{2})=(2N,3)}

\displaystyle=2-\frac{8}{N+7}.

In particular, the first part of
Theorem

1.1
provides results only if
N\geq 2
, while the second part provides results for all
N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
(but only for
p=1
when
N=2
).

Example 2.3

(Heisenberg–Reiter groups)
.

Given some integers
N>0
and
d_{2}>0
, the Heisenberg–Reiter group
\mathbb{H}_{N,d_{2}}
is defined by endowing
\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}\times N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
with the group law

(x,y,u)\cdot(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},u^{\prime})=(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},u+u^{\prime}+(xy^{\prime}-x^{\prime}y)/2),

where
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}\times N}
,
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
u,u^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
.
Note that
\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}\times N}
is the set of the real
d_{2}\times N
matrices, and the products
xy^{\prime},x^{\prime}y
above shall be interpreted in the sense of matrix multiplication.
\mathbb{H}_{N,d_{2}}
is a two-step stratified Lie group of homogeneous dimension
Q=Nd_{2}+N+2d_{2}
and topological dimension
d=Nd_{2}+N+d_{2}
. This group was previously considered in
[
19
]
.

We equip
\mathbb{H}_{N,d_{2}}
with the usual Euclidean inner product. If
\smash{J_{\mu}^{\mathbb{H}_{N,d_{2}}}}
is defined as in
2.1
, then

\langle J_{\mu}^{\mathbb{H}_{N,d_{2}}}(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\rangle=\langle\mu,xy^{\prime}-x^{\prime}y\rangle.

Thus, for
\mu\neq 0
, the kernel of
\smash{J_{\mu}^{\mathbb{H}_{N,d_{2}}}}
is given by
\mu^{\perp}\times\{0\}
, that is,
\smash{J_{\mu}^{\mathbb{H}_{N,d_{2}}}}(x,y)=0
if and only if
\mu^{\intercal}x=0
and
y=0
. So the dimension of
the kernel is
(d_{2}-1)N
. In particular,
\mathbb{H}_{N,d_{2}}
does not fall into the class of Métivier groups unless
d_{2}=1
, in which case it is the
(2N+1)
-dimensional Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{N}
.

An orthogonal decomposition of the first layer is given by the isomorphism

(2.5)

\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}\times N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\cong(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\times\mathbb{R})^{N}.

In other words, the Heisenberg–Reiter group
\mathbb{H}_{N,d_{2}}
is isomorphic to
N
copies of
\mathbb{H}_{1,d_{2}}
with are glued along their centers. Thus, to verify that
\mathbb{H}_{N,d_{2}}
satisfies Assumptions
A
and
B
, it suffices to verify this for each block
\mathbb{H}_{1,d_{2}}
.

For
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mu,xy^{\prime}-x^{\prime}y\rangle

\displaystyle=x^{\intercal}\mu\,y^{\prime}-y\,\mu^{\intercal}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(x^{\intercal}\ y)\begin{pmatrix}0&\mu\\
-\mu^{\intercal}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x^{\prime}\\
y^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

Thus,

J_{\mu}=J_{\mu}^{\mathbb{H}_{1,2}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mu\\
-\mu^{\intercal}&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad J_{\mu}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}-\mu\,\mu^{\intercal}&0\\
0&-|\mu|^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
J_{\mu}
is a skew-symmetric matrix of size
d_{2}+1
and since the kernel of
J_{\mu}
has dimension
d_{2}-1
,
\smash{J_{\mu}^{2}}
must have a unique non-zero eigenvalue, whence
\smash{\mathbb{H}_{1,d_{2}}}
satisfies
Assumption

A
. More explicitly, since the kernel of
J_{\mu}
is given by
\smash{\mu^{\perp}\times\{0\}}
, if an element
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}
lies in the eigenspace of the non-zero eigenvalue, then
x=\lambda\mu
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. This implies that the non-zero eigenvalue is
-|\mu|^{2}
, which vanishes if and only if
\mu=0
.

Moreover, if
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\ker J_{\mu^{0}}
for some
\mu^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\setminus\{0\}
, then
y=y^{\prime}=0
and thus
\langle\mu,xy^{\prime}-x^{\prime}y\rangle=0
for all
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
, so
Assumption

B
is satisfied as well.

We check the remaining conditions of
Theorem

1.1
. Let
P_{n}
be the orthogonal projection associated with the decomposition
2.5
. Then

2r_{n}:=\mathrm{rank}\big((J_{\mu}^{\mathbb{H}_{N,d_{2}}})^{2}P_{n}\big)=\mathrm{rank}\big((J_{\mu}^{\mathbb{H}_{1,d_{2}}})^{2}\big)=1.

Hence
2\left(r_{1}+\dots+r_{N}\right)=2N
. Thus,
Theorem

1.1
only gives results for those Heisenberg–Reiter groups
\smash{\mathbb{H}_{N,d_{2}}}
that satisfy
2N\geq d_{2}-1
.

2.5.
Projections onto the kernel

Let
P_{0}^{\mu}
denote the orthogonal projection onto the kernel of
J_{\mu}
. Then, in view of
2.3
, we have

P_{0}^{\mu}=\id_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-\left(P_{1}^{\mu}+\dots+P_{N}^{\mu}\right)\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\},

and
\mu\mapsto P_{0}^{\mu}
is smooth on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. Thus, since
\mu\mapsto P_{n}^{\mu}
is homogeneous, we have

(2.6)

\smash{\|P_{0}^{\mu}-P_{0}^{\mu^{\prime}}\|\lesssim|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|\quad\text{for all }\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}.}

This estimate will play a role later in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. However, the validity of
2.6
does not depend on
Assumption

A
or
Assumption

B
. Instead, it is sufficient to require that the dimension of
\ker J_{\mu}
remains constant as a function of
\mu
on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, as will be shown in the next lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\mu\mapsto\dim\ker J_{\mu}
is constant on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, then:

(1)

There is some
F\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
F(iJ_{\mu})|_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}=P_{0}^{\mu}
for all
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
.

(2)

The map
\mu\mapsto P_{0}^{\mu}
is smooth and Lipschitz continuous on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, and

\smash{\|P_{0}^{\mu}-P_{0}^{\mu^{\prime}}\|\lesssim\|J_{\mu}-J_{\mu^{\prime}}\|\quad\text{for all }\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}.}

Proof.

As in
Proposition

2.1
, let
b_{1}^{\mu},\dots,b_{N}^{\mu}
be the positive eigenvalues of
iJ_{\mu}
. Then
b_{n}^{\mu}>0
for all
\mu
lying in the Zariski open subset
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
. Since
\mu\mapsto\dim\ker J_{\mu}
is constant on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, we have even
b_{n}^{\mu}>0
for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
. Thus, there are constants
C,c>0
such that

c<b_{n}^{\mu}<C\quad\text{for }|\mu|=1\text{ and }n\in\{1,\dots,N\}.

Note that
iJ_{\mu}
is a self-adjoint endomorphism on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}
. Let
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function with
\supp F\subseteq[-c,c]
and
F(0)=1
. Then, via functional calculus, we have
F(iJ_{\mu})|_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}=P_{0}^{\mu}
for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, and the Fourier inversion formula yields

P_{0}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{F}(\tau)e^{-\tau J_{\mu}}\,d\tau.

Hence,
\mu\mapsto P_{0}^{\mu}
is smooth on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. Moreover,

\|P_{0}^{\mu}-P_{0}^{\mu^{\prime}}\|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\hat{F}(\tau)|\,\|e^{-\tau J_{\mu}}-e^{-\tau J_{\mu^{\prime}}}\|\,d\tau.

By the spectral theorem, we have
\smash{\|e^{iA}\|\lesssim\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}|e^{i\lambda}|=1}
for all symmetric matrices
A
on
\smash{\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}}
. Thus, by the mean value theorem,

\|e^{-\tau J_{\mu}}-e^{-\tau J_{\mu^{\prime}}}\|\leq\tau\left\|J_{\mu}-J_{\mu^{\prime}}\right\|.

Since
\hat{F}
is a Schwartz function, we obtain
\|P_{0}^{\mu}-P_{0}^{\mu^{\prime}}\|\lesssim\|J_{\mu}-J_{\mu^{\prime}}\|
.
∎

3.
Truncated restriction type estimates on caps

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
relies on exploiting suitable restriction type estimates. Similar estimates are also used in
[
29
]
. However, a novelty of the restriction type estimates used here is the additional spectral decomposition into caps on the unit sphere within the center of the Lie group. This additional decomposition is crucial for deriving spectral multiplier results for groups with degenerate group structures. The restriction type estimates stated here hold actually for general two-step stratified Lie group.

Let
G
be a general two-step stratified Lie group. Let again
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be a stratification of its Lie algebra which is orthogonal with respect to a chosen inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. As before, given an orthonormal basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
of the first layer
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, we identify these by left-invariant vector fields via the Lie derivative, and consider the associated sub-Laplacian
L
, which is the second-order differential operator

L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2}).

Let
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. The operators
L,-iU_{1},\dots,-iU_{d_{2}}
admit a joint functional calculus
[
18
]
, which means that we can define the operator
F(L,\mathbf{U})
on
L^{2}(G)
for any Borel function
F:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\to\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathbf{U}
is the vector of differential operators

\mathbf{U}=(-iU_{1},\dots,-iU_{d_{2}})
.

3.1.
A decomposition into caps

Using the joint functional calculus, we introduce an additional truncation along the spectrum of the operator

U=(-(U_{1}^{2}+\dots+U_{d_{2}}^{2}))^{1/2},

which corresponds to a partial Laplacian on the second layer
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. This allows us to write any operator
F(L)
as

F(L)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U),

where
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
(\chi(2^{\ell}\cdot))_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
forms a partition of unity. In fact, as we will see in
Proposition

3.5
below, there exists a number
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)=0\quad\text{for all }\ell<-\ell_{0}.

The additional spectral decomposition into caps on the unit sphere
S^{d_{2}-1}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
is as follows: Given
\delta>0
, let
(\zeta_{j})_{j\in I}
be a a smooth partition of unity subordinate to some open cover
(V_{j})_{j\in I}
of caps of size
\delta>0
of the unit sphere
S^{d_{2}-1}
. Any function
\zeta_{j}:S^{d_{2}-1}\to\mathbb{R}
extends to a smooth function
\zeta_{j}:\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
that is homogeneous of degree 0. This allows us to decompose the operator
F(L)
as

F(L)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j\in I}F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{U}).

In the proof of
Theorem

1.1
, the size of the caps will depend on
\ell
.

3.2.
Restriction type estimates

The restriction type estimates of
Theorem

3.1
below provide bounds for the operators
F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{U})
. These estimates play a crucial role in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

For
M\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, let
\|\cdot\|_{M,2}
be the norm given by

\|F\|_{M,2}=\bigg(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{K\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\sup_{\lambda\in[\frac{K-1}{M},\frac{K}{M})}|F(\lambda)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, let
p_{\mathrm{ST}}(n)
denote the Stein–Tomas exponent on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is

p_{\mathrm{ST}}(n)=\frac{2(n+1)}{n+3}.

Additionally, we set
p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{1},d_{2})=\min\{p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{1}),p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})\}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{1},d_{2})
. Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a compact subset, and let
\delta>0
and
(\zeta_{j})_{j\in I}
be defined as above. If
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded Borel function supported in
A
and
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, then

(3.1)

\lVert F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{U})\rVert_{p\to 2}\leq C_{A,p,\chi}\,\delta^{\frac{d_{2}-1}{2}(1-\vartheta_{p})}\,2^{-\ell d_{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\,\|F\|_{2}^{1-\theta_{p}}\|F\|_{2^{\ell},2}^{\theta_{p}}

for all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\vartheta_{p},\theta_{p}\in[0,1]
satisfy

1/p=(1-\vartheta_{p})+\vartheta_{p}/p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad 1/p=(1-\theta_{p})+\theta_{p}/p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{1},d_{2}).

Note that Theorem
3.1
holds for general two-step stratified Lie groups. However, the proof of
Theorem

1.1
requires restricting to a subclass of stratified Lie groups that satisfies both
Assumption

A
and
Assumption

B
. This restriction arises because, in particular, we rely on Plancherel type estimates, which unfortunately do not hold in general for arbitrary two-step stratified Lie groups.

As indicated by our notation,
3.1
is derived through interpolation between the endpoints
p=1
,
p=p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{1},d_{2})
, and
p=p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})
. Under the assumptions of
Theorem

1.1
, which require
\bar{d}_{1}\geq d_{2}-1
, we observe that
p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})\leq p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{1})
. Therefore, in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
, we apply
Theorem

3.1
with
p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})=p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{1},d_{2})
and
\vartheta_{p}=\theta_{p}
. In that case, it is possible to derive
3.1
via interpolation, combining the estimate for
p=1
from
Proposition

3.5
below and the estimate from
[
28
]
for
p=p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})
.

4.2.
Finite propagation speed

The convolution kernels of the dyadic pieces
\smash{F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})}
in
Theorem

4.1
are compactly supported with respect to a control distance, which we will now discuss.

The Lie group
G\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
admits a natural control distance associated with the sub-elliptic operator
L
. This distance, which is the
Carnot–Carathéodory distance

d_{\mathrm{CC}}
, is defined for any two points
g,h\in G
as the infimum of lengths of curves in
G
connecting
g
and
h
that are horizontal with respect to the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
(see
[
35
, Section III.4]
). The Chow-Rashevskii theorem
[
35
, Proposition III.4.1]
ensures that
d_{CC}
is a metric on
G
inducing the Euclidean topology. Moreover, as shown in
[
10
, Lemma 1.1]
(see also
[
27
, Section 5]
), the Carnot–Carathéodory distance satisfies

(4.2)

d_{\mathrm{CC}}(g,h)\sim\|g^{-1}h\|_{G}\quad\text{for all }g,h\in G,

where
\|\cdot\|_{G}
is the norm defined by
\|(x,u)\|_{G}:=|x|+|u|^{1/2}
for
(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
.

For
(x,u)\in G
, let
B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(x,u)
denote the ball of radius
R>0
centered at
(x,u)
with respect to the distance
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
. Note that
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
is left-invariant with respect to the group multiplication, that is,
d_{\mathrm{CC}}(ag,ah)=d_{\mathrm{CC}}(g,h)
for all
a,g,h\in G
.
Moreover, since the norm
\|\cdot\|_{G}
is homogeneous with respect to the family of dilations
(\delta_{R})_{R>0}
given by
\delta_{R}(x,u)=(Rx,R^{2}u)
, the volume of a ball
B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(x,u)
satisfies

(4.3)

|B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(x,u)|=R^{Q}|B_{1}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)|\sim R^{Q},

where
Q=d_{1}+2d_{2}
, and
0\in G
is the neutral element of the group multiplication under exponential coordinates. In particular, the metric space
(G,d_{\mathrm{CC}})
equipped with the Lebesgue measure is a space of homogeneous type with homogeneous dimension
Q
. Moreover, by (
4.2
), there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(4.4)

B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}=B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)\subseteq B_{CR}(0)\times B_{CR^{2}}(0)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}},

where
B_{CR}(0)
and
B_{CR^{2}}(0)
are balls with respect to the Euclidean distance.

By
[
21
]
(or alternatively
[
25
, Corollary 6.3]
), any sub-Laplacian
L
on a two-step stratified Lie group satisfies the
finite propagation speed property
with respect to the Carnot–Carathéodory distance
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
on
G
.

Lemma 4.2

If

are supported in open subsets

.

Let

. For the results of this section, we do not require Assumptions
A
and
B
. Instead, we only require that

\mu\mapsto\dim\ker J_{\mu}\text{ is constant on }\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\},

where
J_{\mu}
are the matrices from Section
2
. Recall that, by Lemma
2.4
, this implies that there is a constant
\kappa>0

(5.1)

\smash{\|P_{0}^{\mu}-P_{0}^{\mu^{\prime}}\|\leq\kappa\,|\mu-\mu^{\prime}|\quad\text{for all }\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}},

where

5.1.
Convolution kernels

Let
F:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be an even bounded Borel function. Let
F^{(\iota)}
be as in Section
4
. Recall from Section
3
that the operator
F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})
can be decomposed as

F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})=\sum_{\ell\geq-\ell_{0}}\sum_{j\in I}F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\chi(2^{\ell}U)\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{U}).

Here,
\mathbf{U}=(-iU_{1},\dots,-iU_{d_{2}})
and
U=|\mathbf{U}|^{1/2}
, where
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
forms a basis for the second layer
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Moreover,
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
(\chi(2^{\ell}\cdot))_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
forms a dyadic partition of unity of the real axis. The constant
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is given by Lemma
3.4
. The functions
\zeta_{j}:\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are homogeneous of degree 0 and localize to caps
V_{j}
of size
\delta
on the unit sphere
S^{d_{2}-1}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, where they form a partition of unity.

The operators
F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\chi(2^{\ell}U)\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{U})
possess a convolution kernel which can be written down in terms of the Fourier transform on the second layer and Laguerre polynomials. We use the notation of
Proposition

2.1
. Recall that
Proposition

2.1
provides the spectral decomposition

-J_{\mu}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}(b_{n}^{\mu})^{2}P_{n}^{\mu}

for all
\mu
in a Zariski open subset
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, where
P_{n}^{\mu}
is an orthogonal projection of rank
2r_{n}
. Furthermore, for
\lambda>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, let
\smash{\varphi_{k}^{(\lambda,m)}}
be the
\lambda
-rescaled Laguerre function given by

(5.2)

\varphi_{k}^{(\lambda,m)}(z)=\lambda^{m}L_{k}^{m-1}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda|z|^{2}\big)\,e^{-\frac{1}{4}\lambda|z|^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2m},

where
L_{k}^{m-1}
is the
k
-th Laguerre polynomial of type
m-1
. Since
P_{n}^{\mu}
projects onto a subspace of dimension
2r_{n}
, with a slight abuse of notation, we also write

\varphi_{k}^{(\lambda,r_{n})}(P_{n}^{\mu}x)=\lambda^{m}L_{k}^{m-1}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda|P_{n}^{\mu}x|^{2}\big)\,e^{-\frac{1}{4}\lambda|P_{n}^{\mu}x|^{2}}.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
F\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, then the operator
F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{U})
possesses a convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}\in\mathcal{S}(G)
, which is given by

(5.3)

\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}(x,u)=(2\pi)^{-r_{0}-d_{2}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\ker J_{\mu}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\,\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\,\zeta_{j}(\mu)\\
\times\bigg[\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(P_{n}^{\mu}x)\bigg]e^{i\langle\xi,P_{0}^{\mu}x\rangle}\,e^{i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,d\xi\,d\mu,

where

\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(2k_{n}+r_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\mu},\quad\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}.

Proof.

This follows from
[
28
, Proposition 3.4]
.

5.2.
The role of the decomposition into caps

The heuristic idea behind the following
Lemma

5.3
is as follows: As outlined in
Section

4
, the convolution kernel of the operator
\smash{F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})}
is supported within a ball of size
R\times R^{2}
, up to constants, where
R=2^{\iota}
. Suppose we replace
F
in
5.3
with the function
\smash{F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{\cdot\,})}
. Let
\smash{\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell,j}}
be the convolution kernel of the operator
\smash{F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\chi(2^{\ell}U)\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{U})}
.

Note that
b_{n}^{\mu}>0
for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, which follows from our assumption that
\mu\mapsto\dim\ker J_{\mu}
is constant on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. Furthermore, since
\mu\mapsto b_{n}^{\mu}
is homogeneous of degree 1, we have
b_{n}^{\mu}\sim|\mu|
. The estimates from
[
34
, Equations (1.1.44), (1.3.41), Lemma 1.5.3]
suggest that the function
\smash{\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}}
in
5.3
is essentially supported in a ball of radius comparable to

(b_{n}^{\mu})^{-1/2}(k_{n}+1)^{1/2}\sim|\mu|^{-1/2}(k_{n}+1)^{1/2}\lesssim 2^{\ell},

centered at the origin, up to an exponentially decaying term.

Let
R_{\ell}=2^{\ell}
. We distinguish between the cases
-\ell_{0}\leq\ell\leq\iota
, where
R_{\ell}\leq R
, and the case
\ell>\iota
. We consider the case where
-\ell_{0}\leq\ell\leq\iota
. The observation above suggests that we can think of the partial Fourier transform
\smash{(\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell,j})^{\mu}}
(as defined in
3.3
) as being supported in the set of all
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
such that

(5.4)

|P_{0}^{\mu}x|\lesssim R\quad\text{and}\quad|\bar{P}^{\mu}x|\lesssim R_{\ell}.

Here,
P_{0}^{\mu}x
is the orthogonal projection of
x
onto
\ker J_{\mu}
, and
\bar{P}^{\mu}x=x-P_{0}^{\mu}x
is the projection onto the orthogonal complement
(\ker J_{\mu})^{\perp}
. However, this information about the support depends on
\mu
. For instance, if
G
is the group
N_{3,2}
, then
\ker J_{\mu}
is the one-dimensional subspace spanned by the vector
\mu
, see
Example

2.2
.

This is where the additional decomposition provided by the functions
\zeta_{j}
comes into play. We fix
\mu^{j}\in S^{d_{2}-1}
, where
\mu^{j}\in\zeta_{j}
(thought of as the center of the cap). If
\mu\in\supp\zeta_{j}
, we have

|\bar{\mu}-\mu^{j}|<\delta,\quad\text{where}\quad\bar{\mu}=|\mu|^{-1}\mu.

Thus, using
5.1
, if we choose
\delta
sufficiently small, then
5.4
implies

|P_{0}^{\mu^{j}}x|\lesssim R\quad\text{and}\quad|\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}x|\lesssim R_{\ell}\quad\text{for all }(x,u)\in\supp\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell,j}.

Hence,
\smash{\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell,j}}
is essentially supported in a ball with volume comparable to
\smash{R^{r_{0}}R_{\ell}^{\bar{d}_{1}}R^{d_{2}}}
. This idea will be made precise in
Proposition

5.2
.

Let
C>0
be the constant from
4.4
, that is,

B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}=B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)\subseteq B_{CR}\times B_{CR^{2}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}.

For any
j\in I
, we fix
\mu^{j}\in S^{d_{2}-1}
with
\mu^{j}\in\supp\chi_{j}
. As before, given
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, let
\smash{P_{0}^{\mu^{j}}\!x}
be the orthogonal projection of
x
onto
\ker J_{\mu^{j}}
and
\smash{\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}\!x=x-P_{0}^{\mu^{j}}\!x}
be the projection onto its orthogonal complement.

Proposition 5.2

(Localization on the first layer)
.

Let
1\leq p<2
. Consider radii
R=2^{\iota}
and
R_{\ell}=2^{\ell}
for
\iota\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell\in\{-\ell_{0},\dots,\iota\}
. Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a compact subset. Suppose
F\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is supported in
A
, and let
\chi
and
\zeta_{j}
be as before, where we choose
\delta=R/R_{\ell}
for the decomposition into caps
V_{j}
of size
\delta
. Then, for any
\gamma>0
, if
x^{0}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
with
|x^{0}|<CR
and
f\in L^{p}(G)\cap L^{2}(G)
is supported in

B^{(\ell)}(x^{0},\mu^{j})=\big\{(x,u)\in B_{CR}\times B_{CR^{2}}:|\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}(x-x^{0})|<CR_{\ell}\big\},

the
L^{p}
-norm of the function

g_{\iota,\ell,j}=\mathbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}}F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{U})f

is rapidly decaying in
R
outside the ball

\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}(x^{0},\mu^{j})=\big\{(x,u)\in B_{3CR}\times B_{9CR^{2}}:|\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}(x-x^{0})|<6\kappa CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}\big\}.

That is, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there is some
C_{N,\gamma}>0
independent of
x^{0}
such that

(5.5)

\big\|\,g_{\iota,\ell,j}\big|_{\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}(x^{0},\mu^{j})}\big\|_{p}\leq C_{N,\gamma}R^{-\gamma N}\|F\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{p}.

Note that
5.5
is formulated for a general multiplier
F
, rather than specifically for
F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{\,\cdot\,})
. However, the function
g_{\iota,\ell,j}
is inherently localized by definition to the ball
\smash{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}}
via the cutoff function
\mathbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}}
.

The proof of
Proposition

5.2
relies on the following Plancherel type estimate:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}
be the convolution kernel of
F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{U})
. Then

(5.6)

\int_{G}\mathbf{1}_{E_{\ell}(\mu^{j})}(x)\left|\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}(x,u)\right|^{2}d(x,u)\leq C_{N}\big(R^{-\gamma N}\|F\|_{\infty}\big)^{2},

where
E_{\ell}(\mu^{j})
is the set of all
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
satisfying

|x|<4CR\quad\text{and}\quad|\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}x|\geq 5\kappa CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}.

Proof of
Lemma

5.3
.

By
Lemma

5.1
and Plancherel’s theorem, the left-hand side of (
5.6
) equals a constant times

(5.7)

\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{E_{\ell}(\mu^{j})}(x)\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\bigg|\int_{\ker J_{\mu}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\,\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\,\zeta_{j}(\mu)\\
\times\bigg[\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(P_{n}^{\mu}x)\bigg]e^{i\langle\xi,P_{0}^{\mu}x\rangle}\,d\xi\bigg|^{2}\,d\mu\,dx.

For
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, let
E_{\ell}(\mu)
be the set of all
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
such that

|x|<3CR\quad\text{and}\quad|\bar{P}^{\mu}x|\geq\kappa CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}.

Suppose that
x\in E_{\ell}(\mu^{j})
and
\mu\in\supp\zeta_{j}
. Then
|\bar{\mu}-\mu^{j}|<\delta
, where use again the notation
\bar{\mu}=|\mu|^{-1}\mu
. By
5.1
, using
\bar{P}^{\mu}={\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}}-P_{0}^{\mu}
,

|\bar{P}^{\mu}x-\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}\!x|=|P_{0}^{\mu}x-P_{0}^{\mu^{j}}\!x|\leq\kappa\,|\bar{\mu}-\mu^{j}|\,|x|\leq\kappa\delta|x|.

Since
\delta=R_{\ell}/R
and
|x|\leq 3CR
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\bar{P}^{\mu}x|

\displaystyle\geq|\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}x|-\kappa\delta|x|

\displaystyle\geq 5\kappa CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}-4\kappa CR_{\ell}\geq\kappa CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}.

Thus,
E_{\ell}(\mu^{j})\subseteq E_{\ell}(\mu)
if
\mu\in\supp\zeta_{j}
. Hence, if we additionally interchange the order of integration, we may bound
5.7
by

(5.8)

\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{E_{\ell}(\mu)}(x)\,\bigg|\int_{\ker J_{\mu}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\,\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\,\zeta_{j}(\mu)\\
\times\bigg[\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(P_{n}^{\mu}x)\bigg]e^{i\langle\xi,P_{0}^{\mu}x\rangle}\,d\xi\bigg|^{2}\,dx\,d\mu.

Since
|\bar{P}^{\mu}x|\geq\kappa CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}
for
x\in E_{\ell}(\mu)
,
5.8
is bounded by a constant times

(5.9)

(R_{\ell}R^{\gamma})^{-2N}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}|\bar{P}^{\mu}x|^{2N}\,\bigg|\int_{\ker J_{\mu}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\,\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\,\zeta_{j}(\mu)\\
\times\bigg[\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(P_{n}^{\mu}x)\bigg]e^{i\langle\xi,P_{0}^{\mu}x\rangle}\,d\xi\bigg|^{2}\,dx\,d\mu.

Recall that
P_{n}^{\mu}
is an orthogonal projection of rank
2r_{n}
. Thus, for any
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
, there is an orthogonal transformation
R_{\mu}\in O(d_{1})
such that

P_{n}^{\mu}R_{\mu}=R_{\mu}P_{n}\quad\text{for all }n\in\{0,\dots,N\},

where
P_{n}
denotes the orthogonal projection from
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}=\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2r_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2r_{N}}
onto the
n
-th layer. If we substitute
(v,y)=R_{\mu}^{-1}x
with
v\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}-r_{0}}
and use Plancherel, we see that
5.9
equals a constant times

(5.10)

(R_{\ell}R^{\gamma})^{-2N}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}}|y|^{2N}\,\bigg|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\,\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\,\zeta_{j}(\mu)\\
\times\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(P_{n}y)\bigg|^{2}\,dy\,d\xi\,d\mu,

where
\bar{d}_{1}=d_{1}-r_{0}
. We expand

|y|^{2N}=(|P_{1}y|^{2}+\dots+|P_{N}y|^{2})^{N}.

It is sufficient to show that for all
m_{1},\dots,m_{N}\in\mathbb{N}
with
m_{1}+\dots+m_{N}=N
, every term of the form

(5.11)

(R_{\ell}R^{\gamma})^{-2N}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}}\bigg(\prod_{n=1}^{N}|P_{n}y|^{2m_{n}}\bigg)\bigg|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\,\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\,\zeta_{j}(\mu)\\
\times\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(P_{n}y)\bigg|^{2}\,dy\,d\xi\,d\mu

is bounded by the right-hand side of
5.6
.
For
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
\lambda>0
, let

H^{(\lambda)}_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}=-\Delta_{z}+\tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{4}|z|^{2}

denote the (rescaled) Hermite operator on
\mathbb{R}^{2m}
. By
[
2
, Proposition 3.3]
(or alternatively
[
11
]
), the Hermite operator satisfies the sub-elliptic estimate

(5.12)

\left\lVert|\cdot|^{\beta}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2m})}\lesssim\lambda^{-\beta}\big\|\big(H_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}^{(\lambda)}\big)^{\beta/2}f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2m})},\quad\beta\geq 0.

By Equations (1.3.25) and (1.3.42) of
[
34
]
, the Laguerre functions
\smash{\varphi_{k}^{(\lambda,m)}}
are eigenfunctions of the
\lambda
-rescaled Hermite operator, that is,

H^{(\lambda)}_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}\varphi_{k}^{(\lambda,m)}=\left(2k+m\right)\lambda\,\varphi_{k}^{(\lambda,m)},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Using the sub-elliptic estimate (
5.12
) on every block of
\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}=\mathbb{R}^{2r_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2r_{N}}
,
5.11
can be dominated by a constant times

(5.13)

(R_{\ell}R^{\gamma})^{-2N}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}}\bigg|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\,\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\,\zeta_{j}(\mu)\\

Recall that
b_{n}^{\mu}>0
for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
. This extends to all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
since we have assumed that
\mu\mapsto\dim\ker J_{\mu}

\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}

S^{d_{2}-1}
under the continuous map

is a compact subset of

b_{n}^{\mu}\sim 1\quad\text{for all }\mu\in S^{d_{2}-1}\text{ and }n\in\{1,\dots,N\}.

b_{n}^{\mu}=\left|\mu\right|b_{n}^{\bar{\mu}}\sim|\mu|\sim 2^{-\ell}=R_{\ell}^{-1},\quad\text{where }\bar{\mu}=|\mu|^{-1}\mu.

Moreover, if

Hence, using orthogonality on
\smash{\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}}

) can be estimated by a constant times

R^{-2\gamma N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}}\left|F(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\,\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\,\zeta_{j}(\mu)\right|^{2}\\

]

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{k_{n}}^{b_{n}^{\mu}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\left(b_{n}^{\mu}\right)^{r_{n}}\binom{k_{n}+r_{n}-1}{k_{n}}

in the integral, then (

) can be bounded by a constant times

R^{-2\gamma N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}}\left|F(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\,\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\right|^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}^{\mu}\,d\xi\,d\mu.

and substituting

,
5.15
equals a constant times

Up to the additional factor

and a constant, this is
3.6
. Hence, by the same arguments as in the proof of

3.5
, we may bound

by a constant times

, which finishes the proof of

5.3
.

Proof of
Proposition

5.2
.

We interpolate between an
L^{1}
and an

-estimate. For the
L^{2}
-estimate, we discard all cut-off functions, which yields

\big\|\,g_{\iota,\ell,j}\big|_{\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}(x^{0},\mu^{j})}\big\|_{2}\lesssim\|F\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{2}.

Note that this bound is uniform in
j\in I
. For the
L^{1}
-estimate, we consider the sets

A_{\ell}(x^{\prime},\mu^{j}):=\big\{(x,u)\in B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}:|\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}\!(x-x^{\prime})|\geq 5\kappa CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}\big\},\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

Note that a given point
(x,u)\in G
lies in

whenever

(x,u)\in(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}(x^{0},\mu^{j}))\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}\quad\text{and}\quad(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in\supp f.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}
be the convolution kernel of
F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{U})
, that is,

\displaystyle F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{U})f(x,u)

\displaystyle=f*\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}(x,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\,\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\,d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}).

Using Fubini’s theorem, we get

\displaystyle\big\|\,g_{\iota,\ell,j}\big|_{\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}(x^{0},\mu^{j})}\big\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}\mathbf{1}_{(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}(x^{0},\mu^{j}))\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}}(x,u)\,\big|f*\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}(x,u)\big|\,d(x,u)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\supp f}\int_{A_{\ell}(x^{\prime},\mu^{j})}\big|f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\big|\,\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\big|\,d(x,u)\,d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{1}\sup_{(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in\supp f}\int_{A_{\ell}(x^{\prime},\mu^{j})}\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\big|\,d(x,u).

Recall that
|B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}|\sim R^{Q}
by
4.3
, where

denotes the homogeneous dimension of
G
. Hence, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{A_{\ell}(x^{\prime},\mu^{j})}\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\big|\,d(x,u)

(5.18)

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{Q/2}\bigg(\int_{A_{\ell}(x^{\prime},\mu^{j})}\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}\big(x-x^{\prime},u-u^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}[x^{\prime},x]\big)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\bigg)^{1/2}.

Let
E_{\ell}(\mu^{j})
be defined as in
Lemma

5.3
, that is,
E_{\ell}(\mu^{j})
is the set of all
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
such that

|x|<4CR\quad\text{and}\quad|\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}\!x|\geq 5\kappa CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}.

Since
|x^{\prime}|<CR
by assumption, we have
|x-x^{\prime}|<4CR
for
(x,u)\in B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}
. Thus,
x\in A_{\ell}(x^{\prime},\mu^{j})
implies
x-x^{\prime}\in E_{\ell}(\mu^{j})
, so by using
Lemma

5.3
, the right-hand side of
5.18
can be bounded by

R^{Q/2}\bigg(\int_{G}\mathbf{1}_{E_{\ell}(\mu^{j})}(x)\,\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim_{N}R^{Q/2-\gamma N}\|F\|_{2}.

Altogether, we end up with

(5.19)

\big\|\,g_{\iota,\ell,j}\big|_{\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}^{(\ell)}(x^{0},\mu^{j})}\big\|_{1}\lesssim_{N}R^{Q/2-\gamma N}\|F\|_{2}\|f\|_{1}.

Finally, interpolation between
5.19
and
5.17
yields
5.5
.
∎

6.
A localization on the second layer

Up to know, we did not require Assumptions
A
and
B
in the previous sections. From now on, we will require both assumptions. Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
. By
Proposition

5.2
, for
\ell\leq\iota
, the convolution kernel
\smash{\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell,j}}
of the operator
\smash{F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\chi(2^{\ell}U)\zeta_{j}(\mathbf{U})}
is essentially supported in the set of all
(x,u)\in G
such that

and

In this section, we show that we actually even have the stronger localization

on the second layer, which means that

is essentially supported in a ball with volume comparable to
\smash{R^{r_{0}}R_{\ell}^{\bar{d}_{1}}R_{\ell}^{d_{2}}R^{d_{2}}}

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose that
1\leq p<2
. Let
R=2^{\iota}
and
R_{\ell}=2^{\ell}
for
\iota\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell\in\{-\ell_{0},\dots,\iota\}
. Let

,
\zeta_{j}
, and
\mu^{j}\in\supp\zeta_{j}|_{S_{d_{2}-1}}

Proposition

5.2
, where
\delta=R/R_{\ell}
. Moreover, let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
. Given

and

\displaystyle A^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})

\displaystyle=\big\{(x,u)\in B_{C_{1}R}\times B_{C_{2}R^{2}}:|\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}\!x|<C_{1}R_{\ell}\big\}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})

outside the set

, there is some

independent of

(6.1)

\big\|\,h_{\iota,\ell,j}\big|_{\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j},u^{0})}\big\|_{p}\leq C_{N,\gamma,\psi,\chi}R^{-\gamma N}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{p}.

The proof of

is based on the following second layer weighted Plancherel estimate:

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be a bounded Borel function supported in a compact subset

and
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
. If
\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}
denotes the convolution kernel associated with

(6.2)

\int_{G}||u|^{\alpha}\,\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}(x,u)|^{2}\,dx\,du\leq C_{A,\chi}\,R_{\ell}^{d_{2}-2\alpha}\|F\|_{L_{2}^{\alpha}}^{2}

for all
\alpha\geq 0
,
\ell\geq-\ell_{0}
, and
j\in I
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}
be the convolution kernel associated with
F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)
.
By
[

, we have

\int_{G}||u|^{\alpha}\,\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(x,u)|^{2}\,d(x,u)\lesssim R_{\ell}^{d_{2}-2\alpha}\|F\|_{L_{2}^{\alpha}}^{2}.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{\mu}
denote again the partial Fourier transform of
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}
with respect to the second layer. Suppose
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
j_{0}\in J
. Since the sets

have only bounded overlap, we get

\displaystyle\int_{G}||u|^{\alpha}\,\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j_{0}}(x,u)|^{2}\,d(x,u)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{G}||u|^{\alpha}\,\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(x,u)|^{2}\,d(x,u)\lesssim R_{\ell}^{d_{2}-2\alpha}\|F\|_{L_{2}^{\alpha}}^{2}.

\displaystyle\hskip 105.2751pt\times\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(x-x^{\prime},u-u^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}[x^{\prime},x])\big|\,d(x,u)

6.2

\alpha>1/2
. Indeed, we obtain

\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\big\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}\cap(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j}))}F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})f\big\|_{p}\lesssim_{\gamma,N}R^{-\gamma N}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{2}\|f\|_{p}.

The parameter
\gamma>0
will be chosen later in the proof.

Hence, in place of
7.6
, it suffices to show

(7.8)

\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\sum_{j\in J}\big\|\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}}F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})\big(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})}\big)\big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{s}}\|f\|_{p}.

On the other hand, to prove
7.8
, it suffices to show

(7.9)

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}}F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})\big(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})}\big)\big\|_{p}\lesssim J^{-1}2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{s}}\,\big\|f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})}\big\|_{p}.

Indeed, thanks to the bounded overlap of the balls
\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})
, given
7.9
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\sum_{j\in J}\big\|\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}}F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})\big(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})}\big)\big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\sum_{j\in J}\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}}F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})\big(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})}\big)\big\|_{p}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=-\ell_{0}}^{\iota}\sum_{j\in J}J^{-1}2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{s}}\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\big\|f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})}\big\|_{p}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\tilde{\varepsilon}\iota}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{s}}\|f\|_{p}\qquad\text{for }0<\tilde{\varepsilon}<\varepsilon.

7.2.
Translation back to the origin

To show
7.5
, we use translation invariance to translate the balls
B_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})
and
\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})
to the origin.

Let
\lambda_{(x_{m,j}^{(\ell)},0)}
denote the left multiplication by
(x_{m,j}^{(\ell)},0)
on
G
. Note that

(7.10)

\lambda_{(x_{m,j}^{(\ell)},0)}(x,u)\in B_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})

implies
|\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}\!x|<CR_{\ell}
and
\big|u+\tfrac{1}{2}[x_{m,j}^{(\ell)},x]\big|\leq CR^{2}
, and thus in particular

|u|\lesssim R^{2}+|x_{m,j}^{(\ell)}|\left|x\right|\lesssim R^{2}.

Thus, there is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that
7.10
implies
(x,u)\in A^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})
, where

A^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j}):=\big\{(x,u)\in B_{CR}\times B_{C_{2}R^{2}}:|\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}\!x|<CR_{\ell}\big\}.

Similarly, if

\lambda_{(x_{m,j}^{(\ell)},0)}(x,u)\in\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})

then
(x,u)\in\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})
, where

\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j}):=\big\{(x,u)\in B_{3CR}\times B_{9C_{2}R^{2+\gamma}}:|\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}\!x|<6\kappa CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}\big\},

provided that
C_{2}>0
is chosen sufficiently large. Since
F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})
is left-invariant,

\big(F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})\big(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})}\big)\big)(\lambda_{(x_{m,j}^{(\ell)},0)}(x,u))=F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})\tilde{f}_{m,j}^{(\ell)}(x,u),

where
\tilde{f}_{m,j}^{(\ell)}(x,u):=f_{m,j}^{(\ell)}(\lambda_{(x_{m,j}^{(\ell)},0)}(x,u))
is supported in
A^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})
. Note that

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})}F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})\big(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})}\big)\big\|_{p}=\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})}F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})\tilde{f}_{m,j}^{(\ell)}\|_{p}.

Thus, in place of
7.9
, it suffices to show that there is some
\varepsilon>0
such that

(7.11)

\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})}F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})f\|_{p}\lesssim J^{-1}2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{s}}\|f\|_{p}

for all functions
f
supported in
A^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})
.

7.3.
Localization on the second layer

We decompose
A^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})
as

A^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})=\bigcup_{k=1}^{K_{\ell}}A^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j}),

where
A^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})\subseteq A^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})
are disjoint subsets such that

A^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})\subseteq\big\{(x,u)\in B_{CR}\times B_{C_{2}R^{2}}:|\bar{P}^{\mu^{j}}\!x|<CR_{\ell}\text{ and }|u-u^{(\ell)}_{k}|<C_{2}R_{\ell}R\big\},

and
u^{(\ell)}_{k}\in A^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})
satify
|u^{(\ell)}_{k}-u^{(\ell)}_{k^{\prime}}|>R_{\ell}R/2
for
k\neq k^{\prime}
. Moreover, let

\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j}):=\{(x,u)\in\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j}):|u-u^{(\ell)}_{k}|<6\kappa CR_{\ell}R^{1+\gamma}\}.

Then the maximal number
N_{\gamma}
of overlapping sets
\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})
is bounded by
N_{\gamma}\lesssim_{\iota}1
. Now, if
f
is supported in
A^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})
, we decompose it as

f=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{\ell}}f|_{A^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})}.

We decompose the function
h_{k,j}^{(\ell)}:=\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}(\mu^{j})}F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})(f|_{A^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})})
as

h_{k,j}^{(\ell)}=\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})}h_{k,j}^{(\ell)}+\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})}h_{k,j}^{(\ell)}.

Since
K_{\ell}\lesssim(R/R_{\ell})^{d_{2}}\lesssim R^{d_{2}}
,
Proposition

6.1
yields

\bigg\|\sum_{k=1}^{K_{\ell}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})}h_{k,j}^{(\ell)}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim_{\gamma,N}R^{-\gamma N}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{2}\|f\|_{p},\quad N>0.

On the other hand, since the balls
\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})
have bounded overlap, we obtain

\bigg\|\sum_{k=1}^{K_{\ell}}\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})}h_{k,j}^{(\ell)}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim_{\iota}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{K_{\ell}}\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})}h_{k,j}^{(\ell)}\|_{p}^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Moreover, note that

\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{K_{\ell}}\|f|_{A^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})}\|_{p}^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}=\|f\|_{p}.

Thus, to prove
7.11
, it suffices to show that there is some
\varepsilon>0
such that

(7.12)

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})}F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})f\big\|_{p}\lesssim J^{-1}2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{s}}\|f\|_{p}

for all functions
f
supported in
A^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})
.

7.4.
Applying the restriction type estimate

We apply the restriction type estimate of
Theorem

3.1
for caps of size
\delta=R/R_{\ell}
. Recall that our assumptions imply in particular
d_{1}\geq d_{2}
, whence

p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{1},d_{2})=\min\{p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{1}),p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})\}=p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2}).

Thus,
Theorem

3.1
yields

(7.13)

\lVert F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})\rVert_{p\to 2}\lesssim R_{\ell}^{-d_{2}/q}\Big(\frac{R_{\ell}}{R}\Big)^{\frac{d_{2}-1}{2}(1-\theta_{p})}\|(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\cdot}\,)\|_{2}^{1-\theta_{p}}\\
\times\|(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\cdot}\,)\|_{2^{\ell},2}^{\theta_{p}},

where
\theta_{p}\in[0,1]
satisfies
1/p=(1-\theta_{p})+\theta_{p}/p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})
.
Using the same Sobolev type embedding as in
7.4
, we can bound the right-hand side of
7.13
by a constant times

R_{\ell}^{-d_{2}/q}\Big(\frac{R_{\ell}}{R}\Big)^{\frac{d_{2}-1}{2}(1-\theta_{p})-\theta_{p}\tilde{s}}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{2}\quad\text{for all }\tilde{s}>1/2.

Let
J_{\mu}
be again be the matrix of
2.1
. Recall that
\mu\mapsto\dim\ker J_{\mu}
is constant on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
by
Assumption

A
. Let
r_{0}:=\dim\ker J_{\mu}
for
\mu\neq 0
and
\bar{d}_{1}:=d_{1}-r_{0}
. Then

|\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})|\lesssim R^{r_{0}}R_{\ell}^{\bar{d}_{1}}(R_{\ell}R)^{d_{2}}

Let again
q\in[2,\infty)
be such that
1/q=1/p-1/2
. Using Hölder’s inequality in combination with the restriction type estimate
7.13
, we get

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})}F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})f\big\|_{p}\lesssim_{\iota}\big(R^{r_{0}}R_{\ell}^{\bar{d}_{1}}(R_{\ell}R)^{d_{2}}\big)^{1/q}\|F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})f\|_{2}

(7.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(R^{r_{0}}R_{\ell}^{\bar{d}_{1}}(R_{\ell}R)^{d_{2}}\big)^{1/q}R_{\ell}^{-d_{2}/q}\Big(\frac{R_{\ell}}{R}\Big)^{\frac{d_{2}-1}{2}(1-\theta_{p})-\theta_{p}\tilde{s}}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{2}\|f\|_{p}.

Using

\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{2}\sim R^{-d/q}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{d/q}}=R^{-(r_{0}+\bar{d}_{1}+d_{2})/q}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{d/q}},

we see that
7.14
is comparable to

\Big(\frac{R_{\ell}}{R}\Big)^{\bar{d}_{1}/q+\frac{d_{2}-1}{2}(1-\theta_{p})-\theta_{p}\tilde{s}}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{d/q}}\|f\|_{p}.

Recall that the number of caps in our decomposition is

J\sim\delta^{-(d_{2}-1)}=\Big(\frac{R_{\ell}}{R}\Big)^{-(d_{2}-1)}.

This implies

(7.15)

J\,\|\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{A}^{(\ell)}_{k}(\mu^{j})}F_{\ell,j}^{(\iota)}(L,\mathbf{U})f\big\|_{p}\lesssim\Big(\frac{R_{\ell}}{R}\Big)^{\bar{d}_{1}/q+\frac{d_{2}-1}{2}(1-\theta_{p})-\theta_{p}\tilde{s}-(d_{2}-1)}\\
\times\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{d/q}}\|f\|_{p}.

To establish
7.12
and complete the proof of
Theorem

4.1
, it suffices to ensure that

(7.16)

\frac{\bar{d}_{1}}{q}-\theta_{p}\frac{d_{2}}{2}-\frac{d_{2}-1}{2}\geq 0.

Indeed, suppose that
7.16
is satisfied. Note that

\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{d/q}}\sim R^{d/q-s}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{s}}

and that we have assumed
s>d/q
in
Theorem

4.1
. Thus, choosing
\gamma>0
small enough and
\tilde{s}>1/2
sufficiently close to
1/2
, we can ensure that the exponent in
7.15
is positive.

Using the identities

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{p}=\frac{p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}},

we see that
7.16
is equivalent to

\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\leq\frac{\bar{d}_{1}-(d_{2}-1)}{2\bar{d}_{1}+d_{2}\,p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})^{\prime}}.

Since

p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})^{\prime}=\frac{2(d_{2}+1)}{d_{2}-1},

this in turn is equivalent to

p\leq\frac{2\bar{d}_{1}+d_{2}\,p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})^{\prime}}{\bar{d}_{1}+d_{2}\,p_{\mathrm{ST}}(d_{2})^{\prime}+(d_{2}-1)}\\
=\frac{2\bar{d}_{1}(d_{2}-1)+2d_{2}(d_{2}+1)}{\bar{d}_{1}(d_{2}-1)+3d_{2}^{2}+1}.

This finishes the proof of
Theorem

4.1
.
\square

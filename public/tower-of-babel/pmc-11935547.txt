Assessment of Inflammatory Biomarkers and Incident Atrial Fibrillation in Older Adults
Authors: Sanyog G. Shitole, Susan R. Heckbert, Gregory M. Marcus, Sanjiv J. Shah, Nona Sotoodehnia, Jeremy D. Walston, Alexander P. Reiner, Russell P. Tracy, Bruce M. Psaty, Jorge R. Kizer
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAvailable evidence supports the importance of inflammation in atrial fibrillation (AF) pathogenesis, yet general anti‐inflammatory therapies have failed to show benefit for prevention of the arrhythmia. Better understanding of the specific inflammatory pathways involved is necessary to advance therapeutics.Methods and ResultsWe evaluated 9 circulating markers of inflammation measured by immunoassays and incidence of AF in a population‐based older cohort. Biomarkers included measures of general inflammation and the NLR (nucleotide‐binding oligomerization domain‐like receptor) family pyrin domain containing 3 inflammasome, TNF‐α (tumor necrosis factor α), monocyte activation markers, and sIL‐2 (soluble interleukin‐2). Among 5726 participants (median age 72 years), 1836 developed AF over median follow‐up of 11.5 years. After adjustment for conventional risk factors, 5 biomarkers were positively associated with incident AF: IL‐6 (interleukin‐6), hazard ratio (HR), 1.14 (95% CI, 1.07–1.21); hs‐CRP (high‐sensitivity C‐reactive protein), HR, 1.05 (95% CI, 1.01–1.09); white blood cell count, HR, 1.18 (95% CI, 1.04–1.35); sTNFR1 (soluble TNF receptor 1), HR, 1.21 (95% CI, 1.05–1.39); and sIL‐2Rα (sIL‐2 receptor α), HR, 1.16 (95% CI, 1.05–1.29) (all per doubling of biomarker). sCD14, sCD163, IL‐18, and IL‐1 receptor antagonist showed no association with AF. Upon concurrent adjustment for all biomarkers, only IL‐6 remained significantly associated with the arrhythmia, HR, 1.17 (95% CI, 1.07–1.26).ConclusionsAmong older adults, IL‐6, hs‐CRP, white blood cell count, sTNFR1, and sIL‐2Rα were positively associated with incident AF, but only IL‐6 retained significance on concurrent adjustment. These findings newly document associations for sTNFR1 and sIL‐2Rα and lend support to a preeminent role for IL‐6 in development of this arrhythmia. The efficacy of IL‐6 blockade for AF prevention awaits completion of appropriate clinical trials.

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia. 1 It is estimated that AF prevalence in the United States will surpass 12 million by 2030. 2 AF is associated with increased risk of heart failure, stroke, and overall mortality, along with a decrease in quality of life. 3 The pathophysiology and mechanistic underpinnings of AF have not been fully elucidated, hampering efforts to develop effective approaches for its prevention and treatment. 3

It is well recognized that inflammation plays a role in the development of AF, 4 much as it does for cardiovascular disease (CVD) more generally. 5 Both myocardial (atrial) inflammation and systemic inflammation have been implicated in AF pathogenesis. Yet, traditional anti‐inflammatory agents have shown little to no benefit in the treatment of AF, 6 and more targeted anti‐inflammatory therapies that have shown benefit for atherosclerotic CVD 7 have as yet not been tested for AF prevention.

Prospective epidemiological studies have linked several inflammatory markers of innate immune pathways, such as hs‐CRP (high‐sensitivity C‐reactive protein), IL‐6 (interleukin‐6), and white blood cell count (WBC), to incident AF. 8 , 9 , 10 Additional studies have examined contributions of other markers including interleukin‐18 (IL‐18) to AF. 11 Yet several key inflammatory markers and their pathways have not been examined with accurate and precise immunoassays that afford absolute quantification. These have instead been assessed with multiplexed assays of variable precision 12 or high‐throughput proteomics offering relative quantification, 13 which have yielded null results. In particular, the contributions to AF of several major inflammatory markers as measured by high‐quality immunoassay—namely, soluble CD14 (sCD14), sCD163, IL1‐RA (interleukin‐1 receptor antagonist), sTNFR1 (soluble tumor necrosis factor receptor 1), and sIL‐2Rα (soluble interleukin 2 receptor α)—remain uncertain. We leveraged availability of immunoassay measurements of these circulating inflammatory markers in a population‐based study of older adults, previously performed to assess other aging‐related or cardiovascular outcomes, to evaluate their associations with incident AF. Our study included markers of or relating to the central proinflammatory orchestrator TNFα (tumor necrosis factor α); the proximal NLRP3 (NOD‐, LRR‐ and pyrin domain containing protein 3) inflammasome activator IL‐1 or its downstream cytokine, IL‐18; monocyte activation, such as sCD14 and sCD163; or a cytokine regulator of adaptive immunity, IL‐2. In the case of TNFα, IL‐1, and IL‐2, soluble forms of their transmembrane receptors or related antagonists can better reflect pathway activation than their short‐lived ligands in circulation. 14 , 15 , 16 Hence, sTNFR1, IL1‐RA, and sIL‐2Rα were measured to probe these pathways instead. For the present investigation, we sought to evaluate the associations of these 9 available inflammatory markers with AF individually, as well as jointly, to illuminate their relative roles, and motivate their study as potential therapeutic targets for this disorder.

METHODS

Data used for the current study are available from the CHS (Cardiovascular Health Study) Coordinating Center upon reasonable request.

Study Population

The design and rationale of CHS, a prospective population‐based cohort study of older men and women, has been documented before. 17 Briefly, candidates were randomly sampled for recruitment from Medicare eligibility lists from 4 US field centers: Forsyth County, NC; Sacramento County, CA; Washington County, MD; and Pittsburgh, PA. Excluded individuals were those who were institutionalized, wheelchair bound, in hospice, receiving treatment for cancer, not expected to remain in the area for the next 3 years, or unable to give informed consent. The first wave of enrollment (first cohort) consisted of 5201 participants from 1988 to 1989. A second wave of enrollment (second cohort) added 687 primarily Black participants from 1992 to 1993 for a total cohort of 5888 individuals. Participants underwent evaluation for demographic and lifestyle factors, physical examination, ECG, and blood collection for laboratory measurement and storage using standardized protocols during in‐person visits, as described before. 17 , 18 , 19 , 20 Participants were followed through semiannual contacts, with in‐person exams alternating with telephone calls through 1999 and telephone calls thereafter. Follow‐up for the current study was through June 2015. We used the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology cohort reporting guidelines for the current study. 21 Current study was approved by the institutional review board of each field center and all participants provided written informed consent.

Measurement of Biomarkers

Blood samples from the baseline visit stored at −80 °C were used to measure the nine inflammatory biomarkers as described in detail before. 22 , 23 , 24 , 25 , 26 , 27 Commercially available ELISA was used to measure sCD14, IL‐6, sCD163, and sIL‐2Rα (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN). An ultrasensitive ELISA developed at the University of Vermont was used to measure hs‐CRP. WBC count was measured at the participating clinical centers using the following instruments: Coulter Stack S cell counter (Coulter, Inc., Hialeah, FL; University of Pittsburgh, Pittsburgh, PA, and University of California, Davis, Davis, CA) and the Sysmex NE8000 counter (Toa Electronics, Inc., Chicago, IL; Wake Forest University, Winston‐Salem, NC, and Johns Hopkins University, Baltimore, MD). IL‐18, IL‐1RA, and sTNFR1 were measured by the Mesoscale chemiluminescent system (Mesoscale Discovery Technology, Gaithersburg, MD). Assay precisions and detectable limits have been described previously. 22 , 23 , 24 , 25 , 26 , 27

Assessment for AF

Ascertainment of incident AF in CHS has been described before. 28 Inpatient diagnoses were examined throughout the follow‐up period to identify incident AF using the International Classification of Diseases, Ninth Revision (ICD‐9) codes. Incident AF was also ascertained between 1989 to 1990 and 1998 to 1999 using annual study electrocardiograms.

Covariates

Race and ethnicity were defined by self‐report at baseline. Trained personnel performed anthropometric and blood pressure measurements. Data on lifestyle factors, medical history, and medication use were collected by questionnaire. Heavy alcohol use was defined as consumption of >14 drinks/week in men or >7 drinks/week in women. Diabetes was defined as fasting glucose ≥126 mg/dL, random glucose ≥200 mg/dL, or use of antihyperglycemic medication at baseline. History of coronary heart disease (CHD), stroke, transient ischemic attack (TIA), and heart failure at baseline was ascertained using a combination of CHS questionnaires, medical‐record review, and physician confirmation. 18 Estimated glomerular filtration rate was calculated using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology equation from serum creatinine. 29 NT‐proBNP (N‐terminal prohormone of brain natriuretic peptide) was measured as described before. 30

Statistical Analysis

We examined the correlations of the 9 inflammatory biomarkers with each other and with continuous covariates using Spearman's ρ. For categorical covariates, we assessed median and interquartile ranges and tested for differences between categories using the Mann–Whitney U test or Kruskal–Wallis test as appropriate. Because of positive skew and for ease of interpretability, all biomarkers were converted using base 2 logarithms to test associations with incident AF, yielding the risk per doubling of each biomarker. Cox proportional hazards models were used to evaluate associations of biomarkers with incident AF on sequential adjustment. Model covariates were chosen based on known biology and prior literature. Model 1 was adjusted for age, race, and sex. Model 2 was additionally adjusted for height, weight, systolic blood pressure, hypertension medication, diabetes, smoking status, heavy alcohol use, CHD, stroke, TIA, heart failure, and estimated glomerular filtration rate. Model 3 included adjustment for all inflammatory biomarkers. Collinearity among inflammatory biomarkers in this model was assessed by determination of the variance inflation factor. To evaluate the proportional hazards assumption, we examined plots for Schoenfeld residuals for Model 2 for all 9 biomarkers. For the linearity assumption, we examined plots of Martingale residuals for Model 2 for all 9 biomarkers. To assess influential outliers, we examined deviance residual plots. In an exploratory analysis, we adjusted for interval development of CHD, stroke, or TIA before onset of AF in addition to Model 2 covariates. We also examined the associations of hs‐CRP, WBC, sTNFR1, and sIL‐2Rα with AF when adding only IL‐6 to Model 2. Sensitivity analyses included examining the associations restricted to the first 10 years of follow‐up and after moving the start of follow‐up to 2 years later and excluding participants with incident AF during this initial 2‐year interval. We conducted additional sensitivity analyses that assessed the impact on the biomarker–AF associations of excluding participants receiving oral glucocorticoids or colchicine or of additionally adjusting by baseline level of NT‐proBNP. SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC) was used for all analyses. A 2‐tailed P<0.05 was considered statistically significant for main analyses, and P<0.01 for interaction testing.

Over a median follow‐up of 11.5 years (interquartile range 6.2–17.7 years), there were 1836 AF events. Table 2 shows the risk estimates for incident AF per doubling of each of the 9 biomarkers of inflammation at different levels of adjustment. sCD14, sCD163, and IL‐1RA showed significant positive associations with AF in the minimally adjusted model (Model 1), but these associations were attenuated and not significant in the fully adjusted model (Model 2). IL‐18 showed no association with incident AF at either level of adjustment. By contrast, IL‐6, hsCRP, WBC, sTNFR1, and sIL‐2Rα exhibited significant positive associations in the minimal, and after varying degrees of attenuation, in the fully adjusted model, corresponding to 14%, 5%, 18%, 21%, and 16% higher risks of AF, respectively, per doubling of their levels. No violation of the proportional hazards assumption was detected (Figure S1). There was also no evidence of violation of the linearity assumption (Figure S2) or of the presence of influential outliers (Figure S3), because removal of outliers did not meaningfully change in our results (Table S2).

After concurrent adjustment of all inflammatory biomarkers (Model 3), the association remained significant only for IL‐6, with a 17% increased risk of incident AF per doubling. Testing revealed no evidence of collinearity between inflammatory biomarkers in Model 3. The addition of IL‐6 alone as a covariate to the fully adjusted model for hs‐CRP, WBC, sTNFR1, and sIL‐2Rα rendered the associations nonsignificant (Table S3). Additional adjustment for intercurrent CHD, stroke and TIA did not meaningfully alter associations in the fully adjusted model (Table S4).

Sensitivity analyses restricting follow‐up to the first 10 years (yielding 1046 incident AF events) or excluding incident AF during the initial 2 years (resulting in 1642 incident AF events) are shown in Table S5. These showed generally consistent associations for all biomarkers in fully adjusted models, which tended to be stronger with truncation of follow‐up to 10 years. This was also the case for the model adjusting concurrently for all inflammatory biomarkers, except that a significant inverse association emerged for IL‐1RA with AF during the shortened follow‐up interval, corresponding to a 13% lower risk per doubling of the biomarker.

Additional sensitivity analyses excluding participants on oral glucocorticoids or colchicine did not show any material difference compared with the primary results (Table S5). After additional adjustment for NT‐proBNP, the significant associations observed for IL‐6, hs‐CRP, WBC, sTNFR1, and sIL‐2Rα were substantially attenuated, and all except those for IL‐6 became nonsignificant (Table S6).

Exploration of interaction by sex or race revealed one such instance of significant interaction. This was for IL‐1RA's relationship with AF, which was modified by sex (P=0.005). The interaction was such that IL‐1RA was positively associated with incident AF in men (HR, 1.15 per doubling [95% CI, 1.04–1.26], P=0.006), but inversely associated with AF in women (HR, 0.80 per doubling [95% CI, 0.82–0.99], P=0.022).

DISCUSSION

Main Findings

In this study of 9 inflammatory biomarkers in men and women aged ≥65 years, we found that 5—IL‐6, hsCRP, WBC, sTNFR1, and sIL‐2Rα—were significantly associated with increased risk of incident AF after adjusting for demographic or clinical covariates over long‐term follow‐up. The 4 remaining biomarkers—sCD14, sCD163, IL‐18, and IL‐1RA—did not show significant associations with incident AF. All but 1 of the observed biomarker associations became nonsignificant upon concurrent adjustment for other inflammatory biomarkers. Only IL‐6 maintained its significant association with incident AF, and addition of IL‐6 alone to fully adjusted models for hsCRP, WBC, sTNFR1, and sIL‐2Rα also rendered their associations with AF nonsignificant.

Prior Literature

Multiple studies have linked inflammatory markers to incident CVD events. These include general markers of inflammation such as WBC, hsCRP, TNFα, or its receptors. 8 , 9 , 31 They also include markers of NLRP3 inflammasome activation or their receptors, including IL‐6 and IL‐18, 32 , 33 or innate immune markers of monocyte activation, such as sCD14 and sCD163. 22 , 34 The adaptive immune cytokine IL‐2 and its receptor have also been implicated in CVD. 23 For AF in particular, a similar role for inflammation is recognized. 4 Prospective studies of both sexes have identified blood levels of hs‐CRP, IL‐6, and WBC to be positively associated with AF, 8 , 9 , 10 though this was not observed for IL‐1β, TNFα, IL‐7, IL‐8, and IL‐10 in postmenopausal women. 35 In the case of IL‐6, Mendelian randomization analysis has supported a causal relationship of IL‐6‐signaling pathways with AF, 36 as has been seen for CVD. 37

Prior assessment of other inflammatory biomarkers or pathways in AF in the prospective epidemiologic setting has been performed in the setting of a multiplexed antibody‐based assay, 12 , 38 which reported an interassay coefficient of variation of 22.8% for IL1‐RA, and an intraassay coefficient of variation of 30.6% for sIL‐2Rα. These inflammatory biomarkers or pathways have also been evaluated with high‐throughput proteomics platforms that yield relative, but not absolute, quantification and whose accuracy for protein targets is lower than for validated immunoassays. 39 None of these studies identified significant associations for inflammatory markers in multivariable analyses, although in the case of the high‐throughput proteomic studies, whose goal was discovery of novel proteins and pathways in >1000 proteins, the multiple‐testing burden was high. 13 , 40 To our knowledge, the relationships of individually measured sTNFR1, IL1‐RA, sIL‐2Rα, sCD14, and sCD163 by immunoassay with incident AF have not been previously reported. The present investigation is the first to simultaneously evaluate a multitude of circulating inflammatory markers reflecting innate and adaptive immunity assessed by individual immunoassays in a cohort of older adults.

Implications of Findings

Our finding that WBC and hs‐CRP were associated with AF corresponds to their role as generalized markers of inflammation, consistent with known contributions of general and local inflammation in the development of AF. 4 , 41 Similarly, sTNFR1 and IL‐6's associations with AF reflect activation of broad innate immune pathways, namely, activation of TNFα pathways and the NLRP‐3 inflammasome pathway. 41 , 42

As relates to TNFα, this molecule has central proinflammatory actions, inducing inflammatory gene expression and upregulating secretion of multiple cytokines, including IL‐6. 42 Prior work has linked TNFα activity to increased risk of AF. 31 , 43 Yet the association of sTNFR1 with AF became null after concurrent adjustment for IL‐6, suggesting that its effects may be largely driven by the latter cytokine. Hence, although pharmacologic approaches to TNFα antagonism exist, 44 our findings, together with other evidence, suggest that strategies focused on countering IL‐6 should take precedence in future testing.

Turning to IL1‐RA, an inhibitor of IL‐1 receptor type 1 signaling that may reflect upregulation of its ligands, IL‐1α and IL‐1β, showed no association with AF incidence. 16 This finding is consistent with a Mendelian randomization analysis that did not find an association between genetic upregulation of IL‐1RA and AF, though it did document a higher associated risk of CHD. 36 We did, however, uncover effect modification of the IL1‐RA relationship with AF by sex, such that a positive association was seen in men, but an inverse association was instead observed in women. The basis for these opposite sex‐related associations is uncertain but given the exploratory nature of our interaction analyses and in the setting of multiple testing, this finding needs to be interpreted with caution pending further study.

There was no association observed for IL‐18, a downstream cytokine in the NLRP‐3 inflammasome pathway, which is compatible with null results from recent prospective observational 11 and Mendelian randomization studies. 36 Taken together, these findings indicate that IL‐18 is unlikely to be a meaningful actor in AF. Likewise, our finding that soluble monocyte markers were not associated with incident AF suggests that monocyte activation through pathogen‐associated molecular patterns in the case of sCD14, 34 or monocyte commitment to the M2 phenotype in the case of sCD163, 22 are not important contributors to onset of this arrhythmia.

By contrast, the association noted for sIL‐2Rα would tend to implicate adaptive immunity. IL‐2 is a driver of regulatory T‐cell development, 45 which fits with the finding that T cells drive vascular and myocardial inflammation that can lead to the left atrial anatomical and electrical remodeling that constitute the substrate for AF. 41 This association did not persist, however, after simultaneous adjustment for IL‐6, suggesting that, as relates to AF, sIL‐2Rα's actions could arise from, or be mediated by, IL‐6. Our findings are compatible with null results from a Mendelian randomization analysis of sIL‐2Rα and AF, 36 which argue against therapies targeting sIL‐2Rα as a fruitful strategy for AF prevention.

As noted, only IL‐6 remained significant after concurrent adjustment by all other biomarkers, whereas none of the significant biomarker associations with AF retained significance upon adjustment for IL‐6. Our findings extend previous observations linking circulating levels of IL‐6 with AF, supporting the central role of IL‐6 in the pathogenesis of this arrhythmia. Indeed, use of genetic proxies to capture IL‐6 receptor mediated downregulation of IL‐6 signaling, Mendelian randomization analyses have provided evidence to support AF‐risk reduction by IL‐6 signaling blockade. 46 In addition, recent work in murine models has shed light on local inflammatory actions of IL‐6 by amplifying the effects of IL‐1β on Ca2+ handling, 47 which can promote arrhythmias. Systemic IL‐6 can also act locally by downregulating atrial connexin levels leading to electrical remodeling. 48 The ongoing ZEUS (Effects of Ziltivekimab Versus Placebo on Cardiovascular Outcomes in Participants With Established Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease, Chronic Kidney Disease and Systemic Inflammation) and HERMES 49 (Effects of Ziltivekimab Versus Placebo on Morbidity and Mortality in Patients With Heart Failure With Mildly Reduced or Preserved Ejection Fraction and Systemic Inflammation) trials aim to examine the effect of ziltivekimab, an IL‐6 ligand monoclonal antibody, on CVD outcomes with AF count as a secondary outcome. Preliminary results of these studies, if positive, would facilitate a larger AF‐focused trial to elucidate the protective role of IL‐6 blockade for AF.

In a sensitivity analysis that additionally adjusted for NT‐proBNP, all significant biomarker associations with AF were attenuated considerably. Inflammatory processes influencing the myocardium can increase ventricular and atrial stretch, and with it, NT‐proBNP level, 50 , 51 eventuating in the development of AF. 30 , 50 , 51 , 52 , 53 This places NT‐proBNP on the causal pathway between inflammatory markers and AF. High NT‐proBNP can also reflect cardiac dysfunction that may heighten inflammation, placing it upstream of inflammatory markers, though this would tend to be the more minor contributor to the relationship between inflammatory markers and NT‐proBNP. Thus, the observed attenuation of inflammatory marker relations with AF is likely to predominantly reflect mediation, although it is notable that the association for IL‐6 with the arrhythmia remained significant even after such adjustment, reinforcing the robustness of this relationship.

Limitations

Our study has the following limitations. Given the observational study design, the causal basis of significant biomarker associations with incident AF cannot be determined. We used discharge ICD codes and annual ECGs to identify AF, such that paroxysmal AF may have been underascertained. The resulting bias, however, would be toward the null hypothesis. Because of strong prior probabilities for the inflammatory biomarkers considered, and in an effort to reduce type 2 error, we did not correct for multiple comparisons. We had measurements for all biomarkers only at baseline. Thus, the associations reported for 1‐time measurement of biomarkers are weaker than would have been obtained from repeated assessment of the biomarkers over time. The present work focused on association of biomarkers with incident AF, and not on risk prediction. Yet the fact that only 1 inflammatory marker retained a significant association upon concurrent adjustment makes it unlikely that these markers could be combined to improve prediction of AF. Last, study participants were mainly identified as White or Black and ≥65 years of age. Generalizability of our results to wider sections of the population not represented in our study is uncertain.

CONCLUSIONS

In this cohort of men and women ≥65 years old, IL‐6, hsCRP, WBC, sTNFR1, and sIL‐2Rα were significantly associated with increased risk of incident AF. Only IL‐6 continued to maintain a significant association with incident AF after simultaneous adjustment for other markers. Our findings lend further support to the primacy of IL‐6 pathways in the development of AF in older adults and provide impetus for examining IL‐6 blockade as a potential strategy for AF prevention.

Nonlinear fractional-periodic boundary value problems with Hilfer fractional derivative: existence and numerical approximations of solutions Present address
Niels Goedegebure, Kateryna Marynets

Abstract
We prove conditions for existence of analytical solutions for boundary value problems with the Hilfer fractional derivative, generalizing the commonly used Riemann-Liouville and Caputo operators. The boundary values, referred to in this paper as fractional-periodic, are fractional integral conditions generalizing recurrent solution values for the non-Caputo case of the Hilfer fractional derivative. Analytical solutions to the studied problem are obtained using a perturbation of the corresponding initial value problem with enforced boundary conditions. In general, solutions to the boundary value problem are singular for t ↓ 0 t\downarrow 0 . To overcome this singularity, we construct a sequence of converging solutions in a weighted continuous function space. We present a Bernstein splines-based implementation to numerically approximate solutions. We prove convergence of the numerical method, providing convergence criteria and asymptotic convergence rates. Numerical examples show empirical convergence results corresponding with the theoretical bounds. Moreover, the method is able to approximate the singular behavior of solutions and is demonstrated to converge for nonlinear problems. Finally, we apply a grid search to obtain correspondence to the original, non-perturbed system.



Keywords:

fractional boundary value problems,
existence and uniqueness,
Hilfer fractional derivative,
splines,
numerical approximation

Introduction

Fractional calculus generalizes classical differentiation and integration to non-integer orders, offering tools to model memory and hereditary effects in dynamical systems
[
15
,
4
]
.
Fractional differential equations (FDEs), which involve derivatives of order
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, are extensively used in fields such as control theory, viscoelasticity and anomalous diffusion
[
24
,
19
,
23
,
12
,
18
]
.
In particular, fractional boundary value problems (FBVPs) study the solutions to nonlocal FDEs with boundary constraints.
Specifically of interest are FBVPs with the same boundary conditions on all boundaries, as solutions to FDEs are in general shown to be non-periodic
[
27
,
14
,
9
]
.
In what follows, we will denote this class of FBVPs as
fractional-periodic
BVPs.

Two of the most commonly used fractional differential operators are those of the Riemann-Liouville and the Caputo type, as introduced and discussed extensively in e.g.
[
15
,
23
]
.
The Caputo fractional derivative provides two beneficial properties for application in physical systems:
it allows for integer-order initial or boundary conditions and it satisfies the integer-order intuition that constant functions are in the operator kernel set of the fractional derivative.
The Riemann-Liouville operator on the other hand requires fractional-order initial or boundary conditions, but naturally satisfies the property of acting as a left-inverse of the canonical Riemann-Liouville fractional integral operator

[
23
]
.

The Hilfer fractional derivative, as introduced in
[
12
]
, of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
and type
\beta\in[0,1]
interpolates between the Riemann-Liouville (
\beta=0
) and Caputo fractional derivatives (
\beta=1
).
When applied to FDEs, this operator provides a direct intuition on the choice of the fractional differential operator on initial or boundary conditions and solution behavior.
Since
\beta
acts as an interpolation parameter, the Hilfer fractional derivative can also be applied to homotopy-like methods, taking for instance a Caputo fractional derivative solution as starting point for a solution to the corresponding Riemann-Liouville problem.
Moreover, when applied to FDEs, the operator allows for parametrization of the fractional order of initial or boundary conditions by the choice of
\beta
.
Existence and uniqueness for Cauchy-like initial value problems (IVPs) using Hilfer fractional derivatives is proven in
8
, of which we implement a numerical splines method in
[
10
]
.

Existing literature on fractional-periodic BVPs using Caputo fractional derivatives includes the work of
[
13
,
6
,
5
]
, with numerical approximations outlined in e.g.
[
7
]
.
For non-Caputo derivative BVPs,
17
consider existence and approximations to FBVPs for generalized Hilfer-Prabhakar operators.
In the paper of
31
, the Riemann-Liouville derivative operator is chosen with fixed boundary solution values of
0
.

Numerous existence and uniqueness results for boundary value systems with Hilfer fractional derivatives are presented in literature.
Results on systems with a homogeneous left-time domain boundary value are given in e.g.

[
3
,
29
,
20
,
26
,
1
]
.
Homogeneous fractional-periodic problems are studied by
[
22
,
2
]
and in
[
30
]
for a system of fractional partial differential equation (FPDE) with Hilfer fractional derivatives.
A nonhomogeneous, coupled BVP for Hilfer fractional derivatives is studied by
28
.
However, we note that here, the right boundary point is proportional to the solution, whereas the left boundary point depends on the fractional integral as
t\downarrow a
.
This results in a less straightforward interpretation of the boundary conditions when generalizing fractional-periodic Caputo problems, since the two boundary conditions depend on different type of quantities in the non-Caputo case.

As to our knowledge, no literature exists on existence and uniqueness or numerical approximations of Hilfer derivative BVPs with fractional-periodic boundary conditions.
To fill this gap, we consider the following FBVP:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{{}_{0}}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}\mathbf{x}(t)=\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{x}(t)),\quad&0<t<T,\quad 0<\alpha<1,\quad 0\leq\beta\leq 1,\\
{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{0}^{1-\gamma}\mathbf{x}(0)={{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{T}^{1-\gamma}\mathbf{x}(T),\quad&\gamma=\alpha+\beta-\alpha\beta.\end{cases}

(1)

Here,
\mathbf{x}:(0,T]\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{d}
are the unknown solutions to the system characterized by the nonlinear vector-valued function
\mathbf{f}:(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{d}

The fractional differential operator

represents the Hilfer fractional derivative of order

The choice of

[

We remark that, unless otherwise specified, the operations

\|\cdot\|

2.1
Fractional calculus

Definition 2.1

(Left-sided Riemann-Liouville fractional integral,

For

For additional notational clarity, we will use

Proposition 2.1

[

]
)

For

\displaystyle({{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{b}^{\alpha}{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{b}^{\alpha^{\prime}})f(t)={{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{b}^{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}f(t),

almost everywhere for

Furthermore, if

Definition 2.2

(Beta function,
[

]
)

For

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{1}\vartheta^{a-1}(1-\vartheta)^{b-1}\,\mathrm{d}\vartheta.

Furthermore, it holds that

Definition 2.3

(Incomplete beta function,
[

]
)

For

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{z}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{z}\vartheta^{a-1}(1-\vartheta)^{b-1}\,\mathrm{d}\vartheta.

(5)

Proposition 2.2

(Fractional integral of a monomial,
[
15
]
)

For
\alpha>0,k>-1

-fractional integral of a monomial of degree

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}t^{k}=\frac{\Gamma(k+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+k+1)}t^{\alpha+k}=\frac{t^{\alpha+k}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathrm{B}(k+1,\alpha).

Proposition 2.3

(Fractional integral of monomial with left-local support,
[

]
)

For

,

-fractional integral on

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{b}^{\alpha}t^{k}=\frac{t^{\alpha+k}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathrm{B}_{b/t}(k+1,\alpha).

Proposition 2.4

(Fractional integral of monomial with right-local support,

)

For

\displaystyle{{}_{b}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}t^{k}=\frac{t^{\alpha+k}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathrm{B}_{1-b/t}(\alpha,k+1).

We define fractional differentiation in the Hilfer fractional derivative sense:

Definition 2.4

(Hilfer fractional derivative,
[

]
)
.

The Hilfer fractional derivative of order
0<\alpha<1
and type
0\leq\beta\leq 1

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}={{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\beta(1-\alpha)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{(1-\beta)(1-\alpha)}.

Remark 2.1

(Interpolation of Caputo and Riemann-Liouville derivative)
.

The Hilfer fractional derivative interpolates two of the most commonly used fractional derivative operators:

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}=\begin{cases}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{1-\alpha},\quad\text{for}\quad\beta=0,\quad\text{(Riemann-Liouville derivative)},\\
{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{1-\alpha}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t},\quad\text{for}\quad\beta=1,\quad\text{(Caputo derivative)}.\end{cases}

(10)

To obtain solutions to Hilfer FDEs,

introduce the following

\displaystyle C_{1-\gamma}[0,T]=\{f:(0,T]\to\mathbb{R}:t^{1-\gamma}f\in C[0,T]\},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C_{1-\gamma}[0,T]}=\sup_{t\in(0,T]}|t^{1-\gamma}f(t)|.

We note that

is complete with the choice of this norm.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle C^{\vartheta}_{1-\gamma}[0,T]=\{f\in C_{1-\gamma}[0,T],\,{{}_{0}}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\vartheta,0}f(t)\in C_{1-\gamma}[0,T]\}.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{{}_{0}}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}\mathbf{x}(t)=\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{x}(t)),\quad&0<t\leq T,\quad 0<\alpha<1,\quad 0\leq\beta\leq 1,\\
{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{0}^{1-\gamma}\mathbf{x}(0)=\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{0},\quad&\gamma=\alpha+\beta-\alpha\beta.\end{cases}

The following existence and uniqueness result holds:

(IVP existence and uniqueness

2.2
Polynomial splines approximation

Definition 2.5

(Bernstein polynomial operator
[
16
]
)
.

For a function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
, the Bernstein operator of polynomial order
q\in\mathbb{N}
is given as:

\displaystyle B^{q}f(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{q}f\left(a+{j(b-a)}/{q}\right)\binom{q}{j}\left(\frac{t-a}{b-a}\right)^{j}\left(\frac{b-t}{b-a}\right)^{q-j}.

(17)

Definition 2.6

(Modulus of continuity
[
16
]
)
.

For a continuous function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
, the modulus of continuity
\omega
is defined as:

\displaystyle\omega\,(f;\delta)=\sup_{|t-s|\leq\delta}|f(t)-f(s)|,\quad\textrm{ for }t,s\in[a,b].

(18)

Theorem 2.2

(Bernstein operator error bound,
[
16
]
)
.

For a continuous function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
, the following error estimate holds:

\displaystyle|f(t)-B^{q}f(t)|\leq\frac{5}{4}\,\omega\left(f;\frac{(b-a)}{\sqrt{q}}\right),\quad\textrm{for all }t\in[a,b].

(19)

Definition 2.7

(Knot collection)
.

Given a closed interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}

of size

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\{A_{0},...,A_{k}\}=\{[t_{0},t_{1}),...,[t_{k-1},t_{k}),[t_{k},t_{k+1}]\},

(20)

such that

\bigcup_{A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}}A_{i}=[a,b].

Definition 2.8

(Bernstein spline operator,
[

]
)
.

For a function

, and a knot collection

of size

\displaystyle S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}f(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}\,B^{q}\,\{\mathds{1}_{\bar{A}_{i}}f\}(t),

(21)

where
\bar{A}_{i}

Moreover, we note that

as defined above is a continuous operator
[

]

\displaystyle|{t^{\prime}}^{1-\gamma}\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}\mathbf{y}({t^{\prime}})-t^{1-\gamma}\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}\mathbf{y}(t)|

(49)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{({t^{\prime}}-t)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\left({\alpha\mathrm{B}(\gamma,\alpha)}+2\left(\frac{{t^{\prime}}}{t}\right)^{1-\gamma}\right)+\frac{({t^{\prime}}-t)^{\zeta}T^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)T^{\zeta}}\zeta\mathrm{B}(\gamma,\zeta)\right)\|\mathbf{y}\|_{C_{1-\gamma}[\varepsilon,T]},

(50)

satisfying the bound.
Finally, the convergence order
\Omega(t,{t^{\prime}})=\mathcal{O}\left(({t^{\prime}}-t)^{\alpha}\right)
for
({t^{\prime}}-t)\downarrow 0
follows from (
50
) since
({t^{\prime}}-t)\downarrow 0
gives
\left(\frac{{t^{\prime}}}{t}\right)^{1-\gamma}\to 1
and furthermore
\alpha\leq\zeta\leq 1
.
∎

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we present convergence requirements and the construction of analytical and numerical solutions to fractional-periodic BVPs with generalized Hilfer fractional derivatives of type
0\leq\beta\leq 1
and order
0<\alpha<1
as presented in (
1
).
The analytical results are presented in Theorem
, using a perturbed-IVP approach to find solutions in the weighted space
C_{1-\gamma}[0,T]
.
Our results generalize previous perturbed-IVP existence results for Caputo fractional derivative
(\beta=1)
periodic BVPs to both the corresponding Riemann-Liouville operator problem
(\beta=0)
and general Hilfer fractional derivative problems
(0<\beta<1)
.

We implement the analytical solutions numerically, constructing solution approximations with Bernstein splines.
Approximations are obtained on a modified time domain
\varepsilon<t<T
, with
\varepsilon>0
small.
Convergence requirements and asymptotic convergence rates to analytical solutions as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
are presented in Theorem
.
Numerical experimentation results show error convergence rates satisfying the theoretical bounds.
The numerical method can numerically capture the singular behavior of solutions as
t\downarrow 0
and is able to simulate nonlinear problems, as demonstrated in Section
4
.
Moreover, we give an example of the apriori calculation of convergence requirements and an example of a grid-search to find the corresponding periodic recurrence time
T
corresponding to the non-perturbed original BVP such that
\Delta_{T}(\tilde{x}_{0})=0
.

For future work, we note that apriori convergence guarantees to obtain
\Delta_{T}(\tilde{x}_{0})=0
would improve the applicability of the method for applied problems, although we expect that this would in general pose stringent requirements for nonlinear systems.
Another possible strategy would be to formalize a root-finding procedure on
\Delta_{T}(\tilde{x}_{0})
.
Finally, another general extension of the method would be to provide an application for fractional PDEs, incorporating FBVPS by utilizing a multidimensional splines setup.

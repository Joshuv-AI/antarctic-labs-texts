Abstract
. In dimension 5 5 , the contact connected sum of Brieskorn contact spheres is, in general, not a Brieskorn contact sphere.

53D42; 53D40, 37C27, 37J55, 57R17.

1.
Introduction

Brieskorn manifolds

\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
,
initially studied by Brieskorn
[
6
]
,
Hirzebruch–Mayer
[
19
]

and Milnor
[
27
]

in the context of exotic differentiable structures on spheres,
are given as link
\{p_{\mathbf{a}}=0\}\cap S^{2n+1}

of singularity of complex polynomials

p_{\mathbf{a}}(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n})=z_{0}^{a_{0}}+\cdots+z_{n}^{a_{n}}\,,\qquad\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})\,.

Many of them, the so-called
Brieskorn spheres
,
are homotopy equivalent to the unit sphere

S^{2n+1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
,
cf. Section
2.1
below.
Each Brieskorn manifold
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})

carries a Stein fillable contact structure
\xi_{\mathbf{a}}
,
which we will call a
Brieskorn contact structure
.
The contact structure
\xi_{\mathbf{a}}

is defined by intersecting the standard contact structure

TS^{2n+1}\cap\mathrm{i}TS^{2n+1}
of
S^{2n+1}

with
T\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
, see
[
15
, Section 7.1]
.
In case
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
is diffeomorphic
to the standard sphere
S^{2n-1}
,
exoticity questions of the contact structures
\xi_{\mathbf{a}}

become relevant contact-topologically.
Here,
a contact structure on
S^{2n-1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

not contactomorphic to
TS^{2n-1}\cap\mathrm{i}TS^{2n-1}

is called
exotic
.

Bennequin
[
3
]
found the first examples
of exotic contact structures on
S^{3}
.
Eliashberg
[
9
]
observed that these examples are
overtwisted contact structures,
for which an
h
-principle holds and which are not fillable,
see
[
10
,
17
]
and cf.
[
16
, Corollary 3.8]
.
Extensions of overtwistedness and non-fillability phenomena
to higher dimensions also hold true,
see
[
4
]
and
[
28
,
24
,
31
]
, resp.

Eliashberg
[
11
]
, Geiges
[
14
]
and Ding–Geiges
[
8
]

gave examples of exotic contact structures on
S^{2n-1}
for
n\geq 3
.
Exoticity here was established
via the Eliashberg–Floer–McDuff theorem

[
25
, Theorem 1.5]

about smooth uniqueness of symplectically aspherical fillings
of the standard contact sphere;
such a filling must be diffeomorphic to a ball,
see also
[
2
,
35
]
.
Further aspects of exoticity phenomena in terms of fillability,
Reeb dynamics and tightness were investigated in

[
26
,
21
,
1
,
22
,
5
]
.

Exotic Brieskorn contact structures on standard spheres do exist.
Ustilovsky
[
34
]
showed,
based on contact homology as worked out e.g. by Pardon
[
29
]
,
that on
S^{4m+1}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists infinitely many pairwise non-contactomorphic
Brieskorn contact structures
in every homotopy class of almost contact structures.
Fauck
[
12
]
obtained the same result by alternatively using
Rabinowitz–Floer homology;
Gutt
[
18
]
(and in parts Uebele
[
33
]
) via
positive equivariant symplectic homology.

Kwon–van Koert
[
21
]

extended these results, which in particular in dimension
5

are of interest by the following curiosities:
In dimension
5
, no exotic smooth spheres exist
according to the generalised Poincaré conjecture
(verified by Smale
[
32
]
)
and the almost contact structure on
S^{5}

given by
TS^{5}\cap\mathrm{i}TS^{5}
is homotopically unique,
see
[
23
, Lemma 1]
.
The latter implies with Gray stability (cf.
[
15
]
)
and the
h
-principle (see
[
4
]
)
that up to isotopy
S^{5}
admits a unique overtwisted contact structure.
Kwon–van Koert demonstrated
that the restriction of the mean Euler characteristic
(of the
S^{1}
-equivariant symplectic homology)
minus
\frac{1}{2}

to a certain submonoid of contact structures on
S^{5}

yields an epimorphism onto
(\mathbb{Q},+)
,
see
[
21
, Theorem 5.22]
.
Here,
the monoid operation is the contact connected sum
with the standard contact sphere as zero element,
cf.
[
15
]
.
In this article we show
that the submonoid contains infinitely many
contact structures on
S^{5}

that are not Brieskorn.
The examples are obtained by contact connected sum
of Brieskorn contact structures.

The following theorem was discovered in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 1
.

There are
infinitely many pairs of

4
-tuples
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
of natural numbers
such that the contact structure
\xi_{\mathbf{a}}\#\xi_{\mathbf{b}}
on
S^{5}
,
defined by contact connected sum
of the Brieskorn contact spheres

\big(\Sigma(\mathbf{a}),\xi_{\mathbf{a}}\big)
and

\big(\Sigma(\mathbf{b}),\xi_{\mathbf{b}}\big)
,
is not contactomorphic to a Brieskorn contact structure.
Furthermore the contact structures

\xi_{\mathbf{a}}\#\xi_{\mathbf{b}}
are pairwise
not contactomorphic.

Observe, that the method used in this paper shows,
in dimensions
2n-1\geq 7
,
that the subclass of Brieskorn contact structures on
S^{2n-1}

having
pairwise coprime exponents

is not closed under contact connected sum,
see Remark
3.3.1
.
In fact,
there are infinitely many,
pairwise non-contactomorphic such examples.

2.
Topological invariants

2.1.
Brieskorn’s criterion

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})
be an
(n+1)
-tuple
of natural numbers
a_{j}\geq 2
for all
j=0,\ldots,n
.
Assume that
n\geq 3
.
We denote by
\Gamma(\mathbf{a})
the graph
with
n+1
vertices labeled by the
a_{j}
,
j=0,\ldots,n
,
and an edge between
a_{k}
and
a_{\ell}
for all
k\neq\ell

whenever the greatest common divisor of
a_{k}
and
a_{\ell}

satisfies
\gcd(a_{k},a_{\ell})\geq 2
.
The connected component of
\Gamma(\mathbf{a})

consisting of even numbers is denoted by
\Gamma^{2}(\mathbf{a})
.
In this situation
[
6
, Satz 1]
can be phrased as follows:
The Brieskorn manifold
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
is homeomorphic to
S^{2n-1}

if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(i)

\Gamma(\mathbf{a})
has at least two isolated points.

(ii)

\Gamma(\mathbf{a})
has an isolated point and
the connected component
\Gamma^{2}(\mathbf{a})
of
\Gamma(\mathbf{a})

satisfies:

\#\Gamma^{2}(\mathbf{a})>1
is odd
(hence,
\Gamma^{2}(\mathbf{a})\neq\emptyset
) and

\gcd(a_{k},a_{\ell})=2
for all
a_{k},a_{\ell}\in\Gamma^{2}(\mathbf{a})
,
k\neq\ell
.

For
n=2
, the conditions for
\Gamma(\mathbf{a})

are equivalent to the fact that
\Gamma(\mathbf{a})

is an integral homology sphere.

Example 2.1.1
.

For later use we remark:
Given a natural number
m\geq 2
,
a quick application of Bézout’s diophantic identity
ax+by=1
yields,
that the numbers
m,m+1,2m+1
and
4m+3
satisfy
(i) of Brieskorn’s criterion with isolated points
m+1
and
2m+1
.
In fact, they are pairwise coprime provided
\gcd(m,3)=1
.
Therefore,
as in dimension
5
no exotic spheres exist (see
[
32
,
20
]
),

\Sigma_{m}:=\Sigma\big(m,m+1,2m+1,4m+3\big)

is diffeomorphic to
S^{5}
.

Example 2.1.2
.

For integers
\ell=0,1,2,\ldots
,

Fermat numbers

F_{\ell}:=2^{2^{\ell}}+1

can be computed recursively via

F_{\ell}=F_{0}\cdot F_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot F_{\ell-1}+2\,,\quad\ell\geq 1\,,

where
F_{\ell}-2=F_{\ell-1}(F_{\ell-1}-2)

is used to proceed with the inductive step.
In particular,
for
k<\ell
we find
\gcd(F_{k},F_{\ell})=1
.
Therefore,
by Brieskorn’s criterion

\Sigma_{\ell}:=\Sigma\big(F_{\ell},\ldots,F_{\ell+n}\big)

is a topological
(n-1)
-sphere.

2.2.
Positivity of equivariant Euler characteristic

All Brieskorn manifolds
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
,
\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})
,
carry a natural fixed-point free
(finite isotropy groups are allowed)
circle action

\zeta\cdot(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n})=\big(\zeta^{d/a_{0}}z_{0},\ldots,\zeta^{d/a_{n}}z_{n}\big)\,,

where
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\zeta|=1
and

d=d_{\mathbf{a}}:=\lcm(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})\,.

Randell
[
30
]
computed the corresponding
rational equivariant homology using the rational Gysin sequence,
cf.
[
13
, Section 4.2]
.
The related Euler characteristic equals

\chi^{S^{1}}\big(\Sigma(\mathbf{a})\big)=n+(-1)^{n-1}\kappa(\mathbf{a})\,,

see
[
30
, Corollary 1]
,
where
\kappa(\mathbf{a})
denotes the rank
of the singular homology of
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
in degree
n-1

and equals

\kappa(\mathbf{a})=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\sum_{I_{k}}(-1)^{n+1-k}\frac{\Pi_{j\in I_{k}}a_{j}}{\lcm_{j\in I_{k}}a_{j}}\,,

see
[
30
, Section 3]
or
[
21
, Section 3]
.
The inner sum ranges over all subsets
I_{k}
of
\{0,\ldots,n\}

with
k\in\{0,\ldots,n+1\}
elements.
Observe that for
k=2
the summands are given
by
(-1)^{n+1}\gcd_{j\in I_{2}}a_{j}
,
so that the expression starts with

\kappa(\mathbf{a})=(-1)^{n+1}\Big(1-(n+1)+\sum_{I_{2}}\gcd_{j\in I_{2}}a_{j}\mp\cdots\Big)\,.

Remark 2.2.1
.

\chi^{S^{1}}\big(\Sigma(\mathbf{a})\big)
is positive
whenever
n
is odd or
\kappa(\mathbf{a})=0
,
the latter being the case precisely when
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})

is a rational homology sphere
(as
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
is
(n-2)
-connected
cf.
[
21
, Proposition 3.5]
).

2.3.
Invariant Brieskorn submanifolds

Given a Brieskorn manifold
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})

together with the natural circle action
from Section
2.2

one obtains invariant submanifolds
\Sigma(\mathbf{b})

that themselves are Brieskorn
by freezing some but at most
(n-1)
-many coordinates
z_{j}

of
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
at zero.
Here,
the tuple
\mathbf{b}
of integers emerges from
\mathbf{a}

by deleting all entries that correspond to a frozen coordinate.
We will indicate this writing
\mathbf{b}\subset\mathbf{a}
.

Proposition 2.3.1
.

Let
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
be diffeomorphic to
S^{5}
,
i.e.
\mathbf{a}
fulfills Brieskorn’s criterion.
Then the equivariant Euler characteristic satisfies

\chi^{S^{1}}\big(\Sigma(\mathbf{b})\big)>0

for all invariant Brieskorn submanifolds

\Sigma(\mathbf{b})
,
\mathbf{b}\subset\mathbf{a}
.

Proof.

By Remark
2.2.1

it is enough to show that all appearing

3
-dimensional Brieskorn submanifolds

\Sigma(\mathbf{b})
,
\mathbf{b}\subset\mathbf{a}
,
are rational homology spheres.
We will show
\kappa(\mathbf{b})=0

for the rank of the first singular homology

\kappa(\mathbf{b})=2-\Big(\!\gcd(b_{0},b_{1})+\gcd(b_{0},b_{2})+\gcd(b_{1},b_{2})\Big)+\frac{b_{0}b_{1}b_{2}}{\lcm(b_{0},b_{1},b_{2})}

of
\Sigma(\mathbf{b})
.
Observe that
\mathbf{b}
equals

(mp,mq,r)\quad\text{or}\quad(2p,2q,2r)

with natural numbers
p,q,r
and
m

such that
p,q,r
and
m,r
, resp., are pairwise coprime.
Indeed,
deleting the isolated point required in (ii) of Brieskorn’s criterion
(see Section
2.1
)
yields
\mathbf{b}=(2p,2q,2r)
;
otherwise, we obtain
\mathbf{b}=(2p,2q,r)
.
Starting with
\mathbf{a}
fulfilling (i) one gets
\mathbf{b}=(mp,mq,r)

by deleting one of the isolated points.
Here,

m
is the greatest common divisor of the two remaining,
potentially related
a_{j}
’s.
Keeping two isolated points yields
\mathbf{b}=(p,q,r)
.
Hence,

\kappa(\mathbf{b})=2-(m+2)+\frac{m^{2}pqr}{mpqr}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa(\mathbf{b})=2-3\!\cdot\!2+\frac{2^{3}pqr}{2pqr}=0\,,

resp., as claimed.
∎

Remark 2.3.2
.

Positivity of the equivariant Euler characteristic

\chi^{S^{1}}\big(\Sigma(\mathbf{b})\big)

for all odd-dimensional invariant Brieskorn submanifolds

\Sigma(\mathbf{b})
,
\mathbf{b}\subset\mathbf{a}
,
of a general Brieskorn sphere
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})

can be ensured by requiring all
\Sigma(\mathbf{b})

to be rational homology spheres.
By Brieskorn’s criterion this will be satisfied
provided that the
a_{j}
’s in
\mathbf{a}
are pairwise coprime.

3.
Contact homological invariant

The Brieskorn contact structure
\xi_{\mathbf{a}}
on
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})

up to contact isotopy can be given by the kernel of the
contact form defined by

\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}\big(z_{j}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}_{j}-\bar{z}_{j}\mathrm{d}z_{j}\big)\,\Big|_{T\Sigma(\mathbf{a})}\,,

see
[
15
, Section 7.1]
.
The corresponding Reeb vector field

2\pi\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{j}}\big(z_{j}\partial_{z_{j}}-\bar{z}_{j}\partial_{\bar{z}_{j}}\big)\,\Big|_{\Sigma(\mathbf{a})}

induces the flow

\Phi_{t}(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n})=\Big(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t/a_{0}}z_{0},\ldots,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t/a_{n}}z_{n}\Big)

on
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
.
Setting
\zeta=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t/d_{\mathbf{a}}}

we obtain the circle action considered in Section
2.2
,
so that the Reeb flow
\Phi_{t}
is
d_{\mathbf{a}}
-periodic.

3.1.
Positivity of the mean Euler characteristic

Denote by

\Sigma_{T}:=\big\{\mathbf{z}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{a})\>\big|\>\Phi_{T}(\mathbf{z})=\mathbf{z}\big\}

the submanifold
of
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
that consists of all
T
-periodic Reeb orbits.
Observe, that the period
T
necessarily will be an integer.
We assume
T
to be positive.
Furthermore
the orbit space
\Sigma_{T}
is equal to
the invariant Brieskorn submanifold

\Sigma(\mathbf{b})
,
\mathbf{b}\subset\mathbf{a}
,
obtained by setting
z_{j}=0
for all
j\in\{0,\ldots,n\}

for which
T/a_{j}
is not an integer.
The dimension of
\Sigma_{T}
equals

\dim(\Sigma_{T})=2m_{T}-3\,,

where
m_{T}
is the number of
a_{j}
that divide
T
.
By
[
33
, formula (4) on p. 250]

the Robbin–Salamon index of
\Sigma_{T}
equals

\mu_{\RS}(\Sigma_{T})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{T}{a_{j}}\right\rfloor+\left\lceil\frac{T}{a_{j}}\right\rceil\right)-2T\,.

Lemma 3.1.1
.

\mu_{\RS}(\Sigma_{T})\equiv n+1-m_{T}\>(\mathrm{mod}\>2)\,.

Proof.

If
T/a_{j}
is an integer,
then

\left\lfloor\frac{T}{a_{j}}\right\rfloor+\left\lceil\frac{T}{a_{j}}\right\rceil=2\cdot\frac{T}{a_{j}}\,.

Otherwise,

\left\lfloor\frac{T}{a_{j}}\right\rfloor+\left\lceil\frac{T}{a_{j}}\right\rceil\equiv 1\>(\mathrm{mod}\>2)\,.

Therefore, each
a_{j}
not dividing
T
,
contributes
1\>(\mathrm{mod}\>2)
to
\mu_{\RS}(\Sigma_{T})
.
∎

We denote by
\Sigma_{T}/S^{1}

the space of
T
-periodic Reeb orbits
up to parametrisation,
i.e. the quotient orbifold by the natural action,
which has dimension
2m_{T}-4
.

Corollary 3.1.2
.

\mu_{\RS}(\Sigma_{T})-\frac{1}{2}\dim(\Sigma_{T}/S^{1})\equiv n+1\>(\mathrm{mod}\>2)\,.

The minimal periods
T_{1}<\ldots<T_{k}=d_{\mathbf{a}}

of the Reeb flow
\Phi_{t}
are natural numbers
T_{j}

that divide
d_{\mathbf{a}}
.
Following
[
13
]
and
[
21
, Section 5.8]

define the frequencies

\phi_{T_{i}}(T_{i+1},\ldots,T_{k})

to be the number of natural numbers
a

such that
aT_{i}<T_{k}
and
aT_{i}\notin T_{j}\mathbb{N}

for all
j\in\{i+1,\ldots,k\}
.
By convention we have
\phi_{d_{\mathbf{a}}}(\emptyset)=1
.

The mean Euler characteristic
\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{\mathbf{a}})

induced by the
S^{1}
-equivariant symplectic homology
defined in
[
13
]
was computed
in
[
21
, Proposition 5.20]
for the present context:
Whenever
\mu_{\RS}\big(\Sigma(\mathbf{a})\big)\neq 0

the mean Euler characteristic
\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{\mathbf{a}})

is defined, an invariant of the isomorphy class
of the contact structure
\xi_{\mathbf{a}}
,
and equals

\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{\mathbf{a}})=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{\mu_{\RS}(\Sigma_{T_{i}})-\frac{1}{2}\dim(\Sigma_{T_{i}}/S^{1})}\phi_{T_{i}}(T_{i+1},\ldots,T_{k})\cdot\chi^{S^{1}}(\Sigma_{T_{i}})}{|\mu_{\RS}\big(\Sigma(\mathbf{a})\big)|}\,.

Corollary
3.1.2
implies:

\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{\mathbf{a}})=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\phi_{T_{i}}(T_{i+1},\ldots,T_{k})\cdot\chi^{S^{1}}(\Sigma_{T_{i}})}{|\mu_{\RS}\big(\Sigma(\mathbf{a})\big)|}\,.

Notice, that
[
33
, formula (31) on p. 265]
gives

\mu_{\RS}\big(\Sigma(\mathbf{a})\big)=2d_{\mathbf{a}}\cdot\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{j}}-1\right)\,.

Lemma 3.1.3
.

The mean Euler characteristic
\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{\mathbf{a}})

is defined for all Brieskorn manifolds
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
,
for which
\mathbf{a}
admits an isolated exponent.

Proof.

Arguing by contradiction we assume that

\mu_{\RS}\big(\Sigma(\mathbf{a})\big)=0
,
which is equivalent to

\frac{1}{a_{0}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{a_{n}}=1
.
This implies
a_{j}\geq 2
for all
j=0,\ldots,n
.
By assumption,
one of the exponents, say
a_{0}
,
satisfies
\gcd(a_{0},a_{j})=1
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
.
Multiplication with
d_{\mathbf{a}}
yields:

d_{\mathbf{a}}/a_{0}=d_{\mathbf{a}}-d_{\mathbf{a}}/a_{1}-\ldots-d_{\mathbf{a}}/a_{n}
.
Therefore,
a_{0}^{2}
divides
d_{\mathbf{a}}
,
which contradicts
d_{\mathbf{a}}
being
the least common multiple of the
a_{j}

for all
j=0,\ldots,n
.
∎

Proposition 3.1.4
.

Let
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
be a
5
-dimensional Brieskorn sphere.
Then

\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{\mathbf{a}})>0\,.

Proof.

Follows with the positivity statement in Proposition
2.3.1
.
∎

Remark 3.1.5
.

In view of Remark
2.3.2

or the formula at the beginning of Section
3.2
,
we obtain positivity of

(-1)^{n+1}\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{\mathbf{a}})
in all dimensions,
whenever the exponents of
\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
are pairwise coprime.

3.2.
Pairwise coprime

By
[
13
, Proposition 4.6]
or
[
21
, Proposition 5.21]

the mean Euler characteristic
\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{\mathbf{a}})
of Brieskorn manifolds

\Sigma(\mathbf{a})
with pairwise coprime exponents
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}

equals:

(-1)^{n+1}\frac{n+(n-1)\sum_{j_{0}}(a_{j_{0}}-1)+\cdots+\sum_{j_{0}<\cdots<j_{n-2}}(a_{j_{0}}-1)\cdots(a_{j_{n-2}}-1)}{2|(\sum_{j}a_{0}\cdots\hat{a}_{j}\cdots a_{n})-a_{0}\cdots a_{n}|}\,.

Example 3.2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=\big(m,m+1,2m+1,4m+3\big)

for a natural number
m
not divisible by
3
.
Abbreviate the contact structure
\xi_{\mathbf{a}}
on
\Sigma_{m}

by
\xi_{m}
, see Example
2.1.1
.
We obtain

\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{m})=\frac{21m^{2}+17m+3}{16m^{4}-8m^{3}-50m^{2}-34m-6}\,.

Observe that

\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{4})=\frac{407}{2642}<\frac{1}{4}

and that the function
m\mapsto\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{m})

decreases strictly for
m\geq 4
.
Indeed,

\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{m})
,
viewed as a rational function over the complex numbers,
has all its poles on the open disc of radius
3
.
This follows with Rouché’s theorem as
16\cdot 3^{4}=1296

dominates the absolut value of the lower order terms of the denominator

h(m)
evaluated along
|m|=3
,
which is bounded by
216+450+102+6=774
.
Furthermore,
denoting the nominator by
g(m)
,
the polynomial
(g^{\prime}h-h^{\prime}g)(m)
taken over the reals
equals
-672m^{5}-648m^{4}+80m^{3}+208m^{2}+48m

and is, therefore, negative for
m\geq 1
.

Remark 3.2.2
.

Assigning to each natural number
\ell

the
(n+1)
-tuple
\mathbf{a}_{\ell}

of consecutive Fermat numbers

a_{j}=F_{\ell+j}=x^{2^{j}}+1\,,\quad\text{with}\,\,x=2^{2^{\ell}}\,,

as in Example
2.1.2
,
we see that
\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{\mathbf{a}_{\ell}})
is a rational function
in
x
,
whose asymptotic behaviour is given by

(-1)^{n+1}\frac{1}{2(a_{0}-1)(a_{1}-1)}=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{1}{2x^{3}}\,.

Hence,
for sufficiently large
\ell
we find that

\ell\mapsto(-1)^{n+1}\chi_{\mathrm{m}}(\xi_{\mathbf{a}_{\ell}})

is strictly decreasing taking values in
(0,1/4)
.

3.3.
Monoidomorphic contact connected sum

In
[
21
, Section 5.9]
and
[
33
, Proposition 6]

additivity properties of
\chi_{\mathrm{m}}+(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{2}

under contact connected sum were discussed:
Let
\Sigma({\mathbf{a}})
and
\Sigma({\mathbf{b}})
be Brieskorn manifolds

4.
Inverse elements

Contact structures on
S^{2n-1}

form an abelian monoid under contact connected sum.
The standard contact structure

TS^{2n-1}\cap\mathrm{i}TS^{2n-1}

appears as the neutral element.
A natural question is about invertibility
of contact structures within the monoidal structure.
For
n=2
no non–trivial element
will be invertible by overtwistedness.
For
n\geq 3
,
the Eliashberg–Floer–McDuff theorem implies
that no potential inverse of a Brieskorn contact structure
is a Brieskorn contact structure itself.
Indeed,
boundary connected sum with the corresponding
natural critical Stein fillings would yield
a symplectically aspherical filling
of
TS^{2n-1}\cap\mathrm{i}TS^{2n-1}
,
which in turn will be a smooth ball,
see
[
25
,
2
,
35
]
.
But this would contradict criticality
of the boundary connected sum of
the natural critical Stein fillings
in view of the Mayer–Vietoris sequence.

Well-posedness for a Magnetohydrodynamical Model with Intrinsic Magnetisation
Noah Vinod and Thanh Tran

Abstract
. Ferromagnetic magnetohydrodynamics concerns the study of conducting fluids with intrinsic magnetisation under the influence of a magnetic field. It is a generalisation of the magnetohydrodynamical equations and takes into account the dynamics of the magnetisation of a fluid. First proposed by Lingam (Lingam, ‘Dissipative effects in magnetohydrodynamical models with intrinsic magnetisation’, Communications in Nonlinear Science and Numerical Simulation Vol 28, pp 223-231, 2015), the usual equations of magnetohydrodynamics, namely the Navier-Stokes equation and the induction equation, are coupled with the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation. In this paper, the local existence, uniqueness and regularity of weak solutions to this system are discussed.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we develop local-in-time existence and uniqueness theorems the ferromagnetic magnetohydrodynamical model represented by the following system of PDEs

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}+(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}-\mu\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$v$}+\nabla p=\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}+(\mbox{\boldmath$B$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}+\nabla(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$B$})-\div\left[(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$})^{\top}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right],

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\div\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=0,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}+\eta\curl^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}-\curl(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$})=\mbox{\boldmath$0$},

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\div\mbox{\boldmath$B$}=0,

(1.1e)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}+(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}=\gamma\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times(\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}+\mbox{\boldmath$B$})-\chi\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times(\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}+\mbox{\boldmath$B$})).

over the problem domain
(0,T)\times\Omega
. Here
T>0
and the set
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. The vector fields
\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$B$},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
represent the velocity of the fluid, the magnetic field, and the magnetisation, respectively, while the scalar field
p:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
represents the pressure of the fluid. The constants
\mu,\eta
and
\chi
are positive parameters related to the viscosity the fluid, the diffusivity of the magnetic field, and the damping of the magnetisation. The parameter
\gamma
is a non-zero constant representing the electron’s gyromagnetic ratio. We set
\mu=\eta=\gamma=\chi=1
in (
1.1
), for the sake of simplicity. This system is subjected to the boundary conditions

(1.2a)

v

\displaystyle=\mbox{\boldmath$0$},

(1.2b)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$B$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$n$}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.2c)

\displaystyle\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$n$}

\displaystyle=\mbox{\boldmath$0$},

(1.2d)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}

\displaystyle=\mbox{\boldmath$0$},

on
[0,T]\times\partial\Omega
, along with the initial data

\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(0,\cdot)=\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0},\qquad\mbox{\boldmath$B$}(0,\cdot)=\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0},\qquad\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(0,\cdot)=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}.

In 1942, Hannes Alfvén studied the properties of waves created by fluids that conducted electricity under the influence of a magnetic field
[
1
]
. This scenario necessitates that the moving liquid exposed to a magnetic field creates an electric current within it which then influences the motion of the fluid as it interacts with the magnetic field. This field of inquiry, designated ‘magnetohydrodynamics’, had tremendous applications in plasma research, solar physics, geophysics and more
[
32
]
. The dynamics for an incompressible magnetohydrodynamical fluid were modelled by the following system of PDEs, consisting of the Navier-Stokes equation and the induction equation, derived from Maxwell’s equations
[
15
]
:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}+(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}-\mu\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$v$}+\nabla p=\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$},

\displaystyle\div\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}+\eta\curl^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}-\curl(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$})=\mbox{\boldmath$0$},

\displaystyle\div\mbox{\boldmath$B$}=0.

The intrinsic non-linearities of this system of PDEs, and its numerous applications have drawn a number of mathematicians to it. The equations of magnetohydrodynamics have been studied by mathematicians for decades. They have asked questions related to the existence and uniqueness of solutions, including weak-strong uniqueness, and the regularity of those solutions. A non-exhaustive list of their efforts includes
[

,
16
,
18
,
23
,
30
,
31
,
36
]
.

About a decade earlier, in 1935, Landau and Lifshitz studied the distribution of magnetic moments in a ferromagnetic crystal
[
22
]
, and based on phenomenological observations, produced the so-called Landau-Lifshitz (LL) equation

\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\mathrm{eff}}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\mathrm{eff}}).

Here,
\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\mathrm{eff}}
is the effective field, which consists of the terms representing the various energies that influence the dynamics of the magnetisation. Note that in (
1.1e
) we have
\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}+\mbox{\boldmath$B$}
, which corresponds to the exchange and Zeeman energies, respectively. Magnetisation fundamentally arises from quantum mechanical effects
[
21
]
, but the theory of micromagnetism attempts to capture those effects through a macroscopic lens by ignoring its atomic nature and looking at it through a more classical lens
[
20
]
. The purpose of this theory is to calculate the magnetisation of a material under the influence of varying effects such as differing fields, currents, inherent structures of materials, the interactions between magnetisations between different parts of the material and so on
[
12
]
. The LL equation is becoming increasingly relevant in industry as it assists in the development of electronic devices based on magnetic nano-wires, vortexes in nano-elements for sensors, magnetic recording, and so on
[
10
]
. The LL equations thus demonstrate a number of non-linear structures and has interesting geometric properties. This, in general, makes the equation highly complex and non-integrable
[
21
]
. For these reasons, mathematicians have been drawn to it and have studied it very closely over the last couple of decades. The first string of results were theoretical studies by mathematicians on the existence, uniqueness (or non-uniqueness) and regularity of solutions to the equations. A non-exhaustive list includes
[
2
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
17
,
25
,
35
,
37
]
.

More recently, in 2015, Lingam
[
24
]
studied the motion of conducting classical and quantum fluids that have spin, that is, they have an intrinsic magnetisation much like permanent magnets. Hence, in addition to being a magnetohydrodynamical model (
1.3
), Lingam accounted for the dynamics of this intrinsic magnetisation. This model has potential applications in astrophysical and fusion plasmas, with some links to liquid crystals
[
24
]
. Now, the resultant model consists of the Navier-Stokes equation for fluid flow, Maxwell’s equation for magnetic fields and the Landau-Lifshitz equation for magnetisation and resembles the model (
1.1
). However, Lingam develops the model in the compressible case, whereas we look at the incompressible version, and we also leave out the entropy-density equation as it is decoupled from the others. Moreover, we differ from Lingam in that we also conserve the exchange energy of the magnetisation, represented by
|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}
in the Hamiltonian, the term
\div\left[(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$})^{\top}(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$})\right]
in (
1.1a
), and
\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}
in (
1.1e
). The conservation of the exchange energy is necessary because it features prominently in other studies of the LL equations (see the references above). The resultant model (
1.1
) thus consists of the Navier-Stokes equation for fluid flow, Maxwell’s equation for magnetic fields and the Landau-Lifshitz equation for magnetisation.

Equations (
1.1
) are similar to the ferrohydrodynamic model by Rinaldi and Zahn
[
28
]
and Rosensweig
[
29
]
, in that they both contain similar fluid and magnetisation dynamics. However, they differ in that (
1.1
) deals with electrically conducting fluids and does not conserve angular momentum. Equations (
1.1
) are also similar to the magnetoviscoelastic model
[
5
,
14
]
which looks at the dynamics of magnetorheological fluids
[
27
]
. This latter model is composed of the Navier-Stokes equation as well as the Landau-Lifshitz equation, but differs with the inclusion of the elastic deformation equation.

A mathematical inquisition into the existence, uniqueness and regularity of solutions to (
1.1
) has not been performed. Here, we undertake the onus of an analytical study of these coupled equations.

The next two lemmas establish estimates for certain terms that arise in our subsequent analysis.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)

Lemma 2.4
.

For
\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\in\mathbb{H}_{\div}^{1}(\Omega)

From (

\displaystyle\curl(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$v$})

\displaystyle=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\div\mbox{\boldmath$v$})-\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(\div\mbox{\boldmath$u$})+(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}

\displaystyle=(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}.

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\curl(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$v$}),\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\right\rangle\right|

It is a well-known result that the standard Landau-Lifshitz equation preserves the constraint

Proposition 2.5
.

If the solutions
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$B$},\mbox{\boldmath$m$})
are smooth, the magnetisation

Taking the inner product of (

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}+(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla)|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}=0

Setting

we see that

satisfies the PDE

\frac{\partial b}{\partial t}+(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla)b=0,

subject to the Neumann boundary condition
\partial b/\partial\mbox{\boldmath$n$}=0
and the initial condition
b(0,\cdot)=|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}|^{2}-1=0
. As
\div\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=0

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|b\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0,

implying that

\left\|b(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\left\|b(0,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0.

This ends the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$w$})

(2.11)

\displaystyle\Delta(|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2})

\displaystyle=2|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}+2(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}),

and we have

\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$})=-\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}-|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}.

Moreover, by expanding the last term in (
1.1a
) we obtain

\displaystyle\div\left[(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$})^{\top}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\nabla|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}+(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$})^{\top}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$},

thus giving us the equivalent problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}+(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}-\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$v$}+\nabla p=\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}+(\mbox{\boldmath$B$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}+\nabla\left(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$B$}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}\right)

(2.12a)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$})^{\top}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$},

(2.12b)

\displaystyle\div\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=0,

(2.12c)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}+\curl^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}-\curl(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$})=\mbox{\boldmath$0$},

(2.12d)

\displaystyle\div\mbox{\boldmath$B$}=0,

(2.12e)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}+(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}-\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}=|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}+\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times(\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}+\mbox{\boldmath$B$})-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}),

(2.12f)

\displaystyle|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|=1,

on
(0,T)\times\Omega
.

We now define the notion of weak solutions to (
2.12
). We first take the dot-product of (
2.12a
) and a test function
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
, integrate over
\Omega
and (formally) use integration by parts assuming
\div\boldsymbol{\varphi}=0
on
\Omega
and
\boldsymbol{\varphi}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and rearrange to obtain

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle&=-\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$B$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle-\left\langle(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$})^{\top}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}\right\rangle.\end{split}

All the terms on the right-hand side are well-defined if
\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$B$},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)
since
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{6}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{3}(\Omega)
and
\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{3}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{1/2}
by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (Theorem
A.3
). Similarly, we take the dot-product of (
2.12c
) and a test function
\boldsymbol{\omega}
, and integrating (using (
2.4
) and (
1.2c
)) to obtain

(2.14)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$},\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rangle+\left\langle\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$},\curl\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rangle=\left\langle\curl(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}),\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rangle.

Considering Lemma
2.4
, the conditions on
v
and
B
mentioned previously do suffice, along with
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)
. Lastly, we take the dot-product of (
2.12e
) and a test function
\boldsymbol{\xi}
, and integrate (noting (
1.2d
)) to obtain

(2.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle&=-\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle\\
&\qquad+\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}),\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle.\end{split}

Once again, the conditions on
v
and
B
mentioned previously do suffice, with the additional requirement that
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)
, where the latter is required to accommodate for the additional non-linearity. This additional regularity is important because
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, which is clearly required, and because
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{6}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{4}(\Omega)
, which is useful for the term
\left\langle|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle
. This motivates the following definition of solutions to the problem (
2.12
).

Definition 2.6
.

Given
T>0
and
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, the functions
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$B$},\mbox{\boldmath$m$})
are said to be a
weak solution
to (
2.12
) if

(1)

The velocity
v
, magnetic field
B
, and magnetisation
m
have the regularity

v

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{0,\div}^{1}(\Omega)),

B

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{n,\div}^{1}(\Omega)),

m

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)).

(2)

The magnetisation
m
has magnitude

|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|=1\qquad\text{a.e. on }[0,T]\times\Omega.

(3)

For all
(\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\xi})\in\mathbb{H}_{0,\div}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)
, the triplet
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$B$},\mbox{\boldmath$m$})
satisfies

(2.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(t),\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s),\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s)\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s),\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}(s)\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}(s),\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s\\
&\qquad-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$B$}(s)\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(s),\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(s))^{\top}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s=\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle,\end{split}

(2.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$B$}(t),\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}(s),\curl\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\curl(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s)\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}(s)),\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s=\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0},\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rangle,\end{split}

(2.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(t),\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(s),\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s)\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(s),\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(s)|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(s),\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s\\
&\qquad-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(s)\times\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(s),\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(s)\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}(s),\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(s)\times(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(s)\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}(s)),\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle\;{\rm d}s\\
&\qquad\qquad=\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle.\end{split}

The main results of our paper are as follows. The first theorem establishes the existence of solutions in the sense of Definition
2.6
while the second theorem establishes the stability of those solutions.

Theorem 2.7

(Existence)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain with a smooth boundary and assume that the initial data
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0})
satisfies
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{0,\div}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)
,
\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{n,\div}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)
with
\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0}\times\mbox{\boldmath$n$}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}
on
\partial\Omega
, and
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)
with
|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}|=1
on
\Omega
and
\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}/\partial\mbox{\boldmath$n$}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}
on
\partial\Omega
. Given
T>0
, there exists
T^{*}\in(0,T]
such that
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$B$},\mbox{\boldmath$m$})
is a solution to (
2.12
) on
[0,T^{*}]\times\Omega
in the sense of Definition
2.6
with the additional regularity

v

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{W}^{2,6}(\Omega))\cap C([0,T^{*}];\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,\infty}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)),

B

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{W}^{2,6}(\Omega))\cap C([0,T^{*}];\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,\infty}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)),

m

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega))\cap C([0,T^{*}];\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,\infty}(0,T^{*};\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)).

Note that in this case,
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$B$},\mbox{\boldmath$m$})
satisfies (
2.12
) for all
t\in[0,T^{*}]
and almost all
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\Omega
.

Theorem 2.8

(Stability)
.

Let
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1,0},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{1,0},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1,0})
and
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{2,0},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2,0},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2,0})
satisfy the assumptions for the initial data in Theorem
2.7
, and let
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1})
and
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2})
be the two solutions associated with these initial data. If
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}=\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}-\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{2}
,
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}=\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{1}-\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2}
, and
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}
, then there exists a constant
C
independent of the initial data
(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{0},\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}_{0},\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{0})
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T^{\prime}]}\left(\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\right)\leq C\left(\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\right),

where
T^{\prime}=\min(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
. Consequently, if
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1,0},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{1,0},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1,0})=(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{2,0},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2,0},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2,0})
the solutions are unique.

3.
Faedo-Galerkin Approximation

In this section we use the Faedo-Galerkin approach to construct approximate solutions to (
2.12
) and establish critical
a priori
estimates for them.

3.1.
Eigenfunctions

Let
\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty},\{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
denote the orthonormal bases of
\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0,\div}(\Omega)
,
\mathbb{H}_{n,\div}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, respectively. They consist of the smooth eigenfunctions of the Stokes problem
[
34
, Section 2.6]

(3.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{i}+\nabla p_{i}=\lambda_{i}\boldsymbol{\varphi}&\text{on }\Omega,\\
\div\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{i}=0&\text{on }\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{i}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

the steady state magnetic problem
[
15
, Section 2.2.2.2]

(3.2)

\begin{cases}\curl^{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}=\mu_{i}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}&\text{on }\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$n$}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\curl\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\times\mbox{\boldmath$n$}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

and the Laplacian problem
[
19
, Theorem 11.5.2]

(3.3)

\begin{cases}-\Delta\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}=\sigma_{i}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}&\text{on }\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

The eigenvalues
\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}
are all strictly positive and increasing as
i\to\infty
. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\mathbb{V}_{k}:=\mathrm{span}\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\},\qquad\mathbb{X}_{k}:=\mathrm{span}\{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}\},\qquad\mathbb{Y}_{k}:=\mathrm{span}\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k}\}.

We seek a Galerkin solution to (
2.12
) in within these spaces. That is we seek approximate solutions
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k})\in\mathbb{V}_{k}\times\mathbb{X}_{k}\times\mathbb{Y}_{k}
of the form

\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}(t,\mbox{\boldmath$x$})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}(t)\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}),\qquad\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}(t,\mbox{\boldmath$x$})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}(t)\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}),\qquad\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}(t,\mbox{\boldmath$x$})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}(t)\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})

such that for all
(\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\xi})\in\mathbb{V}_{k}\times\mathbb{X}_{k}\times\mathbb{Y}_{k}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\langle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle=-\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\left\langle(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k})^{\top}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}\right\rangle,\end{split}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k},\curl\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rangle=\left\langle\curl(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}),\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\rangle,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\langle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle=-\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle+\left\langle|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}),\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle.\end{split}

Let
\{(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}^{k},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0}^{k},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}^{k})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{V}_{k}\times\mathbb{X}_{k}\times\mathbb{Y}_{k}
be a sequence of functions such that
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}^{k},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0}^{k})\to(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0})
in
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)
and
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}^{k}\to\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}
in
\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)
. Choose
\{a_{i}(0)\}_{i=1}^{k}
,
\{b_{i}(0)\}_{i=1}^{k}
, and
\{c_{i}(0)\}_{i=1}^{k}
such that

(3.7)

\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}(0,\cdot)=\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}^{k},\qquad\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}(0,\cdot)=\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0}^{k},\qquad\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}(0,\cdot)=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}^{k}.

Let
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)=(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}(t),\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(t),\mbox{\boldmath$c$}(t))^{\top}
i.e., the vector collection of the time-dependent coefficients of
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}
. Then as the eigenvectors are orthonormal bases, (
3.4
)–(
3.6
) amounts to finding
z
such that

(3.8)

\mbox{\boldmath$z$}^{\prime}(t)=\mbox{\boldmath$F$}(\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)),

where
F
is a vector-valued multivariate polynomial. Here, the initial values
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(0)
are given by the approximate initial data
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{0}^{k},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0}^{k},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}^{k})
. As
F
is a multivariate polynomial in each of its components, it is locally Lipschitz and thereby the Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem guarantees the local existence and uniqueness of a solution
z
. Hence, there exists
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}
that solves the weak formulation. Moreover, as
F
is smooth
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)
is also smooth in time.

Given that approximate solutions exist, we now seek to bound this sequence of solutions in certain spaces.

3.2.
A Priori
Estimates

For simplicity of notation we let

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}J(t)&:=\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&\qquad+\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2},\end{split}

and will often abbreviate
J(t)
as
J
below.

The following lemmas establish precursory inequalities that will be collated together at the end of this section to produce estimates for the Galerkin solution. The first two lemmas primarily deal with the
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)
norms of the Galerkin solutions.

Lemma 3.1
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J+J^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J^{3/2},

\displaystyle\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J^{2}.

Proof.

It is noted that these estimates hold for
J(t)
with
\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
only. However, we use
\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}
as this will be required in subsequent lemmas. Setting
\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}
in (
3.4
), and using Young’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\left\langle(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k})\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\rangle\right|+\left\||\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}|\,|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}|\,|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\||\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}|\,|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}|\,|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\||\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|\,|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|\,|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim J+J^{2}.

Here, the term involving
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}
vanishes because
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}
is divergence free. The first inequality is thereby proved. The second inequality follows similarly by setting
\boldsymbol{\omega}=\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}
in (
3.5
), integrating by parts and using Lemma
2.4
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\||\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}||\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}||\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\||\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}||\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}||\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{3}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim J^{3/2}.

Finally, the third inequality follows by also setting
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}
in (
3.6
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\lesssim J^{2}.

Once again, the term involving
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}
vanishes because
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}
is divergence free.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J+J^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J+J^{2}.

Proof.

Here we do the same as in Lemma
3.1
and deal with the non-linear terms similarly. First, we set
\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}
in (
3.4
) and use Young’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
to deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim J+J^{2}.

For the second inequality, we set
\boldsymbol{\omega}=\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}
in (
3.5
) and use Lemma
2.4
and Young’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\||\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}||\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}||\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\||\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}||\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}||\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J+J^{2},

thereby proving the second inequality.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J+J^{3}.

Proof.

Setting
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\Delta^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}
in (
3.6
) we see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla\Big((\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\Big),\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla\Big(|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\Big),\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad-\left\langle\nabla\Big(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\Big),\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\Big),\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad-\left\langle\nabla\Big(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k})\Big),\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim|I_{1}|+\cdots+|I_{5}|,

where each
I_{i}
represents the corresponding inner product in the equation above. We produce these estimates by Young’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding theorem. We estimate
I_{1}
using the Sobolev embeddings
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{6}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{3}(\Omega)
and
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
to obtain

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J+J^{2}.

For
I_{2}
, we use the same Sobolev embeddings so that

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{4}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim J^{2}.

The expansion of the product rule for the cross-product terms are fairly obvious; we follow the same strategy as before to produce

|I_{3}|\lesssim J^{3/2},\qquad|I_{4}|\lesssim J+J^{2},\qquad|I_{5}|\lesssim J+J^{3}.

The result is obtained by summing up the estimates.
∎

Due to the non-linearity of the equations in (

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J+J^{3}+\varepsilon\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\partial_{t}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Proof.

As each component of
F
in (
3.8
) is smooth, we conclude that
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\in C^{2}(0,T^{*})
by recursion. Hence, differentiating (
3.4
)–(
3.6
) with respect to time is permissible. Differentiating (
3.4
), we obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle\partial^{2}_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle&=-\left\langle(\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle-\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{t}\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle\\
&\qquad+\left\langle\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\times\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle\\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|I_{1}|+\cdots+|I_{8}|,

I_{i}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{6}+\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{6}+\varepsilon\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J+J^{3}+\varepsilon\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

It is noted that
I_{2}=0
since
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}
is divergence-free. For the inner product
I_{3}
, we use the Sobolev embedding
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
to obtain

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

I_{4}
follows in the same manner as

so that

\displaystyle|I_{4}|

\displaystyle\lesssim J+J^{3}+\varepsilon\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

I_{6}

\displaystyle|I_{5}|+|I_{6}|

Lastly, the inner product
I_{7}

I_{1}

\mathbb{L}^{6}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{3}(\Omega)
to produce

\displaystyle|I_{7}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{3}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{4}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{4}+\varepsilon\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

We estimate for

\displaystyle|I_{8}|

\displaystyle\lesssim J^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\partial_{t}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

The required result is obtained by summing up these estimates and absorbing the

∎

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{4}+\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J+J^{2}.

The inner product
I_{2}=0
because
\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}
is divergence-free. Similarly,
I_{5}=I_{7}=I_{9}=0
due to the orthogonality property of the cross product. The estimates for
I_{3}
and
I_{4}
follow similarly to
I_{1}
so that

\displaystyle|I_{3}|+|I_{4}|

\displaystyle\lesssim J^{2},

The same is true of
I_{6}
, however, we need to ensure that we isolate term

to obtain

\displaystyle|I_{6}|

\displaystyle\lesssim J^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\partial_{t}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

The remaining estimates also follow similarly to
I_{1}
to produce

|I_{8}|\lesssim J+J^{2},\qquad|I_{10}|\lesssim J^{2},\qquad|I_{11}|\lesssim J+J^{3}.

The result is proved by summing up these estimates.
∎

Due to the nature of the non-linearity in (

-norm of the time derivative of

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

. For each

\displaystyle\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim J^{2}+J^{3}.

Proof.

Setting
\boldsymbol{\xi}=-\partial_{t}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}
in (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\partial_{t}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|I_{1}|+\cdots+|I_{11}|,

where each
I_{i}
represents the corresponding inner product in (

to obtain

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

,
I_{3}
and

Throughout Lemmas

–
3.7
we have made liberal use of

which contains

to achieve higher regularity for the solutions. These estimates are summarised in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the Galerkin solutions
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}
are bounded such that:

\displaystyle\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{6}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{6}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{10}.

Proof.

Rearrange (
3.4
) such that

(3.13)

\begin{split}\left\langle-\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle&=-\left\langle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle-\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\curl\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle\\
&\qquad-\left\langle(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k})^{\top}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}\right\rangle.\end{split}

We make the observation that the eigenfunctions of the Stokes problem (
3.1
)
\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
are also eigenfunctions of the Stokes operator
A
, see (
2.1
), since

A\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}_{i}=-\mathbb{P}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi_{i}$}=-\mathbb{P}(-\lambda_{i}\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}+\nabla p_{i})=\lambda_{i}\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}_{i},

noting that the Helmholtz–Leray decomposition is unique (up to some constant for
p_{i}
). Consequently, we have (noting that
A
is linear)

\displaystyle-\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}(t)(-\Delta\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}(t)(\lambda_{i}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{i}-\nabla p_{i})=A\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}(t)\nabla p_{i},

and so by taking the inner product of both sides with
A\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}
and using the fact that
\div(A\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k})=0
, we obtain

\left\langle-\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k},A\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\rangle=\left\|A\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Thus, by setting
\boldsymbol{\varphi}=A\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}
in (
3.13
), using Hölder’s inequality, Young’s inequality, and Lemma
2.3
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|A\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\||\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}|\,|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\||\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}|\,|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\||\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}|\,|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\||\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}|\,|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{3}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{3}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Now, estimating the last term on the right-hand side requires additional effort by way of Lemma
2.2
. We have for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, with the Sobolev embedding
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{6}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left(\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{6}+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Continuing with our analysis, we now obtain

\displaystyle\left\|A\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{6}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{6}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{6}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{6}+\varepsilon\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{6}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{6}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{6}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{6}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Next, we rearrange (
3.5

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{6}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{6}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}

Lastly, we rearrange (

\displaystyle-\left\langle\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle-\left\langle(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle+\left\langle|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k},\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\left\langle\nabla\Big(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\Big),\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{k}\Big),\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim|I_{1}|+\cdots+|I_{6}|,

I_{i}
represents the corresponding inner product in the equation above. Previously, we treated

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{k}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1},p_{1})

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}+\curl^{2}\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}&=\curl(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\times\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}})+\curl(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2}),\end{split}

almost everywhere on

2.7
. Now, taking the

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\Delta\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Similarly, taking the
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)
inner product of (
5.2
) with
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}
and using Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\curl\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\||\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}|\,|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}|\,|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\||\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}|\,|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}|\,|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\||\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2}|\,|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}|\,|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\||\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}|\,|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2}|\,|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left(1+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

The magnetisation equation (
5.3
) requires two steps. But before that, we note that

\displaystyle\left||\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}|^{2}-|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}|^{2}\right|\leq\left(|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}|+|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}|\right)|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}|.

Now we are in a position to estimate the difference. First, in a similar manner, we take the
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)
inner product of (
5.3
) with
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}
, note that

and apply integration by parts to the third right-hand side term to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

) with

and integrate by parts so that we have

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left(\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\Delta\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\Delta\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\Delta\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\Delta\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\Delta\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\Delta\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\Delta\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Using Lemma
2.1
and assuming that
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small, the sum of these estimates satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm{d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\Delta\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\left(1+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left(1+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

By Young’s inequality, we note that

1+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim 1+\left\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2},

and similarly for
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{2}
. Therefore, using the Sobolev embedding
\mathbb{W}^{1,6}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and the Gronwall Lemma we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left(\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T^{*}}1+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{2,6}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{2,6}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{2,6}(\Omega)}^{2}\;{\rm d}s\right).

The required result is proved by applying Proposition
4.6
. This completes the proof of Theorem
2.8
.

Theorem A.1

(Aubin’s Lemma
[
3
,
33
]
)
.

Let
X
,
Y
and
B
be Banach spaces such that
X\subset B\subset Y
, where the injection
X\subset B
is compact and the injection
B\subset Y
is continuous. Assume that
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in
L^{p}(0,T;X)
such that
\{\partial_{t}u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
L^{r}(0,T;Y)
where
1\leq p<\infty
and
r=1
, or
p=\infty
and
r>1
. Then there exists a subsequence
\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
which strongly converges in
L^{p}(0,T;B)
.

Theorem A.2

(Generalised Gronwall Lemma
[
4
]
)
.

Assume
u:[a,b]\to[0,\infty)
and
\beta:[a,b]\to[0,\infty)
along with non-decreasing
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfy

u(t)\leq\alpha+\int_{a}^{t}\beta(s)g(u(s))\;{\rm d}s\quad\forall t\in[a,b],

where
\alpha
is a positive constant and
[a,b]\subset[0,\infty)
. Then

u(t)\leq G^{-1}\left(\int_{a}^{t}\beta(s)\;{\rm d}s\right),\quad t\in\mathcal{R},

where
G^{-1}
is the inverse function of

G(\sigma):=\int_{\alpha}^{\sigma}\frac{1}{g(s)}\;{\rm d}s,\quad\sigma\geq 0,

and

\mathcal{R}=\left\{t\in[a,b]:\int_{a}^{t}\beta(s)\;{\rm d}s\in G([0,\infty))\right\}.

Theorem A.3

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Lipschitz boundary, and
let
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

(A.1)

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{\mathbb{W}^{r,q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s_{1}}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s_{2}}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}

for all
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in\mathbb{H}^{s_{2}}(\Omega)
, where
s_{1},s_{2},r
are non-negative
real numbers satisfying

\displaystyle 0\leq s_{1}<s_{2},\quad\theta\in(0,1),\quad 0\leq r<\theta s_{1}+(1-\theta)s_{2},

and
q\in(2,\infty]
satisfies

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(s_{2}-s_{1})\theta}{d}-\frac{s_{2}-r}{d}.

Moreover, when
2<q<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\theta=\frac{2q(s_{2}-r)-d(q-2)}{2q(s_{2}-s_{1})}.

. 1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the Brezis-Nirenberg problem, namely we let

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u=\lambda u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u,\quad&\mbox{in}\,\Omega,\\
&u=0,\quad&\mbox{on}\,\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega
is a smoothly bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 3
,
\lambda>0
is a parameter and
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
is the critical Sobolev exponent.

1.1.
History of (
1.1
)

Problem (
1.1
) is introduced by Brezis and Nirenberg in their celebrated paper
[
13
]
, where they gave a detailed study on the existence of a positive solution of it. By applying the Pohozaev identity
[
49
]
, Brezis and Nirenberg first pointed out in
[
13
]
that problem (
1.1
) only has the trivial solution when
\Omega
is star-shaped and
\lambda\leq 0
, and by multiplying (
1.1
) with the unique eigenfunction of
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
, where
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
with Dirichlet boundary condition, Brezis and Nirenberg next pointed out in
[
13
]
that problem (
1.1
) has no positive solutions for
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
. Moreover, they also showed in
[
13
]
that for
N\geq 4
the problem (
1.1
) admits a positive solution for every
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{1}(\Omega))
. The situation in dimension
N=3
is delicate and in
[
13
]
Brezis and Nirenberg are able to prove the existence of
\lambda_{*}(\Omega)>0
such that a positive solution of (
1.1
) exists for every
\lambda\in(\lambda^{*}(\Omega),\lambda_{1}(\Omega))
. The existence of the positive value
\lambda_{*}(\Omega)
in the existence theory of positive solutions of (
1.1
) is necessary in general bounded domains in dimension three, since they also showed that if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is strictly star-shaped about the origin, then there are no positive solutions of (
1.1
) for
\lambda
close to zero. When
\Omega
is the unit ball
B_{1}
in dimension three, the existence theory of positive solutions of (
1.1
) and the value of
\lambda_{*}(B_{1})
are completely known, i.e.
\lambda_{*}(B_{1})=\frac{\lambda_{1}(B_{1})}{4}
and a positive solution of (
1.1
) exists if and only if
\lambda\in\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}(B_{1})}{4},\lambda_{1}(B_{1})\right)
. Conversely, Bahri and Coron
[
7
]
presented an existence result for a positive solution to problem (
1.1
) for
\Omega
with a nontrivial topology and
\lambda=0
.

In the last decades problem (
1.1
) has attracted the attention of many researchers due to the critical behavior and the lack of compactness of the Brezis-Nirenberg problem (
1.1
) that prevents to use standard variational methods. A first question is about the asymptotic behavior of the solution founded by Brezis and Nirenberg. This was studied by Han
[
30
]
and Rey
[
52
]
and they showed, with different proofs, that such solutions blow up at exactly one point. They also determined the exact blow-up rate as well as the location of the limit concentration point which is a critical point of the Robin function (see also
[
62
]
in which it was shown that this concentration point is a global minimum of the Robin function). Bahri, Li and Rey
[
8
]
determined the asymptotic behavior and locations of multi-bubbling solutions to Brezis-Nirenberg problem. Most recently, a fine multi-bubbles analysis of positive solutions has been object of two papers by König and Laurin. Indeed, in
[
37
]
the authors give an accurate description of the blow-up profile of a solution with multiple blow-up points when
N\geq 4
while in
[
36
]
they consider the case
N=3
. The 3-dimensional case was firstly faced by Druet
[
25
]
.
In particular, the asymptotic analysis of blowing-up solutions ensures that the blow-up points are nothing but the critical points of a suitable function which involves the Green function of
-\Delta
in
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary condition and the Robin function. Furthermore, a counterpart of these asymptotic results are studied. The first result in this direction is due to Rey that built in
[
51
]
a solution that blows up at one non-degenerate critical point of the Robin function when
N\geq 5
and
\lambda\to 0^{+}
. In
[
42
]
, Musso and Pistoia constructed positive solutions with multiple blow-up points when
N\geq 5
as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
whereas, Musso and Salazar
[
44
]
considered the case of multiple blow-up points in dimension
N=3
as
\lambda
goes to the strictly positive number
\lambda^{*}
predicted by Druet in
[
25
]
(see also
[
21
]
for the construction of one-peak solution as
\lambda\to\lambda^{*}
in dimension
N=3
). It is only very recently that the first true bubbling solutions have been constructed in dimension
N=4
as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
, see
[
47
]
for the case of one peak solution and
[
46
]
for the case of multiple peak solutions.

For what concern sign-changing solutions of (
1.1
), several existence results have been obtained for
N\geq 4
. Indeed one can get sign-changing solutions for every
\lambda>0
,
\lambda\not\in\sigma(-\Delta)
if
N=4
and for every
\lambda>0
if
N\geq 5
(see
[
16
,
59
]
). The case
N=3
presents even more difficulties (as for positive solutions) and the question on what is the range of
\lambda
for which a sign-changing solution of (
1.1
) exists in dimension
N=3
has been raised by Brezis in a collection of his favorite open problem
[
12
]
, which, as said by Brezis, is open for 40 years. We point out that, very recently, by using the del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia sign-changing solutions of the Yamabe problem in
[
22
]
as the building blocks, Sun, Wei and Yang constructed infinitely many non-radial sign-changing solutions of the 3-dimensional Brezis-Nirenberg problem (
1.1
) in
\Omega=B_{1}
the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and for any
\lambda>0
in
[
57
]
, which gives a complete answer to the Brezis’ first open problem proposed in
[
12
]
.

As for the multiplicity results, which were obtained mainly through non-standard variational techniques, the first contribution was due to Cerami, Fortunato, and Struwe in
[
18
]
, in which they showed that the number of nontrivial solutions of (
1.1
), for
N\geq 3
, is bounded below by the number of eigenvalues of
-\Delta
belonging to
(\lambda,\lambda+S|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}})
, where
S
is the best constant for the Sobolev embedding and
|\Omega|
is the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
. Moreover, in
[
28
]
it was also proved that if
N\geq 4
, then for any
\lambda>0
and for a suitable class of symmetric domain
\Omega
, problem (
1.1
) has infinitely many solutions of arbitrarily large energy and if
N\geq 7
and
\Omega
is a ball, then for each
\lambda>0
, problem (
1.1
) has infinitely many sign-changing radial solutions (see
[
55
]
). Here the radial symmetry of the domain plays an essential role, therefore their methods do not work for general domains. Furthermore, Cerami, Solimini and Struwe showed in
[
19
]
that if
N\geq 6
and
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{1}(\Omega))
, problem (
1.1
) has a pair of least energy sign-changing solution. In the same paper the authors studied the multiplicity of nodal solutions proving the existence of infinitely many radial solutions when
\Omega
is a ball centered at the origin. Moreover, Devillanova and Solimini in
[
23
]
showed that, if
N\geq 7
and
\Omega
is an open regular subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, problem (
1.1
) has infinitely many solutions for each
\lambda>0
. For low dimensions, namely
N=4,5,6
and in an open regular subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, in
[
24
]
, Devillanova and Solimini proved the existence of at least
N+1
pairs of solutions provided
\lambda
is small enough. This result was extended to all
\lambda>0
in
[
20
]
by Clapp and Weth.
Neither in
[
23
,
24
]
nor in
[
20
]
there is an information on the kind of solutions obtained. Recently, in
[
54
]
, Schechter and Wenming Zou showed that in any bounded and smooth domain, for
N\geq 7
and for each fixed
\lambda>0
, problem (
1.1
) has infinitely many sign changing solutions.

Concerning the profile of sign-changing solutions some results have been obtained in
[
9
,
10
]
for low energy solutions, namely solutions
u_{\lambda}
such that

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{H^{1}_{0}}^{2}\to 2S^{\frac{N}{2}}
as
\lambda\to 0
. More precisely in
[
9
]
it was considered the case
N=3
and they proved
that these solutions concentrate and blow-up in two different points of
\Omega
, as
\lambda\to 0
, and have the asymptotic profile of two separate bubbles. A similar result is proved in
[
10
]
for
N\geq 4
but assuming that the blow-up rate of the positive and negative part of
u_{\lambda}
is the same. This means that, for
N\geq 4
, a solution whose positive part and negative part blow-up at the same point with different speeds of concentration can exist. Indeed, by analyzing the asymptotic behavior of the least energy nodal radial solutions
u_{\lambda}
in the ball, as
\lambda\to 0
in dimension
N\geq 7
one can prove (see
[
31
]
) that their limit profile is that of a

tower of two bubbles
. In
[
34
]
it was shown that, in some symmetric bounded domains, a sign-changing bubble tower exists for
\lambda\to 0
and
N\geq 7
and moreover in dimensions
N=4,5,6
this kind of solutions do not exist (see
[
32
]
). It is worth pointing out that existence of nodal solutions with two nodal regions concentrating in two different points of the domain
\Omega
as
\lambda\to 0
has been obtained in
[
17
]
,
[
40
]
and
[
6
]
while other king of sign-changing solutions for
N\geq 7
that has a concentration on a boundary point (here the domain has a non-trivial topology since the boundary concentration is not always allowed) were studied in
[
61
]
and
[
43
]
.

From the previous discussions it follows that the critical behavior and the lack of compactness of the Brezis-Nirenberg problem (
1.1
) depend strongly on the geometry of the domain and on the dimensions, and in the physical (
N=3
) and low (
4\leq N\leq 6
) dimensions, there are lots of “nonstandard” profiles of sign-changing solutions of the Brezis-Nirenberg problem (
1.1
). Atkinson, Brezis and Peletier
[
3
]
(see also
[
1
]
for a different proof) showed that for
3\leq N\leq 6
and when
\Omega
is a ball, there is a
\lambda_{0}>0
such that (
1.1
) has no sign-changing radial solutions for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
.
Moreover it was also studied the asymptotic behavior of the values
\lambda
for
3\leq N\leq 6
(see also
[
14
]
). By letting
\lambda_{m}
the eigenvalue of
-\Delta
on
B_{1}
which corresponds to a radial eigenfunctions with
m-1
zeros and letting
u_{\lambda}^{m}
be a solution of (
1.1
) with
m-1
zeros, they showed that

•

if
N=3
then for every
m\geq 2
we have that
\lambda\to\left(\frac{2m-1}{2}\pi\right)^{2}
as
\|u_{\lambda}^{m}\|_{\infty}\to\infty
;

•

if
N=4,5
then for every
m\geq 2
we have that
\lambda\to\lambda_{m-1}
as
\|u_{\lambda}^{m}\|_{\infty}\to\infty
;

•

if
N=6
then for every
m\geq 2
we have
\lambda\to\lambda_{m-1}^{*}
as
\|u_{\lambda}^{m}\|_{\infty}\to\infty
where
\lambda^{*}_{m-1}\in(0,\lambda_{m-1})
.

Moreover if
m=2
(so
u_{\lambda}
has only two nodal regions) Gazzola and Grunau
[
39
]
showed that
\lambda\to\lambda^{+}_{1}
if
N=4
while
\lambda\to\lambda_{1}^{-}
if
N=5
. These analysis show that concentration values which is different from zero for
N\geq 4
and different from
\lambda^{*}
for
N=3
predicted by Druet in
[
25
]
can appear, at least for radial solutions in the physical (
N=3
) and low (
4\leq N\leq 6
) dimensions and thus, it is reasonable to first understand what is the asymptotic behavior of nodal radial solutions in the physical (
N=3
) and low (
4\leq N\leq 6
) dimensions as
\lambda
converges to the limit value which is different from zero for
N\geq 4
and different from
\lambda^{*}
for
N=3
.

In
[
33
]
, the authors showed that given
u_{\lambda}
, a nodal radial solutions of (
1.1
) having two nodal regions and such that
u_{\lambda}(0)>0
then

(i)

if
N=6
then
\lambda_{2}^{*}\in(0,\lambda_{1}(B_{1}))
and, as
\lambda\to\lambda_{2}^{*}
, the positive part of
u_{\lambda}
concentrates at the center of the ball,
\|u_{\lambda}^{+}\|_{\infty}\to\infty
, and a suitable rescaling of
u_{\lambda}^{+}
converges to the standard positive solution of the critical problem in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Instead the negative part
u_{\lambda}^{-}
converges to the unique positive solution of (
1.1
) in
B_{1}
, as
\lambda\to\lambda_{2}^{*}
;

(ii)

if
N=4,5
then
u_{\lambda}^{+}
behaves as for the case

, while
u_{\lambda}^{-}
converges to zero uniformly in
B_{1}
as
\lambda\to\lambda_{1}(B_{1})
;

(iii)

if
N=3
then
\lambda_{2}^{*}=\frac{9}{4}\lambda_{1}(B_{1})
and
u_{\lambda}^{+}
behaves as for the case
N=6
, while
u_{\lambda}^{-}
converges
to zero uniformly in
B_{1}
.

In a recent paper
[
2
]
, Amadori et al. gave a completed description of concentration values and bubbling phenomenon of the Brezis-Nirenberg problem (
1.1
) in the radial setting for all
N\geq 3
, which refine the results in
[
1
,
3
,
14
]
.

The existence of a solution predicted by these asymptotic analysis in
[
1
,
3
,
2
,
14
,
33
]
was first studied in
[
35
]
where the authors considered the case
N=4,5
showing that in general (symmetric) bounded domains of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N=4,5
), there exist a sign-changing solutions of problem (
1.1
) having, as
\lambda\to\lambda_{1}^{\pm}(\Omega)
, the following asymptotic profile

u_{\lambda}\sim\mathcal{W}_{\mu,0}-\tau e_{1}

where
\mathcal{W}_{\mu,0}
is the projection of the standard Aubin-Talenti bubble in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
\mu,\tau\approx 0
and

is the first eigenfunction of
-\Delta
with Dirichlet boundary condition. Very recently, in
[
38
]
it was generalized the result in
[
35
]
. Indeed, by using the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction arguments, we construct multi-bump bubbling solutions of (
1.1
), which look like

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}\approx\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tau_{i}e_{i}

as
\lambda
is close to any fixed eigenvalue of the Laplacian operator
-\Delta
with Dirichlet boundary condition, where
\{e_{i}\}
is an orthogonal system related to the fixed eigenvalue,
\tau_{l}\approx 0
for all
1\leq l\leq m
,
\beta_{j}=\pm 1
,
\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}
is the projection of the standard Aubin-Talenti bubble in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
\mu_{j}\approx 0
and
\xi_{j}\to\xi_{j,0}\in\Omega
for all
1\leq j\leq k
. Here, the governing functions of the bubbling phenomenon of
4D
and
5D
Brezis-Nirenberg problem as
\lambda
approaches any eigenvalue of the Laplacian operator
-\Delta
with Dirichlet boundary condition are also found by studying some variational problems associated to it.

1.2.
New results in dimension six

The existence of a solution predicted by these asymptotic analysis in
[
1
,
3
,
2
,
14
,
33
]
for dimension
N=6
is more delicate, since it is known in
[
1
,
3
,
2
,
14
,
33
]
for the ODE setting that the profile of the bubbling solution of the
6D
Brezis-Nirenberg problem should be very different from that of the
4D
and
5D
cases. Besides, the computations in
[
35
,
38
]
imply that the zero weak limit and the blow-down part of the ansatz can not balance the bubbles near the blow-up points, and the blow-up analysis in
[
26
]
reveals a competitive mechanism between contributions of nonzero weak limit and linear term in dimension
N=6
. Thus, a nonzero weak limit is needed in constructing a ture blow-up solution of the
6D
Brezis-Nirenberg problem (
1.2
), which reads as

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u=\lambda u+|u|u,\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
&u=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Pistoia and Vaira successfully proved in
[
48
]
that (
1.2
) has a bubbling solution, which looks like

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}\approx u_{\lambda_{0}}-\mathcal{W}_{\mu,\xi}+(\lambda-\lambda_{0})v_{\lambda_{0}}

as
\lambda\to\lambda_{0}
in generic bounded domains in the sense that any solution of (
1.2
) is non-degenerate, where
\mu\approx 0
,
u_{\lambda_{0}}
is a ground-state solution of (
1.2
) satisfying

(1.3)

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}=2\max_{\Omega}u_{\lambda_{0}}(x)=u_{\lambda_{0}}(\xi_{0})

and
v_{\lambda_{0}}
is the unique solution of the following equation:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v=\left(\lambda_{0}+2|u_{\lambda_{0}}|\right)v+u_{\lambda_{0}},\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
&v=0\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

To complete the construction, it is assumed in
[
48
]
that
2v_{\lambda_{0}}(\xi_{0})\neq 1
, which is naturally satisfied in the ODE setting (cf.
[
2
]
). We remark that, to our best knowledge, the introduction of nonzero weak limits in constructing bubbling solution of critical elliptic equtions first appeared in
[
61
]
and can be also found in
[
29
,
53
,
43
]
and the references therein.

In this paper, we shall improve the results in
[
48
]
in two fonts by constructing multi-bubble solutions without the assumption
2v_{\lambda_{0}}(\xi_{0})\neq 1
for concentration points
\xi_{0}
. In order to state our main result, we first introduce necessary notations and assumptions. By
[
48
, Theorem 1.2]
, we know that for generic domains
\Omega
with
\partial\Omega\in C^{3,\alpha}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if
u_{\lambda}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
solves (
1.2
) for
\lambda>0
, then
u_{\lambda}
is non-degenerate in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
in the sense that the Kernel of the operator
-\Delta-\left(\lambda+(2^{*}-1)|u_{\lambda}|^{2^{*}-2}\right)
, which is denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
, is empty in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Thus, we say that
\Omega
, with
\partial\Omega\in C^{3,\alpha}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, is
a non-degenerate domain
of the Brezis-Nirenberg problem (
1.2
) if any solution of (
1.2
) in this bounded domain
\Omega
is non-degenerate.

Let
u_{\lambda}
be a solution of the
6D
Brezis-Nirenberg problem (
1.2
) for
\lambda>0
in a non-degenerate domain
\Omega
, then by the Fredholm alternative, the following equation

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v=\left(\lambda+2|u_{\lambda}|\right)v+u_{\lambda},\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
&v=0\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

has a unique solution, which is denoted by
v_{\lambda}
. We introduce the sets

\displaystyle\Omega_{u_{\lambda},\pm\frac{\lambda}{2}}=\left\{x\in\Omega\mid u_{\lambda}(x)=\pm\frac{\lambda}{2}\text{ and }\nabla u_{\lambda}(x)=0\right\}.

For any
\xi\in\Omega_{u_{\lambda},\pm\frac{\lambda}{2}}
, since
u_{\lambda}
solves (
1.2
) for
\lambda>0
, we have

\displaystyle\text{Trace}\left(\nabla^{2}u_{\lambda}(\xi)\right)=\Delta u_{\lambda}(\xi)\not=0.

Thus,
\text{Ker}^{\perp}\left(\nabla^{2}u_{\lambda}(\xi)\right)\not=\emptyset
, where
\text{Ker}\left(\nabla^{2}u_{\lambda}(\xi)\right)
is the kernel of the matrix
\nabla^{2}u_{\lambda}(\xi)
and the orthogonality is in
\mathbb{R}^{6}
. We use
\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots e_{6}\}
to denote the orthogonal system on
\mathbb{S}^{5}
such that
\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots e_{l}\}
and
\{e_{l+1},e_{l+2}\cdots e_{6}\}
are the basis of
\text{Ker}^{\perp}\left(\nabla^{2}u_{\lambda}(\xi)\right)
and
\text{Ker}\left(\nabla^{2}u_{\lambda}(\xi)\right)
, respectively, where
1\leq l\leq 6
. We also introduce the sets

\displaystyle\Omega_{v_{\lambda},\pm\frac{1}{2}}=\left\{x\in\Omega_{u_{\lambda},\pm\frac{\lambda}{2}}\mid v_{\lambda}(x)=\pm\frac{1}{2}\right\}

and

\displaystyle\Omega_{u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda},\pm}=\left\{\xi\in\Omega_{u_{\lambda},\pm\frac{\lambda}{2}}\mid\nabla v_{\lambda}(\xi)\in\text{Ker}^{\perp}\left(\nabla^{2}u_{\lambda}(\xi)\right)\right\}.

We say that
u_{\lambda}
is an
essentially non-degenerate
solution of the
6D
Brezis-Nirenberg problem (
1.2
) in the non-degenerate domain
\Omega
if

(1.5)

\displaystyle\Omega_{u_{\lambda},*}:=\Omega_{u_{\lambda},+}\cup\Omega_{u_{\lambda},-}\not=\emptyset,

where

(1.6)

\displaystyle\Omega_{u_{\lambda},\pm}=\Omega_{u_{\lambda},\pm\frac{\lambda}{2}}\setminus\left(\Omega_{v_{\lambda},\pm\frac{1}{2}}\cap\Omega_{u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda},\pm}\right).

Our first result introduces the sufficient and necessary condition of a solution
u_{\lambda}
of (
1.2
) to be essentially non-degenerate.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u_{\lambda}
be a solution of the
6D
Brezis-Nirenberg problem (
1.2
) in the non-degenerate domain
\Omega
. Then
u_{\lambda}
is essentially non-degenerate if and only if
\Omega_{u_{\lambda},\frac{\lambda}{2}}\cup\Omega_{u_{\lambda},-\frac{\lambda}{2}}\not=\emptyset
.

In the ODE setting, that is,
\Omega=\mathbb{B}(0,1)
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{6}
, it is well known that

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}(0,1)_{u_{\lambda_{0}},v_{\lambda_{0}},+}=\mathbb{B}(0,1)_{u_{\lambda_{0}},\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}}=\{0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{B}(0,1)_{u_{\lambda_{0}},v_{\lambda_{0}},-}=\mathbb{B}(0,1)_{u_{\lambda_{0}},-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}}=\emptyset,

where
u_{\lambda_{0}}

Even though the Brezis-Nirenberg problem (

1.3.1.
Variational proofs of Brezis’ first open problem

Through a highly intricate and technical construction, Sun, Wei, and Yang provided a solution to Brezis’ first open problem. It is natural to ask

Open question 1
: Is there a more direct and possible variational proof of Brezis’ first open problem?

1.3.2.
Infinitely many sign-changing solutions of the Brezis-Nirenberg problem (
1.1
).

In
[
57
]
, it was proved that (
1.1

\Omega=\mathbb{B}(0,1)
has infinitely many non-radial sign-changing solutions with arbitrary large energy for every

[
22

[
58
]
proved the existence of infinitely many sign-changing solutions. Their approach was to populate the domain with a large number of crown-type solutions.
Since there is no reason to think that this strategy will be invalid in general bounded domains, we want to ask

Open question 2
: In a general bounded domain
\Omega
, if necessary, it could be convex, are there infinitely many sign-changing solutions of (
1.1
) with arbitrary large energy for any
\lambda>0
?

In dimension 4, the existence of sign-changing solutions was proved for every
\lambda>0,\lambda\not\in\sigma(-\Delta)
(
[
16
]
). The missing case is

Open question 3
: when
N=4,\lambda\in\sigma(-\Delta)
, are there sign-changing solutions for every
\lambda>0
?

In
[
54
]
, Schechter and Zou proved that for
N\geq 7,\lambda>0
there are infinitely many sign-changing solutions. A natural equation is

Open equestion 4
:  In a general bounded domain, are there infinitely many sign-changing solutions when
N\leq 6
?

\Omega

1.3.7.
Related studies of the Brezis-Nirenberg type problems.

There are lots of Brezis-Nirenberg type problems, such as the fractional version:

(1.13)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta)^{s}u=\lambda u+|u|^{2_{s}^{*}-2}u,\quad&\mbox{in}\,\Omega,\\
&u=0,\quad&\mbox{on}\,\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
0<s<1
and
2_{s}^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2s}
, the high order version:

(1.14)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta)^{m}u=\lambda u+|u|^{2_{m}^{*}-2}u,\quad&\mbox{in}\,\Omega,\\
&u=0,\quad&\mbox{on}\,\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq 2
and
2_{m}^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2m}
, the
p
-Laplacian version:

(1.15)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda u+|u|^{2_{p}^{*}-2}u,\quad&\mbox{in}\,\Omega,\\
&u=0,\quad&\mbox{on}\,\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Delta_{p}u=\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
with
1<p<N
and
2_{p}^{*}=\frac{pN}{N-p}
, and so on. The studies on these Brezis-Nirenberg type problems also have a rich history which we do not want to review in this paper. Thus, we also want to ask

Open question 13
: Could the very recent results of the Brezis-Nirenberg problem (
1.1
) be generalized to these Brezis-Nirenberg type problems?

2.
Setting of the problem and the ansatz

2.1.
Preliminaries

Let
(-\Delta)^{-1}:L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the operator defined as the unique solution of the equation
-\Delta u=v
in
\Omega
and
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
. By the Hölder inequality it follows that

\|(-\Delta)^{-1}(v)\|\leq C\|v\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}},\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,v\in L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)

for some positive
C
independent of
v
. Hence we rewrite the problem (
1.2
) as

(2.1)

u=(-\Delta)^{-1}\left[f(u)+(\lambda_{0}+\varepsilon)u\right],\quad u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

with
f(u)=|u|u
. We denote by
G(x,y)
the Green’s function of the Laplace operator given by

G(x,y)=\frac{1}{4\omega_{6}}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{4}}-H(x,y)\right),

where
\omega_{6}
denotes the surface area of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{6}
and
H
is the regular part of
G
, namely

\Delta H(x,y)=0,\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega,\qquad H(x,y)=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{4}},\quad\mbox{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.

In the previous section we have denoted by
\mathcal{W}_{\mu,\xi}
the projection of the bubble
\mathcal{U}_{\mu,\xi}
onto
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. It is well known that the following expansion holds (see
[
51
]
)

(2.2)

\mathcal{W}_{\mu,\xi}(x)=\mathcal{U}_{\mu,\xi}-\alpha_{6}\mu^{2}H(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(\mu^{4}),\quad\mbox{as}\,\mu\to 0

uniformly with respect to
\xi
in compact sets of
\Omega
. Moreover (see
[
11
]
) it is also known that every solution to the linear equation

-\Delta\Psi_{\mu,\xi}=2\mathcal{U}_{\mu,\xi}\Psi_{\mu,\xi},\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{6}

is a linear combination of the functions
\Psi^{i}_{\mu,\xi}

(i=0,\ldots,6)
given by

\Psi^{0}_{\mu,\xi}(x)=\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{U}_{\mu,\xi}=2\alpha_{6}\mu\frac{|x-\xi|^{2}-\mu^{2}}{(\mu^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2})^{3}}

and

\Psi^{i}_{\mu,\xi}(x)=\partial_{\xi^{i}}\mathcal{U}_{\mu,\xi}=4\alpha_{6}\mu^{2}\frac{x^{i}-\xi^{i}}{(\mu^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2})^{3}},\quad i=1,\ldots,6.

We denote by
\mathcal{Z}_{\mu,\xi}^{i}
the projection of
\Psi^{i}_{\mu,\xi}
onto
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e.

-\Delta\mathcal{Z}_{\mu,\xi}^{i}=2\mathcal{U}_{\mu,\xi}\Psi^{i}_{\mu,\xi},\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega,\qquad\mathcal{Z}_{\mu,\xi}^{i}=0,\quad\mbox{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.

Elliptic estimates give

\mathcal{Z}_{\mu,\xi}^{0}(x)=\Psi^{0}_{\mu,\xi}-2\mu\alpha_{6}H(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(\mu^{3}),\quad\mbox{as}\,\,\mu\to 0

and

\mathcal{Z}_{\mu,\xi}^{i}(x)=\Psi^{i}_{\mu,\xi}-\mu^{2}\alpha_{6}\partial_{\xi^{i}}H(x,\xi)+\mathcal{O}(\mu^{4}),\quad\mbox{as}\,\,\mu\to 0,\,\,i=1,\ldots,6

uniformly with respect to
\xi
in compact sets of
\Omega
.

2.2.
The ansatz

In what follows we look for a solution of (
1.2
) of the form

(2.3)

u_{\lambda}(x):=u_{\lambda_{0}}(x)+\varepsilon v_{\lambda_{0}}(x)+\varepsilon^{2}w_{\lambda_{0}}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)+\varphi_{\lambda}(x)

where

(2.4)

\mu_{j}=\tau_{j}\bar{\mu}\quad\mbox{with}\,\,\lim_{\lambda\to\lambda_{0}}\tau_{j}:=\tau_{j,0}>0\,\,\mbox{being constants}

and
\bar{\mu}>0
with
\lim\limits_{\lambda\to\lambda_{0}}\bar{\mu}=0
. Moreover

(2.5)

\xi_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\xi_{j,0},&\mbox{if}\,\,\xi_{j,0}\in\Omega_{u_{\lambda_{0}},\pm\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}}\setminus\Omega_{v_{\lambda_{0}},\pm\frac{1}{2}},\\
\xi_{j,0}+\rho e_{j,l_{j}+1},&\mbox{if}\,\,\xi_{j,0}\in\left(\Omega_{u_{\lambda_{0}},\pm\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}}\setminus\Omega_{u_{\lambda_{0}},v_{\lambda_{0}},\pm}\right)\cap\Omega_{v_{\lambda_{0}},\pm\frac{1}{2}}.\\
\end{array}\right.

We remark that
\mu_{j}=\mu_{j}(\lambda)
and
\xi_{j}=\xi_{j}(\lambda)
. Without loss of generality, we assume also that

0<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{k}.

Finally,
\varphi_{\lambda}
is a remainder term which is small as
\lambda\to\lambda_{0}
which belongs, as usual, to the space
\mathcal{K}^{\bot}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}
defined as follows. Let

\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}:={\rm span}\left\{\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}\,\,:\,\,j=1,\ldots,k,\,\,i=0,\ldots,6\right\}

and

\mathcal{K}^{\bot}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}:=\left\{\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\,\,:\,\,\langle\varphi,\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}\rangle=0\,\,i=0,\ldots,6\,\,j=1,\ldots k\right\}.

Let
\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}
and
\Pi^{\bot}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}
be the projections of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
onto
\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{\bot}
respectively. Then, solving (
2.1
), is equivalent to solve the system

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Pi^{\bot}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\left\{u_{\lambda}(x)-(-\Delta)^{-1}\left[f(u_{\lambda})+\lambda u_{\lambda}\right]\right\}=0,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\left\{u_{\lambda}(x)-(-\Delta)^{-1}\left[f(u_{\lambda})+\lambda u_{\lambda}\right]\right\}=0,

where
u_{\lambda}(x)
is given by (
2.3
). In what follows, first we will solve (
2.6
) to find the remainder term
\varphi_{\lambda}(x)
and then we study the finite dimensional equation (
2.7
).

3.
The Nonlinear Projected problem

Let us define

(3.1)

\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}:=u_{\lambda_{0}}(x)+\varepsilon v_{\lambda_{0}}(x)+\varepsilon^{2}w_{\lambda_{0}}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}(x)

and

(3.2)

z_{\lambda_{0}}(x):=u_{\lambda_{0}}(x)+\varepsilon v_{\lambda_{0}}(x)+\varepsilon^{2}w_{\lambda_{0}}(x).

The equation (
2.6
) can be rewritten as

(3.3)

\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\varphi_{\lambda})+\mathcal{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}+\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\varphi_{\lambda})=0

where

(3.4)

\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\varphi_{\lambda}):=\Pi^{\bot}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\left\{\varphi_{\lambda}-(-\Delta)^{-1}\left[f^{\prime}(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})\varphi_{\lambda}+\lambda\varphi_{\lambda}\right]\right\}

is the linearized operator at
\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}
,

(3.5)

\mathcal{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}:=\Pi^{\bot}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\left\{\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}-(-\Delta)^{-1}\left[f(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})+\lambda\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right]\right\}

is the error term while

\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\varphi_{\lambda}):=\Pi^{\bot}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\left\{-(-\Delta)^{-1}\left[f(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}+\varphi_{\lambda})-f(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})-f^{\prime}(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})\varphi_{\lambda}\right]\right\}

is a quadratic form. To solve (
3.3
), we need first to estimate the
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)-
norm of the error term
\mathcal{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}
given by (
3.5
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
(\xi_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,k}
satisfying (
2.5
) and any
(\mu_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,k}
satisfying (
2.4
) and for any
|\varepsilon|
sufficiently small it holds

\|\mathcal{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\|\lesssim\bar{\mu}^{2}|\ln\bar{\mu}|^{\frac{2}{3}}+|\varepsilon|^{3}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu,\xi}}
, we have that

\displaystyle-\Delta\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}-(\lambda_{0}+\varepsilon)\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}-f(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})=-f(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})+f(u_{\lambda_{0}})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{U}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}^{2}

\displaystyle-(\lambda_{0}+\varepsilon)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}+\varepsilon^{2}f^{\prime}(u_{\lambda_{0}})w_{\lambda_{0}}+\varepsilon^{2}{\rm sgn}(u_{\lambda_{0}}(x))v_{\lambda_{0}}^{2}-\varepsilon^{3}w_{\lambda_{0}}.

By continuity of
\Pi^{\bot}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}
, (
3.1
) and (
3.5
), we get that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\|\lesssim

\displaystyle\|-\Delta\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}-(\lambda_{0}+\varepsilon)\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}-f(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\underbrace{\|-f(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}^{2}+f(z_{\lambda_{0}})\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}}_{\|(I)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\|-f(z_{\lambda_{0}})+f(u_{\lambda_{0}})+\varepsilon^{2}{\rm sgn}(u_{\lambda_{0}}(x))v_{\lambda_{0}}^{2}+\varepsilon f^{\prime}(u_{\lambda_{0}})v_{\lambda_{0}}+\varepsilon^{2}f^{\prime}(u_{\lambda_{0}})w_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}}_{\|(II)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}}

\displaystyle+\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}(\mathcal{U}^{2}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}-\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}^{2})\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}+(\lambda_{0}+|\varepsilon|)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\underbrace{|\varepsilon|^{3}\|w_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}}_{:=\mathcal{O}(|\varepsilon|^{3})}.

It follows from (
1.9
), (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) that

\|\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{U}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim\bar{\mu}^{2}|\ln\bar{\mu}|^{\frac{2}{3}}

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{W}^{2}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}-\mathcal{U}^{2}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\Omega}\underbrace{|\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}-\mathcal{U}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}|^{\frac{3}{2}}}_{:=\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{3})}|\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}+\mathcal{U}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bar{\mu}^{2}\|\mathcal{U}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}=o\left(\bar{\mu}^{2}|\ln\bar{\mu}|^{\frac{2}{3}}\right).

To estimate
\|(I)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}
, we need to divide the whole domain
\Omega
into two parts. The first part is in a small neighborhood of every point
\xi_{h}
to be
B_{\sqrt{\mu}_{h}}(\xi_{h})
. In every
B_{\sqrt{\xi}_{h}}(\xi_{h})
, we rewrite

\displaystyle f\left(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu,\xi}}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}^{2}-f(z_{\lambda_{0}})=

\displaystyle f\left(\beta_{h}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{h},\xi_{h}}+\sum_{j\neq h}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}+z_{\lambda_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle-\beta_{h}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{h},\xi_{h}}^{2}-\sum_{j\neq h}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}^{2}-f(z_{\lambda_{0}}),

which, together with Taylor expansion, implies that

\displaystyle\left|f\left(\beta_{h}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{h},\xi_{h}}+\sum_{j\neq h}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}+z_{\lambda_{0}}\right)-\beta_{h}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{h},\xi_{h}}^{2}-\sum_{j\neq h}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}^{2}-f(z_{\lambda_{0}})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{h},\xi_{h}}\left(\sum_{j\neq h}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}+|z_{\lambda_{0}}|\right)+\sum_{j\neq h}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}^{2}+|f(z_{\lambda_{0}})|.

In the remaining region
\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{h=1}^{k}B_{\sqrt{\mu}_{h}}(\xi_{h})
, we rewrite

\displaystyle f\left(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu,\xi}}\right)-f(z_{\lambda_{0}})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}^{2}=f\left(z_{\lambda_{0}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}\right)-f(z_{\lambda_{0}})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}^{2},

which, together with Taylor expansion again, implies that

\displaystyle\left|f\left(z_{\lambda_{0}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}\right)-f(z_{\lambda_{0}})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}^{2}\right|\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}+\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}^{2}\right).

Now, by direct computations with (
1.9
), (
2.2
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we get that

\|(I)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim\sum_{h=1}^{k}\|(I)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}},B_{\sqrt{\mu}_{h}}(\xi_{h})}+\|(I)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}},\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{h=1}^{k}B_{\sqrt{\mu}_{h}}(\xi_{h})}\lesssim\bar{\mu}^{2}|\ln\bar{\mu}|^{\frac{2}{3}}.

For what concerning
\|(II)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}
, we notice that

f(z_{\lambda_{0}})-f(u_{\lambda_{0}})-\varepsilon f^{\prime}(u_{\lambda_{0}})v_{\lambda_{0}}-\varepsilon^{2}f^{\prime}(u_{\lambda_{0}})w_{\lambda_{0}}-\text{sgn}(u_{\lambda_{0}})\varepsilon^{2}v_{\lambda_{0}}^{2}=\mathcal{O}(|\varepsilon|^{3}),

Then
\|(II)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim|\varepsilon|^{3}
and the thesis follows.
∎

Then, we deal with the invertibility of the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}
defined in (
3.4
). Since the proof is almost standard, we omit it (see
[
61
]
Lemma 2.4 or
[
53
]
Lemma 4.2).

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
(\xi_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,k}
satisfying (

) and any

satisfying (

In particular,

is invertible with inverse which is uniformly bounded.

With Lemmas
3.1
and

in hands, we are in position now to find a solution of the equation (

) whose proof relies on a standard contraction mapping argument (see for example
[

]
, Proposition 1.8 and
[

]

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
(\xi_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,k}
satisfying (

) and any

satisfying (

such that for any

4.
The finite dimensional problem

Let now
\varphi_{\lambda}
be the solution found in Proposition
3.1
. To solve (
2.7
), we have to find the parameters
\boldsymbol{\mu}(\varepsilon)
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}(\varepsilon)
satisfying (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) such that
\varphi_{\lambda}
satisfies the equation (
2.7
). It is well known that the reduced problem has a variational structure, that is, the solutions of (
1.2
) are critical points of the
C^{2}-
functional
J_{\lambda}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

J_{\lambda}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}F(u)\,dx

where
F(s):=\int_{0}^{s}f(t)\,dt
. Now let
\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}\to\mathbb{R}
be the reduced energy given by

\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}({\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})=J_{\lambda}(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}+\varphi_{\lambda}).

We shall give explicit asymptotic expressions for the reduced energy.

Proposition 4.1
.

(i)

If
({\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})
satisfying (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) is a critical point of the reduced energy
\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}({\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})
then
\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}+\varphi_{\lambda}
is a solution of (
1.2
).

(ii)

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for every
\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})

(4.1)

\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}({\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}):=\mathfrak{c}_{0}(\varepsilon)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,j}(\xi_{j},\mu_{j})+\mathcal{O}(|\varepsilon|^{2}\bar{\mu}^{2}+|\varepsilon|^{6}+\bar{\mu}^{\frac{7}{2}}+\bar{\mu}^{3}\left|\xi_{j}-\xi_{j,0}\right|^{2})

where

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,j}(\xi_{j},\mu_{j})=d_{1}\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}+\beta_{j}u_{\lambda_{0}}(\xi_{j})\right)\mu_{j}^{2}+\varepsilon d_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta_{j}v_{\lambda_{0}}(\xi_{j})\right)\mu_{j}^{2}+\frac{11}{9}d_{2}\mu_{j}^{3}

with
d_{1}=\left\|\mathcal{U}\right\|_{L^{2},\mathbb{R}^{6}}^{2}
and
d_{2}=\alpha_{6}^{\frac{3}{2}}\omega_{6}\left|u_{\lambda_{0}}(\xi_{j,0})\right|^{\frac{3}{2}}
.

Proof.

The proof of
(i)
is quite standard so we omit it(see, for instance, Proposition 2.2 of
[
41
]
).
Now we show
(ii)
. In order to simplify once more the notations we let

\mathcal{W}_{j}:=\mathcal{W}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}},\quad\mathcal{U}_{j}:=\mathcal{U}_{\mu_{j},\xi_{j}}.

First, by using (
3.6
) and by making standard computations we get that

J_{\lambda}(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}+\varphi_{\lambda})=J_{\lambda}(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})+\mathcal{O}\left(|\varepsilon|^{2}\|\varphi_{\lambda}\|+\|\varphi_{\lambda}\|^{2}\right).

Now, we need to estimate the main term of
J_{\lambda}(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}})=\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\|z_{\lambda_{0}}\|^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{0}+\varepsilon}{2}\|z_{\lambda_{0}}\|^{2}-\|z_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{L^{3}}^{3}}_{:=\mathfrak{c}_{1}(\varepsilon)}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\mathcal{W}_{j}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\mathcal{W}_{j}\|_{L^{3}}^{3}}_{(A)}

\displaystyle-\underbrace{\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\mathcal{W}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\langle f\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}\right),u_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}_{(B)}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\left(\left\langle\mathcal{W}_{i},\mathcal{W}_{j}\right\rangle-(\lambda_{0}+\varepsilon)\left\langle\mathcal{W}_{i},\mathcal{W}_{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right)}_{(C)}

\displaystyle-\underbrace{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\mathcal{W}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\varepsilon\left\langle f\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}\right),v_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}_{(E)}-\underbrace{\varepsilon^{2}\left\langle f\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}\right),w_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}_{(F)}-\underbrace{\varepsilon^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\left\langle\mathcal{W}_{j},w_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}_{(G)}

\displaystyle-\underbrace{\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\left\|\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right\|_{L^{3}}^{3}-\left\|z_{\lambda_{0}}\right\|_{L^{3}}^{3}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\|\mathcal{W}_{j}\right\|_{L^{3}}^{3}\right)-\left\langle f\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}\right),z_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}-\left\langle f(z_{\lambda_{0}}),\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right]}_{(H)}

\displaystyle-\underbrace{\left\langle f(z_{\lambda_{0}})-f(u_{\lambda_{0}})-f^{\prime}(u_{\lambda_{0}})\varepsilon v_{\lambda_{0}}-f^{\prime}(u_{\lambda_{0}})\varepsilon^{2}w_{\lambda_{0}}-\varepsilon^{2}{\rm sgn}(u_{\lambda_{0}})v_{\lambda_{0}}^{2},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}_{(I)}.

where
z_{\lambda_{0}}
is given by (
3.2
).
By using (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) we have that
\phi_{j}:=\mathcal{W}_{j}-\mathcal{U}_{j}=\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{2}).
Then it is easy to show that

(A):=\frac{k}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\mathcal{U}^{3}\,dx+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{4})=\mathfrak{c}_{2}+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{4}).

It follows from (
1.9
), (
2.2
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) that

\displaystyle(B)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\|\mathcal{U}_{j}+\phi_{j}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\langle f(\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}),u_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}-\left\langle f\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}f(\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}),u_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\underbrace{\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\left\|\mathcal{U}_{j}\right\|^{2}-\beta_{j}\left\langle\mathcal{U}_{j}^{2},u_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right)}_{(B_{1})}-\underbrace{\left\langle f\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}f(\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}),u_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}_{(B_{2})}+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{4})

and

\displaystyle(B_{1})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\mu_{j}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\mathcal{U}^{2}\,dx-\beta_{j}\mu_{j}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\mathcal{U}^{2}u_{\lambda_{0}}(\mu_{j}y+\xi_{j})\,dy+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{4})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}d_{1}\mu_{j}^{2}-\beta_{j}u_{\lambda_{0}}(\xi_{j})\mu_{j}^{2}\|\mathcal{U}\|_{L^{2},\mathbb{R}^{6}}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{4})+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{3}|\xi_{j}-\xi_{j,0}|^{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}d_{1}\mu_{j}^{2}-\beta_{j}u_{\lambda_{0}}(\xi_{j})d_{1}\mu_{j}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{4})+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{3}|\xi_{j}-\xi_{j,0}|^{2})

while

\displaystyle(B_{2})=

\displaystyle-\sum_{h=1}^{k}\left\langle f\left(\beta_{h}\mathcal{W}_{h}+\sum_{j\neq h}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}\right)-f(\beta_{h}\mathcal{W}_{h})-\sum_{j\neq h}f(\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}),u_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rangle_{L^{2},B_{\rho}(\xi_{h})}

\displaystyle-\left\langle f\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}f(\beta_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}),u_{\lambda_{0}}\right\rangle_{L^{2},\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{h=1}^{k}B_{\rho}(\xi_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{k}\sum_{j\neq k}\left\langle\mathcal{W}_{h},\mathcal{W}_{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2},B_{\rho}(\xi_{h})}+\sum_{h=1}^{k}\sum_{j\neq k}\left\|\mathcal{W}_{j}\right\|_{L^{2},B_{\rho}(\xi_{h})}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\|\mathcal{W}_{j}\right\|_{L^{2},\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{h=1}^{k}B_{\rho}(\xi_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}^{\frac{7}{2}}.

Hence

(B):=-\mu_{j}^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}+\beta_{j}u_{\lambda_{0}}(\xi_{j})\right)d_{1}+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{\frac{7}{2}})+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{3}|\xi_{j}-\xi_{j,0}|^{2}).

In a very similar way, it is possible to show that

(E):=-\varepsilon\mu_{j}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta_{j}v_{\lambda_{0}}(\xi_{j})\right)d_{1}+\mathcal{O}(|\varepsilon|\bar{\mu}^{\frac{7}{2}})+\mathcal{O}(|\varepsilon|\bar{\mu}^{3}|\xi_{j}-\xi_{j,0}|^{2}).

Since
-\Delta\mathcal{W}_{i}=\mathcal{U}_{i}^{3}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
, then it follows directly that
(C)=\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mu}^{\frac{7}{2}})
. Moreover

(F)\lesssim|\varepsilon|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\mathcal{W}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\mathcal{O}(|\varepsilon|^{2}\bar{\mu}^{2})

and

\quad\nabla w_{\eta}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}u_{\lambda}(\xi)(\xi-\eta)-\lambda\nabla v_{\lambda}(\xi)

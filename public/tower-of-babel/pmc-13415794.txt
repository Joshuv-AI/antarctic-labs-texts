UCTLFANet: a low-rank fine-tuning model for microalgae image segmentation
Authors: Ziyue Liu, Yuan Cheng, Dan Liu, Meiyi Jiang, Wenhao Yue, Shuo Yan, Fengyu Tian, Liming Cao, Daoming Jia, Yanbo Jiang, Hai Bi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe fine-tuning module optimizes the model’s parameter structure while conserving computing resources. We introduce UCTLFANet, a low-rank fine-tuning model, for microalgae image segmentation. This model supports morphological analysis, cell counting, and physiological state assessment of microalgae. In this paper, we innovatively propose a low-rank fine-tuning method using a large language model for the image segmentation task of microalgae. This module employs the LoRA-FA method to adjust the model’s original training weights through low-rank increments. After fine-tuning, the model performs segmentation tasks with minimal parameter updates, enhancing the efficiency and accuracy of microalgae segmentation. To explore this module’s role further, we added it to the fully connected and convolutional layers of the UCTLFANet model during experiments and compared their performances. The results indicate that integrating this module into the fully connected layer yields the best outcomes. Compared to the UNet, UNet++, Attention-UNet, and UCTransNet models, our method demonstrates superior performance. Additionally, integrating the LoRA-FA module into the UNet, UNet++, and Attention-UNet models shows that UCTLFANet is more advanced and effective. Furthermore, the proposed model shows promising potential for generalization to other datasets or applications, making it a viable tool for broader marine microbial imaging detection.Keywords: Fine-tuning module, Microalgae, Parameter fine-tuning, LoRA-FA, UCTLFANetSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Microalgae are pivotal in research areas like biopharmaceuticals, alternative energy development, water eutrophication treatment, and heavy metal adsorption. In cultivating microalgae, image analysis technology facilitates the acquisition of multi-dimensional data on cell morphology, concentration, and interactions with metabolites1,2. Image segmentation technology plays a crucial role in analyzing microalgae images, with its primary value being the precise extraction of microalgae from the culture system, thereby offering a visual foundation for further research3,4.

In recent years, the development of large language models (LLM) has achieved remarkable progress5. A notable advance in parameter fine-tuning is the low-rank adaptation method, which substantially reduces the number of trainable parameters for downstream tasks6. Among methods in this area, \:LoRA-FA offers distinctive advantages. It constrains weight updates during fine-tuning to a low-rank subspace and eliminates the need to store full-order input activations, thereby improving deployment efficiency7.Both \:LoRA and \:LoRA-FA operate by adding a low-rank auxiliary matrix to replace computations performed by the original weight matrix. By varying the rank of this auxiliary matrix, one can control the model’s computational cost and reduce redundant parameters. In image segmentation, most operations reduce to matrix computations, and the weights of convolutional and fully connected layers are typically initialized randomly. As a result, many parameter matrices are overparameterized. Motivated by this observation, parameter-efficient fine-tuning that leverages low-rank matrix updates is a viable approach for optimizing image segmentation models that rely on matrix operations.

In microalgae image segmentation, models must extract complex image features deeply, suppress interference from visually similar species, and maintain high accuracy8. The \:UCTransNet model has demonstrated effectiveness in segmenting medical images and excels in complex features tasks. It retains the \:UNet architecture for its encoder and decoder while integrates the Transformer module more flexibly. The computations involved are essentially matrix operations9.

This study aims to evaluate the feasibility of employing low-rank fine-tuning techniques to enhance the performance of microalgae image segmentation. Additionally, it investigates the depth of analysis regarding the practical application of this method within segmentation models. The main contributions of this study are as follows:

Related work

Literature review

In recent years, the rapid advancement of deep learning technology has opened new avenues in image segmentation. Neural network-based image semantic segmentation can effectively differentiate and process various object features in complex scenes, enhancing contrast. Additionally, images can be segmented by analyzing the combination and proportion of objects, allowing for targeted processing based on scene characteristics to optimize segmentation10,11.

In the realm of image segmentation, medical cell image segmentation technology has achieved remarkable success. Doan et al.‘s study on nuclear segmentation utilized a self-guided ordinal regression neural network for concurrent nuclear segmentation and classification. This approach leveraged the inherent properties of nuclei and concentrated on areas of high uncertainty during training, resulting in superior performance12. Jie Ji et al. introduced a segmentation method applicable to both the cytoplasm and nucleus of cervical cells. They developed an automatic cervical cell segmentation algorithm using the Cx22 dataset, achieving a Dice similarity coefficient exceeding 95% for both cytoplasm and nucleus segmentation, which highlights its exceptional performance in cervical cell segmentation13. UCTransNet is a model that employs a Transformer module in place of the hopping connections found in UNet networks. It utilizes a Channel-wise Cross fusion Transformer (CCT) to adaptively integrate coding and decoding features. Additionally, it extracts multiscale features from images through the Channel-wise Cross Attention (CCA) mechanism, demonstrating remarkable efficacy in the accurate recognition and segmentation of medical images9.

The rapid advancement in medical cell image segmentation technology offers valuable insights for algae image segmentation. However, current medical image segmentation techniques cannot be directly applied to microalgae due to the distinct biological characteristics and environments of microalgae compared to human cells. The boundaries between microalgae cells and their surroundings are often indistinct, and even within the same species, microalgae tend to form stratified aggregations in their habitats14,15. These traits present significant challenges for accurately segmenting microalgae images. While few researchers have explored deep learning-based image segmentation algorithms for microalgae, some have achieved notable results. For instance, Yu and Guo introduced a non-interactive GrabCut method combined with salient region detection to segment non-setose microalgae, achieving a recognition accuracy of 81.76%16. Yang et al. developed the AlgaeSeg-YOLO method for segmenting microalgae against various backgrounds, achieving a mean average precision (mAP) of 95.61%17. Samarakoon et al. utilized the U-Net network architecture to segment microalgae on coral reefs, achieving an average intersection over union (IoU) of 0.8518. Despite these advancements, most existing technologies rely solely on surface image information and struggle with images containing complex semantic information. They also underperform in differential image processing tailored to the unique characteristics of microalgae and their environments, which is crucial for enhancing contrast. Addressing the low segmentation accuracy caused by the biological and environmental complexities of microalgae remains an urgent challenge19.

Large language models (LLMs) have become integral to modern applications, serving as valuable assistants in both professional and personal contexts5. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods aim to mitigate the high costs associated with training and deploying large foundation models. A notable method is Low-Rank Adaptation , which facilitates effective fine-tuning by updating only low-rank additive matrices, known as adapter weights. \:LoRA has shown that fine-tuning solely these adapter weights can match the performance of full-parameter fine-tuning. However, the challenge of deploying these fine-tuned variants at scale remains largely unaddressed, prompting scholars to propose various LoRA adaptations to tackle this issue6,20. QA-LORA employs a grouping operator to reduce adaptation degrees of freedom while enhancing quantization degrees of freedom, integrating the LLM and auxiliary weights into a quantized model without compromising accuracy21. MT-LoRA utilizes task-agnostic and task-specific low-rank adaptation modules, optimizing the parameter space during multi-task learning (MTL) fine-tuning. This approach not only boosts accuracy on downstream tasks but also cuts the number of trainable parameters by 3.6 times22. S-LoRA manages dynamic adapter weights with varying ranks and KV cache tensors of different sequence lengths using a unified memory pool. It introduces a novel tensor parallelization strategy and custom CUDA kernels for heterogeneous parallelization of LoRA computation, enabling the service of thousands of LoRA adapters on a single GPU or across multiple GPUs, thus increasing throughput by up to four times and significantly expanding the number of served adapters23. LoRA-FA exemplifies application in image segmentation, exploiting LoRA’s computational benefits to optimize segmentation model parameters, deeply analyze microalgae image features, and resist interference from complex factors7.

Low-rank parameter fine-tuning module

The \:LoRA architecture is conceptually similar to residual connections. Its core principle involves appending two parallel parameter matrices, \:A and \:B, alongside a matrix in the model. During the iterative training process, the original parameter matrix remains fixed, while the newly introduced matrices \:A and \:B are the sole parameters updated.

The training procedure is divided into two stages. First, the original parameter matrix \:W is pre-trained, while matrices \:A and \:B are left uncontainable. In the subsequent formal training phase, the parameters of \:W are frozen, and only the composite matrix \:AB is updated.

The fundamental principle employed by \:LoRA-FA (Zhang et al., 2023) in its segmentation model mirrors that of \:LoRA. It incorporates a low-rank matrix of the sidelines to supplant the computation of the original matrix. However, a key distinction lies in the fact that the original parameter matrices \:W and \:A are frozen following initialization, while matrix \:B is the only matrix whose parameters are updated after being initialized to zero. Specifically, prior to the commencement of formal training, \:A is randomly initialized, and \:B is initialized to zero. Consequently, \:W=AB remains at zero, thereby ensuring that the model’s predictions are unaffected and preserving the initial generalization capacity of the pre-trained model. During the training phase, \:A and the pre-trained \:W remain frozen, allowing only the parameters of \:B to be updated. As a result, changes in weights during the model’s formal training are confined to the low-rank space of \:B. Proposed UCTLFANet approach

The model is constructed on the UCTransNet neural network framework, substituting jump connections with converters that adaptively fuse coding and decoding features. Nevertheless, segmenting microalgae images characterized by blurred edge features presents limitations and may result in parameter redundancy. To address this issue, a low-rank fine-tuning method is proposed in conjunction with a converter that adaptively fuses coding and decoding features. This approach aims to capture finer details of algae images while conserving computational resources, ultimately enhancing segmentation accuracy. Figure 2 illustrates the specific structure of the UCTLFANet network, which retains the overall architecture of the Unet model for its coding-decoding process. The original jump connection has been removed, allowing the feature maps Ei encoded at all levels to be input into the Channel Cross-Transformer (CCT). Within the CCT, features at all levels are re-encoded through the Multi-Channel Attention (MCA) module and processed using the self-attention mechanism. The output of the CCT is then directed to the Channel Cross-Attention (CCA) mechanism, which integrates with the decoder’s features to extract multiscale image features. This approach effectively bridges the semantic gap between the encoder and decoder, simultaneously addressing the issue of inconsistent semantic levels in the model’s handling of multiscale features. Furthermore, performance enhancements are achieved through the implementation of a fine-tuning parameter module, LoRA-FA. This module substitutes a low-rank matrix for traditional matrices during computations, thereby reducing model parameters to a low-rank space and performing parameter operations solely on the initialized model to yield the final segmentation result.

Application analysis of LoRA-FA module

In image segmentation, pre-trained models are commonly utilized to ensure segmentation accuracy. Large language models typically follow a “train first, then optimize” approach. For image segmentation models in the initial training phase, LoRA-FA optimizes redundant parameters within their matrices to enhance segmentation accuracy. For models that have developed specific recognition capabilities during pre-training, the application of LoRA-FA to optimize their architecture can further elevate segmentation performance.Structural optimization through \:LoRA-FA further improves the final segmentation effect of models that have already demonstrated recognition capabilities during pre-training.

The fundamental principle underlying the operation of the \:LoRA-FA module is the integration of a low-rank matrix from the bypass system to substitute the computation of the original matrix. This approach can be applied to various structures, including the fully connected layer, convolutional layer, and Transformer within the model.

\:Dropout is a random regularization technique used in fully connected layers to alleviate overfitting. Its fundamental principle involves randomly deactivating a subset of neuron nodes during training, thereby preventing their participation in parameter updates. This approach effectively reduces the model’s complexity. However, the neuron nodes deactivated by \:Dropout are not permanently frozen. We then used the cv2.circle function to draw \:20 circles, each with a radius of \:3, on the canvas based on the \:xy -axis data from the list. Experiment setup

LoRA-FA module insertion

The \:LoRA-FA module can be integrated into all convolutional and fully connected layers. To determine the optimal insertion position of the \:LoRA-FA module, this study designed four comparative groups: \:LF-CFC-UCTransNet (where \:LoRA-FA modules are incorporated into both convolutional and fully connected layers), \:LF-C-UCTransNet (where \:LoRA-FA modules are inserted solely into convolutional layers), and \:LF-FC-UCTransNet (where \:LoRA-FA modules are placed exclusively in fully connected layers). To mitigate the risk of model overfitting, we developed \:LF-ODFC-UCTransNet, which integrates the \:LoRA-FA module into the fully connected layer while removing the dropout module in that layer to assess the impact of the \:LoRA-FA module. To evaluate the model’s inherent segmentation capability, we bypass the results of pre-training and proceed directly to formal training. We utilize \:CMIC and \:HMIC for the training and validation of the proposed model.The \:UCTransNet architecture incorporates a fully connected layer at the end of the Cross-Channel Transformer module. The \:CCT module’s performance is enhanced by leveraging the self-attention mechanism from the Transformer model. Within the \:CCT, four weight matrices \:(Q,K,V,C) are derived from the encoder. Applying \:LoRA to all of these matrices would result in a further loss of the encoding’s deep information. Unlike the conventional Transformer model, the self-attention mechanism operates on the data channels and utilizes the axial attention mechanism for computation. Forcibly integrating the \:LoRA-FA module would lead to a loss of information in the data channels. Therefore, in this study, the \:LoRA-FA is not added to the fully connected layer of the \:CCT module, to preserve the original structure of the model as much as possible.

Comparison of UCTLFANet models

To further explore the advanced nature of this model in the segmentation effect of algae images, comparative experiments were conducted using \:UNet, \:UNET++, \:Attention-UNet, and \:UCTransNet networks to evaluate the selection of microalgae segmentation models. To ensure the accurate results of the proposed microalgae segmentation network, \:PMIC was used to pre-train each model. After obtaining the pre-training weights, formal training was conducted on the \:CMIC and \:PMIC datasets. Through experimental comparison, the effect of \:UCTLFANet on algal image segmentation was verified.

LoRA-FA module verification

Merely superimposing modules on a single model is not sufficient to fully convince people of the advanced nature of \:UCTLFANet in microalgae image segmentation. In this study, the \:LoRA-FA module is also introduced on the UNet, \:UNET++, \:Attention-UNet and network models. Through experimental comparison, the role of the \:LoRA-FA module in the algae image segmentation in this study was verified. The obtained models, \:LF-UNET, \:LF-UNet++ and \:LF-Attention-UNet, all added \:FA-LoRA to the convolutional module. This is because the \:UNet, \:Unet++ and \:Attention-UNet models are all fully convolutional neural networks, in which there are no fully connected layers.The \:UNet, \:UNet++, and \:Attention-UNet models are fully convolutional neural networks that do not incorporate fully connected layers. Notably, fully convolutional layers are superior in preserving the spatial information of images when compared to fully connected layers. Additionally, all three U-shaped networks employ skip connections to facilitate communication between the encoder and decoder. This design prevents the \:FL-C-UCTransNet model from introducing \:LoRA-FA within the convolutional layer, which could lead to a loss of depth information and a decrease in the model’s sensitivity. Consequently, the contrast effect of the aforementioned models is reliable. Each model undergoes pre-training using \:PMIC. Following the acquisition of pre-training weights, formal training is performed on the \:CMIC and \:PMIC datasets. Through the comparison of experimental results, the advanced nature of the \:UCTLFANet model for algae image segmentation is verified.

Conclusions

The experimental results show that our proposed model \:UCTransNet has better segmentation performance compared to other ensemble methods.

Notably, the Dropout strategy employed in this model significantly contributes to the stability of segmentation outcomes. Pre-training results from \:PMIC, alongside the segmentation outcomes from \:CMIC and \:PMIC, indicate that \:UCTLFANet outperforms \:UNet, \:UNet++, \:Attention-UNet, and \:UCTransNet segmentation models. Furthermore, the performances of \:LF-UNet, \:LF-UNet++, and \:LF-Attention-UNet have improved relative to their original counterparts. This finding suggests that the integration of \:LoRA-FA into the convolutional layer of the U-shaped network enhances the model’s segmentation capability for algae images. Additionally, \:UCTLFANet maintains the highest segmentation performance, further substantiating the advanced nature of our proposed model. Collectively, results from various analyses underscore the advantages of the proposed model in the segmentation of microalgae images.

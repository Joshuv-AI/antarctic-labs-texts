Recognizing Early Career Translational Investigators
Authors: Douglas L Mann
Published: 2024

JACC: Basic to Translational Science is committed to supporting and promoting early career investigators who are pursuing translational science. To achieve this, the Journal will provide biographical information and a photograph of the first or senior authors who are early career investigators in the issue where their article appears. Additionally, this biographical information will be shared on our social media platforms at the time of publication.

We are proud to recognize and showcase the work of the following early career investigators, whose research is featured in this issue of JACC: Basic to Translational Science.

Mona E. Ahmed, MD, PhD

Paper: Endothelial Shear Stress Metrics Associate With Proinflammatory Pathways at the Culprit Site of Coronary Erosion

Dr Ahmed graduated from Medical School in 2013 at the Karolinska Institute, Stockholm, Sweden. She transitioned into a combined research-clinical internship at the Karolinska University Hospital, obtaining her physician license in 2015 and earning her PhD in 2019 on the topic “Development of Innovative Probes for Multi-Modality Imaging Applications to Target High-Risk Atherosclerotic Plaques”. Dr Ahmed continued her clinical training as a fellow in Cardiology at Karolinska University Hospital before pursuing a Postdoctoral Research Fellowship at Brigham and Women's Hospital, Harvard Medical School. She specializes in cardiovascular imaging of high-risk biomechanical patterns of blood flow to predict coronary artery disease.

Ali Fatehi Hassanabad, MD, MSc, PhD (completion date May 2025)

Paper: Pericardial Fluid of Patients With Coronary Artery Disease Can Drive Fibrosis Via TGF-Beta Pathway

Dr Fatehi Hassanabad attended the University of Toronto Medical School and is currently a cardiac surgery resident at the Libin Cardiovascular Institute in Calgary, Canada. His area of research includes heart failure and cardio-immunology. His focus is on better understanding the immune profile of human pericardial space and how the profile is affected by various cardiovascular diseases and surgical interventions.

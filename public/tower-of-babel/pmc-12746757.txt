Comment On: Surgical Outcomes of Lens Removal with or without Intraocular Lens Implantation in Marfan Syndrome: A Retrospective Cohort Study [Response to Letter]
Authors: Halah Bin Helayel, Moustafa S Magliyah, Raed Alnutaifi, Abdulrahman H Badawi
Published: 2025

Dear editor

We have read with interest the letter from Luxing Xu et al in response to our recently published paper “Surgical Outcomes of Lens Removal with or Without Intraocular Lens Implantation in Marfan Syndrome: A Retrospective Cohort Study”.1,2

To our knowledge, all studies which have reported the outcomes of lens removal surgeries with different options of intraocular lens (IOL) implantations were retrospective in nature, so the design was compatible with the reported outcomes.1,3–5 In addition, the retrospective nature of this study was acknowledged as a limitation.

The choice of the surgical procedure and the type of intraocular lens were described in detail in methods.1 In our study, the choice of surgical procedure was based on the degree of zonular weakness and capsular stability,1 so further analysis of the outcomes in relation to the degree of subluxation or capsular stability does not yield any difference from the analysis which was performed according to the type of surgery and the IOL implantation technique.

In the cited references, all statistics were used on an eye-level and the statistical tests were similar or the same of the statistical analysis tests which were used in this study. Of note, the only patient-level analysis used was in the comparison of scleral fixated IOL stability between Marfan syndrome and non-Marfan syndrome patients which is beyond the scope of our article.5

Regarding the safety outcomes, the incidences of glaucoma, IOL stability and the surgical procedures were described in detail in our study. While rhegmatogenous retinal detachments (RRD) are known to occur in Marfan syndrome, our experience show that the duration between the lens surgeries and the RRD might be as long as 8.4 years.5 This indicates that the high axial myopia induced by progressive scleral remodeling is a leading contributor to the RRD development in these patients, which is proved by the significant differences in axial lengths between eyes which developed RRD and eyes with flat retina.6

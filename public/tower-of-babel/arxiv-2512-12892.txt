Non-Compact Proofs
Sergei Artemov

Abstract
Non-compact proofs are a class of reasoning that is used in mathematics but overlooked in the analysis of (un)provability of consistency. We focus on proofs of arithmetical statements (*) “for any natural number n , F ⁡ ( n ) n,F(n) .” A proof of (*) is called compact if all proofs of F ⁡ ( n ) F(n) ’s for n = 0 , 1 , 2 , … n=0,1,2,\ldots in it fit into some finitely axiomatized fragment of Peano Arithmetic PA . An example of non-compact reasoning is given by the standard proof of 1952 Mostowski’s reflexivity theorem: PA proves the consistency of its finite fragments. It turns out that Gödel’s second incompleteness theorem prohibits compact proofs and does not rule out non-compact proofs of PA -consistency formalizable in PA . This explains how the proof of PA -consistency in PA from [ 4 , 5 ] works. It essentially formalizes in PA an appropriate explicit version of Mostowski’s proof, which is not in the scope of Gödel’s theorem.



1
Introduction

We consider contentual proofs of arithmetical statements

“for any natural number
n,F(n)
”

(1)

that use only assumptions corresponding to the axioms of
PA
.

Let (

\cal M

This solution is finite, since both the algorithm

\forall x\mbox{\it``${\cal M}(x)$ is a proof of $F(x)$."}

(2)

(3)

Indeed, a hypothetical

Loosely, a proof of (

It is obvious that any

A specific example of non-compact reasoning is given by the standard proof of Mostowski’s reflexivity theorem, MRT, which states that

In Section

2
Explicit reflection and selector proofs

Selector proofs were introduced in [4] (as proofs of schemas) and in [5], cf. also
[
15
]
; they are a sound (and overlooked) addition to the existing rigorous proof systems. Selector proofs have been tacitly used in everyday mathematics but were not explored in mathematical logic. Acknowledging and studying selector proofs sheds new light on some foundational issues in mathematics, such as the (un)provability of consistency.

A
serial property
{\cal F}
in a theory
T
is an effectively specified collection
\{F_{0},F_{1},\ldots,F_{n},\ldots\}
of formulas in the language of
T
. For example,
{\cal F}
can be given by a primitive recursive enumeration of the codes of formulas
F_{i}
, for
i=0,1,2,\ldots
.

A
selector proof
of a serial property
{\cal F}
is a pair of

i)

selector
: a computable operation that given
n
provides a proof of
F_{n}
in
T
. For the foundational purposes, in this work, we assume that such an operation is primitive recursive and explicitly defined.

.

ii)

verifier
: a proof in
T
that the selector does (i).

The paper
[
5
]
provides a body of examples that demonstrate that selector proofs have been tacitly adopted by mathematicians.

We naturally assume the standard Gödel numbering as well as the usual formalization technique and notations within the limits of Gödel and Feferman papers

10

. In particular, we will not distinguish between a finite syntactic object
X

\ulcorner{X}\urcorner
, and the corresponding

A natural formalization in

{\cal F}
in

\langle s,v\rangle

an arithmetical primitive recursive term

ii)

2.1
Explicit reflection and the soundness of selector proofs

The provability of
explicit reflection
:

for any natural number
m
and formula
\varphi
,
{\sf PA}\vdash m\!\!:\!\!\varphi\!\rightarrow\!\varphi

(4)

was first noticed by Gödel in 1938
[
11
]
. Still, his work remained unpublished until 1995, when the provability of explicit reflection had already been independently noted in
[
1
,
2
]
and used in a similar situation in the then-emerging logic of proofs.

2

For further developments, cf.
[

.
Explicit reflection helps to establish the soundness of selector proofs with respect to the traditional single-formula arithmetical provability.

The following properties of selector proofs in
PA
hold (
[
5
]
):

•

Selector proofs are finite syntactic objects.

•

Selector proofs absorb conventional proofs as special cases.

•

The selector proof predicate in
PA

“
\langle s,v\rangle
is a proof of a serial property
{\cal F}
”

is decidable.

•

Selector proofs are as good as conventional proofs for individual formulas. Selector provability of a serial property
\{F_{0},F_{1},\ldots\}
yields the conventional provability of each of
F_{n}
’s. Selector proofs do not add any new theorems to
PA
.

The last item answers a popular question of whether
PA
enhanced by selector proofs is a stronger theory that
PA
. The answers is no, selector proofs do not increase the conventional deductive power of
PA
. Here is the proof of this item.

Proposition 1

(
[
5
]
) If a serial property
{\cal F}=\{F_{0},F_{1},\ldots\}
is selector provable in
PA
, then
{\sf PA}\vdash F_{n}
for each
n=0,1,2,\ldots
.

Proof.
Suppose

{\sf PA}\vdash\forall x[s(x)\!\!:\!\!F^{\bullet}(x)].

Then for each natural number (numeral)
n
,

{\sf PA}\vdash s(n)\!\!:\!\!F^{\bullet}(n),

i.e.,

{\sf PA}\vdash s(n)\!\!:\!\!F_{n}.

Since
s(n)
is a specific standard natural number, by the explicit reflection (
4
),

{\sf PA}\vdash F_{n}.

\Box

3
Compact and non-compact selector proofs

To make the informal intuition of compact and non-compact proofs from Section
1
mathematically rigorous, we need a framework in which individual proofs

{\cal M}(n)
of
F(n)
for each
n
are well-defined formal objects. Conveniently, selector proofs naturally provide such a framework. For our foundational purposes, we define the notions of compactness and non-compactness for formalizable selector proofs in
PA
and leave their extension to broader classes of proofs to further studies.

A selector proof of a serial property
\cal F
with selector
s(x)
is called
compact
, if for some finitely axiomatizable fragment
W
of
PA
,

{\sf PA}\vdash\forall x[s(x)\!:_{W}\!F^{\bullet}(x)].

A selector proof of
\cal F
is
non-compact
, if it is not compact.
For the rest of the paper, “compact proof” and “non-compact proof” denote “compact selector proof” and “non-compact selector proof.”

Consider the well-known proof of the Mostowski reflexivity theorem MRT:

Let
{\sf PA}_{\upharpoonright n}
be the fragment of
PA
with the first
n
axioms. Then for each natural number
n
,
{\sf PA}\vdash\mbox{\sf Con}_{{\sf PA}_{\upharpoonright n}}.

From this proof, we can extract a primitive recursive function (selector)
s(x)
that given
n
builds a derivation of
\mbox{\sf Con}_{{\sf PA}_{\upharpoonright n}}
in
PA
. Note that

\mbox{\sf Con}_{{\sf PA}_{\upharpoonright n}}
implies “
D
is not a proof of
\bot
” for any specific derivation
D
in
{\sf PA}_{\upharpoonright n}
.

Proposition 2

The given proof of the Mostowski reflexivity theorem is a non-compact selector proof.

Proof.
The fact that the proof of MRT is a selector proof is immediate from its format. An inspection of the proof reveals that it uses a well-known construction called
partial truth definitions
which can be traced back to
[
12
]

(cf. also
[
6
,
8
,
13
,
14
,
16
]
). Namely, for each
n=0,1,2,\ldots
we build, in a primitive recursive way, an arithmetical formula

\mbox{\it Tr}_{n}(x,y),

called
truth definition for
\Sigma_{n}
-formulas
, which satisfies natural properties of a truth predicate.
Intuitively, if
\varphi
is a
\Sigma_{n}
-formula and
y
is a sequence encoding values of the parameters in
\varphi
, then

\mbox{\it Tr}_{n}(\varphi,y)
defines the truth value of
\varphi
on
y
.
The following lemma is well-known in proof theory of
PA
.

Lemma 1

For any
\Sigma_{n}
-formula
\varphi
, the following Tarski’s condition holds and is provable in
PA
:

\mbox{\it Tr}_{n}(\varphi,y)\ \leftrightarrow\ \varphi(y).

(5)

In particular,
PA
proves
\neg\mbox{\it Tr}_{n}(\bot)
.

The analysis of the proof of Lemma
1
(cf.
[
13
]
) shows that the sets of induction instances involved grow with
n
, so this proof is not compact.

\Box

For further proof-theoretic analysis of MRT, cf. Section
4.1
.

4
Selector provability vs. single-formula provability

Consider a serial property generated by a single arithmetical formula
F(x)
,

F(0),F(1),\ldots,F(n),\ldots.

We call such property a
schema
and denote

\{F(x)\}.

Obviously, if
{\sf PA}\vdash\forall xF(x)
, then
\{F(x)\}
is selector provable. Indeed, from the proof of
\forall xF(x)
we immediately obtain a selector
s(x)
and its verification in
PA
, i.e. a proof of

\forall x[s(x)\!\!:\!\!F^{\bullet}(x)].

This selector can be chosen to be compact: the amount of induction needed is limited by a finite sub-theory of
PA
sufficient for proving
\forall xF(x)
.

The following easy but fundamental observation made by Elijah Gadsby in
[
9
]
shows that the converse holds as well.

Proposition 3

If a schema
\{F(x)\}
has a compact proof in
PA
then
{\sf PA}\vdash\forall xF(x)
.

Proof.

If
\{F(x)\}
has a compact proof in
PA
, then there is a finitely axiomatized fragment
W
of
PA
such that

{\sf PA}\vdash\forall x[s(x)\!:_{W}\!F^{\bullet}(x)].

Therefore,

{\sf PA}\vdash\forall x\Box_{W}F(x).

(here
\Box_{W}
is the standard provability predicate in
W
).
By the well-known
PA
-provable uniform reflection principle for finitely axiomatized fragments
W
of
PA
,

{\sf PA}\vdash\forall xF(x).

\Box

Corollary 1

Compact proofs of consistency are ruled out by G2.

Indeed, the
PA
-consistency is a contentual universal statement

\mbox{\it``for all natural numbers $n$, $\neg n\!\!:\!\!(0\!=\!1)$"}.

According to definitions, a selector proof of consistency is a selector proof of the schema
\{\neg x\!\!:\!\!\bot\}
which is covered by Proposition
3
. Since, by G2,
PA
does not prove

\forall x\neg x\!\!:\!\!(0=1)
, the schema
\{\neg x\!\!:\!\!\bot\}
and the consistency property, do not have compact selector proofs in
PA
.

Corollary 2

Non-compact proofs of consistency are not ruled out by G2.

There are (non-compact) selector proofs of consistency property, cf.
[
4
,
5
]
.

All the above offer a well-principled answer to the question posed by Lev Beklemishev: what kinds of proofs are ruled out by Gödel’s Second Incompleteness Theorem? G2 prohibits compact proofs but does not rule out non-compact proofs of
PA
-consistency.

Now we answer a question by Mikhail Katz: whether MRT counts as a

The standard proof of MRT establishes that

5
Discussion

There is a robust intuition of compact and non-compact reasoning, Section
1
. To make this intuition precise, we need some rigorous representation of arithmetical reasoning
R(n)
leading, given
n
, to the conclusion
F(n)
in a proof of (
1
). By the formalization principle, such
R(n)
could be represented by a formal
PA
-derivation of
F(n)
. A standard Gödelian formalization leads directly to the selector proofs format

R(n)\!\!:\!\!F(n).

Then we project the idea of compact/non-compact proofs to the existing selector proofs framework, enabling us to speak about compact and non-compact selector proofs. However, the concept of compact/non-compact proofs is applicable in greater generality, e.g., when
R(n)
s are not assumed to be computable.

Due to Proposition
3
, the provability of
\forall xF(x)
is mathematically equivalent to having a compact selector proof of the schema
\{F(x)\}
. This enables us to answer to Beklemishev’s question, “What proofs of consistency are prohibited by G2?”: compact selector proofs.

Interestingly enough, the notion of selector proofs is not present in the question, but is dominant in the exact answer. The reason for this is intrinsic: conventional provability of
\forall xF(x)
is equivalent to existence of a compact selector proof of the schema
\{F(x)\}
.

Selector proofs of
PA
-consistency in
PA

[
4
,
5
]
complement this answer: a step from compact to non-compact proofs yields a formalizable in
PA
proof of
PA
-consistency.

Non-compact proofs constitute a class of mathematical proofs overlooked in foundational studies and not covered by Gödel’s Second Incompleteness theorem.

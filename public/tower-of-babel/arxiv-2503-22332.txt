Theorem 2.3
.

If
P
is a nonzero sdf-absorbing
\mathcal{C}
-hyperideal of
H
, then
rad(P)=P
.

Proof.

Let
t\in rad(P)
. Then there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
t^{n}\subseteq P
. Here we consider two cases. First, we assume that
n
is an even number. So we have
t^{\frac{n}{2}}\circ t^{\frac{n}{2}}\subseteq P
. Take any
x\in t^{\frac{n}{2}}
. Therefore we have
x^{2}\subseteq P
. We choose
0\neq y\in P
as
P
is a nonzero hyperideal of
H
. Hence we have
x^{2}-y^{2}\subseteq P
which implies
x-y\in P
or
x+y\in P
as
P
is an sdf-absorbing hyperideal of
H
. So we get
x\in P
. Since
P
is a
\mathcal{C}
-hyperideal of
H
, we conclude that
t^{\frac{n}{2}}\subseteq P
. By continuing the process, we obtain
t\in P
. Now, we assume that
n
is an odd number. From
t^{n}\subseteq P
, it follows that
t^{n+1}\subseteq P
. By a similar argument, we get

and so

. Since the inclusion
P\subseteq rad(P)
always holds, we conclude that

.

Theorem 2.4

A novel high-gain isolated quasi Z-source DC-DC converter with self-balancing bipolar outputs
Authors: Sobhan Heidarbozorg, Hamid Radmanesh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper introduces a high-gain isolated quasi-Z-source DC-DC converter designed to achieve a substantial voltage boost through the utilization of transformer turns ratio using only a single power switch. The proposed topology reduces voltage stress on its semiconductors, limiting voltage stress to half the output voltage while ensuring continuous input current, a required feature for renewable energy systems. Furthermore, the converter generates bipolar and symmetrical output voltages, offering enhanced compatibility with bipolar DC microgrids. A significant advantage of the design is its self-balancing capability, maintaining stable bipolar voltages even under microgrid imbalance conditions caused by sudden load changes. This paper performs a comprehensive theoretical analysis, complemented by detailed simulations in the MATLAB Simulink to validate the proposed converter’s functionality. Moreover, experimental validation is conducted using a 315 W prototype, which demonstrated an output voltage of , achieving a high voltage gain of 18 and an efficiency of 92.98%. The converter’s unique features are highlighted through an extensive comparative study with other related topologies, underscoring its advantages over existing topologies. These results, supported by rigorous theoretical analysis, simulation results, and experimental evaluations, collectively establish the proposed converter as a high-performance solution for next-generation renewable energy applications. Additionally, dynamic analysis of the proposed converter is conducted, exhibiting a gain margin of 14.4 dB and phase margin of 93.9 deg that highlights its stability.Keywords: Isolated bipolar DC-DC converter, Bipolar DC micro-grids, Single switch, Photovoltaic (PV) systems, High gain, Self-balancing bipolar outputsSubject terms: Electrical and electronic engineering, Renewable energy

Introduction

The issue of global warming is mainly caused by the extensive use of fossil fuels, environmental pollution, and greenhouse gas emissions. This issue underscores the importance of using renewable sources, which are often DC generators. Furthermore, the increase in direct current (DC) loads and advances in power electronics make the concept of DC microgrids suitable for power grid integration due to DC microgrids’ higher efficiency1,2, and3.

The advantages of DC microgrids, such as higher efficiency due to lower losses, absence of reactive power, and reduction of synchronization and harmonics problems, overcome many disadvantages of AC systems4,5. In general, DC microgrids are divided into two structures: unipolar and bipolar, with the double-pole microgrid structure having two symmetrical voltage levels with respect to the ground pole.

The bipolar microgrid has attracted attention due to its numerous advantages over the unipolar structure2. These advantages include higher reliability; in the event of a fault in one of the buses, the other can continue to operate normally. Additionally, the greater application of the bipolar DC system is attributed to the provision of three different voltage levels for the system. This structure offers higher power capacity and efficiency due to lower current under the same conditions compared to the unipolar DC microgrid, and also the voltage stress relative to the ground is lower in this structure. In general, the bipolar DC microgrid system can provide higher reliability, transmission capacity, and efficiency6,7.

However, the major disadvantage of this structure is the voltage imbalance in the microgrid poles, caused by a sudden change in load at each pole of the bipolar microgrid, creating a voltage drop in the pole that supplies the higher load. Plenty of solutions to eliminate or reduce the imbalance in the bipolar microgrid have been presented, which are mainly based on power electronics8. The first solution involves the use of voltage balancing converters, with new structures proposed in9 and10. Balanced voltage converters increase complexity, cost, and losses due to the addition of an extra power converter with complex control methods and numerous elements in their structure11.

However, the method of direct power injection from the source by an interface converter with two symmetrical voltage outputs relative to the ground can be considered more efficient and cost-effective, as it increases the voltage and helps balance the microgrid, leading to a reduction in power electronic converters12. Therefore, DC-DC converters play a vital role as an interface for adjusting the voltage level for various applications13. Renewable sources require a voltage increase to connect to the microgrid14. In recent years, various methods have been used to increase the voltage gain15. In general, DC-DC converters are divided into two types: isolated and non-isolated structures.

Non-isolated structures achieve high gain with various methods such as multiplier cells and coupled inductors. In16, a non-isolated boost converter is presented using a symmetric voltage multiplier with a self-balanced output. Also, a SEPIC-CUK interleaved converter with a single coupled inductor is presented in17. In18, a bipolar converter with a quasi-Z-source structure is proposed. The converters16–18 utilize only a single power switch and provide low voltage gain. In19, a high-gain bipolar structure with a single switch is presented; however, it suffers from a high impulsive input current.

Also, a multi-switch bipolar voltage boost converter with coupled inductors has been proposed in20, which achieves high gain by increasing the turns ratio, but the output voltage is asymmetric. In21, the proposed converter achieves high voltage gain using the inductance-coupling technique, but its input current is discontinuous.

In non-isolated converters, there are challenges of high stress and losses at high voltage and current and leakage current propagation due to parasitic capacitors in grid-connected PV systems22,23. On the other hand, isolated topologies achieve unlimited voltage gain by properly designing the turn ratio of the transformer windings24. They also provide higher safety due to galvanic isolation and do not transmit EMI leakage currents to the secondary side25. In reference26, a Cuk isolated converter with a bipolar output is presented, which has a low voltage gain. In27, a bipolar flyback converter that suffers from discontinuous input current is proposed. Two isolated multi-input converters are presented in28, one of which is a dual active bridge and the other is a full bridge, both of which also have discontinuous input current.

An isolated quasi-Z-source converter is proposed, which operates with only one power switch. This converter features remarkable characteristics, such as reduced voltage stress on semiconductors, which is independent of the duty cycle and can withstand up to half the output voltage. One of the key advantages of this converter is its utilization of a continuous input current, making it an ideal choice for connection to renewable energy sources. Additionally, the proposed converter provides a bipolar output and, with its self-balancing capability, helps maintain voltage balance in bipolar DC microgrids under voltage imbalance conditions. The symmetrical output of this converter also makes it highly suitable for integration with inverters.

Furthermore, due to the isolation between the input and output, the proposed converter effectively eliminates EMI leakage currents. Overall, this converter offers an efficient solution for connecting renewable energy sources to bipolar DC microgrids.

The non-isolated single-switch quasi-Z source topology in18 has low voltage gain. Reference20 uses two power switches and a non-isolated structure to achieve high voltage gain by using the turns ratio in the coupling inductor. However, the output voltage becomes asymmetric at turn ratios greater than one. Reference19 achieves high and symmetrical voltage gain using the inductive coupling technique but has high impulsive input current. In21, a non-isolated bipolar converter achieved a very high voltage gain using inductive coupling methods; however, it suffers from discontinuous input current. Conclusion

This study presents a high-gain isolated quasi-Z-source DC-DC converter designed for renewable energy applications. The proposed topology offers several advantages, including high voltage gain using only one switch, reduced voltage stress on semiconductors, continuous input current, isolated structure, and self-balanced bipolar outputs. These features ensure robust performance even under load imbalance conditions while maintaining balanced output voltage. MATLAB simulations and theoretical analysis confirm the converter’s functionality. Additionally, experimental results obtained from a 315 W prototype demonstrate a high voltage gain of 18, an output voltage of , and an overall efficiency of 92.98%, highlighting its feasibility. The converter is introduced as a high-performance solution and an interface for renewable energy systems and bipolar DC microgrids. Future research may focus on scaling the design for higher power applications and exploring advanced control strategies to further optimize its dynamic performance.

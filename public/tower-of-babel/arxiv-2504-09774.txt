Links between the integrable systems of CMC surfaces, isothermic surfaces and constrained Willmore surfaces.
K. Leschke

Abstract
. Since constant mean curvature surfaces in 3–space are special cases of isothermic and constrained Willmore surfaces, they give rise to three, apriori distinct, integrable systems. We provide a comprehensive and unified view of these integrable systems in terms of the associated families of flat connections and their parallel sections: in case of a CMC surface, parallel sections of all three associated families of flat connections are given algebraically by parallel sections of either one of the families. As a consequence, we provide a complete description of the links between the simple factor dressing given by the conformal Gauss map, the simple factor dressing given by isothermicity, the simple factor dressing given by the harmonic Gauss map, as well as the relationship to the classical, the μ \mu – and the ϱ \varrho –Darboux transforms of a CMC surface. Moreover, we establish the associated family of the CMC surfaces as limits of the associated family of isothermic surfaces and constrained Willmore surfaces.



1.
Introduction

Surfaces of non–zero constant mean curvature in Euclidean
3
–space,
so–called
CMC surfaces
, are critical with respect to the
variation of area with prescribed fixed volume. CMC surfaces arise
widely in nature but also in man–made structures. For example, soap
bubbles, equilibrium capillary surfaces (in the absence of gravity),
Fermi surfaces in condensed matter physics (and the related constant
potential surfaces in crystals), block copolymers, as well as freeform
structures or pneumatic architectures such as inflatable domes and
enclosures, can be modeled on CMC surfaces. The geometry of CMC
surfaces has profound consequences for the physical properties of the
materials in which they occur, and thus have a large relevance in
applications in shape optimisation. Due to these applications, finding
computational models of CMC surfaces and understanding their
properties is a very active research area in surface theory.

The study of CMC surfaces has been approached from a variety of
directions. For example the Euler–Lagrange equation of a CMC surface
is an important example of a geometric non–linear elliptic partial
differential equation and variational principles and analytic gluing
techniques can been used to construct and investigate shapes of CMC
surfaces, e.g.,
[
59
,
40
,
47
,
31
]
. On the other hand, the
theory of integrable systems via the Gauss–Codazzi equation of a CMC
surface, the sinh–Gordon equation, has led to a classification of
constant mean curvature tori by their spectral data,
[
50
,
37
,
4
,
28
,
38
]
.

Both of these approaches have laid the foundation for finding discrete
approximations of CMC surfaces: variational and integrable system
methods have been successfully used to optimise the energy functional
on a triangulated surface or to find geometric discrete CMC nets by
using the structure of special coordinates and transformations, see
e.g.
[
9
,
52
,
27
,
3
,
17
]
.

Here we follow the integrable system approach for various surface
classes: for the purposes of this paper, the core indicator of
integrability is that the underlying Gauss–Codazzi equations of
integrable surface classes give a Lax pair and allow to introduce a
spectral parameter to reformulate the corresponding Lax equation as
the flatness of the associated family of connections. This way, the
non–linear compatibility equations become a system of linear
differential equations. In the case of a CMC surface, we have three
apriori distinct integrable structures associated to it: CMC surfaces
are special cases of both isothermic surfaces and constrained Willmore
surfaces.

Recall that isothermic surfaces are surfaces which allow (away from
umbilic points) a conformal curvature line parametrisation, the
so–called
isothermal
coordinates. The Calapso equation, the
fourth order integrability condition for isothermic surfaces

[
19
]
, is linked to the zoomeron equation in physics

[
25
]
, and solitonic solutions have been studied in physics
in the context of membrane deformations.

On the other hand, the elastic bending energy of an immersion, the
so–called
Willmore functional
, is an important invariant in
surface theory. Its applications range from the biophysics of
membranes to string theory.
Constrained Willmore surfaces
are
the critical points of the Willmore functional when restricting to
conformal immersions of a fixed Riemann surface. In case of tori,
isothermic surfaces which are constrained Willmore are exactly the CMC
surfaces in space forms
[
53
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to provide a unified view on the
integrable systems of CMC surfaces, isothermic surfaces and
constrained Willmore surfaces. To this end, we first recall the
Ruh–Vilms theorem
[
54
]
: given a conformal immersion

f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
from a Riemann surface
M
into 3–space with
non–vanishing constant mean curvature the Gauss map of
f
is a
harmonic map
N:M\to S^{2}
into the 2–sphere. Theoretical
physicists observed that a harmonic map equation is an integrable
system,
[
51
]
,
[
48
]
,

[
55
]
: by introducing the spectral parameter

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{*}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, one can associate a
\mathbb{C}_{*}
–family
of flat connections
d^{N}_{\lambda}
with simple poles at
0,\infty

CMC surface

associated family of flat connections

d_{\lambda}^{N},\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{*},
on the trivial
\mathbb{C}^{2}
–bundle uniquely
determines the CMC surface. This fact can be used to obtain new CMC
surfaces in various ways, and we will now recall those transformations
which are relevant to this paper. We will assume in the following
without loss of generality that the mean curvature is
H=1
.

For spectral parameter
\lambda
on the unit circle, the connections

d_{\lambda}^{N}
are quaternionic on
\mathbb{C}^{2}=\mathbb{C}\oplus j\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{H}
and give

the

family of CMC surfaces
on the universal cover: gauging the family
of flat connections

d_{\lambda}^{N^{\alpha}}=\alpha^{-1}\cdot d_{\lambda\mu}^{N}
by a

d_{\mu}^{N}
–parallel section
\alpha

\mu\in S^{1}
, gives locally a
new associated family of flat connections with harmonic Gauss map

N^{\alpha}=\alpha^{-1}N\alpha
and thus CMC surfaces
f^{\alpha}
, see

[
14
]
for details on this in our
setting. Another way to obtain new harmonic maps, and thus CMC
surfaces, was introduced by Uhlenbeck and Terng,
[
58
]
,

[
57
]
:
dressing
in the case of a harmonic map

N:M\to S^{2}
is given by a gauge

\hat{d}_{\lambda}^{N}=r_{\lambda}\cdot d_{\lambda}^{N}
of

by a

\lambda
–dependent dressing matrix

, see

[
14
]
. The
dressing
of

is then
the harmonic map
\hat{N}
that has
\hat{d}_{\lambda}
as its associated
family of flat connections. In general, it is difficult to find
explicit dressing matrices preserving the shape of the associated
family of flat connections. However, if
r_{\lambda}
has a simple pole
at the
(CMC) spectral parameter

\mu\in\mathbb{C}_{*}
and is given by a

d_{\mu}^{N}
–parallel bundle (possibly on the universal cover
\tilde{M}

of the Riemann surface

), then a CMC surface
\check{f}
, the
so–called
simple factor dressing
of

, can be computed
explicitly, e.g.,
[
57
]
,
[
26
]
,

[
14
]
.

Finally, a
d_{\mu}^{N}
–parallel bundle can also be used to discuss the
Darboux transformation on CMC surfaces. Recall first that in the
general case of a conformal immersions the classical Darboux
transformation
[
24
]
can be generalised

[
7
]
: generalised Darboux transforms are given by
prolongations of holomorphic sections of an associated quaternionic
holomorphic line bundle of the conformal immersion. In the case of a
CMC surface, a
d_{\mu}^{N}
–parallel section
\alpha
gives such a
holomorphic section, and its prolongation, a so–called

\mu
–Darboux transform
, is CMC in 3–space for

\mu\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
, up to translation in 4–space,

[
20
]
. The classical Darboux transforms of a CMC surface which
are CMC too are a special case of the
\mu
–Darboux transformation
and are exactly given by real or unit length spectral parameters

\mu\in S^{1}\cup\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,1\}
. In general,
\mu
–Darboux
transforms are defined on the universal cover and closing condition
can be expressed via the monodromy matrix of the connection
d_{\mu}^{N}
.

The relevance of the generalised Darboux transform arises from its
link to the spectral data of conformal torus. Recall that in the case
of a harmonic torus the monodromy representation of the family

d_{\lambda}^{N}
with respect to a chosen base point on the torus is
abelian and hence has simultaneous eigenlines,

[
37
]
. From the corresponding eigenvalues one can
define the
spectral curve

\Sigma
, a hyperelliptic curve over

\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
together with the holomorphic eigenline bundle over
\Sigma
.
Geometrically, in case of a CMC torus, the eigenvalues correspond to
so–called multipliers of
d_{\mu}^{N}
–parallel sections and the
spectral curve is essentially the set of all closed
\mu
–Darboux
transforms,
[
20
]
. Conversely, the spectral data can be used to
construct all harmonic tori in terms of theta functions on the
spectral curve
\Sigma
. This approach has been used to classify CMC
tori by spectral data,
[
50
,
4
]
.

The classical Darboux transform was defined for
isothermic
surfaces
, that is, surfaces which allow a conformal curvature line
parametrisation: a Darboux pair
(f,\hat{f})
is a pair of isothermic
surfaces conformally enveloping a sphere congruence. Darboux
transforms are obtained as solutions to a Riccati equation

[
33
]
with real parameter

r\in\mathbb{R}_{*}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, the
(isothermic) spectral
parameter
. This indicates that the class of isothermic surfaces
constitutes an integrable system, as shown in

[
23
]
. In particular, again a family of
flat connections
d_{r}
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
, can be introduced,

[
30
,
39
,
12
]
, which
completely determines the isothermic surface. Note that in contrast
to the associated family of a harmonic Gauss map the family of
connections of an isothermic surface in our setup is defined on a
trivial
\mathbb{H}^{2}
–bundle with real spectral parameter
r
. In this
scenario, one can view classical Darboux transforms as the parallel
sections of the connection
d_{r}
in the associated family of flat
connections
[
36
,
35
]
. The
resulting classical Darboux transforms of a CMC surface are only CMC
surfaces if an additional initial condition is satisfied.

Since CMC surfaces are isothermic we investigate the link between the
Darboux transforms given by parallel sections of
d_{\mu}^{N}
and those
given by parallel sections of
d_{r}
. To this end we expand in Section

3
the family
d_{r},r\in\mathbb{R}
, to a family
d_{\varrho}

with complex parameter
\varrho\in\mathbb{C}
on the trivial
\mathbb{C}^{4}
–bundle,
see also
[
5
]
. We call the isothermic surface arising
from a
d_{\varrho}
–parallel section,
\varrho\in\mathbb{C}_{*}
, a

\varrho
–Darboux transform
. A
\varrho
–Darboux transform is
a generalised Darboux transform, and a classical Darboux transform if
and only if
\varrho\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
. As an example we show that all closed

\varrho
–Darboux transforms, including all closed classical Darboux
transforms, of a surface of revolution are rotation surfaces in
4–space for
\varrho\not\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
or isothermic
bubbletons, which occur for special spectral parameter, the so–called

resonance points
.

Since
\mu
–Darboux transforms are CMC but only classical Darboux
transforms for
\mu\in\mathbb{R}\cup S^{1}\setminus\{0,1\}
and, on the other
hand,
\varrho
–Darboux transforms of isothermic surfaces are CMC for
special initial conditions, we expect to see a link between the CMC
spectral parameter
\mu
and the isothermic spectral parameter

\varrho
. Indeed, we show in Section
4
that each

\mu
–Darboux transform is a
\varrho
–Darboux transform with
isothermic spectral parameter

\varrho=-\frac{(\mu-1)^{2}}{4\mu}\in\mathbb{C}_{*}
. Moreover, this indicates a
2:1 relationship between the corresponding spaces of parallel sections
because the family
d_{\lambda}^{N}
gives flat connections on the trivial

\mathbb{C}^{2}
–bundle, whereas
d_{\lambda}
are defined on the trivial

\mathbb{C}^{4}
–bundle. More precisely, we show that every

d_{\varrho}
–parallel section is obtained algebraically from

d_{\mu_{\pm}}^{N}
–parallel sections
\alpha_{\pm}
(and the Gauss map
N

of the CMC surface), where

\mu_{\pm}=1-2\varrho\pm 2i\sqrt{\varrho(1-\varrho)}
. In
particular, the
\varrho
–Darboux transforms which are CMC are
exactly the
\mu_{+}
– and
\mu_{-}
–Darboux transforms. Conversely, all

d_{\mu}^{N}
–parallel sections are algebraically determined from

d_{\varrho}
–parallel sections where
\varrho
is the isothermic
spectral parameter given by
\mu
.

Additionally, the explicit form of the parallel sections of the
associated family
d_{\lambda}
in terms of sections of
d_{\lambda}^{N}

allows in Section
4
to give a geometric interpretation of
the Sym–Bobenko formula for CMC surfaces as a limit of surfaces in the
isothermic associated family. Here, the associated family in the
isothermic integrable system is analogously to the CMC case given,

[
36
]
,
[
11
]
, by the gauge

d^{\Phi}_{\lambda}=\Phi^{-1}\cdot d_{\lambda+\varrho}
, where
\Phi
is
a
d_{\varrho}
–parallel endomorphism,
\varrho\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
.

To investigate the simple factor dressing of a CMC surface in terms of
the integrable system of the isothermic surface, we first define in
Section
3
a simple factor dressing by the

\lambda
–dependent gauge matrix
r_{\varrho}^{E}(\lambda)
for complex
spectral parameter
\varrho\in\mathbb{C}_{*}
in terms of a

d_{\varrho}
–parallel complex line subbundle

E\subset\tilde{M}\times\mathbb{C}^{4}=\underline{\tilde{\mathbb{C}}}^{4}
. If

\varrho\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
our gauge matrix
r_{\varrho}^{E}(\lambda)
with simple
pole at
\lambda=\bar{\varrho}
coincides with the dressing matrix given
in
[
21
]
. Moreover, the family of flat connections

\hat{d}_{\lambda}=r_{\varrho}^{E}(\lambda)\cdot d_{\lambda}
is the
associated family of the
\varrho
–Darboux transform given by the
twistor projection of

, that is, the quaternionic line bundle

E\mathbb{H}
. In particular, simple factor dressing and
\varrho
–Darboux
transformation coincide in this case.

Furthermore, given two
d_{\varrho}
–stable complex line bundles

E_{1},E_{2}
so that
W\oplus Wj=\tilde{M}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
for

W=E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
, we define a dressing matrix

with

\lambda=\bar{\varrho}
and a

. Note that for
\varrho\not\in\mathbb{R}
the 2–step simple
factor dressing gives surfaces which are not the original isothermic
surface

. Indeed, the 2–step factor dressing is the common
Darboux transform of the
\varrho
–Darboux transforms given by
E_{1}

and
E_{2}
via Bianchi permutability: in particular, the 2–step
Darboux transform is non–trivial for
\varrho\not\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
.

To compare to the simple factor dressing of a CMC surface, we recall

[
14
]
that a
\mu
–Darboux transform of a CMC
surface
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the simple factor dressing of its parallel
surface
g=f+N
with parameter
\mu
. Here we show that this aligns
with the 2–step Darboux transformation given via Bianchi
permutability by a
\mu
–Darboux transform of
f
and the parallel
CMC surface which is a
\mu
–Darboux transform for
\mu=-1
. In
particular, the simple factor dressing via the CMC integrable system
is a 2–step simple factor dressing via the isothermic integrable
system. This duality in the dressing operation appears as well when
considering the third integrable system of a CMC surface.

A CMC surface is
constrained Willmore
, that is, it is a
critical point of the Willmore energy under variations which preserve
the conformal structure. As before, one can associate a family of
flat connections
d_{\lambda}^{S}
on the trivial
\mathbb{C}^{4}
–bundle given by
the conformal Gauss map
S
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{*}
, see

[
8
,
5
]
. In the case of a CMC surface,
we show in Section
5
that the family can be expressed in
terms of the associated family
d^{-N}_{\lambda}
of the parallel
surface
g=f+N
and that hence parallel sections of
d^{S}_{\lambda}
are
given exactly by prolongations of parallel sections of

d^{-N}_{\lambda}
, up to constant sections. In particular, the

\mu
–Darboux transforms of a CMC surface are exactly the Darboux
transforms given by parallel sections of
d_{\lambda}^{S}
which are CMC.

Moreover, for suitable complex rank 2–bundles, the simple factor
dressing given by the constrained Willmore property in

[
18
]
gives surfaces with harmonic right normal.
In particular, amongst these simple factor dressings those with
constant real part are indeed the simple factor dressings given by the
CMC integrable system. This sheds a little light on the duality
appearing in the simple factor dressing: the associated family

d_{\lambda}^{S}
of flat connections in case of a CMC surface is defined
in terms of data of the dual surface. Finally, we show that a surface
in the CMC associated family is obtained as a limit of the associated
family of constrained Willmore surfaces as defined in

[
18
]
up to Möbius transformation.

Since parallel sections of all three associated families of flat
connections can be algebraically recovered from parallel sections of
either one of the families, we thus have demonstrated a unified view
of on the three distinct integrable systems associated to a CMC
surface and their transformations.

2.1.
Conformal immersions

We model
\mathbb{R}^{4}
by the quaternions
\mathbb{H}=\Span_{\mathbb{R}}\{1,i,j,k\}
where

i^{2}=j^{2}=k^{2}=-1
and
ij=k
. The imaginary quaternions

{\rm Im}\,\mathbb{H}=\Span_{\mathbb{R}}\{i,j,k\}
can be identified with
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and for
imaginary quaternions
a,b\in{\rm Im}\,\mathbb{H}
we have

ab=-<a,b>+a\times b

where we consider
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
<,>
denotes the standard
inner product and
\times
the cross product in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In particular,
the 2–sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is given by

S^{2}=\{n\in{\rm Im}\,\mathbb{H}\mid n^{2}=-1\}
.

An immersion
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}=\mathbb{H}
is conformal if and only if there
exists a pair
(N,R):M\to S^{2}\times S^{2}
with

*df=Ndf=-dfR

where
*\omega(X)=\omega(JX)
for
X\in TM
and
\omega
a
1–form. Here
J
is the complex structure on the tangent space of the
Riemann surface
M
. Identifying the Grassmannian of 2–planes in

\mathbb{R}^{4}
with
\Gr_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})=S^{2}\times S^{2}
the pair
(N,R)
is the
Gauss map of the immersion
f
. In particular, the tangent and normal
space of
f
are uniquely determined by
(N,R)
as

(2.1)

df_{p}(T_{p}M)=\{v\in\mathbb{H}\mid Nv+vR=0\},\quad\perp_{f}=\{v\in\mathbb{H}\mid Nv-vR=0\}\,.

We call
N
and
R
the
left
and
right normal
of

f
. Note that
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
if and only if
N=R
.

The
mean curvature vector

\mathcal{H}
of
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
satisfies

\overline{\mathcal{H}}df=\frac{1}{2}(*dR+RdR),\quad df\overline{\mathcal{H}}=-\frac{1}{2}(*dN+NdN)

Denoting
H=-\overline{\mathcal{H}}N=-R\overline{\mathcal{H}}
, the above
equations are equivalent to

(2.2)

-Hdf=(dR)^{\prime},\quad-dfH=(dN)^{\prime}

where
(dR)^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(dR-R*dR),(dN)^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(dN-N*dN)
denote
the
(1,0)
parts of the 1–forms
dR
and
dN
with respect to the
complex structures given by left multiplication by
R
and
N

respectively. If
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
then
H:M\to\mathbb{R}
is the mean curvature
of
f
.

The Willmore energy of a conformal immersion
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
is given
by

W(f)=\int_{M}(|H|^{2}-K-K^{\perp})|df|^{2}

where
K
is the Gauss curvature of
f
and
K^{\perp}
is the normal
curvature of
f
. If
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
then

and we obtain the
classical Willmore energy of a conformal immersion
f
. In terms of
the Gauss map of
f
, the integrand of the Willmore energy is given as

(|H|^{2}-K-K^{\perp})|df|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}|*dR-RdR|^{2}\,.

Since the classes of isothermic surfaces and of constrained Willmore
surfaces are Möbius invariant, we can also consider surfaces into

S^{4}=\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The orientation perserving Möbius transformations are
given by
\Gl(2,\mathbb{H})
:
v\mathbb{H}\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}^{1}\mapsto(Av)\mathbb{H},A\in\Gl(2,\mathbb{H})
.
In the following we identify a map
f:M\to\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}^{1}
into quaternionic
projective space
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}^{1}
with the line subbundle of the trivial
\mathbb{H}^{2}

bundle
\underline{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=M\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
over

which is given by

f(p)=L_{p}\,.

The derivative of
f
is given by

\delta=\pi_{L}d
where
\pi_{L}:\underline{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\to\underline{\mathbb{H}}^{2}/L
denotes
the canonical projection.

If
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
then we will denote by
L=\psi\mathbb{H}
the associated line
bundle of
f
and by
\infty=e\mathbb{H}
the point at infinity where

\psi=\begin{pmatrix}f\\
1\end{pmatrix},\quad e=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}

so that
\underline{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=L\oplus\infty
. Moreover, the left normal

N:M\to S^{2}
induces a complex structure on the line bundle
L
by
setting
J\psi=-\psi N
and
f
is conformal if and only if

*\delta\psi=\delta J\psi

since
\delta\psi=\begin{pmatrix}df\\
0\end{pmatrix}
when identifying

via the

\pi_{L}|_{e\mathbb{H}}:e\mathbb{H}\to\underline{\mathbb{H}}^{2}/L

The conformal Gauss map
S
of a conformal immersion
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
is

[
15
]
the unique complex structure
S\in\End_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
such
that

SL=L,S|_{L}=J,dSL\in\Omega^{1}(L),A\underline{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\subset L

where

is the
Hopf field
of

, that is,

Then

is a Willmore surface, that is, a critical point of the
Willmore energy, if and only if

is harmonic, that is,
d*A=0
,
see
[
10
,
15
]
. The conformal immersion

is called

if there exists

with

and

, see
[
8
]
: a

energy under variations that preserve the conformal structure.

Definition 2.1

such
that

(2.3)

df\wedge df^{d}=df^{d}\wedge df=0\,.

In this case,
f^{d}
is called a
dual surface
(or

Christoffel transform
) of
f
.

Note that (
2.3
) implies by type considerations that

*df^{d}=-Rdf^{d}=df^{d}N
where
(N,R)
is the Gauss map of

f
. Moreover, since (
2.3
) is symmetric in
f
and
f^{d}
, we
see that if
f
is isothermic so is its dual surface
f^{d}
. The dual
surface is unique up to homothety and translation, except in the case
when the surface is part of a round sphere, see
e.g.
[
33
]
. We will exclude these case in the
following discussions.

Let
z=x+iy
be a conformal curvature line parametrisation of an
isothermic surface
f
, that is,
z
is a conformal coordinate

f_{y}=Nf_{x}=-f_{x}R

with
f_{xy}
tangential, that is

Nf_{xy}+f_{xy}R=0\,.

Then

(2.4)

df^{d}=f_{x}^{-1}dx-f_{y}^{-1}dy

satisfies

df^{d}\wedge df=(f_{x}^{-1}f_{y}+f_{y}^{-1}f_{x})dx\wedge dy=(f_{x}^{-1}f_{y}+f_{y}^{-1}f_{x})dx\wedge dy=0\,.

Here we used that
f_{x}=f_{y}R
and thus
f_{x}^{-1}=-Rf_{y}^{-1}
. Similarly, one can show
df\wedge df^{d}=0
and, using
the condition that
f_{xy}
is tangential that
df^{d}
is a closed
1–form. Therefore, we see that there exists locally a dual surface

f^{d}
of
f
: surfaces which admit a (local) conformal curvature line
parametrisation are isothermic.

Example 2.2
.

We will give some examples of isothermic surfaces which will be
relevant in the following.

(1)

If
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a conformal surface of revolution then we can
parametrise

f(x,y)=ip(x)+jq(x)e^{-iy}

where
(p,q)
is a unit speed curve in the hyperbolic plane, that
is,
(p^{\prime})^{2}+(q^{\prime})^{2}=q^{2}
. It is a straight–forward computation, see
e.g.
[
35
]
, to show that the 1–form

\omega=-\frac{ip^{\prime}(x)+jq^{\prime}(x)e^{-iy}}{q^{2}(x)}dx-j\frac{i}{q(x)}e^{-iy}dy

is closed and thus there exists locally a surface of revolution
f^{d}

given by
df^{d}=\omega
. In particular,
f
and
f^{d}
are isothermic
since
df^{d}\wedge df=df\wedge df^{d}=0
.

f^{d}(x,y)=\frac{ix}{1+x^{2}}+j\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}e^{-iy}
.

(2)

Another example of isothermic surfaces are CMC surfaces. If

f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
has non–vanishing constant mean curvature
H
, we
will always assume without loss of generality that
H=1
. Then
(
2.2
) shows that the parallel CMC surface
g=f+N
is a
dual surface of
f
(if
f
is not the round sphere): we have

dg=df+dN=\frac{1}{2}(dN+N*dN)
so that
*dg=-Ndg=dgN
.
Again, this shows that
dg\wedge df=df\wedge dg=0
and both
f

and its parallel surface
g
are isothermic. Note that in general in
isothermic coordinates

dg=\varrho_{0}(f_{x}^{-1}dx-f_{y}^{-1}dy)
with

\varrho_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{*}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
g
is only up to scale (and
translation) the isothermic surface defined by (
2.4
).

In the following we will use the parallel CMC surface

g=f+N
as dual surface, and hence implicitly exclude the round
sphere by assuming that
g
is a branched conformal immersion.

2.3.
The classical Darboux transformation

An alternative way to characterise isothermic surfaces is by the
classical Darboux transformation given by its geometric
properties. For us however it is convenient to describe Darboux
transforms by solutions to a Riccati equation in terms of the dual
surface:

Definition 2.3

(
[
24
,
33
]
)
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
be isothermic with dual surface
f^{d}:\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
. If
T:\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\}
satisfies the Riccati equation

(2.5)

dT=-df+Tdf^{d}rT

for some
r\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
then
\hat{f}=f+T
is called a

classical Darboux transform
of
f
.

Remark 2.4
.

If
T
is not vanishing, but
T(p)=0
for isolated
p\in M
, we call

\hat{f}=f+T
a
singular Darboux transform
, see

[
7
]
. In this case,
\hat{f}
is branched at the
zeros of
T
since
d\hat{f}=Tdf^{d}rT
.

Note that a classical Darboux transform
\hat{f}
is conformal with
left normal
\hat{N}=-TRT^{-1}
and right normal

\hat{R}=-TNT^{-1}
since
d\hat{f}=Tdf^{d}rT
and

*df^{d}=-Rdf^{d}=df^{d}N
.

Since
dT^{-1}=-T^{-1}dTT^{-1}
we see that if
T
satisfies
(
2.5
) then
T^{d}=\frac{1}{r}T^{-1}
satisfies the Riccati
equation
dT^{d}=-df^{d}+T^{d}dfrT^{d}
:

Theorem 2.5

(
[
24
,
33
]
)
.

If
f^{d}
is a fixed dual surface of the isothermic
f
, and

\hat{f}=f+T
is a classical Darboux transform of
f
with parameter

r\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
then
\hat{f}^{d}=f^{d}+\frac{1}{r}T^{-1}
is a classical
Darboux transform of
f^{d}
with parameter
r
. In particular, a
classical Darboux transform
\hat{f}
of an isothermic surface is
isothermic with dual surface
\hat{f}^{d}
.

Proof.

Since
d\hat{f}^{d}=\frac{1}{r}T^{-1}dfT^{-1}
we see that

\hat{f}^{d}
has left normal
\hat{N}^{d}=-\hat{R}
and right normal

\hat{R}^{d}=-\hat{N}
so that

d\hat{f}^{d}\wedge d\hat{f}=d\hat{f}\wedge d\hat{f}^{d}=0
by type
arguments.
∎

Geometrically, a classical Darboux pair
(f,\hat{f})
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is
determined by a sphere congruence which envelopes both surfaces,

[
24
,
35
]
: the radius and centres of the spheres are
given by
r(T)=\frac{|T|^{2}}{2<T,N>}
and

respectively
where

. In the case when the Darboux transform
\hat{f}

is singular, the enveloping sphere congruence degenerates to a point
at

with
T(p)=0
.

2.4.
The associated family of flat connections

It is known that the classical Darboux transformation is given by parallel sections of an
associated
\mathbb{R}
–family of flat connections, see
e.g.
[
35
,
12
,
13
]
: this indicates that isothermic surfaces form an integrable system as shown in
[
23
]
.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
the family

d_{t}=d+\omega_{t},\quad\omega_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}0&df\\
df^{d}t&0\end{pmatrix},

of connections on
M\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
is flat: since

df\wedge df^{d}=df^{d}\wedge df=0
we see that
d\omega_{t}=0
and

\omega_{t}\wedge\omega_{t}=0
. If for fixed
r\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
the section

\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(\underline{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}^{2})
is a non–trivial
d_{r}
–parallel
section of the trivial
\mathbb{H}^{2}
bundle

\underline{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}^{2}=\tilde{M}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
over the universal cover
\tilde{M}

of
M
, that is,

d\alpha=-df\beta,d\beta=-df^{d}\alpha r,

then
T=\alpha\beta^{-1}:\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a solution to the
Riccati equation (
2.5
) with parameter
r
and

\hat{f}=f+T
is a (possibly singular) classical Darboux transform
of
f
. Note that a Darboux transform is closed if and only if

\varphi
is a
section with multiplier
,
[
7
]
,
that is,
\gamma^{*}\varphi=\varphi h_{\gamma}
for
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(M)
,
which is equivalent to
\gamma^{*}\alpha=\alpha h_{\gamma}
since

d\alpha=-df\beta
and
\varphi=e\alpha+\psi\beta
.

Conversely, given a solution
T
to the Riccati equation with
parameter
r
the connection
d^{T}=d+T^{-1}d\hat{f}
,

\hat{f}=f+T
, is flat. Then any
d^{T}
–parallel section
\beta
on

\tilde{M}
gives a
d_{r}
–parallel section by putting

\alpha=T\beta
and
\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(\underline{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}^{2})
:
since
T^{-1}d\hat{f}\beta=df^{d}r\alpha
we have
d\beta=-df^{d}r\alpha
and

d\alpha=dT\beta+Td\beta=-df\beta\,.

If the isothermic surface
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is in 3–space then the
classical Darboux transform is in 3–space with the correct choice of
initial data for the Riccati equation:

Theorem 2.6

(see e.g.
[
35
]
)
.

Let

be isothermic and
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a
3–parameter family of classical Darboux transforms with parameter
r

so that
\hat{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Conversely, every family of flat connections of the correct form
determines an isothermic surface. To see this, we will consider a
second family of flat connections, given by the gauge

\mathrm{d}_{t}=F\cdot d_{t}=d+FdF^{-1}+F\omega_{t}F^{-1},\quad F=\begin{pmatrix}1&f\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,,

of
d_{t}
. We see that
\mathrm{d}_{t}=d+t\eta
with
retraction form

\eta=\begin{pmatrix}fdf^{d}&-fdf^{d}f\\
df^{d}&-df^{d}f\end{pmatrix}\,.

Note that
\im\eta\subset L\subset\ker\eta
where
L=\psi\mathbb{H}=\begin{pmatrix}f\\
1\end{pmatrix}\mathbb{H}
is the line bundle of
f
, and thus the flatness of
\mathrm{d}_{t}

implies that
d\eta=0
since
\eta\wedge\eta=0
.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
35
,
12
,
13
,
21
]
)
.

Let
\eta
be a non–trivial retraction form, that is,

\eta\in\Omega^{1}(\End(\underline{\mathbb{H}}^{2}))
such that
\eta^{2}=0
and
d\eta=0
. Then

\mathrm{d}_{t}=d+t\eta\,,t\in\mathbb{R},

is a
\mathbb{R}
–family of flat connections. Moreover, if the associated line
bundle
L
of an immersion

f:M\to\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{R}^{4}
satisfies
\im\eta\subset L\subset\ker\eta
then
f
is isothermic. Conversely, every isothermic
surface
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
arises this way.

Remark 2.8
.

The above theorem holds more generally for surfaces in the
4–sphere: an isothermic surface in the 4–sphere is the line bundle
given by the kernel of a non–trivial retraction form. This way we
obtain a conformal theory. However, since we want to compare the
integrable systems of a CMC surface, which has an Euclidean theory,
we stated the theorem here for surfaces in 4–space.

The
\mathbb{R}
–family of flat connections
d_{t}
allows to define a variety
of transforms including the associated family of isothermic
surfaces:

Theorem 2.9

(see
[
32
,
11
]
)
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
be isothermic and
\mathrm{d}_{t}=d+t\eta
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, be its
associated family of flat connections. Let

and

\Phi\in\Gamma(\End_{\mathbb{H}}(\underline{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}^{2}))
with

(d\Phi)\Phi^{-1}=-r\eta
. Then

\eta^{\Phi}=\Phi^{-1}\eta\Phi

is a retraction form. In particular, if

\Phi^{-1}L=\begin{pmatrix}f^{\Phi}\\

then the isothermic surface

with associated family

is called a

of

.

Moreover, if

then

is, up to Möbius

Note that the associated family of

is given by

since

2.5.
Holomorphic sections and (generalised) Darboux transformation

In
[
7
]
the notion of a Darboux transformation was
extended to give a geometric interpretation of the spectral curve of a
conformal torus as, essentially, the set of its closed Darboux
transforms. This generalised Darboux transformation can geometrically
be understood as a weakening of the enveloping condition but for our
purposes we will use the following definition:

Definition 2.10

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{H}
be a
conformal immersion with associated line bundle
L
. We denote by

\tilde{L}\subset\underline{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}^{2}
the induced line bundle on the
universal cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
.

If
\varphi\in\Gamma(\underline{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}^{2})
satisfies

d\varphi\in\Omega^{1}(\tilde{L})
and

\mathbb{H}^{2}=\varphi_{p}\mathbb{H}\oplus\tilde{L}_{p}
for all
p\in\tilde{M}
, then

\hat{L}=\varphi\mathbb{H}
is called a
Darboux transform
of
f
.

Remark 2.11
.

As before, if
\varphi(p)\in\tilde{L}_{p}
for some
p\in\tilde{M}

then
\hat{f}
is called a
singular Darboux transform
. In the
case when
f:\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
this means that the enveloping
sphere congruence degenerates to a point, and
\hat{f}
is branched.

Darboux transforms are obtained by prolongation of holomorphic
sections: the left normal of an immersion
f:M\to\mathbb{H}
defines a
quaternionic holomorphic structure
D
, see
[
29
]
, on

\underline{\mathbb{H}}^{2}/L
by setting

D(e\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}e(d\alpha+N*d\alpha)\,.

Here we identify
\infty=\underline{\mathbb{H}}^{2}/L
via the isomorphism

\pi|_{e\mathbb{H}}
where
\pi:\underline{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\to\underline{\mathbb{H}}^{2}/L
is the canonical
projection. For any
e\alpha\in H^{0}(\tilde{M}\times e\mathbb{H})=\ker D
there
exists a unique
prolongation

\varphi\in\Gamma(\underline{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}^{2})
,
that is,
\varphi
is a lift of
e\alpha
such that

\pi d\varphi=0
. Then
\hat{L}=\varphi\mathbb{H}
is a (possibly singular)
Darboux transform of
f
, and every Darboux transform of
f
arises
this way,
[
7
]
.

As before, closed Darboux transforms are given by holomorphic sections
with multipliers, i.e.,
\gamma^{*}\varphi=\varphi h_{\gamma}
, for all

\gamma\in\pi_{1}(M)
.

If
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
is isothermic with dual surface
f^{d}
then any

\mathrm{d}_{r}
–parallel section

\varphi=e\alpha+\psi\beta\in\Gamma(\underline{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}^{2})
,
r\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
,
defines a holomorphic section: Since
d\alpha=-df\beta
we see that

*d\alpha=Nd\alpha
and
e\alpha
is a holomorphic section. Moreover,

\varphi
is a prolongation of
e\alpha
since
\pi\varphi=e\alpha

and
d\varphi=-r\eta\varphi\in\Omega^{1}(\tilde{L})
. But then the
line bundle
\hat{L}=\varphi\mathbb{H}
has affine coordinate

\hat{f}=f+\alpha\beta^{-1}
, and the classical Darboux
transformation is indeed a Darboux transformation in the more general
sense.

The classical Bianchi permutability theorem

[
1
]
extends to the more general
transformation:

Theorem 2.12

(Bianchi permutability
[
7
]
)
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
be conformal and
e\alpha_{1},e\alpha_{2}
be
holomorphic sections with
\alpha_{1}(p)\not=\alpha_{2}(p)
for all

p\in\tilde{M}
and prolongations
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}

respectively. If the Darboux transforms

L_{1}=\varphi_{1}\mathbb{H},L_{2}=\varphi_{2}\mathbb{H}
are immersed, then there is a
common (possibly singular) Darboux transform
\hat{L}=\hat{\varphi}\mathbb{H}

of
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
given by

\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\chi

where
\chi
is given by
d\varphi_{1}=(d\varphi_{2})\chi
.

Proof.

Since
d\varphi_{1},d\varphi_{2}\in\Omega^{1}(\tilde{L})
are 1–forms in
the line bundle
\tilde{L}
of
f
there is
\chi:\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{H}

with
d\varphi_{1}=(d\varphi_{2})\chi
. Then

d\varphi=-\varphi_{2}d\chi\in\Omega^{1}(L_{2})
and
\hat{L}
is a
Darboux transform of
L_{2}
. Similarly,
\hat{L}
is a Darboux
transform of
L_{1}
.
∎

3.
Isothermic surfaces

In this paper, we discuss the link between the various integrable
systems of a CMC surface. Since the Gauss map of a CMC surface is
harmonic, there exists a
\mathbb{C}_{*}
–family of flat connections on the
trivial
\mathbb{C}^{2}
–bundle,
[
37
]
, where we denote

\mathbb{C}_{*}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. On the other hand a CMC surface is isothermic
and we have seen that every isothermic surfaces gives rise to an
associated family of flat connections
d_{t}
with real parameter

t\in\mathbb{R}
on the trivial
\mathbb{H}^{2}
–bundle. To compare these families, we
first extend the family of quaternionic connections
d_{t}
to allow for
a complex parameter. We then discuss the link between generalisations
of the classical Darboux transformation and the simple factor dressing
for complex parameters.

3.1.
A
\mathbb{C}
–family of flat connections

Choosing the complex structure
I
which operates by right
multiplication by the quaternion
i
on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
we can identify

(\mathbb{H}^{2},I)=\mathbb{C}^{4}
. In particular, the associated family
d_{t}
,

t\in\mathbb{R}
, of the isothermic surface
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
can be seen as a
family of flat connections on the trivial
\mathbb{C}^{4}
–bundle

(M\times\mathbb{H},I)=M\times\mathbb{C}^{4}
.

This allows to use a complex parameter
\lambda
instead of the real
parameter
t
to obtain a
\mathbb{C}
–family of connections on

\underline{\mathbb{C}}^{4}=M\times\mathbb{C}^{4}
by setting

d_{\lambda}=d+\omega_{\lambda}

where

\omega_{\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}0&df\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}+\lambda\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
df^{d}&0\end{pmatrix}

and
\lambda
acts by right multiplication by

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}=\Span_{\mathbb{R}}\{1,i\}
. Then
d_{\lambda}
is flat since

d\omega_{\lambda}=\omega_{\lambda}\wedge\omega_{\lambda}=0
where we used
that
df\wedge df^{d}=df^{d}\wedge df=0
. Note that
d_{\lambda}
is a
complex connection which is only quaternionic when
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. A
section
\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{pmatrix}
is
d_{\varrho}
–parallel,
\varrho\in\mathbb{C}_{*}
, if and only

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}d\alpha&=-df\beta\\
d\beta&=-df^{d}\alpha\varrho\,.\end{split}

As before, we consider the gauge by
F
of
d_{\lambda}
to obtain the
family of flat connections as given in
[
5
]

\mathrm{d}_{\lambda}=F\cdot d_{\lambda}=d+FdF^{-1}+F\omega_{\lambda}F^{-1},\quad F=\begin{pmatrix}1&f\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\,,

so that
\mathrm{d}_{\lambda}=d+\lambda\eta
with retraction form

\eta=\begin{pmatrix}fdf^{d}&-fdf^{d}f\\
df^{d}&-df^{d}f\end{pmatrix}\,.

Note that we again use that
\lambda
operates by right
multiplication. As before
\im\eta\subset L\subset\ker\eta
and
thus the flatness of
\mathrm{d}_{\lambda}
implies that
d\eta=0
since

\eta\wedge\eta=0
. Thus, the result in Theorem

2.7
extends from the case
t\in\mathbb{R}
to the case

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\eta
be a non–trivial retraction form, that is,

\eta\in\Omega^{1}(\End(\underline{\mathbb{H}}^{2}))
such that
\eta^{2}=0
and
d\eta=0
. Then

\mathrm{d}_{\lambda}=d+\lambda\eta

is a
\mathbb{C}
–family of flat connections. Moreover, if the associated
line bundle
L
of an immersion
f:M\to\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{R}^{4}
satisfies

\im\eta\subset L\subset\ker\eta
then
f
is
isothermic. Conversely, every isothermic surface
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
arises
this way.

We conclude this section by giving the affine coordinate of a surface
in the associated family of an isothermic surface.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an isothermic surface.
For
r\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
every Calapso transform
A_{\Phi,r}(f)=f^{\Phi}:\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
of

is given by

f^{\Phi}=-\alpha_{1}^{-1}\alpha_{2}

where
\Phi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
and

\varphi_{i}=e\alpha_{i}+\psi\beta_{i}
are
\mathbb{H}
–linearly independent

\mathrm{d}_{r}
–parallel sections.

Proof.

Let
f^{\Phi}:\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a Calapso transform of
f
given
by an invertible
\Phi
satisfying
(d\Phi)\Phi^{-1}=-r\eta
, that
is,
f^{\Phi}:\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
is the affine coordinate of

\Phi^{-1}L
. Since
\varphi_{i}=e\alpha_{i}+\psi\beta_{i}
are

\mathrm{d}_{r}
–parallel and the sections

are holomorphic. Thus,
see
[

3.2.
The
\varrho
–Darboux transformation

We will now consider Darboux transforms which arise from parallel

,

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. These transforms are again isothermic, see

, but still extend the notion of the classical
Darboux transformation:

Definition 3.4
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an isothermic surface with dual surface
f^{d}
,
and
\varrho\in\mathbb{C}_{*}
fixed. Let

\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(\underline{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}^{2})
be a
d_{\varrho}
–parallel section such that

T=\alpha\beta^{-1}\colon\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\}\,.

Then
D_{\phi,\varrho}(f)=f+T
is called a

\varrho
–Darboux transform
of

.

Remark 3.5
.

We also note that as before, we obtain a
singular Darboux
transform
if
f(p)=\hat{f}(p)
for isolated
p\in\tilde{M}
.

We give an independent proof that
\varrho
–Darboux transforms are
isothermic by determining the dual surface of the Darboux transform as
in Theorem

:

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an isothermic surface with dual surface
f^{d}
,
and
\varrho\in\mathbb{C}_{*}
fixed. Let
\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma(\underline{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}^{2})

be a
d_{\varrho}
–parallel section such that

T=\alpha\beta^{-1}\colon\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\}\,.

Then the
\varrho
–Darboux transform
\hat{f}=f+T
is
isothermic and its dual surface
(\hat{f})^{d}=f^{d}+T^{d}
is a

\varrho
–Darboux transform of
f^{d}
where

T^{d}=\beta\varrho^{-1}\alpha^{-1}
, that is

(D_{\phi,\varrho}(f))^{d}=D_{\phi^{d},\varrho}(f^{d})

where
\phi^{d}=\begin{pmatrix}\beta\varrho^{-1}\\
\alpha\end{pmatrix}
. Moreover,
T
satisfies

(3.2)

dT=-df+Tdf^{d}\hat{\varrho}T

where
\hat{\varrho}=\alpha\varrho\alpha^{-1}
.

Remark 3.7
.

The equation (
3.2
) generalises the Riccati type
equation (
2.5
). Note however, that
\hat{\varrho}
is
not constant and depends on the parallel section
\phi
, that is, we
cannot find
\varrho
–Darboux transforms by solving this Riccati
type equation. However, its structure allows to carry over results
from the theory of classical Darboux transforms to the

\varrho
–Darboux transformation.

Proof.

Note that
a section
\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{pmatrix}
is
d_{\varrho}
–parallel if and only if

\varphi=e\alpha+\psi\beta
is
\mathrm{d}_{\varrho}
–parallel. In this
case,
\varphi
is the prolongation of the holomorphic section

e\alpha
. In particular,
\hat{L}=\varphi\mathbb{H}
is a Darboux transform
of
f
.

Next, since
d_{\varrho}\phi=0
we have

d\alpha=-df\beta,\qquad d\beta=-df^{d}\alpha\varrho

so that

dT=-df+Tdf^{d}\hat{\varrho}T

where
\hat{\varrho}=\alpha\varrho\alpha^{-1}
, that is,

(3.3)

d\hat{f}=Tdf^{d}\hat{\varrho}T\,.

Putting
\alpha^{d}=\beta\varrho^{-1}
and
\beta^{d}=\alpha

we obtain a solution of the equations (
3.1
) for the
dual surface
f^{d}
, that is,

d\alpha^{d}=-df^{d}\beta^{d},\quad d\beta^{d}=-df\alpha^{d}\varrho\,.

Therefore,
\phi^{d}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^{d}\\
\beta^{d}\end{pmatrix}
is parallel with respect to the flat connection
d^{d}_{\varrho}
in the
family of flat connections of the dual surface
f^{d}
. In particular,

\widehat{f^{d}}=f^{d}+T^{d}
is a
\varrho
–Darboux transform of
f^{d}

with
T^{d}=\beta\varrho^{-1}\alpha^{-1}
satisfying the Riccati
type equation

(3.4)

dT^{d}=-df^{d}+T^{d}df\beta\varrho\beta^{-1}T^{d}\,,

and thus

(3.5)

d\widehat{f^{d}}=T^{d}df\beta\varrho\beta^{-1}T^{d}\,.

Now

d\hat{f}\wedge d\widehat{f^{d}}=Tdf^{d}\hat{\varrho}T\wedge T^{d}df\beta\varrho\beta^{-1}T^{d}=0
since

\hat{\varrho}TT^{d}=\alpha\varrho\alpha^{-1}\alpha\beta^{-1}\beta\varrho^{-1}\alpha^{-1}=1
and
df^{d}\wedge df=0
. Similarly,
\beta\varrho\beta^{-1}T^{d}T=1
and

df\wedge df^{d}=0
give
d\widehat{f^{d}}\wedge d\hat{f}=0.
This
shows that
\widehat{f^{d}}
is a dual surface of
\hat{f}
and thus
both
\hat{f}
and
\widehat{f^{d}}
are isothermic.
∎

3.3.
Simple factor dressing of isothermic surfaces

We will now discuss the dressing operation introduced by Terng and
Uhlenbeck for harmonic maps
[

in the case of
isothermic surfaces. As we have seen, isothermic surfaces are
determined by their associated family of flat connections. To
construct new isothermic surfaces, one can gauge the associated family
with a suitable gauge matrix, see
[

]
, which depends on
the spectral parameter, to create a new associated family of flat
connections of the correct form as in Theorem
3.1
. A
special case of dressing is the simple factor dressing which is, as we
will show, the
\varrho
–Darboux transformation: we assume that the
map
\lambda\to r(\lambda)
has only a simple pole at some fixed
complex value
\lambda=\bar{\varrho}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 3.11

(Simple factor dressing)
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
be isothermic with associated
family
\mathrm{d}_{\lambda}=d+\lambda\eta
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Let

\varrho\in\mathbb{C}_{*}
and
\varphi
a
\mathrm{d}_{\varrho}
–parallel
section. Assume that

\tilde{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}^{4}=E\oplus Ej\oplus\tilde{L}
, where

E=\varphi\mathbb{C}
and
\tilde{L}
is the line bundle of
f
over the
universal cover
\tilde{M}
, and denote
by
\pi_{E},\pi_{Ej}
and
\pi_{L}
the projections onto

E=\varphi\mathbb{C}
,
Ej
and
\tilde{L}
respectively along the splitting. Let

r_{\varrho}^{E}(\lambda)=\pi_{E}\gamma_{\varrho}(\lambda)+\pi_{Ej}+\pi_{L}\sigma_{\varrho}(\lambda)

where

\gamma_{\varrho}(\lambda)=\frac{\bar{\varrho}(\varrho-\lambda)}{\varrho(\bar{\varrho}-\lambda)},\quad\sigma_{\varrho}(\lambda)=\frac{\bar{\varrho}}{\bar{\varrho}-\lambda}

act on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
by right multiplication.

Then the Darboux transform
\hat{f}=D_{\varphi,\varrho}(f)
is given
by a
simple factor dressing
with dressing matrix
r_{\lambda}
,
that is, the line bundle of
\hat{f}
is given by

r_{\varrho}^{E}(\infty)\tilde{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
and its associated family is given
by
\hat{d}_{\lambda}=r_{\lambda}\cdot d_{\lambda}
. We denote the simple
factor dressing with parameter
\varrho
and complex line bundle
E

by
\hat{f}=r_{E,\varrho}(f)
.

Moreover,
\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda}=d+\lambda\hat{\eta}

where

\hat{\eta}=-(\pi_{E}\circ d\circ\pi_{L}\frac{1}{\varrho}+\pi_{Ej}\circ d\circ\pi_{L}\frac{1}{\bar{\varrho}})\,.

In the following, we will abbreviate

,

\gamma_{\lambda}=\gamma_{\varrho}(\lambda),
and

\sigma_{\lambda}=\sigma_{\varrho}(\lambda)
to simplify notations if the
dependence of the parameters is clear from the context.

Remark 3.12
.

If
\varrho\in\mathbb{R}

[

]
.

Proof.

We first observe that

\gamma_{\lambda}^{-1}=\bar{\gamma}_{\bar{\lambda}}\,,\quad\sigma_{\bar{\lambda}}=\overline{\sigma_{\lambda}}\gamma_{\bar{\lambda}}

and

\pi_{E}(\xi j)=(\pi_{Ej}\xi)j

for
\xi\in\Gamma(\underline{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}^{2})
so that

r_{\bar{\lambda}}(\xi j)=r_{\lambda}(\xi)j\gamma_{\bar{\lambda}}

and hence

r_{\lambda}^{-1}(\xi j)=r_{\bar{\lambda}}^{-1}(\xi)\gamma_{\bar{\lambda}}j\,.

Since
r_{\lambda}\cdot d_{\lambda}=r_{\lambda}\cdot d+\varrho\Ad(r_{\lambda})\eta
we consider

r_{\lambda}\cdot d(\xi j)=(r_{\bar{\lambda}}\cdot d(\xi))j\,,\quad\Ad(r_{\lambda})\eta(\xi j)=(\Ad(r_{\bar{\lambda}})\eta\xi)j

which gives

(3.6)

\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda}(\xi j)=(\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\bar{\lambda}}\xi)j\,.

In particular,
\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda}
is a quaternionic connection for

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

We now claim that the map

\lambda\mapsto\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda},\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, is holomorphic. Since

r_{\lambda}
and
r_{\lambda}^{-1}
are holomorphic away from

\lambda=\varrho,\bar{\varrho}
and
\lambda\mapsto\mathrm{d}_{\lambda}
is
holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}
we only have to investigate holomorphicity at

\lambda=\varrho,\bar{\varrho}
. By the reality condition
(
3.6
) it is enough to show holomorphicity at

\lambda=\varrho
.

Writing

\mathrm{d}_{\lambda}=\mathrm{d}_{\varrho}+(\lambda-\varrho)\eta

we have

\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda}=r_{\lambda}\cdot\mathrm{d}_{\varrho}+(\lambda-\varrho)\Ad(r_{\lambda})\eta\,.

Since

r_{\lambda}^{-1}=\pi_{E}\gamma_{\lambda}^{-1}+\pi_{Ej}+\pi_{L}\sigma_{\lambda}^{-1}
and

L\subset\ker\eta,\im\eta\subset L
we see that

(3.7)

\Ad(r_{\lambda})\eta=\pi_{L}\eta(\pi_{E}\frac{\varrho}{\varrho-\lambda}+\pi_{Ej}\frac{\bar{\varrho}}{\bar{\varrho}-\lambda})

and thus
(\lambda-\varrho)\Ad(r_{\lambda})\eta
is holomorphic at

\lambda=\varrho
. We now investigate
r_{\lambda}\cdot d_{\varrho}
. Since
r_{\lambda}
and

(\pi_{Ej}+\pi_{L}\sigma_{\lambda}^{-1})
are holomorphic at

\lambda=\varrho
and

r_{\lambda}\cdot d_{\varrho}=r_{\lambda}\circ d_{\varrho}\circ(\pi_{E}\gamma_{\lambda}^{-1}+\pi_{Ej}+\pi_{L}\sigma_{\lambda}^{-1})

we only have to consider the term

r_{\lambda}\circ d_{\varrho}\circ(\pi_{E}\gamma_{\lambda}^{-1})\,.

Since
\varphi
is
\mathrm{d}_{\varrho}
–parallel, the bundle
E=\varphi\mathbb{C}
is

\mathrm{d}_{\varrho}
–stable. Therefore,

r_{\lambda}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\varrho}\circ(\pi_{E}\gamma_{\lambda}^{-1})=\pi_{E}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\varrho}\circ\pi_{E}

which is independent of
\lambda
. This shows that the map

\lambda\mapsto\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda}=r_{\lambda}\cdot\mathrm{d}_{\lambda}
is
holomorphic at
\lambda=\varrho
, and by the reality condition it is
holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}
. We now investigate the behaviour of

\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda}
at
\infty
.

We write again

\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda}=r_{\lambda}\cdot d+\lambda\Ad(r_{\lambda})\eta
.
By (
3.7
) we have

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda\Ad(r_{\lambda})\eta=-\pi_{L}\eta(\pi_{E}\varrho+\pi_{Ej}\bar{\varrho})\,.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle r_{\lambda}\cdot d

\displaystyle=(\pi_{E}\gamma_{\lambda}+\pi_{Ej}+\pi_{L}\sigma_{\lambda})\circ d\circ(\pi_{E}\gamma_{\lambda}^{-1}+\pi_{Ej}+\pi_{L}\sigma_{\lambda}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\pi_{E}\circ d\circ\pi_{E}+\pi_{Ej}\circ d\circ\pi_{Ej}+\pi_{L}\circ d\circ\pi_{L}+\pi_{E}\circ d\circ\pi_{Ej}\gamma_{\lambda}+\pi_{E}\circ d\circ\pi_{L}\frac{\varrho-\lambda}{\varrho}

\displaystyle\quad+\pi_{Ej}\circ d\circ\pi_{E}\gamma_{\lambda}^{-1}+\pi_{Ej}\circ d\circ\pi_{L}\frac{\bar{\varrho}-\lambda}{\bar{\varrho}}+\pi_{L}\circ d\circ\pi_{E}\frac{\varrho}{\varrho-\lambda}+\pi_{L}\circ d\circ\pi_{Ej}\frac{\bar{\varrho}}{\bar{\varrho}-\lambda}

gives with

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\gamma_{\lambda}=\frac{\bar{\varrho}}{\varrho}

and

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{\varrho}{\varrho-\lambda}=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{\bar{\varrho}}{\bar{\varrho}-\lambda}=0

that
r_{\lambda}\cdot d
has a simple pole at infinity. Since

\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda=0}=d
we thus can write

\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda}=d+\lambda\hat{\eta}_{\lambda}

where
\hat{\eta}_{\lambda}
is holomorphic on the compact

\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
and thus constant in
\lambda
. Moreover,
combining the previous computations we have

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}=\hat{\eta}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda}\hat{d}_{\lambda}=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\pi_{E}\circ d\circ\pi_{L}\frac{\varrho-\lambda}{\lambda\varrho}+\pi_{Ej}\circ d\circ\pi_{L}\frac{\bar{\varrho}-\lambda}{\lambda\bar{\varrho}}

\displaystyle=-(\pi_{E}\circ d\circ\pi_{L}\frac{1}{\varrho}+\pi_{Ej}\circ d\circ\pi_{L}\frac{1}{\bar{\varrho}})\,.

This shows that
\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda}
is a family of flat connections with

\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda}=d+\lambda\hat{\eta}
and
\hat{\eta}^{2}=0
. Therefore,

\im\hat{\eta}=\ker\hat{\eta}=\varphi\mathbb{H}
defines an isothermic
surface
\hat{L}
with associated family
\hat{\mathrm{d}}_{\lambda}
by Theorem

3.1
. Indeed,
\hat{L}=\varphi\mathbb{H}
is the

\varrho
–Darboux transform
D_{\varphi,\varrho}(f)
given by the

\mathrm{d}_{\varrho}
–parallel section
\varphi
. Finally, since

r_{\varrho}^{E}(\infty)=\pi_{E}\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\rho}+\pi_{Ej}
we see
that
\hat{L}=r_{\varrho}^{E}(\infty)\tilde{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
.
∎

Theorem 3.15
.

Let

be isothermic and
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be two

\mathrm{d}_{\varrho}
–parallel sections such that

\varrho

Proof.

Writing

4.
CMC surfaces

In this section we will now compare results for general isothermic
surfaces to ones given by the integrable system structure of a CMC
surface which is given by the harmonic Gauss map: a CMC surface

f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
from a Riemann surface into 3–space with constant mean
curvature
H=1
is isothermic since the parallel CMC surface is a dual
surface of
f
(if
f
is not a round sphere). We will link the
associated family of flat connections of a CMC surface, which is given
by its harmonic Gauss map, to the isothermic family
\mathrm{d}_{\rho}
of flat
connections. This will enable us to show that
\mu
–Darboux
transforms of a CMC surface
f
as defined in
[
20
]
are exactly
those
\rho
–Darboux transforms of
f
which have constant mean
curvature.

4.1.
The associated family of flat connections of a harmonic map

If
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a conformal immersion then the Ruh–Vilms
theorem,
[
54
]
, follows from (
2.2
):
H
is
constant if and only

d*dN+2|dN|^{2}N=0\,,

that is, if
N:M\to S^{2}
is harmonic. Here we identify a 2–form with
its quadratic form
\omega\wedge\eta=\omega*\eta-*\omega\eta

for
\omega,\eta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

In this case, there is a
\mathbb{C}_{*}
–associated family of flat connections
and, in case of a torus, one can describe harmonic maps via their
spectral data,
[
37
]
. If
f
has constant mean
curvature
H\not=0
then we consider the following
\mathbb{C}_{*}
–family of
flat connections on the trivial
\mathbb{C}^{2}
–bundle which is gauge
equivalent to the Hitchin family, see
[
20
]
: identifying

(\mathbb{H},I)=\mathbb{C}^{2}
, where
I
is the right multiplication by the
quaternion
i
, the family of connections

d^{N}_{\lambda}=d+(\lambda-1)A^{(1,0)}+(\lambda^{-1}-1)A^{(0,1)},\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{*}\,,

is flat if and only if
N
is harmonic. Here
A=\frac{1}{4}(*dN+NdN)

and
A^{(1,0)}=\frac{1}{2}(A-I*A)
and
A^{(0,1)}=\frac{1}{2}(A+I*A)
denote the

(1,0)
and
(0,1)
–parts of
A
with respect to the complex
structure
I
. Without loss of generality we will from now on assume
that our CMC surfaces have mean curvature
H=1
and additionally
exclude the case of a round sphere. We recall from Example

2.2
that in this case the parallel surface
g=f+N
is
a dual surface of
f
where
N
is the Gauss map of
f
. Moreover, we
have
2*A=df
by (
2.2
) and for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{*}
we can
write for
\alpha\in\Gamma(\underline{\mathbb{H}})
, see
[
20
]

(4.1)

d^{N}_{\lambda}\alpha=d\alpha+\frac{1}{2}df(N\alpha(a-1)+\alpha b)

where

a=\frac{\lambda+\lambda^{-1}}{2},b=i\frac{\lambda^{-1}-\lambda}{2}
. In particular,
a^{2}+b^{2}=1
and

\lambda=a+ib
. Moreover,
d^{N}_{\lambda}
is a quaternionic
connection on
\underline{\mathbb{H}}
if and only if
\lambda\in S^{1}
and in this
case
a={\rm Re}\,(\lambda),b={\rm Im}\,(\lambda)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a CMC surfaces with
H=1
, and dual surface

g=f+N
, where
N
is the Gauss map of
f
. Denote by
d_{\mu}^{N}
the
associated family of flat connections of
f
and by
d_{\mu}^{N_{g}}

the associated family of flat connections of
g
.

Let
\alpha\in\Gamma(\tilde{\mathbb{H}})
and for

\mu\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}\,,a=\frac{\mu+\mu^{-1}}{2}
and

b=i\frac{\mu^{-1}-\mu}{2}
define

\beta=\frac{1}{2}(N\alpha(a-1)+\alpha b)\,.

Then
\alpha
is
d_{\mu}^{N}
–parallel if and only if
\beta
is

d_{\mu}^{N_{g}}
– parallel.

Proof.

Recall that
N_{g}=-N
is the Gauss map of
g
. Using
a^{2}+b^{2}=1
we
have

\frac{1}{2}(-N\beta(a-1)+\beta b)=\alpha\frac{1-a}{2}

so that the flat connection
d_{\mu}^{-N}
of
g
is given by

d_{\mu}^{-N}\beta=d\beta+\frac{1}{2}dg(N_{g}\beta(a-1)+\beta b)=d\beta+dg\alpha\frac{a-1}{2}\,.

If
d_{\mu}^{N}\alpha=0
, that is
d\alpha=-df\beta
then

d\beta=\frac{1}{2}((dg-df)\alpha(a-1)-Ndf\beta(a-1)-df\beta b)=dg\alpha\frac{a-1}{2}

shows that
\beta
is
d_{\mu}^{-N}
–parallel.

Conversely, writing
\alpha_{g}=\beta
we have
d_{\mu}^{N_{g}}\alpha_{g}=d\alpha_{g}-dg\alpha\frac{1-a}{2}
so that

\beta_{g}=\alpha\frac{1-a}{2}\,.

Reversing now the roles of
f
and
g
, we see that if

\alpha_{g}=\beta
is
d^{N_{g}}_{\mu}
–parallel then

d^{N}_{\mu}\beta_{g}=0
by symmetry. Since
\mu\not=1
, this shows
that
\alpha
is
d_{\mu}^{N}
–parallel.

∎

From this discussion we immediately see that a
d^{N}_{\mu}
–parallel
section gives, without further integration, a parallel section of the
associated family
d_{\varrho}
where we now view
f
as an isothermic
surface.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a CMC surface with
H=1
and

d_{\varrho}=d+\begin{pmatrix}0&df\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}+\varrho\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
dg&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

where
g=f+N
is the parallel CMC surface of
f
. Then all

d_{\varrho}
–parallel sections,
\varrho\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
, are
given algebraically by

d_{\mu}^{N}
–parallel sections.

More precisely, any
d_{\varrho}
–parallel section is given as

\phi=\phi_{+}+\phi_{-}\,,\qquad\phi_{\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{\pm}\\
\beta_{\pm}\end{pmatrix}\,,

where

\displaystyle d_{\mu_{\pm}}^{N}\alpha_{\pm}

\displaystyle=0\,,\qquad\beta_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(N\alpha_{\pm}(a-1)\pm\alpha_{\pm}b)

with
a=1-2\varrho,b=2\sqrt{\varrho(1-\varrho)}
and

\mu_{\pm}=a\pm ib
. We call
\mu_{\pm}=a\pm ib

associated to the isothermic spectral parameter

\varrho
.

Remark 4.3
.

We note that there is an ambiguity of choice of

\sqrt{\varrho(1-\varrho)}

(\mu_{+},\mu_{-})
.

Proof.

Since
a^{2}+b^{2}=1
we see that
(a+ib)^{-1}=a-ib

\frac{\mu_{\pm}+\mu_{\pm}^{-1}}{2}=a

\frac{2-\mu_{\pm}-\mu_{\pm}^{-1}}{4}=\frac{1-a}{2}=\varrho\,.

Since

\phi_{\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{\pm}\\
\beta_{\pm}\end{pmatrix}
is
d_{\varrho}
–parallel.

Now, if

are complex

\alpha_{+}=\alpha_{-}m

–parallel sections, that is, all

–parallel

.
∎

3–space (up to translation) if and only if

\hat{f}=D^{N}_{\alpha,\mu}(f)
is, up to translation, a

\mu
–Darboux transform of
f
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi=e\nu+\psi\beta
is
d_{\mu}^{S}
–parallel we can
write
\nu=\alpha+n
with
n\in\mathbb{H}
and

\alpha=N\beta-\beta\frac{b}{a-1}
where

a=\frac{\mu+\mu^{-1}}{2}
,
b=i\frac{\mu^{-1}-\mu}{2}
and

\beta
is a
d_{\mu}^{-N}
–parallel section. We compute

d\hat{f}=df+d\nu\beta^{-1}-\nu\beta^{-1}d\beta\beta^{-1}=\nu\beta^{-1}dg\alpha\varrho\beta^{-1}

with
\varrho=\frac{1-a}{2}
. By Corollary
4.15
the
\mu
–Darboux transform
g^{\mu}=g+T_{g}
,

T_{g}=\beta\varrho^{-1}\alpha^{-1}
, of the parallel CMC
surface
g=f+N
has Gauss map

N^{\mu}_{g}=-T_{g}N_{g}T_{g}^{-1}=-T_{g}NT_{g}^{-1}\,.

Hence the right normal

\hat{R}=-T_{g}NT_{g}^{-1}

of
\hat{f}
is the negative of the Gauss map
N^{\mu}_{g}
of
g^{\mu}
.

Put differently,
\hat{R}
is the Gauss map of the parallel CMC
surface
f^{\mu}=f+\alpha\beta^{-1}
of
g^{\mu}
. Therefore,

\hat{R}
is harmonic, and
\hat{f}
is constrained Willmore, see

[
46
,
6
]
.

For
\hat{f}
to be CMC in 3–space (up to translation) the left
and right normal of
\hat{f}
have to coincide. In this case,

\hat{f}
and
f^{\mu}
have the same Gauss map, and
\hat{f}
is a

\mu
–Darboux transform of
f
up to translation.
∎

6.
Conclusion

In this paper we investigated the integrable systems of a CMC surface

f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
which are given by the harmonic Gauss map
N
, by the
isothermicity of
f
and by the conformal Gauss map
S
in terms of
their associated families of flat connections
d_{\lambda}^{N}
,

d_{\varrho}
(and
\mathrm{d}_{\varrho}=F\cdot d_{\varrho}
), and
d_{\lambda}^{S}

respectively. We showed that the parallel sections of one family of
connections give, together with the Gauss map
N
of
f
, all parallel
sections of the other two via algebraic operations. Here there is a
2:1 correspondence of the CMC and Constrained Willmore spectral
parameter
\mu
and the isothermic spectral parameter
\rho
via

\mu_{\pm}=1-2\rho\pm 2i\sqrt{\rho(1-\rho)}
and

\rho=-\frac{(\mu-1)^{2}}{4\mu}
respectively. This provides a
unified view on all three integrable systems of a CMC surface.

In particular, we demonstrated the correspondence between the various
associated families, Darboux transforms and simple factor dressings.

We consider CMC surfaces with
H=1
, and exclude the case of a round
sphere. Recalling that we denote by
g=f+N
the parallel CMC surface
with Gauss map
N_{g}=-N
, and for
\mu\in\mathbb{C}_{*},\rho\in\mathbb{C},r\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
,

a
d_{\mu}^{N}
–parallel section by
\alpha
,

a
d_{\mu}^{N_{g}}
–parallel section by
\beta
,

a
d_{\mu}^{S}
–parallel section by
\varphi
,

a
d_{\rho}
–parallel section by
\phi
,

a
\mathrm{d}_{r}
–parallel endomorphism by
\Phi
,

a
\mathrm{d}_{\rho}
–stable or
d_{\mu}
–stable complex line bundle by
E
,

a
d_{\mu}^{S}
–stable complex rank 2 bundle by
W
,

we summarise our results:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a CMC surface with
H=1
and
f
not a round
sphere. Then a surface

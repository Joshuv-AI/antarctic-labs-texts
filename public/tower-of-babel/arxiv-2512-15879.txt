Entangled two-proton emission from 16 Ne and its sensitivity to diproton correlation
Tomohiro Oishi, Masaaki Kimura

Abstract
We discuss how the spin correlation, which reflects the quantum entanglement between two fermions, can serve as a probe of diproton correlation in the two-proton ( 2 ​ p 2p ) emission. We investigated the Ne 16 {}^{16}{\rm Ne} nucleus using the time-dependent three-body ( O 14 + 2 ​ p {}^{14}{\rm O}+2p ) model, and found that the 2 ​ p 2p -spin correlation exceeded the limit of local-hidden-variable (LHV) theory when the initial state had a spin-singlet diproton configuration. In contrast, for other configurations, it was remarkably reduced. This suggests that a strong initial diproton correlation is essential to generate a spin correlation nearly identical to that of a pure spin-singlet diproton. Such sensitivity indicates that 2 ​ p 2p -spin correlation can serve as a sensitive probe of diproton configurations, which could facilitate future studies on quantum entanglement and spin-dependent phenomena in atomic nuclei as well as in broader multi-fermion systems.



Introduction
.
Spin correlation provides a direct manifestation of quantum entanglement
Bell 2004
;
Bell 1964
;
Clauser et al. 1969
.
Violations of the local-hidden-variable (LHV) bound in terms of Bell-Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt (CHSH) inequality are well established in atomic and optical systems
Aspect et al. 1982
;
Weihs et al. 1998
;
Rowe et al. 2001
;
Giustina et al. 2015
;
Shalm et al. 2015
.
Nuclear systems offer a conceptually distinct arena, where correlated fermions emerge naturally with strong interactions in a finite many-body environment
Sakai et al. 2006
;
Lamehi-Rachti and Mittig 1976
;
Polachic et al. 2004
.
A landmark experiment by Sakai
et al.
measured the spin correlation of two protons emitted in the reaction
{}^{2}\mathrm{H}(p,{}^{2}\mathrm{He})n
and observed a clear violation of the Bell-CHSH inequality
Sakai et al. 2006
, demonstrating the generation of spin-entangled proton pairs in a nuclear reaction.

Two-proton (
2p
) radioactivity in proton-rich nuclei provides another natural setting for producing correlated proton pairs
Grigorenko et al. 2002
;
Pftzner et al. 2002
;
Pfützner et al. 2012
;
Blank and Ploszajczak 2008
;
Blank and Borge 2008
;
Grigorenko 2009
;
Pfützner et al. 2023
;
Qi et al. 2019
.
In so-called “prompt”
2p
emitters, two valence protons are confined inside the Coulomb barrier for a finite lifetime and interact within a finite spatial volume.
The proton-proton interaction can compete with, or even dominate over, the interaction between each proton and the core.
This interaction particularly enhances the spin-singlet component and thereby gives rise to a diproton correlation
Dean and Hjorth-Jensen 2003
;
Brink and Broglia 2005
;
Matsuo 2006
;
Hagino et al. 2007
;
Oishi et al. 2010
;
Oishi et al. 2014
;
Oishi et al. 2017
;
Oishi and Kimura 2025
;
Oishi 2025
.

Once the decay sets in, however, the two protons tunnel through the long-range Coulomb field and evolve under three-body continuum dynamics
Blank and Ploszajczak 2008
;
Grigorenko 2009
. As a result, spatial and kinematic correlations of the emitted protons are governed primarily by the decay dynamics and show only limited sensitivity to the initial structure
Brown et al. 2014
;
Charity 2016
. This behavior is manifested differently in
2p
emitters such as
{}^{6}\mathrm{Be}
,
{}^{16}\mathrm{Ne}
, and
{}^{45}\mathrm{Fe}

Pfützner et al. 2012
;
Pfützner et al. 2023
.
In particular, for
{}^{16}\mathrm{Ne}
, both experimental and theoretical studies indicate that final-state proton-proton correlations provide only a weak probe of the initial configuration, making it a stringent test case for identifying more robust observables
Brown et al. 2014
;
Grigorenko et al. 2012
.
This generic loss of sensitivity in spatial and momentum observables motivates the search for alternative probes for diproton correlations in the initial state.

The Coulomb interaction, which governs the decay dynamics, is essentially spin independent.
While orbital motion and spatial correlations are strongly modified by tunneling and three-body continuum effects, spin correlations are expected to be far less sensitive to them. This suggests that
2p
-spin correlations may provide a robust probe for diproton correlations in
2p
emitters.

In this work, we study the
2p
-emitting decay of
{}^{16}\mathrm{Ne}
to examine whether spin correlations can serve as a robust probe of initial diproton correlations.
We employ a time-dependent three-body model of a
{}^{14}\mathrm{O}+2p
system, which allows us to describe the
2p
emission from an initially localized state. By focusing on spin-dependent observables, we address the situation in which spatial correlations are strongly distorted by decay dynamics.

Spin correlation as observable.

To quantify spin correlations between the two emitted protons, we employ the Bell-CHSH formulation based on spin-resolved measurements
Clauser et al. 1969
;
Sakai et al. 2006
.
For a rotationally invariant
2p
state with total angular momentum
J=0
, spin correlations are independent of the definition of spin-quantization axes and depend only on their relative angles.
This property allows one to characterize the spin correlation by a single angular parameter
\Phi

Sakai et al. 2006
.
That reads

S(\Phi)=\max\left\{\left|S_{1}(\Phi)\right|,\left|S_{2}(\Phi)\right|,\left|S_{3}(\Phi)\right|,\left|S_{4}(\Phi)\right|\right\},

(1)

where

\displaystyle S_{1}(\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Braket{A_{1}B_{1}}+\Braket{A_{2}B_{1}}+\Braket{A_{1}B_{2}}+\Braket{A_{2}B_{2}},

\displaystyle S_{2}(\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Braket{A_{1}B_{1}}-\Braket{A_{2}B_{1}}+\Braket{A_{1}B_{2}}+\Braket{A_{2}B_{2}},

\displaystyle S_{3}(\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Braket{A_{1}B_{1}}+\Braket{A_{2}B_{1}}-\Braket{A_{1}B_{2}}+\Braket{A_{2}B_{2}},

\displaystyle S_{4}(\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Braket{A_{1}B_{1}}+\Braket{A_{2}B_{1}}+\Braket{A_{1}B_{2}}-\Braket{A_{2}B_{2}}.

(2)

For an arbitrary two-fermion state
\ket{\Psi(1,2)}
, these expectation values for the two observers, so-called “Alice” and “Bob” conventionally, are determined as

\Braket{A_{i}B_{j}}=\Braket{\Psi(1,2)\mid\hat{A}_{i,\Phi}(1)\otimes\hat{B}_{j,\Phi}(2)\mid\Psi(1,2)}.

(3)

Operators
\hat{A}_{i,\Phi}
and
\hat{B}_{j,\Phi}
are given as

\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{A}_{1,\Phi}(1)=\hat{\sigma}_{z}(1),\\
\hat{A}_{2,\Phi}(1)=\hat{\sigma}_{z}(1)\cos 2\Phi+\hat{\sigma}_{x}(1)\sin 2\Phi,\\
\hat{B}_{1,\Phi}(2)=\hat{\sigma}_{z}(2)\cos\Phi+\hat{\sigma}_{x}(2)\sin\Phi,\\
\hat{B}_{2,\Phi}(2)=\hat{\sigma}_{z}(2)\cos\Phi-\hat{\sigma}_{x}(2)\sin\Phi.\end{array}

(4)

These operators depend on the orientation angle
\Phi
, which is an experimental parameter.

For computing
S(\Phi)
, the coupled-spin part of two protons can be generally expanded on the spin-singlet (
S_{12}=0
) and spin-triplet (
S_{12}=1
) states

That is

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi(1,2)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{S=0}^{1}\sum_{V=-S}^{S}F_{SV}\ket{S,V}

(5)

\displaystyle=\frac{F_{00}\ket{0,0}+F_{10}\ket{1,0}+F_{11}\ket{1,+1}+F_{1-1}\ket{1,-1}}{\sqrt{N}}

with the normalization factor
\sqrt{N}
, where

\displaystyle\ket{1,+1}=\ket{\uparrow\uparrow},~~~~~~~\ket{1,0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{\uparrow\downarrow+\downarrow\uparrow},

\displaystyle\ket{1,-1}=\ket{\downarrow\downarrow},~~~~~~~\ket{0,0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{\uparrow\downarrow-\downarrow\uparrow}.

(6)

These states satisfy
\hat{S}^{2}_{12}\ket{S,V}=S(S+1)\ket{S,V}
and
\hat{S}_{12,z}\ket{S,V}=V\ket{S,V}
.

In FIG.
1
, for example,
S(\Phi)
is calculated for the pure
S_{12}=0
state (100% spin-singlet).
Namely,
F_{00}=1
and
F_{10}=F_{11}=F_{1-1}=0
with
\sqrt{N}=1
.
At
\Phi=\pi/4,~3\pi/4,~5\pi/4
, and
7\pi/4
, it takes the maximum value,
S=2\sqrt{2}
(Tsirelson’s bound)
Cirel’son 1980
.
We mention that Sakai’s experiment observed a similar pattern to this case
Sakai et al. 2006
.
On the other side, in the “spherical spin-triplet” case,
where
F_{00}=0
and
F_{10}=F_{11}=F_{1-1}=1

\sqrt{N}=\sqrt{3}
,
the
S(\Phi)
becomes reduced.
Indeed for two nucleons in spherical systems,
e.g.
deuteron, their state must include the
V=0
and
V=\pm 1

In this case,
S(\Phi)<2
, and thus, it does not overcome the limit of LHV theory.

Three-body model.

We employ the same three-body model in our previous works
Oishi and Kimura 2025
;
Oishi 2025
, and thus, only a brief review is presented. The three-body Hamiltonian after subtracting the center-of-mass motion reads
Oishi and Kimura 2025

\hat{H}_{3B}=\hat{h}(\bm{r}_{1})+\hat{h}(\bm{r}_{2})+v_{pp}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})+\frac{\bm{p}_{1}\cdot\bm{p}_{2}}{m_{C}}.

(7)

Here
\hat{h}(\bm{r}_{i})
is the Hamiltonian for the subsystem
15
F of the
i
th valence proton and the
14
O core:

\displaystyle\hat{h}(r_{i})=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\frac{d^{2}}{dr_{i}^{2}}+V(r_{i}),

\displaystyle V(r_{i})=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\frac{l(l+1)}{r_{i}^{2}}+V_{WS}(r_{i})+V_{Coul}(r_{i}),

(8)

where
\mu=m_{p}m_{C}/(m_{p}+m_{C})
.
For
V(r_{i})
, we use the same parameters in the “prompt” case of Ref.
Oishi and Kimura 2025
.
The proton-proton interaction reads

v_{pp}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})=v_{pp,vac}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})+v_{pp,add}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2}).

(9)

This interaction includes the vacuum and additional, surface-dependent terms.
Their parameters are the same to Ref.
Oishi and Kimura 2025
, namely, optimized so as to reproduce the experimental
Q
value of
16
Ne.

The time evolution of
2p
state is calculated as
\ket{\Psi(t)}=\exp(-it\hat{H}_{3B}/\hbar)\ket{\Psi(0)}
,
where the initial state is solved as the confined

state inside the Coulomb barrier

.
That can be expanded on the eigenstates of
\hat{H}_{3B}
.
Thus,

\ket{\Psi(t)}=\sum_{N}G_{N}(t)\ket{E_{N}},

(10)

where
G_{N}(t)=\exp(-itE_{N}/\hbar)G_{N}(0)
from
\hat{H}_{3B}\ket{E_{N}}=E_{N}\ket{E_{N}}
.
Notice that the
2p
-energy spectrum is independent of the time:
\sigma(E_{N})=\left|G_{N}(t)\right|^{2}=\left|G_{N}(0)\right|^{2}
.
The continuum states are discretized in the radial box with
r_{\rm max}=80
fm

\displaystyle\Psi_{d}(t,\bm{r}_{1}\sigma_{1},\bm{r}_{2}\sigma_{2})=\Braket{t,\bm{r}_{1}\sigma_{1},\bm{r}_{2}\sigma_{2}\mid\Psi_{d}(t,1,2)}

The time-dependent coordinate parts

Note that the

\displaystyle F_{1V}(t,\bm{r}_{2},\bm{r}_{1})=-F_{1V}(t,\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})~~~(V=0,\pm 1),

\displaystyle F_{00}(t,\bm{r}_{2},\bm{r}_{1})=F_{00}(t,\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2}),

\displaystyle\Braket{A_{i}B_{j}}_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{S^{\prime},V^{\prime}}\sum_{S,V}a_{S^{\prime}V^{\prime},SV}(t)

\displaystyle\iint F^{*}_{S^{\prime}V^{\prime}}(t,\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})F_{SV}(t,\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})d\bm{r}_{1}d\bm{r}_{2}.

Summary
. The spin correlation
S(\Phi)
in the
2p
emission from
16
Ne is calculated with the time-dependent three-body model.
This
S(\Phi)
is predicted as similar to the pure
S_{12}=0
case

, when the following two conditions are satisfied:
(i) the Hamiltonian reproduces the prompt
2p
-decaying scheme, which is consistent to experiments;
(ii) a diproton correlation is dominant in the initial state.
By keeping these conditions, our result converges to
S(\pi/4)\cong 2.8
, exceeding the limit of LHV theory.
The Coulomb interactions do not harm this correlation during the time evolution.
Consequently, the
S(\Phi)
can be a robust probe into the diproton correlation.

Analysis of 24 pro-inflammatory cytokines in aqueous and serum of 94 patients without diabetes
Authors: Stephen Jae Kim, Sapna Gangaputra, Jinsong Sheng, Shriji Patel, Archana A Nair, Sara Al Hussein Al Awamlh, John Fitzpatrick, Edward Cherney, Lindsay Veach, Crystal Nicholson
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/AimsTo assess 24 pro-inflammatory cytokines in the aqueous and serum of normal patients without diabetes.Methods94 consecutive eyes of 94 patients undergoing elective vitrectomy surgery were prospectively enrolled. Blood and aqueous sampling were done in the operating room. A microparticle bead-based multiplex assay was used to measure aqueous and serum levels of: fibroblast growth factor-2, eotaxin, granulocyte colony stimulating factor (G-CSF), FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 ligand (FLT-3L), growth-regulated alpha protein (GROa), interleukin (IL)-10, monocyte chemotactic protein (MCP)-3, macrophage-derived chemokine (MDC), soluble CD40L (sCD40L), IL-17A, interleukin-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1RA), IL-1β, IL-2, IL-4, IL-6, IL-8, interferon gamma-induced protein 10 (IP-10), MCP-1, macrophage inflammatory protein-1β, tumour necrosis factor-α, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), regulated on activation normal T expressed and secreted (RANTES), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF)-AA and PDGF-AB/BB.ResultsA significant positive correlation between aqueous and serum levels was observed for FLT-3L (r=0.45; p<0.001) and positive trends were observed for eotaxin (r=0.27; p=0.04), MDC (r=0.34; p<0.01) and RANTES (r=0.33; p=0.007). There were no significant differences observed based on age or gender. In contrast, aqueous cytokine levels for G-CSF, GROa and MCP-1 were significantly higher in pseudophakic eyes compared with phakic eyes: G-CSF (16.52 pg/mL±22.39 vs 2.77 pg/mL±6.80, p<0.0001), GROa (12.45 pg/mL±6.54 vs 8.26 pg/mL±5.48, p=0.001) and MCP-1 (1340.51 pg/mL±565.21 vs 830.44 pg/mL±454.36, p<0.0001), respectively.ConclusionsWe report the first characterisation of 24 different pro-inflammatory cytokines in 94 patients without diabetes. The absence of the crystalline lens was associated with significantly higher aqueous levels of G-CSF, GROa and MCP-1.Trial registration numberNCT04505566.Keywords: Aqueous humour, Inflammation, Retina, Prospective Studies, Phakic Intraocular Lenses

Introduction

Biomarkers are measurable substances that can indicate or predict the presence, absence or severity of disease. Diabetes is the most common cause of legal blindness in the USA for those less than 74 years of age.1 Despite preventable measures such as blood sugar and blood pressure control, diabetic retinopathy (DR) progresses in many patients.2 Current diagnostic tests fail to identify early disease or adequately predict disease progression. Consequently, biomarkers that allow earlier detection of DR and predict those that are more likely to develop vision loss would address a major unmet need.

DR is an established inflammatory disease.3 4 Chronic hyperglycaemia induces expression of adhesion molecules through many pathways.5 Early in DR, leucocytes migrate to the retinal vasculature and the blood-ocular barrier breaks down.4 6 7 Both phenomena lead to progressive dysfunction and death of endothelial cells and pericytes, which are pathophysiological hallmarks of DR.8,11 Many inflammatory cytokines are consistently elevated in the aqueous and vitreous of patients with advanced DR and diabetic macular oedema.12,16 Chronic inflammation-mediated retinal vascular damage results in vascular leakage, capillary non-perfusion and retinal ischaemia; all key clinical features of advanced DR that lead to irreversible vision loss.4 17

We previously demonstrated that multiple pro-inflammatory cytokines are highly elevated in the aqueous and vitreous of patients with advanced DR.13 18 19 Inflammatory cytokines are candidates for direct biomarkers that may more closely predict progression of DR as well as other retinal diseases. Intraocular cytokines are a real-time measure of biological activity within the eye due to their short intraocular half-lives and therefore directly reflect its current disease state. Consequently, cytokine profiles have the potential to correlate more closely with disease activity and better predict risk of disease progression than currently available indicators. Moreover, multiple cytokines can now be tested with as little as 50 µL of aqueous fluid which can be readily collected clinically and repeated serially to allow for monitoring of disease progression and response to treatment. Finally, cytokines offer novel therapeutic targets for inhibition.

To date, the only validated prognostic DR biomarker is the circulating glycaemia marker glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c).2 20 HbA1c screening, however, reflects systemic glucose control as opposed to direct DR pathology. Consequently, HbA1c only predicts approximately 11% of the risk of DR progression.21

The INflammatory MediatorS in the PathophysIology of Diabetic REtinopathy (INSPIRE) study is a prospective, randomised, double-blinded, placebo-controlled trial currently being conducted at the Vanderbilt Eye Institute, Nashville, Tennessee, USA. In this study, we present the aqueous and serum results of 24 cytokines measured in our cohort of 94 patients without diabetes. Our primary objectives are to: (1) provide reference cytokine values in normal patients without diabetes and (2) to determine any association of cytokines with key patient demographics to provide important baseline information for future cross-comparison analysis.

Methods

All patients were recruited and enrolled at the Vanderbilt University Medical Center (VUMC) from 20 November 2020, until 12 May 2022.

Non-diabetic study population

94 eyes of 94 patients without diabetes undergoing elective vitrectomy surgery at VUMC for non-inflammatory conditions such as epiretinal membrane or vitreous opacities were enrolled consecutively. Exclusion criteria consisted of previous vitrectomy in either eye, presence of diabetes, prior intravitreal injection within 3 months, history of intraocular surgery other than cataract surgery, coexistent macular, retinovascular or inflammatory eye disease, history of ocular trauma, previous use of periocular steroids within 3 months, current use of prescription topical or systemic non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, use of systemic corticosteroids, blood pressure >180/110 mm Hg at baseline and regular aspirin use (defined as taking on average ≥1300 mg per week). Key patient demographic information was obtained from direct review of the medical record.

Of these 94 patients, 61 (65%) had whole blood drawn in the preoperative area of VUMC and then spun down after coagulation to isolate serum. Serum was divided into smaller volumes and individually stored at −80°C for subsequent testing of inflammatory mediators. Aqueous sampling was done in the operating room at the beginning of surgery using a 30-gauge needle on a 1 mL tuberculin syringe that was inserted into the anterior chamber under direct visualisation and used to collect 0.05 mL or more of aqueous fluid. The aqueous sample was immediately placed on dry ice and then apportioned into 50 µL aggregates and stored at −80°C for future testing.

Measurement of pro-inflammatory cytokines

Due to limited volumes, a microparticle bead-based multiplex assay was used to measure inflammatory cytokines as previously described.13 22 In brief, aqueous samples were thawed and prepared using the Millipore Multiplex Human Cytokine/Chemokine/Growth Factor Panel A (MilliporeSigma, Burlington, Massachusetts, USA). Bead samples were analysed with Luminex 200 Analyzer following the manufacturer’s protocols for 24 pro-inflammatory cytokines: fibroblast growth factor (FGF)-2, eotaxin, granulocyte colony stimulating factor (G-CSF), FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 ligand (FLT-3L), growth-regulated alpha protein (GROa), interleukin (IL)-10, monocyte chemotactic protein (MCP)-3, macrophage-derived chemokine (MDC), soluble CD40L (sCD40L), IL-17A, interleukin-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1RA), IL-1β, IL-2, IL-4, IL-6, IL-8, interferon gamma-induced protein 10 (IP-10), MCP-1, macrophage inflammatory protein (MIP)-1β, tumour necrosis factor (TNF)-α, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), regulated on activation normal T expressed and secreted (RANTES), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF)-AA and PDGF-AB/BB. Stored serum was thawed and analysed for the same 24 cytokines in an identical fashion. All aqueous and serum testing was performed on the same batch of test kits.

Statistical analysis

Mean, median and SD were calculated for descriptive characteristics. Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corporation One Microsoft Way, Redmond, Washington, USA) was used to perform Pearson correlation analysis on aqueous and serum cytokine values. An unpaired, two-tailed t-test was used for independent groups. After Bonferroni correction for multiple analyses, a p value of ≤0.003 was considered significant. A Z-score of ±3 SD was used to identify outliers. A total of 57 of 2256 (2.5%) aqueous results and a total of 30 of 1464 (2%) serum results were outliers and excluded.

Discussion

Despite recognition that inflammation contributes to the progression of DR, the lack of well-defined and validated biomarkers prevents reliable assessment of disease risk and early application of anti-inflammatory therapies to prevent disease progression.23 24 Intraocular cytokines are a real-time measure of biological activity due to their short intraocular half-lives and therefore directly reflect current disease state. Consequently, cytokines have the potential to correlate with disease activity more closely, better predict risk of disease progression than currently available indicators and offer novel targets for inhibition. Our results provide the first reference values of 24 prominent inflammatory cytokines in a large cohort of patients without diabetes that will be essential for cross-comparison analysis to understand better the inflammatory drivers of DR progression as well as the role of inflammation in senescence and other disease states.

We observed a significant positive correlation between aqueous and serum levels for FLT-3L and similar positive trends for eotaxin, MDC and RANTES. Such correlations, if confirmed by others, allow for both surrogate testing of serum to determine intraocular cytokine levels and provide novel systemic targets that may slow the progression of systemic diabetic disease. FLT-3L stimulates the proliferation and differentiation of various blood cell progenitors, including B-cells and T-cells, by binding to the receptor tyrosine kinase known as FMS-like tyrosine kinase-3.25 Eotaxin is a chemotactic cytokine that primarily attracts eosinophils which play an integral role in allergic responses.26 MDC is a potent chemoattractant for CD4 and CD8 T-cells, IL-2-activated natural killer cells and dendritic cells. MDC plays a role in chronic inflammation by mediating the continuous homing of dendritic cells and lymphocytes.27 RANTES is a potent proinflammatory cytokine that recruits leucocytes to the site of inflammation.26 RANTES attracts immune cells from the peripheral blood via haptotaxis (directional cell movement in response to adhesive substrates), inducing firm adhesion to the vascular endothelium and subsequent diapedesis through the walls of capillaries and into surrounding tissue.

We observed highly significant increased levels of G-CSF, GROa and MCP-1 in pseudophakic eyes compared with phakic eyes and a similar strong trend with IL-6. G-CSF is a key growth factor that induces bone marrow to produce granulocytes and is administered therapeutically to increase neutrophil counts in patients with neutropenia. GROa is expressed by macrophages, neutrophils and epithelial cells and is strongly chemoattractant for neutrophils and basophils. MCP-1 is a key mediator of innate immunity and is a potent chemotactic factor for monocytes.

IL-6 is a potent pro-inflammatory cytokine that is essential for the inflammatory acute phase response induced by tissue damage. It is presumed that intraocular cytokines in DR are produced mainly by microglia, Müller and retinal pigment epithelium cells.23 24 28 Cytokines produced within the retina would readily permeate through the internal limiting membrane into the vitreous cavity and via normal diffusion and anterograde flow reach the aqueous following a gradient. However, in eyes without DR, cytokines may originate from other cell types such as corneal endothelial cells and ciliary epithelial cells.

It is well known that the crystalline lens can act as a relative physical barrier that retards topically applied ophthalmic drugs from entering the vitreous cavity.18 While the crystalline lens could therefore plausibly impede cytokine diffusion from the vitreous to the aqueous, our observation that the majority of cytokines measured did not demonstrate increased aqueous levels in pseudophakic eyes does not support this mechanism. Although cytokines vary in molecular structure and size and possess inherently different binding kinetics to intraocular structures, continuous homeostatic production of cytokines would theoretically saturate binding to the crystalline lens and other intraocular structures and thereby minimise any variation in aqueous levels due to differences in cytokine binding affinity. It is now well accepted that the crystalline lens regulates oxygen tension and thereby the production of reactive oxygen species.29 In support of this, pseudophakic patients after vitrectomy have much higher oxygen tension levels in the anterior chamber than phakic patients after vitrectomy.30 This higher oxygen tension is presumed to damage trabecular meshwork cells due to reactive oxygen species and provides a mechanism for the clinical observation of increased IOP and rates of glaucoma after vitrectomy surgery.31 32 Therefore, in the absence of the crystalline lens, increased cellular stress and tissue damage from increased reactive oxygen species may possibly prompt an inflammatory response with cytokine upregulation.33 Over time, increased levels of these cytokines may in and of themselves promote further cell death and tissue injury inciting further inflammation.34

As with all prospective studies, our results should be interpreted with caution. Given the obvious confounding effects of cataract surgery on inflammation, we meticulously recorded the timing of cataract surgery before vitrectomy surgery. None of the 53 eyes that were pseudophakic had complicated cataract surgeries and the single aphakic eye had cataract surgery greater than 10 years before vitrectomy. Induced inflammation after uncomplicated cataract surgery typically subsides by 4 weeks35 and none of the pseudophakic eyes underwent cataract surgery or laser capsulotomy within 3 months of vitrectomy surgery. Despite the limitations of clinical studies, we would also like to emphasise the singular strengths of our study which include its prospective design, our established expertise with cytokine testing, the potential importance of cytokines as diagnostic and therapeutic biomarkers for retinal disease, and our novel observations regarding the crystalline lens and inflammation. Furthermore, since the majority of cytokines are known to have short half-lives in vivo and are subject to rapid degradation during sample collection, all aqueous samples were immediately placed on dry ice after collection and then rapidly processed and stored at −80°C for subsequent testing using an established and modern method which should provide confidence in our findings.

In conclusion, we report the first characterisation of 24 pro-inflammatory cytokines in the aqueous and serum of 94 patients without diabetes. This unique and timely information will be relevant in future cross-comparison studies. To our knowledge, we are the first to report that the absence of the crystalline lens is associated with higher aqueous cytokine levels. This novel observation requires independent confirmation but suggests that the crystalline lens may modulate inflammation within the eye.

On relative integral monodromy of abelian logarithms and normal functions.
Yves André

Abstract
. The relative algebraic monodromy of abelian logarithms (defined as the kernel of a map between algebraic monodromy groups attached to an abelian scheme with and without a section) was computed in [ 1 ] : under natural assumptions, this vector group turns out to be maximal. The relative integral monodromy of abelian logarithms is defined similarly as a kernel of integral monodromy groups, without taking Zariski closures. We show that if the integral monodromy of the abelian scheme is a lattice in the algebraic monodromy (which is not always the case), then the relative integral monodromy of the abelian logarithm is also a lattice in the relative algebraic monodromy. The proof uses a Hodge-theoretic interpretation of sections of abelian schemes. We also consider relative integral monodromy groups in the more general context of normal functions.

5.1.

5.1.1.
The Margulis-Starkov vanishing theorem

Let

a lattice (for instance, an arithmetic subgroup of a semisimple algebraic group over

of

.

5.1.2.

This applies to the situation
\Gamma\subset H
of §1.1. In that case,

must be of hermitian type, so that only the case when

is locally isomorphic to

For instance, the case considered in

5.3.
Vista: Integral relative monodromy of normal functions

5.3.1.

Let

Theorem 1.2.1 generalizes to this situation whenever the monodromy of
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a lattice satisfying Starkov’s condition: the relative integral monodromy of
{\bf V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a lattice in the relative algebraic monodromy.

Other examples of admissible normal functions occur in the Green-Griffiths approach to the Hodge conjecture. One starts with a smooth projective variety

of dimension

be constructed from a Lefschetz pencil of hyperplane sections of

(blowing up the axis of the pencil). Assume moreover for simplicity

10

10

otherwise one has to replace
H^{2n-1}(X_{s})(n)
by the vanishing part of the cohomology
that
H^{2n-1}(\bar{X},\mathbb{Z})=0
. This provides a normal function
\nu
,
cf.

e.g.

[
12
, §3]
11
11

11

the Green-Griffiths approach relates the algebraicity of

In this situation,

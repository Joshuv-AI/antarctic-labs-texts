Hitting time for one class of Gaussian processes
Qingsong Wang, A. A. Dorogovtsev

Abstract
In our paper, we consider Gaussian processes of the form η ( t ) = ∫ 0 1 A ( 𝟏 [ 0 ; 1 ] ) ( s ) d w ( s ) , t ∈ [ 0 ; 1 ] , \displaystyle\eta(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}A(\mathbf{1}_{[0;1]})(s)dw(s),\quad t\in[0;1], where w ⁡ ( t ) , t ≥ 0 w(t),t\geq 0 is a standard Wiener process in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} and A A is continuous linear operator in L 2 ​ ( [ 0 ; 1 ] ) L_{2}([0;1]) . For the domain 𝒟 ⊂ ℝ d \mathcal{D}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d} with smooth boundary ∂ 𝒟 \partial\mathcal{D} the following probability is considered for x ∈ 𝒟 x\in\mathcal{D} P ⁡ { ∃ τ ∈ [ 0 ; 1 ] : x + η ⁡ ( τ ) ∈ ∂ 𝒟 } . \displaystyle P\{\exists\tau\in[0;1]:x+\eta(\tau)\in\partial\mathcal{D}\}. It is well known that for Wiener process itself such hitting probabilities satisfy the parabolic boundary value problem for heat equation. Note that in general process η \eta is non-Markovian and even can be non-semi-martingale. Despite of this η \eta can be considered as an application of second quantization operator to w w . Correspondingly we obtain a representation for hitting probabilities as a series of multiple integrals with the kernel obtained from Green function for domain 𝒟 \mathcal{D} and tensor powers of operator A A .

Introduction

In this paper we consider hitting times and hitting probabilities for the special class of Gaussian random processes which are called the integrators. These processes were introduced by A. A. Dorogovtsev in
[
1
]
for the purposes related to the theory of stochastic integration and anticipating stochastic differential equations. Integrators includes Brownian motion, Brownian bridge, Ornstein- Uhlenbeck process and many other frequently used in applications processes. It was discovered in that integrators can be used to model linear polymers. Such important characteristics as localtimes of self-inersection and non-normalized local times of self-intersection also were studied for integrator. Initially integrator was defined as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

[
1
]

Centered Gaussian process
\eta(t),t\in[0;1]
is called by integrator if there exists such positive
C>0
that for arbitrary
n\geq 1
, partition
0=t_{0}<\cdots<t_{n}=1
and real numbers
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}(\eta(t_{k+1})-\eta(t_{k})))^{2}\leq C\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}^{2}\Delta t_{k}^{2}.

This condition means that for every function
f\in L_{2}([0;1])
the integral

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f(t)d\eta(t)

is well-defined as a limit in the square mean of the integrals from the step functions. Also, there is anther equivalent definition of the integrators through the Gaussian white noise in Hilbert space. This definition will be used later.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
\eta(t),t\in[0;1]
be an integrator. Then there exist a Wiener process
w(t),t\in[0;1]
and a continuous linear operator
A
in the space
L_{2}([0;1])
such that

\displaystyle\eta(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}A(\mathbf{1}_{[0;t]})(s)dw(s).

Consider a case when
\|A\|<1
. Denote

\displaystyle Q=(I-A^{*}A)^{\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\zeta(T)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}Q(\mathbf{1}_{[0;1]})(s)dw^{\prime}(s),

where
w^{\prime}
is a Wiener process independent from
w
. It can be easily checked that, the sum

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}(t)=\eta(t)+\zeta(t),\quad t\in[0;1]

is a Wiener process. Suppose that
\Phi:C([0;1])\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a bounded measurable function. Then, formally

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Phi(\eta)={\mathbb{E}}(\Phi(\widetilde{w})|\zeta=0).

Note that, the conditional expectation with respect to
\zeta
can be calculated as a second quantization operator for functionals from
\widetilde{w}
. Namely, since
\zeta
was defined as

\displaystyle\zeta(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}Q(\mathbf{1}_{[0;1]})(s)dw^{\prime}(s),

then for arbitrary
h\in L_{2}([0;1])

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}h(s)d\widetilde{w}(s)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(Ah)(s)dw(s)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(Qh)(s)dw^{\prime}(s),

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(Qh)(s)dw^{\prime}(s)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}h(s)d\zeta(s),

is measurable with respect to
\zeta
. Consequently, the conditional expectation of the stochastic exponent

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathbb{E}}(\exp\{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}h(s)d\widetilde{w}(s)-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}h^{2}(s)ds\}|\zeta)

\displaystyle=\exp\{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(Qh)(s)dw^{\prime}(s)-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(Qh)^{2}(s)ds\}

\displaystyle=\Gamma(Q)\exp\{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}h(s)dw^{\prime}(s)-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}h^{2}(s)ds\}.

Here
\Gamma(Q)
is an operator of second quantization acting in the space of square integrable functionals from
w^{\prime}

[
2
]
. Since the previous equality holds for all stochastic exponents, it remains to be ture for all square integrable functionals from
w^{\prime}
. Let
\alpha
be such functional and has an It
\hat{o}
-Wiener expansion

\displaystyle\alpha={\mathbb{E}}\alpha+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\int_{\Delta_{n}}a_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw^{\prime}(t_{1})\cdots dw^{\prime}(t_{n}).

Then

\displaystyle\Gamma(Q)\alpha={\mathbb{E}}\alpha+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\int_{\Delta_{n}}Q^{\otimes n}a_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw^{\prime}(t_{1})\cdots dw^{\prime}(t_{n}).

Calculation
\Gamma(Q)\alpha
when
\zeta=0
is the same as calculation when
w^{\prime}=0
. Suppose that the kernel
b_{n}\in L_{2}([0;1]^{n})
is symmetric. To find the value of (when it is well-defined)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Delta_{n}}b_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw^{\prime}(t_{1})\cdots dw^{\prime}(t_{n}),

let us prosed as follows. Let
\{e_{k};k\geq 1\}
be am orthonormal basis in
L_{2}([0;1])
. Denote by

\displaystyle\xi_{k}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e_{k}(s)dw^{\prime}(s),\quad k\geq 1

\displaystyle b_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}b_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})e_{k_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots e_{k_{n}}(t_{n})dt_{1}\cdots dt_{n},\quad k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}\geq 1.

Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Delta_{n}}b_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw^{\prime}(t_{1})\cdots dw^{\prime}(t_{n})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}b_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw^{\prime}(t_{1})\cdots dw^{\prime}(t_{n})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}=1}^{\infty}b_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}\xi_{k_{1}}*\cdots*\xi_{k_{n}}.

Here the multiple integral

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}b_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw^{\prime}(t_{1})\cdots dw^{\prime}(t_{n})

is defined in Skorokhod sense and

\displaystyle\xi_{k_{1}}*\cdots*\xi_{k_{n}},

is a Wick product of
\xi_{k_{1}},\cdots,\xi_{k_{n}}
. Such product is obtained from the usual
\xi_{k_{1}},\cdots,\xi_{k_{n}}
by collecting the same multiplies and substituiting the power by Hermite polynomial of the same degree. For example,

\displaystyle\xi_{1}*\xi_{2}*\xi_{3}=H_{2}(\xi_{1})\cdot\xi_{2}=(\xi^{2}_{1}-1)\xi_{2}.

Hence the value

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}b_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw^{\prime}(t_{1})\cdots dw^{\prime}(t_{n})|_{w^{\prime}=0}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}=1}^{\infty}b_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}\xi_{k_{1}}*\cdots*\xi_{k_{n}}|_{\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}=\cdots=0}.

Using the generating function for Hermite polynomials

\displaystyle\exp(xy-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}x^{2})=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{n!}H_{n}(y),

one can easily check that for
n\geq 0

\displaystyle H_{2n+1}(0)=0,\quad H_{2n}(0)=(-1)^{n}(2n-1)!!.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\xi_{k_{1}}*\cdots*\xi_{k_{n}}|_{\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}=\cdots=0}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}(i\xi_{k_{1}})\cdots(i\xi_{k_{n}}),

where
i^{2}=-1
. Hence (if the corresponding series converges and it is allowed to change expectation and infinite sum)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}b_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw^{\prime}(t_{1})\cdots dw^{\prime}(t_{n})|_{w^{\prime}=0}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}=1}^{\infty}b_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}(i\xi_{k_{1}})\cdots(i\xi_{k_{n}})

\displaystyle=i^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}b_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw^{\prime}(t_{1})\circ dw^{\prime}(t_{2})\circ\cdots\circ dw^{\prime}(t_{n}).

Where in the right hand side the Ogawa symmetric integral is used. This integral in simple cases coinsides with the Stratonowich integral. So now our strategy in the calculation of probabilities related to the integrator
\eta
is the following. Firstly, present the probability as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Phi(\eta).

Then find operator

\displaystyle Q=\sqrt{I-A^{*}A}

and

\displaystyle\Gamma(Q)\Phi(w),

as an It
\hat{o}
-Wiener series

\displaystyle\Gamma(Q)\Phi(w)=\displaystyle\sum_{0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\int_{\Delta_{n}}b_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw(t_{1})\cdots dw(t_{n}).

Finally,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Phi(\eta)={\mathbb{E}}\displaystyle\sum_{0}^{\infty}i^{n}\displaystyle\int_{\Delta_{n}}b_{n}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw(t_{1})\circ dw(t_{2})\circ\cdots\circ dw(t_{n}).

Correspondingly to such strategy the paper is divided into two sections. In the first section we consider the functionals from
w
and its second quantization, in the second section the concrete example and estimations for expectation of symmetric integrals series are given.

Existence of continuous version

In this section we establish the existence of continuous version of the functionals related to conditional expectation of
W_{x,t}
and its It
\hat{o}
-Wiener expansion.

Lemma 4.1
.

For fixed
x\in\mathcal{D},t\in[0;1]

W^{h}_{x,t}
is continuous with respect to
h\in C([0;1],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
i.e. for fixed
h_{0}\in C([0;1],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\displaystyle W^{h}_{x,t}\rightarrow W^{h_{0}}_{x,t},\quad a.s.,

\displaystyle\|h-h_{0}\|_{\infty}\rightarrow 0.

Here

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\infty}=\displaystyle\max_{[0;1]}\|h(s)\|.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{D},t\in[0;1]
and
h\in C([0;1],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be fixed. Define the stopping moment
\tau_{h}
as a first moment from the interval
[t;1)
when the process

\displaystyle x+w(r)-w(t)+\displaystyle\int_{t}^{r}h(s)ds,\quad r\in[t;1],

firstly hits
\partial\mathcal{D}
or
1
if it does not hit
\partial\mathcal{D}
or hits it exactly at the time
1
, where

\displaystyle\tau_{h}=\displaystyle\inf\{r\geq t:x+w(r)-w(t)+\displaystyle\int_{t}^{r}h(s)ds\in\partial\mathcal{D}\wedge 1\}.

Fix
h_{0}\in C([0;1],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and consider several case. Firstly, suppose that
\tau_{h_{0}}<1
. Note, that due to the strong Markov property the process

\displaystyle w(\tau+s)-w(\tau),\quad s\geq 0,

is again a standard Brownian motion in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Consequently, for all
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},u\neq 0
the scalar process

\displaystyle(w(\tau+s)-w(\tau),u),\quad s\geq 0,

satisfies the law of iterated logarithm. Then, since
\partial\mathcal{D}
is smooth and the integral from
h_{0}
also is a smooth function, with probability one the process

\displaystyle x+w(r)-w(t)+\displaystyle\int_{t}^{r}h(s)ds,\quad r\in[t;1],

visits
\mathcal{D}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\backslash\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at any small interval of the time after
\tau_{h_{0}}
. Consequently, for such
\omega
that
\tau_{h_{0}}(\omega)<1
and the law of iterated logarithm holds with the vector
u
chosen as a unit normal to
\partial\mathcal{D}
at the point

\displaystyle x+w(\tau_{h_{0}})-w(t)+\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{h_{0}}}^{t}h_{0}(s)ds,

there exists
\delta=\delta(\omega)>0
such that for such
h\in C([0;1],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for which

\displaystyle\|h-h_{0}\|_{\infty}<\delta(\omega),

\displaystyle W^{h}_{x,t}=1.

Note that

\displaystyle P\{x+w(1)-w(t)+\displaystyle\int_{t}^{1}h_{0}(s)ds\in\partial\mathcal{D}\}=0.

Consider last case when
r\in[t;1]
:

\displaystyle x+w(r)-w(t)+\displaystyle\int_{t}^{r}h_{0}(s)ds\notin\partial\mathcal{D}.

For such
\omega
there exists
\delta=\delta(\omega)>0
for which
\forall r\in[t;1]

\displaystyle\rho(x+w(r)-w(t)+\displaystyle\int_{t}^{r}h_{0}(s)ds,\partial D)>\delta,

where

there exists

that for

\displaystyle\|h-h_{0}\|_{\infty}<\delta_{1},

\displaystyle W^{h}_{x,t}=1.

Lemma is proved.
∎

\Gamma(R)w
is a Gaussian process which depends on a finite number of standard independent Gaussian variables of the kind

\displaystyle\xi^{j}_{k}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e_{k}(t)dw^{j}(t),\quad k=1,\cdots,m,\quad j=1,\cdots,d,

where the orthonormal system
\{e_{k};k=1,\cdots,m\}
used. So, due to the condition
\|R\|<1
the random variable
\Gamma(R)\alpha
is infinitely many times stochastically differentiable
[
4
]
. Consequently, due to Sobolev embedding theorem
[
4
]
,
\Gamma(R)\alpha
can be represented as a continuous functional

\displaystyle\Gamma(R)\alpha=F(\xi^{j}_{k},\quad k=1,\cdots,m,\quad j=1,\cdots,d),\quad F\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{m\times d}).

Lemma is proved.
∎

Now we can make rigorous the arguments presented in the introduction. Let
R
be a finite-dimensional operator in
L_{2}([0;1])
with the norm
\|R\|<1
. Take the continuous linear operator
A
such that for
\forall h\in L_{2}([0;1])
,

\displaystyle\|Ah\|^{2}+\|Rh\|^{2}=1.

Consider integrator
\eta
constructed with the help of
A
. The following theorem holds.

Theorem 4.4
.

For arbitrary
x\in\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle P\{\exists\tau\in[0;1]:x+\eta(\tau)\in\partial\mathcal{D}\}=\Gamma(R)W_{x,0}|_{w=0}.

Proof.

Consider, as in introduction, new standard d-dimensional process
w^{\prime}
independent from
w
and construct new integrator

\displaystyle\zeta(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}R(\mathbf{1}_{[0;t]})(s)dw^{\prime}(s).

Then
\zeta
has smooth trajectories. So, due to the lemma
, conditional expectation

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(W^{\widetilde{w}}_{x,0}|\zeta),

is a continuous function of
\zeta
. From other side, by lemma
4.3

\displaystyle\Gamma(R)W^{w^{\prime}}_{x,0},

is a continuous function of the random variables

\displaystyle\xi^{j}_{k}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e_{k}dw^{\prime j}(t),\quad k=1,\cdots,m,\quad j=1,\cdots,d.

Hence from equality

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(W^{\widetilde{w}}_{x,0}|\zeta)=\Gamma(R)W^{w^{\prime}}_{x,0},

one can conclude the equality under
\xi^{j}_{k}=0,\quad k=1,\cdots,m,\quad j=1,\cdots,d
which is the desired statement. Theorem is proved.
∎

Now use the It
\hat{o}
-Wiener expansion of
W_{x,0}
to obtain a series representation for hitting probability of integrator. Due to representation of the operator
R
the It
\hat{o}
-Wiener expansion of
\Gamma(R)W_{x,0}
can be simplified. We will do it in the case
d=1
and then give the general formula just to avoid long calculation. So, let us suppose that
d=1
and for function
a_{n}\in L_{2}([0;1])
consider

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(R^{\otimes n}a_{n})(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw(t_{1}),\cdots,dw(t_{n})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}=1}^{m}\beta_{i_{1}}\cdots\beta_{i_{n}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}a_{n}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n})

\displaystyle\cdot e_{i_{1}}(s_{1})\cdots e_{i_{n}}(s_{n})ds_{1}\cdots ds_{n}e_{i_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots e_{i_{n}}(t_{n})dw(t_{1}),\cdots,dw(t_{n}).

For further transformations recall notation

\displaystyle\xi_{k}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e_{k}(t)dw(t_{1}),\quad k=1,\cdots,m.

Then the stochastic integral can be expressed in terms of Hermite polynomials from the variables
\xi_{k},k=1,\cdots,m
. Denote for the sequence of indexes
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}
by the
r_{1},\cdots,r_{n}
the numbers which indicate how many times
1
repeats among
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}
,
2
repeats among
i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}
and so on. Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e_{i_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots e_{i_{n}}(t_{n})dw(t_{1}),\cdots,dw(t_{n})=H_{r_{1}}(\xi_{1})\cdots H_{r_{m}}(\xi_{m}).

Introduce the coefficients

\displaystyle a^{n}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{m}}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}a_{n}(t_{1}\cdots t_{n})e_{i_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots e_{i_{n}}(t_{n})dt_{1},\cdots,dt_{n}.

Finally

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(R^{\otimes n}a_{n})(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw(t_{1}),\cdots,dw(t_{n})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}=n}\displaystyle\frac{n!}{r_{1}!\cdots r_{m}!}\beta_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots\beta_{m}^{r_{m}}a^{n}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{m}}H_{r_{1}}(\xi_{1})\cdots H_{r_{m}}(\xi_{m}).

Let us calculate the second moment of the stochastic integral using the obtained representation.

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathbb{E}}(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(R^{\otimes n}a_{n})(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})dw(t_{1}),\cdots,dw(t_{n}))^{2}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}=n}\displaystyle\frac{n!^{2}}{r_{1}!\cdots r_{m}!}\beta_{1}^{2r_{1}}\cdots\beta_{m}^{2r_{m}}(a^{n}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{m}})^{2}.

Note, that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\alpha^{2}\geq\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}=n}\displaystyle\frac{n!^{2}}{r_{1}!\cdots r_{m}!}(a^{n}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{m}})^{2}.

Recall the estimation for Hermite polynomials
[
5
]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\max_{[-1;1]}|H_{n}(x)|\leq c\cdot n^{-\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}}\cdot\sqrt{n!},\quad n\geq 1.

Hence for
|\xi_{k}|\leq 1,k=1,\cdots,m

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}=n}\displaystyle\frac{n!}{r_{1}!\cdots r_{m}!}|\beta_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots\beta_{m}^{r_{m}}||a^{n}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{m}}||H_{r_{1}}(\xi_{1})\cdots H_{r_{m}}(\xi_{m})|

\displaystyle\leq c^{m}\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}=n}\displaystyle\frac{n!}{\sqrt{r_{1}!\cdots r_{m}!}}|a^{n}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{m}}||\beta_{1}|^{r_{1}}\cdots|\beta_{m}|^{r_{m}}

\displaystyle\leq c^{m}({\mathbb{E}}\alpha^{2})^{\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}}\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}=n}|\beta_{1}|^{r_{1}}\cdots|\beta_{m}|^{r_{m}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}=n}|\beta_{1}|^{r_{1}}\cdots|\beta_{m}|^{r_{m}}

\displaystyle=(\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}=0}^{\infty}|\beta_{1}|^{r_{1}})\cdots(\displaystyle\sum_{r_{m}=0}^{\infty}|\beta_{m}|^{r_{m}})<+\infty.

Consequently It
\hat{o}
-Wiener series for
\Gamma(R)\alpha
now converges uniformly on the set
|\xi_{k}|\leq 1,k=1,\cdots,m
. Hence

\displaystyle\Gamma(R)\alpha|_{w=0}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}=n}\displaystyle\frac{n!}{r_{1}!\cdots r_{m}!}a^{n}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{m}}H_{r_{1}}(0)\cdots H_{r_{m}}(0).

Now we can formulate the main statement of the paper. Consider operator
R
as above. Suppose that the kernels
g^{n}_{x}\in L_{2}([0;1]^{n},{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times n})
are obtained from the It
\hat{o}
-Wiener expansion for
W_{x,0}
. Let
\eta
be the integrator based on the operator

such that, for

\displaystyle\|Ah\|^{2}+\|Rh\|^{2}=\|h\|^{2}.

Denote by

\displaystyle g^{n}_{x,R}=R^{\otimes d\times n}g^{n}_{x}.

Then
g^{n}_{x,R}
is a kernel from

. More over,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\cdots}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(g^{n}_{x,R}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}),dw(t_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes dw(t_{n}))

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{d}=n}\displaystyle\sum_{j^{1}_{1}+\cdots+j^{1}_{m}=k_{1}}\cdots\displaystyle\sum_{j^{d}_{1}+\cdots+j^{d}_{m}=k_{d}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{n!}{j^{1}_{1}!\cdots j^{1}_{m}!\cdots j^{d}_{1}!\cdots j^{d}_{m}!}\alpha_{j^{1}_{1}\cdots j^{1}_{m}\cdots j^{d}_{1}\cdots j^{d}_{m}}H_{j^{1}_{1}}(\xi_{1}^{1})\cdots H_{j^{1}_{m}}(\xi_{1}^{m})\cdots H_{j^{d}_{1}}(\xi_{d}^{1})\cdots H_{j^{d}_{m}}(\xi_{d}^{m}),

\displaystyle\xi^{j}_{k}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}e_{k}dw^{j}(t),\quad k=1,\cdots,m,\quad j=1,\cdots,d.

Finally, the following statement holds.

Theorem 4.5

\displaystyle P\{\exists\tau\in[0;1]:x+\eta(\tau)\in\partial\mathcal{D}\}

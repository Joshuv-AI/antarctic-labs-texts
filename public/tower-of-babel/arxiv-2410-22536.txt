Which Meyer sets are regular model sets? A characterization via almost periodicity
Daniel Lenz, Christoph Richard, Nicolae Strungaru

Abstract
. In 2012, Meyer introduced the notions of generalized almost periodic measure and almost periodic pattern and proved that regular model sets in Euclidean space are almost periodic patterns. Here, we prove the converse in a slightly more general setting. Specifically, we show that a Meyer set in any σ \sigma -compact locally compact abelian group is a regular model set if and only if it is an almost periodic pattern.



1.
Introduction

This article is concerned with aperiodic order as exposed e.g. in the article collections and monographs
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
10
,
20
]
. The arguably most important examples for aperiodic order are regular model sets. These sets were introduced and studied by Meyer in the seventies of the last century in a harmonic analysis context
[
18
]
. Their rôle in aperiodic order was established by Moody
[
21
]
, and fundamental geometric features were then shown by Lagarias
[
11
]
.

A central problem in the field is to capture aperiodic order via notions of almost periodicity, see e.g. the influential article by Lagarias
[
12
]
. Here we solve this problem for regular model sets, by showing that they can be characterized via generalized almost periodicity. This particular type of almost periodicity was brought forward by Meyer exactly to serve as an appropriate notion of almost periodicity for aperiodic order
[
19
]
. Loosely spoken, a discrete point set is generalized almost periodic if it can be approximated by almost periodic measures from above and from below, up to deviations of arbitrarily small mean. Meyer then showed that regular model sets are generalized almost periodic, and he calls such points sets almost periodic patterns.
Our main result Theorem
4.5
shows that the converse is also true within the class of Meyer sets. It also provides a slight strengthening of Meyer’s original result in that it shows that almost periodic patterns agree with almost periodic measures up to sets of arbitrary small density. This formalizes a viewpoint sometimes expressed by physicists that regular model sets are almost periodic, up to a small error.

Our main result
is a consequence of a more general result, Theorem
4.1
, which characterises generalized-almost-periodic measures with Meyer set support as being weighted model sets with Riemann integrable weight functions.
The proof of that theorem in turn relies on two ingredients: We first re-analyse the construction of regular model sets based on cut-and-project schemes. In this context, we show existence of special cut-and-project schemes, called
universal
below, which allow one to lift Bohr almost periodic functions on the real space to the internal space. As we show, they also allow one to lift strongly almost periodic measures supported within a given Meyer set.
The second ingredient is a certain approximation operator
\mathcal{T}
acting on measures. This operator smoothes a measure and restricts its support to a given Meyer set.
On a conceptual level, the introduction and investigation of
universal
cut-and-project schemes and the operator
\mathcal{T}
can be seen as the key achievements of this work.

As far as applications to aperiodic order are concerned, we could have restricted attention to Euclidean space. However we formulate our arguments in the slightly more general framework of
\sigma
-compact locally compact abelian groups. We feel that this makes the arguments more transparent. Moreover, let us emphasize here that our arguments solely rely on Bohr almost periodic functions, which makes locally compact abelian groups a natural setting.
We impose
\sigma
-compactness for the reader’s convenience, as this allows one to compute densities of point sets along sequences of averaging sets that are called van Hove.
However, all our statements and their proofs below hold for general locally compact abelian groups, with “van Hove sequence” being replaced by “van Hove net”
[
23
]
.

Our results complement recent investigations
[
16
]

to characterize pure point diffraction (which is a key signature of a aperiodic order) via certain types of almost periodicity. Specifically, in
[
16
]
the notions of Weyl almost periodic measure and Besicovitch almost periodic measure are put forward and the implications

generalized almost periodicity

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

Weyl almost periodicity

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

Besicovitch almost periodity

are established. The main results of
[
16
]
can be understood as saying that Besicovitch almost periodic measures are exactly the measures for which a convincing diffraction theory can be set up along a fixed van Hove sequence, and Weyl almost periodic measures are exactly the measures for which a convincing diffraction theory can be set up along any van Hove sequence. In this sense
[
16
]
gives a complete picture for the rôles of Weyl- and Besicovitch almost periodicity in aperiodic order. The rôle of generalized almost periodicity in aperiodic order is now established by the main results of this article: it is exactly the form of almost periodicity present in the key class of examples.

Our article is organized as follows: The setting and basic notions are explained in Section
2
. The core of our argument is presented in Section
3
, which features the notion of
universal
cut-and-project schemes and the operator
{\mathcal{T}}
mentioned above. Our main results are then derived in Section
4
, and (counter)examples are discussed in Section
5
.
As we believe that the concept of
universal
cut-and-project scheme may be of independent interest, we include a further study of it in Section
6
.

2.
Setting and key players

We are concerned with point sets and almost periodicity in a locally compact abelian group. Here we collect the necessary background for our considerations. The material of this section is well-known. For early references see e.g.
[
18
,
8
]
. More references will be given along the way.

2.1.
Functions, measures, and sets

Whenever
G
is a locally compact abelian group, we denote a Haar measure on
G
by
\theta_{G}
or by
|\cdot|
. We denote integration with respect to that Haar measure by
\int\,{\rm d}s
or by
\int\,{\rm d}t
.

The vector space of continuous functions from
G
to
{\mathbb{C}}
is denoted by
C(G)
, the subspace of bounded continuous functions is denoted by
C_{\mathsf{b}}(G)
. The latter space is complete with respect to the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. The closed subspace of uniformly continuous and bounded functions is denoted by
C_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
,
the subspace of continuous and compactly supported functions is denoted by
C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
. A real-valued function
f
on
G
is
Riemann integrable
if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exist
g,h\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
with
g\leq f\leq h
and
\int_{G}(h(s)-g(s))\,{\rm d}s\leq\varepsilon
.
A complex-valued function is Riemann integrable if both its real part and its imaginary part are Riemann integrable.
We denote the space of complex-valued Riemann integrable functions on
G
by
{R}(G)
.

Any linear functional on
C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
which is continuous in the inductive topology on
C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
can be identified with a complex Radon measure
\mu
on
G
via the Riesz representation theorem
[
7
]
. We will simply call such
\mu
a
measure
. A measure
\mu
is
supported
within the closed set
C\subset G
if
\mu(\varphi)=0
for all
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
with
\varphi=0
on
G\setminus C
.
Consider
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
and a measure
\mu
. We mostly write
\int\varphi\,{\rm d}\mu
or
\int\varphi(t)\,{\rm d}\mu(t)
instead of
\mu(\varphi)
.
The
convolution

\varphi\ast\mu
of
\varphi
and
\mu
is the function on
G
given by

(\varphi\ast\mu)(x)=\int_{G}\varphi(x-t)\,{\rm d}\mu(t)

for
x\in G
. A measure
\mu
on
G
is
translation bounded
if

\varphi*\mu
is bounded for all
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
.
The vector space of all translation bounded measures on
G
is denoted by
{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
.

Any subset
\varLambda
of
G
meeting any compact set in only finitely many points comes with a measure, called the
Dirac comb
(of
\varLambda
), defined by

\delta_{\varLambda}=\sum_{x\in\varLambda}\delta_{x}\ .

Clearly,
\varLambda
can be recovered from
\delta_{\varLambda}
. Hence, this construction allows one to think of such point sets also as measures.

In this article, we are mainly interested in subsets of
G
that are both uniformly discrete and relatively dense. Here,
\varLambda\subset G
is called
uniformly discrete
if there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
0
in
G
such that
(s+U)\cap(t+U)=\emptyset
for all
s,t\in\varLambda
with
t\neq s
. Moreover,
\varLambda\subset G
is called
relatively dense
if there exists a compact
K\subset G
with
\varLambda+K=G
. We write
A+B
to denote the pointwise sum
\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}
of two subsets
A,B
of
G
.

We now fix a
\sigma
-compact, locally compact abelian group
G
for the remainder of the article.

2.2.
Almost periodic measures and their mean

We recall background on almost periodic functions and measures.
Let us denote by
\tau_{x}
the operator of translation by
x\in G
, which maps a function
f
on
G
to the function
\tau_{x}f
given by
(\tau_{x}f)(t)=f(t-x)
for
t\in G
.
A function
f\in C_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
is
Bohr almost periodic
if for every
\varepsilon>0
the set of its
\varepsilon
-almost periods

P_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in G:\|f-\tau_{x}f\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\}

is relatively dense in
G
. The vector space of Bohr almost periodic functions on
G
is denoted by
SAP(G)
. It is a function algebra with respect to pointwise multiplication that is closed in
(C_{\mathsf{u}}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
.

There exists a unique compact group
G_{\mathsf{b}}
admitting an injective group homomorphism
i:G\longrightarrow G_{\mathsf{b}}
with dense range such that
SAP(G)=\{f_{\mathsf{b}}\circ i:f_{\mathsf{b}}\in C(G_{\mathsf{b}})\}
.
It is called the
Bohr compactification
of
G
. Define
M:SAP(G)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
by
M(f)=\theta_{G_{\mathsf{b}}}(f_{\mathsf{b}}\circ i)
, where
f=f_{\mathsf{b}}\circ i\in SAP(G)
and where
\theta_{G_{\mathsf{b}}}
is the normalised Haar measure on
G_{\mathsf{b}}
. Then
M
is positive linear and satisfies
M(1)=1
and
M(\tau_{x}f)=M(f)
for any
x\in G
and
f\in SAP(G)
. In fact the latter properties uniquely determine
M
, as follows from the Riesz representation theorem for
G_{\mathsf{b}}
. The map
M
is called the
mean
on
SAP(G)
, and we call
M(f)
the mean of
f
.

The mean of a Bohr almost periodic function can be computed by an averaging procedure: If
(A_{n})
is any Følner sequence in
G
, we have

M(f)=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{x+A_{n}}f(t)\,{\rm d}t\ ,

uniformly in
x\in G
. This is a consequence of Eberlein’s ergodic theorem
[
6
]
.
Recall that a
Følner sequence
in
G
consists of compact subsets
(A_{n})
of positive Haar measure such that

|A_{n}\triangle(x+A_{n})|=o(|A_{n}|)
as
n\to\infty
, for any
x\in G
.

A translation bounded measure
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
is
strongly almost periodic
if the function
\varphi\ast\mu
is Bohr almost periodic for any
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
. The vector space of all strongly almost periodic measures is denoted by
\mathcal{SAP}(G)
.
The mean on
SAP(G)
carries over to a map on
\mathcal{SAP}(G)
, again denoted by
M
and called the
mean
on
\mathcal{SAP}(G)
. Indeed, the following holds as discussed in
[
22
, Lem. 4.10.6]
. For the convenience of the reader we include the short proof.

Lemma 2.1

(The mean on
\mathcal{SAP}(G)
)
.

There exists a unique linear map
M:\mathcal{SAP}(G)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
such that

M(\varphi*\mu)=\theta_{G}(\varphi)\cdot M(\mu)

for all
\mu\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
and
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
. The map
M
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

M
is positive.

(b)

M(\theta_{G})=1
.

(c)

M(\mu\circ\tau_{x})=M(\mu)
for any
x\in G
and
\mu\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
.

Proof.

Uniqueness is clear. As for existence we note that for positive
\mu
the map

C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}\ ,\qquad\varphi\mapsto M(\varphi\ast\mu)\ ,

is positive and invariant under translations. Hence it must be a multiple of the Haar measure. So we find a unique number
M_{\mu}
with
M(\varphi\ast\mu)=\theta_{G}(\varphi)\cdot M_{\mu}
for all
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
. Now, the statement follows easily for general
\mu
by taking linear combinations. The properties (a), (b), (c) are now clear.
∎

Similarly to
SAP(G)
, it is possible to compute the mean on
\mathcal{SAP}(G)
by an averaging procedure. This procedure involves special Følner sequences
(A_{n})
in
G
introduced by Schlottmann
[
26
]
that are called
van Hove sequences
. Their characteristic feature is that

\lim_{n}\frac{\mu(\partial^{K}A_{n})}{|A_{n}|}=0

holds for any compact
K
and any translation bounded measure
\mu
, where the
K
-boundary

\partial^{K}A
is defined by

\partial^{K}A=((A+K)\cap(G\setminus A^{\circ}))\cup((-K+\overline{(G\setminus A)})\cap A)\ .

Note that a compact set
A
has a compact
K
-boundary.

Remark 2.2
.

(a)
In Euclidean space, any sequence
(A_{n})
of compact balls of radius
n
constitutes a van Hove sequence.

(b) Existence of van Hove sequences for the
G
treated in this article was shown in
[
26
]
. In fact, any Følner sequence yields a van Hove sequence by a simple thickening procedure
[
23
]
.

We have the following result
[
22
, Lem. 4.10.7]
.

Proposition 2.3

(Computing the mean via averaging)
.

Let
(A_{n})
be any van Hove sequence in
G
. We then have for all
\mu\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
that

M(\mu)=\lim_{n}\frac{\mu(A_{n})}{|A_{n}|}\ .

Furthermore, this limit exists uniformly in translates. ∎

Having discussed almost periodic measures and their mean we can now define generalized almost periodicity. This notion has been introduced by Meyer for translation bounded measures on Euclidean space
[
19
, Def. 2.31]
. The extension to
\sigma
-compact locally compact abelian groups has been discussed in
[
16
]
.

Definition 2.4

(Generalized almost periodicity and almost periodic patterns)
.

(a)

A real measure
\varrho\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
is called
generalized-almost-periodic (g-a-p)
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exist
\mu_{\varepsilon},\nu_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
such that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq\varrho\leq\nu_{\varepsilon}
and
M(\nu_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon
. A complex measure
\varrho\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
is called
generalized-almost-periodic (g-a-p)
if both its real and imaginary parts are g-a-p. The vector space of complex g-a-p measures is denoted by
\mathcal{GAP}(G)
.

(b)

A point set
\varLambda
is called an
almost periodic pattern
if its Dirac comb
\delta_{\varLambda}
is a generalized-almost-periodic measure.

Remark 2.5
.

(a) We note that
\varrho\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty}(G)
is g-a-p if and only if it is a linear combination of positive g-a-p measures. (Observe that, if
\varrho
is real and g-a-p, there exist strongly almost periodic
\mu,\nu
such that
\mu\leq\varrho\leq\nu
. Then

\varrho=\frac{1}{2}\left((\varrho-\mu)-(\nu-\varrho)+(\nu-\mu)\right)

is a linear combination of positive g-a-p measures.)

(b) It is not hard to see that the mean
M
on
\mathcal{SAP}(G)
can be extended to
\mathcal{GAP}(G)
. As we do not need this we refrain from further discussion.

2.3.
Meyer sets, cut-and-project schemes, and the map
\varOmega

Here we discuss the particular point sets most relevant to the study of aperiodic order.

Definition 2.6

(Meyer set)
.

A uniformly discrete and relatively dense set
\varLambda
in
G
is called a
Meyer set
if there exists a finite set
F
in
G
with
\varLambda-\varLambda\subset\varLambda+F
.

Remark 2.7
.

(a) Meyer sets were introduced and intensely studied by Meyer
[
18
]
. They were later called Meyer sets and placed in a central position in aperiodic order by Moody in his article
[
21
]
whose terminology we adopt here.

(b) A relatively dense set
\varLambda
is a Meyer set if and only if
\varLambda-\varLambda-\varLambda
is uniformly discrete, see
[
18
, Ch. II 14.2]
and
[
27
, Lem. 5.7.1]
. In Euclidean space, it has been shown by Lagarias that a relatively dense set
\varLambda
is a Meyer set if and only if
\varLambda-\varLambda
is uniformly discrete
[
11
]
. As shown by Lev and Olevskii, the latter condition can be weakened to
\varLambda-\varLambda
having finite uniform upper density
[
17
]
. Lagarias’ characterisation extends to compactly generated locally compact abelian groups
[
3
]
.

Meyer gave a constructive description of all Meyer sets, which is based on the notion of cut-and-project scheme.

Definition 2.8

(Cut-and-project scheme over
G
)
.

A
cut-and-project scheme
(H,{\mathcal{L}})
over
G
consists of a locally compact abelian group
H
and a discrete co-compact subgroup
{\mathcal{L}}\subset G\times H
, called the
lattice
, such that

•

the canonical projection

\pi^{G}:{\mathcal{L}}\longrightarrow G,(x,y)\mapsto x,
is one-to-one;

•

the canonical projection

\pi^{H}:{\mathcal{L}}\longrightarrow H,(x,y)\mapsto y,

has dense range.

When working with a cut-and-project scheme
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
over
G
, we will often abbreviate
L=\pi^{G}({\mathcal{L}})
. Any cut-and-project scheme comes with a canonical map on
L
called
star map
given by

\star:L\to H,\qquad x\mapsto x^{\star},

where for
x\in L
the point

x^{\star}\in H
is the unique element of
H
such that
(x,x^{\star})\in{\mathcal{L}}
. (Here, uniqueness follows from injectivity of
\pi^{G}
on
{\mathcal{L}}
.) To any
W\subset H
we can then associate the
cut-and-project set

\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}_{S}(W)=\{x\in L:x^{\star}\in W\}\ .

Whenever the cut-and-project scheme
S
is clear from context, we will simply write
\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)
instead of
\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}_{S}(W)
. By standard theory,
\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)
is uniformly discrete if
\overline{W}
(the closure of
W
) is compact, and
\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)
is relatively dense if
W^{\circ}
(the interior of
W
) is not empty. We call
W
the
window
underlying
\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}_{S}(W)
. A point set
\varLambda
in
G
is called a
model set
if there exist a cut-and-project scheme
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
over
G
and
W\subset H
with non-empty interior and compact closure such that
\varLambda=\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)
holds. For us the following strengthening of the concept of model set will be crucial.

Definition 2.9

(Regular model set)
.

A point set
\varLambda
in
G
is a
regular model set
if there exist a cut-and-project scheme
(H,{\mathcal{L}})
over
G
and
W\subset H
with non-empty interior and compact closure whose
topological boundary
\partial W
has zero Haar measure
such that
\varLambda=\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)
holds. Such
W
is then called a regular window.

The following result readily derives from Meyer’s work on harmonious sets
[
18
]
. A proof based on almost periodicity is given in
[
27
]
. A related result about constructing a cut-and-project scheme out of suitable diffraction data appears in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 2.10

(Meyer’s characterization)
.

Let
\varLambda
be a relatively dense set in
G
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

\varLambda
is a Meyer set.

(ii)

\varLambda
is a subset of a model set.

(iii)

\varLambda
is a subset of a regular model set.

∎

The construction of point sets out of cut-and-project sets via
\curlywedge
can be generalized to give – so to speak – weighted point sets. To make this precise we think of point sets as measures by invoking Dirac combs. Specifically, if
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
is some cut-and-project scheme over
G
, then any point set
\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)
in
G
with a window
W
having compact closure gives rise to the translation bounded measure

\delta_{\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)}=\sum_{x\in L}1_{W}(x^{\star})\delta_{x}\ .

This is a special case of the map

\varOmega=\varOmega_{S}
from bounded functions on
H
vanishing outside some compact set to the translation bounded measures on
G
defined by

\varOmega(h)=\sum_{x\in L}h(x^{\star})\delta_{x}\ .

Indeed, for
h:H\to{\mathbb{C}}
bounded by a finite number
C
and vanishing outside of a compact
K\subset H
, the measure
\varOmega(h)
is a point measure vanishing outside the uniformly discrete set
\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(K)
, and the mass of a single point of
\varOmega(h)
is bounded by
C
, whence
\varOmega(h)
is translation bounded. For relatively compact
W\subset H
we then find
\delta_{\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)}=\varOmega(1_{W})
.

This construction produces many strongly almost periodic measures
[
15
, Lem. 7.3]
.

Proposition 2.11

(Strong almost periodicity of
\varOmega(h)
)
.

Let
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
be a cut-and-project scheme over
G
with associated map
\varOmega
. Then
\varOmega(h)\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
for any
h\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
. ∎

The mean of such a strongly almost periodic measure can be computed using a similar approach as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
. This gives the following result, see
[
15
, Thm. 9.1]
for a proof.

Lemma 2.12

(Computing the mean of
\varOmega(h)
)
.

Let
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
be a cut-and-project scheme over
G
with associated map
\varOmega
. Then
there exists a constant
D_{S}>0
such that

(2.1)

M(\varOmega(h))=D_{S}\cdot\int_{H}h\,{\rm d}\theta_{H}

for all
h\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
.
∎

Remark 2.13

(Density formulae)
.

The positive constant
D_{S}
in the lemma can be understood as the density of the lattice
{\mathcal{L}}
in
G\times H
and the above equation is sometimes called the density formula for
\varOmega(h)
. Density formulae for model sets date back to the early works of Meyer and have been re-derived and extended using a variety of methods such as harmonic analysis, dynamical systems and almost periodicity. A discussion of the literature and a recent geometric proof can be found in
[
23
]
.

The previous lemma can be used to infer generalized almost periodicity of
\varOmega(h)
for Riemann integrable weight functions
h
:

Proposition 2.14

(Generalized almost periodicity of
\varOmega(h)
)
.

Let
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
be a cut-and-project scheme over
G
with associated map
\varOmega
. Then
\varOmega(h)\in\mathcal{GAP}(G)
for any
h\in R(H)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we assume that
h
is real-valued.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Since
h:H\to\mathbb{R}
is Riemann integrable, there exist
f,g\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
such that
f\leq h\leq g
and
\int_{H}(g-f)\,\,{\rm d}\theta_{H}\leq\varepsilon
.
Define
\mu=\varOmega(f)
and
\nu=\varOmega(g)
.
Then,
\nu
and
\mu
belong to
\mathcal{SAP}(G)
by Proposition
2.11
. Moreover, by construction we have

\mu\leq\varrho\leq\nu
. From Lemma
2.12

we now find

M(\nu-\mu)=D_{S}\cdot\int_{H}(g-f)\,{\rm d}\theta_{H}\leq D_{S}\cdot\varepsilon\ .

As
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this shows that
\varOmega(h)
is a g-a-p measure.
∎

Remark 2.15
.

It is not hard to see that the density formula Eqn. (
2.1
) continues to hold for the mean of the g-a-p measure
\varOmega(h)
for Riemann integrable
h
. Moreover, the mean exists uniformly in translates. This extends the particular case
h=1_{W}
being the characteristic function of a regular window
W
. This case is treated in
[
19
, Cor. 4.3]
and motivated the definition of generalized almost periodicity.

3.
The core of the argument

In this section we present the core of our argument to characterize g-a-p measures of Meyer set support.
The two main ingredients are a certain cut-and-project scheme, which we call
universal
cut-and-project scheme, and a certain operator on measures, denoted
{\mathcal{T}}
below.

Definition 3.1

(The
universal
cut-and-project scheme)
.

A cut-and-project scheme
(H,{\mathcal{L}})
over
G
is called
universal
if
there exists a map

{}^{\sharp}:SAP(G)\to C_{\mathsf{b}}(H)\text{ with }f(x)=f^{\sharp}(x^{\star})\text{ for all }x\in L\ .

Remark 3.2
.

A
universal
cut-and-project scheme satisfies a certain uniqueness property and a certain factorisation property. Moreover, the function
f^{\sharp}
belongs even to
SAP(H)
for any
f\in SAP(G)
. These features are not relevant for the proof of our main result. Thus, we only prove them later, together with various further characterizations in Section
6
.

We note that any
universal
cut-and-project scheme has an injective star map,
since the Bohr almost periodic functions on
G
separate the points of
G
. This property will be important below.

For us the following feature of a
universal
cut-and-project scheme
(H,{\mathcal{L}})
will be most relevant: For any
h\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
and any
f\in SAP(G)
we have

f\cdot\varOmega(h)=\varOmega(f^{\sharp}\cdot h)\ .

Indeed, both sides are point measures with point masses on the points
x\in L
given by

f(x)\cdot h(x^{\star})=f^{\sharp}(x^{\star})\cdot h(x^{\star})
. Whenever
(H,{\mathcal{L}})
is a
universal
cut-and-project scheme and
h\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
and
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
are given, we can then define the operator

{\mathcal{T}}:{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)\to{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)\ ,\qquad{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)=(\varphi\ast\mu)\cdot\varOmega(h)\ .

Then, for any strongly almost periodic measure
\mu
the function
\varphi\ast\mu
is Bohr almost periodic and we obtain

{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)=(\varphi\ast\mu)\cdot\varOmega(h)=\varOmega((\varphi\ast\mu)^{\sharp}\cdot h)\ .

Hence,
{\mathcal{T}}
maps
\mathcal{SAP}(G)
into
\varOmega(C_{\mathsf{c}}(H))
. Moreover, for suitable
\varphi
and
h
the operator
{\mathcal{T}}
will fix all measures supported on a given Meyer set. These properties of
{\mathcal{T}}
will be a key to our reasoning below.

The next proposition settles the existence of a
universal
cut-and-project scheme. For a fixed cut-and-project scheme
S
over
G
, we say that a subset of
G
is
associated to
S
if it is contained in a model set from
S
. Clearly, a subset
\varLambda
of
G
is associated to
S
if and only if there exists an
h\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
with
\delta_{\varLambda}\leq\varOmega(h)
.
Denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}(G)\subset{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
the collection of translation bounded measures whose support is associated to
S
.

We will show that to any cut-and-project scheme there exists a
universal
cut-and-project scheme with the same associated Meyer sets. To ease the formulation we call two cut-and-project schemes over
G

compatible
if they have the same associated sets. Equivalently, two cut-and-project schemes
S
and
S^{\prime}
over
G
are compatible if
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}(G)={\mathcal{M}}_{S^{\prime}}(G)
holds.

Proposition 3.3

(Existence of a compatible
universal
cut-and-project scheme)
.

To any cut-and-project scheme there exists a compatible
universal
cut-and-project scheme. In particular, for any Meyer set there exists a
universal
cut-and-project scheme to which it is associated.

Proof.

Let
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
be any cut-and-project scheme over
G
with
L=\pi^{G}({\mathcal{L}})
. We define a cut-and-project scheme
S_{\mathsf{u}}=(H_{\mathsf{u}},{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathsf{u}})
using the Bohr compactification
G_{\mathsf{b}}
of
G
as follows:

H_{\mathsf{u}}=\overline{\{(x^{\star},i(x)):x\in L\}}\subset H\times G_{\mathsf{b}}\ ,

where
i:G\to G_{\mathsf{b}}
is the canonical injection map.
We define
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathsf{u}}\subset G\times H_{\mathsf{u}}
by
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathsf{u}}=\{(x,x^{\star},i(x)):x\in L\}
. Then,

S_{\mathsf{u}}=(H_{\mathsf{u}},{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathsf{u}})
is a cut-and-project scheme over
G
(compare

[
14
, Lem. 4]
as well).

S
and
S_{\mathsf{u}}
are compatible:

Let
\varLambda
be any set associated to
S
. Then, from
\delta_{\varLambda}\leq\varOmega_{S}(h)
for some
h\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
we easily find
\delta_{\varLambda}\leq\varOmega_{S_{\mathsf{u}}}(h\otimes 1)
. Conversely, for any set
\varLambda
associated to
S_{\mathsf{u}}
there exists an
h_{\mathsf{u}}\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H_{\mathsf{u}})
with
\delta_{\varLambda}\leq\varOmega_{S_{\mathsf{u}}}(h_{\mathsf{u}})
. Then, there exists a compact set
K
in
H
such that
h_{\mathsf{u}}
is supported in
(K\times G_{\mathsf{b}})\cap H_{\mathsf{u}}
. It follows that
\delta_{\varLambda}\leq\varOmega_{S}(h)
for any
h\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
with
h=1
on
K
.

S_{\mathsf{u}}
is a
universal
cut-and-project scheme
: Clearly, the map

()^{\sharp}:SAP(G)\longrightarrow C_{\mathsf{u}}(H_{\mathsf{u}})\ ,\quad f\mapsto(1\otimes f_{\mathsf{b}})|_{H_{\mathsf{u}}}

has the desired properties. Here
|_{H_{\mathsf{u}}}
denotes restriction to
H_{\mathsf{u}}
.

The “in particular” statement now follows with Theorem
2.10
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The cut-and-project scheme appearing in the preceding proof has already been used in the different context of deformed model sets
[
14
]
.

We next come to the operator
{\mathcal{T}}
acting on measures. It produces a measure supported within a given fixed Meyer set via smoothing.

Proposition 3.5

(The operator
{\mathcal{T}}
)
.

Let
\varLambda
be a Meyer set in
G
. Take
\omega\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
and a Meyer set
\varGamma
in
G
be such that
\delta_{\varLambda}\leq\omega\leq\delta_{\varGamma}
. Fix one
\psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
with
0\leq\psi\leq 1
and
\psi(0)=1
, such that any translate of
\mbox{\rm supp}(\psi)
meets
\varGamma
in at most one point.
Define the operator
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{T}}_{\psi,\omega}:{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)\to{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
via

{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)=(\psi\ast\mu)\cdot\omega\,.

Then the following hold.

(a)

{\mathcal{T}}
is linear and positive.

(b)

For all
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
we have
\mbox{\rm supp}({\mathcal{T}}(\mu))\subset\varGamma
.

(c)

{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)=\mu
for all
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
supported within
\varLambda
.

(d)

{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{SAP}(G))\subset\mathcal{SAP}(G)
.

(e)

For any translation bounded non-negative measure
\mu
and any compact
A
the inequality
{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)(A)\leq\mu(A-K)
holds, where
K=\mbox{\rm supp}(\psi)
.

(f)

For all non-negative
\mu\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
we have
M({\mathcal{T}}(\mu))\leq M(\mu)
.

Remark 3.6
.

It is not hard to see that to any Meyer set
\varLambda
a measure
\omega
with the claimed properties exists.
An explicit construction will be given in the proof of our next lemma.

Proof.

(a) and (b) are obvious.

(c) Consider arbitrary
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
supported within
\varLambda
. Note first
\mbox{\rm supp}({\mathcal{T}}(\mu))\subset\varGamma
as
\mbox{\rm supp}(\omega)\subset\varGamma
.
But for any
x\in\varGamma
we have

\left((\psi*\mu)\cdot\omega\right)(\{x\})=(\psi*\mu)(x)\cdot\omega(\{x\})=\omega(\{x\})\sum_{y\in\varLambda}\psi(x-y)\mu(\{y\})\,.

For
y\in\varGamma
we have
\psi(x-y)=0
if
y\neq x
by choice of
\psi
, which shows

\left((\psi*\mu)\cdot\omega\right)(\{x\})=\omega(\{x\})\psi(0)\mu(\{x\})=\omega(\{x\})\mu(\{x\})=\mu(\{x\})\ .

Here the last equality holds as
\mu(\{x\})\neq 0
implies
x\in\varLambda
and
\omega(\{x\})=1
. We thus have
{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)=\mu
.

(d) We have
\omega\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
and
\psi*\mu\in SAP(G)
as
\mu\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
. Then
{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
by
[
8
, Thm. 6.1]
.

(e)
For any compact
A\subset G
, we use non-negativity of
\mu,\psi,\omega
and the inequality
\omega\leq\delta_{\Gamma}
in order to estimate

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\varGamma\cap A}(\psi*\mu)(x)\cdot\omega(\{x\})\leq\sum_{x\in\varGamma\cap A}(\psi*\mu)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\varGamma\cap A}\int_{G}\psi(x-y)\,{\rm d}\mu(y)=\int_{G}\sum_{x\in\varGamma\cap A}\psi(x-y)\,{\rm d}\mu(y)\ .

Note that
x\in A
and
\psi(x-y)\neq 0
imply
y\in A-K
. We thus have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)(A)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\sum_{x\in\varGamma\cap A}\psi(x-y)\,{\rm d}\mu(y)=\int_{G}\sum_{x\in\varGamma\cap A}1_{A-K}(y)\psi(x-y)\,{\rm d}\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}1_{A-K}(y)\sum_{x\in\varGamma\cap A}\psi(x-y)\,{\rm d}\mu(y)\leq\int_{G}1_{A-K}(y)\left(\sum_{x\in\varGamma}\psi(x-y)\right)\,{\rm d}\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}1_{A-K}(y)\,{\rm d}\mu(y)=\mu(A-K)\ .

(f)
Let
(A_{n})
be a van Hove sequence in
G
. Due to the elementary estimate
A-K\subset A+\partial^{-K}A
we then have for all
\mu\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)

\displaystyle M({\mathcal{T}}(\mu))

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)(A_{n})\leq\limsup_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\mu(A_{n}-K)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\left(\mu(A_{n})+\mu(\partial^{-K}A_{n})\right)=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\mu(A_{n})=M(\mu)\ .

This finishes the proof.
∎

Based on our study of
{\mathcal{T}}
and the concept of
universal
cut-and-project scheme, we can now state and prove the following approximation lemma. It will be a key argument in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Lemma 3.7

(Approximation in a compatible
universal
cut-and-project scheme)
.

Let
\varLambda
be a Meyer set in
G
and let
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
be a
universal
cut-and-project scheme over
G
such that
\varLambda
is associated to
S
. Then, for any
\varrho\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
supported within
\varLambda
the following hold.

(a)

Assume
\varrho\leq\nu
for some
\nu\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
. Then there exists
g\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
such that

\varrho\leq\varOmega(g)
and
M(\varOmega(g))\leq M(\nu)
.

(b)

Assume
\varrho\geq\mu
for some
\mu\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
. Then there exists
f\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
such that
\varrho\geq\varOmega(f)
and
M(\varOmega(f))\geq M(\mu)
.

Proof.

(a) As
\varLambda
is a Meyer set, we find
W\subset H
with non-empty interior and compact closure such that
\delta_{\varLambda}\leq\varOmega(1_{W})
. Let
V\subset H
be an open set with compact closure such that
\overline{W}\subset V
. By Urysohn’s lemma
[
25
, Thm. 2.12]
, we find
h\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
such that
1_{\overline{W}}\leq h\leq 1_{V}
. Then
\omega=\varOmega(h)\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
satisfies
\delta_{\varLambda}\leq\omega\leq\delta_{\varGamma}
for the Meyer set
\varGamma=\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(V)
.
Choose
\psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
with
\psi\geq 0
and
\psi(0)=1
and such that any translate of the support of
\psi
meets
\varGamma
in at most one point.
Then the operator defined by
{\mathcal{T}}(\mu)=(\psi\ast\mu)\cdot\omega
for
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)

satisfies the statements of Proposition
3.5
.
In particular
{\mathcal{T}}(\varrho)=\varrho

holds, as
\varrho
is supported within
\varLambda
. As
\nu
is strongly almost periodic, the function
f=\psi\ast\nu
is Bohr almost periodic. As
S
is a universal cut-and-project scheme, we find

{\mathcal{T}}(\nu)=(\psi\ast\nu)\cdot\varOmega(h)=f\cdot\varOmega(h)=\varOmega(f^{\sharp}\cdot h)=\varOmega(g),

where
g=f^{\sharp}\cdot h\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
. Let now
(A_{n})
be any van Hove sequence.
With
K=\mbox{\rm supp}(\psi)
we then obtain by Proposition
3.5
(e) for each
n
the estimate

\displaystyle\left(\varOmega(g)-\varrho\right)(A_{n})

\displaystyle=\left({\mathcal{T}}(\nu)-{\mathcal{T}}(\varrho)\right)(A_{n})={\mathcal{T}}(\nu-\varrho)(A_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\nu(A_{n}-K)-\varrho(A_{n}-K)\ .

It follows that

\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\varOmega(g)(A_{n})\leq\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\nu(A_{n}-K)+\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\left(\varrho(A_{n})-\varrho(A_{n}-K)\right)\,.

The van Hove condition then gives
M(\varOmega(g))\leq M(\nu)
. Moreover, the properties of
{\mathcal{T}}
give

\varrho={\mathcal{T}}(\varrho)\leq{\mathcal{T}}(\nu)=\varOmega(g)\ .

(b) This follows from (a) by passing from
\varrho
to
-\varrho
and from
\mu
to
-\mu
.
∎

To give an impression of the power of the approximation lemma, we use it to derive the following result on representing strongly almost periodic measures via
\varOmega
. This result is of independent interest, but is not needed in the proof of Theorem
4.1
. However it provides some perspective as the latter theorem can be seen as an extension of it, with
C_{\mathsf{c}}(H_{\mathsf{u}})
replaced by Riemann integrable functions
{R}(H_{\mathsf{u}})
, and with strongly almost periodic measures replaced by generalized almost periodic measures.

Corollary 3.8

(Representing
\mathcal{SAP}(G)
via
universal
cut-and-project schemes)
.

Let
S
be a cut-and-project scheme over
G
and let
S_{\mathsf{u}}=(H_{\mathsf{u}},{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathsf{u}})
be any compatible
universal
cut-and-project scheme.
Then, the map

C_{\mathsf{c}}(H_{\mathsf{u}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{SAP}(G)\cap{\mathcal{M}}_{S}(G),\quad h\mapsto\varOmega_{S_{\mathsf{u}}}(h),

is a bijection. ∎

Remark 3.9

(Characterization of universal cut-and-project scheme)
.

Note that the above corollary applies in particular to
S=S_{\mathsf{u}}
, as any universal cut-and-project scheme is compatible with itself. In fact, the assertion of the corollary for
S=S_{\mathsf{u}}
characterises the universal cut-and-project scheme compatible with
S
up to isomorphism. This follows from Theorem
6.6
below, in conjunction with Proposition
6.2
.

Proof of Corollary
3.8
.

As
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}(G)={\mathcal{M}}_{S_{\mathsf{u}}}(G)
, we assume without loss of generality
S=S_{\mathsf{u}}
in the following.

The map
\varOmega
is clearly injective (as the range of the star map is dense in
H
). We now show that it is surjective. Let
\varrho
be an almost periodic measure supported on the Meyer set
\varLambda
, which is associated to
S
. Assume without loss of generality that
\varrho
is real. Then, from the approximation lemma with
\mu=\varrho=\nu
we infer that there exist
f,g\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
satisfying
\varOmega(f)\leq\varrho\leq\varOmega(g)
with
M(\varOmega(g))\leq M(\varrho)
and
M(\varrho)\leq M(\varOmega(f))
. Using monotonicity of the mean, we infer from these inequalities
M(\varOmega(f))=M(\varOmega(g))
.
Observe also that the first inequality implies
f\leq g
by continuity, as the range of the star map is dense in
H
.
Now invoking Lemma
2.12
we find

\int_{H}(g-f)\,{\rm d}\theta_{H}=0\ .

From
f\leq g
and continuity of
f
and
g
we conclude
f=g
, and
\varOmega(g)=\varrho
follows.
∎

4.
G-a-p measures supported within Meyer sets

Here we characterize regular model sets as almost periodic patterns. This characterization will be a consequence of a more general result on representing g-a-p measures whose support is contained in some Meyer set. Recall that
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}(G)
denotes the collection of translation bounded measures whose support is associated to the cut-and-project scheme
S
over
G
.

Theorem 4.1

(Representing
\mathcal{GAP}(G)
via universal cut-and-project schemes)
.

Let
S
be a cut-and-project scheme over
G
and let
S_{\mathsf{u}}=(H_{\mathsf{u}},{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathsf{u}})
be a compatible
universal
cut-and-project scheme. Then, the map

{R}(H_{\mathsf{u}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{GAP}(G)\cap{\mathcal{M}}_{S}(G),\quad h\mapsto\varOmega_{S_{\mathsf{u}}}(h),

is onto.

Remark 4.2

(The map
\varOmega
)
.

The above map
h\mapsto\varOmega_{S_{\mathsf{u}}}(h)
has a few additional properties: It is linear and its kernel consists exactly of the Riemann integrable functions vanishing on
L^{\star_{\mathsf{u}}}\subset H_{\mathsf{u}}
. Moreover, it is not hard to see that it is an isometry when
R(H_{\mathsf{u}})
is equipped with the seminorm
\|h\|_{R}=D_{S_{\mathsf{u}}}\cdot\theta_{H_{\mathsf{u}}}(|h|)
and
\mathcal{GAP}(G)
is equipped with the seminorm
\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{GAP}}=M(|\mu|)
, where
|\mu|
denotes the total variation of the measure
\mu
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

As
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}(G)={\mathcal{M}}_{S_{\mathsf{u}}}(G)
, we assume without loss of generality
S=S_{\mathsf{u}}
in the following.

Note that
\varOmega(h)
is indeed a g-a-p measure by Proposition
2.14
. As
h
is Riemann integrable, we can take a compact set
W\subset H
having non-empty interior such that
\mbox{\rm supp}(h)\subset W
. Then
\mbox{\rm supp}(\Omega(h))\subset\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)
, which means that
\varOmega(h)
has support associated to
S
.
We show that the map
\varOmega
is onto: Let
\varrho
be an arbitrary g-a-p measure with support associated to
S
. Without loss of generality we assume that
\varrho
is a real measure.
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can find
\mu_{n},\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{SAP}(G)
with

\mu_{n}\leq\varrho\leq\nu_{n}\mbox{ and }M(\nu_{n}-\mu_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0\ .

By Lemma
3.7
, we can choose
f_{n},g_{n}\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
with

\varrho\geq\varOmega(f_{n})\mbox{ and }M(\varOmega(f_{n}))\geq M(\mu_{n})\ ,

and

\varrho\leq\varOmega(g_{n})\mbox{ and }M(\varOmega(g_{n}))\leq M(\nu_{n})\ ,

respectively for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
In particular we have for arbitrary
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
that

f_{m}(x^{\star})\leq\varrho(\{x\})\leq g_{n}(x^{\star})

for
x\in L
. This implies
f_{m}\leq g_{n}
for each
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
by denseness of
L^{\star}
in
H
. We thus have
\sup_{n}f_{n}\leq\inf_{n}g_{n}
. We now define
h:H\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

h(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\varrho(\{\star^{-1}(y)\})&y\in L^{\star}\\
\sup_{n}f_{n}(y)&y\notin L^{\star}\end{array}\right.\ ,

where we used injectivity of the star map. Then by construction
\varOmega(h)=\varrho
and
f_{n}\leq h\leq g_{n}
.
Moreover we have

0\leq M(\varOmega(g_{n}))-M(\varOmega(f_{n}))\leq M(\nu_{n})-M(\mu_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0\ .

Using the density formula Lemma
2.12
,
we conclude that
h
is Riemann integrable.
∎

With Theorem
4.1
we find the following characterization of g-a-p measures supported within Meyer sets.

Corollary 4.3

(Characterization of g-a-p measures supported within Meyer sets)
.

Let
\varrho\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
be a discrete measure supported within some Meyer set. Then
\varrho\in\mathcal{GAP}(G)
if and only if there exists a cut-and-project scheme
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
over
G
such that
\varrho=\varOmega_{S}(h)
for some
h\in R(H)
.

Proof.

“if part”
: This is the statement of Proposition
2.14
.

“only if part”
: Assume that
\varrho\in\mathcal{GAP}(G)
is supported within some Meyer set
\varLambda
. By Proposition
3.3
, we might take a universal cut-and-project scheme
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
to which
\varLambda
is associated. Now the claim follows from Theorem
4.1
, as any universal cut-and-project scheme is compatible with itself.
∎

We now specialize to Meyer sets.
For our reasoning we need the following auxiliary statement. It shows that a Riemann integrable function that attains its values in
\{0,1\}
on a dense set must essentially be the characteristic function of a set. This is certainly known. As we have not found a reference, we include a proof for completeness.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
H
be a locally compact abelian group and let
h\in{R}(H)
. Assume that there exists a dense set
D\subset H
such that
h(D)\subset\{0,1\}
.
Then there exists a set
W\subset H
with compact closure such that
1_{W}\in{R}(H)
and

h(y)=1_{W}(y)
holds for all
y\in D
. In particular, the topological boundary of
W
has Haar measure
0
.

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that
h
is real-valued. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
pick
f_{n},g_{n}\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
such that
f_{n}\leq h\leq g_{n}
and

\int_{H}(g_{n}-f_{n})\,{\rm d}\theta_{H}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Note
f_{n}\leq 1
and
g_{n}\geq 0
on
H
by continuity and define the set
W\subset H
by

W=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\{f_{m}>0\}\cup\{h=1\}\ .

We claim
h(y)=1_{W}(y)
for all
y\in D
: Recall that
h(y)\in\{0,1\}
for all
y\in D
by assumption. If
h(y)=1
, then
y\in W
and hence
1_{W}(y)=1
. If
h(y)=0
, then
f_{n}(y)\leq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
y\notin W
in this case, which means
1_{W}(y)=0
. Thus
h(y)=1_{W}(y)
for all
y\in D
.

Moreover we claim
f_{n}\leq 1_{W}\leq g_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which implies
1_{W}\in R(H)
:

We first show
f_{n}\leq 1_{W}
. As
f_{n}\leq 1
, we only have to consider
y\notin W
. Then
f_{n}(y)\leq 0=1_{W}(y)
, where the first inequality follows from the definition of
W
.

We next show
1_{W}\leq g_{n}
. Since
0\leq g_{n}
, we only need to show that
g_{n}(y)\geq 1
for all
y\in W
. Let thus
y\in W
. If
h(y)=1
, then
1_{W}(y)=1=h(y)\leq g_{n}(y)
, and the claim follows. On the other hand, if
f_{m}(y)>0
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, then
0<f_{m}\leq h\leq g_{n}
on some neighborhood
V
of
y
by continuity.
By assumption on
D
and continuity of
g_{n}
we thus get
1\leq g_{n}
on
\overline{V\cap D}
. Noting that
y\in V\subset\overline{V\cap D}
, we infer
1_{W}(y)=1\leq g_{n}(y)
as claimed.

We finally show
\theta_{H}(\partial W)=0
: As
1_{W}
is Riemann integrable, we find for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
functions
f,g\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
with
f\leq 1_{W}\leq g
and
\theta_{H}(g-f)<\varepsilon
. As continuity implies
f\leq 1_{W^{\circ}}
and
1_{\overline{W}}\leq g
, we obtain
\theta_{H}(\partial W)\leq\theta_{H}(g-f)<\varepsilon
. Thus
\theta_{H}(\partial W)=0
.
∎

Our characterization of regular model sets is now obtained by specializing Theorem
4.1
to point sets and invoking the preceding lemma. We will also show a third equivalent condition in this case, which does not seem to extend to arbitrary g-a-p measures with Meyer set support.

We need the following pieces of notation. For two point measures
\mu
and
\nu
on
G
we denote

\{\mu\neq\nu\}=\{x\in G:\mu(\{x\})\neq\nu(\{x\})\}\ .

Moreover, we fix a van Hove sequence
(A_{n})
and define
for a uniformly discrete subset
\varLambda
of
G
its uniform upper density by

{\overline{\mbox{\rm dens}}}(\varLambda)=\limsup_{n}\sup_{x\in G}\frac{{\rm card}(\varLambda\cap(A_{n}+x))}{|A_{n}|}\ .

The uniform upper density can be seen to be independent of the van Hove sequence
[
16
,
23
]
. However, we do not need this and refrain from further discussion.

Theorem 4.5

(Characterization of regular model sets)
.

Let
\varLambda
be a Meyer set in
G
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

\varLambda
is a regular model set.

(ii)

There exists a Meyer set
\varGamma\supset\varLambda
with the following property: For every
\varepsilon>0
there are strongly almost periodic measures
\mu_{\varepsilon},\nu_{\varepsilon}
with

0\leq\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq\delta_{\varLambda}\leq\nu_{\varepsilon}\leq\delta_{\varGamma}\mbox{ and }M(\nu_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon})\leq{\overline{\mbox{\rm dens}}}(\{\mu_{\varepsilon}\neq\nu_{\varepsilon}\})<\varepsilon.

(iii)

\varLambda
is an almost periodic pattern.

Remark 4.6
.

(a) For Euclidean space
G
, the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) has already been established by Meyer in
[
19
, Cor. 4.3]
.

(b) Note that we have

\{\mu_{\varepsilon}\neq\nu_{\varepsilon}\}=\{\delta_{\varLambda}\neq\mu_{\varepsilon}\}\cup\{\delta_{\varLambda}\neq\nu_{\varepsilon}\}\ .

Therefore, (ii) asserts that any regular model set
\varLambda
can be approximated from above and below by discrete strongly almost periodic measures that only differ from
\delta_{\varLambda}
on sets of arbitrary small density. This is a precise version of a viewpoint sometimes expressed by physicists (see the introduction).

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii): As
\varLambda
is a regular model set, we can find a cut-and-project scheme

S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
and a regular window

such that
\varLambda=\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)
. The following argument only uses the inclusions

\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W^{\circ})\subset\varLambda\subset\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(\overline{W})
.

Fix a compact set

such that
\overline{W}\subset C^{\circ}
and define the Meyer set
\varGamma=\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(C)
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. As the Haar measure

on

is a Radon measure, the regularity properties of
\theta_{H}
together with
\theta_{H}(\partial W)=0
imply that there exist a compact set
K\subset H
and an open set

such that
K\subset W^{\circ}\subset\overline{W}\subset O^{\prime}
and
\theta_{H}(O^{\prime}\setminus K)\leq\varepsilon
.
Then the open set

also satisfies
K\subset W^{\circ}\subset\overline{W}\subset O
and
\theta_{H}(O\setminus K)\leq\varepsilon
.

By
[
25

and by

we find nonempty open sets

with compact closure such that
K\subset U\subset\overline{U}\subset W^{\circ}\subset\overline{W}\subset V\subset\overline{V}\subset O
.
By Urysohn’s lemma, we find

such that

1_{\overline{U}}\leq f\leq 1_{W^{\circ}}\leq 1_{\overline{W}}\leq g\leq 1_{V}
.
Then

and

belong to
\mathcal{SAP}(G)
by Lemma

. Moreover both

Note that for any

with
1_{\overline{V}\setminus U}\leq h
we have the estimates

{\rm card}(\{\mu\neq\nu\}\cap A)\leq{\rm card}(\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(\overline{V}\setminus U)\cap A)\leq\sum_{x\in L\cap A}h(x^{\star})=\varOmega(h)(A)

on any compact

.
From Lemma
2.12
and Proposition

{\overline{\mbox{\rm dens}}}(\{\mu\neq\nu\})\leq M(\varOmega(h))=D_{S}\cdot\theta_{H}(h)\ .

As

is compact in

with

such that

is arbitrarily close to

. Hence, we find

{\overline{\mbox{\rm dens}}}(\{\mu\neq\nu\})\leq D_{S}\cdot\theta_{H}(\overline{V}\setminus U)\leq D_{S}\cdot\theta_{H}(O\setminus K)\leq D_{S}\cdot\varepsilon\ .

We finally note
0\leq\nu-\mu\leq\delta_{\{\mu\neq\nu\}}
, which implies

M(\nu-\mu)\leq{\overline{\mbox{\rm dens}}}(\{\mu\neq\nu\})\ .

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii): This is obvious.

(iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i):
By Proposition

, we may choose a universal cut-and-project scheme

to which the Meyer set
\varLambda

\delta_{\varLambda}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{S}(G)

\delta_{\varLambda}\in\mathcal{GAP}(G)

Now we invoke Theorem

on

.
Thus, by Lemma

5.
Examples and counter-examples

This section gathers various remarks and examples to illuminate facets of our results.

5.1.
Meyer sets in a lattice

Let
L
be a lattice in
G
. In order to describe arbitrary Meyer sets in
L
in a common cut-and-project scheme, we may use the Bohr compactification
L_{\mathsf{b}}
of
L
with injection map
i:L\to L_{\mathsf{b}}
.

Proposition 5.1

(A cut-and-project scheme for lattice subsets)
.

Let
L
be a lattice in
G
and define
{\mathcal{L}}=\{(x,i(x)):x\in L\}\subset G\times L_{\mathsf{b}}
. Then
S=(L_{\mathsf{b}},{\mathcal{L}})
is a cut-and-project scheme over
G
with injective star map, to which
L
is associated. Moreover the spaces
{\mathcal{M}}_{S}(G)
,
{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(L)
, and
C_{\mathsf{u}}(L)
are canonically isomorphic.

Proof.

We note that the Meyer set
L\subset G
is associated to the cut-and-project scheme
S^{\prime}=(\{0\},L\times\{0\})
over
G
. The construction in the proof of Proposition
3.3
, with
G_{\mathsf{b}}
replaced by
L_{\mathsf{b}}
, then produces the compatible cut-and-project scheme
S
. The injection maps
C_{\mathsf{u}}(L)\to{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(L)\to{\mathcal{M}}_{S}(G)
given by
f\mapsto f\cdot\theta_{L}\mapsto f\cdot\delta_{L}
are isomorphisms as
L
is uniformly discrete in
G
. ∎

Remark 5.2
.

(a)
For discrete
G
, we may take
L=G
. In that case, the above cut-and-project scheme is universal. In fact,
\mathcal{GAP}(G)
can then be identified with a class of functions that are called Riemann almost periodic by Hartman
[
9
]
.

(b)
The above cut-and-project scheme may not be universal, but allows to lift all functions that are Bohr almost periodic on
L
. This has been used in
[
14
, Sec. 6]
to analyse modulated lattices, which give rise to g-a-p measures beyond having Meyer set support.

5.2.
On regular windows

A model set may well be associated to more than one cut-and-project scheme. Then, the question arises whether regularity of the window is independent of the chosen cut-and-project scheme. Our considerations above give the following.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\varLambda
be a Meyer set in
G
, and let
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
be any
universal
cut-and-project scheme over
G
to which
\varLambda
is associated.
Then,
\varLambda
is a regular model set if and only if there exists a regular window
W\subset H
such that
\varLambda=\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)
.

Proof.

“if part”
: This holds by definition.

“only if part”
: As
\varLambda
is a regular model set, its Dirac comb is g-a-p by Theorem
4.5
. The proof (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) of Theorem
4.5
then gives
\varLambda=\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)
for a regular window
W\subset H
in the universal cut-and-project scheme
S
.
∎

The proposition asserts that regularity necessary holds with respect to
universal
cut-and-project schemes. However the following examples in
G={\mathbb{Z}}
show that regularity may be lost by passing to arbitrary cut-and-project schemes over
G
:

Consider for any prime
p
the group of
p
-adic integers
H_{p}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
together with
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}=\{(n,i_{p}(n)):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
, where
i_{p}:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
denotes the canonical embedding. Define
H_{0}=\{0\}
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}=\{(n,0):n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
. Then
S_{0}=(H_{0},{\mathcal{L}}_{0})
and
S_{p}=(H_{p},{\mathcal{L}}_{p})
are cut-and-project schemes over
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Moreover, a Meyer set in
{\mathbb{Z}}
is associated to
S_{0}
or
S_{p}
, respectively, if and only if it is a relatively dense subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Therefore, all these cut-and-project schemes are compatible. Now the following holds true.

•

The only cut-and-project sets from
S_{0}
are
\varLambda=\varnothing
and the regular model set
\varLambda={\mathbb{Z}}
. Every subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}
is a cut-and-project set from
S_{p}
, as
S_{p}
has an injective star map
i_{p}
.

•

Consider
\varLambda_{p,r}=p{\mathbb{Z}}+r
for some prime
p
and some
r\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
. Then
\varLambda_{p,r}=\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}_{S_{p}}(W_{p,r})
is a regular model set, where
W_{p,r}\subset H_{p}
is the closed ball of radius 1 centered at
i_{p}(r)
.

•

Let now
q
be a prime different from
p
. Then no window
W\subset H_{q}
such that
\varLambda_{p,r}=\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}_{S_{q}}(W)
can be regular. Indeed, otherwise
\varLambda_{p,r}
would have density 1 as
i_{q}(\varLambda_{p,r})
is dense in
H_{q}
, which is contradictory.

5.3.
Regular inter model sets

When studying Pisot substitutions or model set dynamical systems, so-called
regular inter model sets play an important rôle
[
3
,
13
,
24
]
. These are Meyer sets
\varLambda
satisfying
\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W^{\circ})\subset\varLambda\subset\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(\overline{W})
in some cut-and-project scheme with regular window
W
.

Clearly any regular model set is a regular inter model set.
Moreover, observe that the proof (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) in Theorem
4.5
carries over verbatim from regular model sets to regular inter model sets. Thus generalized almost periodicity also characterizes regular inter model sets and we obtain the following statement.

Proposition 5.4
.

A subset of
G
is a regular inter model set if and only if it is a regular model set. ∎

For Euclidean space
G
, this has recently been established in
[
24
]
by a geometric approach that does not seem to be extendable to arbitrary locally compact abelian groups.

5.4.
G-a-p measures and Weyl almost periodicity

As discussed in the introduction, Weyl almost periodic measures constitute an important subclass of models in aperiodic order. It is shown in
[
16
]
that any g-a-p measure is Weyl almost periodic. Here, we show that the converse does not hold. So, g-a-p is a strictly stronger notion that Weyl almost periodicity. In fact there is a crucial difference between these two concepts: Weyl almost periodicity is stable under perturbations of zero density while g-a-p is not. One might even think of g-a-p measures as (almost) being locally determined.

Recall that a locally integrable function
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(G)
is Weyl almost periodic if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a trigonometric polynomial
P_{\varepsilon}
such that

\|f-P_{\varepsilon}\|_{w}=\limsup_{n}\sup_{x\in G}\frac{1}{|A_{n}|}\int_{A_{n}}\left|f(x-t)-P_{\varepsilon}(x-t)\right|\,{\rm d}t<\varepsilon\ ,

where
(A_{n})
is any van Hove sequence. In fact the above limit is independent of the choice of the van Hove sequence
[
16
, Prop. 4.11]
.
A measure
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
is
Weyl almost periodic
if the function
\varphi\ast\mu
is Weyl almost periodic for all
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
.

Denote the total variation of the translation bounded measure
\mu
by
|\mu|
and define
its
uniform upper density
by

\|\mu\|_{u}=\limsup_{n}\sup_{x\in G}\frac{|\mu|(x+A_{n})}{|A_{n}|}\ .

This is independent of the choice of the van Hove sequence
[
23
, Prop. 5.14]
. Also note
\|\delta_{\varLambda}\|_{u}={\overline{\mbox{\rm dens}}}(\varLambda)
for any uniformly discrete point set
\varLambda
.
The following is immediate.

Proposition 5.5

(Stability of Weyl almost periodicity under zero uniform density perturbations)
.

If
\mu
is Weyl almost periodic and
\nu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
is such that
\|\mu-\nu\|_{u}=0
then
\nu
is also Weyl almost periodic. ∎

The stability property given in the previous proposition does not hold for g-a-p measures. We rather have the following result.

Theorem 5.6
.

There exists a relatively dense point set
\varLambda\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\|\delta_{\varLambda}-\delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\|_{u}=0
, and
\varLambda
is not a regular model set in
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
In particular,
\delta_{\varLambda}
is Weyl almost periodic but not generalized-almost-periodic.

We will prove the theorem building on an example due to Meyer
[
19
, Sect. 2]
. This requires some preparation. For any transcendental number
\theta>3
consider

\varLambda_{\theta}=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}\theta^{j}:n\in\mathbb{N},c_{0},\ldots,c_{j}\in\{0,1\}\right\}\ .

The set
\varLambda_{\theta}
gives rise to a set of non-negative integers
M_{\theta}
via

M_{\theta}=\left\{\lfloor x\rfloor,\lfloor x\rfloor+1:x\in\varLambda_{\theta}\right\}\ ,

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the largest integer smaller or equal to
x
. The following version of
[
19
, Lem. 2.30]
is adapted to our needs.

Lemma 5.7
.

Assume that
\theta>3
is transcendental. Then
M_{\theta}
has uniform density
0
, and
M_{\theta}
is dense in the Bohr compactification of
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

Denseness of
M_{\theta}
in the Bohr compactification of
{\mathbb{Z}}
is proved in
[
19
, Lem. 2.36]
. We show that
M_{\theta}
has indeed uniform density
0
by arguing that
\varLambda_{\theta}
has uniform density
0
. By transcendence, each nonzero
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\theta]
can be written as
x=P(\theta)
for a unique non-zero polynomial
P
having integer coefficients. Denote its degree by
d(x)
and its leading coefficient by
c(x)
. Consider now

\varGamma_{\theta}=\varLambda_{\theta}-\varLambda_{\theta}=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}\theta^{j}:n\in\mathbb{N},c_{j}\in\{-1,0,1\}\right\}\ .

Observe that we have for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
the estimate

1+\theta+\ldots+\theta^{n-1}<\theta^{n}\ ,

where we used
\theta>1
. Thus
x\in\varGamma_{\theta}
positive implies
c(x)=1
, due to
0<x<\theta^{d(x)}+c(x)\theta^{d(x)}
.
Furthermore, we have for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
the estimate

1+\theta+\ldots+\theta^{n-1}<\theta^{n}-(1+\theta+\ldots+\theta^{n-1})\ ,

where we used
\theta>3
.
Indeed, the above inequality is equivalent to
\theta^{n}(\theta-3)+2>0
. Thus if positive numbers
x,y\in\varGamma_{\theta}
satisfy
x\leq y
, then we have
d(x)\leq d(y)
. This holds as
d(x)>d(y)
implies

x-y\geq\theta^{d(x)}-\sum_{j=0}^{d(x)-1}\theta^{j}-y>\sum_{j=0}^{d(x)-1}\theta^{j}-\sum_{j=0}^{d(y)}\theta^{j}\geq 0\ .

We now estimate the number of elements in
F=\varLambda_{\theta}\cap[t,t+n]
for fixed
t\geq 0
and arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Denote by
\alpha
the smallest and by
\beta
the largest element in
F
. Then
\beta-\alpha\leq n
and

F-\alpha\subseteq\varGamma_{\theta}\cap[0,\beta-\alpha]=\{0\}\cup\left(\varGamma_{\theta}\cap(0,\beta-\alpha]\right)\ .

Now, for all
x\in\varGamma_{\theta}\cap(0,\beta-\alpha]
we have
d(x)\leq d(\beta-\alpha)
. Writing
d=d(\beta-\alpha)
, we get

\varGamma_{\theta}\cap(0,\beta-\alpha]\subseteq\{c_{0}+c_{1}\theta+\ldots+c_{d-1}\theta^{d-1}+c_{d}\theta^{d}:c_{0},\ldots c_{d}\in\{-1,0,1\}\}\ .

This shows that
{\rm card}(F)={\rm card}(F-\alpha)\leq 3^{d+1}+1
. Now a standard estimate yields

n\geq\beta-\alpha\geq\theta^{d}-(1+\theta+\ldots+\theta^{d-1})>1+\theta+\ldots+\theta^{d-1}>\theta^{d-1}\ ,

and hence
\theta^{d-1}<n
. We thus have
d<1+\log_{\theta}(n)
. This gives the estimate

\frac{{\rm card}(\varLambda_{\theta}\cap[t,t+n])}{n}<\frac{3^{2+\log_{\theta}(n)}+1}{n}=\frac{9n^{\log_{\theta}(3)}+1}{n}\ .

Note that when
t<0
, due to
\varLambda_{\theta}\subset[0,\infty)
we have
\varLambda_{\theta}\cap[t,t+n]\subset\varLambda_{\theta}\cap[0,n]
. Thus the above estimate holds in fact for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. As
\theta>3
, this shows that
\varLambda_{\theta}
has indeed uniform density
0
.
∎

We can now show that the point set
{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus M_{\theta}
has the properties stated in Theorem
5.6
.

Proof of Theorem
5.6
.

Assume that
\theta>3
is transcendental and define
\varLambda={\mathbb{Z}}\backslash M_{\theta}
. Then
\delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}-\delta_{\varLambda}=\delta_{M_{\theta}}
and hence
\|\delta_{\varLambda}-\delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\|_{u}=0
.
Thus Weyl almost periodicity of
\varLambda
holds by Proposition
5.5
. Moreover relative denseness of
\varLambda
holds as
M_{\theta}
has uniform density
0
.

Now assume by contradiction that
\varLambda\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
is a regular model set. Then, by Proposition
5.1
,
\varLambda
is a cut-and-project set in the
universal
cut-and-project scheme
S=({\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathsf{b}},{\mathcal{L}})
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Therefore, by Proposition
5.3
, there exists a regular window
W\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathsf{b}}
such that
\varLambda=\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}_{S}(W)
.
Since
i({\mathbb{Z}}\backslash\varLambda)
is dense in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathsf{b}}
, the regular window
W
must have empty interior, which is contradictory.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

(a)
The point set
M_{\theta}
is Weyl almost periodic, as it has uniform density
0
. Moreover
M_{\theta}=\mbox{\Large$\curlywedge$}(W)
in the universal cut-and-project scheme from Proposition
5.1
for some windows
W\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathsf{b}}
. Any such window satisfies
W^{\circ}=\varnothing
due to density
0
and
\overline{W}={\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathsf{b}}
by Lemma
5.7
.

(b) The Weyl almost periodic point set
{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus M_{\theta}
is not a regular model set, but agrees with the regular model set
{\mathbb{Z}}
up to a set of uniform density
0
. In particular, it has the same diffraction properties as
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

6.
More about
universal
cut-and-project schemes

In this section we have a closer look at
universal
cut-and-project schemes. We show uniqueness of compatible
universal
cut-and-project schemes up to a suitable notion of isomorphism, discuss universality in terms of a factorization property on compatible cut-and-project schemes, and provide a number of characterizations.

6.1.
Uniqueness and factorization

We will use the following notion of isomorphy of cut-and-project schemes.

Definition 6.1

(Isomorphy of cut-and-project schemes)
.

Consider two cut-and-project schemes
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
and
S^{\prime}=(H^{\prime},{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime})
over
G
. Then
S
and
S^{\prime}
are called isomorphic if there exists a group isomorphism
\Phi:H^{\prime}\to H
that is a homeomorphism such that
{\mathcal{L}}=\{(x,\Phi(y^{\prime})):(x,y^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\}
.

Universal cut-and-project schemes are uniquely determined in the following sense.

Proposition 6.2

(Uniqueness of a
universal
cut-and-project scheme up to isomorphism)
.

Any two compatible
universal
cut-and-project schemes are isomorphic.

Proof.

Let
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
and
S^{\prime}=(H^{\prime},{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime})
be two compatible
universal
cut-and-project schemes. Denote the star maps of
S,S^{\prime}
by
\star,\star^{\prime}
, respectively, and note
L=\pi^{G}({\mathcal{L}})=\pi^{G}({\mathcal{L}}^{\prime})
by compatibility. As the star maps are injective by universality, there exists a group isomorphism
\phi:L^{\star^{\prime}}\to L^{\star}\subset H
.
Equip
L^{\star^{\prime}}\subset H^{\prime}
with the topology inherited from
H^{\prime}
. We show continuity of
\phi:L^{\star^{\prime}}\to H
by arguing that
\phi^{-1}(U)
is open for all nonempty open
U\subset H
. Consider any such
U
. Take any
y\in U
and any
V
with compact closure such that
y\in V\subset U
. Invoke Urysohn’s lemma to find
h\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
satisfying
0\leq h\leq 1
such that
h(y)=1
and
h=0
outside
V
. Consider the set
U_{y}=\{h>0\}
which is open in
H
. For the strongly almost periodic measure
\mu=\varOmega_{S}(h)
we use Corollary
3.8
to find
h^{\prime}\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H^{\prime})
such that
\mu=\varOmega_{S^{\prime}}(h^{\prime})
. We then have

\displaystyle\phi^{-1}(U_{y})

\displaystyle=\phi^{-1}(\{h>0\})=\phi^{-1}(\{x^{\star}:\mu(\{x\})>0\})

\displaystyle=\{x^{\star^{\prime}}:\mu(\{x\})>0\}=\{h^{\prime}>0\}\cap L^{\star^{\prime}}\ ,

which shows that
\phi^{-1}(U_{y})
is open in
L^{\star^{\prime}}
. As
y\in U
was arbitrary and satisfies
y\in U_{y}\subset U
, we conclude that
\phi:L^{\star^{\prime}}\to H
is continuous.
Since
\phi
is a group homomorphism, it is uniformly continuous, and hence, by denseness of
L^{\star^{\prime}}
in
H^{\prime}
it extends uniquely to a continuous group homomorphism
\Phi:H^{\prime}\to H
.
We thus have
\Phi(x^{\star^{\prime}})=\phi(x^{\star^{\prime}})=x^{\star}
for all
x\in L
.
Repeating the argument with
H
and
H^{\prime}
interchanged, we infer the existence of a continuous group homomorphism
\Psi:H\to H^{\prime}
satisfying

\Psi(x^{\star})=x^{\star^{\prime}}
. This implies
\Psi\circ\Phi=\mbox{Id}
on
L^{\star^{\prime}}
and
\Phi\circ\Psi=\mbox{Id}
on
L^{\star}
. By denseness and continuity, we get that
\Phi
is a homeomorphism with inverse
\Psi
, and that
\Phi
is a group isomorphism. We finally note

{\mathcal{L}}=\{(x,x^{\star}):x\in L\}=\{(x,\phi(x^{\star^{\prime}})):x\in L\}=\{(x,\Phi(x^{\star^{\prime}})):x\in L\}={\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\ .

This shows that
S
and
S^{\prime}
are isomorphic.
∎

For our discussion of universality, we will use the following notion of factor of a cut-and-project scheme.

Definition 6.3

(Factor cut-and-project scheme)
.

Consider two cut-and-project schemes
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
and
S^{\prime}=(H^{\prime},{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime})
over
G
. Then
S
is called a factor of
S^{\prime}
if there exists a continuous group homomorphism
\Psi:H^{\prime}\to H
such that
{\mathcal{L}}=\{(x,\Psi(y^{\prime})):(x,y^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\}
.

We note that such a continuous group homomorphism must necessarily be unique (by denseness of the range of the projection of
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
to
H^{\prime}
). From the preceding proposition we obtain the following consequence.

Proposition 6.4

(Factorization over universal cut-and-project schemes)
.

Let
S^{\prime}
be a universal cut-and-project scheme over
G
. Then
S^{\prime}
has the factorization property that any compatible cut-and-project scheme
S
over
G
is a factor of
S^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Consider the cut-and-project scheme
S_{\mathsf{u}}=(H_{\mathsf{u}},{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathsf{u}})
over
G
that was constructed from
S
in order to prove Proposition
3.3
. Recall
H_{\mathsf{u}}\subset H\times G_{\mathsf{b}}
and denote by
\pi_{1}:H_{\mathsf{u}}\to H
the canonical projection on the first factor. Observe that
S_{\mathsf{u}}
and
S^{\prime}
are compatible, since
S
and
S_{\mathsf{u}}
are compatible by construction and since
S
and
S^{\prime}
are compatible by assumption. As
S_{\mathsf{u}}
is universal, there exists a continuous group homomorphism
\Phi:H^{\prime}\to H_{\mathsf{u}}
such that
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathsf{u}}=\{(x,\Phi(y^{\prime})):(x,y^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\}
by Proposition
6.2
. Define the continuous group homomorphism
\Psi:H^{\prime}\to H
by
\Psi=\pi_{1}\circ\Phi
. We then have

\begin{split}{\mathcal{L}}&=\{(x,y):(x,y)\in{\mathcal{L}}\}=\{(x,\pi_{1}(y_{\mathsf{u}})):(x,y_{\mathsf{u}})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathsf{u}}\}\\
&=\{(x,\pi_{1}(\Phi(y^{\prime}))):(x,y^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\}=\{(x,\Psi(y^{\prime})):(x,y^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\}\ .\end{split}

We have shown that
S
is a factor of
S^{\prime}
.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

It is not hard to see that compatible universal cut-and-project schemes are characterized by the factorization property. Indeed, let
S=(H,{\mathcal{L}})
and
S^{\prime}=(H^{\prime},{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime})
over
G
be compatible and both have the factorization property. Let
\Psi:H^{\prime}\longrightarrow H
and
\Phi:H\longrightarrow H^{\prime}
be the arising factor maps. Then, for any
(x,y)\in\mathcal{L}
we find that
(x,\Psi\circ\Phi(y))
belongs to
\mathcal{L}
. This gives
y=x^{\star}=\Psi\circ\Phi(y)
. Hence,
\Psi\circ\Phi
is the identity on
H
(first on a dense subset and then, by continuity, on the whole of
H
). Similarly, we find
\Phi\circ\Psi
is the identity on
H^{\prime}
. This shows that
S
and
S^{\prime}
are isomorphic.

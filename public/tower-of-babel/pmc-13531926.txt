Prediction of intravenous cyclophosphamide treatment efficacy in idiopathic inflammatory myopathies-associated interstitial lung disease patients: a nomogram based on radiomics and clinical factors
Authors: Deying Huang, Yanhong Li, Yinlan Wu, Lu Cheng, Tong Wu, Xiuping Liang, Jing Zhao, Lizhi Zhang, Chunyu Tan, Yi Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIntravenous cyclophosphamide (IV-CYC) is a first-line treatment for idiopathic inflammatory myopathy (IIMs) complicated with interstitial lung disease (ILD) but has been hampered by a lack of targeted clinical trials and heterogeneous patient responses. Therefore, this study established an evaluation model for IV-CYC treatment response in IIMs-ILD patients by combining clinical characteristics and radiomics.MethodsRetrospectively included total of 62 IIMs-ILD patients were enrolled and categorized into two groups on the basis of treatment response: the Effective group (n = 18) and the Non-effective group (n = 44). Baseline clinical and imaging characteristics were analyzed, with no significant differences observed in demographic or clinical factors between the groups. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression identified five key predictors of treatment response: ROl_Lobe2 infected rates, ROl_Segment1 infected rates, anti-MDA5 antibody, anti-Jo1 antibody, and IgA levels.ResultsA nomogram incorporating these factors was developed, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.876 (95% CI: 0.788–0.964), a sensitivity of 0.889 and a specificity of 0.790. The model’s Youden’s index was 0.639. The calibration curve indicated strong agreement between the predicted and actual outcomes. Decision curve analysis (DCA) and a clinical impact curve (CIC) confirmed the model’s clinical utility, showing significant net benefits in predicting the treatment response to IV-CYC in IIMs-ILD patients.ConclusionThe proposed nomogram provides a reliable tool for predicting treatment response in IIMs-ILD patients receiving IV-CYC therapy, potentially guiding personalized treatment strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12890-026-04370-4.Keywords: Idiopathic inflammatory myopathies (IIMs), Interstitial lung disease (ILD), Cyclophosphamide, Radiomics, Nomograms

Introduction

Idiopathic inflammatory myopathies (IIMs) are autoimmune disorders characterized by chronic skeletal muscle inflammation and various extramuscular complications [1]. Among these, interstitial lung disease (ILD) is the most common and severe complication, affecting up to 78% of patients and contributing to increased mortality [2, 3]. At present, glucocorticoids and immunosuppressants are still the main drugs used for the treatment of IIMs. In the 2023 American College of Rheumatology (ACR)/American College of Chest Physicians (CHEST) Guideline for the Treatment of Interstitial Lung Disease in People with Systemic Autoimmune Rheumatic Diseases, for patients with IIMs-ILD, cyclophosphamide (CYC) is recommended as one of the first-line immunosuppressive options [4]. CYC is an alkylating agent that has been widely employed in the treatment of severe manifestations of various autoimmune and inflammatory disorders [5, 6]. For the treatment of IIMs-ILD, intravenous cyclophosphamide (IV-CYC) is recommended early in the treatment regimen at doses of 500–1000 mg/m2 and is administered every 4 weeks for 3–6 months, even in patients who are resistant to other treatments [7]. However, despite the widespread use of IV-CYC in IIMs treatment, there are still many uncertainties about its efficacy in IIMs-ILD treatment. The existing reports are mostly based on expert opinions, and there is a lack of controlled clinical trials specifically for IIMs-ILD. In addition, the clinical manifestations and disease progression of IIMs-ILD are significantly heterogeneous, and the response of different patients to IV-CYC varies greatly. Therefore, it is highly important to carry out controlled clinical studies on IV-CYC in IIMs-ILD patients to clarify the efficacy and safety of IV-CYC in IIMs-ILD patients and to provide a more accurate treatment reference for clinicians.

High-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) is a noninvasive technique used to diagnose ILD and is very important in determining the scope and severity of ILD. Patients with IIMs-ILD are classified according to the predominant HRCT pattern: nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP), usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) and organizing pneumonia (OP) [8]. Previous studies have also revealed that visual HRCT scores are related to ILD prognosis [9], but their subjectivity limits their use in accurate assessment. As a new technology, Radiomics is the high-throughput mining of quantitative image features from standard nursing medical imaging and is capable of extracting data and applying them to clinical decision support systems [10]. In addition, Radiomics analysis utilizes sophisticated image analysis tools and the rapid development and verification of medical imaging data, using image-based features to provide prognostic and diagnostic value for a variety of diseases [11–13]. In recent years, an increasing number of studies have focused on the application of Radiomics in ILD. For example, Yang et al. reported that radiotin characteristics derived from pretreatment of HRCT scans can predict treatment response in patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) who are receiving antidisease therapy [14]. Feng et al. used radiomics to identify patients with connective tissue diseases that are sensitive to glucocorticoid treatment [15]. Recently, Xu et al. confirmed that radiographic characteristics can be used to predict the prognosis of ILD patients with anti-MDA5-positive dermatomyositis [16]. Radiomics has a promising role in the clinical management of CTD-ILD patients, but there are currently no studies on the use of imaging and clinical application to predict the efficacy of IV-CYC. Therefore, this study aimed to analyze clinical indicators and imaging data in patients with IIMs-ILD to develop predictive models to explore the potential of this model to identify IV-CYC treatment sensitivity.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient population

This study utilized a retrospective design to establish a clinical prediction model for predicting the probability of treatment response in IIMs-ILD patients receiving IV-CYC. Data were extracted from the electronic medical records (EMRs) of IIMs-ILD patients who received IV-CYC at West China Hospital of Sichuan University between January 2019 and January 2025.

In this study, IV-CYC was defined as intravenous CYC at 500 ~ 1000 mg/m2 body surface area every 4 weeks for 3–6 months.

The inclusion criteria for participants were as follows: (1) aged ≥ 18 years; (2) IIMs were diagnosed according to the 2017 European League Against Rheumatism (EULAR)/American College of Rheumatology (ACR) criteria [17]; (3) ILD was diagnosed according to the 2013 American Thoracic Society (ATS)/European Respiratory Society (ERS) classification [18]; (4) patients were > 1 year from their last dose of IV-CYC; and (5) patients underwent HRCT initially and 3 months after IV-CYC. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) had a diagnosis of malignant tumor; (2) had a diagnosis combined with other connective tissue diseases (CTDs); (3) had < 2 IV-CYC cycles; (4) had used any biological agents within the last 3 months; and (5) had missing first-time or 3-month imaging data.

CT imaging

Image information was extracted from two HRCT scans of each patient: the initial scan and 3 months after IV-CYC treatment. The flowchart of this study is shown in Fig. 1. The specific methods are as follows:

Image processing

High-resolution chest CT scans at baseline were analyzed using the uAI-Discover-NCP platform (United Imaging Healthcare, Shanghai, China), which applies deep learning-based algorithms to automatically segment the lungs and quantify disease burden. Specifically, an established deep learning model called VB-Net [19] was used to automatically segment areas of lung infection, as well as the whole lung, 5 lobes and 18 segments. In previous work [19], model performance was evaluated on the basis of a Dice similarity coefficient of 91.6% (S.D. 10.0). This value has also been applied in many lung infection segmentation studies [20, 21].

Imaging features

Fifty-eight imaging features from each patient were calculated automatically on the basis of the segmentation results of VB-Net and included the pulmonary inflammation index, lung volume, infection volume and infection rate for the whole lung, 5 lobes, 18 segments and 4 different Hounsfield units (HU) ranges (<=-751, -751~-301, -301~-49, >=-50). For each anatomical region of interest (ROI), including entire lungs, lobes, and bronchopulmonary segments, the infected rate (%) was calculated as the ratio of abnormal lesion volume (ground-glass opacities, consolidations, etc.) to the total volume of the ROI: Infected Rate (%) = (Lesion volume in ROI / Total ROI lung volume) × 100%. This quantitative approach ensures consistency across subjects and minimizes observer-dependent bias.

Pulmonary Inflammation Index (PII)

Semiquantitative evaluation of lung inflammation involves scoring lesions on chest CT on the basis of their distribution and extent. A total of 20 segments across both lungs are assessed, with each affected segment contributing to the score. The distribution score is determined by identifying the lung segments affected by the lesions. If a lesion is present in a segment, 1 point is assigned, with a maximum possible score of 20. The extent score is based on the proportion of the lesion relative to the segment volume. Lesions occupying more than 50% of a segment receive 1 point, whereas those covering less than 50% receive 0 points, with a maximum score of 20. The pulmonary inflammation index (PII) was calculated as follows: PII = (Lesion Distribution Score + Lesion Extent Score)/Total Score (40) *100%. This metric provides a quantifiable measure of the inflammatory lesion burden, ranging from 0 to 100%. Higher values indicate more severe inflammation. When integrated with clinical data, this scoring system aids in assessing disease progression in patients with IIMs-ILD. Definition of treatment response

Clinical response was assessed according to symptoms and HRCT data 3 months after the initial administration of IV-CYC. Treatment response was defined by comparison with the condition before treatment: respiratory symptoms improved, and lung HRCT lesions were reduced. The difference was obtained by subtracting the PII posttreatment (PIIpost) from the patient’s PII pretreatment (PIIpre). A PIIpre-PIIpost value > 0 indicated a decrease in the PII after treatment, whereas a PII greater than the median difference was defined as a significant improvement in the patient’s PII after CYC treatment, indicating that the number of HRCT lesions was reduced.

Feature selection and model construction

Variables demonstrating a univariate analysis p-value < 0.05 were initially identified and included in a least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression model, from which the most useful predictors were selected. The area under the curve (AUC) of the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to evaluate the discriminatory capacity of the nomogram. Moreover, the clinical utility of the nomograms was measured via decision curve analysis (DCA) and clinical impact curves (CIC) for a population size of 1000.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed via IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 27.0 (IBMCorp., Armonk, N.Y., USA) and R software (version 4.3.2; http://www.Rproject.org). For continuous characteristics, the Kolmogorov‒Smirnov test was used to test the normality of the distribution. Normally distributed variables were compared via the t test, and the data are presented as the means (S.D.). Otherwise, the Mann‒Whitney U test was used, and the data are presented as the median [interquartile range (IQR)]. For categorical characteristics, the chi-square test or Fisher’s test was used, and the data are presented as numbers (%). The p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Predictors for treatment response in IIMs-ILD patients following IV-CYC

In our study, 5 variables had nonzero coefficients in the LASSO regression model on the basis of the analysis of the trial. These variables included ROl_Lobe2 infected rates, ROl_Segment1 infected rates, and MDA5, Jo1 and IgA levels (Fig. 3a‒b).

Validation of the nomogram for the prediction of treatment response

These 5 factors were used to develop a treatment response prediction nomogram (Fig. 4). When the nomogram was applied, the point for each predictor was analyzed by drawing a straight line upward from each predictor with the specific status to the ‘Point’ axis. The total points for an individual patient were calculated by summing all the separate points for the four predictors. The estimated incidence of treatment response in IIMs-ILD patients following IV-CYC can be finally determined by drawing a straight line down from the ‘Total Point’ axis to the ‘Risk’ axis.

Evaluation of the clinical and radiomics models

Furthermore, the AUC of the nomogram was 0.876 (95% CI: 0.788–0.964) (Fig. 5a). The model demonstrated a sensitivity of 0.889 and a specificity of 0.790. Youden’s index of the model was 0.639. The calibration curve is also shown in Fig. 5b. The DCA results revealed that the nomogram had greater net benefits for the prediction of treatment response in the IIMs-ILD patients following the IV-CYC trial (Fig. 5c). In addition, on the basis of the results of DCA, the CIC of the nomogram revealed that the predicted probability coincided well with the actual probability in the training sets (Fig. 5d).

Discussion

Previous studies have shown that IIMs-ILD exhibits a high degree of heterogeneity, and the optimal treatment strategies remain a subject of debate [6]. Although CYC has been widely employed in clinical practice for the treatment of CTD-ILD, prolonged IV-CYC therapy can lead to severe adverse effects such as infections and gastrointestinal bleeding [7]. However, identifying the characteristics of IIMs-ILD patients treated with IV-CYC that can help them achieve optimal therapeutic outcomes is an unsolved clinical challenge. Addressing this issue may require identifying patients who are more likely to respond favorably to IV-CYC therapy.

Pulmonary function tests (PFTs), including forced vital capacity (FVC), total lung capacity (TLC), and diffusing capacity of the lungs for carbon monoxide (DLCO), are considered gold standards for evaluating disease severity and treatment response in ILD. However, in our cohort, a substantial proportion of patients were unable to undergo reliable PFT assessments due to severe respiratory compromise, resulting in a high rate of missing data. To overcome this limitation, we adopted radiomics-based PII features as surrogate indicators of lung function. Correlation analysis revealed that PII values were significantly negatively associated with FVC, TLC, and DLCO (Supplementary Fig. 2), suggesting that increased radiographic parenchymal involvement reflects deteriorated lung function. Therefore, we consider that radiomics-derived PII may serve as a clinically meaningful, non-invasive alternative to traditional PFTs for assessing ILD severity and predicting treatment response in patients with IIMs-ILD.

In this study, we developed a novel predictive model based on clinical and radiomic parameters collected from hospitalized IIMs-ILD patients to identify those who are sensitive to IV-CYC treatment. Using the LASSO regression model, we identified 5 independent predictive factors: ROl_Lobe2 infected rates, ROl_Segment1 infected rates, and MDA5, Jo1 and IgA levels. The application of this nomogram demonstrated excellent discriminative performance in identifying IIMs-ILD patients responsive to IV-CYC therapy.

Among the imaging techniques enhanced by computer-aided quantification, radiomics stands out for its ability to extract image-based biomarkers from numerous quantitative features within medical images. Previous studies have focused on using single imaging omics to predict the ILD treatment response [14, 15], and this study compared imaging omics before and after treatment. Our study revealed that IIMs-ILD patients with radiographic involvement of Loble2 and Segment1 were more responsive to IV-CYC treatment, highlighting the value of radiomics in capturing subtle lung pathological changes that may not be evident via conventional HRCT analysis. Radiomics allows quantitative, high-dimensional data to be extracted from medical images, providing a more fine-grained assessment of disease progression and treatment response. Previous research has shown that MDA5-ILD patients who develop RP-ILD often exhibit a greater initial degree of left lung involvement on HRCT, including the Loble2 and Segment1 regions [22]. However, no specific studies have explored the associations between CYC treatment and interstitial lung abnormalities in the left lungs of patients with myositis. These findings may be attributed to anatomical and physiological peculiarities of the left lung. The left lung is narrower, longer, and consists of just two lobes due to the presence of the cardiac notch—resulting in a smaller lung volume when compared to the right lung [23]. Therefore, during the early stages of IIMs-ILD when localized inflammation is present, these anatomical characteristics may exacerbate ventilation-perfusion (V/Q) mismatch, making the accumulation of inflammatory exudates and radiological features such as ground-glass opacities more likely in the left lung regions. These lesions, being more inflammatory than fibrotic, are likely more responsive to immunosuppressive therapy like CYC. The integration of radiological features with clinical and laboratory parameters further improved the prediction accuracy of the treatment response. This integrated approach leverages the benefits of clinical assessment and advanced imaging analysis to provide a more comprehensive assessment of disease status and treatment effectiveness. The AUC of 0.876, sensitivity of 0.889, and specificity of 0.790 indicate that our model is robust in predicting treatment response and superior to models based solely on clinical or radiological features.

Interestingly, in terms of clinical features, negative anti-MDA5 antibodies were further identified as a favorable factor for IV-CYC treatment response. MDA5 antibody-positive dermatomyositis (MDA5-DM) is a distinct subtype of idiopathic inflammatory myopathies [24]. MDA5-DM patients have a high incidence of RP-ILD and usually respond poorly to glucocorticoids and immunosuppressive therapy [25], resulting in poor prognosis and high mortality [26].

A recent clinical cluster analysis suggested that anti-MDA5 antibody positivity is a high-risk factor for early infection in patients with IIMs [27]. Jennifer et al. reported that anti-MDA5 positivity (MDA5+) was linked to a significantly increased mortality risk within the first three months compared with anti-MDA5 negativity [hazard ratio (HR) 65.2, 95% CI 14.1–302.0] [28]. IIMs-ILD patients who are anti-MDA5(-) may derive greater benefit from IV-CYC therapy.

Positive anti-Jo-1 antibodies have been further identified as favorable factors for IV-CYC treatment response. A study has shown that Jo-1 + DM-ILD patients with more radiological patterns on HRCT, including diffuse patchy ground-glass opacities and basal irregular lines, exhibit more significant ILD improvement after 3 months of treatment [29]. Consistent with what Jennifer et al. reported in their IIMs-ILD cohort, anti-Jo1 antibody positivity was associated with improved early survival in the autoantibody profile of myositis spectrum diseases, which was relatively protective [28]. Similarly, our study suggests that IIM patients with positive anti-Jo-1 antibodies may experience greater radiological improvements within the first three months of IV-CYC treatment.

IgA has traditionally been recognized as a B-cell-derived immunoglobulin and a key immune factor involved in respiratory mucosal immunity [30]. IgA secreted by AT2-like cells is captured by the extracellular matrix and exacerbates pulmonary fibrosis by activating fibroblasts [31]. IPF patients exhibit an increased number of circulating activated B cells and autoreactive IgA [32], which are associated with disease progression [33], and elevated serum IgA antibody levels are associated with decreased survival [34]. In a study predicting the progression of pulmonary exudation in COVID-19 patients, IgA levels were positively correlated with the extent of lung involvement, with IgA levels peaking earlier than those in affected lung regions [35]. Compared with non-ILD-DM patients, patients with ILD-DM have higher IgG, IgA and IgM levels [36]. Our study also suggested that serum IgA levels are negatively correlated with the response to IV-CYC treatment, possibly because patients with lower IgA levels have relatively lower pulmonary mucosal immunity, leading to better early treatment outcomes for ILD.

Our study has several limitations. First, some biomarkers that are predictors or prognostic factors for ILD (such as lung function test data, ferritin, and IL-ß) are incomplete. Second, the majority of patients did not undergo long-term treatment response assessment because of the retrospective nature of the present investigation. Moreover, this study was only a single-center study with a small sample size, and a multicenter study with a large sample size is still needed.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the nomogram developed by integrating radiographic features and clinical factors can predict the response to IV-CYC in patients with IIMs-ILD in the early stage of treatment.

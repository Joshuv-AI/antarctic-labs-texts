One Health Approach for Mitigation of Impact of Climate Change on Early Childhood Development
Authors: Abhay Gaidhane, Manoj Patil, Shital Telrandhe, Zahiruddin Quazi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Climate change is an unavoidable process and its effects on the environment is varied in different geographies. In developing world, the effects of climate change have an enormous impact on young children. Climate change deeply impacts early childhood development, posing distinctive challenges to young children through disruptions in health resources, such as air, water, food, and shelter. These disruptions result in increased vulnerability to extreme weather events, respiratory diseases, malnutrition, and vector-borne illnesses, all of which can hinder physical, cognitive, and emotional growth. The one health approach, emphasizing the interconnectedness of human, animal, and environmental health, offers a holistic framework for addressing these challenges. This article serves to raise significant risks in climate change and its effects on early childhood, such as health impacts of extreme weather, deterioration of air quality, nutritional risks, and diseases. It espouses cross-sector collaboration to drive integrated services that strengthen resilience in health systems, as well as further develop environmental education for empowerment of communities. Moreover, it puts emphasis on research-informed policymaking for the effective creation of targeted interventions for vulnerabilities in children. One health approach would be appropriate for societies with resource limitations and political barriers. Adapting to this approach can sustainably lead to multidisciplinary solutions. Resilient health systems, community awareness, early childhood priority in climate action plans, and healthy and sustainable futures for current and subsequent generations can all be ensured through mitigation of adverse effects of climate change.

Introduction

The effects of climate change are affecting every aspect of life on the Earth. Young children are one of the most vulnerable populations affected by climate-related disruptions and the effect is profound during early years of life, especially during critical stages of development. The risks faced by the are unique as climate change affects the basic resources for health, such as air, water, food and shelter.[1] Disasters due to climate change leads to displacements, malnutrition, mental stress, increased risks of infections and increased mortality of children.[2] The period of early childhood is marked with rapid physical, cognitive, and emotional development. Environmental stability is essential during this period for optimal development.[3] The one health approach offers a robust framework for the mitigation of adverse effects of climate change through the interconnectedness of human, animal, and environmental health.[4] This paper discusses on how these impacts can be addressed through the one health approach holistically and mitigation of the threats posed by climate change through coordinated action to protect early childhood development.

A One Health Approach: An Integrated Strategy

All health problems are interconnected to different sectors and systems. The one health approach emphasizes the interconnectedness of human, animal, and environmental health. Through multisectoral collaborations, the one health approach provides a practical, holistic response for mitigating the effects of climate change on early child development.

Cross-Sectoral CollaborationServices related to early child development encompass the specific issues in health, social, educational domains. Many issues related to these services are faced by families and these issues are interrelated to each other. A synergistic approach is needed for the delivery of these services to ensure the holistic development of children. Therefore, it calls for collaboration across all sectors and levels of service to meet the needs of children and families.[13] Sustainable health outcomes are often anchored by cross-sectoral collaboration and coordination, within a multidisciplinary inter-disciplinary and transdisciplinary context. Universal strategies: Cross-sectoral collaboration in the one health approach to mitigate climate impacts on early childhood development to illustrate, the health and agricultural sectors can collaborate to promote sustainable farming methods. It will contribute towards food security and nutrition initiatives targeting children and pregnant women.[14] There could be a development of coordination between the health, agriculture, education sectors, and government policy as well. A climate resilient health system is defined as the one that can avoid, prepare for, mitigate, respond to and recover from the impacts of climate change shocks and stress.[15] Health systems are better able to anticipate and respond to the health risks of climate change if they are designed in a way that makes them resilient to climate impacts, incorporated within a one health approach. Challenges and the Way Forward

Legal measures are in place in the Southeast Asia region for safeguarding the children’s rights to a healthy, safe, clean, and sustainable environment. These legal protections are aligned with Target 13.3 of the SDG 2030 framework. The emphasis is on promotion of climate change education. But for effectiveness of these protections, implementation at international and national level is needed.[20] The one health approach is a highly potential strategy. Despite its potential, implementation of one health approach is complex and requires to tackle several challenges. These challenges include resource limitations, political barriers, and inadequate policy integration.[14] Nonetheless, prioritizing early childhood within climate action plans is an essential step for fostering resilient societies. Collaborations between governments, non-profits, and local communities are necessary to support one health initiatives that address the health needs of young children in climate-affected areas. By advocating for sustainable practices, improving healthcare infrastructure, and promoting resilience-building initiatives, a one health approach can offer tangible benefits to both children and their communities.

Conclusions

The issue of climate change is a deep matter for early childhood development and needs immediate coordinated action. The one health approach will give a framework for tackling health, environmental, and social issues regarding climate change on young children. Cross-sectoral integration strategies, the promotion of climate-resilient health systems, and education of communities can help build a sustainable future for vulnerable populations. One health perspective on how to confront the effects of climate change will benefit the current generation and preposition subsequent generations to thrive in a healthier, more resilient world.

The Bohr’s Phenomenon for the class of K-quasiconformal harmonic mappings
Raju Biswas and Rajib Mandal

Abstract
. The primary objective of this paper is to establish several sharp versions of improved Bohr inequality, refined Bohr-type inequality, and refined Bohr-Rogosinski inequality for the class of K K -quasiconformal sense-preserving harmonic mappings f = h + g ¯ f=h+\overline{g} in the unit disk 𝔻 := { z ∈ ℂ : | z | < 1 } \mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\} . In order to achieve these objectives, we employ the non- negative quantity S ρ ​ ( h ) S_{\rho}(h) and the concept of replacing the initial coefficients of the majorant series by the absolute values of the analytic function and its derivative, as well as other various settings. Moreover, we obtain the sharp Bohr-Rogosinski radius for harmonic mappings in the unit disk by replacing the bounding condition on the analytic function h h with the half-plane condition.

Introduction and Preliminaries

Let
f
be a bounded analytic function on the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with the Taylor series expansion

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}.

(1.1)

Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=\rho\leq\frac{1}{3},

(1.2)

where
\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|
. It is observed that, if
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z_{0})|=1
for some point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
, then
f(z)
reduces to a unimodular constant function (see
[
27
, Strict Maximum Principle (Complex Version), P. 88]
).
In this context, the quantity
1/3
is known as Bohr radius and it can’t be improved. The inequality (
1.2
) is known as the Bohr inequality.
In fact, H. Bohr
[
17
]
derived the inequality (
1.2
) for values of
r\leq 1/6
. However, subsequently Weiner, Riesz, and Schur
[
22
]
independently improved it to
1/3
.

Analytic functions of the form (
1.1
) with modulus less than 1 satisfying (
1.2
) for
|z|\leq 1/3
, are said to satisfy the classical Bohr phenomenon. The concept of Bohr phenomenon can be generalized to the class
\mathcal{F}

consisting of analytic functions of the form (
1.1
) which map from
\mathbb{D}
into a given domain
G\subseteq\mathbb{C}
such that
f(\mathbb{D})\subseteq G
.
The class
\mathcal{F}
is said to satisfy the Bohr phenomenon if there exists largest radius
\rho_{\Theta}\in(0,1)
such that (
1.2
) holds for
|z|=\rho\leq\rho_{\Theta}
.
Here
\rho_{\Theta}
is known as Bohr radius for the class
\mathcal{F}
.

In addition to the notion of the Bohr radius, there is another concept known as the Rogosinski radius
[
50
]
, which is defined as follows:
Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|f(z)|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then, for every
N\geq 1
, we have

\left|S_{N}(z)\right|=\left|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}a_{n}z^{n}\right|<1
in the disk
|z|<1/2
, where
S_{N}(z)
denotes partial sum of
f
. The number
1/2
is the best possible. Motivated
by the Rogosinski radius, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
33
]
have considered the Bohr-Rogosinski sum
R_{N}^{f}(z)
which is defined as

\displaystyle R_{N}^{f}(z):=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n},\quad\text{where}\quad N\in\mathbb{N}.

It is evident that
|S_{N}(z)|=\left|f(z)-\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\right|\leq R_{N}^{f}(z)
. Moreover, the Bohr-Rogosinski sum

R_{N}^{f}(z)
is related to the classical Bohr sum (Majorant series) in which
N=1
and
f(z)
is replaced by
f(0)
. Let
f
be an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|<1

in
\mathbb{D}
. Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
33
]
defined the Bohr-Rogosinski radius as the largest number
\rho_{0}\in(0,1)
such that the inequality
R_{N}^{f}(z)\leq 1
holds for
|z|<\rho_{0}
.

Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[

]
obtained the following results pertaining to the Bohr-Rogosinski radius.

Theorem A
.

[
33
]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}\leq 1

for
|z|=\rho\leq R_{N}
, where
R_{N}
is the positive root of the equation
\psi_{N}(\rho)=0
,
\psi_{N}(\rho)=2(1+\rho)\rho^{N}-(1-\rho)^{2}
. The radius
R_{N}
is the best possible. Moreover,

\displaystyle|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}\leq 1

for
|z|=\rho\leq R_{N}^{\prime}
, where
R_{N}^{\prime}
is the positive root of the equation
(1+\rho)\rho^{N}-(1-\rho)^{2}=0
. The radius
R_{N}^{\prime}
is the best possible.

In addition, if
f(z)

in

relative to

Let
f=u+iv
be a complex-valued function in a simply connected domain
\Omega
. If
f
satisfies the Laplace equation
\Delta f=4f_{z\overline{z}}=0
, then
f
is said to be harmonic in
\Omega
. In other words, the functions
u
and
v
are real harmonic in
\Omega
. It should be noted that every harmonic mapping
f
has the canonical representation
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and

g
are analytic in
\Omega
, known respectively as the analytic and co-analytic parts of
f
, and
\overline{g(z)}
denotes the complex conjugate of
g(z)
. This representation is
unique up to an additive constant (see
[
23
]
). The inverse function theorem and a result of Lewy
[
39
]
shows that a harmonic function
f
is locally univalent in
\Omega
if, and only if, the Jacobian of
f
, defined by
J_{f}(z):=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}
is non-zero in
\Omega
. A harmonic mapping
f
is locally univalent and sense-preserving
in
\Omega
if, and only if,
J_{f}(z)>0
in
\Omega
or equivalently if
h^{\prime}\not=0
in
\Omega

and the dilatation
\omega_{f}:=\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
of
f
has the property that
|\omega_{f}|<1
in
\Omega
(see
[
39
]
).

If a locally univalent and sense-preserving harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
satisfies the condition
|g^{\prime}(z)/h^{\prime}(z)|\leq k<1
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
f
is said to be

K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping on
\mathbb{D}
, where
K=(1+k)/(1-k)\geq 1
(see
[
31
,
46
]
). Clearly,
k\to 1
corresponds to the limiting case
K\to\infty
.

In 2018, Kayumov
et al.

[
37
]
established the harmonic extension of the classical Bohr theorem and obtained the following results.

Theorem E
.

[
37
]
Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, where
h(z)
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\rho^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|\rho^{n}\leq\|h(z)\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\leq\frac{K+1}{5K+1}.

The number
(K+1)/(5K+1)
is sharp. Moreover,

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)\rho^{n}\leq\|h(z)\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\leq\frac{K+1}{3K+1}.

The number
(K+1)/(3K+1)
is sharp.

Theorem F
.

[
37
]
Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, where
h(z)
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\rho^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|b_{n}|\rho^{n}\leq\|h(z)\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\leq\rho_{K},

where
\rho_{K}
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}+\left(\frac{K-1}{K+1}\right)\rho^{2}\sqrt{\frac{1+\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{3}}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-1}=\frac{1}{2}.

The number
\rho_{K}
cannot be replaced by a number greater than
R=R(K)
, where
R
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{4R}{1-R}\left(\frac{K}{K+1}\right)+2\left(\frac{K-1}{K+1}\right)\log(1-R)=1.

In 2018, Kayumov
et al.

[
37
]
established the following result by imposing the half-plane condition in place of the bounding condition on
h
.

Theorem G
.

Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, where
h(z)
satisfies the conditions
\text{Re}\;h(z)\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
h(0)=a_{0}>0
. Then

\displaystyle a_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\rho^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|\rho^{n}\leq 1\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\leq\frac{K+1}{5K+1}.

The number
(K+1)/(5K+1)
is sharp.

In light of the aforementioned findings, several questions naturally arise with regard to this study.

Question 1.1
.

Can we establish a sharply improved Bohr inequality and a sharply refined Bohr-type inequality using non-negative
S_{\rho}(h)
without compromising the radius?

Question 1.2
.

Can we establish the refined Bohr-Rogosinski inequality of Theorem E?

Question 1.3
.

Can we establish several sharply improved versions of the Bohr inequality of Theorem E by the idea of replacing the initial coefficients of the majorant series with the absolute values of the analytic function and its derivative?

Question 1.4
.

Can we establish the sharply refined version of the Bohr-type inequality in the context of Theorem F by replacing the initial coefficients of the majorant series by the absolute values of the analytic function and its derivative?

Question 1.5
.

Can we establish the sharp version of Theorem F?

Question 1.6
.

Can we establish the sharp Bohr-Rogosinski inequality in the context of Theorem G?

The purpose of this paper is primarily to provide the affirmative answers to Questions
1.1
-
1.6
.

The following lemmas are needed for this paper and will be used to prove the main results.

Lemma 2.1
.

[

, Pick’s invariant form of Schwarz’s lemma]
Suppose
f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with

, then

\displaystyle|f(z)|\leq\frac{|f(0)|+|z|}{1+|f(0)||z|}\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

.

[
20
,
51
]
Suppose
f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with

, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left|f^{(n)}(z)\right|}{n!}\leq\frac{1-|f(z)|^{2}}{(1-|z|)^{n-1}(1-|z|^{2})}\quad\text{for each}\quad n\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad|z|<1.

.

[

]

h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}\rho^{n}\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\rho^{n}\;\;\text{for}\;\;|z|=\rho<1.

By employing the concept of quasi-subordination and the result derived in

3.
Main results

In the following, we obtain the sharp improved version of Bohr inequality in the settings of Theorem E by utilizing the non-negative quantity

S_{\rho}(h)
without reducing the radius.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, where
h(z)
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\rho^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|\rho^{n}+\frac{8K^{2}(3K+1)^{2}}{(5K+1)^{2}(K+1)^{2}}\frac{S_{\rho}(h)}{\pi}\leq\|h(z)\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\leq\rho_{0}=\frac{K+1}{5K+1}.

The numbers
8K^{2}(3K+1)^{2}/((5K+1)^{2}(K+1)^{2})
and
(K+1)/(5K+1)
cannot be replaced by larger values.

Proof.

For simplicity, we suppose that
\|h(z)\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. In view of lemma
2.2
, we have
|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}
for
n\geq 1
. Since
f
is locally univalent and
K
-quasiconformal sense-preserving harmonic mapping on
\mathbb{D}
, Schwarz’s lemma gives that the dilatation
\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|\omega(z)|\leq k
,
i.e.
,
|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|h^{\prime}(z)|
in
\mathbb{D}
, where
K=(1+k)/(1-k)\geq 1
,
k\in[0,1)
. Let
|a_{0}|=a\in[0,1)
.
By Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}\rho^{n}\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\rho^{n}\leq k^{2}(1-a^{2})^{2}\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}.

(3.1)

Using (
3.1
) and in view of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|\rho^{n}\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}\rho^{n}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho^{n}\right)^{1/2}\leq k(1-a^{2})\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}.

From the definition of
S_{\rho}(h)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{S_{\rho}(h)}{\pi}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\rho^{2n}\leq(1-a^{2})^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\rho^{2n}=(1-a^{2})^{2}\frac{\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}.

(3.2)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\rho^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|\rho^{n}+\lambda\frac{S_{\rho}(h)}{\pi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a+(1+k)(1-a^{2})\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}+\lambda(1-a^{2})^{2}\frac{\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+F_{1}(a,\rho),

where

\displaystyle F_{1}(a,\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+k)(1-a^{2})\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}+(1-a^{2})^{2}\frac{\lambda\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}-(1-a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-a^{2})}{2}\left(1+\left(\frac{2(1+k)\rho}{1-\rho}-1\right)+(1-a^{2})\frac{2\lambda\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{2}}-\frac{2}{1+a}\right).

Differentiating partially with respect to
\rho
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}F_{1}(a,\rho)=\frac{(1+k)(1-a^{2})}{(1-\rho)^{2}}+(1-a^{2})^{2}\frac{2\lambda\rho(1+\rho^{2})}{(1-\rho^{2})^{3}}>0.

Therefore
F_{1}(a,\rho)
is a monotonically increasing function of
\rho
in
[0,1)
and it follows that
F_{1}(a,\rho)\leq F_{1}(a,\rho_{0})
for
\rho\leq\rho_{0}=1/(2k+3)
. Now,

\displaystyle F_{1}(a,\rho_{0})=\frac{(1-a^{2})}{2}\left(1+(1-a^{2})\frac{\lambda(2k+3)^{2}}{8(k+1)^{2}(k+2)^{2}}-\frac{2}{1+a}\right).

Let
F_{2}(a)=1+\lambda(2k+3)^{2}(1-a^{2})/(8(k+1)^{2}(k+2)^{2})-2/(1+a)
,
a\in[0,1)
. It is easy to see that

. Let

\displaystyle|h_{8}(-\rho)|+|h_{8}^{\prime}(-\rho)|\rho+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|A_{n}|\rho^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|A_{0}|}+\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|A_{n}|^{2}\rho^{2n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|B_{n}|\rho^{n}

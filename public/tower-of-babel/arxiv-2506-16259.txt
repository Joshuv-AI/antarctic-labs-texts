Two-dimensional Rademacher walk
Satyaki Bhattacharya, Stanislav Volkov

Abstract
We study a generalisation of the one-dimensional Rademacher random walk introduced in [ 2 ] to ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} (for d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , the Rademacher random walk is always transient, as follows from Theorem 8.8 in [ 4 ] ). This walk is defined as the sum of a sequence of independent steps, where each step goes in one of the four possible directions with equal probability, and the size of the n n th step is a n a_{n} where { a n } \{a_{n}\} is a given sequence of positive integers. We establish some general conditions under which the walk is recurrent, respectively, transient.



Keywords
: recurrence, transience, Rademacher distribution, non-homogeneous Markov chains.

AMS subject classification
: 60G50, 60J10.

1
Introduction

A one-dimensional Rademacher walk was introduced in
[
2
]
, as a generalisation of a simple symmetric random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
with step sizes following some pre-determined sequence of integers
a_{1},a_{2},\dots
(as opposed to all step sizes being one). It was shown that this walk is recurrent for some very slowly growing sequences, and transient, for example, if
a_{k}=\lfloor k^{\gamma}\rfloor
for all
\gamma>0
. Some further generalisations of the Rademacher walk with non-integer step sizes were also considered, and they were studied further in
[
1
]
. This paper studies the extension of the walk to higher dimensions.

We say that
\xi
has a
two-dimensional Rademacher distribution
, if
\xi
takes one of the values
\{\mathrm{e}_{1},\mathrm{e}_{2},-\mathrm{e}_{1},-\mathrm{e}_{2}\}
where
\mathrm{e}_{1}=(0,1)
,
\mathrm{e}_{2}=(1,0)
are the unit vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with equal probability
1/4
.

Let
\xi_{n}
,
n=1,2,\dots
be a sequence of i.i.d. two-dimensional Rademacher random variables. Let
a_{n}
be a deterministic sequence of positive real numbers. The two-dimensional Rademacher walk is defined as
S_{n}=Z_{1}+Z_{2}+\dots+Z_{n}
where
Z_{i}=a_{i}\xi_{i}
.

Definition 1
.

We say that the walk
S_{n}
is transient if
\|S_{n}\|\to\infty
a.s.

We say that the walk
S_{n}
is recurrent, if
S_{n}=z
for infinitely many
n
s a.s., for each
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Note that the transience and recurrence are zero-one events. Also, in general, we do not have the dichotomy between recurrence vs. transience; for example, if all
a_{n}\equiv 2
then the walk is neither recurrent nor transient according to our definition, as it returns to the origin (further denoted by
\mathbf{0}=(0,0)
) infinitely often and yet never visits points with odd coordinates.

We study the conditions for transience in Section
2
and for recurrence in Section
3
.

2
Transience

2.1
Non-decreasing steps

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
a_{n}
is a non-decreasing sequence of integers converging to infinity (i.e.
a_{n}\uparrow\infty
). Then
S_{n}
is transient.

First, we prove a necessary technical result.

Lemma 1
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\dots
be a sequence of distinct positive integers, and let
T_{k}=d_{1}\varepsilon_{1}+\dots+d_{k}\varepsilon_{k}
where
\varepsilon_{i}
are i.i.d. Rademacher. Then for every integer
\displaystyle m\geq\max_{1\leq i\leq k}d_{i}
and
M\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
we have

\mathbb{P}(T_{k}=M\,\mathrm{mod}\ m)\leq\frac{C\log k}{k}

for some universal constant
C>0
.

Proof.

By the result of
[
6
]
, for each
z\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
\mathbb{P}(T_{k}=z)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{k^{3/2}}
. On the other hand, by Hoeffding’s inequality

\mathbb{P}(|T_{k}|\geq m\sqrt{2k\log k})\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{4\,m^{2}\,k\,\log k}{4d_{1}^{2}+\dots+4d_{k}^{2}}\right)\leq 2\exp(-\log k)=\frac{2}{k}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(T_{k}=M\,\mathrm{mod}\ m)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(T_{k}=M\,\mathrm{mod}\ m,|T_{k}|<m\sqrt{2k\log k})+\mathbb{P}(|T_{k}|\geq m\sqrt{2k\log k})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(T_{k}=M+\ell d_{k+1}\text{ for some $\ell\in\mathbb{Z}$ s.t. }|M+m\ell|<m\sqrt{2k\log k})+\frac{2}{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+2m\sqrt{2k\log k}}{m}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{P}(T_{k}=z)+\frac{2}{k}\leq\frac{3m\sqrt{k\log k}}{m}\times\frac{C_{1}}{k^{3/2}}+\frac{2}{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{3C_{1}\sqrt{\log k}}{k}+\frac{2}{k}\leq\frac{C\log k}{k}

for
C=3C_{1}+2>0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
b_{1}=a_{1}\geq 1
,
b_{2}=\min\{a_{n}:a_{n}>b_{1}\}\geq a_{1}+1
,

b_{3}=\min\{a_{n}:a_{n}>b_{2}\}\geq a_{2}+1
, etc.
Denote by
m_{i}=\card\{n:\ a_{n}=b_{i}\}
,
i=1,2,\dots
. Then the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
can be represented as

\underbrace{b_{1},b_{1},\dots b_{1},}_{m_{1}}\underbrace{b_{2},b_{2},\dots b_{2},}_{m_{2}}\underbrace{b_{3},b_{3},\dots b_{3},}_{m_{3}}\dots\underbrace{b_{j},b_{j},\dots b_{j},}_{m_{j}}b_{j+1},\dots.

Let
k_{j+1}=m_{1}+m_{2}+\dots+m_{j}+1=\inf\{n:\ a_{n}=b_{j+1}\}
be the first index of the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
equal to
b_{j+1}
.

Let
\kappa_{i}=1
if
\xi_{i}\in\{\mathrm{e}_{1},-\mathrm{e}_{1}\}
and
=0
otherwise. Let
\varepsilon_{i}=\pm 1
equal to the non-zero coordinate of
\xi_{i}
, Then
\{\kappa_{i}\}
are i.i.d. Bernoulli and
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}
are i.i.d. Rademacher; moreover, these two sequences are independent. The values of
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
for which
\kappa_{i}=1
(
=0
resp.) correspond to horizontal (vertical, resp.) steps. Formally, let

H_{n}=\{1\leq i<n:\ \kappa_{i}=1\},\quad V_{n}=\{1\leq i<n:\ \kappa_{i}=0\}

be the indices corresponding to horizontal (resp. vertical) steps. Note that
Z_{i}=X_{i}\mathrm{e}_{1}+Y_{i}\mathrm{e}_{2}
where

X_{i}=\begin{cases}a_{i}\varepsilon_{i},&\text{if }\kappa_{i}=1;\\
0,&\text{if }\kappa_{i}=0\end{cases}\qquad Y_{i}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\kappa_{i}=1;\\
a_{i}\varepsilon_{i},&\text{if }\kappa_{i}=0,\end{cases}

and denote

S_{n}=S^{X}_{n}\mathrm{e}_{1}+S^{Y}_{n}\mathrm{e}_{2}\quad\text{ where }S_{n}^{X}=X_{1}+\dots+X_{n},\quad S_{n}^{Y}=Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{n}.

Fix a large integer
j
and let

A_{j}=\left\{\card\{b_{i},i\in H_{k_{j}}\}<\frac{j}{3}\text{ \ or \ }\card\{b_{i},i\in V_{k_{j}}\}<\frac{j}{3}\right\}.

For each
1\leq i<k_{j}
,
\kappa_{i}
equals
0
or
1
with equal probability
\frac{1}{2}
. Hence, by Hoeffding’s inequality, there exists a constant
c_{0}
such that

\mathbb{P}(A_{j})\leq 2e^{-c_{0}j},

i.e., there are at least
j/3
horizontal and
j/3
vertical steps with probability very close to one. As a result, on
A_{j}^{c}
, we can write

S^{X}_{k_{j}-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{h_{j}}b^{\prime}_{i}\varepsilon^{\prime}_{i},\quad S^{Y}_{k_{j}-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{v_{j}}b^{\prime\prime}_{i}\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}_{i}

where
h_{j}=\card\{H_{k_{j}-1}\}
,
v_{j}=\card\{V_{k_{j}-1}\}
, the positive integer sequences
\{b_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{h_{j}}
and
\{b_{i}^{\prime\prime}\}_{i=1}^{v_{j}}
are non-decreasing and each has at least
j/3

distinct
elements, while
\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime},\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime\prime}
are i.i.d. Rademacher.

Now, by Lemma
1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S^{X}_{k_{j}-1}=0\,\mathrm{mod}\ b_{j+1}\mid A_{j}^{c})\leq\frac{C\log(j/3)}{j/3},\qquad\mathbb{P}(S^{Y}_{k_{j}-1}=0\,\mathrm{mod}\ b_{j+1}\mid A_{j}^{c})\leq\frac{C\log(j/3)}{j/3}

and, moreover, conditioned on the sequence
\kappa_{i}
and
A_{j}^{c}
, the events above are independent.
Since for the walk to hit
\mathbf{0}
at time
n
,
k_{j+1}\leq n\leq k_{j+2}-1
, we need that both its coordinates are divisible by
b_{j}
at time
k_{j}-1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{n}=\mathbf{0}\text{ for some }n\in[k_{j+1},k_{j+2}))\leq\mathbb{P}(A_{j})+\left(\frac{C\log(j/3)}{j/3}\right)^{2}=O\left(\frac{\log^{2}j}{j^{2}}\right).

(1)

Since the quantity in (
1
) is summable in
j
, by the Borel-Cantelli lemma we conclude that the event
\{S_{n}=\mathbf{0}\}
occurs finitely often. By the same token, we get that
\{S_{n}={\bf w}\}
occurs finitely often for each
{\bf w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Hence the transience follows.
∎

2.2
Transience for non-integerer sequences
\{a_{n}\}

In this section, we allow
a_{n}
to take any positive real values (not only integers). We will use the same Definition
1
for the transience of the walk.

Definition 2
.

Let
a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
n=1,2,\dots
. We call the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
good, if for each large
n
there is a positive integer
K_{n}
such that

\frac{K_{n}}{\log_{2}a_{n}}\to 1\text{ as }n\to\infty,

and a subset of
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
with
K_{n}
distinct elements, say
\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{K_{n}}\}
, such that
i_{K_{n}}\equiv n
and
a_{i_{k+1}}\geq 2a_{i_{k}}
for every
k=1,2,\dots,K_{n}-1
.

Later in the Section, in Proposition
1
, we present a sufficient condition for a sequence to be good.

Definition 3
.

Let
r,s\geq 1
be some constants. We call the sequence
\{a_{n}\}

(r,s)
-monotone, if for all large
n
we have
a_{n}\leq sa_{m}
whenevever
m\geq rn
.

Remark 1
.

Note that the case
r=s=1
corresponds in the above definition to the monotonicity in the usual sense.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that the sequence
a_{n}
is good and
(r,s)
-monotone, and for some
\epsilon>0
we have
a_{n}\geq(\log n)^{1/2+\epsilon}
for all sufficiently large
n
. Then the two-dimensional Rademacher walk is transient.

Proof.

To show transience, it suffices to show that for each
(x,y)\in R^{2}
we have
S_{n}\in(x,x+1)\times(y,y+1)
for at most finitely many
n
s a.s. W.l.o.g. we can assume that
a_{n}>1
for all
n
(this is, of course, true for all large
n
due to the assumption of the theorem, and a few initial values of the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
do not affect transience).

The proof will follow an argument involving anti-concentration inequalities on two different scales, similarly to the method used in
[
1
]
. Define the event

\displaystyle A_{n}=\{

The walk makes at least
n/5
horizontal and
n/5
vertical steps

\displaystyle\text{among the last $n/2$ steps of the $n$--step walk}\}

By Hoeffding’s inequality, it is easy to check that
\mathbb{P}(A_{n}^{c})\leq 2e^{-0.01n}
.

Let
T_{m}
be a one-dimensional Rademacher random walk with step sizes
d_{1},d_{2},\dots
. By the Kolmogorov-Rogozin inequality
[
5
]
, there exists a universal constant
c_{1}>0
such that if all
d_{i}>D
for some constant
D>0
, then
\mathbb{P}(T_{m}\in[x-D,x+D])\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{m}}
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and all
m\geq 1
.

Let
n_{1}:=\lfloor n/2\rfloor
and consider the walk
S_{n}
during the times from
n_{1}+1
to
n
. Let

\displaystyle D=D_{n}=(\log n_{1})^{1/2+\epsilon}\geq\min_{n_{1}<i\leq n}a_{i}.

(2)

Assuming
A_{n}
, we then get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=n_{1}+1}^{n}Z_{i}\cdot\mathrm{e}_{1}\in\left(x-D,x+D\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{n}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=n_{1}+1}^{n}Z_{i}\cdot\mathrm{e}_{2}\in(y-D,y+D)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{n}}

for some
c_{1}>0
, since
a_{i}\geq D
for all
i\geq n_{1}+1
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(S^{\prime}\in(x-D,x+D)\times(y-D,y+D)\right)\leq\frac{c_{2}^{2}}{n}+\mathbb{P}(A_{n}^{c})=\frac{c_{1}^{2}+o(1)}{n}.

(3)

where
S^{\prime}=\sum_{i=n_{1}+1}^{n}Z_{i}
.

Now let us observe the walk during its first
n_{0}:=\left\lfloor\frac{n_{1}}{r}\right\rfloor\leq n_{1}
steps. By the assumption of the theorem, it is possible to extract a subsequence
i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{K}
from
\{1,2,\dots,n_{0}\}
of size

K=(1+\gamma_{n_{0}})\log_{2}a_{n_{0}}\quad\text{where }\gamma_{n_{0}}\to 0

such that
a_{i_{k+1}}\geq 2a_{i_{k}}
for
k=1,2,\dots,K-1
, and
i_{K}=n_{0}
.

Figure 1:
The disjoint union of unit squares in case
K=2
; the left-bottom corners of each square are the elements of
B
.

Let
I=I=(i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{K-\ell})
where
\ell
is the smallest positive integer such that
2^{\ell-1}\geq s
, and
S^{\prime\prime}=\sum_{j\in I}Z_{j}
. Every step
Z_{j}
where
j\in I
, can be in one of the four directions, creating
4^{K-1}
possible values. Since
a_{i_{1}}>1
and the sequence is ‘‘super-doubling’’, no two such values lie in the same square with side
1
; let us denote by
B
the set of all possible values of
S^{\prime\prime}
. As a result, for any
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\bigcup_{(x,y)\in B}(x^{\prime}-x,x^{\prime}-x+1)\times(y^{\prime}-y,y^{\prime}-y+1)\subseteq(x^{\prime}-\tilde{D},x^{\prime}+\tilde{D})\times(y^{\prime}-\tilde{D},y^{\prime}+\tilde{D})

is a collection of
disjoint
unit squares (see Figure
1
); here

\tilde{D}=1+a_{i_{1}}+a_{i_{2}}+\dots+a_{i_{K-\ell}}\leq 2a_{i_{K-\ell}}\leq 2^{1-\ell}a_{i_{K}}=2^{1-\ell}a_{n_{0}}\leq\frac{a_{n_{0}}}{s}\leq D

where the last inequality follows from
(r,s)
-monotonicity by (
2
) and the fact that
n_{1}\leq n_{0}/r
. Also note that
S^{\prime\prime}
has a uniform distribution over the set
B
and

\mathrm{card}(B)=4^{K-\ell}=4^{(1+\gamma_{n_{0}})\log_{2}a_{n_{0}}-\ell}=\frac{a_{n_{0}}^{2(1+\gamma_{n_{0}})}}{4^{\ell}}\geq\frac{\left(\log\lfloor n/(2r)\rfloor\right)^{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon\right)\cdot 2(1+\gamma_{n_{0}})}}{4^{\ell}}>(\log n)^{1+\epsilon}

where the last inequality holds for all large
n
since
|\gamma_{n_{0}}|\to 0
as the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
is good.

Next, for every
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(S^{\prime}+S^{\prime\prime}\in(x^{\prime},x^{\prime}+1)\times(y^{\prime},y^{\prime}+1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(x,y)\in B}\mathbb{P}(\{S^{\prime\prime}=(x,y)\}\cap\{S^{\prime}\in(x^{\prime}-x,x^{\prime}-x+1)\times(y^{\prime}-y,y^{\prime}-y+1)\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(x,y)\in B}\mathbb{P}(S^{\prime\prime}=(x,y))\cdot\mathbb{P}(S^{\prime}\in(x^{\prime}-x,x^{\prime}-x+1)\times(y^{\prime}-y,y^{\prime}-y+1))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\log n)^{1+\epsilon}}\cdot\sum_{(x,y)\in B}\mathbb{P}(S^{\prime}\in(x^{\prime}-x,x^{\prime}-x+1)\times(y^{\prime}-y,y^{\prime}-y+1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\log n)^{1+\epsilon}}\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(S^{\prime}\in\bigcup_{(x,y)\in B}(x^{\prime}-x,x^{\prime}-x+1)\times(y^{\prime}-y,y^{\prime}-y+1)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\log n)^{1+\epsilon}}\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(S^{\prime}\in(x^{\prime}-\tilde{D},x^{\prime}+\tilde{D})\times(y^{\prime}-\tilde{D},y^{\prime}+\tilde{D})\right)\leq\frac{c_{1}^{2}+o(1)}{n(\log n)^{1+\epsilon}}

by (
3
) and the fact that
\tilde{D}\leq D
.

Finally, denoting by
S^{\prime\prime\prime}=\sum_{i\in\{1,\dots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}\setminus I}Z_{i}
the sum of the remaining steps, since
S^{\prime},S^{\prime\prime},S^{\prime\prime}
are independent, for all
(x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\in(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime}+1)\times(y^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime\prime}+1)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(S^{\prime}+S^{\prime\prime}+S^{\prime\prime\prime}\in(x^{\prime},x^{\prime}+1)\times(y^{\prime},y^{\prime}+1)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}(S^{\prime}+S^{\prime\prime}\in(x^{\prime},x^{\prime}+1)\times(y^{\prime},y^{\prime}+1))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}^{2}+o(1)}{n(\log n)^{1+\epsilon}}

which is summable over
n
as
\epsilon>0
. Hence, the result follows from the Borel-Cantelli lemma.
∎

The next statement tells us when we can apply Theorem
2
.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose there is some
C>0
such that
a_{1}=1
,
a_{n}\geq 1
,
|a_{n+1}-a_{n}|\leq C
for all
n
, and
a_{n}\to\infty
. Then the sequence
a_{n}
is good.

Remark 2
.

The above proposition covers the case when
a_{n}=n^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Fix an
n
and let
j_{1}=n
.
Now let us find
j_{2}
such that
a_{j_{2}}\approx\frac{1}{2}a_{j_{1}}
. Since the distances between consecutive
a_{i}
s do not exceed
C
, we can find some
j_{2}
such that

\frac{a_{n}}{2}-C=\frac{a_{j_{1}}}{2}-C<a_{j_{2}}\leq\frac{a_{j_{1}}}{2}.

Similarly, we can find
j_{3}
such that
a_{j_{3}}\leq a_{j_{2}}/2
and at the same time

a_{j_{3}}>\frac{a_{j_{2}}}{2}-C\geq\frac{a_{n}}{2^{2}}-\frac{C}{2}-C.

By induction, we get that

a_{j_{k}}\geq\frac{a_{n}}{2^{k-1}}-C\left(1+\frac{1}{2}+\dots+\frac{1}{2^{k-2}}\right)=\frac{a_{n}+C/2}{2^{k-1}}-2C,

and we can continue the process as long as
a_{j_{k}}\geq 1
. Hence, the maximum possible
k
for which the process is feasible is

\left\lfloor\log_{2}\left(\frac{a_{n}+C/2}{C+1/2}\right)\right\rfloor=(1+\gamma_{n})\log_{2}a_{n}

where
\gamma_{n}\to 0
since
\log_{2}a_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

3
Recurrence for some general sequences

Let
B=\{b_{1},b_{2},\dots\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
. We say that the set
B
is
good
if for any
b\in B
there are infinitely many coprimes of
b
in
B
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
is good. Then there exists a sequence
a_{1},a_{2},\dots
which contains all elements of the set
B
, and each element only finitely many times, such that the
a
-walk
S_{n}
is recurrent.

Lemma 2
.

Let
C>0
and
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
\|z\|\leq C
, and
b^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime\prime}
are positive integers which are coprime. Then there exist three positive integers
c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime},N_{0}
such that
N_{0}
depends on
c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime}
and
C
only and

\mathbb{P}(T_{m}=z\text{ for some }m\leq N_{0})\geq 1/2

where

T_{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{Z}_{i}\text{ with }\tilde{Z}_{i}=b^{\prime}(\xi_{i,1}^{\prime}+\dots+\xi_{i,c^{\prime}}^{\prime})+b^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{i,1}^{\prime\prime}+\dots+\xi_{i,c^{\prime\prime}}^{\prime\prime})

and
\xi^{\prime}_{i,k},\xi^{\prime\prime}_{i,k},
are i.i.d. two-dimensional Rademacher random variables.

Proof.

Let
c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime}
be such that
c^{\prime}b^{\prime}-c^{\prime\prime}b^{\prime\prime}=1
; the existence of such
c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime}
follows from Bézout’s identity. This implies that
\mathbb{P}(\tilde{Z}_{i}=\mathbf{f})\geq 0
for each
\mathbf{f}=\mathrm{e}_{1},-\mathrm{e}_{1},\mathrm{e}_{2},-\mathrm{e}_{2}
and thus the Markov chain
T_{m}
is irreducible on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Next, by the Chung–Fuchs theorem (see
[
3
]
)
T_{m}
is recurrent and thus there exists
N_{0}
such that

\inf_{z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\ \|z^{\prime}\|\leq C}\mathbb{P}(T_{m}\text{ visits }z^{\prime}\text{ for some }m\leq N_{0})\geq\frac{1}{2}

which concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

The construction of the sequence
a_{i}
will be done recursively. Let
n_{0}=0
and suppose that we have already constructed the sequence up to time
i=n_{k}
. Let
\alpha_{k}=\max\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n_{k}}\}
, then
\|S_{n_{k}}\|\leq\alpha_{k}n_{k}
. Find the element of
B
not already used, with the smallest index, call it
b
, and let
b^{\prime}
be another element of
B
, not already used so far, such that
b^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime\prime}
are coprimes. By Lemma
2
there exist
c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime},N_{0}
corresponding to
b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime}
and
C:=\alpha_{k}n_{k}
. Now define

a_{n_{k}+i+1}=\begin{cases}c^{\prime},&\text{if }i\,\mathrm{mod}\ (c^{\prime}+c^{\prime\prime})\in\{0,1,\dots,c^{\prime}-1\},\\
c^{\prime\prime},&\text{if }i\,\mathrm{mod}\ (c^{\prime}+c^{\prime\prime})\in\{c^{\prime},c^{\prime}+1,\dots,c^{\prime}+c^{\prime\prime}-1\}\end{cases}

for
i=0,1,2,\dots,(c^{\prime}+c^{\prime\prime})N_{0}-1
. Define also
n_{k+1}=n_{k}+(c^{\prime}+c^{\prime\prime})N_{0}
.
It is clear that by following this procedure, we will eventually use all elements of
B
, and, moreover, we will use each element of
B
only finitely many times.

Now, by Lemma
2
, since
\|S_{n_{k}}\|\leq C
,

\mathbb{P}(S_{n}=0\text{ for some }n\in[n_{k}+1,n_{k+1}]\mid\mathcal{F}_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Consequently,
S_{n}=0
for infinitely many
n
s a.s., by Lévy’s extension of the Borel-Cantelli lemma
(see e.g.
[
7
]
).
∎

3.1
An explicit recurrent sequence

In this section, we present an explicit example of a sequence
a_{n}
such that
a_{n}\to\infty
and yet the walk is recurrent. The elements of this sequence will consist of blocks numbered
1,2,\dots
, and the
k
th block will be a consecutive sequence of exactly
k
sub-blocks, denoted by
B_{k,1},B_{k,2},\dots,B_{k,k}
, where

\displaystyle B_{k,i}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{k,k,\dots,k,}_{L_{k,i}\text{ elements}}\,\underbrace{k-1,k-1,\dots,k-1}_{3k^{2}\text{ elements}}\quad\text{for }i=1,2,\dots,k-1;

\displaystyle B_{k,k}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{k,k,\dots,k}_{L_{k,i}\text{ elements}},\qquad\text{where }L_{k,i}=2^{2^{k^{2}+i-1}},\ i=1,2,\dots,k.

Now suppose that the sequence
{a_{n}}
is given by
\{a_{n}\}=B_{1}B_{2}\dots
where
B_{k}=B_{k,1}B_{k,2}\dots B_{k,k}
.

Theorem 4
.

A two-dimensional Rademacher walk with the above sequence is recurrent.

We begin with a (probably) well-known result.

Lemma 3
.

Consider a simple random walk
T_{n}=(X_{n},Y_{n})
on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with steps
\pm\mathrm{e}_{1},\pm\mathrm{e}_{2}
and
T_{0}=(x_{0},y_{0})
. Let
\tau_{0}=\inf\{n:\ T_{n}=\mathbf{0}\}
. Then there exists a universal constant
\alpha>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{0}<r^{3})\geq\alpha,\qquad\text{where }r=\|(x_{0},y_{0})\|.

Proof.

The fact that
\tau_{0}<\infty
a.s. follows from the recurrence of the two-dimensional walk. Let
R=r^{1.25}
and

\tau=\inf\{n:\ T_{n}=\mathbf{0}\text{ or }\|T_{n}\|\geq R\}.

Define

f(x,y)=\begin{cases}\log(x^{2}+y^{2}-1/2),\text{if }(x,y)\neq\mathbf{0};\\
-5,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

An easy calculation

1

Indeed, let
\Delta_{x,y}:=\frac{1}{4}(f(x+1,y)+f(x-1,y)+f(x,y+1)+f(x,y-1))-f(x,y)
.
Then
\exp(4\Delta_{x,y})=1-\frac{64(x^{2}-y^{2})^{2}}{(2x^{2}+2y^{2}-1)^{4}}\leq 1
when
|x|+|y|\geq 2

\exp(4\Delta_{x,y})=126e^{-5}<0.85
, and when

\xi_{n}=F(T_{n})

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(T_{\tau}\neq\mathbf{0})\leq\frac{\ln(r)}{\ln(R)}+o(1)=\frac{4}{5}+o(1).

(4)

On the other hand,

\mathbb{P}(\tau>r^{3})=o(1)\qquad\text{as }r\to\infty.

Indeed, since during

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
n_{k}
be the index of the last element in the
k
th block and

n_{k,j}
be the index of the last element in the
j
th sub-block of the
k
th block.

Note that
L_{k,j}=L_{k,j-1}^{2}
for
j\geq 2
, and also
L_{k+1,1}\gg L_{k,k}^{2}

hence, by e.g. Hoeffding’s inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|S_{n_{k,j}}|\geq k\sqrt{L_{k,j}\log L_{k,j}})\leq\frac{2+o(1)}{L_{k,j}^{2}},\qquad j=2,3,\dots,k.

(5)

Let
{E}_{k,j}=\left\{|S_{n_{k,j}}|<k\sqrt{L_{k,j}\log L_{k,j}}\right\}
.
On this event, since
L_{k,j+1}=L_{k,j}^{2}>\left(\sqrt{L_{k,j}\log L_{k,j}}\right)^{3}
, by Lemma
3
with probability at least
\alpha
the walk
T_{n}
,
n\in[n_{k,j},n_{k,j+1}-3k^{2}]
will visit the square
[0,k-1]^{2}
at least once, since this is a two-dimensional simple random walk modified such that its step sizes equal exactly
k
.

Now, Corollary 3.2 from
[
2
]
implies that with probability of order
\frac{1}{k^{2}}
, the walk starts this sub-block at a position whose
x-
and
y-
coordinates are both divisible by
k
. This follows from the fact that, with high probability, at least
k^{2}
steps were taken in each of the two directions during the last

3k^{2}
steps of the preceding block. As a result,

\mathbb{P}(A_{k,j+1}\mid{E}_{k,j})\geq\frac{c+o(1)}{k^{2}}\qquad\text{where }A_{k,j+1}=\{T_{n}=\mathbf{0}\text{ for some }n\in[n_{k,j},n_{k,j+1})\}

for some
c>0
independent of
k
and
j
.

We will use the extension of the conditional Borel-Cantelli lemma from
[
2
]
to conclude recurrence. Define
\mathcal{F}_{k,j}
as the sigma-algebra generated by the walk up to the time
n_{k,j}
. We have

\sum_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{P}(A_{k,j+1}\mid E_{k,j},\mathcal{F}_{k,j})\geq\sum_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{c+o(1)}{k^{2}}=\infty.

At the same time,

\sum_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{P}(E_{k,j}^{c})<\infty.

by (
5
).
Hence, the events
A_{k,j+1}

[

]
, thus implying recurrence of site
\mathbf{0}
. The fact that the walk visits all other
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
a.s. follows from the same argument.

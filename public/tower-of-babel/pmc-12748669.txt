From cognitive gap to innovation synergy: public cognitive evolution law and implications in the new unmanned-driven business model
Authors: Yiming Zhang, Wenying Zhuang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid advancement of digital technology provides core support for the iterative evolution of new business formats across industries. In the field of unmanned driving, the industry is undergoing a critical upgrade from assisted driving technology application to unmanned vehicle operation mode. The identification of challenges and large-scale promotion of this new business model highly depend on the public’s cognitive evaluation and emotional attitude. Thus, a systematic analysis of the public’s focus areas and attitude levels toward the new unmanned driving model holds significant practical value for advancing technology implementation and healthy industrial development. From the cognitive gap perspective, this study selects public comments from the “Apollo Go” platform as samples via text data mining. Combined with the division of public opinion communication life cycle stages, it integrates BERTopic topic modeling and BERT-BiLSTM sentiment analysis models to deconstruct the phased evolutionary characteristics of media and public opinion. It explores the mechanism of social opinion shaping public cognition and reveals the dynamic evolution of public cognitive gap in unmanned driving—from formation and fluctuation to mitigation, i.e., the gap gradually dissolves and transforms into innovative synergy. Ultimately, by integrating Diffusion of Innovations theory, this study theoretically clarifies the resolution mechanism of cognitive gaps and proposes a three-level innovation synergy strategy, thereby providing important academic basis and decision-making references for accelerating the social integration of new unmanned driving business models.Keywords: Unmanned driving, Apollo Go, BERTopic, BERT-BILSTM, Cognitive gap, Innovation synergySubject terms: Engineering; Mathematics and computing; Science, technology and society

Introduction

In recent years, science and technology have driven global progress, and the continuous advancement of digital technology has injected new vitality into traditional industries. Digital transformation has thus become an imperative for every industry. Fueled by digital transformation, new business formats are constantly emerging across various sectors1. Within the automotive industry, the advent of unmanned vehicles represents a key manifestation of its digital transformation. The innovation and iteration of unmanned driving technology reflect the bidirectional enabling effect between contemporary scientific and technological innovation and social demand. At the social level, the growing public demand for enhanced driving experience and safety continues to propel the intelligent development of automobiles. At the technical level, this field relies on multiple technological pillars, including the iterative upgrading of artificial intelligence algorithms, collaborative innovation in cloud computing platforms, the evolution of environmental perception systems (e.g., the fusion of lidar and millimeter-wave radar), and the optimization of intelligent decision-making algorithms. These technical underpinnings will significantly improve the prediction accuracy and driving safety redundancy in complex traffic scenarios, thereby establishing a multi-dimensional guarantee system for intelligent mobility. Currently, unmanned driving technology has been widely adopted by major automakers, with assisted driving becoming a standard feature in vehicles. Furthermore, intelligent driving technologies developed by companies such as Huawei and Tesla have gained broad recognition.

With the further optimization of unmanned driving technology, the concept of the new unmanned driving mode has been brought onto the agenda. Presently, unmanned taxis that are represented by Apollo Go have been deployed on the streets of various regions. The upgrade of unmanned driving technology from auxiliary functions to fully autonomous operation has become a focal point of public attention. As technical conditions are gradually fulfilled, the role of social media public opinion in shaping public acceptance has grown increasingly prominent. However, the public opinion orientation of mainstream media may widen the cognitive gap between the public and this emerging technology. Specifically, public acceptance fails to truly align with the actual development of unmanned driving technology and its business models. The existence of this cognitive gap may not only impede the promotion of unmanned driving business models but also mislead the formulation of industrial policies. Therefore, clarifying the actual state of public opinion and acceptance in the unmanned driving field, as well as identifying potential cognitive gaps, has become a key prerequisite for advancing technology implementation and industrial development.

In the absence of large-scale behavioral data, the public emotional tendencies embedded in public text data (such as news comments and social media posts) can serve as an effective and quantifiable early indicator for measuring and predicting public acceptance of new unmanned driving models. Against this backdrop, this paper focuses on analyzing user and mainstream media comment data related to “Apollo Go” on the social media platform Weibo. Drawing on the life cycle theory, it integrates the BERTopic and BERT-BiLSTM models, and combines large-model-based analysis to conduct phased analyses of topic extraction and sentiment analysis for both users and mainstream media. The objective is to explore the evolutionary law of public cognition regarding the new unmanned driving mode. Meanwhile, by linking with the actual development of unmanned driving technology and its business models, this paper conducts an in-depth analysis of the existence of cognitive gaps and their specific dimensions of manifestation. On this basis, countermeasures are proposed based on the analysis results, providing practical references for the future popularization and promotion of unmanned driving technology.

The marginal contributions of this study are as follows: (1) The cognitive gap theory has been widely used in previous literature to analyze traditional issues such as the “digital divide” and “educational resource disparity”. his study introduces the theory into the field of unmanned driving technology. It reveals the connection between the evolution of public opinion on unmanned driving technology and differences in public cognition, expands the application scenarios of the cognitive gap theory in cutting-edge technology fields, and provides a referable analytical framework for research on the correlation between technological public opinion and public cognition. (2) It enriches the explanatory power of Diffusion of Innovations Theory in addressing social controversies over complex technologies. This study systematically applies the core elements of Rogers’ Diffusion of Innovations Theory to analyze the public cognitive gaps regarding new unmanned driving models. On this basis, the study constructs a “information-scenario-subject” three-level innovation synergy strategy, providing a universal theoretical explanation and intervention path for resolving cognitive gaps and promoting the social acceptance of new unmanned driving models.

Research status

Current research status in the field of unmanned driving

At present, in the field of unmanned driving research, assisted driving has achieved partially mature commercial implementation in China. Most scholars have focused on the improvement of unmanned driving technology and changes in operational modes. Hongwei Zhu et al.2 incorporated marginal activity utility and in-vehicle time utilization utility into the utility function, proposing a new research framework for commuting behavior changes. Xiangwen Luo et al.3 identified the issue of obstacle target misrecognition in unmanned vehicles and developed a novel unmanned path planning algorithm based on Swin Transformer. With the deployment of unmanned taxis such as Apollo Go, unmanned driving applications have entered a brand-new phase, where new business models and practical challenges emerge simultaneously. In this context, understanding the public’s emotional attitude of and attention to unmanned taxis has become crucial for identifying industry development bottlenecks and directions for model innovation. Currently, there remains a lack of research on public opinion dynamics, characteristics of public emotional attitude, and cognitive evolution regarding unmanned vehicles at this stage. In particular, no systematic analysis has been conducted on core issues such as whether a cognitive gap exists between public perception and the actual industrial development, as well as the specific dimensions of such a gap.

Current research status of emotion analysis methods

Sentiment analysis is the process of analyzing, summarizing, evaluating, and mining opinions from public opinion texts with subjective emotional tendencies, while identifying their emotional orientations. It is mainly categorized into two types: semantic analysis-based and machine learning-based approaches4. In semantic analysis-based methods, the core technique is sentiment dictionary discrimination. Although this method achieves high accuracy in sentiment analysis of user comments in specific fields, it has weak generalization ability and requires dynamic updates of adaptive sentiment dictionaries for different research scenarios. In machine learning-based studies, traditional algorithms represented by Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Naive Bayes are widely applied with strong robustness. For instance, Jian Wang et al.5 used the SnowNLP library (based on the Naive Bayes principle) to conduct sentiment analysis on ChatGPT-related online comments, and found that the public’s emotional tendency toward ChatGPT was mainly positive and optimistic; the hybrid Naive Bayes model (HNB) proposed by Ghaith et al.6 also achieved favorable results in sentiment classification tasks on financial domain review datasets. However, traditional machine learning methods have significant limitations—they struggle to fully capture contextual information in texts and tend to overlook semantic correlations, thereby restricting classification accuracy.In recent years, deep learning centered on neural networks has gradually become the mainstream technical path in the field of sentiment analysis. Hongli Wang et al.7 used a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to perform sentiment analysis on Brexit-related comments on the Twitter platform, and the results showed that its performance was superior to traditional models. With the advent of the Transformer model, sentiment analysis methods have further shifted toward Transformer and its derivative pre-trained models. The Transformer-based deep learning model proposed by Vaswanid et al.8 achieves fast parallel computing by integrating a self-attention mechanism, effectively addressing the issues of low training efficiency and overfitting in traditional models. In terms of pre-trained model applications, compared with Word2Vec, pre-trained models represented by BERT better capture semantic correlations between sentences and significantly improve classification performance, thus being widely used in Natural Language Processing (NLP) tasks.

Currently, research in the field of sentiment analysis mostly focuses on multi-model fusion to optimize classification performance on specific datasets: Yiyong Zhou9 constructed a BiGRU model integrated with CNN and attention mechanism (SCB model), which effectively overcomes the shortcomings of existing sentiment analysis models for product reviews; the BERT-MLFF-TextCNN model proposed by Kun Han et al.10 improves the traditional BERT model by further extracting [CLS] features from each layer, and combines the attention mechanism with TextCNN for classification—its accuracy is higher than that of single models and the traditional BERT-TextCNN model; the BEBA method proposed by Ziming Zeng et al.11 also verifies the advantages of the fused model over single models in public opinion sentiment classification tasks by comprehensively applying BERT and BiLSTM models.

Given the advantages of fused models in sentiment analysis tasks, and considering that this study focuses on the Chinese public opinion text scenario of “Apollo Go” Weibo comments—requiring both contextual semantic capture and accuracy in emotional orientation identification—this study draws on the above multi-model fusion approach and adopts the BERT-BiLSTM model to conduct phased sentiment extraction.

Current research status of thematic analysis methods

In existing studies on topic analysis, Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) and its improved algorithms are the mainstream tools for scholars to conduct topic identification, which are widely applied in multi-disciplinary text mining scenarios12–14. For instance, Yue Ren et al.15 integrated LDA topic modeling, content analysis, and the PMC index model to systematically analyze the core characteristics and existing shortcomings of traditional village protection policies, and put forward targeted optimization suggestions accordingly; Jiahui Zhang et al.16 adopted the improved LDA algorithm (PLDA) to carry out emerging topic detection research in the field of cultural heritage, providing technical support for identifying research hotspots in this field. From the perspective of technical characteristics, LDA and its derivative models can realize cluster analysis of the implicit semantic structure of text, mine the potential topic distribution in the field from large-scale document collections, and possess strong basic capabilities in text semantic mining. However, it should be noted that LDA essentially belongs to the category of traditional bag-of-words models. Its core assumption is that words in the text are independent of each other, which makes it unable to effectively capture the contextual correlation and deep semantic logic between words17. This limitation restricts the accuracy of topic identification to a certain extent. In addition, the LDA model also has two key problems in practical application: first, it is prone to generating topic overlap, leading to ambiguous topic boundaries; second, the number of topics required by the model needs to be manually set based on indicators such as perplexity, lacking adaptive adjustment capabilities18, which increases the complexity of model parameter optimization.

In recent years, topic modeling has no longer been limited to the LDA algorithm. Grootendorst19 proposed BERTopic, a neural topic model integrated with BERT. As a semantic analysis tool that combines pre-trained BERT models and topic modeling, BERTopic offers distinct advantages over LDA and its improved variants: unlike traditional models, BERT represents words as multi-dimensional vectors with rich semantic information, accounting for the semantics, structure, and sequence of words in text data. This enables more accurate topic identification with richer semantic features. Additionally, BERTopic maps documents in large-scale text datasets to a topic space, clusters the captured semantic information through topic modeling, and ultimately achieves automatic identification of latent topics—eliminating the need for manual setting of the number of topics.Currently, scholars have begun to apply the BERTopic model to extract policy topics20,21, information resource management topics22,23, thesis topics24–26, and science and technology report topics27,28. These studies confirm BERTopic’s universality and strong performance across different text types. In light of these advantages, this paper employs the BERTopic model for topic identification of online comments related to Apollo Go.

Study design

Research framework

The research technical roadmap of this paper is shown in Fig. 1. It consists of four core components: First is corpus construction: this study uses the Scrapy crawler to collect Weibo comment data with “Apollo Go” as the keyword, including original user comments and mainstream media comments. The final dataset is obtained after data preprocessing. Second is data period division: based on the time-series characteristics of the collected text data and the public opinion life cycle theory, the data is divided into distinct cycles to support subsequent research. Third is topic and sentiment extraction: for the cycle-divided mainstream media comments and original user comments, this study applies the BERTopic and BERT-BiLSTM models for public opinion analysis. Since the volume of mainstream media comment data is relatively small, large-model and manual inductive analysis are introduced; for user comment data, a model is built to extract emotional topics. Fourth, based on the results of public opinion evolution, this study analyzes the existence of cognitive gaps and their specific manifestations. By introducing Rogers’ Diffusion of Innovations Theory as the core analytical framework, it systematically diagnoses the specific causes and dynamic manifestations of cognitive gaps across four dimensions: “innovation characteristics, communication channels, time dimension, and social system.” Ultimately, it proposes a theoretical countermeasure of “information-scenario-subject” three-level synergy, and theoretically clarifies the internal mechanisms and feasible paths for resolving cognitive gaps and promoting social acceptance of technologies.

Sample selection

Sina Weibo is one of China’s earliest and most influential online communication platforms, which has broad user coverage and strong social impact. Therefore, this study selects Weibo as the data collection platform. Given the characteristics of public opinion—fast transmission speed, short timeliness, and easy shifts in public focus—this study selects the period from July 7, 2024 to September 10, 2024, and uses the Scrapy crawler to collect content, including both user comments and media reports, from Weibo with “Apollo Go” as the keyword. To ensure data quality, this study excludes invalid data such as that related to financial stocks, obviously irrelevant user comments, random codes, null values, and duplicate comments from the same user. Finally, 27,743 valid user comments and 120 valid media reports are obtained.

This stage may be attributed to the rationalization of public opinion: the public has shifted its focus to new topics or events, and most people’s discussions about Apollo Go have tended to normalize, forming a small but stable level of attention.

Design and implementation of public opinion analysis model

Topic recognition modeling

In the topic recognition module, the BERTopic model is used to recognize topics in Apollo Go user comments: first, the Jieba word segmentation tool is applied to the collected user comment texts for Chinese word segmentation, and during this process, stop words are removed using the Harbin Institute of Technology’s stop word list and a custom user dictionary, resulting in preprocessed user comment text data; next, the Bert-Base-Chinese embedding model is selected for text embedding vector representation, and for the dimensionality reduction and clustering steps, the default UMAP dimensionality reduction method and HDBSCAN clustering method of BERTopic are adopted respectively—by referring to existing research and comparing modeling results multiple times, the parameters are finally determined as follows: the UMAP parameter n_neighbors(number of adjacent sample points) is set to 15, the embedding dimension n_components (for dimensionality reduction) of the embedded data is set to 60, and the HDBSCAN parameter min_cluster_size (minimum cluster size) is set to 30; in the feature serialization and weighting step, the CountVectorizer feature extraction method is selected, followed by weighting using the c-TF-IDF algorithm, and finally topic recognition is performed.

Sentiment analysis modeling

In the emotion recognition module, this study adopts the BERT-BiLSTM fusion model for emotion extraction. This model optimizes sentiment analysis performance by organically integrating the core advantages of BERT and BiLSTM. On the one hand, it leverages the BERT model’s strong contextual semantic understanding capability to accurately capture the deep emotional tendencies and semantic associations hidden in texts, avoiding the misjudgment of ambiguous expressions and context-dependent sentences that occurs with traditional models. On the other hand, by utilizing BiLSTM’s temporal modeling capability for sequential data, the progressive logic and contextual dependency of text sentiment can be effectively explored—addressing the limitation of the standalone BERT model in analyzing the sentiment coherence of long texts. This integrated architecture, combining semantic understanding and temporal modeling, not only significantly enhances the model’s adaptability to complex public opinion texts and the accuracy of emotion recognition but also provides reliable technical support for subsequent accurate quantification of the public’s acceptance tendency toward unmanned driving models from an emotional perspective.

This study constructed a manually annotated dataset for sentiment analysis of public opinion on unmanned driving. Specifically, 7,743 pieces of data were randomly extracted from comment texts and manually annotated for sentiments toward “Apollo Go”. Three annotators trained under a unified protocol performed the sentiment classification (positive, neutral, negative), with examples of the classification provided in Table 2. To evaluate and ensure annotation quality, we calculated inter-annotator agreement based on a 10% validation set (n = 774), and the Krippendorff’s Alpha(Kalpha) coefficient reached 0.82. The dataset has a balanced label distribution, with the proportion of each of the three sentiment categories ranging from 31.6% to 35.4%, indicating no significant class imbalance. Controlled through rigorous procedures (including training, consistency verification, and dispute arbitration), this dataset demonstrates high consistency and reliability, making it suitable as benchmark data for training and evaluating sentiment analysis models.

Analysis of evolution of media public opinion

Thematic results analysis

This study comprehensively uses large models and manual induction to analyze public opinion in media comment data, and finally presents the phased focus differences in media comment themes regarding the autonomous driving service “Apollo Go” based on the dimensions of “employment impact, industry competition, policy supervision, user experience, technological development, safety responsibility, and price disputes”, with the thematic results of its three stages shown in Fig. 3: In Stage 1 (Rapid Rising Period), media comments exhibit a focus on “employment impact, safety responsibility, and price disputes”, with “employment impact” and “safety responsibility” being relatively prominent, which reflects that in the initial stage of the new unmanned driving mode’s emergence, the media prioritized discussions on the impact of unmanned driving on the employment structure (mostly mentioning unemployment), potential safety hazards of technological application, and disputes over the price rationality of commercialization, a high-level examination of the fundamental value and risks of emerging technologies; in Stage 2 (Fluctuating Decline Period), the theme distribution tends to be scattered, attention to the “employment impact” topic persists, and attention to “technological development” and “user experience” increases but still accounts for a low overall proportion, indicating that as time evolved, media comments gradually shifted from initial fundamental disputes to multi-faceted discussions on technological iteration and users’ actual experience, though no new focus areas had yet formed and public opinion remained relatively scattered; in Stage 3 (Stable Development Period), it can be observed that the “employment impact” topic runs through the entire process, yet in terms of specific details, media comments have shifted from emphasizing the negative impact of employment to discussing the job demands brought by Apollo Go, “technological development” and “industry competition” have become core themes, and the media’s focus has shifted to “technological deepening (e.g., breakthroughs in autonomous driving levels)” and “industrial ecological construction (e.g., enterprise competition and collaboration)”, which indicates that the media’s attention to unmanned driving has shifted from multi-faceted focus to discussions on industrial ecological value.

Emotion evolution results analysis

Combined with the proportions of positive, negative, and neutral emotions and the aforementioned theme evolution results, the emotions in media reports exhibit distinct phased changes, as shown in Fig. 4. In Stage 1 (Rapid Rising Period), negative emotions dominate the dimensions of “employment impact”, “price disputes”, and “safety responsibility”; negative emotions also remain high in “technological development” and “industry competition”, while only “policy supervision” shows neutral emotions due to limited relevant information. This reflects that during the rapid rising Period, the media held a negative reporting tendency toward the new unmanned driving mode, framing the new unmanned driving mode as an “employment threat, source of safety risks, and disruptor of market prices”—a tendency that made negative emotions the backdrop of public opinion during this period; in Stage 2 (Fluctuating Decline Period), positive emotions regarding “technological development” increased significantly, and negative emotions related to “user experience” decreased; however, negative emotions still dominated “employment impact”, “price disputes”, and “safety responsibility”. This indicates that media reports fell into an emotional tug-of-war between optimism about technology and worries about practical issues; in Stage 3 (Stable Development Period), emotions related to “employment impact” became more diversified, with the proportion of negative reports dropping sharply. As evident from the theme results, media reports shifted their focus to the new job opportunities brought by Apollo Go. Positive emotions toward “technological development” were relatively prominent, while negative emotions regarding “safety responsibility” remained notable. This reflects that technological innovation has gained a certain degree of recognition in current public opinion reports, though media concerns about safety issues persist.

Emotional accumulation histogram of topics in different stages.

Through the analysis of media public opinion evolution, it can be clearly observed that with the passage of time, themes and emotions have undergone a process from single-focused to multi-differentiated: initial public opinion concentrated on a few themes with prominent negative emotions, conveying a one-sided risk perception to the public. Subsequently, the scope of themes expanded, emotional tendencies diversified, and the information available to the public through media reports became more comprehensive. This evolution of information, which moves from single-dimensional to multi-dimensional, resulted in the public’s initial cognition of the new unmanned driving mode being limited to one-sided risks; only later, as they gradually accessed more comprehensive information, could the public form a fuller understanding. This process is likely to cause cognitive biases among the public toward emerging technologies, which in turn leads to excessive misunderstandings of such technologies in the early stages.

Analysis of Apollo Go public emotion evolution

The overall distribution and trend of public sentiments

The BERT-BiLSTM model was used to predict the public’s emotional tendencies toward Apollo Go. Based on the time-series characteristics of comment data, the emotional prediction results were analyzed, and Fig. 6 illustrates the public’s overall acceptance of Apollo Go. Among the results, the proportion of the public holding a positive acceptance attitude toward Apollo Go is 29%, indicating that nearly 30% of the audience recognizes and supports the launch of Apollo Go’s unmanned taxis. This positive sentiment may stem from the service’s innovativeness or the convenience it brings in certain aspects. However, the proportion of the public with a negative attitude is slightly higher than that of positive attitudes, accounting for 31%, which suggests that a relatively high percentage of users still have doubts or dissatisfaction with this new service, potentially due to inadequate service quality or safety concerns. In addition, up to 40% of the public holds a neutral attitude. This group may remain on the sidelines, either because they have not fully experienced the service or because they have reservations about the service’s advantages and disadvantages. Overall, the public’s emotional attitude of Apollo Go presents a challenging outlook.

Sentiment analysis results.

Conclusions and recommendations

Research conclusions

Through the evolution analysis of the above themes and emotions, we can draw the development trend and challenges faced by the new unmanned driving mode, which can be summarized as the following two parts.

The trade-off between advantages and disadvantages of Apollo Go

With the promotion and implementation of the new unmanned driving mode, the convenience it brings and the potential problems it entails continue to emerge as the public gains more experience with it. As shown in Fig. 9, these two aspects interact in a dynamic balance, influencing the public emotional attitude of the mode. Based on the aforementioned thematic evolution results, the convenience of unmanned taxis is primarily reflected in two aspects: cost reduction and service experience optimization. Through technological automation, unmanned taxis can provide more standardized and efficient services, reducing service inconsistencies caused by human factors. However, the popularization of this technology is accompanied by numerous challenges. The issue of driver unemployment is the primary concern of the public: the adoption of automation and intelligent technologies directly impacts the traditional ride-hailing industry, which may result in massive job losses among drivers. This issue not only affects the livelihoods of individuals but also poses risks to social stability. Additionally, unmanned driving technology is not yet fully mature, and safety remains a critical issue that unmanned vehicle operators must address. Thematic analysis indicates that the public is particularly concerned about information security and road safety. In terms of road safety, software and hardware malfunctions, as well as the inability to adapt to complex traffic environments, may become potential safety hazards. In terms of information security, unmanned vehicles collect large volumes of private data during operation—including driving trajectories and passenger information. All these factors heighten public concerns about data leakage, data tampering, and data misuse.

The evolution law of public opinion from cognitive gap to innovation synergy in the new mode of unmanned driving

The evolutionary law of public opinion confirms the initial cognitive gap among the public regarding the new unmanned driving model, which is shaped by media discourse orientation. Against the backdrop of competing perspectives on the pros and cons of unmanned driving, this initial cognitive gap, driven by media narratives, significantly undermined the public’s relatively rational weighing of its advantages and disadvantages. Due to the excessive focus of public opinion on negative issues (e.g., unemployment risks), the originally balanced trade-off between pros and cons tilted markedly toward “disadvantages” in public perception, forming a “risk-oriented initial gap”.

Specifically, cognitive biases at this stage manifested in three dimensions: First, an imbalanced assessment of unemployment, where the public overestimated the short-term impact of the technology on traditional driving jobs while relatively ignoring new employment opportunities emerging in R&D, maintenance, and data operations. Second, excessive concerns about safety performance; some views amplified the current imperfections of the system, failing to fully recognize the long-term safety potential of unmanned driving in avoiding human errors and maintaining stable control. Third, distrust in information security; despite continuous enhancements in data encryption and regulatory compliance by enterprises and authorities, the early exaggeration of negative information still left the public with significant anxiety about data leakage and abuse.

Over time, this cognitive gap underwent dynamic changes. In the mid-term of public opinion evolution, the media’s overemphasis on negative themes persisted, sustaining the gap. It was not until the later stage that, with the diversification and expansion of media coverage, the information accessible to the public became more comprehensive, and the initial cognitive gap began to gradually ease. However, the persistence of core controversies, such as the attribution of “safety liabilities,” revealed the long-term nature and recalcitrance of the cognitive gap. Throughout the entire cycle of public opinion evolution, although the public’s overall attitude toward the new unmanned driving model remained dominated by negative and neutral views for an extended period, a critical shift occurred. Phased data indicated that public cognition gradually transitioned from initial “resistance and fragmentation” to later “rationality and acceptance”. This positive shift not only confirms the practical possibility of “cognitive gap resolution” but also suggests the emergence of an “Innovation Synergy Cognition”, providing a crucial positive signal for the new unmanned driving model to ultimately gain widespread social recognition and achieve in-depth market penetration.

To further verify the internal logic and practical feasibility of innovation synergy in resolving the cognitive gap, this study introduces Rogers’ Diffusion of Innovations theory as the core analytical framework. The theory posits that the diffusion rate of an innovation within a social system is not determined solely by its technological advancement but profoundly depends on the synergistic interaction of four core elements: the characteristics of the innovation (e.g., relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability), communication channels (e.g., mass media and interpersonal communication), time (i.e., the cognitive process of innovation decision making), and the social system (e.g., the structure and interaction of actors). As revealed by the aforementioned evolutionary law of public opinion, the “risk-oriented initial gap” encountered by the new unmanned driving model in its early stage, and its subsequent mitigation, essentially reflect the dynamic process of these four elements moving from systemic imbalance to re-synergy.

Specifically, as shown in Fig. 10, in the early stage of diffusion, the formation of the cognitive gap stemmed from multiple imbalances in the theoretical elements: First, at the level of innovation characteristics, public perceptual biases toward “safety” and “employment impact” led to misjudgments of the innovation’s “relative advantages” (e.g., higher efficiency, lower costs). Meanwhile, the lack of experience with “trialability” and “compatibility” (alignment with existing travel habits) amplified doubts arising from the technology’s “black box” nature. Second, at the level of communication channels, over-reliance on mass media focused on negative issues resulted in a single-dimensional information structure, while “interpersonal communication” and “experiential communication”—channels that could effectively enhance understanding and trust—were severely lacking. Third, at the time dimension, the core issue lay in a severe mismatch between “information input quality” and “the speed of public cognitive updating.” In the early stage, the information ecosystem was dominated by “single-dimensional negative” issues, and the public’s cognitive system, lacking prior knowledge for reference, updated far more slowly than the one-sided information dissemination, leading to the rapid entrenchment of cognitive biases and the formation of a “risk-oriented initial gap.” Finally, at the social system level, the core problem was the absence of an effective integration and communication structure around this innovation within the system. According to Diffusion of Innovations theory, the structure of the social system, particularly its opinion leadership network and communication channels, determines the efficiency of innovation diffusion. In the early stage of unmanned driving promotion, key actors such as enterprises, governments, media, and the public had not established a synergistic interaction model or norms. Each party, based on distinct roles, interests, and knowledge systems (e.g., enterprises focused on technological breakthroughs, governments prioritized regulatory compliance, media emphasized risk narratives, and the public worried about personal safety), exhibited a state of “systemic communication fragmentation.” This initial, inefficiently integrated social system structure failed to provide a consistent framework or guidance for evaluating the innovation cognitively; instead, the contradictory information it transmitted exacerbated public cognitive confusion, seriously hindering the natural diffusion process.

Mechanism of innovation synergy in resolving cognitive gaps.

However, the subsequent evolutionary law of public opinion, i.e., the gradual mitigation of the cognitive gap alongside the diversification of media coverage, reveals a turning point at the empirical level. This “mitigation” is not accidental; it essentially reflects the results of spontaneous, initial synergistic efforts within the social system. When some media shifted to reporting on technological deepening and ecological value, it essentially improved “information input quality”; when public positive sentiment rose due to user experience sharing, it marked the effective operation of “interpersonal communication” channels; when governments and enterprises actively collaborated on reemployment for taxi drivers, they were attempting to build a new “authoritative communication structure.” These scattered but positive changes, though not yet systematic, have preliminarily verified that targeted innovation synergy interventions to systematically correct imbalances in the elements of innovation diffusion can effectively guide public cognition toward rational acceptance. The formation of the early gap and its later mitigation demonstrate that “Innovation Synergy” is not only theoretically necessary but also practically feasible for resolving the cognitive gap.

Corresponding recommendations

Based on the above analysis, this study proposes a three-level “Information-Scene-Subject” innovation synergy strategy. Through systematic intervention, this strategy aims to reshape the path of innovation diffusion. Information synergy focuses on reconstructing communication channels and calibrating perceptions of innovation characteristics. It builds a multi-level information network featuring “authoritative release-rational interpretation-authentic sharing,” which not only breaks the channel monopoly of a single mass media but also systematically presents the innovation’s true “relative advantages” and “trialability” to the public through empirical data, technical interpretations, and personal experiences. This fundamentally improves the quality of information input and provides correct guidance for cognitive updating. Scene synergy directly acts on the time dimension, addressing barriers to cognitive updating by creating abundant experiential opportunities. Through layered scenes such as opening technology experience camps, deploying daily transit points, and organizing community-based guidance, it continuously inputs embodied positive experience information to the public. As a high-quality information input, such “personal experience” can effectively counteract the public’s cognitive gap toward the new unmanned driving model, directly accelerating the cognitive system’s update process from negativity and fluctuation to positivity. Subject synergy focuses on integrating internal forces within the social system. It establishes a governance network with joint participation of the government, enterprises, media, and the public, implementing risk-sharing, feedback optimization, and ecological co-construction at different stages of diffusion. This ensures the formation of continuous, consistent, and authoritative information flows and action guidance over time, integrating scattered forces into a cohesive driver for deepening public cognition.

Research limitations and future prospects

This study explored the evolutionary law of public opinion under the new unmanned driving model from the perspective of cognitive gaps, revealing the possibility of innovation synergy promoting the implementation and acceptance of the new model. However, this study still has certain limitations, which also point out directions for future research. First, limitations in data sources. The data of this study are mainly derived from the Weibo platform. Although Weibo is an important public opinion field for public discussions, its user structure has characteristics such as younger age and urbanization, which may limit the generalization of this study’s conclusions to a broader public (e.g., older groups, residents in rural areas). Future research can integrate multi-platform data from WeChat Official Accounts, Zhihu, Douyin, etc., or combine methods such as questionnaires and interviews to construct a more three-dimensional and universal public cognitive map. Second, the measurement boundary between emotional attitudes and behavioral intentions. Due to the unavailability of behavioral data on the new unmanned driving model, this study mainly infers the public’s attitude tendencies through sentiment analysis of public texts, focusing on public acceptance of the new model from an emotional perspective. However, according to classic theories such as the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the transition from positive emotions to actual adoption behavior is influenced by multiple factors, including perceived risk, facilitating conditions, and social norms. Subsequent studies can design structured questionnaires, introduce structured latent variable measurement models, and directly measure constructs such as usage intention and willingness to pay. Elevating public emotional attitudes to the concept of acceptance can further enhance the research value and significance.

Future research will focus on cross-platform, multi-method, and long-term continuous exploration to more accurately grasp the dynamic panorama of the synergistic evolution of complex technological innovations and social cognition, providing more in-depth theoretical references and practical paths.

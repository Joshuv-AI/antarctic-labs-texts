Abstract
. The set of forks is a class of quivers introduced by M. Warkentin, where every connected mutation-infinite quiver is mutation equivalent to infinitely many forks. Let Q Q be a fork with n n vertices, and 𝒘 {\boldsymbol{w}} be a fork-preserving mutation sequence. We show that every c c -vector of Q Q obtained from 𝒘 {\boldsymbol{w}} is a solution to a quadratic equation of the form ∑ i = 1 n x i 2 + ∑ 1 ≤ i < j ≤ n ± q i ​ j x i x j = 1 , \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\pm q_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}=1, where q i ​ j q_{ij} is the number of arrows between the vertices i i and j j in Q Q . When

Due to the multifaceted appearance of

In

Definition 2.6
.

A

(GIM) is a square matrix
{A}=[a_{ij}]
with integral entries such that
(1) for diagonal entries,
a_{ii}=2
;
(2)
a_{ij}>0
if and only if
a_{ji}>0
;
(3)
a_{ij}<0
if and only if
a_{ji}<0
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the (unital)
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra generated by
s_{i},e_{i}
,
i=1,2,\dots,n
, subject to the following relations:

s_{i}^{2}=1,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}=1,\quad s_{i}e_{i}=-e_{i},\quad e_{i}s_{j}=\begin{cases}s_{i}+e_{i}-1&\text{if }i=j,\\
e_{i}&\text{if }i\neq j,\end{cases}\quad e_{i}e_{j}=\begin{cases}e_{i}&\text{if }i=j,\\
0&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

Let
\mathcal{W}
be the subgroup of the units of
\mathcal{A}
generated by
s_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
. Note that
\mathcal{W}
is (isomorphic to) the universal Coxeter group. An element
\mathbf{r}\in\mathcal{W}
is called a reflection if
\mathbf{r}^{2}=1
. Let
\mathfrak{R}\subset\mathcal{W}
be the set of reflections.

From now on, let
{A}=[a_{ij}]
be an
n\times n
symmetric GIM.
Let
\Gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
be the lattice generated by the formal symbols
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}
. Define a representation
\pi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(\Gamma)
by

\pi(s_{i})(\alpha_{j})=\alpha_{j}-a_{ji}\alpha_{i}\quad\text{ and }\quad\pi(e_{i})(\alpha_{j})=\delta_{ij}\alpha_{i},\quad\text{ for }i,j\in\{1,...,n\}.

We suppress
\pi
when we write the action of an element of
\mathcal{A}
on
\Gamma
.

Given a skew-symmetric matrix
B
, for each linear ordering
\prec
on
\{1,...,n\}
, we define the associated GIM

(2.7)

a_{ij}=\begin{cases}b_{ij}&\text{ if }i\prec j,\\
2&\text{ if }i=j,\\
-b_{ij}&\text{ if }i\succ j.\end{cases}\

An ordering

.

When
{\boldsymbol{w}}=[\,]

Lemma 3.1
.

Every fork admits at least one ordering such that the corresponding GIM is admissible.

Proof.

Let
F
be a fork with point of return
r
.
Then

is an acyclic quiver by definition.
As such, the GIM
A
corresponding to
F\setminus\{r\}
where each entry
a_{ij}=-|f_{ij}|
is admissible.
Now, construct the matrix
A
where
a_{kk}=2
,
a_{rj}=|f_{rj}|
for all

,
a_{ir}=-|f_{ir}|
for all
i\in F^{-}(r)
, and
a_{ij}=-|f_{ij}|
for all

.
The vertices
{i,j,k}
form a triangle in
F
.
Then either it is an undirected triangle or it is directed with
r\in\{i,j,k\}
.
If it is undirected, then
(-a_{ij})(-a_{jk})(-a_{ki})=|f_{ij}||f_{jk}||f_{ki}|>0
whenever

, which makes it positive.
If
r\in\{i,j,k\}
and the cycle is undirected, assume without loss of generality that
r=k
.
Then
i,j\in F^{-}(r)
or
i,j\in F^{+}(r)
from the definition of a fork.
This forces
(-a_{ij})(-a_{jk})(-a_{ki})=|f_{ij}||f_{jk}||f_{ki}|
or
(-a_{ij})(-a_{jk})(-a_{ki})=|f_{ij}|(-|f_{jk}|)(-|f_{ki}|)=|f_{ij}||f_{jk}||f_{ki}|

In either case, the product is positive as desired.

If the vertices
{i,j,k}
form a triangle in

Then

.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

\mu_{k}(A)
is an admissible GIM of

.

Proof.

Assume that
\mu_{k}(B)
is both complete and vortex-free.
Let
Z
be a chordless cycle of
Q
.
Then

is either a directed 3-cycle or an undirected 3-cycle, as

We will first show that if

This will allow us to show the same for the cases where

is not a vertex of

Assume that
k
is a vertex of

, and let

and

be the remaining two vertices.
If

-[\mu_{k}(a_{ik})\mu_{k}(a_{kj})\mu_{k}(a_{ji})]=-\sgn(b_{ik}c_{k})\sgn(b_{jk}c_{k})a_{ik}a_{kj}a_{ji}

=-\sgn(b_{ik}b_{jk})a_{ik}a_{kj}a_{ji}

=-a_{ik}a_{kj}a_{ji}.

-[\mu_{k}(a_{ik})\mu_{k}(a_{kj})\mu_{k}(a_{ji})]>0
as

to be an undirected cycle in both

\mu_{k}(B)
.

As
b_{ik}b_{kj}\neq 0
from

.
This forces

-[\mu_{k}(a_{ik})\mu_{k}(a_{kj})\mu_{k}(a_{ji})]=-\sgn(b_{ik}c_{k})\sgn(b_{jk}c_{k})a_{ik}a_{kj}(a_{ji}-a_{jk}a_{ki})

In the second, we have

l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}-a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}l_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}&\text{if $b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}>0$}\end{cases},

where
a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}
is the
(i,j)
entry of
A^{\boldsymbol{w}}
.
Additionally,
a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}=-P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})
, where
P_{j}=\pi(\mathbf{r}_{j})-I
.

Proof.

We need only show that
\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=-a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
for each
{\boldsymbol{w}}
, where the case
{\boldsymbol{w}}=[]
is trivial.
Assume then that
\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=-a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
for some mutation sequence
{\boldsymbol{w}}
, and we argue by induction.

As
\beta_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}
is the coefficient of
\alpha_{j}
in
P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{-1}(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})
, we split into several cases depending on the sign of
b_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
and
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
, assuming that
k\notin\{i,j\}
.
If both are less than or equal to zero, then
\beta_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=\beta_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}
.
This implies that
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}b_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0
, forcing

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=-\beta_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}=-\beta_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}.

If
b_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0
and
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
, then

\beta_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\alpha_{j}=P_{j}(g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\mathbf{r}_{k}(g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})

=P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\mathbf{r}_{k}(g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})

=P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}(P_{k}+I)(g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})

=P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}P_{k}(g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})+P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})

=P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}(\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\alpha_{k})+\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\alpha_{j}

=\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})+\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\alpha_{j}

=(\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\alpha_{j}.

Additionally, we get that
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}b_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
.
Then

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}a_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}.

Thus we have that

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}a_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=-(\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}})=-\beta_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}.

If instead
b_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
and
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0
, then a similar argument follows.

Now, if
b_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
and
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
, then

\beta_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\alpha_{j}=P_{j}(g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\mathbf{r}_{k}(g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}+\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})

=P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\mathbf{r}_{k}(g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})+\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\mathbf{r}_{k}(g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})

=(\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\alpha_{j}+\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}(P_{k}+I)(g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})

=(\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\alpha_{j}+\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}[P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}(P_{k})(g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})+P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})]

=(\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\alpha_{j}+\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}[P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}g_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}(\beta_{kk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\alpha_{k})+\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\alpha_{j}]

=(\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\alpha_{j}+\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}[\beta_{kk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})+\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\alpha_{j}]

=(\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\alpha_{j}+\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}[\beta_{kk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\alpha_{j}+\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\alpha_{j}]

=(\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\alpha_{j}

=(\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+(\beta_{kk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+2)\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\beta_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\alpha_{j}.

From Corollary
3.8
and the inductive hypothesis, we know that
\beta_{kk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=-a_{kk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=-2
.
Thus

\beta_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}.

As
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}b_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}<0
, we get that

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=-\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=-\beta_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}.

We now deal with the cases of
\beta_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}
and
\beta_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}
.
As
\beta_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
whenever
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0
and
\beta_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
whenever
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
, we get in either case that

a_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-\sgn(b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\pm a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\mp\beta_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=-\beta_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}.

A similar argument shows that
a_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-\beta_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}
.

Finally, we always get that

a_{kk}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{kk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=-\beta_{kk}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}=-\beta_{kk}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-2.

This completes the inductive step, and shows that
a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=-P_{j}(g_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{-1}(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=-\beta_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}

∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
B
be a quiver that is both complete and vortex-free and
A
be any admissible GIM.
For any reduced mutation sequence
{\boldsymbol{w}}
such that every quiver arrived at is both complete and vortex-free and
i,j\in\mathcal{I}
, we have that

a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}A(l_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{T}.

In particular, when
i=j
,

2=l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}A(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})^{T},

which implies that
l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}
is a solution to the quadratic equation of the form

(3.11)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\pm a_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}=1.

Proof.

If
{\boldsymbol{w}}=[]
, then the result is trivial.
Assume then that it holds for some
{\boldsymbol{w}}
, and we will investigate
{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]
.
To do so, we look at
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}
and
b_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}
, first considering the case when
i=j
.

If
i=j
, then
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}b_{ki}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0
.
Thus

a_{ii}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{ii}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=2.

If
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
, then

l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}A(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{T}=(l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})A(l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}

=l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}+a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}a_{ki}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}

=l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}-2a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}+(a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{2}a_{kk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}

=l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}

If
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
, then

l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}A(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{T}=l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}.

In either case, we get that

a_{ii}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{ii}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=2=l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}=l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}A(l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{T}.

Thus we may now assume
i\neq j
.
If
i=k
and
b_{kj}\neq 0
, then
b_{kk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=0
and

a_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-\sgn(b_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})a_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}.

We know that
b_{kk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}=0
.
Thus we only need look at
b_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}
.

If
b_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
, then

l_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}A(l_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{T}=l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}-a_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}

=l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}-a_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}

=a_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}-2a_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=-a_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}

This then forces

a_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-a_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=l_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}A(l_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{T}

If
b_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
, then

l_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}A(l_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{T}=l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}

This then forces

a_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}=l_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}A(l_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{T}

If
j=k
, then a similar argument results in

a_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}A(l_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{T}.

We may now assume that
i\neq j
and
k\notin\{i,j\}
.
If
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
and
b_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
, then
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}b_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}<0
.
Thus

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}

and

l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}A(l_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{T}=(l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})A(l_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}-a_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}

=l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}-a_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}+a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}a_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}

Hence,

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}A(l_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{T}.

If
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
and
b_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
, then
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}b_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}<0
again.
Thus

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}=l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}A(l_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{T}.

If
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
and
b_{jk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
, then
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}b_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}>0
.
Thus

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}a_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}

and

l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}A(l_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})^{T}=(l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})A(l_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}

=l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}l_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}A(l_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})^{T}

=a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}a_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}.

Hence,

A similar argument gives the case where

and

.

Finally, as

is complete, we do not have

.
Therefore, this completes the inductive step and proves that

∎

4.
C

To prove Theorem

.
In this proof, we will find several situations where it is desirable to disallow certain situations on the signs of the
c

As such, we will start by classifying what the signs of these
c
-vectors look like, beginning with Proposition
4.1

Proposition 4.1
.

[
8
]

Let
B
be a fork and
{\boldsymbol{w}}
a non-trivial fork-preserving mutation sequence.
If
r
is the last mutation of
{\boldsymbol{w}}
, then

•

If
b_{rj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
for some vertex
j\neq r
, then either
\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(c_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}})

\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq\sgn(c_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}})c_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}}

•

If
b_{rj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0
for some vertex

, then
\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=-\sgn(c_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}})
;

•

If
b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
for distinct vertices
i
and
j
that are neither

, then
\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})
.

From this, we have an immediate corollary on the case where

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
B
be a fork and
{\boldsymbol{w}}
a non-trivial fork-preserving mutation sequence.
If
r
is the last mutation of
{\boldsymbol{w}}
—hence the point of return of
B^{\boldsymbol{w}}
—and
b_{ir}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
for some vertex

, then
\sgn(c_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})=-\sgn(c_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}})
and
\sgn(c_{i})^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq\sgn(c_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}})c_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}}

Corollary
4.2
leads to some results on the possible configurations the signs of three different

-vectors can take during a fork-preserving mutation sequence.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
B
be a fork and
{\boldsymbol{w}}
a non-trivial fork-preserving mutation sequence.
Then

\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})

for three distinct vertices
\{i,j,k\}
only when the subquiver induced by
\{i,j,k\}
does not form an oriented 3-cycle.

Proof.

First assume that
\{i,j,k\}
induces an oriented 3-cycle as a subquiver of
B^{\boldsymbol{w}}
.
Then that implies that
r\in\{i,j,k\}
, as
B^{\boldsymbol{w}}
is a fork with point of return
r
.
Assume without loss of generality that
r=k
.
Then

\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})

We then have one final condition on the signs of the

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
B
be a fork and
{\boldsymbol{w}}
a non-trivial fork-preserving mutation sequence.

\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=-\sgn(c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})

is the last mutation of
{\boldsymbol{w}}

Definition 4.5
.

Define two
n

\epsilon^{[k]}

\epsilon_{i}^{[k]}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }k\in B^{-}(r)\text{ and }i\neq k\\

{\boldsymbol{w}}

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
B
be a fork and
A
the GIM given in Corollary
3.2

For any non-trivial fork-preserving mutation sequence
{\boldsymbol{w}}
and

l_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\tau_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
and that

a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\begin{cases}2&\text{if }i=j\\
-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|&\text{if }b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0\\
-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\sgn(c_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|&\text{if }b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0.\end{cases}

Proof.

It trivially holds for
{\boldsymbol{w}}=[\ell]
, so we just need to investigate
{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]
.
Assume then that our result holds for some
{\boldsymbol{w}}
ending in
r\neq k

If
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0
for

and
c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=c_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
.
For

l_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=l_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
and
c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-c_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}

In both situations, this would force

We just need to check the case where

=\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\tau_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}.

We are then left with the arduous task of proving that

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=\begin{cases}2&\text{if }i=j\\
-\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\epsilon_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}|&\text{if }b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\geq 0\\
-\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\epsilon_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}|&\text{if }b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\leq 0.\end{cases}

The case of
i=j
is already done, as shown in Lemma
3.10
.
If
j=k
, we split into two cases.
Either
b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
or
b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
.
Then

a_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-\sgn(b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}.

In the first case, this reduces to

a_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=\sgn(b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|

=-\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\epsilon_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}|b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}|

as expected.
In the second case, we find that

a_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=\sgn(b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\sgn(c_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\sgn(c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|

=-\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\epsilon_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})\sgn(c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})|b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|,

again, as expected.
If
i=k
instead, then a similar argument holds.
We may now assume that
k\notin\{i,j\}
and
i\neq j
.

If
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}b_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0
, then
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
and
a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
.
When
b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
and
b_{jk}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
, this forces

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\epsilon_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})|b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|

=-\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\epsilon_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}|

if
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0
.
Similarly, if
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
, then

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\epsilon_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|

=-\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\epsilon_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}|,

as desired.

When
b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
and
b_{jk}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
instead, we know that
\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})=\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})=-\sgn(c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})
by Corollary
4.2
.
Additionally, if
\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})
or
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
, then

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|

=-\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\epsilon_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}|

as desired. If
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
and
\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=-\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=-\sgn(c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})
, then
b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
,
b_{ki}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
, and
b_{ki}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}>0
, an impossible situation according to Lemma
4.4
.
If
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
and
\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=-\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})
, then
b_{ki}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
,
b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
, and
b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}b_{ji}^{\boldsymbol{w}}>0
, another impossible situation according to Lemma
4.4
.
Hence, either
\sgn(c_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})=\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})
or
b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
, showing our result for
b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}b_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0
.

If
b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}>0
, then
\sgn(b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})=\sgn(b_{jk}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})
as
k
is the point of return of
\mu_{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}(B)
and

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}-a_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}a_{kj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}.

Assume that
b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
and
b_{jk}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
.
If
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
as well, then

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\sgn(c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|\epsilon_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|

=-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}(|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|+\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\sgn(c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|)

=-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})(\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|+\sgn(b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|)

=-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}

=-\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\epsilon_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})\sgn(b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}|.

If
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\geq 0
, then we are done.
If
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\leq 0
, then
\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})=\sgn(c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})
from
\sgn(b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})=\sgn(b_{jk}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})
and
b_{jk}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\leq 0
.
As
\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})=-\sgn(c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})
by Corollary
4.2
, we find that
\sgn(b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})=\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})
, proving our result.

If instead
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
, then

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\sgn(c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|\epsilon_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|

=-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})(\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|+\sgn(c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|)

=-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})(\sgn(c_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|+\sgn(b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|).

If
\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})
, then
\sgn(b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(b_{jk}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(b_{ki}^{\boldsymbol{w}})
.
However, this implies that the subquiver induced by
\{i,j,k\}
is 3-cycle, a contradiction of Lemma
4.3
.
If
\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})=-\sgn(c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})
, then
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0
,
b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}>0
, and
b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
, a contradiction of Lemma
4.4
.
Hence, we know that
\sgn(c_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})=-\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})
.
Then we find that

a_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})(-\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|+\sgn(b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|)

=-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})(b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}+\sgn(b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}|)

=-\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\epsilon_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}

=-\epsilon_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\epsilon_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})\sgn(b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})|b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}|.

If
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\geq 0
, then we are done.
If
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\leq 0
and
\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})=-\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})
, then we are also done.
Finally, if
b_{ij}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\leq 0
and
\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})=\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})
, then

\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})=\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]})=-\sgn(c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}),

b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}b_{kj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}>0
, and
b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\geq 0
, a contradiction of Lemma
4.4
.
A similar argument holds when
b_{ik}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\leq 0
and
b_{jk}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}c_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\geq 0
, completing the inductive step.

Therefore, for any reduced mutation sequence
{\boldsymbol{w}}
not beginning with
r
and
i,j\in\mathcal{I}
, we have that
l_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\tau_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
and that

a_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\begin{cases}2&\text{if }i=j\\
-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|&\text{if }b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0\\
-\epsilon_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\epsilon_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\sgn(c_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})|b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}|&\text{if }b_{ij}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0.\end{cases}

∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.10

To prove Theorem
1.10
, we need to first note the careful structure that Proposition
4.1
gives to a fork and its
c
-vectors.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
B
be a fork and
{\boldsymbol{w}}
a non-trivial fork-preserving mutation sequence ending in
r
.
Then either there exists a vertex
v\neq r
in
B^{\boldsymbol{w}}
such that

•

\sgn(c_{v}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})>0
,

•

\sgn(c_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})>0
for all
i\neq r
such that
b_{iv}^{\boldsymbol{w}}>0
,

•

\sgn(c_{j}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}})<0
for all
j\neq r
such that
b_{vj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}>0
,

or
c_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
is the only
c
-vector with positive sign.

We refer to
v
as the last green vertex of
B^{\boldsymbol{w}}
.
If
c_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
is the only
c
-vector with positive sign, we refer to
r
as the last green vertex.

Proof.

First suppose that
\sgn(c_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})<0
for all
i\neq r
. Let
j
be the source of
B^{\boldsymbol{w}}\setminus\{r\}
. Then
b_{rj}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}>0
by Definition
1.3
, so
b_{rj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\leq 0
.
As such, Proposition
4.1
tells us that

\sgn(c_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}})=-\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})>0,

hence
c_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
is the only
c
-vector with positive sign.

Next suppose that there is at least one vertex that is not the point of return with positive
c
-vector.
Let
v
be the maximal vertex in the acyclic ordering of
B^{\boldsymbol{w}}
with positive
c
-vector.
Then
b_{iv}^{\boldsymbol{w}}>0
for some vertex
i\neq r
implies
i\neq v
,
b_{iv}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{v}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
, and

\sgn(c_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})=\sgn(c_{v}^{\boldsymbol{w}})>0

by Proposition
4.1
.
If
b_{vj}^{\boldsymbol{w}}>0
for some vertex
j\neq r
, then
v\prec j
.
As
v
is maximal with respect to the acyclic ordering, this forces
\sgn(c_{j}^{\boldsymbol{w}})<0
.
Thus our result is shown.
∎

5.1
culminates in the following corollary.

.

Let
B
be a fork and
{\boldsymbol{w}}
a non-trivial fork-preserving mutation sequence ending in

.

have positive

-vectors, all the vertices succeeding
r
have negative
c
-vectors, and at least one of the vertices in

has a positive
c
-vector.
Equivalently, the vertex
v_{j}
is the last green vertex of

.

A straightforward application of Proposition
4.1
then gives the following corollary of how the last green vertex is modified under mutation.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
B

{\boldsymbol{w}}

.

r\prec v_{n-1}\prec\dots\prec v_{1}

F
given in Corollary

.

v_{j}

v_{j}

v_{j-1}

v_{j}=v
and

;

•

r

v_{j}=v

.

F

r

.

Let
B
be a fork and
{\boldsymbol{w}}

.

We now can proceed to a proof of Theorem
1.10
.

Theorem 5.6

(Theorem
1.10
)
.

Let
B
be a fork and
{\boldsymbol{w}}
a non-trivial fork-preserving mutation sequence.
We have that

\mathbf{r}_{\lambda(1)}^{\boldsymbol{w}}...\mathbf{r}_{\lambda(n)}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\mathbf{r}_{\rho(1)}...\mathbf{r}_{\rho(n)}

for some permutations
\lambda,\rho\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, where
\lambda(i)
is determined by the
i
th element of the ordering of
F=\mu_{\boldsymbol{w}}(B)
given in Corollary
5.2
and
\rho
is determined by the first mutation of
{\boldsymbol{w}}
.

Proof.

We argue by induction, beginning with the base case
{\boldsymbol{w}}=[k]
.
Let
r\prec n-1\prec\dots\prec 1
be the linear ordering of
B
given in Corollary
3.2
, where
r
is the point of return of
B
.
If
i\neq r
, then we naturally have
b_{ik}c_{k}\geq 0
if and only if
k\prec i
in this ordering.
Additionally, Corollary
5.5
tells us that either

k\prec n-1\prec\dots\prec k+1\prec k-1\prec\dots\prec 1\prec r

or

k\prec r\prec n-1\prec\dots\prec k+1\prec k-1\prec\dots\prec 1

is the linear ordering of
F=\mu_{\boldsymbol{w}}(B)
given in Corollary
3.2
, depending on whether
k\in B^{+}(r)
or
k\in B^{-}(r)
.
If
k\in B^{+}(r)
, then
b_{rk}c_{k}\geq 0
.
As
k
is the only vertex of
F
with negative
c
-vector, all other vertices have positive
c
-vectors.
Thus the cyclic permutation of the linear ordering of
F
given in Corollary
5.2
is either

n-1\prec\dots\prec k+1\prec k-1\prec\dots\prec 1\prec r\prec k

or

r\prec n-1\prec\dots\prec k+1\prec k-1\prec\dots\prec 1\prec k

respectively.
Thus

\mathbf{r}_{n-1}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\dots\mathbf{r}_{k+1}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\dots\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\mathbf{r}_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\mathbf{r}_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\mathbf{r}_{n-1}\dots\mathbf{r}_{k+1}\mathbf{r}_{k}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\dots\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{r}

or

\mathbf{r}_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\mathbf{r}_{n-1}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\dots\mathbf{r}_{k+1}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\dots\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\mathbf{r}_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\mathbf{r}_{r}\mathbf{r}_{n-1}\dots\mathbf{r}_{k+1}\mathbf{r}_{k}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}\dots\mathbf{r}_{1}.

proving the base case.

To proceed with the inductive step, assume that

\mathbf{r}_{\lambda(1)}^{\boldsymbol{w}}...\mathbf{r}_{\lambda(n)}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\mathbf{r}_{\rho(1)}...\mathbf{r}_{\rho(n)}

for some
\lambda,\rho\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, where
\lambda(i)
is determined by the
i
th element of the ordering of
F=\mu_{\boldsymbol{w}}(B)
given in Corollary
5.2
,

j\prec j-1\prec\dots\prec 1\prec r\prec n-1\prec n-2\prec\dots\prec j+1,

where
j
is the last green vertex of
F
—with the possibility of
j=r
.
Suppose we further mutate at some vertex
k\neq r
.

If
\sgn(c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})>0
, then
j\preceq k\prec r
.
Since our linear ordering on
F
comes from the acyclic ordering on
F\setminus\{r\}
, this would force
b_{ik}^{\boldsymbol{w}}c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\geq 0
for all
i\neq r
if and only if
k\prec i\prec r
.
Hence, there is a sub-ordering

k-1\prec\dots\prec 1\prec r

consisting of exactly the vertices
i
such that
\mathbf{r}_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=\mathbf{r}_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\mathbf{r}_{i}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\mathbf{r}_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}}
, where
r
is included if and only if
b_{rk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}>0
.
For the case where
b_{rk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}>0
,

\mathbf{r}_{k-1}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\dots\mathbf{r}_{1}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\mathbf{r}_{r}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\mathbf{r}_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=\mathbf{r}_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\dots\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\mathbf{r}_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}}.

For the case where
b_{rk}^{\boldsymbol{w}}<0
,

\mathbf{r}_{k-1}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\dots\mathbf{r}_{1}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\mathbf{r}_{k}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\mathbf{r}_{r}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=\mathbf{r}_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\mathbf{r}_{k-1}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\dots\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\mathbf{r}_{r}^{\boldsymbol{w}}.

These two cases correspond to the linear orderings

j\prec\dots\prec k+1\prec k-1\prec\dots\prec 1\prec r\prec k\prec n-1\prec\dots\prec j+1

and

j\prec\dots\prec k+1\prec k-1\prec\dots\prec 1\prec k\prec r\prec n-1\prec\dots\prec j+1

respectively.
Corollary
5.5
and Corollary
5.3
then show that each respective ordering is the ordering given in Corollary
5.2
.
If
\sigma
is the permutation sending
i
to the
i
th element of this linear ordering, then our two equalities of reflections give that

\mathbf{r}_{\sigma(1)}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}\dots\mathbf{r}_{\sigma(n)}^{{\boldsymbol{w}}[k]}=\mathbf{r}_{\lambda(1)}^{\boldsymbol{w}}...\mathbf{r}_{\lambda(n)}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\mathbf{r}_{\rho(1)}...\mathbf{r}_{\rho(n)},

proving the desired result when
\sgn(c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})>0
.
A similar argument holds when
\sgn(c_{k}^{\boldsymbol{w}})<0
.

Therefore, we have shown by induction that

\mathbf{r}_{\lambda(1)}^{\boldsymbol{w}}...\mathbf{r}_{\lambda(n)}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\mathbf{r}_{\rho(1)}...\mathbf{r}_{\rho(n)}

for some permutations
\lambda,\rho\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, where
\lambda(i)
is determined by the
i
th element of the ordering of
F
given in Corollary
5.2
and
\rho
is determined by the first mutation of
{\boldsymbol{w}}
.
∎

From our proof of the base case, we have the following description of
\rho
.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
B
be a framing of a fork and
v
be the first mutation of a fork-preserving mutation sequence
{\boldsymbol{w}}
.
We have that

\mathbf{r}_{\lambda(1)}^{\boldsymbol{w}}...\mathbf{r}_{\lambda(n)}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\mathbf{r}_{\rho(1)}...\mathbf{r}_{\rho(n)}

for some permutations
\lambda,\rho\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, as in Theorem
1.10
.
If

r\prec n-1\prec n-2\prec\dots\prec 1

is the ordering of
F
given in Corollary
3.2
, then the permutation
\rho
is determined by

\mathbf{r}_{\rho(1)}...\mathbf{r}_{\rho(n)}=\mathbf{r}_{n-1}\mathbf{r}_{n-2}\dots\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{r}

or

\mathbf{r}_{\rho(1)}...\mathbf{r}_{\rho(n)}=\mathbf{r}_{r}\mathbf{r}_{n-1}\mathbf{r}_{n-2}\dots\mathbf{r}_{1}

depending on whether
v\in B^{+}(r)
or
v\in B^{-}(r)
respectively.

Corollary 5.8

(Corollary
1.11
)
.

Let
n
be any positive integer, and let
Q
be a fork with
n
vertices.
For each fork-preserving mutation sequence
{\boldsymbol{w}}
from
Q
, there exist pairwise non-crossing and non-self-crossing admissible curves
\eta_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}
(see Definition
2.11
) such that
\mathbf{r}_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\nu(\eta_{i}^{\boldsymbol{w}})
for every
i\in\{1,...,n\}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
1.10
we know that
\mathbf{r}_{\lambda(1)}^{\boldsymbol{w}}\dots\mathbf{r}_{\lambda(n)}^{\boldsymbol{w}}=\mathbf{r}_{\rho(1)}\dots\mathbf{r}_{\rho(n)}
is a Coxeter element.
In
[
22
]
, they showed that if the product of
n
reflections is a Coxeter element, then each reflection and its corresponding root can be represented by a non-self-crossing loop and a non-self-crossing admissible curve in an
n
-punctured disc, respectively.
Moreover, the order of vertices of the quiver corresponds to the order of its Coxeter element.
Applying this result with the order determined by the permutation
{\rho}
, the corollary is proved.
∎

\displaystyle=(-4\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2\alpha_{3})S_{2}

\displaystyle=-4\alpha_{1}+3\alpha_{2}-2\alpha_{3}.

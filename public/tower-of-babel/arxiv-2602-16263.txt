Introduction

This paper is concerned with the existence and nonexistence of solutions for the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation involving combined power nonlinearities

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\omega u=\mu u^{p-1}+u^{q-1},\penalty\ u>0\quad&&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
&u=0&&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\\
&\|u\|_{2}^{2}=\rho>0,\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
N\geq 1
,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
1<p\leq q
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded smooth domain. Here the parameter
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
is not fixed, which arises as a Lagrange multiplier with respect to the mass constraint
\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\rho
.

Over the past three decades, many researchers have considered the following Schrödinger equation

-\Delta u+\omega u=f(u)\;\;\text{ in }\Omega

(1.2)

with the mass constraint

\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\rho.

(1.3)

A couple
(\omega,u)
satisfying (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) is usually called a
normalized solution
to (
1.2
). Problem (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) comes from the study of standing waves for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation

i\psi_{t}-\Delta\psi=f(\psi)\;\;\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega\times(0,\infty),

(1.4)

where
\psi
has the form

\psi(x,t)=e^{-i\omega t}u(x),\;\;(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty),

(1.5)

and
u
is a real function. For problem (
1.4
)-(
1.5
), the
L^{2}
-norm of
u
stands for the mass of a particle, and
\omega
stands for the frequency. There are already a lot of papers concerning the fixed frequency problem. Recently many authors are focused on the prescribed mass problem. If
f(\psi)=e^{-i\omega t}f(u)
and the mass is prescribed, (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) will be reduced to the problem (
1.2
)-(
1.3
). Here, we consider (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) under the zero Dirichlet boundary condition, that is

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\omega u=f(u)\quad&&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
&u=0&&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\\
&\|u\|_{2}^{2}=\rho.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.6)

One can derive solutions to (
1.6
) by looking for critical points of the associated energy functional

\displaystyle E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}F(u)\mathrm{d}x\quad\;\;\mbox{with}\;\;F(u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(t)\mathrm{d}t

(1.7)

on the constraint

S_{\rho}:=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\rho\}.

When
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, according to
Gagliardo-Nirenberg
inequality (see (
2.2
) below), the functional (
1.7
) is coercive on
S_{\rho}
if
F(u)
grows slower than
|u|^{2+\frac{4}{N}}
at infinity, and
E
is unbounded from below on
S_{\rho}
if the growth of
F(u)
at infinity is faster than
|u|^{2+\frac{4}{N}}
. Accordingly the value
2+\frac{4}{N}
is crucial for deciding the shape of
E
, which is usually called
mass-critical
or
L^{2}
-critical
exponent.

Jeanjean
[
10
]
did a seminal work and considered a class of Sobolev supcritical and mass-subcritical problem where
f(u)
can be chosen as
\sum\limits_{1\leq j\leq k}a_{j}|u|^{\sigma_{j}-2}u
with
k\geq 1,a_{j}>0
and
2+\frac{4}{N}<\sigma_{j}<\frac{2N}{(N-2)^{+}}
. In this case, the energy functional possesses a mountain pass geometry on
S_{\rho}
. He derived a Palais-Smale (for short (PS)) sequence approaching Pohozaev manifold, by using a crucial scaling transformation

u_{t}=t^{\frac{N}{2}}u(tx),\;\;t>0,

(1.8)

and constructing the augmented functional

\widetilde{E}(u,t):=E(u_{t}),\;\;(u,t)\in S_{\rho}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}.

As a consequence, this (PS) sequence can be proved to be bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Due to this method, a lot of articles concerning normalized solutions appear in recent years, see for example
[
1
,
13
,
17
,
25
,
26
]
and reference therein.

Unfortunately, for general domain
\Omega\subsetneqq\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the scaling (
1.8
) may escape from the energy space, hence Jeanjean’s method could be invalid. This brings the main difficulties in obtaining a bounded (PS) sequence at a minimax level. When
\Omega
is a bounded domain, as far as we know, Struwe’s monotonicity trick was valid for getting a (PS) sequence satisfying Pohozaev identity, so that this sequence is bounded. There are some papers dealing with the existence issue of (
1.6
). We state the current results in next subsection.

1.1
The current results in bounded domain

In this subsection, we assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded smooth domain. For different
\mu
,
p
and
q
, we will introduce the existence about positive solutions of (
1.6
) for the model
f(u)=\mu|u|^{p-2}u+|u|^{q-2}u
. Let
2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{(N-2)^{+}}
, which denotes the Sobolev critical exponent if
N\geq 3
,

\bullet
Case
\mu=0
,
N\geq 1
and
2<q<2^{*}

For the simplest case
f(u)=|u|^{q-2}u
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\geq 1)
is a ball, Noris, Tavares and Verzini
[
19
]
gave the first result about the existence and non-existence of positive solutions for problem (
1.6
). They proved that

(i)

when
2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
, (
1.6
) has a unique positive solution for every
\rho>0
, which is a global minimizer;

(ii)

when
q=2+\frac{4}{N}
, there exists
\rho^{*}>0
such that (
1.6
) has a unique positive solution that is a global minimizer for
0<\rho<\rho^{*}
and no positive solution for
\rho\geq\rho^{*}
;

(iii)

when
2+\frac{4}{N}<q<2^{*}
, there exists
\rho^{*}>0
such that (
1.6
) admits a positive solution if and only if
0<\rho\leq\rho^{*}
. Moreover, (
1.6
) has at least two positive solutions for
0<\rho<\rho^{*}
.

\bullet
Case
\mu=0
,
N\geq 3
and
q=2^{*}

When
N\geq 3
and
f(u)=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u
, Noris, Tavares and Verzini in
[
20
, Theorem 1.11]
proved that if
0<\rho\leq\frac{2}{N\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}(\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}})^{\frac{N-2}{2}}
, (
1.6
) admits a positive local minimizer for some
\omega\in(-\lambda_{1}(\Omega),0)
, where
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
is the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
-\Delta
, and
\mathcal{S}
is the best Sobolev constant, see (
2.1
). Furthermore, when
\Omega
is star-shaped, Pierotti, Verzini and Yu
[
22
]
established a bounded (PS) sequence by taking advantage of monotonicity trick. By exploiting sharp estimates of the mountain pass level, they showed the strong convergence of this sequence, thus (
1.6
) admits a positive mountain pass type solution for
\rho
sufficiently small. Recently, Chang, Liu and Yan
[
6
]
use Sobolev subcritical case to approach the Sobolev critical case. They improved the results of
[
22
]
to the general bounded domain.

For
N\geq 6
, Lv, Zeng and Zhou
[
16
]
constructed a positive
k
-spike solution of (
1.6
) in some suitable bounded domain
\Omega
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, where
k
depends on the Robin function and Green function of
\Omega
. Using blow-up analysis and local Pohozaev identity, they also proved that the
k
-spike solutions are locally unique.

\bullet
Case
\mu\neq 0
,
N\geq 1
and
2<p<q\leq 2^{*}

For
\mu\neq 0
, the problem is much less understood. Liu and Zhao
[
15
]
treated the general nonlinearity
f
with Sobolev subcritical near infinity and mass-critical or mass-supcritical near the origin, that is
2+\frac{4}{N}\leq p\leq q<2^{*}
. They obtained the existence of a local minimizer solution if
N\geq 3
, and a mountain pass type solution if
\Omega
is a star-shaped domain and
N\geq 3
. It seems that there is no literature concerning the case
2<p<2+\frac{4}{N}
and
p\leq q<2^{*}
so far.

Song and Zou
[
28
]
considered the existence of positive solutions to (
1.6
) with
f(u)=\mu|u|^{p-2}u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\penalty\ (N\geq 3)
on a bounded star-shaped domain. When
\mu=0
, or
\mu>0
and
2+\frac{4}{N}<p<2^{*}
, or
\mu<0
and
2<p<2^{*}
, they obtained the existence of a local minimizer for small
\rho
, which is a positive solution to (
1.6
). Using the monotonicity trick and the Pohozaev identity, when
\mu=0
, or
\mu>0
,
2+\frac{4}{N}<p<2^{*}
and
N\in\{3,4,5\}
, or
\mu<0
,
2<p<2^{*}-1
and
N\in\{3,4,5\}
, they established the existence of positive mountain pass type solution of (
1.6
) for small
\rho
. To our knowledge, there are no papers dealing with the case
2<p\leq 2+\frac{4}{N}
and
\mu>0
.

\bullet
Some other solutions and nonlinearities

Other significant results concerning normalized solutions of problem (

1.2
Our main results

In this paper, we are interested in the existence and nonexistence of solutions to (
1.1
). Provided
q\leq 2^{*}
, solutions for problem (
1.1
) will be obtained as positive critical points of the energy functional

\displaystyle I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\mu}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall\,u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

on the constraint
S_{\rho}
. Motivated by the ideas in
[
20
]
, we first consider the existence of local minimizer of
I|_{S_{\rho}}
with
q\leq 2^{*}
. Let
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
be the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
-\Delta
in
\Omega
, and
\varphi_{1}
be the corresponding first eigenfunction satisfying
\|\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
and
\varphi_{1}>0
in
\Omega
. For
\alpha\geq\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
, we denote

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}:=

\displaystyle\Big\{u\in S_{\rho}:\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\rho\alpha\Big\},

\displaystyle\partial A_{\alpha}:=

\displaystyle\Big\{u\in S_{\rho}:\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\rho\alpha\Big\}.

In particular, one has
A_{\alpha}=\partial A_{\alpha}=\{\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{1},-\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{1}\}
in case
\alpha=\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
. We shall now consider the following local minimization problems,

\displaystyle m_{\alpha}:=\inf_{A_{\alpha}}I(u),\quad\widetilde{m}_{\alpha}:=\inf_{\partial A_{\alpha}}I(u).

(1.9)

Obviously,
m_{\lambda_{1}}=\widetilde{m}_{\lambda_{1}}
, which is attained by
\{\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{1},-\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{1}\}
. For the case
p\leq q<2^{*}
, the embeddings
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
and
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
are compact, hence it is obvious that
m_{\alpha}
can be achieved. However, when
N\geq 3
and
p<q=2^{*}
, the compactness for the minimizing sequence of
m_{\alpha}
depends on the best Sobolev constant
\mathcal{S}
, see (
2.1
). For any
\alpha>\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
, to prove the reachability of
m_{\alpha}
, we will verify in Proposition
2.1
that any minimizing sequence of
m_{\alpha}
is precompact in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
provided there holds

\rho<\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{\alpha-\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}\Big(\frac{2^{*}}{2}\mathcal{S}^{\frac{2^{*}}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}-2}}.\end{aligned}

(1.10)

Since the minimizer in
A_{\alpha}
may be located on the boundary
\partial A_{\alpha}
, we shall rule out the occurrence of this situation. To this aim, for
\alpha>\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
, we will show in Section
2
that
\widetilde{m}_{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}<\widetilde{m}_{\alpha}
if
\rho
is small enough, which means that the minimizer is an interior point of
A_{\alpha}
. Our first result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
2<p\leq q\leq 2^{*}
. Then there exists a
\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}(N,p,q,\Omega,\mu)>0
such that
for any
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
, (
1.1
) has a solution
(\omega,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times S_{\rho}
, which is a local minimizer of
I|_{S_{\rho}}
.

Remark 1.2
.

(i)

Actually, for the special cases
2<p=q\leq 2^{*}
and
\mu\leq-1
, or
1<p\leq q<2+\frac{4}{N}
, the energy functional is coercive on
S_{\rho}
. Therefore there exists a global minimizer for all
\rho>0
.

(ii)

Theorem
1.1
can improve some previous results. As we introduced before,
[
28
]
obtained a local minimizer but the domain is required to be star-shaped. This constraint can be removed in our Theorem. In
[
15
]
, the authors only dealt with the mass-supercritical case and non-negative nonlinearities. Theorem
1.1
is valid for mass-subcritical case and sign-changing nonlinearities.

In virtue of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain a local minimizer of
I|_{S_{\rho}}
. A natural problem is: could we get another positive solution with mountain pass type? In section
3
, when mass
\rho
is sufficiently small, we can construct a mountain pass structure for the functional
I
on
S_{\rho}
, thus the existence of a (PS) sequence is obvious. As we mentioned before, the main difficulty is that we could not prove the boundedness of this sequence in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Inspired by
[
22
]
, we will use Struwe’s monotonicity trick to deal with our problem with
\mu\geq 0
. At first, we consider the functional

I_{\eta}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\eta\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\mu}{p}|u|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}|u|^{q}\right)\mathrm{d}x,\quad u\in S_{\rho}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\eta>0.

When
\eta
is sufficiently close to
1
,
I_{\eta}
possesses the same mountain pass structure with
I
. We denote by
c_{\eta}
the corresponding mountain pass level, which is non-increasing and continuous from left with respect to
\eta
. Hence
c_{\eta}
is almost everywhere differentiable, and thus there exists a sequence
\eta_{n}\to 1
such that
c_{\eta}
is differentiable at
\eta_{n}
. Since the Sobolev subcritical case is very similar to the Sobolev critical case. Therefore we just concentrate on the proof of the case
q=2^{*}
.

With the help of
[
9
, Theorem 4.5]
(see Proposition
3.4
below), we will show in Lemma
3.5
that there exists a bounded (PS) sequence at every
c_{\eta_{n}}
. By bubbling estimate, provided
\rho
is small, this (PS) sequence is convergent. We actually get a sequence of solutions
(\omega_{n},u_{n})
of

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\omega u=\eta_{n}\left(\mu u^{p-1}+u^{q-1}\right),\penalty\ u>0\quad&&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
&u=0&&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\\
&\|u\|_{2}^{2}=\rho>0.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Therefore, there holds the Pohozaev identity

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\sigma\cdot\nu\mathrm{d}\sigma=N\left[\eta_{n}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\mu}{p}|u_{n}|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}|u_{n}|^{q}\right)\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\omega_{n}\rho}{2}\right]-\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

(1.11)

When
\Omega
is star-shaped, this identity can deduce that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Still the bubbling estimate can show that
\{u_{n}\}
has a convergent subsequence.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mu\geq 0
,
\Omega
is a star-shaped domain. If

(i)

N\geq 1
,
2<p<2+\frac{4}{N}<q<2^{*}
or
N\geq 4
,
2<p<q=2^{*}
.

(ii)

N=3
,
p\in(2,\frac{10}{3}]\cup(\frac{14}{3},6)
,
q=2^{*}
, and

\lambda_{1}(B_{R_{\Omega}})<\begin{cases}\frac{4}{3}\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\quad\;&\mbox{if}\;\;2<p\leq\frac{10}{3},\\
\left(2-\frac{8}{3(p-2)}\right)\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\quad\;&\mbox{if}\;\;\frac{14}{3}<p<6,\\
\end{cases}

(1.12)

where
R_{\Omega}
is the inradius of
\Omega
.

Then for sufficiently small
\rho>0
, problem (
1.1
) has at least two solutions.

Remark 1.4
.

(i)

Note that for the case
\mu=0
, up to a scaling, Pierotti, Verzini and Yu
[
22
]
used the mass
\rho
as a parameter in monotonicity trick. However, for the combined nonlinearities, we need to add an extra parameter
\eta
to take use of monotonicity trick. Then the mass
\rho
and
\eta
shall be controlled simultaneously in constructing mountain pass structure. On the other hand, the estimates for the energy of solutions to (
1.1
) is also very different, see for example, Lemma
3.6
.

(ii)

Compared with
[
28
]
, Theorem
1.3
can obtain two positive solutions for all
N\geq 3
, however the same result in
[
28
]
holds only for
N\in\{3,4,5\}
. Hence we improve the results of
[
28
]
.

(iii)

When
N=3
, we just derive the existence for
p\in(2,\frac{10}{3}]\cup(\frac{14}{3},6)
, this may be a technical restriction. The condition (
1.12
) can be satisfied if
\Omega
is a small perturbation of balls in for example
C^{1}
topology.

In the following, we will consider the problem (
1.1
) with
q\geq 2^{*}
in a convex domain. Assume
u
is a solution of (
1.1
). Recall the moving-plane method in
[
18
]
, if all sectional curvatures at every point of
\partial\Omega
are positive, we can use moving-plane method near the boundary. As de Figueiredo, Lions and Nussbaum
[
8
]
said, the moving-plane method is still valid for general convex domains. We will see in Section
4
that
u
is monotonic along some directions around the boundary
\partial\Omega
. For the sake of the nonexistence of solutions to (
1.1
), we first estimate the range of the frequency
\omega
in Lemma

. Thanks to the monotonicity of

near the boundary, when
\rho
is large enough, the mass will be concentrated on the center of
\Omega
. This can deduce that
\omega
will converge to infinity as
\rho\to\infty
, which will reach a contradiction.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
1<p\leq 2
,
q\geq\max\{2^{*},3\}
,
\mu>0
and
\Omega
is a bounded convex domain with smooth boundary. Then problem (

) has no positive normalized solution provided that

Remark 1.6
.

When

and

, this problem is the classical Brézis-Nirenberg type problem. Many results (see for instance
[

,

,

deduce that the nonexistence result for Brézis-Nirenberg type problem only holds in

, that is

Theorem 1.7
.

Assume that

,
\mu=0
,

(i)

In fact, when

\begin{cases}-\Delta u-\lambda u=u^{2^{*}-1},\penalty\ u>0\quad\text{ in }B_{1}(0),\\
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1}(0)).\end{cases}

(1.13)

A well-known result in

(i)

. Thus the solution of (

]

Remark 1.8
.

Our results above provide complete answers for the normalized solutions of Brézis-Nirenberg problem in a unit ball. However, when
\Omega
is not a ball, our method will be invalid since there might be multiple solutions for fixed
\lambda
if the topology of
\Omega
is not simple.

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we are devoted to proving the existence of local minimizer. Section
3
is dedicated to establishing the mountain pass structure and using monotonicity trick to show the existence of the second positive solution. In Section
4
, we consider the nonexistence issue for the case that

,

and
\Omega
is convex domain. In Section

, we will give a dichotomy result for normalized solutions for Brézis-Nirenberg problem in a ball.

\displaystyle\text{either }\mathrm{(a)}\penalty\ \penalty\ \|\nabla\tilde{u}_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ or }\mathrm{(b)}\penalty\ \penalty\ \|\nabla\tilde{u}_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow\ell>0.

This is a contradiction whenever one let

.

(ii) For

) holds and

, then

\frac{I_{\widetilde{\eta}}(\widetilde{\gamma}(t))-I_{\eta}(\widetilde{\gamma}(t))}{\eta-\widetilde{\eta}}\leq\frac{c_{\widetilde{\eta}}+B(\eta-\widetilde{\eta})-c_{\eta}+(\eta-\widetilde{\eta})}{\eta-\widetilde{\eta}}=\frac{c_{\widetilde{\eta}}-c_{\eta}}{\eta-\widetilde{\eta}}+B+1.

exists, when
\widetilde{\eta}
is close to 1, we have

\frac{c_{\widetilde{\eta}}-c_{\eta}}{\eta-\widetilde{\eta}}<-c^{\prime}_{\eta}+1,

thus

Hence there exists some
\eta_{1}\in(\eta_{0},\eta)
, such that for

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\widetilde{\gamma}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=I_{\widetilde{\eta}}(\widetilde{\gamma}(t))+\widetilde{\eta}\int_{\Omega}F(\widetilde{\gamma}(t))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{\widetilde{\eta}}+B(\eta-\widetilde{\eta})+\widetilde{\eta}(-c^{\prime}_{\eta}+B+2)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\eta}+(1+B-c^{\prime}_{\eta})(\eta-\widetilde{\eta})+\widetilde{\eta}(-c^{\prime}_{\eta}+B+2)

\displaystyle\leq C.

(iii) We can select

satisfying

\underset{t\in[0,1]}{\max}I_{\eta}(\gamma(t))\leq c_{\widetilde{\eta}}+\eta-\widetilde{\eta}.

(3.14)

Notice that if we replace

by
|\gamma|
, then (

) still holds true. Without loss of generality, we can assume

. When

is close to 1,

The proof is done.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N(q-2)}\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{2}_{2}-\frac{q-p}{(q-2)p}\eta_{n}\mu\|u_{n}\|^{p}_{p}+\frac{1}{N(q-2)}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\sigma\cdot\nu\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N(q-2)}\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{2}_{2}-\frac{q-p}{(q-2)p}\eta_{n}\mu\cdot\mathcal{C}^{p}_{N,p}\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{p\gamma_{p}}_{2}\|u_{n}\|^{p(1-\gamma_{p})}_{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle+\infty

as
n\to\infty
, which contradicts
I_{\eta_{n}}(u_{n})\to c_{1}
.

4
Non-existence for normalized solutions with large mass in a bounded convex domain

In this section, we consider the problem (
1.1
) with
N\geq 3
and
q\geq 2^{*}
in a convex domain. If
u
is a solution of (
1.1
), using moving-plane method, we will see that
u
is monotonic along some directions around the boundary. This could deduce some nonexistence result of (
1.1
) when the mass is large. First, we shall estimate the frequency
\omega
of (
1.1
) in a star-shaped domain.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
\Omega
is a smooth star-shaped domain with respect to 0,
\mu>0
,
1<p\leq 2
and
q\geq 2^{*}
. If
(\omega,u)
is a solution of (
1.1
), then there holds that

-\lambda_{1}(\Omega)<\omega\leq N\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\mu|\Omega|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\rho^{\frac{p-2}{2}}.

(4.1)

Proof.

As (
3.28
), one can get that
-\lambda_{1}(\Omega)<\omega
. Next, by the Pohozaev identity

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\sigma\cdot\nu\mathrm{d}\sigma=N\left[\frac{\mu\|u\|_{p}^{p}}{p}+\frac{\|u\|_{q}^{q}}{q}-\frac{\omega\rho}{2}\right]-\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

(4.2)

and the Nehari-type identity

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\omega\rho=\mu\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\|u\|_{q}^{q},

(4.3)

we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\sigma\cdot\nu\mathrm{d}\sigma+\omega\rho

\displaystyle=N\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\mu\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\left(\frac{N}{q}-\frac{N-2}{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\mu|\Omega|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\|u\|_{2}^{p}

\displaystyle=N\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\mu|\Omega|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\rho^{\frac{p}{2}},

which implies the right side of (
4.1
). The proof is completed.
∎

Recall the classical moving-plane method as in
[
18
]
. If all sectional curvatures at every point of
\partial\Omega
are positive, one can carry out the steps of moving-plane method near the boundary. As mentioned in
[
8
]
, the above arguments also work in general convex domains. Accordingly, the positive solutions of (
1.1
) along some directions near the boundary are monotonic. Furthermore, one can obtain the following lemma. The interested readers can refer to the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded convex domain with smooth boundary. There exist
\beta,t_{0}>0
(depending only on
\Omega
) such that any positive solution to (
1.1
) satisfies

\begin{cases}u(x-t\nu)\mbox{ is non-decreasing for $t\in[0,t_{0})$ where $\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ such that}\\
\mbox{$|\nu|=1$ and $\nu\cdot n(x)\geq\beta$ for $x\in\partial\Omega$, $n(x)$ is the outer normal direction at $x$.}\end{cases}

(4.4)

As a corollary, there exist some
r>0
and
C=C(\Omega,N,p,q,r)>0
such that any classical positive solution to (
1.1
) satisfies

\int_{\Omega_{r}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\Omega_{2r}\backslash\Omega_{r}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

where
\Omega_{r}:=\{x\in\Omega:{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<r\}
.

\mathbf{Proof\penalty\ of\penalty\ Theorem\penalty\ \ref{T051401}}
. Assume that
(\omega,u)\subset\mathbb{R}\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a solution of (
1.1
). Using the first eigenfunction
\varphi_{1}>0
as a test function to multiply (
1.1
), we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\omega\geq\frac{\int_{\Omega}u^{q-1}\varphi_{1}\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}u\varphi_{1}\mathrm{d}x}.

(4.5)

By Lemma
4.2
, there exist some
r>0
,
C_{1}=C_{1}(\Omega,N,p,q,r)>0
such that

\displaystyle\rho>\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\backslash\Omega_{r})}\geq C_{1}\rho.

(4.6)

By Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\varphi_{1}\mathrm{d}x\leq\|\varphi_{1}\|_{2}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.7)

Let us denote
m:=\inf\limits_{x\in\Omega\backslash\Omega_{r}}\varphi_{1}(x)>0
. By (
4.5
), (
4.6
), Hölder inequality and
q-1\geq 2
, we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u^{q-1}\varphi_{1}\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\Omega\backslash\Omega_{r}}u^{q-1}\varphi_{1}\mathrm{d}x\geq m\int_{\Omega\backslash\Omega_{r}}u^{q-1}\mathrm{d}x\geq mC_{1}^{\frac{q-1}{2}}|\Omega\backslash\Omega_{r}|^{\frac{3-q}{2}}\rho^{\frac{q-1}{2}}.

(4.8)

Using (
4.5
)-(
4.8
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\omega\geq\frac{mC_{1}^{\frac{q-1}{2}}|\Omega\backslash\Omega_{r}|^{\frac{3-q}{2}}\rho^{\frac{q-1}{2}}}{\|\varphi_{1}\|_{2}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

By Lemma
4.1
,
\rho
is bounded from above provided
1<p\leq 2
. So the proof is done.

5
Dichotomy result for Brézis-Nirenberg problem in a ball

In this section, we consider the Brézis-Nirenberg problem in a ball subject to prescribed mass, namely

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\omega u=u^{2^{*}-1},\penalty\ u>0\quad\text{ in }B_{1}(0),\\
\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1}(0))}=\rho,\\
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1}(0)).\end{cases}

(5.1)

We learn from
[
31
]
that for
N\geq 4
and
\lambda\in\left(0,\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))\right)
, or
N=3
and
\lambda\in\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))}{4},\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))\right)
, the solution of (
1.13
) is unique and radially symmetric. We denote this unique solution by
u_{\lambda}
. Comparing (
5.1
) with (
1.13
), we only don’t know the
L^{2}
-norm of
u_{\lambda}
, which motivates us to explore the tendency of
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{2}
as
\lambda
varies in either
(0,\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0)))
when
N>3
or
\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))}{4},\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))\right)
when
N=3
. The first question is whether
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{2}
is continuous with respect to the parameter
\lambda
. Since the solution of (
1.13
) is radial and unique, (
1.13
) is equivalent to the following ordinary differential equation

\begin{cases}w^{\prime\prime}+\frac{N-1}{r}w^{\prime}+\lambda w+w^{2^{*}-1}=0,\\
w^{\prime}(0)=0,w(1)=0.\end{cases}

By the continuity theorem of ordinary differential equation on initial values and parameter, it can be seen that

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
N\geq 3
and
u_{\lambda}
is the unique solution to (
1.13
). Then when
N>3
,
u_{\lambda}
is continuous in
L^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))
with respect to
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0)))
; when
N=3
,
u_{\lambda}
is continuous in
L^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))
with respect to
\lambda\in\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))}{4},\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))\right)
.

The another question is: what’s the tendency of
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{2}
as
\lambda\to 0
for
N>3
, and as
\lambda\to\frac{\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))}{4}
for
N=3
, and as
\lambda\to\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))
for
N\geq 3
? It is answered by our next lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
N\geq 3
and
u_{\lambda}
is the unique solution to (
1.13
). Then when
N>3
,
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{2}\to 0
as
\lambda\to 0
and
\lambda\to\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))
, and when
N=3
,
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{2}\to 0
as
\lambda\to{\frac{\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))}{4}}
and
\lambda\to\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))
.

Concerning the proof of Lemma
5.2
, some related results were established in
[
24
,
31
]
. For completeness, we give it here.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

We consider the minimization problem

\theta_{\varepsilon}:=\inf\left\{J_{\varepsilon}(u)=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\varepsilon\|u\|_{2^{*}}^{2},\;\;u\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{1}(0)),\;\|u\|_{2}=1\right\}.

By
[
31
, Lemma 4.1]
, for
\varepsilon\in(0,\mathcal{S})
,
\theta_{\varepsilon}
can be attained, where
\mathcal{S}
is the best Sobolev constant. By
[
31
, Lemma 4.2]
, we have that
\theta_{\varepsilon}
is continuous and decreasing with respect to the parameter
\varepsilon
, and

\displaystyle\theta_{\varepsilon}\to\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))\quad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

\displaystyle\theta_{\varepsilon}\to\begin{cases}0,\quad&N\geq 4,\\
\frac{\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0))}{4},\quad&N=3,\end{cases}\quad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\to\mathcal{S}.

The minimizer of
\theta_{\varepsilon}
can be denoted by
v_{\varepsilon}
, which satisfies

\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{\varepsilon}=\theta_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}^{2-2^{*}}v_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}-1},\;\;\;v_{\varepsilon}>0\;\;\mbox{in }B_{1}(0),\\
v_{\varepsilon}=0\;\;\mbox{on }\partial B_{1}(0).\end{cases}

(5.2)

By the uniqueness,

u_{\lambda}=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2^{*}-2}}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}}{\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}},\quad\mbox{with }\lambda=\theta_{\varepsilon}.

(5.3)

We claim that
\{u_{\lambda}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(B_{1}(0))
as
\lambda\to\lambda_{1}
. In fact, it follows from (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) that

\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}=\theta_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}^{2},

(5.4)

and

\|\nabla u_{\theta_{\varepsilon}}\|_{2}^{2}=\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{2^{*}-2}}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|^{-2}_{2^{*}}\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}.

For
\varepsilon<\frac{\mathcal{S}}{2}
, we conclude from (
5.4
) and Sobolev inequality that

\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{\mathcal{S}}\right)\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}-\varepsilon\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}^{2}=\theta_{\varepsilon}<\lambda_{1}(B_{1}(0)).

As a consequence,
\{v_{\varepsilon}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(B_{1}(0))
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. By (
5.2
),
\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}=1
and
\theta_{\varepsilon}\to\lambda_{1}
,
v_{\varepsilon}
must converge to the first eigenfunction
\varphi_{1}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. It follows from (
5.3
) that
u_{\lambda}\to 0
in
H_{0}^{1}(B_{1}(0))
.

In the sequel, we shall show that
\{u_{\lambda}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(B_{1}(0))
as either
\lambda\to 0
for
N\geq 4
, or
\lambda\to\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4}
for
N=3
. In the following, we only consider
N\geq 4
, the case
N=3
is completely a same procedure.

For
\varepsilon\in(\frac{\mathcal{S}}{2},\mathcal{S})
, if
\{v_{\varepsilon}\}
is unbounded in
H_{0}^{1}(B_{1}(0))
, we can assume that as
\varepsilon\to\mathcal{S}
,

\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}\to\infty.

Form (
5.4
) and
\theta_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to\mathcal{S}
, we have

\frac{\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}}{\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}^{2}}\to\mathcal{S}.

(5.5)

It follows from (
5.3
) and (
5.5
) that

\|\nabla u_{\theta_{\varepsilon}}\|_{2}=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2^{*}-2}}\frac{\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}}{\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2^{*}}}\to\mathcal{S}^{\frac{2^{*}}{2(2^{*}-2)}}

as
\varepsilon\to\mathcal{S}
. So
\{u_{\lambda}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(B_{1}(0))
as
\lambda\to 0
.

Next, we assume
\{v_{\varepsilon}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(B_{1}(0))
as
\varepsilon\to\mathcal{S}
. Since
\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}=1
,
\{v_{\varepsilon}\}
has a positive lower bound in
L^{2^{*}}(B_{1}(0))
, which together with (
5.3
) implies that
\{u_{\lambda}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(B_{1}(0))
.

Up to a subsequence, there exists some
u\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{1}(0))
such
that
u_{\lambda}\rightharpoonup u
as
\lambda\to 0
. Therefore
u
is a solution of

-\Delta u=u^{2^{*}-1}

thus
u=0
. Finally, we have
u_{\lambda}\to 0
in
L^{2}(B_{1}(0))
as
\lambda\to 0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.7
completed.
From Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
, it is easily known that there are two solutions to (
5.1
) as
\rho
is small, and no solutions as
\rho
is large, and Theorem
1.7
can be proved.

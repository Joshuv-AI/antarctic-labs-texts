Relationship between personality characteristics and stress-load among obstetrics and gynaecologic nurses and midwives: the mediating effect of coping style
Authors: Qiqi Ye, Li Yuan, Kefu Zhong, Suqing Peng, Qihua Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStress is one of the factors that damage the mental health of nurses, part of which can likely be offset by individual personality characteristics and coping styles, and the degree of stress is related to some differences in marriage, night shift, department and so on. The purpose of this study is to investigate the situation and influencing factors of stress-load among nurses and midwives in obstetrics and gynaecology.MethodsFrom February 2023 to March 2023, a cross-sectional study was carried out involving 540 obstetric and gynaecological nurses and midwives from 21 hospitals in Guangdong Province, using the Chinese Big Five Personality Inventory (CBF-PI-B), Trait Coping Style Questionnaire (TCSQ), and Chinese version of Stress Load Scale (SOS). A bootstrap method was used to analyse the mediating effect of positive coping and negative coping between personality traits and stress load.ResultsThe survey achieved a response rate of 92.31% and a total effective rate of 78.89%. The stress load of obstetric and gynaecologic nurses and midwives was moderate, with a total score of 65.30 ± 17.27 points. The most common personality characteristics of the nurses and midwives was conscientiousness. There were significant positive correlations between neuroticism, negative coping, event load, and individual vulnerability (p < 0.01). Conscientiousness, openness, and positive coping were significantly negatively correlated with negative coping and individual vulnerability (p < 0.01). 45.4% of the difference can be explained by positive coping (R2 = 0.454, F = 352.873, p < 0.001), indicating a prominent contribution. Positive coping partially played a mediating role in the relationship between agreeableness (ab=-0.076, 95% CI: -0.145 to -0.020) and event load, while it fully mediated in the relationship between conscientiousness (ab = 0.219, 95% CI: -0.328 to -0.120) and openness (ab = 0.176, 95% CI: -0.277 to -0.091) and individual vulnerability. Negative coping partially played a mediating role in the association between neuroticism and event load (ab = 0.206, 95% CI: 0.151 to 0.269) and individual vulnerability (ab = 0.287, 95% CI: 0.215 to 0.355), while fully played a mediating role in the association between conscientiousness (ab=-0.176, 95% CI: -0.310 to -0.056) and openness (ab=-0.161, 95% CI: -0.261 to -0.063) and individual vulnerability, agreeableness (ab = 0.132, 95% CI: 0.023 to 0.251) and event load.ConclusionAt present, the stress load of obstetrics and gynaecological nurses and midwives is moderately high. Both positive coping and negative coping plays a mediating effect between neuroticism, conscientiousness, openness, agreeableness and stress load of nurses.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-24615-x.Keywords: Nurse, Midwives, Psychological stress, Personality, Coping skills, Mediation analysis

Background

Nurses, serving as key practitioners within the healthcare sector, face heightened risks of role conflict due to their multifaceted identities, which can precipitate occupational stress. The prevalence of gynaecological diseases among women aged 15 and above in China was 24.94%, representing approximately 20% of global gynaecological disease cases [1]. For instance, caring for common gynaecological cancer patients, it involves managing complex symptoms and providing psychological support to help patients maintain their dignity and social connections [2]. Studies have shown that gynaecology nurses need to conduct comprehensive health assessments and continuously enhance their knowledge and skills to better serve these patients [3]. Chronic occupational stress is recognized as a primary factor contributing to nurses’ negative emotions [4, 5]. Additionally, related researches highlight that the incidence of negative emotions among patients with gynaecological cancer is as high as 50–90%[6, 7], requiring nurses to operate in an emotionally intensive care environment. In China’s obstetric care context, 2020 data show, the overall perinatal mortality rate was about 4.14‰[8]. With rising rates of high-risk pregnancies, adverse outcomes including perinatal mortality show increasing incidence [9, 10]. Perinatal mortality constitutes a severe traumatic event in obstetric care, causing psychological trauma to patients [11, 12] and adversely affecting clinical staff [13, 14]. Consequently, nurses and midwives managing perinatal trauma cases are at risk of developing psychological distress [15]. Those with high stress loads show significant likelihood of developing post-traumatic stress reactions [16]. Prolonged exposure increases risks of burnout and other adverse psychological outcomes [17].

“Stress load” describes how anxious people feel as a result of stressful situations [18, 19]which is manifested as a series of nervous reactions caused by certain things or phenomena to people’s physiology and psychology. High stress loads frequently hamper the development of one’s occupational mental health and interfere with one’s ability to succeed in their work and fit into their company. Mihdawi [20] disclosure that the low nurse-to-patient ratio, resulting from insufficient human resources and the associated occupational stress, is highly likely to compromise the quality of nursing care and lead to poor patient outcomes. Currently, there remains a significant shortage of obstetrics and gynaecology nurses and midwives in China [21, 22]a situation that continues to threaten nursing safety and the provision of high-quality care. Therefore, hypothesis 1 is proposed: the stress-load of obstetrics and gynaecological nurses have demographic characteristics.

There are individual differences in people’s vulnerability perception in stressful events. Whether the person produces a stress response to the individual is closely related to the individual perception of person [23]. Personality is one of the common terms used to explain the differences in individual psychological behaviour in psychology, which means that among the factors the compose personality, they can trigger people’s behaviour and actively guide people’s behaviour, and enable individuals to show the same type of psychological structure in the face of different stimuli, with the characteristics of uniqueness, commonality and stability. Therefore, with the help of the analysis of personality characteristics, persons’ current behaviour can be derived from his future behaviour [24]. Individuals with high scores of “neuroticism” in personality characteristics are often susceptible to stress and easy to perceive more stress. The negative impact of personality characteristics is closely related to the secondary traumatic stress of nurses [19, 25–27]. It is necessary to identify the negative personality characteristics of nurses in the department of obstetrics and gynaecology under high stress, so as to help them reduce the perception of stress. Therefore, hypothesis 2 is put forward that personality characteristics are related to stress-load.

The personality model, comprising five factors—neuroticism, conscientiousness, agreeableness, openness and extroversion—is currently recognized as one of the prominent models in the field [28]. Furthermore, these five dimensions have been found to be closely associated with cognitive functions [29]. Coping-style is an individual’s constant cognitive and behavioural effort to reduce or eliminate the stress state and the accompanying emotional pain, which is an individual’s social adaptive behaviour in essence. Lazarus and Folkman put forward the stress-coping interaction model based on the theory of stress, appraisal, and coping which described relationship between effects of stress on individuals lie in the differences in the evaluation results of the relationship between person and individual needs [30]. Any positive or negative psychological or behavioural changes will affect the effect of stressful events.

Coping, as an important mediating condition of psychological stress, reflects the individual’s cognition and assessment of stressful events [23]. Related studies [31, 32] have also confirmed that the stress coping style is affected by many factors such as individual personality characteristics, cognitive evaluation, and so on. Individuals with different personality characteristics show different coping strategies in the same stressful situation. As one of the decisive factors of stress response [33]coping-style is a mediating factor between individual personality characteristics and mental health [34, 35].

Consequently, personality characteristics can affect the individual’s attitude towards stressful events through coping-style, and then changes the level of individual’s stress-load [24]. hypothesis 3 and hypothesis 4 are proposed: there is a correlation between personality traits and coping styles, and coping styles may play a mediating role in the relationship between personality traits and stress-load.

Currently, research on stress-related factors among nurses and midwives primarily focuses on negative aspects such as burnout [36]compassion fatigue [37]and workplace violence [38]. However, studies that focus solely on negative factors have not significantly alleviated the psychological issues long troubling the nursing population. Additionally, research on stress-related factors among obstetrics and gynaecology nurses and midwives needs to increase in quantity and should further explore factors such as personality and coping strategies, which encompass both negative and positive aspects and may have transformational characteristics. This could help transform negative factors and enhance the resilience of obstetrics and gynaecology nurses and midwives [39]. Based on the above hypothesis and current research gap, this study plans to investigate the current situation of personality characteristics, coping-style and stress-load of obstetrics and gynaecological nurses and midwives, explore the influencing mechanism among them, and analyse the possible mediating effect of coping-style, so as to provide theoretical basis and empirical evidence for the management and resource optimization of nurses, and promote the career success of nurses. Furthermore, it may contribute to the establishment of a more robust workforce of specialized gynaecology nurses and midwives in China and promote the development of social capital and organizational support, as well as the improvement of relevant welfare policies.

Methods

Study design and samples

A cross-sectional study was carried out to explore the level and influencing factors of stress-load among nurses and midwives in obstetrics and gynaecology, and a multistage stratified cluster random sampling method was used to collect data in Guangdong Province from the data were collected from February 2023 to March 2023.

We used random ball to select 22 public hospitals that belong to the Obstetrics and Gynaecology Nursing Professional Committee of Guangdong Association of Integrated Chinese and Western Medicine, including 11 Western medicine hospital and Traditional Chinese medicine hospital respectively, and ensured that the selected hospitals included obstetrics department, gynaecology department or the integrated department. The count of nurses or midwives in the unit of these hospitals ranges between 15 and 28. A total of 585 nurses and midwives were included in this study, with 21 hospitals and 540 volunteers agreed to participate finally.

The Questionnaire Star (https://www.wjx.cn/) online questionnaire mini program was used to conduct a survey, and unified guidance language was used to introduce the research purpose and content to the heads of each department. After obtaining their informed consent, the survey subjects were invited to anonymously fill out the research questionnaire. Finally, after excluding questionnaires with a response time of less than 5 min and a single or wavy answer composition, 426 valid questionnaires were collected, with a total effective rate of 78.89%. Flowchart of the sampling method

Inclusion criteria: (1) The participation has obtained a nurse professional qualification certificate; (2) Engaged in clinical nursing work in obstetrics or gynaecology department for at least 1 year; (3) Being on duty within the past month; (4) Volunteer to participate in this study.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Employees who are re-hired, retired, or pursuing further education; (2) The respondent has been clinically diagnosed with mental illness; (3) Simultaneously, participating in multiple psychological surveys and researchers.

Measures

Based on the stress-coping interaction model of Lazarus [30]there is an exact and ongoing cycle between individuals and their coping strategies. Personality, as an important factor affecting coping styles and perception of stress, plays a key role in this cycle. Coping is personality in action under stress [40]which reflects the dispositional response to a specific stressful episode [41]. Dimensions of personality derived from the five-personality factor model had important associations with coping responses [42]. Therefore, coping responses were best predicted by models that included effects of person and situation factors. A structured questionnaire was designed to collect information including general information, personality traits, coping style and stress level of participants. Here, the Stress Overload Scale, the Chinese Big Five Personality Inventory Brief, and the Trait Coping Style Questionnaire are positioned within the stress, appraisal, and coping components of the Lazarus’ model, respectively.

General information questionnaire

The instrument was used to collect characterized sociodemographic data that were self-designed by research team members. The items included age, gender, marital status, economic status, education level, job position, length of service in a specialized field, professional title, hospital level, employment type, family career support, and work motivation.

Personality

The instrument used to evaluate personality traits consisted of 40 items in neuroticism, agreeableness, extraversion, conscientiousness and openness dimensions from The Chinese Big Five Personality Inventory Brief (CBF-PI-B), which was prepared by Wang and colleagues [43]. Compared to other personality scales, the Big Five model was more comprehensive, allowing for greater interconnectivity and compatibility with other scales. Building on this, Wang developed the CBF-PI and its brief version, tailored for a broad Chinese population and incorporating Chinese cultural characteristics. Likert scoring method is utilized to indicate levels from “very inconsistent” to “very consistent” on a scale of 1 to 6. Each dimension is scored between 6 and 48 points. The higher the dimension score is, the more prominent the personality. In this study, the personality with the highest score was designated as the primary personality. Cronbach’s α coefficients for this scale in each dimension of the nurse population were 0.843, 0.760, 0.861, 0.792, and 0.732[19].

Coping-style

The instrument used to evaluate coping strategies related to personality traits consisted of 20 items in both positive and negative dimensions from the Trait Coping Style Questionnaire (TCSQ), which was prepared by Chinese scholars Jiang Qianjin and Zhu Yihong in 1999[44]. The scale was developed taking into consideration the following features: emotional coping, cross-situational consistency, and correlation with personality traits. The Likert scoring method is employed to rate responses from “very inconsistent” to “very consistent” on a scale of 1 to 5. The total score ranges from 20 to 100. The coefficients of Cronbach’s α in negative and positive coping are 0.70 and 0.69 respectively [45].

Stress-load

The instrument used to measure psychological stress level consisted of 22 items in both event load and individual vulnerability dimensions from the Chinese version of the Stress Overload Scale (SOS), which was translated and revised by Su [18] to fit the Chinese native culture. The advantage of SOS primarily include: subjective and widely applicable. This approach fosters a higher level of tolerance towards demographic variations, facilitating the assessment of their stress levels and the potential risk of secondary psychological issues concurrently. The Likert scoring method is utilized to express “strongly disagree” to “strongly agree” from 1 to 5 points. A higher total score indicates an increased risk of mental health issues and stress levels. The total reliability of the Chinese version of the SOS questionnaire in the nurse population was 0.936, with Cronbach’s α coefficients of 0.902 and 0.896 for the two dimensions, respectively [18].

Statistical analyses

SPSS 24.0 and AMOS 24.0 for Windows (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA) were used to analyse the valid data. First of all, Harman’s common method deviation analysis was conducted to assess the potential common method bias of each scale [46]. Subsequently, frequencies and descriptive analysis were utilized to delineate the characteristics of the participants and their stress-load level. Categorical data were presented in terms of frequency and composition ratio (%). Measurement data with normal or approximate a normal distribution were represented by (χ̅ ± s), while those with a skewed distribution were represented by the median and quartile range. Depending on the characteristics of each variable and adherence to the assumption of homogeneity of variance, paired t-tests, analysis of variance, or rank sum tests were employed for one-way analysis.

Next, correlations among various variables were assessed using Spearman correlation tests to detect collinearity, and multiple linear regression analysis was applied to analyse independent influencing factors.

Finally, a structural equation model was constructed with personality and its domains as the independent variable X, positive coping and negative coping as the mediating variable M, and stress-load and its dimensions as the dependent variable Y in the mediation analysis. The mediating analysis was performed using AMOS 24.0 for Windows to explore the mediating effect of coping style and their pathway of influence. Bootstrap method was utilized to sample the unconditional model for 2000 times to verify the significance of the initial value and changes [47, 48]. The standardized model was refined by model fits indices, including χ², the normed fit index (NFI), relative fit index (RFI), incremental fit index (IFI), Tucker Lewis index (TLI), comparative fit index (CFI) and root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA). A significant mediation path was identified when the 95% CI of coefficient ab did not encompass zero [48].

The study conducted significance test on the path coefficients of the conditional model to confirm the interrelationship among the developmental trajectories of each variable. Significance level α=0.05.

Ethical consideration

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Guangdong Provincial Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Project number: ZE2023-031).

The bootstrap method was used to repeat sampling 2000 times to verify the significance of the mediating effect, and the results showed that positive coping mediated stress load and all domains of personality, except neuroticism. Likewise, negative coping mediated stress load and all domains of personality, except extroversion. The mediating effect of negative coping and positive coping were further analysed.

The mediating effect of negative coping and positive coping were observed in personality traits and SOS domains (Table S1 in Supplement file). Positive coping mediated between various personality traits and individual vulnerability (ab = 0.219, 95% CI: −0.328 to −0.120; ab=−0.135, 95% CI: −0.232 to −0.050; ab = 0.176, 95% CI: −0.277 to −0.091; ab = 0.202, 95% CI: −0.331 to −0.101), except neuroticism. In the relationship between neuroticism and event load, as well as individual vulnerability, positive coping did not act as a mediator.

Negative coping (ab = 0.206, 95% CI: 0.151 to 0.269; ab=−0.130, 95% CI: −0.229 to −0.042; ab = 0.132, 95% CI: 0.023 to 0.251; ab=−0.118, 95% CI: −0.195 to −0.050) mediated the relationship between event load and neuroticism, conscientiousness, agreeableness and openness. Similarly, the mediation exists between individual vulnerability and four dimensions of personality (ab = 0.287, 95% CI:0.215 to 0.355; ab=−0.176, 95% CI: −0.310 to −0.056; ab = 0.188, 95% CI: 0.021 to 0.359; ab=−0.161, 95% CI: −0.261 to −0.063). What was more, negative coping did not mediate extroversion and event load, nor the individual vulnerability.

Discussion

The primary purpose of this study was to investigate the characteristics of stress load, personality traits, and coping strategies among obstetrics and gynaecology nurses and midwives, and to examine the mediating role of coping style in the relationship between personality and stress after controlling for relevant confounding factors. According to our findings, negative coping played a partial mediating role between neuroticism and stress load, while positive coping had the same performance between agreeableness and event load, and fully mediated the relationship between conscientiousness, openness, and individual vulnerability. A previous similar study indicated that nurses with prominent neuroticism tend to have lower adversity quotient levels [50]. Additionally, similar research has also found that Individuals with pronounced neuroticism, upon encountering stressors, are inclined to employ negative coping strategies to evade or neglect issues [30]which is consistent with the results of our study. When individuals encounters a situation beyond their processing capabilities, the negative coping of self-indulgence and self-abandonment often become predominant avoidance mechanisms [30]. in our study, the significant positive correlations between individual vulnerability and neuroticism, as well as between negative coping and event load, also show that negative coping plays the key role in the incidence of stress overload. Cai [51] reported that nurses with neuroticism, even after receiving training in workplace violence prevention and undergoing situational exercises, continue to experience repeated exposure to workplace violence. It suggests that nurses with this significant trait are prone to excessive and worthless self-blame and internal friction, depleting their psychological capital without being able to extricate themselves from adversity, obstetrics and gynaecology nurses with obvious neurotic personality need to cultivate internal positive coping strategies and improve their endogenous motivation to effectively cope with adversity, instead of relying solely on external positive psychological support.

The stress-load, reflecting an individual’s subjective perception of stress events, was found to be notably high among obstetrics and gynaecology nurses and midwives. This is consistent with previously reported stress load levels among Chinese nurses [52]and exceeds those of paediatrics [19]oncology [53, 54] and haemodialysis [55]nurses. This is similar to previous studies on stress perception among gynaecology and obstetrics nurses [56, 57]. Women’s cognitive processing tends to involve emotional aspects [58]and obstetrics and gynaecology nurses often empathic with their patients [59]. Over-empathic potentially leads to emotional strain and psychological fatigue. Consequently, it can well explain why stress-load levels of obstetrics and gynaecology nurses is higher than that of some peers. The two previous research also found that the most common sources of stress for obstetrics and gynaecology nurses are the imbalance between workload and time allocation, and more than half of the nurses have concerns about nursing errors and relationship with patients. This indicates that the time sensitivity of task load leading to these nurses perceiving an overload of stress, as found in our study, is objectively present.

Personality, serving as a symbolic expression of individuals traits, exerts a broad influence on basic emotions and behavioural patterns [60]. In our study, we identified an interesting phenomenon that may differ from previous research, which is worth exploring. We found that agreeableness, like neuroticism, has a positive correlation with event load. However, in meta-analysis [64] and empirical studies [65]agreeableness is typically viewed as a positive personality trait, closely linked with positive emotions and coping strategies. This suggests that agreeableness may have a complex relationship with stress perception and coping mechanisms. This discrepancy may stem from the commonality between neuroticism and agreeableness: both are associated with impaired cognitive function and cognitive levels [61]. Those high in agreeableness tend to adopt a more proactive stance, seeking assistance from others to address difficulties. Yet, due to cognitive limitations, their reasoning abilities may respond sluggishly, leading some individuals to over-rely on external help and exhibit a negative coping tendency, thereby intensifying the stress impact. This helps to provide a reasonable yet often overlooked perspective to explain why external social factors such as marital status, family relationships, and workplace [62]when they manifest negatively, can impact the stress load of obstetrics and gynaecology nurses, with personality driving the emergence of cognitive differences.

As we hypothesized, positive coping, as a crucial defensive factor of mental health, mediating the relationship between personality traits and event load and individual vulnerability. Ray [63] pointed out that coping strategies include self-control, crying, talking, thinking, self-consumption, etc. A favourable and harmonious interpersonal relationship is a pivotal factor in mitigating nurses’ stress responses [64]. Individuals who employ positive coping strategies during stressful events to counteract the negative impacts and enhance inner mindfulness typically exhibit personality traits of conscientiousness and openness. Common to both these traits is the proactive approach of seeking assistance from others and leveraging external conditions to diminish the risk of psychological stress. In a previous study exploring the association between personality and brain physiological functions, Adelstein [65] investigated the praecuneus and anterior cingulate cortex as seed regions, examining their correlation with personality traits. Findings of this research indicate that conscientiousness can predict the functional connectivity between the seed region and the medial frontal lobe system, suggesting that individuals with conscientiousness excel in situational judgment and planning. Their coping strategies are predominantly problem-cantered, enabling them to thoroughly assess environmental risks and adopt a positive stance to utilize surrounding resources, including interpersonal ones, to devise solutions to dilemmas. In a previous research [66]openness, regarding to reflect individual creativity and a thirst for knowledge, is positively associated with verbal fluency, working memory, and episodic memory. Nurses with openness can effectively express their negative emotions. Additionally, their superior communication skills and work capabilities provide them with abundant positive coping resources, allowing them to adeptly manage the impact of stress load on their professional lives [67].

The assessment of the severity of stressful events and personality differences are crucial factors in determining the choice of coping strategies [68]. Personality itself is neither inherently good nor bad. Our study effectively confirms that personality traits, traditionally viewed as positive, can also be negatively perceived under individuals’ negative coping strategies. This also illustrates that the impact of personality on stress perception can indeed be effectively modulated by different coping strategies.

The findings from our study hold significant implications for healthcare institution administrators and policymakers in relevant government departments. At the clinical settings, managers should pay attention to the interactive effects of obstetrics and gynaecology nurses under “person-situation” dynamics. By organizing mindfulness training and leveraging the positive empathy and cognitive effects of these nurses in the “place of life birth,” the sense of professional fulfilment among this group can be enhanced [69]. Furthermore, healthcare administrators must pay attention to obstetrics and gynaecology nurses who have been involved in the care of perinatal deaths, hysterectomies, or are suffering from post-traumatic stress responses in the process. In the absence of relevant coping experience or training guidance, this group is particularly prone to negative feelings related to nursing events, thereby affecting the balance of coping strategies. It is essential to establish a multidisciplinary professional team to provide psychological assistance to patients who have experienced related stressful events and to nurses and midwives involved in the care process [16, 70]. This is also what many nurses and midwives have called for and hoped for.

In China, there is still a certain lack of understanding towards nurses and midwives, and in terms of professional recognition, midwives are often categorized alongside nurses, which is closely related to the policy regulations of the childbirth industry in China. Therefore, we propose that at the policy-making, enhancing the identity recognition and differentiation of nurses and midwives can help strengthen social support for these groups [71, 72]improve their professional identity and sense of well-being, and greatly benefit the reduction of stress load.

Strengths and limitations

A strength of the study lies in its sampling scope, encompassing a total of 21 hospitals across two levels, thereby enriching the sample source. Additionally, the study incorporated demographic information questionnaire design from previous studies, and introduced important and novel indicators such as family-career support, and work motivation. These indicators were substantiated through subsequent statistical analysis to exhibit significant differences.

However, constraints in manpower and time precluded a deeper analysis of the effect pathway of various influencing factors on stress load. The research predominantly focused on hospitals classified as 3rd Grade A and did not involve male nurses, potentially introducing bias into the research outcomes.

Conclusion

This study revealed novel insights into demographic information, personality traits and coping styles. The influencing factors of family, organization and society were found to significantly impact stress loads of these nurses and midwives. The mediating effect of coping style on the relationship between neuroticism, agreeable, openness and conscientiousness and the stress load of nurses and midwives has been proven, potentially offering empirical evidence for relevant psychological research. The elevated levels of stress load among the nurses in obstetrics and gynaecology departments underscore the necessity for nursing managers to establish a robust psychological health monitoring mechanism. Actively responding to the appeals of obstetrics and gynaecology nurses and midwives for the establishment of professional teams to intervene in post-traumatic stress responses to nursing events, and effectively utilizing positive situational factors in the workplace to cultivate positive coping abilities among these groups, holds significant practical value.

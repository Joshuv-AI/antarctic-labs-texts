Impacts of fluorophenyl-terminal side chains on crystal structures and charge-transport properties of n-type organic semiconductors with layered brickwork packing motif
Authors: Shohei Kumagai, Takeru Koguma, Eiji Fukuzaki, Takuya Seki, Go Watanabe, Ryoya Kimura, Masafumi Yano, Hiroyuki Ishii, Jun Takeya, Toshihiro Okamoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMolecular packing is crucial for charge-transport properties of organic semiconductors (OSCs). Nowadays, most OSCs consist of the π-electron core and insulating side-chain substituents. While the former is the primary factor of molecular packing and resultant charge transport, the latter is also the significant tuning element for them. Hence, it is worth addressing their individual roles. In this work, a potential impact of fluorine substitution on molecular packing and charge-transport properties is reported based on the positional isomers of n-type OSCs which have phenethyl side chains: para-, meta- and ortho-substituted derivatives called p-FPhC2−BQQDI, m-FPhC2−BQQDI and o-FPhC2−BQQDI, respectively. Crystallographic analyses showed that the fluorine substitution position varies the intermolecular interactions through noncovalent C−H∙∙∙F interactions. Although resultant brickwork-type molecular packings suggested similar charge-transport capabilities, single-crystal thin-film transistor assessments revealed distinct differences in the electron mobility: 0.44, 0.92 and 3.56 cm2 V−1 s−1 for o-FPhC2−BQQDI, m-FPhC2−BQQDI and p-FPhC2−BQQDI, respectively. The difference between m-FPhC2−BQQDI and p-FPhC2−BQQDI is attributed to dynamic disorder effects based on molecular dynamics simulations, whereas the lowest mobility of o-FPhC2−BQQDI may further result from static, orientational disordering of the side chains revealed by X-ray crystallography. Introduction

Molecular packing of π-conjugated compounds is a crucial factor in their charge-transport capabilities to act as organic semiconductors (OSCs). Aside from a π-electron core which principally contributes to the charge-transport system, side-chain substituents are a significant tuning element of molecular packing1,2. Therefore, insights into the impact of side chains on molecular packing and charge-transport properties will contribute to the development of high-performance OSCs and organic electronic devices. Organic fluorine is a potential tool of crystal engineering of organic compounds due to its ability to form various noncovalent interactions, such as C−F∙∙∙F−C, C−H∙∙∙F and F∙∙∙π3. For electron-transporting (n-type) OSCs, fluorine and fluorinated functional groups have also shown advantages in enhancing charge-transport properties in n-channel thin-film transistors (TFTs) owing to their ability to lower the lowest-unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energies and their hydrophobic properties4–9. Hence, the crystal engineering of n-type OSCs by utilizing organic fluorine is of great interest for tackling the long-standing issue of lower performance of n-type OSCs compared to hole-transporting (p-type) counterparts.

Perylene diimide (PDI) is one of the most prominent π-electron cores for n-type OSCs10,11. Recently, nitrogen-doped PDI analogue, namely, 3,4,9,10-benzo[de]isoquinolino[1,8-gh]quinolinetetracarboxylic diimide (BQQDI), was developed for n-type OSCs with the low-lying LUMO enough for air-stable n-channel organic TFTs (ca. −4.1 eV vs. vacuum level)12,13. In addition, multiple C−H∙∙∙O and C−H∙∙∙N attractive interactions between BQQDI π-electron cores are observed in various derivatives, leading to the layered brickwork packing motif. As for the role of side chains in the crystal structure, the benchmarked BQQDI derivative with 2-phenylethyl substituents (PhC2−BQQDI) shows interlayer C−H∙∙∙π interactions between the phenyl groups (Fig. 1a)12. Such C−H∙∙∙π interactions could result in a robust aggregated structure and high electron mobility of 3.0 cm2 V−1 s−1 accompanied by band-like charge-transport properties14,15. By replacing the terminal phenyl groups with perfluorophenyl analogue, F5PhC2−BQQDI shows the similar brickwork structure, while an interlayer F∙∙∙π interaction appeared instead of the C−H∙∙∙π interactions (Fig. 1b)9. Hence, PhC2−BQQDI analogues with functionalized terminal phenyl rings are anticipated to be suitable for studying the impact of fluorine-containing side chains on crystal structures.

In this work, isomeric PhC2−BQQDI analogues with mono-fluorinated phenyl moieties at either ortho (o-FPhC2−BQQDI), meta (m-FPhC2−BQQDI) or para (p-FPhC2−BQQDI) position are investigated (also, the corresponding 2-(fluorophenyl)ethyl groups are hereafter abbreviated as o-FPhC2, m-FPhC2 and p-FPhC2, respectively, for simplification). While their crystal packing manner still resembled that of PhC2−BQQDI, the fluorine substitution position modified the inter- and intralayer intermolecular interactions through the fluorine. TFT studies based on solution-processed single crystals revealed electron mobilities (µe) dependent on the fluorine substitution position; best µe were 3.56, 0.92 and 0.44 cm2 V−1 s−1 for p-, m- and o-FPhC2−BQQDI, respectively. However, charge-transport capabilities conventionally calculated on the crystal structures did not differ significantly. By using molecular dynamics (MD) simulation, the discrepancy in µe between p-FPhC2−BQQDI and m-FPhC2−BQQDI was attributed to dynamic disorder16–19, while structural disorder in o-FPhC2−BQQDI may further reduce its µe.

Results

Synthesis and fundamental characterization

Three 2-(fluorophenyl)ethyl-substituted BQQDI derivatives were synthesized by the similar procedure with that for PhC2−BQQDI12 (Scheme S1), where a fluorine was introduced to ortho, meta or para position, respectively, of the phenyl group (o-FPhC2−BQQDI, m-FPhC2−BQQDI and p-FPhC2−BQQDI; Fig. 1c). Thermogravimetry−differential thermal analysis (TG−DTA) revealed their high thermal stability up to 390 °C and no phase transition behaviors (Fig. S1 and Table S1). In addition, their chemical stability in the solid state was verified by tracing the ultraviolet−visible absorption spectra in air over one month (Fig. S2).

Single crystal structures were determined by X-ray diffraction analysis (Table S2). All derivatives demonstrate the layered brickwork packing motif as PhC2−BQQDI (Fig. 2a). The brickwork structure is composed of two kinds of π-stacked geometries (Fig. 2b). The π-stacking geometries are nearly identical in the present derivatives (Table 1), which implies similar charge-transport capabilities.

Despite similar π-stacking geometries, the fluorine substitution position affects intermolecular interactions. As shown in Figs. 2c and S3, in p-FPhC2−BQQDI, a C−H∙∙∙F interaction forms between the F atom of fluorophenyl (FPh) group and an H atom at the β-position of the ethyl moiety of an adjacent molecule within the same brickwork layer (corresponding to the dπ2 direction). The key parameters for this intralayer interaction are an H∙∙∙F distance (dH∙∙∙F) of 2.55 Å and a C−H−F angle (∠(C − H∙∙∙F)) is 118°.

In contrast, m-FPhC2−BQQDI shows two simultaneous C–H∙∙∙F interactions between interdigitated m-FPh groups of adjacent layers (Figs. 2d and S4). These interlayer interactions (dH∙∙∙F = 2.52 Å; ∠(C−H∙∙∙F) = 172°) result in a different crystal symmetry, leading to the triclinic space group, unlike the monoclinic P21/n of p-FPhC2−BQQDI.

As for o-FPhC2−BQQDI, site disorder of the o-FPh moiety accompanied by an opposite orientation of fluorine was found at occupancy ratio of 0.56:0.44 (Fig. S5). Although these o-FPh moieties are intrinsically disordered, we will discuss two simplified, ordered packing structures—one presenting each orientation—for clarity. These will be referred to as the ‘major form’ (site occupancy of 0.56) and ‘minor form’ (0.44). In the major form, F presents interlayer close contacts with H at the meta position of the phenyl ring (dH∙∙∙F = 2.42 Å; ∠(C−H∙∙∙F) of 152°) (Fig. 2e), whereas F in the minor form shows an intralayer close contact with H on the BQQDI π-electron core (dH∙∙∙F = 2.46 Å; ∠(C−H∙∙∙F) = 165°) (Fig. 2f). Thus, the abovementioned crystal structures could be classified into those with intralayer (p-FPhC2−BQQDI and minor o-FPhC2−BQQDI) or interlayer (m-FPhC2−BQQDI and major o-FPhC2−BQQDI) C−H∙∙∙F interactions.

Computational calculation of charge-transport capability

Based on the crystallographic data, transfer integrals (t; t1, t2 and t3 are defined as shown in Fig. 2a) between the LUMOs of the adjacent molecules were calculated to examine the charge-transport capability. As implied above, t values of three derivatives are roughly comparable with each other (Table 1). Electronic band calculations also gave similar effective masses (m*) of electron carriers in a conducting plane (Fig. S6 and Table S3).

Solution-processed single-crystal TFTs

To investigate the experimental charge-transport properties, single-crystal thin-film transistors (SCTFTs) were fabricated. Single-crystal thin films were grown by a solution-crystallization method, namely, the edge-casting method21,22, on n++-Si/SiO2 substrates modified by trimethoxy(2-phenylethyl)silane (β-PTS), and devices were fabricated in the bottom-gate/top-contact geometry (Figs. 3a − 3b). All compounds exhibited the typical n-channel TFT operation (Figs. 3c−f and S7), but their µe revealed remarkable differences depending on the fluorine substitution position (Table 2 and S4). The best/average µe was 3.56/2.10, 0.92/0.80 and 0.44/0.35 cm2 V−1 s−1 for p-FPhC2−BQQDI, m-FPhC2−BQQDI and o-FPhC2−BQQDI, respectively. Although the absolute differences are not orders of magnitude apart, the small device-to-device variations confirm the reliability of this trend.

These experimental results contrast sharply with the computational calculations, which suggested comparable charge-transport capabilities among the isomers. The threshold voltage (Vth) also depended significantly on the substitution position, as will be addressed in the Discussion section. Therefore, the differences in experimental µe cannot be readily explained by the equilibrium crystal structures or the calculated transfer integrals and effective masses (Table S3).

Molecular dynamics simulation for insights into dynamic disorder

To get further insights into the discrepancy between calculated and experimental charge-transport properties, the effect of molecular motions (dynamic disorder16–19 was investigated. Impacts of side chains on dynamic disorder have been reported in several literatures, such as the suppression of translational sliding motions by alkyl substituents23,24 and dynamic electronic coupling of thiophene substituents with the π-electron core through torsional motions25. In this work, the dynamics was addressed by molecular dynamics (MD) simulations based on the crystal structure (Fig. 4 and Table S7). Thermal motions are visually assessed by the B-factor, which describes atom-resolved isotropic thermal factors (Fig. 4a). Focusing on the B-factor of the BQQDI π-electron core elements, bluish colors indicate relatively small thermal motions partly owing to the attractive C−H∙∙∙N and C−H∙∙∙O interactions. On the other hand, FPh moieties tend to exhibit relatively large B-factors in all the derivatives. Among the compounds studied herein, p-FPhC2−BQQDI shows the smallest B-factors.

The dynamic disorder is deduced by the statistical analysis of transfer integrals based on the MD results. Figure 4b,c and Table 3 summarize the statistics of t1 and t2 calculated on the molecular geometries and coordinates as a consequence of MD simulations. The statistical analysis on m-FPhC2−BQQDI shows an average t (tavg) value for the t1 and t2 direction lower than p-FPhC2−BQQDI by 19% and 15%, respectively. In addition, the coefficient of variation σ/tavg, where σ stands for the standard deviation, is significantly larger in both directions for m-FPhC2−BQQDI. Since the large σ/tavg suggests a significant dynamic disorder and low carrier mobility18,26, the lower µe of m-FPhC2−BQQDI than that of p-FPhC2−BQQDI is attributable to the dynamic disorder effects. On the other hand, the dynamic disorder could not reasonably explain the lowest µe of o-FPhC2−BQQDI because its σ/tavg values are somewhat smaller than that of m-FPhC2−BQQDI. Although o-FPhC2 −BQQDI was simulated for the major or minor ordered systems, the obtained σ/tavg values are in-between those for p-FPhC2−BQQDI and m-FPhC2−BQQDI, suggesting that our simplification of simulating ordered packing structures does not critically affect the comparative conclusions regarding dynamic disorder among the isomers. At this stage, the low µe of o-FPhC2−BQQDI would be attributed to the site (i.e., static) disorder which may induce carrier trap states27 in addition to dynamic disorder.

Discussion

The effects of fluorine substitution of the phenethyl side chain are herein discussed for positional isomers p-FPhC2−BQQDI, m-FPhC2−BQQDI and o-FPhC2−BQQDI in terms of the packing structure and charge-transport properties. They exhibit similar brickwork packing manner, also resembling the molecular packing of the parent PhC2−BQQDI despite the fluorine substitution (Table 1). These facts reveal that the space-filling effect of the PhC2−BQQDI skeleton plays a primary role in the formation of a brickwork molecular packing in cooperation with the characteristic C−H∙∙∙N and C−H∙∙∙O attractive interactions between BQQDI π-electron cores. Then, the fluorine substitution induced weakly attractive C−H∙∙∙F interactions (0.6 − 1.0 kcal mol−1 for each; see Figs. S4 and S5) within or between brickwork-motif charge-transport layers dependent on the substitution position, which leads to fine-tuning of the molecular packing.

The impact of C−H∙∙∙F interactions was experimentally found on the charge-transport properties principally through dynamic disorder, although static disorder is likely a significant factor contributing to the lower µe in o-FPhC2−BQQDI. p-FPhC2−BQQDI showed µe of 3.56 cm2 V−1 s−1 at best, indicating the charge-transport property comparable with (or rather higher than) the benchmarked PhC2−BQQDI. However, the effective mass (Table S3) and dynamic disorder parameter σ/tavg (Table 3) suggest better charge-transport properties of PhC2−BQQDI. Although the difference in σ/tavg between p-FPhC2−BQQDI and m-FPhC2−BQQDI supports the observed trend in µe, the difference does not seem large enough to be conclusive, especially since σ/tavg is not quantitatively linked to µe at this stage. The discrepancy likely arises because our calculations model the bulk crystal, whereas the experiments probe charge transport at semiconductor-dielectric interface. Particularly in m-FPhC2−BQQDI, the lack of the C−H∙∙∙F interaction pair at the gate dielectric interface probably intensifies the molecular motions, which increases the effective σ/tavg thus reduces µe. Hence, more advanced computational approaches taking interfaces into account may provide more accurate insights, which will be attempted in our future work.

In addition, as mentioned above, not only µe but also Vth were remarkably varied in the SCTFTs (Table S2). A possible origin might be attributed to the dipolar character of FPhC2 side chains which are aligned at the OSC−gate dielectric interface like self-assembled monolayers28,29 (Fig. S10 and Table S8). Although further investigations are required for deeper understanding, the structure−property relationships of the current derivatives are too complicated to investigate more details because of the difficulty in estimating the interface structure and the static disorder observed in o-FPhC2−BQQDI, etc. Future work employing interface-sensitive analytical techniques, such as advanced spectroscopic methods or operando measurements, coupled with more sophisticated interface simulations, will be crucial for fully elucidating the phenomena highlighted in this study.

The dipolar character pointed out also implies its influence on µe. Previous work on single-crystal TFT reported little influence of SAM species on charge mobility despite a significant Vth shift30. In the present work, the subordinate influence of side chain on the interfacial trap density is suggested by the comparable subthreshold swing (Fig. S11 and Table S9). Also owing to similar side-chain structures and lengths31, the present dipolar effect is not likely to have a dominant impact on the µe of these single-crystal TFTs, which appears to be governed primarily by dynamic and static disorder.

Finally, we believe this work will stimulate further experimental, theoretical and computational research on device-level understandings of charge transport properties in organic TFTs, leading to developments of high-mobility OSCs and practical TFTs.

Methods

Chemical characterization methods

1H nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were acquired with a Bruker AVANCE III HD spectrometer at the Facility Station Division, the Core Facility Center in Institute of Science Tokyo. Chemical shifts (δ) are reported in parts per million (ppm). 1H NMR spectra are referenced to residual protons of 1,1,2,2-tetrachloroethane (δ 5.93 ppm) as an internal standard. The data are presented in the following format: chemical shift, multiplicity (s = singlet, d = doublet, t = triplet, td = triplet of doublets, m = multiplet), coupling constant in Hertz (Hz), signal area integration in natural numbers. Elemental analysis with a J-Science Lab JM10 CHN analyzer and time-of-flight high-resolution mass (TOF-HRMS) spectrometry with a Thermo Fisher LTQ-Orbitrap XL with the atmospheric pressure chemical ionization method were carried out at Comprehensive Analysis Center, The Institute of Scientific and Industrial Research, Osaka University. Thermogravimetry − differential thermal analysis (TG − DTA) was performed with a Rigaku Thermo plus EVO2 TG8120 under a stream of nitrogen (100 cm3 min− 1). Temperature was swept from room temperature to 500 °C at 1 K min− 1. α-Alumina was used as a reference. Ultraviolet − visible (UV − vis) absorption spectra were recorded on a JASCO V670 spectrometer. Polycrystalline films with the nominal thickness of 100 nm were deposited by vacuum evaporation on a synthetic quartz substrate at a deposition rate of 0.5 Å s− 1. The substrate temperature was maintained at room temperature during the depositions.

Single-crystal X-ray crystallography

Single crystal X-ray diffraction data were collected either on a Rigaku R-AXIS RAPID II imaging plate diffractometer with CuKα radiation (λ = 1.54187 Å) or a Rigaku R-AXIS IV at BL02B1 beamline32 at SPring-8 with synchrotron X-ray radiation (λ = 0.43034 Å). Measurements were performed at ambient temperature. The structures were solved by dual-space method (SHELXT33 (Ver. 2014/4 or 2014/5)) and refined by full-matrix least-squares procedures on F2 for all reflections (SHELXL34 (Ver. 2014/7 or 2017/1)) by using Yadokari-XG 200935 or CrystalStructure 4.2.2 interface (Rigaku Corporation). While all hydrogen atoms were placed at calculated positions, the other atoms were refined anisotropically.

Quantum chemical and band calculations

Molecular orbitals were calculated. Dipole moment was calculated based on density functional theory (DFT) by using Gaussian 09 interface36. For simplification, (fluorophenyl)ethane isomers were calculated at the B3LYP/aug-cc-pVDZ level of DFT accompanied by geometry optimization. The force constant calculations were conducted at the M06-2X/6–31++G** level of DFT with counterpoise correction37,38 for the basis set superposition error. Transfer integrals between the LUMO of neighboring molecules were calculated using dimer method39 at the PBEPBE/6-31G* level of DFT. SCTFT fabrication and evaluation

The SCTFTs were prepared in the bottom-gate/top-contact geometry. All the procedures except thermal annealing and vacuum deposition were carried out in air. A highly doped silicon wafer (n++-Si, acting as a gate electrode) with a thermally oxidized SiO2 insulating layer (200 nm) was used as the substrate. The surface of SiO2 was modified by chemisorption of trimethoxy(2-phenylethyl)silane (β-PTS). The n++-Si/SiO2 wafer was washed sequentially by ultrasonication in acetone and 2-propanol for 10 min each, then dried for 10 min at 100 °C on a hotplate. UV−O3 cleaning was performed for 10 min, followed by exposure to vaporized β-PTS for 3 h at 130 °C. The wafer was then washed by ultrasonication in toluene and 2-propanol, followed by drying on a hotplate at 100 °C for 10 min. Crystalline thin films of the BQQDI derivatives were deposited by a solution-crystallization method, namely, the edge-casting method21. A drop of 0.02-wt% solutions in 1-methynaphthalene dissolved at 150 °C were dried on the substrates at about 120 °C. The thin films were further annealed for 12 h under vacuum at 100 °C. Source and drain electrodes were formed by vacuum deposition of Au (40 nm) through a metal shadow mask. The OSC thin films were edged by laser ablation to define the SCTFT. The channel length was 100 μm, whereas channel width was dependent on the device, ranging from 50 to 200 μm. Before electrical evaluations, the SCTFTs were thermally annealed at 100 °C for 2 h in a vacuum oven.

Synthesis

All reagents and solvents were used without further purification unless otherwise noted. As the starting material, 3,4,9,10-benzo[de]isoquinolino[1,8-gh]quinolinetetracarboxylic dianhydride (BQQ−TCDA) was synthesized according to the literature procedure12. The general reaction scheme is shown in Scheme S1. After the products were recrystallized once from 1,2-dichlorobenzene (oDCB), further purification was performed by vacuum sublimation before fundamental and device characterizations.

General synthetic procedure

A flamed-dried, Teflon screw-stoppered tube was purged with argon and charged with BQQ−TCDA (1.0 equiv.), oDCB (0.03 M), 2-(fluorophenyl)ethylamine (3.0 equiv.) and propionic acid (100 equiv.), and the reaction mixture was stirred at 150 °C. The crude products were precipitated by adding excess methanol and collected by suction filtration, followed by recrystallization from oDCB.

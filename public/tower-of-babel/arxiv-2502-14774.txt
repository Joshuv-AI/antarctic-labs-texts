Branching with selection and mutation II: Mutant fitness of Gumbel type
Su-Chan Park, Joachim Krug, Peter Mörters

Abstract
We study a model of a branching process subject to selection , modeled by giving each family an individual fitness acting as a branching rate, and mutation , modeled by resampling the fitness of a proportion of offspring in each generation. For two large classes of fitness distributions of Gumbel type we determine the growth of the population, almost surely on survival. We then study the empirical fitness distribution in a simplified model, which is numerically indistinguishable from the original model, and show the emergence of a Gaussian travelling wave.

Introduction

We consider the branching processes with selection and mutation introduced in
[
1
]
. These are models of a population evolving in discrete non-overlapping generations with model parameters given by a probability distribution
\mu
on
(0,\infty)
, which serves as a
means to sample a random fitness of a mutant, and a mutation probability
\beta\in(0,1)
.
For later reference, we denote the tail function by

.
Note that

is a right-continuous-left-limit function that may be discontinuous.

A brief description of the two model variants goes as follows: In each generation a population consists of finitely many individuals each equipped with a positive fitness. Any individual lives only for one generation. Every generation produces a random number of offspring, which is Poisson distributed with the mean given by the sum over all the fitnesses of the individuals in the generation. Now every offspring individual independently

•

with probability
1-\beta

a parent with a probability proportional to its fitness. The offspring becomes an individual of the next generation with the fitness inherited from the parent;

•

otherwise, with probability
\beta
, it is a mutant and gets a fitness randomly sampled from
\mu
.

–

In the
fittest mutant model (FMM)
only one mutant with largest fitness among all mutants, if it exists, joins the next generation and the others die immediately.

–

In the
multiple mutant model (MMM)
all mutants join the next generation.

We write
X(t)
for the number of individuals in generation

t
, irrespective of what initial condition is used and which model
variant is under consideration.
Further

[

. Other branching models including selection or mutation are

Similar models have been applied for the description of the genetic structure of proliferating tumors and growing populations of pathogens

In the case of a slowly decreasing tail at infinity, i.e. when

The present paper is concerned with unbounded fitness distributions with

For unbounded fitness distributions of Gumbel type the population grows at a rate between exponential and doubly exponential.
This is a wide range that cannot be easily covered by a single functional expression. Therefore we introduce parametrised subclasses of fitness distributions and show how the population grows for these subclasses in dependence of the parameters. Before stating our full results in Section 2 we describe an interesting example to give a flavour.

We look at fitness distributions with stretched exponential tail satisfying

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log(1/G(x))}{x^{\alpha}L(x)}=1,

for a slowly varying function

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log X(t)}{t\log t}=\frac{1}{\alpha},\qquad\text{
almost surely on survival.}

The superexponential growth is driven by the fitness

of the fittest mutant in generation

\lim_{t\to\infty}\,\frac{W_{t}}{t^{1/\alpha}(\log t)^{1/\alpha}(\alpha L(t^{1/\alpha}))^{-1/\alpha}}=1.

In Section 3 we describe the subtle interplay of population size and fittest mutant heuristically in terms of a differential equation. Simulations demonstrated in Section 7 show that the distribution of fitness in a positive proportion of the population in generation
t
concentrates

4

is replaced by its deterministic asymptotics.

The rest of this paper is organised as follows. Full results on the growth of the population and the driving fitness are formulated as Theorem 1 and 2 in Section 2. The section also formulates, as Theorem 3, the conjectured behaviour of the travelling wave for the full model. Section 3 heuristically describes the interplay of these quantities. Section 4 contains preparation for the proofs of Theorem 1, given in Section 5, and Theorem 2, given in Section 6. Section 7 explains the approximations needed to simulate and prove the travelling wave result restated now in rigorous form as Theorem 4. We finish the paper with concluding remarks in Section 8.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
0<b<1
,
\theta>1
,

\lfloor b\theta\rfloor\geq 1
, and
(1-b)\theta\geq 1
,
then

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\lfloor b\theta\rfloor}e^{-\theta}\frac{\theta^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle\leq\theta e^{-\theta(1-b+b\log b)}.

Proof.

Let
\ell:=\lfloor b\theta\rfloor
and

a_{m}:=\theta^{m}/m!
.
Note that
\ell\leq b\theta\leq\theta-1
by the assumption.
Since
a_{m}/a_{m-1}=\theta/m
,
we have
a_{m}\leq a_{\ell}
for all
m\leq\ell<\theta
and, therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}\frac{\theta^{m}}{m!}\leq(\ell+1)\frac{\theta^{\ell}}{\ell!}\leq\theta\frac{\theta^{\ell}}{\ell!}.

Using
m!\geq m^{m}e^{-m}
(
m\geq 1
), we find

\log\frac{\theta^{\ell}}{\ell!}\leq\ell\log\theta-\ell\log\ell+\ell.

Observing that
x\log\theta-x\log x+x
is an increasing function
in the region
0<x<\theta
, we finally have

\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}e^{-\theta}\frac{\theta^{m}}{m!}\leq\theta e^{-\theta+b\theta\log\theta-b\theta\log(b\theta)+b\theta}=\theta e^{-\theta(1-b+b\log b)},

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
B>1
and
\theta>0
,
then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=\lceil B\theta\rceil}^{\infty}e^{-\theta}\frac{\theta^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{B}{B-1}e^{-\theta(1-B+B\log B)}.

Proof.

Let
m:=\lceil B\theta\rceil
. Since
(m+k)!\geq m!m^{k}
and
\theta/m\leq 1/B<1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{k}}{k!}\leq\frac{\theta^{m}}{m!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\theta}{m}\right)^{k}=\frac{\theta^{m}}{m!}\frac{1}{1-(\theta/m)}\leq\frac{B}{B-1}e^{m\log\theta-m\log m+m}\leq\frac{B}{B-1}e^{B\theta-B\theta\log B},

where we have used
m!\geq m^{m}e^{-m}
and that

x\log\theta-x\log x+x
is a decreasing function
in the region
\theta<x
.
Multiplying by
e^{-\theta}
, we get the desired inequality.
∎

Lemma 4.4

If

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{X}_{t}\geq bxf|\mathcal{X}_{t-1}\geq x)\geq 1-xfe^{-xf(1-b+b\log b)}.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
A_{t}:=\{a_{t}\leq\mathcal{X}_{t}\leq b_{t}\}
, where

\mathbb{P}(A_{t}|\mathcal{X}_{t-1}=m)\geq f_{t}>0

\mathbb{P}\left(E_{t}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}(A_{\tau})\prod_{k=\tau+1}^{t}f_{k}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(E_{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=\lceil a_{t-1}\rceil}^{\lfloor b_{t-1}\rfloor}\mathbb{P}(A_{t}|\mathcal{X}_{t-1}=m)\mathbb{P}\left(\{\mathcal{X}_{t-1}=m\}\cap E_{t-2}\right)

Lemma 4.6
.

If
0<b<1
,
\theta\geq f\geq 1/(1-b+b\log b)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{X}_{t}\geq b^{t}f^{t}\text{ for all }t\geq\tau|\mathcal{X}_{\tau}\geq b^{\tau}f^{\tau}\right)\geq 1-f\big(1+\tfrac{1}{\log(bf)}\big)e^{-f(1-b+b\log b)},

for any nonnegative integer
\tau

Note that the right hand side does not depend on
\tau

Proof.

For any event
E
, we write

\mathbb{P}_{c}(E):=\mathbb{P}(E|\mathcal{X}_{\tau}\geq b^{\tau}f^{\tau})

in this proof.
Define

Note that

\mathbb{P}_{c}(A_{\tau})=1,\quad\mathbb{P}_{c}\left(\mathcal{X}_{t}\geq b^{t}f^{t}\text{ for all }t\geq\tau\right)=\mathbb{P}_{c}(C)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{c}(C_{t}).

Using (
18
) with

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{t}|A_{t-1})\geq 1-f(bf)^{t-1}\exp\left[-f(bf)^{t-1}(1-b+b\log b)\right]=:1-d_{t}.

By Lemma
4.5
we can write

\mathbb{P}_{c}(C)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{c}(C_{t})\geq\prod_{t=\tau+1}^{\infty}(1-d_{t})\geq 1-\sum_{t=\tau+1}^{\infty}d_{t}\geq 1-\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}d_{t}.

Since

(c^{t-1}\exp(-ac^{t-1}))_{t\geq 1}
is a decreasing

and

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}c^{t-1}\exp\left(-ac^{t-1}\right)=e^{-a}+\sum_{t=2}^{\infty}c^{t-1}\exp\left(-ac^{t-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-a}+\int_{1}^{\infty}c^{t-1}\exp\left(-ac^{t-1}\right)dt=\left(1+\frac{1}{a\log c}\right)e^{-a}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\log c}\right)e^{-a}.

(19)

for all large

In this context, we are motivated to introduce

Definition of the DFMM.

At each generation
k>0
a new mutant with fitness
W_{k}=u_{n}(k)
appears.
In case

is ill defined, we set
W_{k}=1/(1-\beta)

The number of non-mutated descendants of

\displaystyle N_{k}^{\mathrm{D}}(t):=(1-\beta)^{t-k}W_{k}^{t-k}.

(58)

Notice that we added the superscript

Since no fluctuation is present, the limit behavior of the EFD for the DFMM becomes a problem of calculus.
In what follows, we will find a limit theorem of the EFD for the DFMM.
To this end, we begin with the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 7.8
.

Assume
f
is a positive continuous function that has a unique local maximum at
x_{c}

in a domain
[a-1,b+1]
, where
a,b
are integers and
x_{c}
need not be an integer. That is,
f(x)<f(y)
if
a-1\leq x<y\leq x_{c}
and

f(x)>f(y)
if
x_{c}\leq x<y\leq b+1
. Assume
a<x_{c}<b
.
Let

F(x):=\sum_{k=a}^{\lfloor x\rfloor}f(k).

Then, for any
a\leq x\leq b
,

\left|F(x)-\int_{a}^{x}f(y)dy\right|\leq 7f(x_{c}).

Proof.

Define

f_{-}(x):=f(\lfloor x\rfloor)
and
f_{+}(x)=f(\lceil x\rceil)
.
Then, for
a\leq x\leq b
,

F(x)=\int_{a}^{\lfloor x\rfloor+1}f_{-}(y)dy=\int_{a-1}^{\lfloor x\rfloor}f_{+}(y)dy.

Note that
f_{-}(x)\leq f(x)\leq f_{+}(x)
if
x<\lfloor x_{c}\rfloor
and
f_{-}(x)\geq f(x)\geq f_{+}(x)

if
x\geq\lceil x_{c}\rceil
.
We abbreviate
m_{c}:=\lfloor x_{c}\rfloor
and
m:=\lfloor x\rfloor
.
If
m<m_{c}
, then

F(x)=\int_{a-1}^{m}f_{+}(y)dy\geq\int_{a-1}^{a}f(y)dy+\int_{a}^{x}f(y)dy-\int_{m}^{x}f(y)dy

and

F(x)=\int_{a}^{m+1}f_{-}(y)dy\leq\int_{a}^{x}f(y)dy+\int_{x}^{m+1}f(y),

which gives

\left|F(x)-\int_{a}^{x}f(y)dy\right|\leq 2f(x_{c}).

For
m=m_{c}
, we use

\int_{a-1}^{a}f(y)dy+\int_{a}^{x}f(y)dy-\int_{m-1}^{x}f(y)dy\leq F(m-1)\leq\int_{a}^{x}f(y)dy-\int_{m}^{x}f(y)dy

and
F(x)=F(m-1)+f(m)
, to get

\left|F(x)-\int_{a}^{x}f(y)dy\right|\leq 4f(x_{c}).

If
m>m_{c}
, we consider

F(x)-F(x_{c})=\sum_{k=m_{c}+1}^{m}f(k)=\int_{m_{c}+1}^{m+1}f_{-}(y)dy=f(m_{c}+1)+\int_{m_{c}+1}^{m}f_{+}(y)dy,

which gives

\displaystyle F(x)-F(x_{c})

\displaystyle\leq f(x_{c})+\int_{x_{c}}^{x}f(y)dy-\int_{x_{c}}^{m_{c}+1}f(y)-\int_{m}^{x}f(y)dy,

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\widetilde{X}(t)}\left|\int_{z}^{z^{\prime}}H(x,t)dx\right|\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{-(1-\varepsilon_{0})/2}|z-z^{\prime}|,

for any
0<\varepsilon_{0}<1
,
we need to show that there is
\varepsilon_{0}
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{-(1-\varepsilon_{0})/2}|z-z^{\prime}|=0
.
First observe that

S^{\mathrm{D}}_{t}+\sigma^{\mathrm{D}}_{t}y=S_{t}^{\prime}+\sigma_{t}^{\prime}y^{\prime}
for

y^{\prime}:=\rho_{2}(t)+y\sqrt{\rho_{4}(t)}.

Assume
t
is so large that
|y^{\prime}|\leq 2\kappa_{t}^{-1/8}
.
By Taylor’s theorem, there is

y_{1}
such that

|y_{1}|\leq|y^{\prime}|\leq 2\kappa_{t}^{-1/8}
and

\displaystyle z^{\prime}

\displaystyle=u_{n}^{-1}(S_{t}^{\prime})+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{\prime}}{u_{n}^{\prime}(z_{1})}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=z+\left(\frac{\sigma_{t}^{\prime}}{u_{n}^{\prime}(z_{1})}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{t}}}\right)y+\frac{\sigma_{t}^{\prime}}{u_{n}^{\prime}(z_{1})}\left[\rho_{2}(t)+y(\sqrt{\rho_{4}}-1)\right],

where
z_{1}=u_{n}^{-1}(S_{t}^{\prime}+\sigma_{t}^{\prime}y_{1})
.
Using
z_{1}\sim x_{c}
,
u_{n}(x_{c})=\sigma_{t}^{\prime}\sqrt{\kappa_{t}}
, (
77
), and

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{-\varepsilon_{4}}/(\kappa_{t}x_{c})=0
for any
\varepsilon_{4}>0
, we have

|z^{\prime}-z|\leq\kappa_{t}^{-1/6}\leq t^{1/4}

for all sufficiently large
t
. Hence, if we choose
\varepsilon_{0}=1/8
,
we have the desired result.
Since
\rho_{4}(t)\sim 1
, the proof is completed. ∎

Remark 7.10
.

Since
u_{n}(t)/v_{n}(t)\sim(\log t)^{\delta_{n,1}/\alpha}
,
we have

\displaystyle S^{\mathrm{D}}_{t}\sim W_{t}

n\geq 2
, while

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}S^{\mathrm{D}}_{t}/W_{t}=0

for

.

In other words, when

, the mean fitness at generation

is hardly discernible from the largest fitness at the same generation. Another interesting observation is that if

or if

and

, then

which implies that the width of the traveling wave decreases to zero and
the EFD becomes a delta function
in the sense that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Phi(S^{\mathrm{D}}_{t}+y,t)=\begin{cases}0,&y<0,\\
1,&y>0.\end{cases}

This should be compared with the case of

and

in which the width of the traveling wave increases with generation.
For

and

, the behaviour of

depends on the slowly varying function

entering the tail function in
(

).

7.3
Semi-deterministic FMM and its EFD

Definition of the SFMM.

At each generation
k\geq 0
a new mutant with fitness

\theta_{k}:=(1-\beta)u_{n}(k)

appears and
(N_{k}(t)\colon t\geq k)
are mutually independent Galton-Watson processes with Poisson-distributed offspring with mean
\theta_{k}
for each
k
. In case
u_{n}(k)
is ill-defined, we set
\theta_{k}=1
.
By definition,
N_{k}(k)=1
and
N_{k}(\tau)=0
for
\tau<k

and no extinction is possible in the SFMM.
Since we will use Lemma
7.5
to prove Theorem
4
below,
we limit the definition of the SFMM to the case

n=1
or the case
n=2
and
\alpha<1
; see also Remark
7.2
.

We denote the total population size of the SFMM at generation
t
by

X^{\mathrm{S}}(t):=\sum_{k=0}^{t}N_{k}(t).

The EFD
\Psi_{s}(f,t)
of the SFMM and its mean fitness
S_{t}
are defined as

\displaystyle\Psi_{s}(f,t):=\frac{1}{X^{\mathrm{S}}(t)}\sum_{k=0}^{t}N_{k}(t)\Theta(f-u_{n}(k)),\quad S^{\mathrm{S}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{X^{\mathrm{S}}(t)}\sum_{k=0}^{t}u_{n}(k)N_{k}(t).

Since
N_{k}(t)
is the number of non-mutated descendants, we put
(1-\beta)
in the definition of the fitness of a new mutant
in the SFMM.
In a sense, the SFMM is closer to the FMM than the DFMM due to fluctuations of
N_{k}(t)
.
We redefine
u_{n}(k):=\theta_{k}/(1-\beta)
for convenience.
Now we prove that the EFD of the SFMM in the long time limit
becomes almost surely a Gaussian traveling wave just as the DFMM.

Theorem 4
.

For the SFMM with
n=1
or with
n=2
and
\alpha<1
,
almost surely

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{s}(v_{n}(t)+\mathfrak{s}_{n}(t)y,t)=\Upsilon(y),\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{S^{\mathrm{S}}_{t}}{v_{n}(t)}=1,

where

\displaystyle v_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-\delta_{n,1}/\alpha}\left(\log^{(n-1)}\left(t\right)\right)^{1/\alpha}L\left(\left(\log^{(n-1)}\left(t\right)\right)^{1/\alpha}\right),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{n}(t)}{\sqrt{\alpha t}}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\log^{(k)}\left(t\right)\right)^{-1/2}

have been introduced previously in (
66
) and (
67
).

Proof.

We define
J
and
E
as in Lemma
7.5
.
It is obvious that Lemma
7.5
is applicable to the SFMM.
Note that by definition
J
in (
52
) for the SFMM can be regarded as the sample space and, accordingly,
\mathbb{P}(E)=1
.
For any
0<\varepsilon<1/2
and for any outcome
\omega\in E
, there exists
\tau_{1}
such that

(1-\varepsilon)\theta_{k}^{t-k}\leq N_{k}(t)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\theta_{k}^{t-k}

for all
t\geq k\geq\tau_{1}
.
Notice that
\tau_{1}
can vary from outcome to outcome.
Let

X^{\mathrm{S}}(t,\tau_{1}):=\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{1}}N_{k}(t),\quad X^{\mathrm{D}}(t):=\sum_{k=0}^{t}\theta_{k}^{t-k},\quad X^{\mathrm{D}}(t,\tau_{1}):=\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{1}}\theta_{k}^{t-k}.

Then, for
t\geq\tau_{1}
, we have

(1-\varepsilon)\left(X^{\mathrm{D}}(t)-X^{\mathrm{D}}(t,\tau_{1})\right)+X^{\mathrm{S}}(t,\tau_{1})\leq X^{\mathrm{S}}(t)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left(X^{\mathrm{D}}(t)-X^{\mathrm{D}}(t,\tau_{1})\right)+X^{\mathrm{S}}(t,\tau_{1}).

Since
X^{\mathrm{D}}_{s}(t,\tau_{1})
and
X^{\mathrm{D}}(t,\tau_{1})
grow at most exponentially and

X^{\mathrm{D}}(t)
grows super-exponentially,
we have almost surely

\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{X^{\mathrm{S}}(t)}{X^{\mathrm{D}}(t)}\geq 1-\varepsilon,\quad\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{X^{\mathrm{S}}(t)}{X^{\mathrm{D}}(t)}\leq 1+\varepsilon.

Hence there is almost surely
\tau_{2}
such that

(1-2\varepsilon)X^{\mathrm{D}}(t)\leq X^{\mathrm{S}}(t)\leq(1+2\varepsilon)X^{\mathrm{D}}(t)
for all
t\geq\tau_{2}
.

Now set
\tau=\max\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\}
and assume
t>\tau
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{s}(f,t)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1+2\varepsilon}\frac{1}{X^{\mathrm{D}}(t)}\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{1}}N_{k}(t)\Theta(f-u_{n}(k))+\frac{1}{1+2\varepsilon}\frac{1}{X^{\mathrm{D}}(t)}\sum_{k=\tau_{1}+1}^{t}N_{k}(t)\Theta(f-u_{n}(k))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1+2\varepsilon}\frac{1}{X^{\mathrm{D}}(t)}\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{1}}N_{k}(t)\Theta(f-u_{n}(k))+\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+2\varepsilon}\frac{1}{X^{\mathrm{D}}(t)}\sum_{k=\tau_{1}+1}^{t}\theta_{k}^{t-k}\Theta(f-u_{n}(k)).

Hence by Lemma
7.12
, we conclude

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{s}(v_{n}(t)+\mathfrak{s}_{n}(t)y,t)\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+2\varepsilon}\Upsilon(y).

By the same token, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{s}(v_{n}(t)+\mathfrak{s}_{n}(t)y,t)\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-2\varepsilon}\Upsilon(y).

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we proved the first part of the theorem.

Let

S^{\mathrm{D}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{X^{\mathrm{D}}(t)}\sum_{k=0}^{t}u_{n}(k)\theta_{k}^{t-k}.

By Lemma
7.12
, we have
S^{\mathrm{D}}_{t}\sim v_{n}(t)
.
Inspecting the above proof, we can conclude that
for any
0<\varepsilon<1/2
and for any outcome
\omega\in E
, there is
\tau
such that

(1-\varepsilon)S^{\mathrm{D}}_{t}\leq S^{\mathrm{S}}_{t}\leq(1+\varepsilon)S^{\mathrm{D}}_{t},

for all
t\geq\tau
.
Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary,
the proof is completed.
∎

Numerical study for the MMM with

Since the largest fitness is expected to dominate the evolution of the

.
For the MMM, however, we only present some numerical results, which

8
Concluding remarks

We provided strong analytical and numerical evidence for the emergence of a travelling wave for the branching process with selection and mutation for unbounded fitness distributions of Gumbel type. For type I tail functions with tail index
n=1
, or in other words stretched exponential fitness distributions, we show that if the tail parameter satisfies
\alpha>2
, the standard deviation of the traveling Gaussian
wave decreases and eventually the EFD becomes highly peaked like a delta function.
Traveling wave solutions of Gaussian form were found previously in a study of the deterministic (infinite population) limit of the model, which amounts to solving the recursion (
79
) with
\tilde{p}_{k}=p_{k}
, see
[
15
]
. The expressions for the mean and variance of the EFD obtained in
[
15
]
for a particular type I tail function match Eqs. (
66
) and (
67
), see also
[
16
]
.

We conjecture a similar behaviour for bounded fitness distributions of Gumbel type in the condensation case discussed in Section 1. In that case the Gaussian wave is expected to travel to the essential supremum of the fitness distribution, while its standard deviation goes to zero faster than the distance of its mean to the essential supremum.
For bounded fitness distributions of Weibull type we conjecture, as in Ref.
[
9
]
for a branching model in continuous time, that the condensate emerges in the shape of a Gamma distribution. The conjecture is justified by the rigorous analysis of the deterministic model in Ref.
[
17
]
.

In our model every individual has a Poisson number of offspring with mean given by its fitness. It is natural to conjecture that results like emergence of the travelling wave, doubly exponential growth rates or condensation also hold for other distributions with the same mean and not too large variance. Verifying this universality conjecture rigorously would be an interesting future project.

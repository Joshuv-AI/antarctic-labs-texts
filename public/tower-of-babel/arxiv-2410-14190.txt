Definition 1

Let

\displaystyle=q+2q^{2}+5q^{3}+8q^{4}+15q^{5}+26q^{6}+43q^{7}+68q^{8}+109q^{9}+\cdots

\displaystyle C_{3}(q)

\displaystyle=q+2q^{2}+5q^{3}+8q^{4}+14q^{5}+24q^{6}+39q^{7}+60q^{8}+95q^{9}+\cdots.

{}_{2}\phi_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c};q\mathchar 59\relax\mkern 6.0muz\biggr]=\frac{(b,az;q)_{\infty}}{(c,z;q)_{\infty}}{}_{2}\phi_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{c/b,z}{az};q\mathchar 59\relax\mkern 6.0mub\biggr].

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(\alpha;q)_{n}\tau^{n}}{(\beta;q)_{n}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(\alpha;q)_{n}(q\alpha\tau/\beta;q)_{n}\beta^{n}\tau^{n}q^{n^{2}-n}(1-\alpha\tau q^{2n})}{(\beta;q)_{n}(\tau;q)_{n+1}}.

We shall also make use of Watson’s transformation

{}_{8}\phi_{7}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{a, qa^{1/2}, -qa^{1/2}, b, c, d, e, f}{a^{1/2}, -a^{1/2}, aq/b, aq/c, aq/d, aq/e, aq/f};q\mathchar 59\relax\mkern 6.0mua^{2}q^{2}/(bcdef)\biggr]=

3.
Proof of Theorem
1

We need two lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

There holds

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{2n-1}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}d(2n-1)q^{n}.

Proof.

We first note the basic fact

\sum_{n\geq 1}d(n)q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}d(2n-1)q^{2n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big(\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}-\frac{(-q)^{n}}{1-(-q)^{n}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big(\frac{q^{2n-1}}{1-q^{2n-1}}+\frac{q^{2n-1}}{1+q^{2n-1}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{2q^{2n-1}}{1-q^{4n-2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2n-1}}{1-q^{4n-2}}.

Now multiply both sides by
q
and then replace
q^{2}
by
q
to get the desired result.
∎

For a power series
A(q):=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}q^{n}

and a nonnegative integer
N
, we let
[q^{N}]A(q):=a_{n}
.
Our next lemma is in terms of the set
\mathbb{T}
given by

\mathbb{T}=\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\ :\ n=k(k+1)\ \text{for some $k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$}\}.

Lemma 2
.

For any nonnegative integer
n
, we have

\displaystyle[q^{n}]\frac{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv[q^{n}]\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)

\displaystyle\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)\ \text{if and only if $n\in\mathbb{T}$.}

Proof.

The first congruence in (
2
) follows from the following basic congruence

(1-q)^{2}\equiv(1+q)^{2}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4).

(3.1)

For the second congruence, recall first the Gauss formula
[
1
, (2.2.13)]

\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}.

(3.2)

Then we get

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n\geq 0}\big(q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\big)^{2}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n(n+1)}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2),

implying the desired congruence.
∎

First proof of Theorem
1
.

Note that

\frac{q^{n^{2}+n}(1-q^{2n+1})}{1+q^{2n+1}}=q^{n^{2}+n}\Big(1-\frac{2q^{2n+1}}{1+q^{2n+1}}\Big),

which combined with Theorem
4
yields

\displaystyle C_{1}(q)

\displaystyle=\Big(2q\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n^{2}+n}\bigl(1-q^{2n+1}\bigr)}{1+q^{2n+1}}\Big)\frac{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}-q\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-q)^{n}}{1+q^{2n+1}}

\displaystyle=\left(2q\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n^{2}+n}\Big(1-2\frac{q^{2n+1}}{1+q^{2n+1}}\Big)\right)\frac{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}-q\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-q)^{n}}{1+q^{2n+1}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2q\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n^{2}+n}-q\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-q)^{n}}{1+q^{2n+1}}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4).

(3.3)

Note that by Lemma
2
, if
n\not\in\mathbb{T}
, then

[q^{n}]\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2).

(3.4)

On the other hand, if
n\in\mathbb{T}
, say
n=k(k+1)
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then

[q^{n}]\Big(\sum_{m\geq 0}q^{m^{2}+m+1}\Big)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)

(3.5)

because the equation
k^{2}+k=m^{2}+m+1
has no solutions in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Then appealing to the identities (
3.3
)-(
3.5
), we achieve

\displaystyle C_{1}(q)

\displaystyle\equiv-q\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-q)^{n}}{1+q^{2n+1}}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-q)^{n}}{1+q^{2n-1}}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4).

Equivalently,

C_{1}(-q)\equiv-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{2n-1}}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4).

(3.6)

Besides, from (
1.1
) and (
3.1
),
we derive

\displaystyle C_{1}(-q)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n+1}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(-q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv-\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n+1}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)

\displaystyle=-C_{1}(q)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4).

(3.7)

Now combine (
3.7
) and (
3.6
) with Lemma
1
to obtain

\displaystyle C_{1}(q)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{2n-1}}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}d(2n-1)q^{n},

which is the desired result.

Second proof of Theorem
1
.
By virtue of (
3.1
), (
2.1
), and Lemma
1
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}C(1,n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n+1}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n+1}(q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)

\displaystyle=q\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{}_{2}\phi_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{q,q}{q^{2}};q^{2}\mathchar 59\relax\mkern 6.0muq^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle=q\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\frac{(q,q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{}_{2}\phi_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{q,q^2}{q^{3}};q^{2}\mathchar 59\relax\mkern 6.0muq\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{1-q}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n}(q;q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{3};q^{2})_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n+1}(q;q^{2})_{n}}{(q;q^{2})_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{2n-1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}d(2n-1)q^{n},

as desired.

4.
Proofs of Corollaries
1
-
3

We start with a lemma.

Lemma 3
.

Let
N
be a positive odd integer with prime factorisation

N=\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}^{e_{i}},

where
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
are distinct odd primes and
e_{i}\geq 1
. Let
t
be the number of indices
i
for which
e_{i}
is odd. Then

d(N)\equiv\begin{cases}1\ \text{or}\ 3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4),&t=0,\\
2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4),&t=1,\\
0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4),&t\geq 2.\end{cases}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle d(N)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(e_{i}+1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{e_{i}\ \text{odd}}(e_{i}+1)\prod_{e_{j}\ \text{even}}(e_{i}+1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{e_{i}\ \text{odd}}(2a_{i}+2)\prod_{e_{j}\ \text{even}}(2b_{j}+1)

\displaystyle=2^{t}M,

where
M
is an odd integer. If
t=0
, then
d(N)=M
is odd, hence
d(N)\equiv 1
or
3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
. If
t=1
, then
d(N)=2M\equiv 2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
, and if
t\geq 2
, then
4\mid d(N)
, so
d(N)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
. This confirms the desired congruences.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1
.

By Theorem
1
we have

C(1,n)\equiv d(2n-1)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)

for all positive integers
n
. In particular,
C(1,n)
is odd if and only if
d(2n-1)
is odd. Writing

2n-1=\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}^{e_{i}}

as in Lemma
3
, we see that
d(2n-1)
is odd if and only if
t=0
, i.e., all exponents
e_{i}
are even. This is equivalent to
2n-1
being a perfect square.

Since
2n-1
is odd, its square root
s
must be odd, say
s=2k+1
with
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then

2n-1=(2k+1)^{2}=4k^{2}+4k+1,

so
n=2k(k+1)+1
, as desired.

Proof of Corollary
2
.

Again by Theorem
1
we have
C(1,n)\equiv d(2n-1)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
. Writing
2n-1
as in the statement of the corollary and applying Lemma
3
with
N=2n-1
gives the three cases for
C(1,n)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
.

For the final assertion, observe that
C(1,n)\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
if and only if
t\leq 1
. If
t=0
, then all exponents
e_{i}
are even and
2n-1
is a square. If
t=1
, say
e_{1}
is odd and
e_{i}
is even for
i\geq 2
, then we may write

2n-1=p_{1}^{2a+1}\prod_{i=2}^{r}p_{i}^{2b_{i}}=p_{1}\Big(p_{1}^{a}\prod_{i=2}^{r}p_{i}^{b_{i}}\Big)^{2},

so
2n-1
is a prime times a square. This completes the proof.

Proof of Corollary
3
.

By Theorem
1
we have

C(1,9n+8)\equiv d(18n+15)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)

for all
n\geq 0
. Since
18n+15=3(6n+5)
, the prime
3
occurs to an odd exponent in the factorisation of
18n+15
.

We claim that
6n+5
is never a perfect square. Indeed, if
6n+5=s^{2}
for some integer
s
, then reducing modulo

(y,z)=(q,-1)
and appealing to (
2.6
), we find

\displaystyle C_{2}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n+1}(-q^{2n+4};q^{2})_{\infty}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q^{2},-q^{4};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n+1}(q,q;q^{2})_{n}}{(-q^{2},-q^{4};q^{2})_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2q(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n}(q,q;q^{2})_{n}}{(-1,-q^{2};q^{2})_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2q(-q^{2};q^{2})^{2}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{(1+q)^{2}}-\frac{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(1+q)^{2}(-1,-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2q(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(1+q)^{2}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}-\frac{q}{(1+q)^{2}}.

This competes the proof.

9.
Proof of Theorem
6

Noting that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n+1}(-q^{2n+6},-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+3},q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n+1}(1-q^{2n+1})(-q^{2n+6},-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}

\displaystyle=C_{3}(q)-\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{4n+2}(-q^{2n+6},-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}},

we get

C_{3}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n+1}(-q^{2n+6},-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+3},q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}}+\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{4n+2}(-q^{2n+6},-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}.

(9.1)

We will now evaluate the two sums on the right hand-side of (
9.1
).
As for the first sum, with the help of (
2.7
) applied to
q\to q^{2}
and
(y,z)=(q,-q^{-2})
and an appeal to (
2.6
), we derive

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{2n+1}\frac{(-q^{2n+2},-q^{2n+6};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1},q^{2n+3};q^{2})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q(-q^{2},-q^{6};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q,q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n}(q,q^{3};q^{2})_{n}}{(-q^{2},-q^{6};q^{2})_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(1+q^{4})q(-q^{2},-q^{6};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q,q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2n-2}(q,q^{3};q^{2})_{n-1}}{(-1,-q^{4};q^{2})_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2q^{-1}(-q^{2},-q^{4};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q,q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}}\frac{1}{q+q^{-1}+q^{2}+q^{-2}}\Big(1-\frac{(q,q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(-1,-q^{4};q^{2})_{\infty}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2q(-q^{2},-q^{4};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(1+q)(1+q^{3})(q,q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}}-\frac{q}{(1+q)(1+q^{3})}

\displaystyle=\frac{2q(1-q)(-q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}{(1+q)(1+q^{2})(1+q^{3})(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}-\frac{q}{(1+q)(1+q^{3})}

\displaystyle=\frac{2q(1-q)(-q;q)_{\infty}(-q^{4};q)_{\infty}}{(q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}^{2}}-\frac{q}{(1+q)(1+q^{3})}

(9.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{2q(-q^{2};q)_{\infty}(-q^{4};q)_{\infty}}{(q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}(q^{6};q^{4})_{\infty}}-\frac{q}{(1+q)(1+q^{3})}

(9.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{2q}{(1+q)(1+q^{3})}\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}(-q^{3};q)_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}(q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}(-q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}}-\frac{q}{(1+q)(1+q^{3})}

\displaystyle=\frac{2q}{(1+q)(1+q^{3})}\frac{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(-q^{4};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}(q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}}-\frac{q}{(1+q)(1+q^{3})}.

(9.4)

As for the second sum,
using (
2.8
) with
q\to q^{2}
and
(y,z)=(q,-q^{-2})
, making an appeal to (
2.6
), and simplifying, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{4n+2}\frac{(-q^{2n+2},-q^{2n+6};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1},q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q^{2},-q^{6};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q,q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{4n+2}(q,q;q^{2})_{n}}{(-q^{2},-q^{6};q^{2})_{n}}

\displaystyle=2q^{2}(1+q^{4})\frac{(-q^{2},-q^{6};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{4n}(q,q;q^{2})_{n}}{(-1,-q^{4};q^{2})_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=2q^{2}(1+q^{4})\frac{(-q^{2},-q^{6};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\Big(\frac{(1-q^{2})}{(1+q)^{2}(1+q^{3})^{2}}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(-1,-q^{4};q^{2})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(1-q^{2})(1+q^{-2})}{-q^{-2}(1+q)^{2}(1+q^{3})^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1+q^{3})^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2q^{3}(2+q+q^{3})}{(1+q)^{2}(1+q^{3})^{2}}\frac{(-q^{2},-q^{4};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}+\frac{q^{2}(1-q)}{(1+q)(1+q^{3})^{2}}.

(9.5)

Then we achieve the desired result by adding (
9.4
) and (
9.5
), and using (
9.1
).

10.
Remarks and conjectures

In this section we present several conjectures on Ramanujan-type congruences modulo
4
(and higher powers of
2
)
for the sequences
C(k,n)
and for the limiting sequence
c(n)
.

Remark
1
.

Write
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
with
\Im(\tau)>0
and let
\eta(\tau)
denote the Dedekind eta-function given by

\eta(\tau)=q^{1/24}(q;q)_{\infty}=q^{1/24}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n}).

Using

(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}=\prod_{n\geq 1}(1+q^{2n})=\frac{(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}

and
(q;q)_{\infty}=(q;q^{2})_{\infty}(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}
, we obtain

\frac{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}=\frac{(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}=q^{-1/4}\,\frac{\eta(4\tau)^{2}}{\eta(\tau)^{2}}.

In particular, up to the elementary factor
q^{-1/4}
this common factor is an eta-quotient of weight
0
on
\Gamma_{0}(4)
. Thus suitable linear combinations of
C_{1}(q)
,
C_{2}(q)
, and
C_{3}(q)
can be
expressed as modular functions on
\Gamma_{0}(4)
with integral Fourier coefficients, see Ono
[
20
]
for general background.

1.
As for
C(1,n)
, Corollary
3
gives the Ramanujan-type congruence

C(1,9n+8)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)

for all
n\geq 0
. Together with Corollary
2
, this describes the possible residue classes of

C(1,n)
modulo
4
in terms of the prime factorisation of
2n-1
.

2.
Let

C(q)=\lim_{k\to\infty}C_{k}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n+1}(-q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}

and let
C(q):=\sum_{n\geq 0}c(n)q^{n}
.
Extensive computations of the coefficients
c(n)
suggest that
c(8n+4)
is always divisible by
4
, whereas
c(8n+6)

appears to be divisible by
8
(and hence also by
4
). This leads to the following Ramanujan-type conjectures for the limiting sequence
c(n)
.

Conjecture 1
.

For any nonnegative integer
n
, we have

c(8n+4)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4).

Conjecture 2
.

For any nonnegative integer
n
, we have

c(8n+6)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 8).

These congruences are similar to Ramanujan’s classical congruences for the ordinary partition function, such as

p(5n+4)\equiv 0\pmod{5},\qquad p(7n+5)\equiv 0\pmod{7},

but here they concern the limiting two-color partition sequence
c(n)
. It would be interesting to obtain a direct modular-forms proof of Conjectures
1
and
2
.

It is worth mentioning that
c(8n+4)
is not always divisible by
8
. For instance, for
n=1
we have
8n+4=12
and

c(12)=284\equiv 4\pmod{8},

so the two conjectures above appear to be genuinely distinct.

3.
Note that we may write

C(q)=\frac{q(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n}(q;q^{2})_{n}^{2}}{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}=A(q)S(q),

where

\displaystyle A(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{q(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}a(n)q^{n},

\displaystyle S(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n}(q;q^{2})_{n}^{2}}{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}s(n)q^{n}.

Then one can show that for any
n\geq 0
,

a(4n)\equiv a(4n+3)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)

As for
S(q)
, we have the following conjecture.

Conjecture 3
.

For any nonnegative integer
n
, we have

s(4n+1)=0.

One can show that

S(q)=\frac{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1+q^{2n+1}}{1-q^{2n+1}}q^{3n^{2}+3n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}(-1)^{j}q^{-2j^{2}}.

(10.1)

Remark
2
.

The double sum on the right-hand side of (
10.1
) can be seen as a Hecke-type series where the exponent
on q is an indefinite quadratic form

Q(n,j)=3n^{2}+3n-2j^{2}.

It is therefore natural to expect modular or mock-modular behaviour of
S(q)
, and in particular that the congruences in Conjectures
1
–
4
might be dealt with using the arithmetic of the quadratic form
Q
.

We close with the following conjecture about the even parts of
(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}S(q)
.

Conjecture 4
.

For any nonnegative integer
N
, we have

\displaystyle[q^{2N}]\Big((q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}S(q)\Big)

\displaystyle=[q^{2N}]\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{6n^{2}+4n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}(-1)^{j}q^{-2j^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}q^{6n^{2}-4n}\sum_{j=-n+1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}q^{-2j^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=[q^{2N}]\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{6n^{2}+4n}(1+q^{4n+2})\sum_{j=-n}^{n}(-1)^{j}q^{-2j^{2}}\Big).

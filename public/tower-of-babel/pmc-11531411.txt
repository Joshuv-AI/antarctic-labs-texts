The causal relationship between anti-CD20 antibodies and endometrial cancer: a Mendelian randomization study
Authors: Jinqiu Su, Zhuang Li, Jianli Wang, Nairong Liu, Liyi Bao, Jiayuan Du, Yan Li, Yaojie Yu, He Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo determine the effects of anti-B cell surface marker CD20 antibody on the occurrence and progression of endometrial cancer to provide insights into clinical treatment in the future.MethodsWe performed a two-sample Mendelian randomization analysis (MR) to analyze the causal relationship between EC and immune cell markers. The genome-wide association study (GWAS) data of MS4A1 single nucleotide polymorphism encoding gene of CD20 were collected, and a drug target MR analysis was performed to detect the causal relationship between anti-CD20 antibody and the risk of EC. The risk of follicular lymphoma was used as a positive control, and EC was used as the outcome.ResultsEC exerted an effect on 28 immunophenotypes, predominantly on CD20. CD20 in immunoglobulin D+ CD38− B cells, memory B cells, and unsw memory B cells increased after EC onset. In addition, we detected one immunophenotype that exerted a dangerous effect on EC, i.e., CD3/TD CD4+ (mature stage of the T cell group). Simultaneously, CD20 could be used as both a risk factor and a protective factor for EC (P > 0.05). Anti-CD20 antibodies significantly reduced the risk of follicular lymphoma, including two GWAS pooled datasets. In addition, anti-CD20 antibodies exerted a protective effect on EC in two GWAS pooled datasets (P > 0.05).ConclusionOur study confirms the close relationship between CD20 and EC. Anti-CD20 antibodies may exert a protective effect on EC and reduce the risk of EC morbidity, providing a reference for future clinical diagnosis and treatment. However, further clinical verification of our results is warranted.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01491-2.

Introduction

Endometrial cancer (EC) refers to a group of malignant tumors originating from the endometrial epithelium, and its incidence in China is second only to cervical cancer. The prevalence of EC has been increasing, particularly in young people [1]. Treatment options for EC consist of surgery in the early stages, followed by adjuvant therapy based on the presence of high-risk factors that affect the prognosis. Advanced-stage EC is treated by a combination of surgery-based, chemoradiotherapy, and immunotargeted therapy. Immunotherapy drugs are predominantly used with programmed death-1 (PD-1)/PD-ligand 1 inhibitors; however, their overall response rates range from 13.5 to 52% [2]. In addition, L1CAM and other molecular factors are frequently mutated in endometrial cancer [3, 4]. So L1CAM may be a potential target.

The stroma of EC cells consists of numerous immune cells, including tumor-associated macrophages and helper T cells (Th cells). The tumor microenvironment refers to the local internal environment composed of immune cells and mesenchymal cells, which can infiltrate the tumor tissues and cells. Furthermore, the tumor microenvironment consists of immune cells and inflammatory cells, which play a decisive role in tumor immunity [5]. The immune-tumor interaction can not only promote tumor genesis and progression but also exert anti-tumor effects, which may demonstrate a causal relationship with and therapeutic significance for EC occurrence and development.

Immune cells and their cytokines can influence EC progression [6]. The immune system can negatively regulate tumor cells, such as regulatory T cells (Treg cells), which can disrupt the anti-tumor immune response and induce immune tolerance or the escape of tumor cells, thereby promoting the proliferation and growth of tumor cells [7]. By contrast, the immune system prevents tumor formation and eliminates tumors. For example, tumor infiltration of the lymphoid tissue can exert anti-tumor effects. The higher the number of infiltrated lymphocytes, the longer the survival time of patients with cancer [8]. The human immune system exerts dual effects on tumors, such as promoting and inhibiting tumors. Analyzing the regulatory mechanism underlying the immune cells and tumor cells in patients with EC is conducive to the prevention and treatment of EC.

Mendelian randomization (MR) is an analytical method principally used for epidemiological and etiological inference. MR is based on the law of Mendelian independent distribution. It is essential to determine whether the causal relationship of MR is reasonable [9]. Previous observational studies have identified several associations between immune cell traits and EC, supporting the hypothesis of their correlation [10]. In this study, we performed an MR analysis to determine the causal relationship between immune cell characteristics and EC, while we performed MR of the drug targets based on the immune cell characteristics. An MR analysis of drug targets uses genetic variants that mimic the pharmacological inhibition of pharmacogenetic targets as the instrumental variables. Regression analysis can elucidate the effects of long-term drug use and enhance causal inferences about the potential effects of these pharmacogenetic targets on EC [11]. In this study, we collected statistics at the summary level of a genome-wide association study (GWAS) to investigate the causal relationship between genetically predicted cluster of differentiation 20 (CD20) antibodies and EC.

Method

Study design

We evaluated the causal relationship between 731 immune cell signatures (seven groups) and EC based on a two-sample MR analysis. MR uses genetic variation to represent the risk factors. Therefore, an effective instrumental variable (IV) in a causal inference must meet three key assumptions as follows: (1) genetic variation is directly related to the exposure; (2) genetic variation is not associated with the possible confounders between the exposure and outcomes; and (3) genetic variation does not affect the outcomes through pathways other than the exposure.

GWAS data sources

In this study, the genome-wide association study (GWAS) of EC (ebi-a-GCST90018838) and CD20 (ebi-a-GCST90001761) were obtained from the IEU GWAS database (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/) of the University of Bristol, UK. These datasets have been created from European populations. The GWAS for each immune trait was collected at an identical time. The aggregate statistics are available publicly from the GWAS catalog (accession numbers GCST0001391 to GCST0002121) [10]. We used two diseases as results of the MR analysis of the drug target. In addition to EC, we selected follicular lymphoma (FL) as the positive control dataset. This is because anti-CD20 antibodies are widely used in FL treatment. The lymphoma dataset originates from the IEU GWAS database. In addition, we collected the aggregate GWAS dataset of ECs as the primary outcome.

Selection of instrumental variable

The IV significance level for each immune trait was set at 1 × 105 [12]. Based on the linkage disequilibrium reference panel, we used the 1000-human genome project for aggregation (R2 < 0.001 at a 1000-kb distance). Concerning the relatively modest GWAS for immune cells, we applied a cut-off P-value of 1 × 105 and a more relaxed aggregation threshold (R2 < 0.1 within 500 kb distance). Through the National Center for Biotechnology Information database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), we identified MS4A1 as the gene encoding CD20. Furthermore, we obtained the instrumental variable that could target MS4A1 to affect CD20. Instrumental variables were selected for the MS4A1 locus ± single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) present within locus ± 1 and associated with CD20 levels. To avoid the influence of a strong linkage imbalance on the results, we set a linkage imbalance threshold (r2 < 0.3). E, two significant SNPs of MS4A1 were retained.

Data analysis

All analyses were performed in the R 4.3.2 software (www.Rproject.org) to evaluate the causal relationship between 731 immunophenotypes and EC. MR-Egger, weighted median, inverse variance-weighted (IVW), simple mode, and weighted mode were used for the analysis, with IVW being the predominantly used method [13]. We assessed heterogeneity using the MR-Egger and IVW methods. A common approach (i.e., MR-Egger) is used to eliminate the effects of horizontal pleiotropy, which implies horizontal multiplicity in the case of a significant intercept term [14]. The heterogeneity of genetic tools was assessed by IVW, and P > 0.05 indicated insignificant heterogeneity. The level of pleiotropy of the genetic tools was assessed using the MR-Egger regression equation, and P > 0.05 indicated no horizontal pleiotropy [15]. The pooled data of FL GWAS were selected as the positive control to verify the validity of the instrumental variables. We reconciled the instrumental variables related to drug targeting with the outcome dataset because MS4A1 retained only two significant SNPs. Moreover, we analyzed only the predominantly used IVW.

Results

Effects of EC occurrence on immunophenotype

We identified 28 suggestive immunophenotypes, namely seven in the B cell group, three each in the DC and mature T cell groups, one in the monocyte group, four each in the myeloid cell and TBNK (T, B, NK lymphocyte subsets) groups, and six in the Treg group (Supplementary Table 1). In the IVW method, EC increased the number of phenotypic immune molecules, predominantly CD20, including CD20 levels in the immunoglobulin D+ CD38− B cells, memory B cells, and unsw mem B cells (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1, Supplementary Tables 1 and 2). The results of the sensitivity analysis demonstrated the robustness of the observed causal relationship (Supplementary Tables 2, 3, and 4 and Supplementary Figure). Pathogenic effects of immunophenotype on EC

In a reverse MR study of 28 suggestive immunophenotypes, we detected one immunophenotype as an EC risk factor as follows: CD 3/TD CD4+ (mature stage of the T cell group). Using the IVW method, the odds ratio (OR) of CD 3/TD CD4+ for the risk of EC was 1.0835 (P = 0.0312) (Fig. 2 and Supplementary Table 6). Simultaneously, we performed a reverse MR study of three immunophenotypes related to CD20. Causal relationship between anti-CD20 antibody and EC

In the IVW method, the six GWAS pooled datasets suggested that anti-CD20 antibodies exerted a protective effect against EC (P > 0.05) ((Fig. 3 and Supplementary Table 6). For the sensitivity analysis, we used the Cochrane Q regression equation to evaluate the level of heterogeneity. The level of pleiotropy could not be evaluated because of insufficient SNPs (Supplementary Table 8).

Discussion

Relationship between immunization and EC progression

Immunity is closely related to the occurrence and development of EC [16]; however, the mechanism underlying this association is still unclear. The interstitium of EC is characterized by immune cell infiltration, primarily consisting of Th cells and B cells, which play a key role in the occurrence and development of EC. Th1/Th2 cells play an important role in immune responses, such as immune tolerance and immune response. Th1 cells mediate hypersensitivity and cellular immunity to exert anti-tumor effects. By contrast, Th2 cells promote B lymphocyte production and exert anti-tumor effects along with humoral immune responses. In addition, Th2 cells can promote the depolarization of macrophages, cause tumor immune tolerance, and promote tumor angiogenesis and tumor progression. The tissue-infiltrating lymphocytes of polymerase ε hypermutant tumors have demonstrated the best prognosis in The Cancer Genome Atlas classification of EC. They are characterized by a high proportion of Programmed Cell Death Protein 1 expression and CD8 T cell infiltration, in addition to an increased response of CD4 T cells [17]. These studies highlight the importance of the immune system in EC pathophysiology.

Relationship of CD20 with EC progression and immunity

Ectopic lymphoid tissue, also termed tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS), is an ectopic lymphoid organ that forms in non-lymphoid tissues in response to chronic inflammation; it predominantly consists of B lymphocytes [18]. TLS is structurally characterized by numerous CD20-expressing positive B lymphocyte clusters in the inflammatory tissues [19]. B cells in the TLS exert effective anti-tumor immune responses in several solid tumors, such as breast cancer, lung cancer, melanoma, and EC [20–22]. However, these cells play an equally important role in the anti-tumor immune evasion process of other cancers, such as gastric cancer [23]. T cells and B cells may exert dual effects on promoting and inhibiting EC; however, the application of immunotherapy in EC is limited to T cell-targeted drugs. Moreover, B cell-targeted drugs are expected to become a potential treatment option for EC.

Application status of anti-CD20 antibody and the prospect of application in EC

Researchers have conducted several studies on monoclonal antibody drugs against CD20, a leukocyte differentiation antigen on the surface of B cells. CD20 is a first-line diagnostic marker for B-cell lymphoma. It functions as a specific marker on the surface of B cells [24]. CD20 is an important target for monoclonal antibodies in the treatment of B-cell malignancies and certain autoimmune diseases [25]. Rituximab, the first CD20 monoclonal antibody that has been approved for cancer, is a human/mouse chimeric anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody. It has demonstrated a favorable and unambiguous safety profile in patients with CD20+ lymphoid malignancies [26]. Since its approval, intravenous rituximab has revolutionized the treatment of B-cell malignancies and has become one of the standard protocols for the treatment of FL, diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, and chronic lymphocytic leukemia. In such patients, rituximab prolongs not only the time to disease progression but also the overall survival [27]. Anti-CD20 antibodies are effective in patients with melanoma [28], consistent with the inhibition of MS4A1, the gene encoding CD20. Furthermore, the anti-CD20 antibody significantly reduces the risk of FL.

Conclusions

In summary, we conducted an MR study of the MS4A1 gene in patients with EC. The anti-CD20 antibody protected EC in six GWAS pooled datasets, despite statistical insignificance. Therefore, researchers should elucidate the feasibility of anti-CD20 antibodies as B-cell targets in the future. Moreover, they should explore whether anti-CD20 antibodies can be combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors for tumor treatment.

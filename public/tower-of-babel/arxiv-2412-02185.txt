Abstract
. Using some estimates in [J. Funct. Anal. 278 (2020), Article No. 108401], we completely characterized the boundedness and compactness of the Stević-Sharma type operators with different weights and different composition symbols between Bergman spaces induced by two-sides doubling weights. Keywords : Stević-Sharma type operator; differentiation composition operator; Bergman space; doubling weight.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
30H20, 47B10, 47B35

1.
Introduction

Let
H(\mathbb{D})
be the space of all analytic functions defined in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
. For a nonnegative function
\omega\in L^{1}([0,1])
, its extension to
\mathbb{D}
, given by
\omega(z)=\omega(|z|)
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
, is known as a radial weight. The collection of doubling weights, represented by
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
, includes all radial weights
\omega
that satisfy (see
[
13
]
)

\hat{\omega}(r)\leq C\hat{\omega}\left(\frac{r+1}{2}\right),\,\,\,0\leq r<1

for a constant
C=C(\omega)\geq 1
. Here
\hat{\omega}(z)=\int_{|z|}^{1}\omega(t)dt
.
Additionally, if
\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
and satisfies the inequality

\hat{\omega}(r)\geq C\hat{\omega}\left(1-\frac{1-r}{K}\right),\quad 0\leq r<1,

for some constants
K=K(\omega)>1
and
C=C(\omega)>1
, we refer to
\omega
as a two-sided doubling weight and denote it by
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, the Carleson square at
\lambda
is defined as

S(\lambda)=\left\{re^{i\theta}:|\lambda|\leq r<1,\,|\text{Arg }\lambda-\theta|<\frac{1-|\lambda|}{2}\right\}.

For a radial weight
\omega
, we define
\omega(S(\lambda))=\int_{S(\lambda)}\omega(z)\,dA(z)
. It is evident that
\omega(S(\lambda))\approx(1-|\lambda|)\hat{\omega}(\lambda)
. For further properties of doubling weights, refer to
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
and the references therein.

For
0<p<\infty
and a given
\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
, the Bergman space
A_{\omega}^{p}
generated by a doubling weight
\omega
comprises all functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
that satisfy

\|f\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\omega(z)\,dA(z)<\infty,

where
dA
represents the normalized area measure on
\mathbb{D}
. We denote
A_{\alpha}^{p}
as the standard weighted Bergman space associated with the radial weight
\omega(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}
for
-1<\alpha<\infty
. In this paper, we will assume that
\hat{\omega}(z)>0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
; otherwise,
A_{\omega}^{p}=H(\mathbb{D})
.

Let
S(\mathbb{D})
denote the set of all analytic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
\varphi\in S(\mathbb{D})
, and
u\in H(\mathbb{D})
, the generalized weighted composition operator
uD_{\varphi}^{(n)}
is defined as

uD_{\varphi}^{(n)}f=u\left(f^{(n)}\circ\varphi\right),\quad f\in H(\mathbb{D}).

The operator
uD_{\varphi}^{(n)}
was introduced by Zhu in
[
28
]
. This generalized weighted composition operator is also referred to as a weighted differentiation composition operator (see
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
). Specifically, when
n=0
,
uD_{\varphi}^{(n)}
reduces to the weighted composition operator
uC_{\varphi}
. Furthermore, when
n=0
and
u\equiv 1
,
uD_{\varphi}^{(n)}
becomes the composition operator
C_{\varphi}
.
For more discussion on composition operators and weighted composition operators, we refer to
[
5
,
9
,
11
,
16
,
27
]
and the references therein. When
u\equiv 1
,
uD_{\varphi}^{(n)}
is the differentiation composition operator
D_{\varphi}^{(n)}
. When
u\equiv 1
and
\varphi(z)=z
,
uD_{\varphi}^{(n)}
is the
n
-th differentiation operator
D^{(n)}
. So, the generalized weighted composition operator attracted a lot of attentions since it covers a lot of classical operators. See
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
for further information and results on generalized weighted composition operators on analytic function spaces.

In 2011, Stević, Sharma, and Bhat
[
20
,
21
]
presented an operator defined as

T_{u_{0},u_{1},\varphi}=u_{0}D_{\varphi}^{(0)}+u_{1}D_{\varphi}^{(1)}

and explored its boundedness, compactness, and essential norm for the mapping
T_{u_{0},u_{1},\varphi}:A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\alpha}^{p}
under the condition that

\displaystyle u_{0}\in H^{\infty}\,\,\,\mbox{or}\,\,\,\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{|u_{1}(z)|}{1-|\varphi(z)|^{2}}<\infty.

(1)

For further studies related to these operators, see, for instance,
[
10
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
.

Recently, in
[
7
]
, the authors utilized Khinchin’s inequality, Ger
\check{\mbox{s}}
gorin’s theorem, and the atomic decomposition of weighted Bergman spaces to eliminate the restriction (
1
). They demonstrated that for
1<p,q<\infty
and
\omega,\upsilon\in\mathcal{D}
, the following holds:

\|T_{u_{0},u_{1},\varphi}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to A_{\upsilon}^{q}}\approx\|u_{0}D_{\varphi}^{(0)}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to A_{\upsilon}^{q}}+\|u_{1}D_{\varphi}^{(1)}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to A_{\upsilon}^{q}}

and

\|T_{u_{0},u_{1},\varphi}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to A_{\upsilon}^{q}}\approx\|u_{0}D_{\varphi}^{(0)}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to A_{\upsilon}^{q}}+\|u_{1}D_{\varphi}^{(1)}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to A_{\upsilon}^{q}}.

Here,
\|T_{u_{0},u_{1},\varphi}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to A_{\upsilon}^{q}}
represents the essential norm of the operator
T_{u_{0},u_{1},\varphi}:A_{\omega}^{p}\to A_{\upsilon}^{q}
, which quantifies the distance between
T_{u_{0},u_{1},\varphi}
and the compact operators acting from
A_{\omega}^{p}
to
A_{\upsilon}^{q}
.
In the general scenario, the essential norm of a bounded operator
T
mapping from a Banach space
X
to another Banach space
Y
is defined as follows:

\|T\|_{e,X\to Y}=\inf\left\{\|T-K\|_{X\to Y}:K:X\to Y\text{ is compact}\right\}.

It is clear that
T
is compact if and only if
\|T\|_{e,X\to Y}=0
.

In 2021, Choe et al.
[
3
]
provided comprehensive characterizations regarding Carleson measures for the boundedness and compactness of differences of weighted composition operators operating on the standard weighted Bergman spaces over the unit disk. This work also led to a characterization of compactness for the differences of unweighted composition operators acting on Hardy spaces, addressing a question posed by Shapiro and Sundberg in
[
22
]
three decades earlier.
Building upon their work, the results were generalized to various standard weighted Bergman spaces on the unit disk
[
4
]
and the unit ball
[
2
]
, as well as to weighted Bergman spaces defined by two-sided doubling weights
[
1
]
.

Motivated by these researches, we prove in this short note that

\|u_{0}C_{\varphi}+u_{1}D_{\psi}^{(1)}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}\approx\|u_{0}C_{\varphi}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}+\|u_{1}D_{\psi}^{(1)}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}

and

\|u_{0}C_{\varphi}+u_{1}D_{\psi}^{(1)}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}\approx\|u_{0}C_{\varphi}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}+\|u_{1}D_{\psi}^{(1)}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}

for
1<p,q<\infty
,
u_{0},u_{1}\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
\varphi,\psi\in S(\mathbb{D})
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
\upsilon
being a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: In Section 2, we present the essential preliminaries. Section 3 is devoted to the proof of the main theorem.

In this paper, the symbol
C
will denote constants that may change from one instance to another.
For two positive functions
f
and
g
, we use the notation
f\lesssim g
to signify
that there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of the variables, such that
f\leq Cg
.
Likewise,
f\approx g
indicates that both
f\lesssim g
and
g\lesssim f
hold true.

2.
preliminaries

In this section, we state some lemmas which will be used in the proof of main result of this paper.

Using the pull-back measure, the operators
uC_{\varphi}
and
uD_{\varphi}^{(n)}
in Bergman spaces associated with doubling weights were studied in
[
8
,
12
]
. To establish the necessary conditions for
uC_{\varphi}
and
uD_{\varphi}^{(n)}
to be bounded and compact, the authors employed a family of test functions
\{f_{\lambda,\gamma}\}
from
A_{\omega}^{p}
.

When
\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
and
0<p<\infty
, let

\displaystyle f_{\lambda,\gamma,\omega,p}(z)=\left(\frac{1-|\lambda|^{2}}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{1}{\omega(S(\lambda))^{\frac{1}{p}}},\,\,\lambda,z\in\mathbb{D}.

According to
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
, there exists
\gamma_{\#}=\gamma_{\#}(\omega,p)
, whenever
\gamma>\gamma_{\#}
,

\displaystyle\|f_{\lambda,\gamma,\omega,p}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}}\approx 1,\,\,\,\,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}.

(2)

For the sake of simplicity, we will denote
f_{\lambda,\gamma,\omega,p}
as
f_{\lambda,\gamma}
.
It is clear that if
\gamma
is held constant,
f_{\lambda,\gamma}
converges uniformly to 0 on any compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
as
|\lambda|
approaches 1.

When
u\in H(\mathbb{D}),\varphi\in S(\mathbb{D}),0<q<\infty,\upsilon
is a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
, for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{D}
, let

\mu_{u,\varphi,q,\upsilon}(E)=\int_{\varphi^{-1}(E)}|u(z)|^{q}d\upsilon(z).

Then,

\displaystyle\|uD_{\varphi}^{(n)}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}=\|D^{(n)}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu_{u,\varphi,q,\upsilon}}^{q}}.

(3)

To express
\|D^{(n)}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu_{u,\varphi,q,\upsilon}}^{q}}
, as provided in
[
15
]
, we need to introduce some additional notations.

As usual, we define
\rho(a,z)=|\varphi_{a}(z)|=\left|\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z}\right|
to represent the pseudohyperbolic distance between
z
and
a
, and let
E_{s}(a)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\rho(a,z)<s\}
denote the pseudohyperbolic disc centered at
a\in\mathbb{D}
with radius
s\in(0,1)
. A sequence
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is referred to as a
\delta
-lattice if
D=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{\delta}(a_{i})
and the sets
E_{\delta/2}(a_{i})
are pairwise disjoint. Thus, Theorem 3 in
[
15
]
(see also
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
) can be stated as follows.

Theorem A
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\omega\in\mathcal{D},0<s<1,\mu
be a positive Borel
measure on
\mathbb{D}
. Then the following
statements hold.

(i)

When
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
D^{(n)}:A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}
is bounded if and only if

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\mu(E_{s}(z))}{(1-|z|)^{nq}\omega(S(z))^{\frac{q}{p}}}<\infty.

Moreover,

\|D^{(n)}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}}^{q}\approx\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\mu(E_{s}(z))}{(1-|z|)^{nq}\omega(S(z))^{\frac{q}{p}}}.

(ii)

When
0<q<p<\infty
, the following statements are equivalent:

(iia)

D^{(n)}:A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}
is bounded;

(iib)

D^{(n)}:A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}
is compact;

(iic)

the maximum function

M_{\omega}(\mu,n)(z):=\frac{\mu(E_{s}(z))}{(1-|z|)^{nq}\omega(S(z))},\,\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}

belongs to
L_{\omega}^{\frac{p}{p-q}}
.

Moreover,

\|D^{(n)}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}}^{q}\approx\|M_{\omega}(\mu,n)\|_{L_{\omega}^{\frac{p}{p-q}}}.

When
\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
and
1<p<\infty
, by
[
14
, Theorem 7]
,
(A_{\omega}^{p})^{*}\simeq A_{\omega}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
.
By using
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
1<p,q<\infty
,
\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
,
\mu
is a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
. If
K:A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}
is bounded,
K
is compact if and only if
\|K{f_{n}}\|_{L_{\mu}^{q}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
whenever
\{f_{n}\}
is bounded in
A_{\omega}^{p}
and uniformly converges to 0 on any compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
as
n\to\infty
.

Using this lemma, the essential norm of
D^{(n)}:A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}
can be estimated in a standard way.
For the benefit of readers, we display the proof of it.

Theorem 2
.

Let
1<p\leq q<\infty,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\omega\in\mathcal{D},0<s<1,\mu
be a positive Borel
measure on
\mathbb{D}
. If
D^{(n)}:A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}
is bounded,

\|D^{(n)}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}}^{q}\approx\limsup_{|z|\to 1}\frac{\mu(E_{s}(z))}{(1-|z|)^{nq}\omega(S(z))^{\frac{q}{p}}}.

Proof.

For any
r\in(0,1)
, let
\chi_{r}
be the characteristic function of
r\mathbb{D}
. That is
\chi_{r}(z)=1
when
|z|<r
and
\chi_{r}(z)=0
when
|z|\geq r
. By Lemma
1
and Theorem
A
,
\chi_{r}D^{(n)}:A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}
is compact and

\|D^{(n)}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}}^{q}\leq\|D^{(n)}-\chi_{r}D^{(n)}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}}\approx\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{(\mu-\mu\chi_{r})(E_{s}(z))}{(1-|z|)^{nq}\omega(S(z))^{\frac{q}{p}}}.

Letting
r\to 1
, we have

\|D^{(n)}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}}^{q}\lesssim\limsup_{|z|\to 1}\frac{\mu(E_{s}(z))}{(1-|z|)^{nq}\omega(S(z))^{\frac{q}{p}}}.

On the other hand, for any compact operator
K:A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}
, for a given
\gamma>\gamma_{\#}
, when
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\displaystyle\|(D^{(n)}-K)f_{\lambda,\gamma}\|_{L_{\mu}^{q}}^{q}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|D^{(n)}f_{\lambda,\gamma}(z)|^{q}d\mu(z)-\int_{\mathbb{D}}|Kf_{\lambda,\gamma}(z)|^{q}d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{E_{s}(\lambda)}|D^{(n)}f_{\lambda,\gamma}(z)|^{q}d\mu(z)-\int_{\mathbb{D}}|Kf_{\lambda,\gamma}(z)|^{q}d\mu(z).

Letting
|\lambda|\to 1
, by Lemma
1
, we have

\|D^{(n)}-K\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}}^{q}\gtrsim\limsup_{|\lambda|\to 1}\frac{\mu(E_{s}(\lambda))}{(1-|\lambda|)^{qn}\omega(S(\lambda))^{\frac{q}{p}}}.

Since
K
is arbitrary, we get the desired lower estimate. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3
.

By (
3
), Theorem
A
and Theorem
2
, when
1<p,q<\infty
,
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
\upsilon
is a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
, the norm and essential norm of

3.
The main result and proof

In
[
3
]
, the authors characterized the boundedness and compactness of the operator
uC_{\varphi}-vC_{\psi}:A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\alpha}^{q}
. To derive the necessary conditions, they decomposed
\varphi^{-1}(E_{s}(a))
into six parts for any
a\in\mathbb{D}
in order to obtain a lower estimate for
|u-vQ_{b}^{\gamma}|
. Here and throughout, we define

\displaystyle Q_{b}=\frac{1-\overline{b}\varphi}{1-\overline{b}\psi}.

(4)

The process is quite complicated. For the convenience of the readers, all the symbols used are the same as those in
[
3
]
. We will summarize some necessary estimates as follows.

For any
a\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}
,
\varepsilon,N>0
,
s\in(0,1)
, let

a_{N}:=ae^{-N(1-|a|)\mathrm{i}},\qquad\Gamma_{N}(a):=\big\{a\zeta:|\zeta|=1\mbox{ and }|\mbox{Arg}\zeta|\leq N(1-|a|)\big\},

\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}(a):=\left\{w\in\mathbb{D}:\frac{|1-\overline{a}w|}{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}<\sup_{z\in E_{s}(a)}|1-\overline{a}z|\right\},\qquad\mathrm{C}_{a}^{+}:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{Im}(\overline{a}w)>0\}.

Then, fix
s\in(0,1)
and
\gamma>\max\{1,\gamma_{\#}\}
. Choose
N>0
such that

\mbox{Arg}\left(1+\frac{8\mathrm{i}}{N(1-s)}\right)<\frac{\pi}{12\gamma}.

So, there exists
\delta=\delta(N)\in(0,1)
such that
\rho(a,a_{N})<\delta
. By Lemma 4.30 in
[
27
]
, for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\displaystyle|1-\overline{a_{N}}z|\approx|1-\overline{a}z|.

(5)

By
[
3
, (4.9)]
, we can choose
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(s,N,\gamma)\in(0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in E_{s}(a)\atop{w\in\Omega_{1,s}(a)\cap\mathrm{C}_{a}^{+}}}\left|\frac{1-\overline{a_{N}}z}{1-\overline{a_{N}}w}\right|^{\gamma}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}((1+\varepsilon)^{2}-1)}

(6)

as
|a|\to 1
.
Then, put

\displaystyle F_{1}(a)

\displaystyle:=\varphi^{-1}(E_{s}(a))\cap\psi^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}^{+}(a)),\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle F_{2}(a)

\displaystyle:=\varphi^{-1}(E_{s}(a))\cap\psi^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}^{-}(a)),\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle F_{3}(a)

\displaystyle:=\varphi^{-1}(E_{s}(a))\cap\psi^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}^{\prime}(a)),

in which,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}^{+}(a):=\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}(a)\cap\mathrm{C}_{a}^{+},\quad\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}^{-}(a):=\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}(a)\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}^{+}(a),\quad\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}^{\prime}(a):=\mathbb{D}\backslash\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}(a).

Therefore,

\mathbb{D}=\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}^{+}(a)\cup\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}^{-}(a)\cup\Omega_{\varepsilon,s}^{\prime}(a),\,\,\,\,\,\varphi^{-1}(E_{s}(a))=F_{1}(a)\cup F_{2}(a)\cup F_{3}(a).

To accomplish our goals, we can give a more assumption

\displaystyle u=1,\quad v=2.

(7)

Keep this in mind, because we will use it several times.

On
F_{1}(a)
, noting that

\displaystyle u-vQ_{a_{N}}^{\gamma}=u(1-Q_{a_{N}}^{\gamma})+(u-v)Q_{a_{N}}^{\gamma},

we further decompose the region
F_{1}(a)
into two parts, namely,

F_{1,1}(a):=\left\{z\in F_{1}(a):|u(z)||1-Q_{a_{N}}^{\gamma}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}|u(z)-v(z)||Q_{a_{N}}^{\gamma}(z)|\right\}

and

F_{1,2}(a):=F_{1}(a)\backslash F_{1,1}(a).

By Lemma 3.3 in
[
3
]
, we have

\displaystyle|Q_{a_{N}}|\geq\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a_{N}}\psi|}\gtrsim 1\mbox{ on }\psi^{-1}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}(a)).

Since
F_{1}(a))\subset\psi^{-1}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}(a))
,

\displaystyle|Q_{a_{N}}|\gtrsim 1\mbox{ on }F_{1}(a)).

(8)

Then, (4.12) and (4.13) in
[
3
]
demonstrate that

\displaystyle\left|u-vQ_{a_{N}}^{\gamma}\right|^{q}\approx|u|^{q}\left|1-Q_{a_{N}}^{\gamma}\right|^{q}+|u-v|^{q}|Q_{a_{N}}^{\gamma}|^{q\gamma}\gtrsim 1\,\,\mbox{ on }\,\,F_{1,1}(a)

(9)

and

\displaystyle\left|u-vQ_{a_{N}}^{2\gamma}\right|\gtrsim|u|^{q}\left|1-Q_{a_{N}}^{\gamma}\right|^{q}+|u-v|^{q}|Q_{a_{N}}^{\gamma}|^{q\gamma}\gtrsim 1\,\,\mbox{ on }\,\,F_{1,2}(a).

(10)

Here, the last
\gtrsim
in equations (
9
) and (
10
) are derived from equations (
7
) and (
8
).

Then, on
F_{3}(a)
, we decompose the region
F_{3}(a)
into two parts as in the case of
F_{1}(a)
. Set

F_{3,1}(a):=\left\{z\in F_{3}(a):|u(z)|\leq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}|v(z)||Q_{a}^{\gamma}(z)|\right\}

and

F_{3,2}(a):=F_{3}(a)\backslash F_{3,1}(a).

Then, using the assumption (
7
), and applying Lemma 4.3 and equation (4.8) in
[
3

, we obtain

\displaystyle|u-vQ_{a}^{\gamma}|^{q}\approx|u|^{q}+|vQ_{a}^{\gamma}|^{q}\gtrsim 1\quad\text{on }F_{3,1}(a);

(11)

and from equations (4.19), (4.20), and Lemma 4.3 in

\displaystyle|u-vQ_{a}^{\gamma+1}|^{q}\approx|u|^{p}=1\quad\text{on }F_{3,2}(a).

(12)

From the definition of the set

, we have

\displaystyle|Q_{a}|\leq\frac{1}{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}\quad\text{on }F_{3}(a).

(13)

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
1<p,q<\infty,\omega\in\mathcal{D}
,
\upsilon
is a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
,
\varphi,\psi\in S(\mathbb{D})
and
u_{0},u_{1}\in H(\mathbb{D})
. Then,

\|u_{0}C_{\varphi}+u_{1}D^{(1)}_{\psi}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}\approx\|u_{0}C_{\varphi}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}+\|u_{1}D^{(1)}_{\psi}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}.

Moreover, if
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
u_{0}C_{\varphi}+u_{1}D^{(1)}_{\psi}:A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}
is bounded,

\|u_{0}C_{\varphi}+u_{1}D^{(1)}_{\psi}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}\approx\|u_{0}C_{\varphi}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}+\|u_{1}D^{(1)}_{\psi}\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}.

Proof.

The upper estimates of the norm and essential norm of
u_{0}C_{\varphi}+u_{1}D^{(1)}_{\psi}
are obvious.
Put

T=u_{0}C_{\varphi}+u_{1}D^{(1)}_{\psi},\,\quad\|\cdot\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}=\|\cdot\|,\quad\|\cdot\|_{e,A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\upsilon}^{q}}=\|\cdot\|_{e}

for simplicity.
Then we will get the lower estimates in three parts.

Part (a):
1<p\leq q<\infty

Part (c):
1<q<p<\infty
and the lower estimates of
\|T\|
.

Let
\{r_{k}(t)\}
be Rademacher functions,
\vec{a}=\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a
\frac{s}{2}
-lattice in
\mathbb{D}

and
\vec{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{k}\}
be a sequence given by one of
\{a_{k}\}
,
\{a_{k,N}\}
and
\{\overline{a_{k,N}}\}
.
Here,
a_{k,N}=(a_{k})_{N}
.
We claim that
\{a_{k,N}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is separated. Otherwise, for any
0<x<\frac{1}{3}
, there exist
a_{i,N}
and
a_{j,N}
such that

E_{x}(a_{i,N})\cap E_{x}(a_{j,N})\neq\O
. By
[
4
, Lemma 4.3]
,
\rho(a_{i},a_{j})<2(1+8N)x
.
This is contradictory to
\vec{a}
is a lattice.
Moreover, we can assume
\inf|a_{k}|>0
and
\{|a_{k}|\}
is a increasing sequence.
Therefore, there exists a
M>0
such that
|a_{k}|>\tau
if and only if
k>M
.
Here, the constant
\tau
is that decided by (
15
).

Let
y
be one of
\gamma,\gamma+1,2\gamma
. By
[
15
, Thoerem 1]
, for any
\vec{c}=\{c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in l^{p}
,

\|g_{\vec{c},y,t,\vec{\lambda}}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}}\lesssim\|\vec{c}\|_{l^{p}}
, where

g_{\vec{c},y,t,\vec{\lambda}}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}r_{k}(t)f_{\lambda_{k},y}(z).

Let
\chi_{k}
be the characteristic function of
E_{s}(a_{k})
.
By Fubini’s theorem, Khinchin’s inequality and (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\|Tg_{\vec{c},y,t,\vec{\lambda}}\|_{L_{\upsilon}^{q}}^{q}dt=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}r_{k}(t)Tf_{\lambda_{k},y}\right|^{q}dtd\upsilon

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{2}|Tf_{\lambda_{k},y}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}d\upsilon\geq\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{2}|Tf_{\lambda_{k},y}|^{2}(\chi_{k}\circ\varphi)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}d\upsilon

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{q}\int_{\varphi^{-1}(E_{s}(a_{k}))}|Tf_{\lambda_{k},y}|^{q}d\upsilon

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|c_{k}|^{q}}{\omega(S(a_{k}))^{\frac{q}{p}}}\int_{\varphi^{-1}(E_{s}(a_{k}))}\left|u_{0}+\frac{y\overline{\lambda_{k}}u_{1}(1-\overline{\lambda_{k}}\varphi)^{y}}{(1-\overline{\lambda_{k}}\psi)^{y+1}}\right|^{q}\frac{(1-|\lambda_{k}|^{2})^{qy}}{|1-\overline{\lambda_{k}}\varphi|^{qy}}d\upsilon.

Then, as we did in Part (a), when
|a_{k}|>\tau
, on every region
F_{i,j}(a_{k})(i=1,2,3;j=1,2)
,
we can choose
\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{a_{k},i}
and
y=\gamma_{i,j}>\gamma_{\#}
properly to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\|Tg_{\vec{c},y,t,\vec{\lambda}}\|_{L_{\upsilon}^{q}}^{q}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\|Tg_{\vec{c},2y,t,\vec{\lambda}}\|_{L_{\upsilon}^{q}}^{q}dt\gtrsim

\displaystyle\sum_{k=M}^{\infty}\frac{|c_{k}|^{q}}{\omega(S(a_{k}))^{\frac{q}{p}}}\int_{F_{i,j}(a_{k})}\left|u_{0}\right|^{q}d\upsilon.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|T\|^{q}\|\vec{c}\|_{l^{p}}^{q}\gtrsim\sum_{i=M}^{\infty}\frac{|c_{i}|^{q}}{\omega(S(a_{i}))^{\frac{q}{p}}}\int_{\varphi^{-1}(E_{s}(a_{i}))}|u_{0}|^{q}d\upsilon.

By (
16
), we have

\displaystyle\|T\|^{q}\|\vec{c}\|_{l^{p}}^{q}\gtrsim

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{|c_{i}|^{q}}{\omega(S(a_{i}))^{\frac{q}{p}}}\int_{\varphi^{-1}(E_{s}(a_{i}))}|u_{0}|^{q}d\upsilon.

Let
\eta_{i}=\frac{1}{\omega(S(a_{i}))^{\frac{q}{p}}}\int_{\varphi^{-1}(E_{s}(a_{i}))}|u_{0}|^{q}d\upsilon
.
Since
\{|c_{i}|^{q}\}\in l^{\frac{p}{q}}
and
\|\{|c_{i}|^{q}\}\|_{l^{\frac{p}{q}}}=\|\vec{c}\|_{l^{p}}^{q}

\|\{\eta_{i}\}\|_{l^{\frac{p}{p-q}}}\leq\|T\|^{q}
.
Let the Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
be defined by

\mu(E)=\int_{\varphi^{-1}(E)}|u_{0}|^{q}d\upsilon.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{\mu(E_{\frac{s}{2}}(z))}{\omega(S(z))}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-q}}\omega(z)dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{\frac{s}{2}}(a_{i})}\left(\frac{\mu(E_{\frac{s}{2}}(z))}{\omega(S(z))}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-q}}\omega(z)dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(E_{s}(a_{i}))}{\omega(S(a_{i}))}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-q}}\omega(E_{\frac{s}{2}}(a_{i}))

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(E_{s}(a_{i}))}{\omega(S(a_{i}))^{\frac{q}{p}}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|T\|^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}.

By (
3
) and Theorem
A
, we have

\|u_{0}C_{\varphi}\|\approx\|I_{d}\|_{A_{\omega}^{p}\to L_{\mu}^{q}}\approx\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{\mu(E_{\frac{s}{2}}(z))}{\omega(S(z))}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-q}}\omega(z)dA(z)\right)^{\frac{p-q}{pq}}\lesssim\|T\|.

Therefore, we also have

\|u_{1}D_{\psi}^{(1)}\|\lesssim\|T\|.

Moreover, by Theorem
A
, the boundedness of

implies the compactness of it.
The proof is complete.

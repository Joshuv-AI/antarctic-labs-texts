Coh zeta functions for inert quadratic orders
Yifeng Huang

Abstract
. We study the Coh zeta function for a family of inert quadratic orders, which we conjecture to be given by t t -deformed Bressoud q q -series. This completes a trilogy connecting the zeta functions of ramified and split quadratic orders to the classical Andrews–Gordon and Bressoud identities, respectively [ HJ23a , Che24 ] . We provide strong evidence for this conjecture by deriving the first explicit formulas for the finitized Coh zeta function of the simplest order in the family, and for the t = 1 t=1 specialization of the finitized Coh zeta functions for all orders in the family. Our primary tool is a new method based on Möbius inversion on posets.



1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a commutative ring with suitable finiteness conditions (e.g., finitely generated over
{\mathbb{Z}}
or a completion thereof). We define the
Coh zeta function
by the formal Dirichlet series

(1.1)

\widehat{\zeta}_{R}(s):=\sum_{Q}\frac{1}{\lvert\Aut_{R}(Q)\rvert}\lvert Q\rvert^{-s},

where the sum is over all isomorphism classes of finite-cardinality
R
-modules
Q
; cf.
[
Hua23
]
. This appears as a zeta function considered by Cohen and Lenstra in their seminal work
[
CL84
]
in arithmetic statistics, and it is directly connected to the stack of coherent sheaves, the counting of matrix points on varieties, and the (unframed version of) degree 0 Donaldson–Thomas theory
[
BBS13
,
BM15
,
HOS23
]
.

When
R
is the coordinate ring of a singular curve over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, the study of
\widehat{\zeta}_{R}(s)
has surprisingly led to new sources of
q
-series, identities, and modular functions
[
Hua23
,
HJ23a
]
. A central open problem is to understand the nature of these
q
-series and to develop a framework for predicting their structure, potentially drawing connections to fields like knot theory or mathematical physics (for a strong hint, see
[
ORS18
]
). However, progress has been challenging. Explicit computation of
\widehat{\zeta}_{R}(s)
is notoriously difficult, with only a couple of families beyond the initial case
[
Hua23
]
having been computed
[
HJ23a
]
. Furthermore, the initial formulas for these families were often complicated multi-sums that did not immediately reveal their key properties. While the two-variable series (in
q
and
t=q^{-s}
) in the examples known so far did not appear to have product forms, their specializations at
t=\pm 1
do, which strongly suggested the existence of a richer, more elegant structure beyond the Rogers–Ramanujan type identity observed in the specializations
[
HJ23a
]
.

The author’s joint work with Jiang
[
HJ23a
]
studied two specific families of non-maximal orders in quadratic extensions of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}((X))
. The first is the family of
ramified
orders
R_{2,2m+1}:={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[X,Y]]/(Y^{2}-X^{2m+1})
, whose geometry corresponds to the
(2,2m+1)
torus knot. The second is the family of
split
orders
R_{2,2m}
, which correspond to the
(2,2m)
torus link; these rings are defined as
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[X,Y]]/(Y(Y-X^{m}))
, which for odd
q
are isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[X,Y]]/(Y^{2}-X^{2m})
; cf.
[
HJ23a
]
. These two families (with
m\geq 1
) completely classify the non-maximal orders in the ramified quadratic extension
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}((\sqrt{X}))
and the split quadratic extension
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}((X))\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q}((X))
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}((X))
, respectively; cf.
[
Sai88
]
. The Coh zeta function of
R_{2,2m+1}
was identified as a
t
-deformed version of the
m
-fold sum in one of the Andrews–Gordon identities, while that of
R_{2,2m}
was a mysterious
2m
-fold sum at that time.

A significant simplification for the split case was recently achieved by Shane Chern
[
Che24
]
, in response to questions communicated by the author in June 2024 during the
Legacy of Ramanujan
conference. This work was pivotal, as it allows the full picture for the ramified and split cases to be presented in the following unified and elegant form. To state this precisely, we first recall the
lattice zeta function
(or
Quot zeta function
) for an
R
-module
M
:

\zeta_{M}^{R}(s):=\sum_{L\subseteq_{R}M}(M:L)^{-s},

where the sum is over all finite-index submodules
L
of
M
. With this, we define the
finitized Coh zeta function
as
\widehat{\zeta}_{R,n}(s):=\zeta_{R^{n}}^{R}(s+n)
; cf.
Theorem

2.3
. Define also the following polynomials, whose
t=1
specializations appear as finitized Andrews–Gordon and Bressoud multi-sums in
[
Pau85
,
ASW99
]
:

\mathbf{AG}_{n}(q,t;2m+3):=(q;q)_{n}\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}\frac{q^{\sum n_{i}^{2}}t^{2\sum n_{i}}}{(q;q)_{n-n_{1}}(q;q)_{n_{1}-n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{m}}},

\mathbf{Br}_{n}(q,t;2m+2):=(q;q)_{n}\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}\frac{q^{\sum n_{i}^{2}}t^{2\sum n_{i}}}{(q;q)_{n-n_{1}}(q;q)_{n_{1}-n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{m}}(-tq;q)_{n_{m}}}.

Throughout the paper, we use the change of variables
t=q^{-s}
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
HJ23a
,
Che24
]
)
.

For each
m\geq 1
, the finitized Coh zeta functions for the ramified and split orders are given by:

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{R_{2,2m+1},n}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(tq^{-1};q^{-1})_{n}}\mathbf{AG}_{n}(q^{-1},t;2m+3)

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{R_{2,2m},n}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(tq^{-1};q^{-1})_{n}}\mathbf{Br}_{n}(q^{-1},-t;2m+2).

This newly clarified picture naturally raises two questions. First, what geometric object corresponds to the more direct
t
-deformation,
\mathbf{Br}_{n}(q,t;2m+2)
? Second, the formula for the ramified case is a polynomial in
t^{2}
, making
\mathbf{AG}_{n}(q,t;2m+3)=\mathbf{AG}_{n}(q,-t;2m+3)
. Is there a structural explanation for this symmetry?

In this paper, we propose that the answers to both questions lie with the third and final type of quadratic extension: the
inert
case. We consider the family of inert quadratic orders
R^{\prime}_{2,2m}:={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]+T^{m}{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}[[T]]
, which classify the non-maximal orders of the inert extension
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}((T))/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}((T))
. Our central conjecture provides the missing piece to the puzzle.

Conjecture 1.2
.

For each
m\geq 1
, the finitized Coh zeta function of the inert quadratic order
R^{\prime}_{2,2m}
is given by the
t
-deformed Bressoud sum:

\widehat{\zeta}_{R^{\prime}_{2,2m},n}(s)=\frac{1}{(tq^{-1};q^{-1})_{n}}\mathbf{Br}_{n}(q^{-1},t;2m+2).

A key structural insight, which motivates our work, is that these three classes of orders are related by
quadratic twists
. For odd
q
, let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
be a non-square. The inert order
R^{\prime}_{2,2m}
is isomorphic to the ring
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[X,Y]]/(Y^{2}-\alpha X^{2m})
, making it a quadratic twist of the split order
R_{2,2m}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[X,Y]]/(Y^{2}-X^{2m})
. At the same time, we can define the quadratic twist of the ramified order as
R^{\prime}_{2,2m+1}:={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[X,Y]]/(Y^{2}-\alpha X^{2m+1})
, which turns out to be isomorphic to
R_{2,2m+1}
itself via a simple change of variables. This isomorphism provides a powerful structural explanation for the second question: since the rings are the same, their zeta functions must be identical, which in the context of the proposed framework forces the identity
\mathbf{AG}_{n}(q^{-1},-t;2m+3)=\mathbf{AG}_{n}(q^{-1},t;2m+3)
and thus explains the vanishing of the odd powers of
t
.

This suggests a complete and uniform picture where
1.2
and
Theorem

1.1
can be listed together:

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{R_{2,2m+1},\,n}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(tq^{-1};q^{-1})_{n}}\mathbf{AG}_{n}(q^{-1},-t;2m+3),

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{R^{\prime}_{2,2m+1},\,n}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(tq^{-1};q^{-1})_{n}}\mathbf{AG}_{n}(q^{-1},t;2m+3),

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{R_{2,2m},\,n}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(tq^{-1};q^{-1})_{n}}\mathbf{Br}_{n}(q^{-1},-t;2m+2),

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{R^{\prime}_{2,2m},\,n}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(tq^{-1};q^{-1})_{n}}\mathbf{Br}_{n}(q^{-1},t;2m+2).

The primary contribution of this paper is to provide the first explicit formulas for the finitized Coh zeta function of the inert orders,
\widehat{\zeta}_{R^{\prime}_{2,2m},n}(s)
, in two crucial cases: the case
m=1
(for all
s
) and the
s=0
(i.e.,
t=1
) specialization for all
m\geq 1
(see
Theorem

5.3
and
Theorem

4.1
, respectively). We have numerically verified that our new formulas match the
t
-deformed Bressoud sums predicted in
1.2
. This paper does not contain a proof of the corresponding
q
-series identities; a proof will appear in forthcoming joint work with Chern.

Our results are achieved by introducing a new computational technique based on
Möbius inversion on the poset of submodules
(see
Section

3
). This approach is different from prior work
[
HJ23a
]
and allows us to handle the inert case, where previous methods faced technical obstacles. As a byproduct of this new technique, we can also re-derive some formulas for the already-solved ramified and split cases (see
Theorem

4.1
(a)(b)). Comparing our new formulas with the known ones yields several
q
-series identities, providing them with proofs that originate from the module-counting framework rather than from traditional
q
-series manipulations.

Plan of the paper

In
Section

2
, we collect the necessary background on
q
-series and results about lattice zeta functions from
[
HJ23a
]
. In
Section

3
, we introduce our main computational tool, Möbius inversion on posets, to prove the key technical result,
Theorem

3.2
. This result is then applied in
Section

4
to compute the
s=0
specializations for all three families of orders, as well as in
Section

5
to compute the finitized Coh zeta function at all
s
for the
m=1
inert case.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Basic hypergeometric series

We begin by defining the standard notation used in basic hypergeometric series. The
q
-Pochhammer symbol
is defined for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
by

(a;q)_{n}:=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{k}).

For brevity, we often write
(a_{1},\dots,a_{r};q)_{n}:=(a_{1};q)_{n}\cdots(a_{r};q)_{n}
. The
q
-binomial coefficient
is defined for integers
n,k
by

{n\brack k}_{q}:=\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{k}(q;q)_{n-k}}.

We use the standard convention
1/(q;q)_{n}=0
if
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{<0}
, which implies that sums over integers are naturally truncated. For example,
\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{1}{(q;q)_{n-r}(q;q)_{n+r}}=\sum_{r=-n}^{n}\frac{1}{(q;q)_{n-r}(q;q)_{n+r}}
.

The finitizations of the multi-sums from the introduction can be expressed as single sums.

Fact 2.1

(
[
Pau85
,
ASW99
]
)
.

For
m\geq 1
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have the following identities:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{AG}_{n}(q,1;2m+3)

\displaystyle=(q;q)_{n}\sum_{r}\frac{(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}+(m+1)r^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n-r}(q;q)_{n+r}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Br}_{n}(q,1;2m+2)

\displaystyle=(q;q)_{n}\sum_{r}\frac{(-1)^{r}q^{(m+1)r^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n-r}(q;q)_{n+r}}.

These limits imply the corresponding Rogers–Ramanujan type identities by taking
n\to\infty
and applying the Jacobi triple product formula.

Corollary 2.2
.

The
n\to\infty
limit of (
2.1
) is the central Andrews–Gordon identity
[
And98
, Corollary 7.8]
:

(2.3)

\mathbf{AG}_{\infty}(q,1;2m+3):=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}\frac{q^{\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}-n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{m-1}-n_{m}}(q;q)_{n_{m}}}=\frac{(q^{m+1},q^{m+2},q^{2m+3};q^{2m+3})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

The
n\to\infty
limit of (
2.2
) is the central Bressoud identity
[
Bre80
]
:

(2.4)

\mathbf{Br}_{\infty}(q,1;2m+2):=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}\frac{q^{\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n_{1}-n_{2}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{m-1}-n_{m}}(q^{2};q^{2})_{n_{m}}}=\frac{(q^{m+1},q^{m+1},q^{2m+2};q^{2m+2})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

We call them the “central” identities because each is part of a larger family; for instance, the general Andrews–Gordon identities include product sides of the form
(q^{a},q^{2m+3-a},q^{2m+3};q^{2m+3})_{\infty}/(q;q)_{\infty}
for other values of
a
.

2.2.
Formal Dirichlet series

For any commutative ring
A
with
1
, the ring of
formal Dirichlet series
with coefficient ring
A
, denoted by
\mathrm{Dir}(A)
, is a ring whose additive group is

(2.5)

\mathrm{Dir}(A)=A^{\mathbb{N}}:=\{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots):a_{n}\in A\},

and whose multiplication is given by

(2.6)

(a_{n})_{n}\cdot(b_{n})_{n}:=(\sum_{d|n}a_{d}b_{n/d})_{n}.

We identify an element
(a_{n})_{n}\in\mathrm{Dir}(A)
with a formal series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}
and write
\mathrm{Dir}(A)=\mathrm{Dir}(A;s)=\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}:a_{n}\in A\}.
We call
a_{1}
the constant term. Unless otherwise specified, we view every Dirichlet series formally, without assuming convergence. Given a sequence
f,f_{1},f_{2},\dots\in\mathrm{Dir}({\mathbb{C}};s)
, we say
f_{n}(s)
converges coefficient-wise to
f(s)
as
n\to\infty
if for each
N\geq 1
, the
N
-th coefficient of
f_{n}(s)
converges to the
N
-th coefficient of
f(s)
.

2.3.
Quot zeta functions

For a commutative ring
R
and an
R
-module
M
with a finite number of submodules of any given index, the
Quot zeta function
of
M
over
R
is the formal Dirichlet series

(2.7)

\zeta_{M}(s)=\zeta_{M}^{R}(s):=\sum_{L\subseteq_{R}M}(M:L)^{-s},

where
L
ranges over finite-index
R
-submodules of
M
. We also recall the
Coh zeta function

(2.8)

\widehat{\zeta}_{R}(s):=\sum_{Q/R}\lvert\Aut(Q)\rvert^{-1}\lvert Q\rvert^{-s},

where the sum is over all isomorphism classes of finite
R
-modules
Q
. From now on, the free module
A^{\oplus n}
over any ring
A
will be simply written as
A^{n}
.

Theorem 2.3

(cf.
[
Hua24
, Proposition 3.15]
)
.

Let
R
be any commutative ring with
1
. The following are equivalent:

(a)

\widehat{\zeta}_{R}(s)
is well-defined (i.e., there are finitely many isomorphism classes of
R
-modules of any given finite cardinality).

(b)

\zeta_{R^{n}}^{R}(s)
is well-defined for all
n\geq 0
(i.e., there are finitely many
R
-submodules of
R^{n}
of any given finite index).

If these conditions hold, the formal Dirichlet series

(2.9)

\widehat{\zeta}_{R,n}(s):=\zeta_{R^{n}}^{R}(s+n)

converge coefficient-wise as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, the limit is

(2.10)

\widehat{\zeta}_{R}(s)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{\zeta}_{R,n}(s).

This theorem justifies calling
\widehat{\zeta}_{R,n}(s)
the
finitized Coh zeta function
.

Proof.

For
n\geq 0
and
Q
a finite-cardinality module over
R
, let
\mathrm{Sur}_{R}(R^{n},Q)
be the set of
R
-linear surjections from
R^{n}
to
Q
. The group
\Aut(Q)
acts freely on
\mathrm{Sur}_{R}(R^{n},Q)
on the left by composition. Since every surjection
R^{n}\twoheadrightarrow Q
gives rise to an exact sequence
0\to L\to R^{n}\to Q\to 0
, we have a canonical bijection

(2.11)

\mathrm{Sur}_{R}(R^{n},Q)/\Aut(Q)\overset{\simeq}{\to}\mathrm{Quot}_{R}(R^{n},Q):=\{L\subseteq_{R}R^{n}:R^{n}/L\simeq Q\}.

It follows that
\lvert\mathrm{Quot}_{R}(R^{n},Q)\rvert=\lvert\mathrm{Sur}_{R}(R^{n},Q)\rvert/\lvert\Aut(Q)\rvert
. The equivalence of the conditions (a) and (b) is now straightforward.

To prove (
2.10
), the key observation is that for any finite
R
-module
Q
,

(2.12)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\lvert\mathrm{Sur}_{R}(R^{n},Q)\rvert}{\lvert\Hom_{R}(R^{n},Q)\rvert}=1,

where
\Hom_{R}(R^{n},Q)\simeq Q^{n}
. This limit follows from a simple inclusion-exclusion argument. The
N
-th Dirichlet coefficient of
\widehat{\zeta}_{R,n}(s)
is
\sum_{\lvert Q\rvert=N}N^{-n}\lvert\mathrm{Quot}_{R}(R^{n},Q)\rvert
. Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\lvert Q\rvert=N}N^{-n}\frac{\lvert\mathrm{Sur}_{R}(R^{n},Q)\rvert}{\lvert\Aut(Q)\rvert}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lvert Q\rvert=N}\frac{1}{\lvert\Aut(Q)\rvert}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\lvert\mathrm{Sur}_{R}(R^{n},Q)\rvert}{\lvert Q\rvert^{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lvert Q\rvert=N}\frac{1}{\lvert\Aut(Q)\rvert},

which is the
N
-th coefficient of
\widehat{\zeta}_{R}(s)
, as required.
∎

If
R
is a local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, the Quot zeta functions of
R^{n}
and of the submodule
\mathfrak{m}R^{n}
are related as follows.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
HJ23b
, Corollary 2.7]
)
.

If
R
is a local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residue field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, then for any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
,

(2.13)

\zeta_{R^{n}}^{R}(s)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}{n\brack r}_{q}q^{-rs}\zeta_{\mathfrak{m}R^{r}}^{R}(s-n+r).

2.4.
Lattices

Let
F
be a local non-archimedean field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
and residue field
k={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let
E
be a finite product of finite extensions of
F
. An
order
over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
in
E
is an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-subalgebra
R\subseteq E
that is finitely generated as an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-module and spans
E
over
F
. A (full)
R
-
lattice
in
E^{n}
is a finitely generated
R
-submodule
L\subseteq E^{n}
that spans
E^{n}
over
F
. An order
R
over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
is
monogenic
if
R=\mathcal{O}_{F}[\alpha]
for some
\alpha\in R
.

Let
\widetilde{R}
be the normalization of
R
. The
conductor
of
R
is the largest ideal of
\widetilde{R}
contained in
R
, denoted by
\mathfrak{c}_{R}
.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
HJ23a
]
)
.

Let
R
be an order. For the inclusion of quotient rings
A=R/\mathfrak{c}_{R}\subseteq B=\widetilde{R}/\mathfrak{c}_{R}
, we have

(2.14)

\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(s)=\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{\widetilde{R}}(s)\varepsilon_{B^{n}}^{A\subseteq B}(s),

where
\varepsilon_{M}^{A\subseteq B}(s):=\sum_{L\subseteq_{A}M,\;BL=M}(M:L)^{-s}
is the
saturation zeta function
.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
Sol77
]
)
.

If
\widetilde{R}=\prod_{i=1}^{l}\widetilde{R}_{i}
, where each
\widetilde{R}_{i}
is a DVR with residue field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q_{i}}
, then

(2.15)

\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{\widetilde{R}}(s)=\prod_{i=1}^{l}(q_{i}^{-s};q_{i})_{n}^{-1},

and equivalently,

(2.16)

\widehat{\zeta}_{\widetilde{R},n}(s)=\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{\widetilde{R}}(s+n)=\prod_{i=1}^{l}(q_{i}^{-1-s};q_{i}^{-1})_{n}^{-1}.

2.5.
Reflection principle

The
normalized lattice zeta function
for a lattice
M\subseteq E^{n}
is
\nu_{M}^{R}(s):=\zeta_{M}^{R}(s)/\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{\widetilde{R}}(s)
. This is known to be a Dirichlet polynomial
[
HJ23a
, Proposition 4.7]
, and hence entire in
s
. Correspondingly, the
normalized finitized Coh zeta function
is

(2.17)

\widehat{\nu}_{R,n}(s):=\nu_{R^{n}}^{R}(s+n)=\frac{\widehat{\zeta}_{R,n}(s)}{\widehat{\zeta}_{\widetilde{R},n}(s)}.

These normalized functions satisfy two crucial properties.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
HJ23a
, Remark 4.10]
)
.

For any order
R
,
\nu_{R^{n}}^{R}(0)=\nu_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(0)
.

An order is
Gorenstein
if its dualizing module is projective. The quadratic orders
R_{2,2m+1},R_{2,2m}
, and
R^{\prime}_{2,2m}
studied in this paper are all monogenic, and thus Gorenstein
[
JT15
, Prop. 3.6]
.

Theorem 2.8

(Reflection Principle
[
HJ23a
, Theorem 1.7]
)
.

If
R
is a Gorenstein order and
D=\lvert\widetilde{R}/R\rvert
, then
\nu_{R^{n}}^{R}(s)=D^{n^{2}-2ns}\nu_{R^{n}}^{R}(n-s)
.

Rewriting these results in terms of
\widehat{\nu}_{R,n}(s)
, the reflection principle becomes

\widehat{\nu}_{R,n}(s)=D^{-n^{2}-2ns}\widehat{\nu}_{R,n}(-s-n),

and
Lemma

2.7
implies
\widehat{\nu}_{R,n}(-n)=\nu_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(0)
. Combining these gives a fundamental formula for the
s=0
evaluation:

(2.18)

\widehat{\nu}_{R,n}(0)=D^{-n^{2}}\nu_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(0).

This relation explains why the
s=0
specialization is computationally special, as
\widehat{\nu}_{R,n}(0)
can be computed from the zeta function of the simpler module
\widetilde{R}^{n}
.

3.
Saturation zeta function and the key technical theorem

In this section, we prove our main technical result,
Theorem

3.2
, which provides explicit formulas for the lattice zeta function
\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(s)
for the three families of quadratic orders. Our primary new tool is Möbius inversion on posets of modules. We begin by recalling the combinatorial definitions needed to state the theorem.

For two tuples of integers
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{m})
and
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})
, we define a version of the Hall polynomial as follows.

Definition 3.1

(cf.
Theorem

3.7
)
.

Let
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{m})
and
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})
be tuples of integers. We define

(3.1)

G^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{s}}(q):=q^{\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}(r_{i}-s_{i})}\prod_{i=1}^{m}{r_{i}-s_{i+1}\brack r_{i}-s_{i}}_{q^{-1}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[q],

where
r_{m+1}=s_{m+1}=0
by convention. Note that
G^{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbf{s}}(q)=0
unless
r_{1}\geq\dots\geq r_{m}\geq 0
,
s_{1}\geq\dots\geq s_{m}\geq 0
, and
r_{i}\geq s_{i}
for all
i
. We also use the notation
\lvert\mathbf{s}\rvert:=s_{1}+\dots+s_{m}
.

With this, we can now state the main technical theorem of the paper, which provides explicit formulas for the lattice zeta function of
\widetilde{R}^{n}
considered as an
R
-module.

Theorem 3.2

(Key Theorem)
.

For any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1},n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and any prime power
q
, using the notation
t:=q^{-s}
and the multi-index notation such as
((2n)^{m-1},2n-r):=(\underbrace{2n,\dots,2n}_{m-1},2n-r)
, the following hold:

(a)

(Ramified) If
R=R_{2,2m+1}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[X,Y]]/(Y^{2}-X^{2m+1})
, then

(3.2)

(q^{-s};q)_{n}\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{\oplus n}}^{R}(s)=\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbf{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}{n\brack r}_{q}(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}}t^{2mn-\lvert\mathbf{s}\rvert}G^{((2n)^{m-1},2n-r)}_{\mathbf{s}}(q).

(b)

(Split) If
R=R_{2,2m}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[X,Y]]/(Y(Y-X^{m}))
, then

(3.3)

(q^{-s};q)_{n}^{2}\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{\oplus n}}^{R}(s)=\sum_{r_{1},r_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbf{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}{n\brack r_{1}}_{q}{n\brack r_{2}}_{q}(-1)^{r_{1}+r_{2}}q^{\binom{r_{1}}{2}+\binom{r_{2}}{2}}t^{2mn-\lvert\mathbf{s}\rvert}G^{((2n)^{m-1},2n-r_{1}-r_{2})}_{\mathbf{s}}(q).

(c)

(Inert) If
R=R^{\prime}_{2,2m}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]+T^{m}{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}[[T]]

(3.4)

(q^{-2s};q^{2})_{n}\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{\oplus n}}^{R}(s)=\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbf{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}{n\brack r}_{q^{2}}(-1)^{r}q^{r^{2}-r}t^{2mn-\lvert\mathbf{s}\rvert}G^{((2n)^{m-1},2n-2r)}_{\mathbf{s}}(q).

The remainder of this section will be dedicated to the proof of this theorem.

3.1.
Posets and Möbius function

Let
(\mathcal{P},\preceq)
be a finite poset. Recall (say,
[
Sta24
, §3.7]
) that the Möbius function
\mu_{\mathcal{P}}(x,y)
is the unique function with inputs in
x,y\in\mathcal{P},x\preceq y
and values in
{\mathbb{Z}}
that satisfies the Möbius inversion property: For functions
f,g
on
\mathcal{P}
that take values in any abelian group, we have

(3.5)

f(x)=\sum_{y\preceq x}g(y)\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{P}\iff g(x)=\sum_{y\preceq x}\mu_{\mathcal{P}}(y,x)f(y)\text{ for all }y\in\mathcal{P}.

The Möbius function also satisfies that the value
\mu_{\mathcal{P}}(x,y)
only depends on the structure of the poset
[x,y]:=\{z\in\mathcal{P}:x\preceq z\preceq y\}
.

For any finite ring
B
and a finite module
M
, let
\mathcal{P}_{B}(M)
be the poset of
B
-submodules of
M
ordered by inclusion. For any
W_{1}\subseteq_{B}W_{2}\subseteq_{B}M
, let
\mu_{B}(W_{2}/W_{1})=\mu_{B}(W_{1},W_{2})
denote the Möbius value with respect to
\mathcal{P}_{B}(M)
(the notation
\mu_{B}(W_{2}/W_{1})
is justified because the poset
[W_{1},W_{2}]
is determined by the structure of the quotient
B
-module
W_{2}/W_{1}
).

Lemma 3.3
.

For any inclusion of finite rings
A\subseteq B
and finite
B
-module
\widetilde{M}
, we have

(3.6)

\varepsilon_{\widetilde{M}}^{A\subseteq B}(s)=\sum_{\widetilde{W}\subseteq_{B}\widetilde{M}}\mu_{B}(\widetilde{M}/\widetilde{W})(\widetilde{M}:\widetilde{W})^{-s}\zeta_{\widetilde{W}}^{A}(s).

Proof.

On the poset
\mathcal{P}_{B}(\widetilde{M})
, construct the functions that take values in Dirichlet polynomials

(3.7)

f(\widetilde{W})=\sum_{W\subseteq_{A}\widetilde{W}}(\widetilde{M}:W)^{-s},\;g(\widetilde{W})=\sum_{W\subseteq_{A}\widetilde{W},\;BW=\widetilde{W}}(\widetilde{M}:W)^{-s},\;\widetilde{W}\in\mathcal{P}_{B}(\widetilde{M}).

It is trivial to verify that
f(\widetilde{W})=\sum_{\widetilde{W}^{\prime}\subseteq_{B}\widetilde{W}}g(\widetilde{W}^{\prime})
,
f(\widetilde{W})=(\widetilde{M}:\widetilde{W})^{-s}\zeta_{\widetilde{W}}^{A}(s)
, and
g(\widetilde{W})=(\widetilde{M}:\widetilde{W})^{-s}\varepsilon_{\widetilde{W}}^{A\subseteq B}(s)
. The desired identity follows from Möbius inversion and specializing
\widetilde{W}=\widetilde{M}
.
∎

3.2.
The poset of DVR modules

We recall an important setting where the poset
\mathcal{P}_{B}(M)
can be understood fully combinatorially and explicitly. Let
(V,\pi,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
be an DVR with uniformizer
\pi
and finite field

. Given a finite module

over
V
, the
type
of
M
over

is the unique partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
such that
M\simeq_{V}M_{V}(\lambda):=\bigoplus_{i}V/\pi^{\lambda_{i}}
.

Let
B=V/\pi^{m}
for some

. Then a
B
-module

is simply a
V
-module whose type
\lambda
satisfies
\lambda_{1}\leq m
. In the world of
B
-modules,
\simeq_{B}
and
\simeq_{V}
are equivalent, and
\subseteq_{B}
and
\subseteq_{V}
mean the same thing. For a finite
B
-module

, we refer to the type of

over

as the type of

over
B
, and denote
\mathrm{type}_{B}(M)=\mathrm{type}_{V}(M)
. Conversely, for
\lambda_{1}\leq m
, denote
M_{B}(\lambda)=M_{V}(\lambda)
. For
V
-modules

, the
cotype
of

in

is the type of

.

The following two fundamental results are due to Philip Hall.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
Mac15
, §II]
)
.

For any partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
, there is a universal polynomial (called the
Hall polynomial
)
g^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
such that for any DVR
(V,\pi,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, the number of
V
-submodules of
M_{V}(\lambda)
of type
\mu
and cotype
\nu
is given by
g^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}(q)
. Moreoever,
g^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}(t)=g^{\lambda}_{\nu\mu}(t)
.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
Sta24
, 3.9.5, 3.10.2]
)
.

Let
L\subseteq M
be finite modules over
(V,\pi,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. Then

(3.8)

\mu_{V}(M/L)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}},&\mathrm{type}_{V}(M/L)=(1^{r});\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where as usual,
(1^{r})
denotes the partition consisting only of
r

1
’s.

We will only use a coarser version of the Hall polynomial.

Definition 3.6
.

For partitions
\lambda,\mu
, let
g^{\lambda}_{\mu}(t):=\sum_{\nu}g^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}(t)
.

Clearly,

counts submodules of

of type
\mu

of cotype

Theorem 3.7

(
[
War13
]
)
.

For partitions
\lambda,\mu
, we have

(3.9)

g^{\lambda}_{\mu}(t)=t^{\sum_{i\geq 1}\mu_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda_{i}^{\prime}-\mu_{i}^{\prime})}\prod_{i\geq 1}{\lambda_{i}^{\prime}-\mu_{i+1}^{\prime}\brack\lambda_{i}^{\prime}-\mu_{i}^{\prime}}_{t^{-1}},

where as usual,
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}
denotes the

-th part of the conjugate partition
\lambda^{\prime}
of
\lambda
.

Remark
.

For partitions
\lambda,\mu
, say

We also need a well-known fact about rectangular partitions.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

denote the partition

with

We will often expand the Hall polynomial of the following simple form without mentioning:

The key lemma

A

\widehat{\nu}_{R,n}(0)=q^{-mn^{2}}\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbf{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}{n\brack r}_{q}(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}}G^{((2n)^{m-1},2n-r)}_{\mathbf{s}}(q).

(b)

(Split) For
R=R_{2,2m}
,

\widehat{\nu}_{R,n}(0)=q^{-mn^{2}}\sum_{r_{1},r_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbf{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}{n\brack r_{1}}_{q}{n\brack r_{2}}_{q}(-1)^{r_{1}+r_{2}}q^{\binom{r_{1}}{2}+\binom{r_{2}}{2}}G^{((2n)^{m-1},2n-r_{1}-r_{2})}_{\mathbf{s}}(q).

(c)

(Inert) For
R=R^{\prime}_{2,2m}
,

\widehat{\nu}_{R,n}(0)=q^{-mn^{2}}\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbf{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}{n\brack r}_{q^{2}}(-1)^{r}q^{r^{2}-r}G_{\mathbf{s}}^{((2n)^{m-1},2n-2r)}(q).

Proof.

Each of the three formulas is a direct consequence of the evaluation identity (
2.18
):
\widehat{\nu}_{R,n}(0)=D^{-n^{2}}\nu_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(0)
. For all three families of quadratic orders, we have
D=\lvert\widetilde{R}/R\rvert=q^{m}
. The term
\nu_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(0)=\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(0)/\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{\widetilde{R}}(0)
is computed by specializing the corresponding formula for
\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(s)
from
Theorem

3.2
at
s=0
(so
t=1
) and dividing by the specialization of
Lemma

2.6
. Substituting these components into the evaluation identity yields the results.
∎

We now rewrite these formulas into a form that makes it clear that they converge coefficient-wise as
n\to\infty
. The transformation involves expanding the
G
-polynomials, converting all
q
-binomial coefficients to a common base of
q^{-1}
(or
q^{-2}
), and applying the change of variables
t_{i}=n-s_{m+1-i}
. After significant simplification, including the telescoping of a product of
q
-binomials, we arrive at the following equivalent expressions.

Proposition 4.2
.

The formulas in
Theorem

4.1
are equivalent to the following:

(a)

(Ramified)

(4.1)

\begin{multlined}\widehat{\nu}_{R_{2,2m+1},n}(0)=\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbf{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}\frac{(-1)^{r}q^{-\left(\binom{s_{1}}{2}+\binom{r-s_{1}}{2}+r\right)-\sum_{k=2}^{m}s_{k}^{2}}}{\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(q^{-1};q^{-1})_{s_{k}-s_{k+1}}}\\
\times{n\brack r}_{q^{-1}}{2n-r\brack n-r+s_{1}}_{q^{-1}}\frac{(q^{-1};q^{-1})_{n+s_{1}}}{(q^{-1};q^{-1})_{n+s_{m}}}.\end{multlined}

(b)

(Split)

(4.2)

\begin{multlined}\widehat{\nu}_{R_{2,2m},n}(0)=\sum_{r_{1},r_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbf{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}\frac{(-1)^{r_{1}+r_{2}}q^{-\left(\binom{s_{1}}{2}+\binom{r_{1}+r_{2}-s_{1}}{2}+r_{1}+r_{2}\right)-\binom{r_{1}+1}{2}-\binom{r_{2}+1}{2}-\sum_{k=2}^{m}s_{k}^{2}}}{\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(q^{-1};q^{-1})_{s_{k}-s_{k+1}}}\\
\times{n\brack r_{1}}_{q^{-1}}{n\brack r_{2}}_{q^{-1}}{2n-r_{1}-r_{2}\brack n-r_{1}-r_{2}+s_{1}}_{q^{-1}}\frac{(q^{-1};q^{-1})_{n+s_{1}}}{(q^{-1};q^{-1})_{n+s_{m}}}.\end{multlined}

(c)

(Inert)

(4.3)

\begin{multlined}\widehat{\nu}_{R^{\prime}_{2,2m},n}(0)=\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbf{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}\frac{(-1)^{r}q^{-r-(s_{1}-r)^{2}-\sum_{k=2}^{m}s_{k}^{2}}}{\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(q^{-1};q^{-1})_{s_{k}-s_{k+1}}}\\
\times{n\brack r}_{q^{-2}}{2n-2r\brack n-s_{1}}_{q^{-1}}\frac{(q^{-1};q^{-1})_{n+s_{1}}}{(q^{-1};q^{-1})_{n+s_{m}}}.\end{multlined}

These explicit formulas allow for a direct comparison with the
t=1
specializations of the finitized Coh zeta functions from the introduction. To do so, we first express the results of
Theorem

1.1
in terms of the normalized zeta function
\widehat{\nu}_{R,n}(s)
. For the ramified case,
\widetilde{R}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]
, so
\widehat{\zeta}_{\widetilde{R},n}(s)=(tq^{-1};q^{-1})_{n}^{-1}
. For the split case,
\widetilde{R}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T_{1}]]\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T_{2}]]
, so
\widehat{\zeta}_{\widetilde{R},n}(s)=(tq^{-1};q^{-1})_{n}^{-2}
. This gives:

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}_{R_{2,2m+1},n}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{AG}_{n}(q^{-1},t;2m+3)

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}_{R_{2,2m},n}(s)

\displaystyle=(tq^{-1};q^{-1})_{n}\mathbf{Br}_{n}(q^{-1},-t;2m+2).

Specializing at
s=0
(
t=1
) and using the known identity
\mathbf{Br}_{n}(q,-1;2m+2)=1/(q;q)_{n}
, we find that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}_{R_{2,2m+1},n}(0)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{AG}_{n}(q^{-1},1;2m+3)

(4.5)

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}_{R_{2,2m},n}(0)

\displaystyle=1.

Equating these known expressions with the formulas in
Proposition

4.2
(a) and (b) yields two complex multi-sum

-series identities. Since both sides of each identity are derived from the same zeta function, these identities are now theorems with “
q
-combinatorial” proofs.

For the inert case,
1.2
predicts what
\widehat{\zeta}_{R^{\prime}_{2,2m},n}(0)
should be. To compare, we must normalize correctly using
\widehat{\zeta}_{\widetilde{R},n}(0)=(q^{-2};q^{-2})_{n}^{-1}
. This yields the prediction:

(4.6)

\widehat{\nu}_{R^{\prime}_{2,2m},n}(0)\overset{?}{=}(-q^{-1};q^{-1})_{n}\mathbf{Br}_{n}(q^{-1},1;2m+2).

Therefore, the
s=0
assertion of our main conjecture is equivalent to the
q
-series identity obtained by equating the formula in
Proposition

4.2
(c) with the right-hand side of (
4.6
).

5.
The case
m=1
, inert

In this section, we compute an explicit
q
-series formula for the finitized Coh zeta function of the simplest inert order,
R=R^{\prime}_{2,2}
. This computation achieves a strong form of verification for the
m=1
case of our main conjecture. We reduce the conjecture to a new, explicit
q
-series identity which, being a multi-sum identity with a fixed number of summation variables, is algorithmically provable by the
q
-Wilf-Zeilberger method
[
PWZ96
]
.

5.1.
A simpler formula for
\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(s)

For
m=1
, the ring is
R={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]+T{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}[[T]]
. The conductor is
\mathfrak{c}_{R}=T\widetilde{R}
, so the quotient rings in
Lemma

2.5
are simply
A={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
B={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
. The saturation zeta function
\varepsilon_{B^{n}}^{A\subseteq B}(s)
in this case can be computed by a direct combinatorial counting argument, which provides a simpler alternative to the general formula in
Theorem

3.2
(c). This counting problem is equivalent to enumerating
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-vector space with a particular spanning property.

Proposition 5.1
.

(a)

We have the identity in
{\mathbb{Z}}[q]
:

(5.1)

q^{\binom{r}{2}}\frac{(q^{2n};q^{-2})_{r}}{(q;q)_{r}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}q^{i^{2}-i}{n\brack i}_{q^{2}}{2n-2i\brack 2n-r}_{q}.

When
q
is a prime power, both sides count the number of
r
-codimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector subspaces
W
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}^{n}
satisfying
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}\cdot W={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}^{n}
.

(b)

Let
R=R^{\prime}_{2,2}
. The lattice zeta function
\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(s)
has the alternative formula:

(5.2)

(q^{-2s};q^{2})_{n}\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(s)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}q^{\binom{r}{2}}\frac{(q^{2n};q^{-2})_{r}}{(q;q)_{r}}t^{r},\quad t:=q^{-s}.

Proof.

Part (b) follows from part (a) and the definition of the saturation zeta function. Part (a) is a combinatorial identity proven by counting the same set of vector subspaces in two ways. The right-hand side is the formula derived from the Möbius inversion machinery (the
m=1
case of the proof of
Theorem

3.2
(c)). The left-hand side is derived by a counting argument using dual vector spaces, which we outline below.
∎

5.1.1.
Proof of
Proposition

5.1
(a) via dual spaces

Let
k={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
l={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
. For a finite-dimensional
l
-vector space
V
, we define the dual space
V^{\vee}:=\Hom_{k}(V,k)
, which is itself an
l
-vector space via
(c\cdot\alpha)(v):=\alpha(cv)
for
c\in l
. For a
k
-subspace
W\subseteq V
, we define
W^{\perp}:=\{\alpha\in V^{\vee}:\alpha(W)=0\}
. The map
W\mapsto W^{\perp}
is a dimension-reversing bijection between
k
-subspaces of
V
and
V^{\vee}
.

A key fact for the quadratic extension
l/k
is that for any
k
-subspace
W\subseteq V
, the dimensions of its
l
-closure
\overline{W}:=lW
and its
l
-interior (the largest
l
-subspace contained in
W
, denoted by
\underline{W}
) are related by the following.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional
l
-vector space, and
W
be a
k
-subspace of
V
. Then

(5.3)

\dim_{l}\overline{W}+\dim_{l}\underline{W}=\dim_{k}W.

Proof.

Since
l/k
is a quadratic extension, we observe that for any element
c\in l\setminus k
, we have

(5.4)

\overline{W}=W+cW,\quad\underline{W}=W\cap cW.

For these
k
-vector spaces, we apply a general dimension identity:

(5.5)

\dim_{k}(W+cW)+\dim_{k}(W\cap cW)=\dim_{k}W+\dim_{k}cW=2\dim_{k}W.

Halving both sides yields the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

5.1
(a).

The relation in
Lemma

5.2
, along with the property
(\overline{W})^{\perp}=\underline{(W^{\perp})}
, implies that a
k
-subspace
W\subseteq V
of dimension
2n-r
satisfies
\overline{W}=V
if and only if its dual
W^{\perp}\subseteq V^{\vee}
is an
r
-dimensional
k
-subspace whose
l
-closure
\overline{W^{\perp}}
is also
r
-dimensional over
l
.

The counting problem thus reduces to counting the number of
r
-dimensional
k
-subspaces of
V^{\vee}\simeq l^{n}
whose basis vectors are linearly independent over
l
. The number of ways to choose an ordered
l
-linearly independent set of
r
vectors in
l^{n}
is
(q^{2n}-1)(q^{2n}-q^{2})\cdots(q^{2n}-q^{2(r-1)})
. Dividing by the number of ways to choose an ordered
k
-basis for an
r
-dimensional
k
-subspace, which is
(q^{r}-1)(q^{r}-q)\cdots(q^{r}-q^{r-1})
, yields the formula
q^{\binom{r}{2}}\frac{(q^{2n};q^{-2})_{r}}{(q;q)_{r}}
.
∎

5.2.
The finitized Coh zeta function

We can now compute the full finitized Coh zeta function for
R^{\prime}_{2,2}
, incorporating the simplified formula for
\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{n}}^{R}(s)
along the way.

Theorem 5.3
.

For
R=R^{\prime}_{2,2}
, the finitized Coh zeta function is given by the double sum:

(5.6)

\widehat{\zeta}_{R,n}(s)=\sum_{i\geq j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}q^{-(i^{2}+ij+j)}{n\brack i}_{q^{-1}}\frac{(q^{2i};q^{-2})_{j}}{(q^{-1};q^{-1})_{j}(t^{2}q^{-2};q^{-2})_{i}}t^{i+j}.

Proof.

A key observation for
m=1
is that the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=T{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}[[T]]
of
R
is isomorphic to the normalization
\widetilde{R}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}[[T]]
as an
R
-module. Thus,
\zeta_{\mathfrak{m}R^{r}}^{R}(s)=\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{r}}^{R}(s)
for any
r
. We apply
Lemma

2.4
:

\zeta_{R^{n}}^{R}(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\brack i}_{q}q^{-is}\zeta_{\mathfrak{m}R^{i}}^{R}(s-n+i)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{n\brack i}_{q}q^{-is}\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{i}}^{R}(s-n+i).

Substituting the simplified formula for
\zeta_{\widetilde{R}^{i}}^{R}(s-n+i)
from
Proposition

5.1
(b) gives an explicit expression for
\zeta_{R^{n}}^{R}(s)
. The formula for
\widehat{\zeta}_{R,n}(s)=\zeta_{R^{n}}^{R}(s+n)
is then obtained by substituting
s\mapsto s+n
and simplifying.
∎

We now connect this result to our main conjecture.
1.2
for
m=1
predicts that
\widehat{\zeta}_{R^{\prime}_{2,2},n}(s)
is given by a finitization of a Bressoud sum, which is a single summation:

(5.7)

\widehat{\zeta}_{R^{\prime}_{2,2},n}(s)\overset{?}{=}\frac{1}{(tq^{-1};q^{-1})_{n}}\mathbf{Br}_{n}(q^{-1},t;4).

Theorem

5.3
provides the first explicit
q
-series formula for the left-hand side—a double sum—derived from algebraic principles. Therefore, the
m=1
case of our main conjecture is equivalent to a new
q
-series identity equating a double sum to a single sum. Crucially, an identity of this type is algorithmically verifiable using the
q
-Wilf-Zeilberger (WZ) method
[
PWZ96
]
. While we do not carry out the WZ proof in this paper, this reduction shows that the
m=1
case of the conjecture is, in principle, provable by established techniques.

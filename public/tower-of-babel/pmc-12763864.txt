Spatiotemporal heterogeneity of online HIV self-testing sales and its potential driving factors in China, 2015–2020: a retrospective nationwide study
Authors: Yi Lv, Qiyu Zhu, Chengdong Xu, Yan Jiang, Yidan Wang, Zhijun He, Guanbin Zhang, Mengjie Han, Cong Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSince the UNAIDS introduced HIV self-testing in 2014, its application has been widely adopted globally. Developed in China approximately 2015, HIV self-testing (HIVST), which involves the online purchase of testing kits via e-commerce platforms, has emerged as the most significant distribution method. This approach offers numerous benefits, including user-friendliness, speed, and enhanced privacy protection.ObjectiveTo understand the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of online HIVST purchasing behavior and its potential driving factors in China.MethodsThe online retail sales data of HIVST kits from the two largest e-commerce platforms in China from 2015 to 2020 were collected for this study. The Bayesian spatiotemporal hierarchical model (BSTHM) was used to investigate the spatiotemporal characteristics and identify the rapidly growing high-demand regions of online HIVST kit sales. Furthermore, the BSTHM was used to identify potential driving factors associated with online sales, including college students per 10,000 persons, GDP per capita, net per person, phone per person, population density, road density and HIV screening cases per 10,000 persons.ResultsFrom 2015 to 2020, the sales of online HIVST kits experienced a dramatic surge, increasing from 837,061 kits to reaching 7,827,411 kits annually, with a total of 23,987,155 kits sold, with annual peak sales in December. Four economically superior regions in China, the Pearl River Delta, Yangtze River Delta, Beijing-Tianjin areas, and Shandong Peninsula, presented relatively high spatial preferences for online purchased HIVST kits. Thirty-eight rapidly growing high-demand regions were identified, which were located mainly in northeastern China (28.9%, 11/38), the Pearl River Delta (28.9%, 11/38) and the Beijing-Tianjin areas (15.8%, 6/38). The number of college students per 10,000 persons, GDP per capita, net per person, population density and number of HIV screening cases per 10,000 persons were identified as the driving factors that were positively associated with the online purchase rate of HIVST kits.ConclusionsOur study illuminates the spatiotemporal dynamics and driving factors behind online HIVST kit sales across mainland China, offering pivotal insights for crafting detailed HIVST guidelines. We pinpointed regions with significant sales demand, guiding policymakers in resource reallocation and HIV care continuum optimization, particularly in identified critical ' rapidly growing high-demand’ regions.Keywords: HIV self-testing, Spatiotemporal heterogeneity, China, Online purchase, Rapidly growing high-demand regions

Introduction

HIV self-testing (HIVST) is a critical development in the global fight against HIV/AIDS, offering a convenient and confidential method for individuals to learn their HIV status [1]. The World Health Organization (WHO) has championed HIVST as a pivotal approach to extend the reach of HIV testing to populations that might not otherwise seek testing, including female sex workers (FSW), injecting drug users (IDU), and men who have sex with men (MSM) [2]. Recognizing its potential, the WHO has provided guidelines to optimize HIVST implementation, which include effective service delivery models, linkages to care, and support tools [3, 4]. The accessibility and reliability of HIVST allow it to serve as a cornerstone in global health strategies aiming to increase testing coverage and connect individuals with necessary care and prevention services.

HIV self-testing (HIVST) has become a significant public health strategy in China, particularly because of its convenience, confidentiality, and ability to reach populations that are less likely to access traditional testing services. The introduction of HIVST in China approximately 2015 [5, 6] and its promotion through online e-commerce platforms has marked a pivotal shift in how individuals engage with HIV testing, highlighting a user-friendly, fast, and privacy-ensuring approach to testing. The online distribution channels for HIVST kits have played a crucial role in this shift, with major e-commerce platforms becoming key providers of these kits. This development has not only facilitated wider access to HIVST but also helped in overcoming stigma and confidentiality concerns associated with conventional HIV testing methods [7].

A study analyzing the spatiotemporal distribution of HIVST kit purchases in China between 2016 and 2019 revealed a significant increase in the rates of online purchases, with a notable peak in December of each year, aligning with World AIDS Day. This trend underlines the growing acceptance and reliance on HIVST as a primary method for HIV testing among the Chinese population. The study also revealed that high-demand clusters for HIVST kits were located primarily in economically prosperous and highly urbanized regions, suggesting that socioeconomic factors play a significant role in the adoption of HIVST [8].

In this study, we further analyzed online HIVST kit sales data from 2015 to 2020 from JD and Taobao, the top two e-commerce platforms in China, and revealed the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of online HIVST kit purchases from a long-term perspective in China. Moreover, we investigated the potential driving factors associated with the online purchase of HIVST kits and identified rapidly growing high-demand regions and the associated mechanisms. As HIVST continues to play a vital role in China’s HIV prevention and control efforts, understanding these dynamics will be crucial for tailoring interventions and support to maximize the public health benefits of self-testing.

Methods

Source of data

JD and Taobao, two paramount e-commerce entities in China, were the sources for the acquisition of city-level monthly online retail data on HIVST kits covering the period from 2015 to 2020. The dataset harvested from these platforms included the city locations of the purchasers, yet it did not encompass specific customer shipping addresses. To complement this dataset, a detailed extraction of city-level socioeconomic and demographic information was conducted from the China City Statistical Yearbook, the Provincial Statistical Yearbooks, or the official websites of the respective local statistics departments. This comprehensive extraction process yielded critical data points, including the gross domestic product (GDP), GDP per capita, and counts of college students, mobile phone users, and internet service subscribers. Additionally, population metrics were sourced from the China Population Census Yearbook, and population density data (individuals per square kilometer) were acquired from WorldPop (https://www.worldpop.org). Road network information was downloaded from the National Catalog Service for Geographic Information [9]. Moreover, city-specific data regarding HIV screening and confirmed positive cases per 10,000 individuals were procured from the National HIV/AIDS Laboratory Management Information System of China, providing a substantial basis for subsequent analyses.

Spatiotemporal distribution of the online purchase of HIVST kits

The spatial and temporal characteristics of the online purchase of HIVST kits were investigated via the Bayesian spatiotemporal hierarchical model (BSTHM). To eliminate the influence of population size and better reflect the behavioral characteristics of people in cities purchasing HIVST kits online, the online purchase rate of HIVST kits per capita was calculated by dividing the number of HIVST kits sold in a city by the number of people living in that city. Data management and statistical analysis

ArcGIS software was used to calculate the population density (1/km2) and road density (km/km2) of each city. The BSTHM was conducted with WinBUGS 14. All geographic maps were created with ArcGIS 10.2 software.

Results

Temporal trends in online HIVST kit sales from 2015 to 2020

From 2015 to 2020, sales of HIVST increased from 837,061 kits to 7,827,411 kits, cumulatively reaching a total of 23,987,155 kits sold (Fig. 1A). Each hour of 2020, approximately 893 HIVST kits were sold online in China. The sales of HIVST kits online have clearly increased over time, especially after 2017. Furthermore, these sales display seasonal fluctuations, with the highest sales recorded in December each year. (Fig. 1B). In addition, from 2015 to 2020, compared with the national average growth rate of online HIVST kit sales, some cities experienced greater growth rates, primarily Henan, Inner Mongolia, Hebei, Shandong, Jilin, and Ningxia (Fig. 1C), which is closely related to the low starting point in these cities.

Geographic distribution of online HIVST kit sales from 2015 to 2020

The geographic distribution analysis revealed that, in 2015, only a few 7 major cities, including Shanghai, Beijing, Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Hangzhou, Chengdu, and Chongqing, had annual online sales of HIVST kits exceeding 20,000, whereas in 2017 and 2020, this number reached 29 and 86, respectively (Fig. 2A-F). Furthermore, the geographical dispersion of online HIVST kit sales exhibited marked disparities across the nation. Upon examining the Heihe‒Tengchong Line, colloquially known as the “Hu Line,” drawn in 1934 to underscore the stark disparities in population distribution within China [11], it was observed that a predominant volume of HIVST kits sold online were dispatched to cities located southeast of this demarcation.

To mitigate the influence of population density on the online sales metrics of HIVST kits, our analysis focused on examining the per capita online purchase rate of HIVST kits across different geographic regions (Fig. 3A-F). Our analysis revealed that, at an aggregate level, the per capita online purchase rate of HIVST kits in cities in eastern China significantly surpassed that in western China. Moreover, the purchasing patterns in northern cities were found to be broadly similar to those in southern cities, indicating a relatively uniform distribution of HIVST kits across these latitudinal zones.

Spatial distribution characteristics of the online purchase rate of HIVST kits per 100,000 people

The results of the Bayesian hierarchical model analysis concerning the spatial distribution revealed that the online sales of the HIVST kits were not uniformly distributed, as illustrated in Fig. 4A. Notably, four regions demonstrated a significantly greater spatial inclination toward the online purchase of HIVST kits. These regions include the Pearl River Delta, the Yangtze River Delta, the Beijing-Tianjin conurbation and the Shandong Peninsula. Notably, these regions not only present superior economic conditions relative to other parts of China [12] but also present the top 20 cities identified with a pronounced spatial preference for online purchases of HIVST kits, which predominantly belong to these regions, as depicted in Fig. 4B.

Distribution of hot and cold spots of online purchases of HIVST kits in China

We classified 337 cities across the nation, including 4 municipalities directly under the central government, 293 prefecture-level cities, 7 regions, 30 autonomous prefectures, and 3 leagues, into 3 categories, namely, hot spots, cold spots, and neither hot nor cold spots. Among the 337 cities in China, 103 (30.56%) and 111 (32.94%) cities were identified as hot and cold spots, respectively. The other 123 (36.5%) cities were classified as neither hot nor cold spots. As shown in Fig. 5A, the hot spot areas were distributed mainly in northeastern China (Liaoning, Jilin, and Heilongjiang) and southeastern China (Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Fujian, and Guangdong), whereas the cold spots were distributed mainly in northwestern China (Shaanxi, Gansu, and Xinjiang) and southwestern China (Sichuan, Guizhou, and Yunnan).

Cold and hot spot analysis of online purchased HIVST kits in China from 2015–2020. A Spatial pattern of cold and hot spots and growth trends in the online purchase of HIVST kits. B The 38 cities with the most popular online purchased HIVST kits. HIVST HIV self-testing

Among the 103 hot spots, 38 cities showed a rapidly increasing trend rather than an overall increasing trend, primarily in northeastern China (28.9%, 11 out of 38), the Pearl River Delta (28.9%, 11 out of 38), and the Beijing‒Tianjin regions (15.8%, 6 out of 38). Among these cities, eight are provincial capital cities: Shenyang, Changchun, Harbin, Shijiazhuang, Hohhot, Jinan, Zhengzhou, and Nanning. Thus, these cities will likely continue to face high risk and remain hot spots in the future (Fig. 5B). In addition, among the 111 cold spots, 61 cities presented a faster increasing trend than an overall increasing trend. Consequently, the risk in these regions will likely be faster than the overall risk, and they will likely become hot spots in the future. Public health departments should therefore focus on these cities.

Discussion

Principal findings

In this retrospective analysis of the study, we observed a steady increase in HIVST kit purchases over the 6-year study period. From January 1, 2015, to December 31, 2020, the number of online HIVST kits sold increased by 9.35 (from 837,061 kits to 7,827,411 kits). The growth in the size of HIVST kit sales accelerated after 2017. This growth may have been prompted by China’s 13th Five-Year Plan for HIV Prevention and Control, a national policy framework for HIV response adopted in 2017, which encouraged innovative strategies to expand HIV testing, including the distribution of HIVST kits through web-based and pharmacy platforms [13]. In addition, our temporal analysis revealed that the sale of HIVST kits through online channels in China exhibited marked seasonal fluctuations within a single year, culminating in pronounced peaks of sales activity in December, which is likely due to the effectiveness of public education campaigns designed to encourage HIV testing on World AIDS Day (December 1) [14–16]. To promote sustained and effective HIV prevention and testing awareness, it is recommended to develop a year-round campaign calendar with multiple key dates, carrying out continuous activities targeting different groups during important periods such as Valentine’s Day, International Day Against Drug Abuse, and the back-to-school season, to prevent the decline in promotional impact. Additionally, establishing a regular annual cooperation mechanism with e-commerce platforms like Tmall and JD.com to set up permanent “HIV Prevention and Self-Testing” sections and launch joint campaigns or public education activities on a quarterly basis would help overcome the previous limitation of concentrating efforts only in December. Furthermore, promoting HIV self-test kit subscription services and post-risk testing reminders via SMS or app notifications can guide the public toward adopting long-term health management habits.

Consistent with our prior research [7], online HIVST kit sales exhibit an economic “long-tail” effect, where a large number of smaller sales collectively form a major market share. This is the inverse of traditional HIV testing, which follows the Pareto principle [17]: a small proportion of laboratories conduct 80% of screenings [18]. The decline in sales share from major cities (41.76% to 34.3%) further confirms that online distribution achieves a wider geographic reach than conventional methods. The above research highlights the need to reallocate public health resources based on “long-tail” data, with more prevention and awareness resources directed to small and medium-sized cities and rural areas where online sales are growing rapidly but traditional testing resources remain scarce. Meanwhile, logistics accessibility in remote regions should be optimized through government subsidies to delivery companies or centralized procurement to reduce express shipping costs, thereby ensuring the affordability and availability of HIV self-test kits. Furthermore, locally adapted and dialect-tailored promotional materials and instructional guides should be developed for different regions and cultural groups, and disseminated via influential local social media accounts to enhance information reach and acceptance.

In addition, the regression analysis revealed that the number of college students per 10,000, GDP per capita, net per person, population density and number of HIV screening cases per 10,000 were positively associated with the online purchase rate of HIVST kits per 100,000 persons for the whole country. The college student population is sexually active and often has higher levels of health awareness and access to sexual health education. Moreover, college students are particularly concerned about privacy and are likely to be comfortable purchasing items online, including HIVST [19, 20]. A higher GDP per capita usually reflects higher individual disposable income. With more financial resources, individuals are more likely to afford health-related products, including HIVST kits purchased online. In addition, populations in regions with higher GDP per capita might have greater health awareness and access to information about HIV prevention and testing, partly due to better education and health services. This awareness can translate into increased demand for HIVST kits [21, 22]. “Net per person” likely refers to the level of internet access or usage by individuals. The internet and mobile devices can facilitate social networking. These technologies can promote sexually risky behaviors through dating apps and websites that facilitate casual sexual encounters [23, 24]. The internet could be a source of valuable sexual health information [19, 25], playing a crucial role in enhancing awareness of HIVST. This dual-faceted nature of the internet—while potentially increasing exposure to sexually risky behaviors—also offers unparalleled opportunities for health education and promotion. In densely populated areas, the accessibility of online shopping, including health products such as HIVST kits, is often greater because of better logistics and delivery systems. Moreover, in such areas, individuals might prefer the anonymity of online purchases because of privacy concerns related to HIV testing [26]. High-density areas often have more targeted health awareness campaigns and better access to information, which could increase awareness and subsequently demand HIVST kits [27]. Regions with higher rates of HIV screening often benefit from more robust public health campaigns and better access to healthcare, which in turn raises awareness about HIV and its prevention. This heightened awareness could lead to increased interest in and demand for HIVST kits, especially for those seeking privacy and convenience, which online purchases could provide [28, 29]. Traditional HIV screening and HIVST kits can complement each other. As the overall screening rate increases, individuals who test negative or want regular monitoring might opt for HIVST kits for periodic testing at home, especially those in high-risk groups. Similarly, people who are identified as needing further testing in traditional settings may use HIVST kits for follow-up testing [30].

The findings from spatial Bayesian analyses in our study (Fig. 4A) indicate that cities situated in economically advanced regions exhibit a notably heightened spatial preference for procuring HIVST kits through web-based platforms, which is consistent with the findings of a previous study [7, 8]. In general, cities with more advanced economies tend to present several distinct characteristics: higher per capita GDP, greater population density, a greater number of college students, and increased internet usage per capita. These indicators are often interlinked, reflecting the multifaceted nature of economic development [31]. As delineated in prior discussions, these factors are positively correlated with the online purchase of HIVST kits. Therefore, in more economically developed areas, more people purchase HIVST kits online. This study suggests adopting differentiated strategies based on regional development levels: in economically developed areas, “Internet+” innovations can be introduced, such as partnering with food delivery and ride-hailing platforms to embed health service entry points in their apps or push discount notifications for express delivery of HIVST kits from nearby pharmacies to users with relevant search histories. Meanwhile, big data on social platforms can be utilized to deliver targeted HIVST advertisements and educational content to users with high-risk behavior profiles. In less developed regions, enhanced support measures should be implemented, such as launching e-vouchers for low-income groups or pilot health insurance reimbursement schemes to reduce cost barriers, and displaying public service ads in rural internet cafes and public libraries to guide users in accessing HIVST services.

Some studies have shown that most individuals who purchase HIVST kits online have recently engaged in high-risk sexual behaviors [32–34]. Thus, web-based HIVST kit–purchasing behavior could largely indicate that recent high-risk sexual behavior may have been a factor in HIV acquisition, and the rapidly growing high-demand cities identified in our study could serve as early warning signs for new HIV epidemics. Notably, our study revealed many rapidly growing high-demand cities located in northeastern China, an aspect previously overlooked in research. The identified driving factors from our model help explain this phenomenon. Northeastern China retains a strong historical infrastructure, including high internet penetration (net per person) and a relatively well-educated population with numerous universities (college students per 10,000 persons), as seen in cities like Shenyang, Changchun, and Harbin. Our model confirms that these factors are positively associated with online HIVST sales. This suggests that even amidst economic challenges, the population’s ability to access and utilize e-commerce platforms for health purposes remains high. The positive association with HIV screening cases per 10,000 persons indicates that cities with more active traditional testing programs also see higher online sales, suggesting that online HIVST may be complementing rather than replacing existing efforts, perhaps by reaching a different demographic within the same at-risk environment. Our study suggests establishing an online sales data-based “hotspot early warning and response mechanism” to serve as a supplementary indicator for real-time monitoring within the national comprehensive AIDS prevention and control information system. Once abnormal fluctuations or new hotspot areas are detected, an early warning should be triggered immediately, followed by targeted investigations and emergency responses. Meanwhile, resources should be prioritized for these hotspot cities, including deploying additional professionals to strengthen VCT and PITC, as well as launching pilot “Internet + comprehensive intervention” projects to seamlessly integrate online testing with offline confirmation, treatment, and partner notification services. In addition, qualitative research should be promptly conducted in newly emerging hotspots such as Northeast China to gain in-depth understanding of the socio-cultural and economic factors (e.g., population mobility, specific industries, etc.) driving online purchasing and high-risk behaviors, so as to provide a basis for developing precisely targeted intervention measures.

In the future, artificial intelligence (AI) will enable accurate identification of high-risk populations for HIV and sexually transmitted infections through personal risk assessments. This will increase identification accuracy and promote HIV self-testing, allowing more individuals to proactively understand their health and effectively prevent the spread of infections [35]. AI can leverage big data to construct screening models that predict an individual’s risk of infection. For example, by analyzing anonymously collected medical data, online surveys, social media interactions, etc., AI models can detect high-risk behavioral patterns and automatically recommend HIV self-testing, particularly for those who do not undergo regular traditional screening. Furthermore, AI-powered chatbots could be developed to provide round-the-clock, anonymous, and confidential preliminary consultation and psychological support to individuals before or after purchasing testing kits, helping alleviate anxiety and seamlessly referring those in need to further professional human services.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. First, our data are derived exclusively from e-commerce platforms, and therefore primarily reflect trends in online purchasing behavior rather than the entire landscape of HIVST utilization in China, which also includes offline channels such as pharmacies and community-based organizations. Second, owing to the absence of detailed information on the specific usage and outcomes of the post-HIVST kits, we are unable to accurately determine the kits’ direct contributions to the reported HIV cases. This lack of follow-up data limits our ability to assess the true impact of self-testing on detection rates and public health outcomes. Furthermore, our data capture only the total number of kits sold, not the actual number of unique purchasers, meaning that we cannot rule out the possibility that some individuals purchased multiple self-testing kits. This could lead to an overestimation of the overall reach and adoption of HIVST. Finally, our analysis of the potential driving factors behind the sales of HIVST kits is restricted by data availability, as we were only able to assess trends via data from 2018 to 2020. This limited timeframe may not fully capture longer-term trends or the influence of external factors such as changes in public health policies or shifts in societal attitudes toward HIV self-testing. Consequently, our findings should be interpreted with caution, and further research incorporating more comprehensive data and longer observation periods is needed to provide a deeper understanding of these dynamics.

Conclusion

Through a retrospective study spanning six years and involving over twenty million online HIV self-testing (HIVST) kits, our investigation highlights the spatiotemporal dynamics and driving factors behind online HIVST kit purchasing patterns across mainland China. These findings provide critical insights that can inform the development of more nuanced national and local HIVST protocols. Our study highlights key regions with a relatively high demand for online HIVST kits, offering public health policymakers valuable data to help reallocate resources and optimize the HIV care continuum. This is particularly important for addressing the “hot spots” and “fast spots” of demand that we have identified, enabling targeted interventions and more effective prevention strategies in these areas.

Versatile optical accordion lattices using binary phase transmission gratings Journal: opticajournal Publication type: Research Article
Hyok Sang Han, 1,* Ahreum Lee, 1 Sarthak Subhankar, 1 S. L. Rolston, 1,3 and Fredrik K. Fatemi 2,3

Abstract
Optical accordion lattices are routinely used in quantum simulation and quantum computation experiments to tune optical lattice spacings. Here, we present a technique for creating tunable optical lattices using binary-phase transmission gratings. Lattices generated using this technique have high uniformity, contrast, lattice spacing tunability, and power efficiencies. These attributes are crucial for exploring collective quantum phenomena in highly ordered atomic arrays coupled to optical waveguides for quantum networking and quantum simulation. In this paper, we demonstrate adjustable-period lattices that are ideally suited for use with optical nanofibers.

Theory

In our approach, shown in Fig.
2
, the two beams are generated by diffraction of a laser beam passing through a binary phase transmission grating.
The beams are relayed through a
4f-
imaging system that only transmits the
\pm 1
diffracted orders. The far-field pattern of the electric field of light diffracted by a grating is given by the Fraunhofer diffraction integral:

\mathcal{E}(u)=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kz}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kx^{2}/{2z}}}{i\lambda z}\int\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}2\pi ux^{\prime}}dx^{\prime},

(1)

where
u=x/(z\lambda)
,
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is the electric field of the laser light illuminating the grating,
\lambda
is the wavelength of light,
k=2\pi/\lambda
, and
f(x)
is the structure function of the grating. The Fourier decomposition of the structure function is as follows:

f(x)=\sum_{m}a_{m}e^{-2\pi imx/{d_{\text{grt}}}},

(2)

where
a_{m}
is the
m
th Fourier coefficient with
m=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\cdots
, and
d_{\text{grt}}
is the grating period.

The structure function for a periodic binary phase transmission grating
[
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
in air/vacuum is (Fig.
2
c)

f(x)=\begin{cases}e^{i\varphi}&\text{ if }x\in\left[0,l\right]\\
1&\text{ if }x\in\left[l,d_{\text{grt}}\right]\end{cases},

(3)

with

\varphi=\frac{2\pi h}{\lambda}(n_{0}-1),

(4)

where
h
is the relief depth and
n_{0}
is the refractive index of the grating material. The Fourier coefficient
a_{m}
for the binary phase transmission grating is

\displaystyle a_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{\text{grt}}}\int_{0}^{d_{\text{grt}}}f(x)e^{i{2\pi mx}/{d_{\text{grt}}}}dx

(5)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i\left(e^{i\varphi}\left(-1+e^{2i\pi Dm}\right)-e^{2i\pi Dm}+e^{2i\pi m}\right)}{2\pi m},

(6)

where
D=l/{d_{\text{grt}}}
is the duty factor of the grating.
The diffraction efficiency of the
m
th order is

\eta_{m}=\left|{a_{m}}\right|^{2}=\frac{4}{\pi^{2}m^{2}}{\sin^{2}(\pi mD)\sin^{2}(\varphi/2)}.

(7)

For
D=0.5
and
\varphi=\pi
, the diffraction efficiency for all even diffraction orders is 0,
a_{\pm 1}=\mp 2i/\pi
, and
\eta_{\pm 1}=40.5\%
.
Additionally, the angle of the
m
th order diffracted beam (
\xi_{m}
) with respect to the optical axis is given by the Bragg condition:

\displaystyle\sin(\xi_{m})

\displaystyle=-\frac{m\lambda}{d_{\text{grt}}}.

(8)

The
4f-

\mathcal{I}_{\text{lattice}}(x)=\frac{16\mathcal{E}^{2}_{0}}{\pi^{2}M^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{Md_{\text{grt}}}x\right),

(9)

where
M=f_{2}/f_{1}
is the magnification of the imaging system. The accordion lattice spacing is:

d_{\text{lat}}=M\frac{d_{\text{grt}}}{2}.

(10)

Note that the lattice spacing is independent of the wavelength of light used to generate the lattice.

For a grating rotated about the
y

\kappa

d_{\text{lat}}=M\frac{d_{\text{grt}}}{2}\cos{\kappa}.

(11)

Note that this rotation changes the phase

3

within
\pm 0.1

All accordion lattice implementations are susceptible to aberration-induced changes in lattice spacing over the entire extent of the lattice. To inspect large-scale lattice uniformity, we analyze the lattice spacing over the entire 2-mm lattice region by translating the MOL stage along the
x
-axis in 0.5-mm increments. We observed a minor amount of lattice spacing variation
\partial d^{\text{meas}}_{\text{lat}}/\partial x=2.05(7)
nm/mm, corresponding to a 0.2

We attribute this minor frequency chirp to aberrations in the
4f-

4
Conclusion and outlook

In this paper, we have presented a technique for generating optical accordion lattices which makes uniform, high-contrast lattice sites (
\sim 0.2
% chirp in the lattice frequency over the entire 2 mm lattice region with contrast larger than 98%)
with highly tunable lattice spacings (
0.8-2

\mu
m) and high power efficiencies (
\sim 80
%).
We propagate laser light illuminating binary phase transmission gratings through a spatially filtered plane-symmetric
4f-
imaging system to create these lattices. The lattices have a large
190:1
aspect ratio and are therefore ideal for interfacing atoms trapped in these lattices to longitudinally extended nanophotonic waveguides such as an optical nanofiber or a nanophotonic crystal waveguide. The number of lattice sites lies in the thousand range, making these lattices well-suited for exploring photon-mediated collective quantum phenomena arising from large, highly ordered arrays of atoms.
Although our current implementation uses a set of gratings with discrete spacings, grating plates with continuously varying grating spacing can be fabricated. Such a grating plate combined with fast RF beam steering can allow for dynamic tuning of the spacing of these optical accordion lattices necessary for quantum gas and quantum optics experiments.

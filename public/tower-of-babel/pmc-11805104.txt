Assessing the Different Nanoparticle-Reinforced Bonding Agents on the Shear Bond Strength of Orthodontic Brackets
Authors: Sam Paul, Sneha Surendran, Altaf Hussain Thekiya, Mariea Francis, Saloni Verma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The strength of the binding between brackets and tooth surfaces has a major impact on how well orthodontic therapy works. The shear bond strength of orthodontic brackets may be strengthened by the invention of bonding agents supplemented with nanoparticles, thanks to recent developments in nanoparticle technology.Materials and Methods:In order to perform a randomized controlled experiment, 90 human premolars were excised for orthodontic purposes. Three groups of 30 teeth were randomly assigned to each group: Group A used a bonding agent augmented with nanoparticles of titanium dioxide (TiO2), Group B used nanoparticles of zirconium dioxide (ZrO2), and Group C used a standard bonding agent as a reference. The appropriate bonding chemicals were used to attach the brackets to the teeth, and a universal testing equipment was used to measure the shear bond strength. Stereomicroscopic analysis was used to evaluate the failure mechanisms. To do statistical analysis, Tukey’s post hoc test and analysis of variance were used.Results:There were significant differences (P < 0.05) in the mean shear bond strength values across the groups. The average shear bond strength for Group A (TiO2) was 18.5 ± 2.3 MPa, that for Group B (ZrO2) was 17.2 ± 2.6 MPa, and that for Group C (control) was 14.8 ± 2.4 MPa. In comparison to Groups B and C, Group A exhibited a statistically greater bond strength, although Group B also showed an improvement over the control. According to the failure mode analysis, the control group had mostly adhesive failures, whereas the nanoparticle groups saw mixed failures.Conclusion:When TiO2 and ZrO2 nanoparticles are added to bonding agents, the shear bond strength of orthodontic brackets is greatly increased in comparison to traditional bonding agents. TiO2-reinforced materials performed better, pointing to possible benefits for orthodontic applications. It is advised that further study be done to examine the long-term impacts and therapeutic implications of these results.

INTRODUCTION

The bonding mechanism that brackets are attached to on enamel surfaces plays a critical role in the orthodontic treatment’s outcome. In order to minimize bracket failures and ensure treatment effectiveness, enough shear bond strength (SBS) is necessary to resist the varied stresses used during orthodontic treatment.[1] While conventional bonding agents have been sufficient for this purpose, developments in materials science have prompted researchers to investigate the possibility of improving bonding qualities via the use of nanoparticle technology.[23]

Because of their tiny size and large surface area, nanoparticles have special benefits when added to bonding agents. Particularly, zirconium dioxide (ZrO2) and titanium dioxide (TiO2) nanoparticles have drawn interest because of their potential to enhance mechanical characteristics including bond strength and durability while also providing antibacterial qualities.[45] TiO2 nanoparticles are well known for their high refractive index and photocatalytic activity, which may enhance the dental materials’ mechanical and cosmetic qualities.[6] In a similar vein, ZrO2 nanoparticles are known for their exceptional hardness and strength, which makes them a good choice for strengthening the binding between orthodontic brackets.[7]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Research plan

In this randomized controlled study, bonding agents enhanced with nanoparticles were used to evaluate the SBS of orthodontic brackets. The Institutional Review Board at [Institution Name] granted ethical permission for the research, and all participants or their legal guardians provided signed informed consent.

Sample setup

Ninety removed human premolars were gathered, washed, and kept at room temperature in a 0.1% thymol solution to avoid drying out. After that, the teeth were split into three groups at random (n = 30) in order to link them with various substances:

Reinforced bonding agents with nanoparticles

TiO2 or ZrO2 nanoparticles at a weight percentage of 2% were added to orthodontic bonding solutions that are sold commercially. After being created using the sol–gel technique, the nanoparticles’ size and shape were confirmed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and they were mixed with the bonding agents for 30 minutes using a magnetic stirrer to guarantee homogeneity.

Procedure for bonding

Every tooth’s enamel surface was polished using rubber cups and pumice, then washed with water, and allowed to air dry. After 30 s of etching with a 37% phosphoric acid gel, the teeth were cleaned and allowed to air dry until they took on a dazzling white look. Each tooth group received the appropriate bonding agent application, done in accordance with the manufacturer’s guidelines. After that, the buccal surfaces of the teeth were covered with metal orthodontic brackets [0.022-inch slot, (Manufacturer)], which were light-cured for 20 s using an LED curing light.

Testing the shear bond strength

Following bonding, the samples were kept for a full day at 37°C in distilled water. A universal testing apparatus (Instron 3345, Instron Corp.) was used to conduct SBS testing, with a crosshead speed of 1 mm/min. After the load at failure was measured in Newtons (N), it was divided by the bracket base area to convert it to megapascals (MPa).

Analysis of failure modes

Following SBS testing, the debonded enamel surfaces were viewed at 10x magnification using a stereomicroscope to identify the failure mechanism, which was classified as cohesive (failure inside the adhesive), adhesive (failure at the bracket–adhesive interface), or mixed.

Analytical statistics

The statistical study was carried out using IBM Corp.’s SPSS version 26.0.

Statistical analysis

The one-way ANOVA confirmed significant differences in SBS among the groups (F = 12.47, P < 0.05). Post hoc analysis using Tukey’s test revealed that the SBS of Group A was significantly higher than that of Groups B and C (P < 0.05), and the SBS of Group B was significantly higher than that of the control group (P < 0.05) [Tables 1 and 2].

DISCUSSION

The impact of bonding agents enhanced with nanoparticles on the SBS of orthodontic brackets was examined in this research. The findings showed that in comparison to the standard bonding agent, the SBS was greatly enhanced by bonding agents supplemented with nanoparticles of titanium dioxide (TiO2) and zirconium dioxide (ZrO2). Among the studied groups, TiO2-reinforced agents notably exhibited the greatest SBS.

TiO2 nanoparticles’ special physicochemical characteristics, such as their large surface area, photocatalytic activity, and capacity to enhance the mechanical qualities of the bonding resin, are responsible for the increased bond strength that has been seen with them.[12] These results are in line with earlier research that showed that adding TiO2 nanoparticles enhanced adhesive performance and raised SBS.[34] TiO2 nanoparticles have also been shown to possess antibacterial qualities, which may lessen the possibility of biofilm development around orthodontic brackets.[5]

Though not as much as TiO2, ZrO2 nanoparticles also increased SBS when compared to the control group. By strengthening the filler-matrix contact inside the bonding agent, ZrO2’s strong mechanical strength and toughness may improve bonding performance (6). Consistent with the results of this work, prior research has shown that ZrO2 nanoparticles may improve the mechanical characteristics of dental materials.[7]

CONCLUSION

This research concludes that in comparison to traditional bonding agents, TiO2 and ZrO2 nanoparticle-reinforced bonding agents considerably increase the SBS of orthodontic brackets. TiO2-reinforced materials perform better than unreinforced materials, which implies that they might have significant therapeutic benefits in orthodontics.

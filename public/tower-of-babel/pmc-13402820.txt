An improved attention guided convolutional neural network and transformer hybrid model for emotion classification in traditional Chinese paintings
Authors: Xiao Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTraditional Chinese paintings pose unique challenges for computational emotion analysis due to culturally-specific aesthetic principles that differ fundamentally from Western art paradigms. This study proposes an attention-guided CNN-Transformer hybrid model that integrates local feature extraction with global contextual modeling. The architecture employs spatial, channel, and cross-attention modules to fuse CNN-extracted brushwork details with Transformer-captured compositional relationships. Evaluated on a dataset of 7842 traditional Chinese paintings across seven emotion categories—tranquility, melancholy, vigor, elegance, desolation, joy, and solemnity—the model achieves 91.4% classification accuracy. Comparative experiments demonstrate superior performance over ResNet-101, DeiT-B, and ConvNeXt-T baselines. Ablation studies confirm the critical role of the attention-guidance module, while visualization analysis reveals alignment with traditional art theory principles. These results provide empirical support for domain-specific architectural designs in culturally-sensitive visual analysis within Han Chinese literati painting traditions, with generalizability to broader artistic domains constituting an important direction for subsequent investigation.Keywords: Traditional Chinese paintings, Emotion classification, CNN-transformer hybrid model, Attention mechanism, Cultural heritage, Digital humanitiesSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Computational science

Introduction

Traditional Chinese paintings, having aesthetically evolved over a period exceeding two millennia, are one of the most unique types of visual art in the world that carry profound philosophical ideas and cultural values that are radically different from those in Western art traditions1. This art form follows a distinct visual vocabulary, where calligraphy, poetry, and painting go hand in hand in a single artwork, invoking emotional experience through the subtle interplay of ink, brush technique, and negative space. As interest in digital humanities keeps growing, several approaches have been taken towards applying computational methods in art analysis; however, the present study focuses specifically on Han Chinese literati painting traditions (wenrenhua), which constitute the dominant scholarly and aesthetic paradigm in traditional Chinese art history spanning the Tang through Qing dynasties, while acknowledging that Chinese visual culture encompasses diverse artistic systems including Buddhist thangka painting, Dunhuang murals, folk New Year prints (nianhua), and minority ethnic artistic traditions that merit separate computational investigations beyond the scope of this work. The emotional classification of traditional Chinese paintings is quite challenging due to the culturally embedded nature of their art principles 2.

Unlike Western art traditions that focus on photorealistic representation and direct emotional expression through facial expressions and engaging narrative strategies, traditional Chinese art expresses emotion through abstract brushwork, symbols, and philosophical principle of "qi yun," literally spiritual vibration3. Use of negative space, classically called "liu bai," is not an ornamental backdrop but an essential aspect in emotional expression, in harmony with Daoist doctrine regarding emptiness and fulfillment. This inherent difference in art philosophy is a significant barrier to applying traditional computer vision methods, developed largely in response to Western paintings or photographic depictions4.

The gap between traditional art analysis methods based on cultural understanding and aesthetic judgment and current computational methods is both a challenge and an opportunity. While art historians and specialists established complex models to interpret the emotional themes conveyed in Chinese art, such approaches are inherently subjective and face major scaling challenges5. Deep-learning methods promise to span this gap by recognizing complex interrelations in data; however, precise attention is required during their design so that they can incorporate and honor culturally unique insights instead of purely analytical approaches based on Western views6.

Modern emotion classification methodologies based on photographic images or Western paintings are strongly deficient when applied in the analysis of traditional Chinese paintings. While CNNs are good at detecting localized features such as texture and edge formations, they are typically found lacking in understanding the global compositional features necessary in comprehending the emotional subtleties inherent in Chinese art traditions7. CNNs’ hierarchical feature extraction methods can sometimes ignore the complex interactions between painted objects and the voids in the surrounding spaces, which are often endowed with much the same emotional value in the context of the traditional Chinese art framework. In addition, the characteristic features of traditional paintings—such as the rich variety of brush strokes representing different degrees of emotional content, the symbolically rich meanings imparted by objects like plum blossoms embodying strength and lotus flowers representing purity, and the global compositional balance—require models well-equipped to interpret both detailed subtleties and global contextual schemes8.

Recent breakthroughs in Vision Transformers proved to be capable of capturing global dependencies and long-term relations in an image; nonetheless, applying them without any adjustments in the context of traditional Chinese paintings poses a tremendous challenge. Self-attention machinery intrinsic to Transformers excels in capturing global context while potentially missing some critical local details, like brushstrokes and gradation that are crucial in traditional paintings in China9. In addition, computational costs of unmodified pure Transformer models and their requirement for large datasets for training put practical limits on them, especially in consideration of insufficient availability of their digitized and tagged datasets in relation to Chinese paintings10.

Current emotion classification approaches exhibit specific limitations when applied to Chinese paintings. CNN-based methods capture local features effectively but fail to model global compositional relationships essential for understanding emotional expression through spatial harmony, while Vision Transformers, despite their global receptive fields, often overlook fine-grained brushwork details that convey emotional subtlety in traditional techniques. These limitations become particularly apparent when analyzing the hermeneutic paradigms in traditional Chinese art criticism. Concepts of "yi jing," or the artwork or poem’s essence beyond the literal portrayal, and the term "shen yun," referring to the spiritual harmony contained in a work of art, are inherently based in Chinese philosophical and aesthetic paradigms 11. Abstract notions, while central to the emotional understanding of art in China, are challenging to encode in traditional machine learning paradigms.

While recent hybrid architectures including ConvNeXt, CvT, and CMT attempt to address these challenges through various CNN-Transformer integration strategies, they exhibit fundamental architectural limitations rather than mere performance deficits. ConvNeXt modernizes CNN design with transformer-inspired components but operates within a fully convolutional paradigm lacking explicit self-attention mechanisms, preventing the model from capturing long-range spatial relationships essential for understanding compositional harmony in Chinese paintings where emotional expression emerges from canvas-wide element interactions. CvT incorporates convolutional operations within transformer blocks but processes all features through unified sequential layers without architectural mechanisms to distinguish and adaptively weight localized brushwork characteristics versus holistic compositional patterns, treating detailed gongbi and sparse xieyi paintings identically despite their fundamentally different feature requirement profiles. CMT employs parallel CNN and transformer branches providing separate processing pathways, yet relies on fixed-weight fusion through simple addition or concatenation, lacking adaptive modulation capabilities to dynamically adjust local–global feature importance according to painting-specific characteristics. These architectural constraints across existing hybrid methods motivate a fundamentally different approach. The core innovation of the proposed architecture lies not in increased architectural complexity but in introducing a new modeling assumption: that optimal feature fusion for culturally-specific visual analysis requires painting-adaptive weighting mechanisms rather than universal fixed combinations. This is achieved through attention-guided fusion implementing three synergistic mechanisms—spatial attention identifying emotionally salient regions, channel attention selecting culturally-relevant feature types, and bidirectional cross-attention establishing semantic correspondences between local and global representations—enabling the model to learn painting-specific weighting strategies that dynamically balance fine-grained brushwork analysis with holistic compositional understanding according to individual artwork characteristics.

To address these challenges, the current research suggests developing an innovative hybrid model that combines the strengths of both CNN and Transformer models, while also overcoming their respective weaknesses. The use of attention mechanisms is aimed at guiding the model’s focus towards emotionally relevant features that could include both subtle brush strokes as well as the overall compositional aspects. By achieving higher accuracy in emotion classification compared to prevailing approaches, this project hopes to demonstrate that culturally-aware architectural choices can significantly contribute to improved model performance. Additionally, the availability of interpretable results through attention visualization ensures that the model’s decisions can be validated by art historians and scholars, and hence foster trust and adoption in the digital humanities community12.

The key contributions of this research include the development of an attention-based hybrid model that combines CNN and Transformer components specifically formulated to support the unique qualities of traditional Chinese paintings. We provide an exhaustively constructed domain-specific dataset of Chinese paintings tagged with emotional qualities based on broadly accepted aesthetic principles, thus fulfilling the critical requirement of culturally adapted training data. In a thorough comparison with current state-of-the-art methods, we demonstrate significant improvements while maintaining model interpretability. Through attention visualization analysis, we provide insight into the model’s decision-making processes, explaining how it identifies and evaluates different components of an image according to the principles of traditional art theory.

Literature review

Traditional Chinese painting analysis

The interpretation of emotional representation in classical Chinese paintings developed through a long history of scholarly discourse and thus established a set of analytical models sharply different from those employed in Western art criticism. Classical interpretive approaches to emotion in Chinese painting focus on philosophical ideals beyond literal representation, invoking the imaginative world and atmospheric depth, in contrast to the Western tendency to classify emotions into discrete types of joy, sadness, or anger13. Aesthetic philosophy in the traditional Chinese context admits a richer range of emotional experience, formulated in terms of spiritual vibration and life-affirmation, successfully transmitting the quality of life and vital power contained in the painting through rich details of vision14.

The scope of this investigation specifically addresses Han Chinese literati painting (wenrenhua), which represents the scholarly aesthetic mainstream documented in canonical art historical texts and museum collections. Related computational work addresses other Chinese artistic traditions through domain-specific architectural designs. Recent investigations in Thangka painting analysis employ multi-attribute fusion networks combining multi-granularity features, global contextual representations, and color style characteristics through graph convolutional integration15. These approaches utilize ResNet-50 backbone networks enhanced with multi-scale feature pyramids and attention mechanisms alongside Vision Transformer modules for contextual modeling, achieving effective discrimination of subtle stylistic variations across religious iconographic traditions. Parallel developments in Dunhuang mural preservation leverage deep learning for restoration tasks fundamentally distinct from emotion classification. Structure-guided inpainting networks reconstruct damaged regions in ancient wall paintings through attention-based feature fusion16, while comprehensive AI frameworks encompass restoration, style transfer, detection, and retrieval for cultural heritage protection17. These domain-specific methodologies share architectural principles with hybrid CNN-Transformer approaches—particularly the integration of local feature extraction with global modeling and attention-guided fusion mechanisms—yet address fundamentally different analytical objectives. Thangka research prioritizes religious iconography classification and symbolic element retrieval within Tibetan Buddhist visual systems, whereas Dunhuang studies focus on physical deterioration restoration and pigment reconstruction. The literati painting emotion classification task requires distinct architectural adaptations beyond iconographic symbol detection or structural damage inpainting. Emotion analysis in Chinese literati paintings necessitates modeling abstract aesthetic principles including spatial harmony, compositional balance, and brushwork expressiveness that convey affective states through philosophical rather than religious frameworks. This methodological divergence motivates attention-guided fusion strategies designed specifically to capture emotionally salient features aligned with Confucian-Daoist aesthetic traditions. The present focus on literati traditions reflects both their art historical centrality and the availability of well-documented museum collections with scholarly annotations, providing a foundation for developing computational methods that may subsequently be adapted to other Chinese artistic systems through transfer learning and domain adaptation strategies.

The self-evident emotional pairs defined in Chinese aesthetics include tranquility and detachment, purity and softness, and dynamism and magnificence, thus constituting complex emotional pairings instead of discrete states of effect. Such pairs are rooted in the philosophical schools of Confucianism, Daoism, and Buddhism, where the importance placed on harmony in natural life and human emotions is stressed18. In contrast to Western art’s focus on psychological verity and intensified expressiveness, Chinese paintings express emotion in terms of symbols, compositional balance, and interplay of positive and negative spaces. Computer-based analysis approaches in recent times pointed to the challenges encountered in linking culturally unique emotional pairings to global emotion models, thus calling for novel methods that respect the inherent qualities of Chinese philosophical aesthetics while being computable in nature19.

Computer vision in art analysis

Computer vision methods applied in the analysis of art have evolved extensively, evolving from simple hand-designed feature approaches to advanced deep learning architectures. Early attempts in art style classification were heavily based on intrinsic perceptual features; however, most relied on color histograms, texture descriptors, and edge distributions to discriminate between different art styles and artists20. Even while traditional methods had the advantage of descriptive features, they had limitations in capturing the richer semantic and compositional features that define art style and carry emotional information.

The introduction of deep learning has radically changed the practice of emotion recognition in Western art, where convolutional neural networks have achieved state-of-the-art accuracy in detecting affective content through automatic feature learning. Studies using datasets of Western paintings have revealed that deep learning-based models are capable of distinguishing between emotions by learning hierarchical representations that capture both localized brushwork styles and overarching composition structures21. More recent models using more advanced architectures, including attention mechanisms and multi-scale feature fusion, have allowed for more fine-grained analysis of artistic elements22. The use of transfer learning from large-scale image datasets has been especially useful, allowing models to leverage generalized visual knowledge while adapting to art-specific tasks. However, most existing studies have overwhelmingly focused on datasets of Western art, overlooking non-Western artistic practices that may require different analysis strategies and feature representations23.

CNN-based approaches

CNN-based approaches for art emotion classification have shown success through hierarchical feature extraction but exhibit limitations in capturing global compositional relationships crucial for Chinese paintings24. While architectures like ResNet and EfficientNet achieve strong performance on Western art datasets25,26 , their inherent bias toward local feature extraction limits their ability to model the holistic spatial arrangements crucial for understanding emotional expression in Chinese paintings, where overall harmony and negative space carry equal emotional weight as painted elements27.

Transformer models in computer vision

Vision Transformer (ViT) marked a paradigm shift by demonstrating that pure attention-based architectures could match CNN performance when trained on sufficient data28. Self-attention mechanisms compute pairwise interactions between all spatial positions, enabling models to capture long-range relationships from early layers29. Recent applications in fine-grained classification demonstrated transformer superiority in distinguishing subtle visual differences through multi-head attention that simultaneously attends to multiple discriminative regions30. Despite impressive performance, vision transformers require larger datasets and computational resources, leading to research in efficient architectures31. Hybrid CNN-transformer models

The emergence of hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures represents a natural evolution in computer vision, seeking to combine the complementary strengths of both paradigms. Recent models such as ConvNeXt, CvT (Convolutional vision Transformer), and CMT (Convolutional Meets Transformer) demonstrate various strategies for integrating convolutional operations with self-attention mechanisms, achieving superior performance while maintaining computational efficiency32. These hybrid approaches leverage CNNs’ inductive biases for local feature extraction in early stages while employing transformers’ global modeling capabilities in deeper layers, creating a hierarchical representation that captures both fine-grained details and long-range dependencies.

The benefits of combining local and global features extend beyond simple performance gains, offering more robust and interpretable representations for complex visual tasks. Hybrid models typically exhibit better data efficiency compared to pure transformers while surpassing traditional CNNs in tasks requiring global context understanding. Applications spanning medical image analysis, remote sensing, and video understanding have demonstrated the versatility of these architectures, with particularly impressive results in scenarios requiring multi-scale feature analysis33. The flexible design space of hybrid models allows for task-specific customization, enabling researchers to balance the trade-offs between computational cost, model capacity, and domain-specific requirements while achieving state-of-the-art performance across diverse computer vision benchmarks.

Attention mechanisms

Attention mechanisms have revolutionized deep learning by enabling models to dynamically focus on relevant features, with various forms developed for different computational purposes. Spatial attention selectively emphasizes informative regions within feature maps, while channel attention weights the importance of different feature channels, and self-attention computes relationships between all positions to capture global dependencies34. These mechanisms can be combined synergistically, as demonstrated in models like CBAM (Convolutional Block Attention Module) and DANet (Dual Attention Network), which integrate both spatial and channel attention to enhance feature representation quality.

In emotion recognition tasks, attention mechanisms have proven particularly valuable by identifying emotionally salient regions and features that contribute most significantly to affective predictions. Studies have shown that attention-guided models not only achieve superior classification accuracy but also provide interpretable insights into the decision-making process through attention weight visualization35. The visualization of attention maps enables researchers to validate whether models focus on semantically meaningful regions, fostering trust and understanding in automated emotion analysis systems. This interpretability aspect is crucial for applications in sensitive domains such as mental health assessment and human–computer interaction, where understanding the reasoning behind predictions is as important as accuracy itself. The ability to visualize and analyze attention patterns bridges the gap between black-box deep learning models and explainable AI systems.

Research methodology

Dataset preparation

The construction of a comprehensive dataset for traditional Chinese painting emotion classification required systematic collection from multiple authoritative sources. We gathered high-resolution digital images from the Palace Museum Digital Archive, National Museum of China, and various provincial museum collections, ensuring representation across different dynasties and artistic schools. The dataset encompasses landscape paintings, figure paintings, and bird-and-flower paintings from the Tang, Song, Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties, totaling 8,650 unique artworks from publicly accessible museum digital collections. All paintings in the dataset are historical works in the public domain, created before 1925, and obtained from openly accessible museum digital archives for academic research purposes.

The emotion annotation process employed five expert annotators with formal training in Chinese art history. The dataset is designated as CPED-7 (Chinese Painting Emotion Dataset with 7 categories), with annotation guidelines requiring evaluators to consider compositional balance, brushwork characteristics, and symbolic elements when assigning emotion labels. Each painting was labeled with one of seven emotion categories derived from traditional Chinese aesthetic theory: tranquility, melancholy, vigor, elegance, desolation, joy, and solemnity. Inter-annotator agreement was measured using Fleiss’ kappa coefficient, achieving , indicating substantial agreement. For cases with disagreement, final labels were determined through majority voting when at least three annotators agreed, resulting in 7,842 reliably annotated paintings.

The five expert annotators possess extensive qualifications in Chinese art history and cultural studies. Three annotators hold doctoral degrees in Art History with specializations in traditional Chinese painting, averaging 12 years of research experience in emotion analysis of classical artworks. Two annotators are practicing traditional Chinese painting artists with master’s degrees in Fine Arts and over 15 years of experience in traditional painting techniques. All annotators completed a standardized training session covering the seven emotion categories, annotation criteria based on compositional elements (spatial arrangement, brushwork intensity, color tonality), and resolution protocols for ambiguous cases. During annotation, evaluators examined each painting’s visual elements including compositional balance (symmetrical versus asymmetrical arrangements), brushwork characteristics (controlled versus spontaneous execution), ink density (heavy versus light application), spatial treatment (positive space versus negative space emphasis), and symbolic motifs (natural elements and their cultural connotations). The annotation process required approximately 3–5 min per painting, with annotators encouraged to provide reasoning for their emotion assignments, which facilitated subsequent quality control and inter-annotator discussion. The two practicing artist annotators hold master’s degrees in Fine Arts, with one focusing on gongbi (meticulous) painting technique with 12 years of professional practice and another specializing in xieyi (freehand) ink painting with 10 years of artistic experience. All five annotators performed independent labeling for each painting with equal authority in the annotation process, ensuring diverse perspectives from both art historical scholarship and hands-on artistic creation. The standardized training protocol involved collaborative annotation of 50 calibration paintings with group discussion to establish shared interpretation criteria, followed by independent annotation of 30 validation paintings with subsequent agreement analysis and resolution of disagreements through majority voting when at least three annotators concurred. During the main annotation phase, annotators worked independently to minimize bias, documenting reasoning for emotion assignments to facilitate quality control and enable retrospective analysis of inter-annotator patterns.

Proposed model architecture

The proposed attention-guided CNN-Transformer hybrid model integrates complementary strengths of convolutional and transformer architectures through a sophisticated fusion mechanism designed specifically for traditional Chinese painting analysis. The architecture comprises parallel CNN and Transformer branches that process input images through distinct feature extraction pathways, connected via an attention-guided module that facilitates adaptive feature interaction. Specifically, the 448 × 448 input generates 784 patches, each embedded to 768 dimensions with positional encoding, then processed in parallel through both branches before fusion.

Figure 2 illustrates the complete architecture processing traditional Chinese paintings through four interconnected stages for emotion classification. The preprocessing module divides 448 × 448 input images into 784 non-overlapping 16 × 16 patches, applying linear projection to generate 768-dimensional embeddings combined with positional encoding that preserves spatial relationships. The dual-branch feature extraction employs parallel pathways with complementary computational paradigms. The CNN branch (upper pathway, pink blocks) performs spatial reorganization of patch embeddings into 28 × 28 × 768 feature maps, followed by modified ResNet-50 processing where the initial stem layer employs three parallel 3 × 3 convolutions with dilation rates d = 1, 2, 3 to capture multi-scale brushwork patterns, and subsequent ResNet blocks with [3,3,4,6] configuration progressively downsample features to produce 2048-dimensional representations after global average pooling, encoding local textural characteristics such as ink density variations and stroke directionality. The Transformer branch (lower pathway, blue blocks) processes position-encoded patch sequences through 12 encoder layers, each containing 12-head self-attention at 768 dimensions with layer normalization and feed-forward networks, enabling modeling of long-range compositional relationships across all patches without spatial downsampling constraints. The attention-guided fusion module (center, yellow blocks) integrates features through three complementary mechanisms: spatial attention generating location-aware weights to identify emotionally salient regions, channel attention computing feature-wise importance scores to select relevant visual characteristics, and bidirectional cross-attention establishing semantic correspondences between CNN-extracted local details and Transformer-captured global patterns. The fused 1024-dimensional representations pass through a classification head with two linear layers and dropout, producing probability distributions over seven emotion categories while enabling interpretability through attention weight visualization.

The architecture employs distinct input processing strategies for each branch to accommodate their inherent computational paradigms. The input image undergoes patch-based tokenization, divided into non-overlapping 16 × 16 patches, generating N = 784 patches from the 448 × 448 input. Each patch is linearly projected to a 768-dimensional embedding space.

For the CNN branch, these patch embeddings cannot be directly processed by convolutional operations, which require spatial feature maps rather than sequential token representations. The embeddings are spatially reorganized by reshaping the 784 patches back into their original 28 × 28 spatial grid arrangement, producing a feature map of dimensions 28 × 28 × 768. A series of convolutional projection layers then adapts this representation to match the input requirements of the ResNet backbone. The projection employs strided convolutions to achieve the desired spatial resolution and channel depth, yielding feature maps suitable for hierarchical processing through subsequent ResNet stages. Results

Experimental setup

The implementation of our attention-guided CNN-Transformer hybrid model was conducted on a high-performance computing cluster equipped with NVIDIA V100 GPUs with 32GB memory, Intel Xeon Gold 6248 CPUs with 40 cores, and 256GB RAM. The software environment consisted of Python 3.8, PyTorch 1.12.0 with CUDA 11.6, and additional libraries including torchvision 0.13.0, timm 0.6.7 for transformer implementations, and OpenCV 4.6.0 for image preprocessing. All experiments were conducted with mixed precision training using NVIDIA Apex to accelerate computation while maintaining numerical stability.

The implementation details include custom data loaders with multi-threaded preprocessing, achieving a throughput of 850 images per second during training. The CNN backbone was initialized with ImageNet pre-trained weights, while the transformer encoder and attention modules were initialized using Xavier uniform initialization with gain 1.0. Gradient accumulation over 4 steps was employed to simulate larger batch sizes within GPU memory constraints, effectively achieving a batch size of 128.

Discussion

The comprehensive evaluation of our attention-guided CNN-Transformer hybrid model reveals several key insights into emotion classification in traditional Chinese paintings. The superior performance achieved across multiple datasets, with an average improvement of 4.9% over state-of-the-art baselines, provides empirical support for the complementary value of combining local feature extraction through CNNs with global context modeling via Transformers, with the magnitude of observed gains reflecting the specific dataset composition and annotation conditions of the present study. The attention mechanisms prove particularly crucial, contributing 4.2% to the overall accuracy gain by enabling the model to dynamically focus on emotionally salient features. Visualization analysis suggests potential correspondence between learned attention patterns and traditional Chinese aesthetic principles—including the emotional significance of negative space and the expressive quality of brush strokes—though this interpretive alignment constitutes an exploratory observation given the systematic co-variation of compositional, temporal, and stylistic attributes inherent to the dataset structure.

Compared to prior work, our approach addresses fundamental limitations of single-architecture models. CNN-only methods struggle with the holistic compositional understanding essential to Chinese art, while pure Transformer models often overlook fine-grained textural details that convey emotional nuance. Previous hybrid attempts typically employed simple concatenation or addition for feature fusion, whereas our attention-guided mechanism enables adaptive, context-aware integration. The interpretability analysis reveals strong correlations between learned features and traditional art theory, with expert evaluations confirming that attention maps highlight aesthetically meaningful regions consistent with classical Chinese art criticism.

The interpretation of attention mechanisms in relation to traditional aesthetic principles adopts an exploratory framework rather than a confirmatory approach due to uncontrolled confounding factors in the dataset. Painting attributes including temporal period, stylistic school, compositional archetype, and artist identity systematically co-vary, precluding statistical isolation of individual aesthetic variables through conventional control methods such as covariate regression. Observed correlations between attention patterns and compositional characteristics documented in Tables 5 and 8 represent preliminary findings rather than definitive evidence of architectural encoding of cultural concepts such as liubai or yi-jing. Establishing causal relationships would require controlled experiments systematically manipulating individual aesthetic variables while holding confounding factors constant, investigations beyond the scope of the current cross-sectional analysis.

The practical deployment of this work requires alignment between computational demands and application contexts due to substantial hardware requirements including 142.8M parameters, 28.6 GFLOPs computation, and 23.6GB GPU memory during training. Digital humanities research represents the primary intended deployment scenario where the 4.3% accuracy improvement over baseline methods and 4.5–5.6% improvements across cross-dataset evaluations justify computational investment for large-scale retrospective analysis of emotional patterns across museum collections spanning thousands of artworks, utilizing high-performance computing infrastructure that accommodates 18.2 ms inference latency on V100 GPUs or batch processing workflows tolerating 342.5 ms CPU inference times. Museum interactive installations constitute feasible secondary deployment where consumer-grade GPUs such as RTX 3080 achieve 30 FPS near-real-time performance sufficient for visitor engagement applications including exhibition kiosks displaying emotion classifications with attention visualizations or educational displays explaining aesthetic principles through model interpretations. Edge deployment scenarios utilizing Jetson AGX hardware achieve 5–10 FPS through mixed-precision quantization and structured pruning, enabling specialized mobile applications such as portable documentation devices for field surveys or temporary exhibition installations, though reduced throughput constrains interaction paradigms. Conversely, consumer mobile applications requiring seamless real-time responsiveness remain beyond current capabilities, as CPU-only inference at 3 FPS limits practical deployment to offline batch analysis rather than interactive smartphone-based experiences, while accuracy-preserving model compression to enable mobile deployment would require substantial architectural modifications potentially degrading performance below research utility thresholds. This tiered deployment framework clarifies that computational requirements align with scholarly research and professional curation applications prioritizing classification accuracy, while acknowledging limitations for mass consumer deployment where the accuracy-efficiency trade-off currently favors computational investment for applications where reliable emotion annotations directly impact scientific conclusions or curatorial decisions rather than casual consumer experiences.

Despite these advances, several limitations warrant consideration. The current dataset focuses on Han Chinese literati painting traditions spanning the Tang through Qing dynasties within the scholarly aesthetic paradigm, and may not generalize to other Chinese artistic systems requiring fundamentally different computational approaches. Tibetan Buddhist thangka painting prioritizes religious iconography classification through multi-attribute feature fusion architectures15, whereas Dunhuang mural analysis addresses physical deterioration restoration and pigment reconstruction challenges through structure-guided inpainting and comprehensive AI frameworks16,17. These domain-specific methodologies demonstrate that each artistic system necessitates specialized architectural adaptations respecting distinct visual conventions, symbolic vocabularies, and analytical objectives beyond the emotion-focused framework developed herein. Han Dynasty stone engravings, folk New Year prints (nianhua), and contemporary ink art incorporating Western influences similarly require dedicated computational investigation. Within the literati tradition itself, the dataset may not fully capture regional school variations such as Wu School versus Zhe School landscape conventions or individual master styles that deviate from canonical aesthetic norms. The computational requirements, particularly the 23.6GB GPU memory consumption, limit deployment in resource-constrained environments. Additionally, the inherently subjective nature of emotional interpretation in art presents challenges for establishing ground truth labels, though our inter-annotator agreement of 0.783 suggests reasonable consensus.

The dataset also exhibits considerable class imbalance, with the Solemnity category comprising 4.7% of annotated samples compared to 27.4% for Tranquility; although the weighted focal loss function applied during training partially addresses this disparity, underrepresented categories remain susceptible to systematic misclassification that aggregate accuracy metrics do not fully expose. The single-label annotation protocol, while producing acceptable inter-annotator agreement, does not capture the potential co-presence of multiple emotional qualities within individual artworks, a characteristic frequently observed in classical Chinese paintings where aesthetic complexity resists discrete categorical assignment. Beyond dataset-level constraints, the attention visualization framework employed in this study does not establish causal correspondence between computational attention distributions and the aesthetic principles documented in traditional Chinese art criticism; the correlations observed between attention patterns and compositional characteristics constitute correlational observations, as controlled experimental manipulation of individual aesthetic variables was not feasible within the cross-sectional analytical design adopted here. Cross-cultural generalization similarly constitutes a boundary condition of the present framework, as the substantially reduced accuracy observed on non-Chinese artistic traditions relative to the primary evaluation domain indicates that the learned representations encode culture-specific visual patterns that do not transfer broadly across diverse artistic systems, a constraint that practitioners should consider when applying this framework beyond Han Chinese literati painting traditions and interpreting the reported performance figures as domain-specific benchmarks rather than general-purpose baselines.

Conclusion

This paper presented an attention-guided CNN-Transformer hybrid model for emotion classification in traditional Chinese paintings, addressing the unique challenges posed by culturally-specific aesthetic principles and visual characteristics. Our approach successfully integrates local feature extraction capabilities of CNNs with the global context modeling strength of Transformers through a novel attention-guidance mechanism, achieving 91.4% classification accuracy and outperforming state-of-the-art methods by an average of 4.9%. The key innovations include the multi-scale CNN architecture adapted for Chinese painting characteristics, the cross-attention mechanism enabling effective feature fusion, and the interpretable attention visualizations that suggest potential correspondence with traditional art theory principles, with the interpretive basis for such correspondence remaining subject to the dataset constraints discussed in the limitations analysis.

The impact of this work extends beyond technical improvements to advance the intersection of artificial intelligence and cultural heritage. By demonstrating that culturally-aware architectural designs can meaningfully enhance model performance within the domain examined, this study contributes a methodological reference for computational art analysis that incorporates domain-specific knowledge, with broader applicability contingent upon validation across more diverse artistic traditions. The contributions to cultural computing encompass not only the proposed technical framework but also the curated CPED-7 dataset and evaluation protocols intended to support future research in this emerging field.

Future work will focus on immediate extensions such as incorporating multi-modal information from historical texts and artist seals, as well as long-term directions including the development of culture-specific emotion taxonomies and cross-cultural comparative studies. The ultimate goal is to create AI systems that can serve as bridges between traditional cultural knowledge and modern computational methods, preserving and making accessible the rich emotional dimensions of artistic heritage for future generations while maintaining respect for cultural authenticity and scholarly rigor.

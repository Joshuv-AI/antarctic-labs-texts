On a class of involutive Yang-Baxter groups
H. Meng, A. Ballester-Bolinches, P. Jiménez-Seral

Abstract
A group is called an involutive Yang-Baxter group (IYB-group) if it is isomorphic to the permutation group of an involutive, non-degenerate set-theoretic solution of the Yang-Baxter equation. This paper investigates finite soluble groups whose Sylow subgroups have nilpotency class at most two, addressing Cedó and Okniński’s question [ 9 ] of whether such groups are IYB-groups. We establish that a finite soluble group with Sylow subgroups of class at most two is an IYB-group if its nilpotent residual is Q 8 \Q_{8} -free. We also prove that a finite soluble group with Sylow subgroups of class at most two and Sylow 2 2 -subgroups isomorphic to Q 8 Q_{8} is an IYB-group. Our approach depends on a careful study of the arithmetic and normal structure of a finite soluble group which is of independent interest. Mathematics Subject Classification (2010): 16T25, 20D10, 20F16. Keywords: left braces, Yang–Baxter equation, soluble groups, system normalisers

Introduction

The Yang-Baxter equation plays a fundamental role in mathematical physics and serves as a cornerstone in the theory of quantum groups. In
[
11
]
, Drinfeld proposed investigating set-theoretic solutions to this equation. A set-theoretic solution of the Yang–Baxter
equation is a pair
(X,r)
, where
X
is a nonempty set and
r:X\times X\rightarrow X\times X
is a map such that

r_{12}r_{23}r_{12}=r_{23}r_{12}r_{23},

where
r_{12}=r\times\Id_{X},r_{23}=\Id_{X}\times r
are maps from
X^{3}
to
X^{3}
. Subsequent research by Etingof, Schedler and Soloviev
[
12
]
, as well as Gateva-Ivanova and Van den Bergh
[
13
]
,
led to the study of a specific family of solutions known as
involutive non-degenerate set-theoretic solutions.

For all
x,y\in X
, we define two maps
f_{x}:X\longrightarrow X
and
g_{y}:X\longrightarrow X
by setting
r(x,y)=(f_{x}(y),g_{y}(x))
. We say that the solution
(X,r)
is
involutive
if
r^{2}=\text{id}_{X\times X}
, and that
(X,r)
is
non-degenerate
if
f_{x},g_{y}
are bijective maps for all
x,y\in X
. By a
solution of the YBE
, we will understand an involutive, non-degenerate set-theoretic solution of the Yang-Baxter equation.

Let
(X,r)
be a solution of the YBE. The
permutation group
of
(X,r)
is the subgroup
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
of
\text{Sym}(X)
generated by the bijections
f_{x}
for all
x\in X
, that is,

\mathcal{G}(X,r)=\langle f_{x}\mid x\in X\rangle\leq\text{Sym}(X).

A group
G
is called an
involutive Yang-Baxter group
, or an IYB-group for short, if there exists a solution
(X,r)
of the YBE such that
G\cong\mathcal{G}(X,r)
.

To further explore such solutions, Rump
[
18
]
introduced a new algebraic structure called left brace, which was extended to a non-commutative setting by Guarnieri and Vendramin in
[
14
]
.

A
skew (left) brace
is a triple
(B,+,\cdot)
where
(B,+)
and
(B,\cdot)
are groups, referred to as the additive group (non-necessarily abelian) and the multiplicative group respectively, satisfying the following compatibility condition for all
a,b,c\in B
:

a\cdot(b+c)=a\cdot b-a+a\cdot c.

Given a class of groups
\mathfrak{X}
, a skew left brace
B
is said to be of
\mathfrak{X}
-type if its additive group belongs to
\mathfrak{X}
. Rump’s left braces are exactly the skew left braces of
\mathcal{A}
-type, where
\mathcal{A}
is the class of all abelian groups.

Following
[
5
]
, a group
G
is called an
\mathfrak{X}\YB
-group if
G
is isomorphic to the multiplicative group of a skew left brace of
\mathfrak{X}
-type.

Applying
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
, it follows that a group
(G,\cdot)
is an IYB-group if and only if it can be defined a binary operation
+
on
G
such that
(G,+,\cdot)
is a left brace, that is,
(G,\cdot)
is an
\mathcal{A}\YB
-group.

If
(G,\cdot)
is a group in
\mathfrak{X}
, then we can regard
(G,+,\cdot)
as a
trivial skew left brace
of
\mathfrak{X}
-type with with the addition "+" defined by
a+b:=ab,a,b\in G.
Hence
\mathfrak{X}
is a subclass of the class of all
\mathfrak{X}\YB
-groups.

The class of all finite IYB-groups is a subclass of the class
\mathcal{S}
of all finite soluble groups (
[
12
]
) and, by a result of Bachiller (
[
4
]

. However, it includes the class of all finite nilpotent groups of class two (
[

]
), the class of all finite abelian-by-cyclic groups (

This section contains the results needed for the proof of Theorem

Let

A celebrated result of Hall states that the existence of Sylow

A set

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}:a\mapsto b;b\mapsto ab;\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \beta_{1}:a\mapsto ab;b\mapsto a;

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}:a\mapsto b;b\mapsto(ab)c;\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \beta_{2}:a\mapsto(ab)c;b\mapsto a;

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}:a\mapsto bc;b\mapsto ab;\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \beta_{3}:a\mapsto(ab)c;b\mapsto ac;

\displaystyle\alpha_{4}:a\mapsto bc;b\mapsto(ab)c;\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \beta_{4}:a\mapsto ab;b\mapsto ac;

Note that
\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i}^{2}
for each
1\leq i\leq 4
and
\Oo^{2}(A)=\langle\alpha_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq 4\rangle
.
Note that
(ab)^{y}=a^{y}b^{y}
for
y=0,1
. We can check that
\alpha_{i}\in\Aut(G,+)
for each
1\leq i\leq 4
.

Since
\alpha_{1}(a^{x}b^{y}c^{z})=b^{x}(ab)^{y}c^{z}=b^{x}a^{y}b^{y}c^{z}=a^{y}b^{x}[b^{x},a^{y}]b^{y}c^{z}=a^{y}b^{x}b^{y}c^{z+xy}=a^{y}b^{x+y}c^{xy}c^{z+xy}=a^{y}b^{x+y}c^{z}
for
x,y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(a^{x_{1}}b^{y_{1}}c^{z_{1}}+a^{x_{2}}b^{y_{2}}c^{z_{2}})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}(a^{x_{1}+x_{2}}b^{y_{1}+y_{2}}c^{z_{1}+z_{2}})

\displaystyle=a^{y_{1}+y_{2}}b^{x_{1}+x_{2}+y_{1}+y_{2}}c^{z_{1}+z_{2}}

\displaystyle=a^{y_{1}}b^{x_{1}+y_{1}}c^{z_{1}}+a^{y_{2}}b^{x_{2}+y_{2}}c^{z_{2}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}(a^{x_{1}}b^{y_{1}}c^{z_{1}})+\alpha_{1}(a^{x_{2}}b^{y_{2}}c^{z_{2}}).

Hence
\alpha_{1}\in\Aut(G,+,\cdot)
. Similarly, for
x,y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}(a^{x}b^{y}c^{z})

\displaystyle=b^{x}(abc)^{y}c^{z}=a^{y}b^{x+y}c^{z+y}

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}(a^{x}b^{y}c^{z})

\displaystyle=(bc)^{x}(ab)^{y}c^{z}=a^{y}b^{x+y}c^{z+x}

\displaystyle\alpha_{4}(a^{x}b^{y}c^{z})

\displaystyle=(bc)^{x}(abc)^{y}c^{z}=a^{y}b^{x+y}c^{z+x+y}

It can be directly verified that
\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\in\Aut(G,+,\cdot)
as well. Hence such IYB-structure on
\Q_{8}
is
\Oo^{2}(A)
-equivariant and the proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Since

is soluble and all Sylow subgroups of

have nilpotency class at most two, by Theorem
12
and Lemma

,
G
has an
\mathcal{N}
-decomposition

such that

for each

.
Write
H_{i}=A_{i+1}A_{i+2}\cdots A_{k}
for each

.
We will apply Theorem
C
for

A_{i}
has an
H_{i}
-equivariant IYB-structure
(A_{i},+,\cdot)

Let

2

by Lemma

. Fixed some

. If

, it implies that
P_{i}

3
Some corollaries: skew braces of
\mathcal{N}
-type.

In this section we show that
[
9
, Theorem 3.2,Theorem 3.5 ]
are natural consequences of our results.

Let
(B,+,\cdot)
a finite skew left brace of
\mathcal{N}
-type. Then
(B,+)
is nilpotent and so it has a
\lambda
-invariant Hall
\pi
-subgroup for all set of primes
\pi
. In particular,
(B,\cdot)
has Hall
\pi
-subgroups for all set of primes
\pi
. By
[
10
, Theorems I.3.3 and I.3.5]
),
(B,\cdot)
is soluble. Hence every finite
\mathcal{N}\YB
-group is soluble (see
[
7
]
).

Corollary 19
.

Let
G
be a group with the Sylow tower property. Then
G
is an
\mathcal{N}\YB
-group.

Proof.

Since
G
is a group with the Sylow tower property, we may assume that
G=A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{k}
with
\pi(G)=\{p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{k}\}
, where
A_{i}
is a Sylow
p_{i}
-subgroup of
G
such that
A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{i}\unlhd G
and
A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{i}\cap A_{i+1}\cdots A_{k}=1
. Since
A_{i}
is nilpotent, we can get
(A_{i},+,\cdot)
the trivial skew brace of nilpotent type, which implies that
A_{i}
has fully equivariant
\mathcal{N}\YB
-structure
(A_{i},+,\cdot)
with
\Ker(A_{i})=A_{i}
, moreover,
\Z(A_{i})=\Z(A_{i},+)=\Z(A_{i},+)\cap\Ker(A_{i})=\Soc(A_{i})
.
Applying Theorem
C
,
G
is an
\mathcal{N}\YB
-group.
∎

Corollary 20
.

Let
G
be a soluble group with Sylow subgroups
of nilpotency class at most
2
. Then
G
is a
\mathcal{N}\YB
-group.

Proof.

We can apply Theorem
12

15

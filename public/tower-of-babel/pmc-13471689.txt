Prenatally detected fetus in fetu with progressive imaging organization: Multimodality radiology-pathology correlation
Authors: Connor Latterman, Joseph Junewick
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFetus in fetu is a rare congenital anomaly that may mimic other suprarenal masses including neuroblastoma, teratoma, or adrenal hemorrhage. We present a case of prenatally detected fetus in fetu recognized at 26 weeks gestation as a cystic suprarenal mass. Serial imaging with fetal ultrasounds and MRI at 29 weeks and follow-up ultrasound at 34 weeks demonstrated progression from a nonspecific cystic mass to a lesion with increasing internal organization. Postnatal MRI demonstrated a vertebral axis, a highly characteristic feature of fetus in fetu, and the diagnosis was confirmed by surgical pathology and postoperative radiography. The infant had an uncomplicated postoperative course. This case highlights the value of serial prenatal imaging in demonstrating progressive organization and eventual recognition of a vertebral axis, assisting in distinction of fetus in fetu from other fetal suprarenal masses and guiding appropriate treatment.Keywords: Fetus in fetu, Prenatal diagnosis, Fetal MRI, Multimodality imaging

Introduction

Fetus in fetu is a rare congenital anomaly characterized by an abnormal embryologic development of monozygotic, diamniotic twins, with an estimated incidence of 1 in 500,000 births [[1], [2], [3]]. Although the exact pathogenesis remains debated, the most widely accepted theory proposes abnormal monozygotic diamniotic twinning with incorporation of the smaller embryo into the developing fetus [4]. It most commonly presents as a retroperitoneal mass detected in infancy or early childhood but may be detected prenatally on ultrasound or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).

Prenatal suprarenal masses include a broad list of differentials including teratoma, neuroblastoma, and adrenal hemorrhage, often presenting similarly to fetus in fetu on prenatal ultrasound and MRI. Accurate differentiation is critical as management ranges from observation to oncologic workup to surgical excision.

We present a case of prenatally detected fetus in fetu with multimodality imaging, demonstrating progressive development of internal organization, culminating in identification of a vertebral axis. The case highlights key imaging features that may allow for earlier diagnosis of fetus in fetu from other similarly presenting suprarenal masses and provides radiology-pathology correlation including a postoperative specimen radiograph, infrequently documented in the literature. While cases of fetus in fetu have previously been described, serial prenatal and postnatal imaging with progressive structural organization in conjunction with postoperative specimen radiographs remain limited in the literature. The objective of this report is to demonstrate the evolving prenatal imaging appearance of fetus in fetu across serial ultrasound and MRI examinations and correlate these findings with surgical pathology and postoperative radiography.

Case presentation

Routine 20-week anatomy ultrasound in a 32-year-old gravida 2 para 1 patient found no abnormalities. At 26 weeks, ultrasound identified a right suprarenal cystic mass measuring 3.9 × 2.9 × 3.3 cm with an internal mural nodule displacing the kidney inferiorly (Fig. 1). Targeted fetal ultrasonography was performed using grayscale and color Doppler imaging. No vascularity was identified inside the lesion. The presence of a mural nodule in a cystic mass broadens the differential beyond adrenal hemorrhage and raises suspicion for a neoplastic or complex developmental process. Initial differential diagnosis included adrenal hemorrhage and fetal neuroblastoma. Fetal MRI was performed at 29 weeks gestation using multiplanar T2-weighted HASTE sequences, multiplanar TRUFISP sequences, T1-weighted VIBE imaging, axial T1 gradient-echo imaging, and diffusion-weighted imaging targeted to the fetal abdomen. Imaging confirmed a complex, encapsulated retroperitoneal mass measuring 4.7 × 4.4 × 3.0 cm (Fig. 2). The lesion was cystic peripherally, with a complex central component with marked T2 hypointensity. The liver was displaced anteriorly and the right kidney posteriorly.

Fetal MRI at 29 weeks gestation. Sagittal (A) and coronal (B) T2-weighted HASTE images of the right retroperitoneum demonstrate a complex, encapsulated cystic mass with a central T2 hypointense component (white arrows).

Follow-up ultrasound at 34 weeks demonstrated strong acoustic shadowing of the central component (Fig. 3). The patient delivered at 36 weeks 5 days, complicated by preeclampsia. A live-born infant with weight 2.86 kg and Apgars of 8 and 9 was delivered by C-section. Postnatal MRI confirmed a mass measuring 6.5 × 5.3 × 5.5 cm characterized by fetiform tissues surrounded by fluid within a sac-like capsule (Fig. 4). The fetiform tissue demonstrated a vertebral axis, a finding characteristic of fetus in fetu. Excision via laparotomy on day 1 of life removed the mass without complications (Fig. 5).

Prenatal ultrasound at 34 weeks gestation. Transverse (A) and longitudinal (B) grayscale images reveals development of strong acoustic shadowing (white arrow), suggesting calcification or ossification.

Postnatal MRI obtained on day 1 of life. Coronal T2-weighted image demonstrates a 6.5 × 5.3 × 5.5 cm encapsulated cystic mass with complex internal structure. Central hyperintense structure represents a possible vertebral axis (white arrow), a characteristic feature of fetus in fetu.

Intraoperative photographs during laparotomy. Initial exposure of the mass arising from the right suprarenal region (A) and subsequent dissection reveals a thinly encapsulated mass with lack of invasion into adjacent bowel or great vessels (B).

Gross pathology showed an encapsulated mass with internal cranio-caudal organization (Fig. 6). Postoperative specimen radiology demonstrated curvilinear bony segments forming a vertebral axis with rudimentary bony limb elements (Fig. 7). Recovery was uneventful with normal follow-up imaging.

Gross pathology specimen. External appearance of the encapsulated mass (A). Internal complex structure showing rudimentary cranial-caudal organization with recognizable fetal features, supporting partial embryological development (B).

Postoperative specimen radiograph. Curvilinear ossified segments form a vertebral axis (white arrow) with rudimentary bony features of limbs (yellow arrow), supporting the diagnosis of fetus in fetu and distinguishing it from teratoma.

Discussion

Fetus in fetu is thought to arise during monozygotic diamniotic twin development when unequal division of the totipotent inner cell mass of the blastocyst leads to inclusion of a smaller cell mass inside the maturing sister embryo [5]. This results in partial organogenesis and varying degrees of axial skeleton development.

Fetal suprarenal masses have a broad differential diagnosis including neuroblastoma, teratoma, and organizing hematoma. Neuroblastoma is the most common fetal suprarenal mass and typically presents as a solid or heterogeneous mass, frequently with areas of calcification and internal vascularity [6]. In contrast, organizing hematomas may appear echogenic on ultrasound with progression to a cystic lesion over time, lacking internal vascularity. Although neuroblastoma and adrenal hemorrhage may demonstrate interval changes on serial imaging, neither develops structural organization or fetal anatomy.

Teratoma may be difficult to distinguish from fetus in fetu. Teratomas may appear as mixed cystic and solid masses containing fat, calcification, and/or fluid; however, unlike fetus in fetu, teratomas do not have a well-defined vertebral axis or recognizable fetal structures [7]. While both entities may contain calcified components, fetus in fetu presents with a higher degree of embryological organization, including axial skeletal development.

Compared with hematoma, teratoma, and neuroblastoma, fetus in fetu is uniquely characterized by progressive development of recognizable fetal structures with eventual identification of a vertebral axis. The progressive appearance of increased organized structure across serial prenatal imaging may provide a clue to the diagnosis before a vertebral axis can be confidently identified.

In this case, serial prenatal and postnatal imaging played a crucial role in narrowing the differential diagnosis. Initial ultrasound demonstrated a cystic suprarenal mass with a mural nodule. Lack of vascularity on prenatal Doppler evaluation decreased suspicion for neuroblastoma. Subsequent fetal MRI demonstrated a complex internal mass with marked T2 hypointensity, while follow-up ultrasound at 34 weeks showed acoustic shadowing. This suggested ossification. This finding raised suspicion for internal structural organization rather than the expected evolution of an adrenal hemorrhage. Ultimately, postnatal MRI demonstrated a vertebral axis with fetiform soft tissue components. For radiologists examining a prenatal suprarenal mass, absence of Doppler vascularity, interval development of acoustic shadowing suggesting ossification, and progressive development of internal structures should raise suspicion for fetus in fetu, especially once a vertebral axis is confidently identified. The presence of a vertebral axis remains the most reliable feature to distinguish fetus in fetu from teratoma, representing true embryological organization rather than random tissue proliferation [8].

Awareness of fetus in fetu is important because it may prevent unnecessary oncologic evaluation or treatment. Unlike neuroblastoma and some teratomas, fetus in fetu is usually benign and surgical excision is curative [9]. Although prognosis is excellent, rare cases of fetus in fetu with malignant reoccurrence have been reported in the literature and clinical or imaging follow-up may be considered following resection [9]. In this case, surgical resection was uncomplicated and the patient had a benign postoperative course and continues routine clinical follow-up with her pediatrician.

Like previously reported prenatal cases, this lesion demonstrated progressive structural organization over serial imaging. However, the combination of serial prenatal ultrasound, fetal MRI, postnatal MRI, pathology, and specimen radiography provides a comprehensive demonstration of the evolution of diagnostic imaging findings, specifically the vertebral axis.

This case is notable for its comprehensive imaging evaluation, including serial prenatal ultrasounds, prenatal MRI, postnatal MRI, intraoperative findings, and a postoperative radiograph (Table 1). The ability to demonstrate progressive internal structure development throughout multiple imaging modalities and an eventual vertebral axis provides a unique illustration of the diagnostic features of fetus in fetu. Additionally, this progression underscores the value of serial imaging in distinguishing fetus in fetu from other diagnoses that may initially appear similar but lack increasing organization over time. Recognition of these imaging features may allow for earlier and more confident diagnosis in similar cases.

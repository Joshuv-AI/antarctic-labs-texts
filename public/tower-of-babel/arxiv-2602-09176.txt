Abstract
We consider finite blocklength lossy compression of information sources whose components are independent but non-identically distributed. Crucially, Gaussian sources with memory and quadratic distortion can be cast in this form. We show that under the operational constraint of exceeding distortion d d with probability at most ϵ \epsilon , the minimum achievable rate at blocklength n n satisfies R ⁡ ( n , d , ϵ ) = ℝ n ​ ( d ) + 𝕍 n ​ ( d ) n ​ Q − 1 ​ ( ϵ ) + O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n n ) R(n,d,\epsilon)=\mathbb{R}_{n}(d)+\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}_{n}(d)}{n}}Q^{-1}(\epsilon)+O\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right) , where Q − 1 ​ ( ⋅ ) Q^{-1}(\cdot) is the inverse Q Q -function, while ℝ n ​ ( d ) \mathbb{R}_{n}(d) and 𝕍 n ​ ( d ) \mathbb{V}_{n}(d) are fundamental characteristics of the source computed using its n n -letter joint distribution and the distortion measure, called the n n th-order informational rate-distortion function and the source dispersion, respectively. Our result generalizes the existing dispersion result for abstract sources with i.i.d. components. It also sharpens and extends the only known dispersion result for a source with memory, namely, the scalar Gauss-Markov source. The key novel technical tool in our analysis is the point-mass product proxy measure, which enables the construction of typical sets. This proxy generalizes the empirical distribution beyond the i.i.d. setting by preserving additivity across coordinates and facilitating a typicality analysis for sums of independent, non-identical terms.



I
Introduction

Rate-distortion theory studies the minimum coding rate required for data compression when imperfect reproduction within a distortion level
d
is permitted. In the asymptotic regime where the blocklength
n
tends to infinity, Shannon
[
12
]
proved a coding theorem for discrete memoryless sources, showing that the fundamental operational limit equals the informational

)

n

[
5
,
2

The classical results above characterize optimal performance as the blocklength
n
grows without bound, but they do not directly quantify the operational limits at finite blocklength, where practical compression schemes operate. This motivates finite blocklength analyses that quantify the convergence rate of the optimal coding rate to the
rdf

[
16

characterized the redundancy for finite-alphabet
iid

iid

[
15
]

[

established refined asymptotics for the pointwise redundancy of abstract sources in terms of distortion
d
-ball probabilities. For finite-alphabet

sources, second-order asymptotics were derived by Ingber and Kochman
[
8
]
, and for abstract
iid
sources by Kostina and Verdú
[
11
]
. Second-order asymptotic analyses show that the minimum achievable rate
R(n,d,\epsilon)
at blocklength
n
under the operational constraint of exceeding distortion
d
with probability at most

[

,
8

, and the second term quantifies the convergence rate to
\mathbb{R}(d)
through the source characteristic termed source dispersion. For sources with memory, the finite blocklength analysis remains limited to the work of Tian and Kostina on stationary and nonstationary Gauss-Markov sources
[
13
,
14
]
. See
[
17
]
for a recent monograph on finite blocklength results in lossy compression.

In this paper, we extend the second-order asymptotic analysis of
[

]

[
13
,
14
]
beyond the scalar Gauss-Markov source. We consider sources whose components are independent but not necessarily identically distributed, and, like most prior work in rate-distortion theory, we consider separable distortion measures. Our framework covers Gaussian sources under squared error distortion since any Gaussian source can be decorrelated to obtain independent components via an orthogonal transform preserving Euclidean distances. We show that the minimum achievable rate admits a Gaussian approximation

R(n,d,\epsilon)=\mathbb{R}_{n}(d)+\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}_{n}(d)}{n}}\,Q^{-1}(\epsilon)+O\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right),

(1)

where

[

II
Problem Setup

II-A

Operational Definitions

We study sources with independent but not necessarily identical components. Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be independent random variables with
X_{i}\sim P_{X_{i}}
, each taking values in the alphabet
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
. For each blocklength
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the source block
{\boldsymbol{X}}\triangleq(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{X}}}
, where the product alphabet is
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{X}}}\triangleq\prod_{i=1}^{n}{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}}
and the joint distribution factorizes as
P_{{\boldsymbol{X}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}{P_{X_{i}}}
. The reproduction sequence takes values in the product alphabet
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{Y}}}\triangleq\prod_{i=1}^{n}{{\mathcal{Y}}_{i}}
. The discrepancy between the source and its reproduction is measured via single-letter distortion functions
\mathsf{d}_{i}(\cdot,\cdot)\colon{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times{\mathcal{Y}}_{i}\mapsto[0,+\infty]
, which induce the separable block distortion
\mathsf{d}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathsf{d}_{i}(x_{i},y_{i})}
.

Definition 1
.

For a fixed blocklength
n\in\mathbb{N}
, distortion threshold
d\geq 0
, and excess distortion probability
\epsilon\in[0,1)
, an
(n,M,d,\epsilon)
code consists of an encoder
{\mathsf{f}}\colon\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{X}}}\mapsto\{1,\ldots,M\}
and a decoder
{\mathsf{g}}\colon\{1,\ldots,M\}\mapsto\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{Y}}}
such that

\mathbb{P}\left[{\mathsf{d}({\boldsymbol{X}},{\mathsf{g}}({\mathsf{f}}({\boldsymbol{X}})))>d}\right]\leq\epsilon

(2)

The rate associated with an
(n,M,d,\epsilon)
code is
R\triangleq\frac{\log M}{n}.

The minimum achievable code size for given blocklength
n
, distortion
d
, and excess distortion probability
\epsilon\in[0,1)
is

M^{\star}(n,d,\epsilon)\triangleq\min\{M\colon\exists\text{ an }(n,M,d,\epsilon)\text{ code}\},

(3)

and the corresponding minimum source coding rate is

R(n,d,\epsilon)\triangleq\frac{\log M^{\star}(n,d,\epsilon)}{n}.

(4)

II-B

Informational Definitions

For a given source distribution and distortion measure, the
n
th-order informational
rdf
is

\mathbb{R}_{n}(d)\triangleq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{{\boldsymbol{Y}}|{\boldsymbol{X}}}\colon\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{X}}}\mapsto\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{Y}}}\\
\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{d}({\boldsymbol{X}},{\boldsymbol{Y}})]\leq d\end{subarray}}\kern 5.0pt\frac{1}{n}I({\boldsymbol{X}};{\boldsymbol{Y}}).

(5)

Here,
I({\boldsymbol{X}};{\boldsymbol{Y}})
denotes the mutual information, and the infimum is over all conditional distributions
P_{{\boldsymbol{Y}}|{\boldsymbol{X}}}
satisfying the distortion constraint. We define

d_{\min}\triangleq\inf\{d\colon\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{R}_{n}(d)<\infty\}.

(6)

Throughout the paper, we impose the following restrictions:

(I)

The infimum in (
5
) is achieved by a unique conditional distribution
P_{{\boldsymbol{Y^{\ast}}}|{\boldsymbol{X}}}
.

(II)

The distortion level satisfies
d>d_{\min}
.

We refer to
{\boldsymbol{Y^{\ast}}}
as the
rdf
-achieving reproduction random vector. Under restrictions
(I)
-
(II)
,
differentiation of
\mathbb{R}_{n}(d)
with respect to
d
is justified
[
11
, Eq. 16]
, and we define
\lambda_{n}^{\ast}=-\mathbb{R}_{n}^{\prime}(d)
. The associated tilted information density introduced in
[
11
, Def. 6]
is defined for
d>d_{\min}
and any
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{X}}}
as

\jmath({\boldsymbol{x}},d)\triangleq-\lambda_{n}^{\ast}nd-\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\lambda_{n}^{\ast}n\mathsf{d}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{Y^{\ast}}})\right)\right],

(7)

where the expectation is with respect to the marginal distribution
P_{{\boldsymbol{Y^{\ast}}}}
. The
d
-tilted information is the key quantity appearing in the nonasymptotic bounds in
[
11
]
, which are also the starting point of our analysis. Since the joint distribution
P_{{\boldsymbol{X}}}P_{{\boldsymbol{Y^{\ast}}}|{\boldsymbol{X}}}
factorizes and the distortion is separable, the
d
-tilted information single-letterizes as

\jmath({\boldsymbol{x}},d)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\jmath(x_{i},d_{i}),\kern 5.0pt\text{where}\kern 5.0ptd_{i}=\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{d}_{i}(X_{i},Y_{i}^{\ast})].

(8)

Furthermore, it satisfies
[
4
]

\mathbb{R}_{n}(d)=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[{\jmath({\boldsymbol{X}},d)}\right].

(9)

Next, we introduce a related variational problem first introduced by Blahut
[
3
]
, in which the reproduction marginal is fixed in advance. This decouples the choice of the output distribution from the choice of the kernel in (
5
). Specifically, for a given source distribution
P_{{\boldsymbol{X}}}
and a fixed reproduction distribution
P_{{\boldsymbol{Y}}}
, define

\mathbb{R}_{n}({\boldsymbol{X}},{\boldsymbol{Y}},d)\triangleq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{{\boldsymbol{Z}}|{\boldsymbol{X}}}\colon\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{X}}}\mapsto\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{Y}}}\\
\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{d}({\boldsymbol{X}},{\boldsymbol{Z}})]\leq d\end{subarray}}\kern 5.0pt\frac{1}{n}D(P_{{\boldsymbol{Z}}|{\boldsymbol{X}}}\|P_{\boldsymbol{Y}}|P_{\boldsymbol{X}}).

(10)

Here,
D(P_{{\boldsymbol{Z}}|{\boldsymbol{X}}}\|P_{\boldsymbol{Y}}|P_{\boldsymbol{X}})
denotes the conditional relative entropy, and the optimization in (
10
) upper bounds that in (
5
), with equality if and only if
P_{\boldsymbol{Y}}=P_{\boldsymbol{Y^{\ast}}}
.
We introduce the corresponding tilted information analogously to (
7
): for
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{X}}}
and
\lambda>0
, we define
[
11
]

J_{\boldsymbol{Y}}({\boldsymbol{x}},\lambda)\triangleq-\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\lambda n\mathsf{d}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{Y}})\right)\right],

(11)

where the expectation is with respect to the reproduction marginal. Letting
\lambda_{{\boldsymbol{X}},{\boldsymbol{Y}}}^{\ast}\triangleq-\mathbb{R}^{\prime}_{n}({\boldsymbol{X}},{\boldsymbol{Y}},d)
, we obtain

\mathbb{R}_{n}({\boldsymbol{X}},{\boldsymbol{Y}},d)=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[{J_{\boldsymbol{Y}}({\boldsymbol{X}},\lambda_{{\boldsymbol{X}},{\boldsymbol{Y}}}^{\ast})}\right]-\lambda_{{\boldsymbol{X}},{\boldsymbol{Y}}}^{\ast}d.

(12)

Choosing
P_{\boldsymbol{Y}}=P_{\boldsymbol{Y^{\ast}}}
in (
10
) yields
\jmath({\boldsymbol{x}},d)=-\lambda_{n}^{\ast}nd+J_{\boldsymbol{Y^{\ast}}}({\boldsymbol{x}},\lambda_{n}^{\ast})
.

The second moment of the
d
-tilted information plays a central role in the nonasymptotic analysis. The informational dispersion is defined as

\mathbb{V}_{n}(d)\triangleq\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Var}[\jmath({\boldsymbol{X}},d)].

(13)

We also define the limiting
rdf
and source dispersion as
\mathbb{R}(d)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{R}_{n}(d)
,
\mathbb{V}(d)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{V}_{n}(d)
.

III
Main Results

We consider sources satisfying the following regularity assumptions:

(i)

The dispersion is uniformly bounded away from zero for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

0<\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{V}_{n}(d).

(14)

(ii)

The distortion level satisfies
d\in(d_{\min},d_{\max})
, where
d_{\min}
is defined in (
6
) and

\bar{d}_{n}\triangleq\inf_{{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{Y}}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathsf{d}({\boldsymbol{X}},{\boldsymbol{y}})}\right],\qquad d_{\max}\triangleq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{d}_{n}.

(15)

(iii)

There exists a constant
K_{0}<\infty
such that, for all sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{d}_{i}^{6}(X_{i},Y_{i}^{\ast})\right]\leq K_{0},

(16)

where the expectation is with respect to
P_{X_{i}}\times P_{Y_{i}^{\ast}}

(iv)

There exist constants
\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1}>0
and
\eta>0
such that, for all sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and all
\lambda\in(\lambda_{n}^{\ast}-\eta,\lambda_{n}^{\ast}+\eta)

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n}\bigl|J_{{\boldsymbol{Y^{\ast}}}}^{\prime\prime}({\boldsymbol{X}},\lambda)\bigr|\right]\geq\kappa_{0},\hskip 5.0pt\Var\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}J_{{\boldsymbol{Y^{\ast}}}}^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{X}},\lambda_{n}^{\ast})\right]\geq\kappa_{1}

(17)

where
(\cdot)^{\prime}
denotes differentiation with respect to
\lambda

While the assumptions on the distortion level and bounded moments are standard in the literature

Theorem 1

(Gaussian approximation)
.

Consider a source
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
with independent but not identically distributed components under a separable distortion measure. Suppose that assumptions
(i)
–
(iv)
hold. Then for every
\epsilon\in(0,1)

R(n,d,\epsilon)=\mathbb{R}_{n}(d)+\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}_{n}(d)}{n}}Q^{-1}(\epsilon)+O\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right).

(18)

In the

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we extend the second-order rate-distortion analysis of Kostina and Verdú
[
11
]
from
iid
sources to sources with independent but non-identically distributed components (Theorem
1
). Independence of the source components and separability of the distortion enable single-letterization of the
d
-tilted information, which allows us to invoke the Berry–Esseen theorem. For Gaussian sources with memory satisfying regularity conditions, Corollary
2
shows that the second-order term can be computed explicitly from the finite-dimensional eigenspectrum, recovering the familiar reverse water-filling expressions. In Corollary
3
, we derive a second-order approximation for the stationary Gauss–Markov source in terms of the limiting
rdf
and the source dispersion, with a smaller order remainder term than that in
[
13
]
. Our main technical novelty appears in the achievability proof, where we introduce a point-mass product proxy measure to facilitate the typicality arguments for sources with non-identical components.

Future work includes relaxing the regularity assumptions, verifying them for broader classes of sources beyond the stationary Gauss-Markov source, such as general autoregressive Gaussian sources, and developing a systematic treatment of the zero dispersion regime.

Abnormal fat distribution in two patients with RAB7A-associated Charcot-Marie-Tooth type 2B: a case report
Authors: Louise Sloth Kodal, Tina Dysgaard, Nanna Witting
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCharcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2B (CMT2B) is an autosomal dominant neuropathy associated with pathogenic variants in the RAB7A gene, which encodes a GTPase involved in lysosomal and lipid metabolism. Although altered lipid metabolism has been observed in skin fibroblasts derived from patients with CMT2B, a clinical presentation with abnormal fat distribution has not been reported.Case presentationWe report two sisters with a confirmed pathogenic RAB7A variant (NM_004637.6:c.484G > A, (p.Val162Met)), who presented not only with classic CMT2B features but also with abnormal fat distribution, including excessive fat accumulation in the neck, upper back, and abdomen. Both patients underwent neurological evaluations, nerve conduction studies, and extensive endocrine workup, which ruled out other metabolic or endocrine causes. MRI confirmed atypical fat deposition. One patient underwent liposuction due to functional impairment with partial relief, but fat accumulation recurred.ConclusionOur case suggests that abnormal fat distribution may be a novel phenotypic feature in individuals with RAB7A-related CMT2B. Recognizing this manifestation may provide clinical insight into the lipid dysregulation and systemic effects in patients with CMT2B.

Introduction

Charcot-Marie-Tooth (CMT) encompasses a group of hereditary motor and sensory peripheral polyneuropathies characterized by progressive muscle weakness and sensory loss. CMT type 2B (CMT2B) is an axonal subtype caused by a pathogenic variant in the RAB7A gene [1, 2]. It encodes a guanosine triphosphatase (GTPase) essential for lysosomal transport, phagolysosome fusion, and lipophagy [3].

Clinical manifestations of CMT2B typically include distal muscle atrophy and sensory deficits [4]. Recent studies have suggested that RAB7A may influence lipid metabolism, and one study demonstrated that fibroblasts from patients with CMT2B accumulate lipid droplets [5]. Despite these cellular findings, no clinical reports have described abnormal body fat distribution in patients with RAB7A-associated CMT. Here, we present two sisters with genetically confirmed CMT2B who developed abnormal fat distribution, a phenotype not previously reported in association with this condition. This case report presents two sisters with a confirmed pathogenic variant in RAB7A and expands the clinical spectrum of CMT2B, highlighting the importance of considering metabolic features in patients with hereditary polyneuropathy.

Case presentation

Patient A

A woman in her thirties presented with clumsiness and unsteady ankles since childhood, and an inability to run like her peers. She reported a longstanding issue with recognizing temperature, notably mistaking cold water for lukewarm. For years, she had complained of lukewarm water, but one day, when she splashed water on her elbow, she found it to be ice cold. She reported rapid weight gain and progressive fat accumulation in the neck, upper back, and abdomen, noticed since age seven, where a doctor noted excessive fat around the neck despite the patient's normal weight. At BMI 25, she underwent liposuction in public care due to functional impairment, including neck pain and difficulty lying supine and as the fat accumulation was considered disproportionate to the patient’s body weight. The fat accumulation recurred postoperatively despite her stable weight.

She underwent multiple endocrinologic assessments, most recently at age 35, due to suspected Cushing’s syndrome based on buffalo hump, abnormal fat deposition, and abdominal striae. All test results, including TSH (1.13 mIU/L), oral glucose tolerance test, HbA1c (36 mmol/mol), and repeated 24-h urinary cortisol test (48 and 59 nmol/d), were normal and endocrine causes were ruled out. Neurological examination revealed slender ankles, bilateral pes cavus, distal sensory loss, decreased vibration at the tibial tuberosity, and areflexia at the Achilles. Muscle strength was preserved. The patient scored six out of 30 points on the CMT Examination Score (CMTES), with zero indicating no symptoms and 28 indicating the most severe symptoms, including the inability to walk [6].

MRI of Patient A confirmed abnormal fat deposition. The accumulation was most prominent around the neck and upper back, with relative sparing of the abdomen and thighs. Patient B

Her sister, also in her thirties, had a similar childhood history of clumsiness and inability to run. She had less pronounced fat accumulation than her sister, yet exhibited regional deposits that prevented her from touching the back of her head to the floor when lying down. Her primary complaint was persistent pain with a sensation of pins and needles, as well as hyperesthesia in both feet.

On neurological examination, Patient B exhibited distal muscle weakness in the hands and feet (Medical Research Council (MRC) Scale = 4), as well as distal sensory deficits with decreased pinprick sensation at the ankle level and reduced vibration sense at the great toe. Bilateral pes cavus and a positive Romberg sign were also found. The total CMTES score was 4. Nerve conduction study (NCS) showed a distal symmetric sensorimotor axonal polyneuropathy, most pronounced in the lower extremities. Peak amplitude in the peroneal nerve was 0.2 mV (−9.6 standard deviation (SD) from the normal mean) in the left and 0.7 mV (−6.3 SD from the normal mean) in the right. There was no sensory responses from either peroneal nerve. Tibial nerves also showed severely reduced amplitude with 0.7 mV (−7.9 SD from the normal mean) in the left and 0.8 mV (−7.4 SD from the normal mean) in the right. Sensory response from sural nerves showed severely reduced amplitudes (left: 1,2 µV (- 6 SD from the normal mean), right: 1,3 µV (−5.7 SD from the normal mean)) and dispersed waveform. Median nerve motor conduction velocity was 62 m/s. NCS results were in line with the phenotype and genotype of CMT2B.

Genetic testing using Sanger sequencing confirmed the presence of the heterozygous pathogenic RAB7A variant c.484G > A, (p.Val162Met) (NM_004637.6) in both patients, with their mother as proband, consistent with autosomal dominant sensory-motor CMT2B. Pathogenic variants in MFN2, LMNA, and other common CMT genes were excluded using a targeted gene panel. The patients' mother and maternal uncle also carried the heterozygous RAB7A variant and had similar polyneuropathy symptoms and fat distribution. The uncle had also undergone liposuction for neck fat accumulation.

Discussion and conclusions

Our case provides a clinical description of abnormal fat distribution in CMT2B. Differential diagnoses, including Cushing’s syndrome, were ruled out. The localized, symmetric fat deposition and the recurrence of lipid accumulation after liposuction support a genetic and possibly metabolic origin and suggest a potential link between the variant in the RAB7A gene and abnormal fat distribution.

The RAB7A p.Val162Met variant is a known pathogenic variant previously reported in patients with CMT2B [4, 7, 8]. However, none of the previously described individuals exhibited abnormal fat distribution and may represent an expansion of the clinical phenotype associated with RAB7A variants.

Similar phenotypes have been reported in individuals with pathogenic variants in MFN2 and LMNA, known to affect mitochondrial and nuclear function, respectively [9–11].

Fat replacement of muscles on MRI is a well-recognised feature in several CMT subtypes and has been explored as a potential marker of disease severity and progression [12]. In contrast, we describe abnormal subcutaneous fat accumulation primarily of the neck.

Overexpression of RAB7A has been shown to increase acetyl-CoA carboxylase and fatty acid synthase —enzymes involved in lipid biosynthesis. In addition, studies have demonstrated altered lipid metabolism in patients with CMT caused by pathogenic variants in RAB7A, with accumulation of lipid droplets in fibroblasts from patients with CMT2B [5].

Despite the cellular findings, abnormal fat distribution has not been reported in the previously described individuals. However, these findings are consistent with the clinical observations in our patients and support our proposed association between the pathogenic variant in RAB7A and the abnormal fat distribution. Although more common endocrine causes and genetic causes were excluded, other rare etiologies cannot be ruled out. Further studies are needed to confirm the association and to investigate underlying mechanisms.

Patient A described years of frustration over the unexplained fat accumulation, which clinicians had often dismissed. Both sisters recalled that during a childhood check-up, a doctor commented on the atypical fat distribution despite normal weight—an observation they had not considered unusual, as other family members, including their mother and uncle, shared similar features. As an adult, Patient A did not feel that her concerns were taken seriously and felt that she was often just reassured that Cushing’s syndrome had been ruled out. Receiving a diagnosis that linked her neuropathy and fat distribution provided a sense of validation and understanding.

Our case highlights the complexity of hereditary polyneuropathies and suggests that abnormal fat distribution is a phenotypic feature in RAB7A-associated polyneuropathy.

Comparative effectiveness totally endoscopic thyroidectomy via completely submental tri-hole approach and transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy without insufflation
Authors: Yi Wang, Pan Li, Fei Fan, Yangyang Guan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aims to evaluate the therapeutic effectiveness of totally endoscopic thyroidectomy via the completely submental tri-hole approach and transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy without insufflation.MethodsA total of 60 patients with thyroid tumors who were admitted to Panyu College Affiliated Hospital from August 2022 to August 2023 were collected in this study. The patients were divided into two groups, the transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy group (20 cases) and the totally endoscopic thyroidectomy via the completely submental tri-hole approach group (40 cases). Then, we evaluated the difference of surgical data, operative details, postoperative complications, duration of postoperative swelling, cosmetic satisfaction, discomfort during eating, and pain scores between two groups.ResultsThere were no significant differences between two groups in terms of operation time, intraoperative blood loss, postoperative drainage, extubation time, length of hospital stay, autotransplantation of parathyroid glands, or postoperative swelling. And neither group experienced voice hoarseness, postoperative bleeding, or lymphatic leakage. The incidence of hypocalcemia did not differ significantly between two groups, although the transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy group had a significantly higher incidence of chin numbness (p<0.01). Futhermore, there was no obvious differences in neck pain scores or cosmetic satisfaction scores at 3 days, 7 days, 2 weeks, and 1 month postoperatively. However, the discomfort during eating score was markedly lower in the totally endoscopic thyroidectomy group compared to the transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy group (P < 0.05).ConclusionTotally endoscopic thyroidectomy via the completely submental tri-hole approach offers advantages in reducing postoperative discomfort during eating compared to transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy, indicating promising prospects for clinical application.

Introduction

Recently, thyroid cancer rates have risen which has prompted attention to thyroid cancer and the search for improved treatments [1–3]. Simultaneously, as living standards improve, there is a greater emphasis on surgical outcomes, including aesthetics and recovery speed [4]. Minimally invasive surgery is now a major focus of research and clinical interest. Endoscopic thyroid surgery, as a minimally invasive option, is increasingly preferred by both doctors and patients [5–7].

.The development of endoscopic thyroid surgery traces back to the late 1990s [8–10]. Initially, it mainly used the axillary approach, but due to limited space and high surgical difficulty, it wasn't widely adopted [11, 12]. Over time, various approaches emerged, such as thoracoscopic breast, axillary, transoral vestibular, and chin-mouth combined, providing more options for patients [13–16]. Our team has focused on research and practice many years. With a skilled team and advanced equipment, we offer high-quality endoscopic thyroid surgery. Our clinical studies show that totally endoscopic thyroidectomy via the completely submental tri-hole approach effectively treats thyroid diseases while meeting patients' needs for aesthetics and recovery speed.

This study aims to compare the effectiveness of two endoscopic thyroidectomy techniques: Totally Endoscopic Thyroidectomy via Completely Submental Tri-hole Approach and Transoral Endoscopic Thyroidectomy without Insufflation. Totally Endoscopic Thyroidectomy via Completely Submental Tri-hole Approach involves surgery through three small incisions made under the chin, while Transoral Endoscopic Thyroidectomy involves surgery through the mouth without abdominal insufflation [17]. Here,we intend to compare two surgical methods for endoscopic thyroidectomy, focusing on their differences in safety, effectiveness, and postoperative patient experience. By analyzing surgical outcomes and patient feedback, we seek to provide valuable insights for surgeons, enabling them to select the most appropriate surgical approach to improve both surgical safety and patient satisfaction. Additionally, the results of this study are expected to provide references for further development in thyroid surgical techniques, promoting continuous improvement in the treatment of thyroid diseases.

Materials and methods

General information

A total of 60 patients with thyroid tumors, admitted to the Affiliated Hospital of Panzhihua College between August 2022 and August 2023, were randomly assigned to two groups: the chin-neck combined surgery group (chin-neck group, 20 cases) and the complete submental three-hole surgery group (Submental group, 40 cases). The randomization was performed using a computer-generated random number table, ensuring the groups were comparable in terms of age, gender, maximum tumor diameter, histopathological type, and surgical site (P > 0.05). For the chin-neck combined surgery group, inclusion criteria included :patients with strong cosmetic needs, benign nodules ≤ 4 cm (with some flexibility for cystic-dominant nodules), differentiated thyroid cancer with tumors ≤ 2 cm and no lymph node metastasis, primary hyperthyroidism with tumors ≤ 3 cm, and thyroid nodules behind the sternum ≤ 4 cm. Exclusion criteria included: oral deformities or infections, medullary or undifferentiated thyroid carcinoma, severe inflammatory thyroid disease, tumors near the recurrent laryngeal nerve or at the upper pole, and a history of neck surgery or radiation. The complete submental three-hole surgery group had similar inclusion criteria but allowed for a broader range of tumors, including those that may be slightly larger or located in areas less suitable for the chin-neck approach. This group also included patients with strong cosmetic needs but with somewhat less stringent aesthetic requirements. Exclusion criteria were similar, but with more flexibility regarding tumor location and size, and this group allowed for larger tumors or those located near critical structures like the recurrent laryngeal nerve. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Panzhihua College, and all patients provided informed consent after preoperative counseling.

Surgical Methods

Both procedures were performed by the same surgeon.

Chin-neck group

Endoscopic thyroidectomy via chin-neck approach was used. After general anesthesia, the patient's neck was extended, and a 1.5 cm transverse incision was made at the midline wrinkle of the chin to create an observation hole. Two 0.5 cm operation holes were made on the buccal mucosa on both sides of the mouth angle, respectively. The surgical field was separated to the suprasternal notch, and both sides of the clavicular mastoid muscle were completely exposed outside the 2 cm range. The skin and mucosa were superficially cut, and local injection of expansion fluid (20 mL ropivacaine + 1 mg adrenaline + 500 mL saline) was performed in the puncture tunnel. According to the incision position, the gap was separated, and the white line of the neck was opened along the superficial layer of the neck deep fascia. The two sides of the zygomatic muscle were separated until the thyroid gland was exposed, and the mitoxantrone hydrochloride injection was injected into the gland. The diseased lobe was removed, and if it was papillary carcinoma, lymph node dissection in the VI area was included. The trachea and pre-laryngeal lymph nodes were cleared, and the relevant gaps were freed. The thyroid isthmus was cut off with an ultrasonic knife. A small amount of zygomatic muscle can be removed, and the thyroid upper pole was exposed. The gland was pulled up, the annular ligament space was expanded, and the upper pole of the gland was freed and electrically coagulated and cut off the blood vessels. The gland was pulled up to the upper pole, along the interstitial membrane, and the laryngeal nerve was dissected under nerve monitoring, and the Berry ligament was cut off. The dissection continued towards the lower pole along the interstitial membrane. The surrounding blood vessels were coagulated with an ultrasonic knife, but the thyroid was not removed temporarily because the inferior thyroid artery and vein are close to the lower pole of the thyroid. During the operation, careful separation of the parathyroid gland was ensured. If it cannot survive, immediate autologous transplantation was performed. For patients with thyroid cancer, central group lymph nodes needed to be cleared, and the scope was the same as that of the Submental group. The release and exposure of the laryngeal nerve were ensured, and the outer edge was determined according to the carotid artery, while the inner edge was determined according to the tracheal opposite edge. The surrounding lymph node tissue at the laryngeal nerve plane was removed. The removal bag was inserted into the observation hole through the laparoscope, and the gland and the removed lymph node tissue were placed into the bag for removal. After confirming that there was no active bleeding, the wound was washed with warm distilled water, a negative pressure drainage tube was placed, and another incision was made from the chin incision to lead it out. The neck white line was sutured with 3-0 non-absorbable thread, the skin incision was sutured with 4-0 absorbable thread, and the mucosa was sutured with 5-0 fast-absorbing thread. The operation was completed.

Complete submental three-port endoscopic thyroidectomy (submental group)

The complete submental three-port endoscopic thyroidectomy was conducted in the submental group. After administering general anesthesia, the patient's neck was extended, and a 1.5 cm transverse incision was made along the midline of the submental crease to serve as the observation port. Two additional 0.5 cm incisions were made adjacent to the central incision as working ports. The surgical area was carefully dissected down to the sternal notch, ensuring full exposure of the medial edge of the sternocleidomastoid muscle on both sides, up to 2cm beyond the muscle margin. The skin and superficial mucosa were incised, and a local expansion fluid (consisting of 20 mL of lidocaine, 1 mg of adrenaline, and 500 mL of saline) was injected at the tunnel site. Based on the precise location of the incisions, the tissue planes were meticulously separated, and the cervical fascia was opened along the superficial layer of the deep neck fascia. The bilateral strap muscles were meticulously separated until adequate visualization of the thyroid gland was achieved. Absorbable sutures were used for suspension traction, and mitomycin hydrochloride injection was administered into the gland. For patients with benign tumors, a partial thyroidectomy was performed, while those with malignant tumors underwent total thyroidectomy. The intraoperative techniques and precautions were consistent with those employed in the Submental group. Finally, the skin incision was closed using 4-0 absorbable sutures.

Evaluation indicators and scoring scheme

Evaluation indicators [18, 19]

The evaluation will include the following indicators: surgical time, intraoperative blood loss, postoperative drainage volume, extubation time, length of hospital stay, number of parathyroid transplants, loss of recurrent laryngeal nerve signal, duration of surgical site swelling, postoperative complications, neck pain score, discomfort during eating, and cosmetic satisfaction score.

Scoring scheme

The scoring scheme will be as follows: At 3 days, 7 days, 2 weeks, and 1 month after the surgery, patients will be asked to rate the pain level in the surgical area subjectively on a scale of 0 to 10, with 0 indicating no pain and 10 indicating extreme pain. Similarly, they will rate the level of discomfort during eating on a scale of 0 to 10, with 0 indicating no discomfort and 10 indicating extreme discomfort. Additionally, at 1 month postoperatively, patients will be asked to rate their satisfaction with the cosmetic outcome on a scale of 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating greater satisfaction.

Statistical methods

Statistical analysis will be performed using the SPSS 22.0 software. Quantitative data will be presented as mean ± standard deviation (X±s), and t-tests and ANOVA analysis will be used for the comparisons of two and more than two groups. Qualitative data will be presented as counts and percentages, and chi-square tests will be used for intergroup comparisons. P < 0.05 indicates statistically significant.

Discussion

In addition to medical safety and surgical efficacy, minimally invasive and cosmetic outcomes are important factors to consider in future surgical treatment plans. The development of endoscopic thyroidectomy has achieved good clinical outcomes based on cosmetic principles. The chin-neck approach can achieve a scarless state on the surface, but it may damage structures at the floor of the mouth and nerves such as the lingual and mental nerves, and is only suitable for certain special populations [20]. Although the chin-neck combined endoscopic thyroid surgery reduces the risk of floor of mouth injury, it is still not completely avoided [21].

The submental approach has its unique advantages. The total submental approach has smaller surgical trauma, a shorter operative tunnel, avoids important anatomical structures, reduces the probability of postoperative swelling, and allows for faster recovery. In addition, shifting the incision from the anterior neck area to the submental area allows for complete scar coverage by the chin and skin creases, avoiding direct exposure of scars and reducing the risk of hypertrophic scar formation. Furthermore, this technique does not disrupt the subcutaneous lymphatic network and veins in the anterior neck area, resulting in shorter recovery time for postoperative swelling. Compared to the chin-neck approach, the total submental approach reduces the incision classification from Class II back to Class I [22] reducing the use of antibiotics. This approach uses a non-inflatable technique, reducing the occurrence of air embolism and the retention of carbon dioxide, resulting in less pain in the surgical area and improved surgical and anesthesia safety [23].

Currently, we have observed good anterior neck visibility and a more natural operating angle with the total submental approach. It is similar to direct visualization, with a smaller area of dissection and reduced tumor implantation risk for the surgeon. If neck lymph node dissection is needed, the operation can be completed by extending the incision below the jaw. Currently, we still cannot apply this technique to patients with bilateral diseases or patients requiring lateral neck lymph node dissection. Additionally, the "chopsticks effect" is more prominent in close proximity to the three ports, placing higher demands on the surgeon, and has not significantly reduced the surgical time.

Despite these advantages, it is important to note that the quality and significance of this work are affected by the fact that all surgeries were performed by a single surgeon. Further elaboration on this surgeon’s skills and experience with both techniques is necessary for a comprehensive understanding. A true comparison of the two surgical methods would ideally involve multiple surgeons to account for individual variations in proficiency. As it stands, it remains unclear whether the observed differences in surgical outcomes stem from the inherent qualities of the procedures themselves or the surgeon's relative expertise in one technique over the other. Additionally, the extent to which the surgeon may favor one approach could introduce bias, which needs to be addressed.

In summary, with the continuous improvement of human living standards, the demand for cosmetic outcomes will continue to increase. In addition to medical safety and surgical efficacy, minimally invasive and cosmetic outcomes are important considerations for future surgical procedures. Only after achieving scarless treatment on the surface can we ensure that patients are psychologically satisfied. This requires surgeons to provide reasonable and individualized treatment plans for each patient. Currently, the number of endoscopic thyroidectomies using these two approaches is still relatively small. However, they have resolved the contradiction between trauma and aesthetics in previous thyroid surgeries, providing aesthetically pleasing and safe treatment options for patients with thyroid tumors. The total submental three-port non-inflatable thyroid surgery is worth promoting in clinical practice.

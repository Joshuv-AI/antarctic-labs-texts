Data element valorization, innovation strategies and entrepreneurial quality
Authors: Xinyue Qin, Haiqing Hu, Tong Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe effective utilization of data element valorization to decode innovation decision-making processes, thereby achieving high-quality entrepreneurship, has emerged as a critical challenge for enterprises operating within the competitive digital economy landscape. Grounded in resource-based view theory and dynamic capability theory, this study develops a theoretical model that investigates how scenario-driven data applications mediate the impact of data valorization on entrepreneurial quality. Through empirical analysis of 355 entrepreneurial firms, the research reveals three key insights: First, data element valorization significantly enhances entrepreneurial quality by optimizing innovation pathways. Second, while data valorization exhibits a linear positive relationship with focused innovation strategies, it shows a concave nonlinear association with dispersed innovation strategies, indicating diminishing marginal returns rather than a fully realized inverted U-shaped pattern within the observed sample range. Third, focused strategies improve entrepreneurial quality through resource clustering effects, whereas decentralized approaches constrain such improvements. Additionally, scenario-driven data application orientation moderates the interplay between data valorization, innovation strategies, and entrepreneurial outcomes. This study advances innovation management theory by challenging data homogeneity assumptions and proposing a dual-balance framework for optimizing data-driven innovation efficiency alongside entrepreneurial quality. The findings further offer policymakers actionable insights for shaping institutional mechanisms that enhance data ecosystem governance.Keywords: Data element valorization, Innovation strategies, Entrepreneurial quality, Startups, Data application scenarioSubject terms: Business and management, Business and management, Economics, Economics, Information systems and information technology

Introduction

Amid deepening integration between digital and physical economies, data elements are reshaping global value competition as the “fifth fundamental factor of production”. International Data Corporation (IDC) projections indicate global data volume will exceed 175 zettabytes (ZB) by 2025, with China potentially contributing 30% of this growth through its unparalleled market scale. This data deluge is giving rise to a “data alchemy” paradigm-a transformative process where enterprises achieve innovation capability leapfrogging through value conversion of data elements. According to the China Digital Economy Development White Paper (2022) released by the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology, China’s digital economy reached 45.5 trillion yuan in 2021, accounting for 39.8% of GDP. The Opinions on Establishing Fundamental Data Systems to Enhance Data Element Utilization issued in 2022 explicitly proposed a tripartite property rights mechanism encompassing data resource ownership, data processing rights, and data product operation rights, institutionalizing the transition from data resource accumulation to value realization. In 2024, the China National Data Administration and 16 other ministries jointly implemented the Three-Year Action Plan for Data Elements × (2024–2026), targeting 12 key industries to accelerate data value creation. Within this policy-driven context, an increasing number of entrepreneurial ventures are prioritizing data element valorization to enhance innovation-driven decision-making, thereby improving entrepreneurial vitality and quality. However, despite the expanding application of data valorization in startups, its operational mechanisms and efficacy remain insufficiently validated. Sector-specific analyses reveal substantial variations in how data valorization influences innovation decisions and entrepreneurial activities across industries and application scenarios. Meanwhile, nascent ventures face systemic challenges in implementing data valorization, including constrained resources and underdeveloped technological capabilities. The critical question persists: how can resource-constrained startups effectively leverage data-driven innovation to achieve sustainable high-quality development?

Empirical observations reveal that data element valorization is fundamentally restructuring the driving logic of entrepreneurial activities. On one hand, digital-native enterprises like ByteDance and SHEIN achieve exponential growth through data-driven precision decision-making, compressing the timeline from startup to unicorn status compared to traditional industries. Conversely, as evidenced by the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences’ Institute of Industrial Economics survey, 82% of manufacturing firms remain entrenched in data silos that severely constrain operational and innovation efficiency. This emerging digital divide demonstrates significant heterogeneity in data valorization capabilities across enterprises amidst institutional advancements in data element market development. Notably, the data capitalization market exhibits concerning valuation bubbles, where certain enterprises obtain inflated valuations through data assetization and securitization processes, yet fail to correspondingly enhance the efficiency of translating data elements into innovation capabilities.

Current research has yet to systematically elucidate the intrinsic mechanisms through which data element valorization influences entrepreneurial quality. While the traditional resource-based view emphasizes heterogeneous resources in constructing competitive advantages, it fails to account for how data elements’ unique non-rivalrous nature and real-time iterativity reshape innovation decision-making. Resource-based theory posits that enterprises achieve sustained competitive advantages through effective management of distinctive resources1. As a unique resource, the process of data valorization not only enhances the operational efficiency and market competitiveness of enterprises, but also creates new business models and growth points for them. However, most of the existing research focuses on the application of data resources, and there is a lack of research on the process of how the valorization of data elements can enhance the quality of entrepreneurship by influencing firms’ innovation decisions. The dynamic capability theory, though addressing organizational adaptability in changing environments, remains silent on how the tri-phase transition (resourceization-assetization-capitalization) in data valorization reconstitutes innovation strategies. Recent explorations into data-driven innovation transitions propose a digital-enabled innovation paradigm, yet treat innovation decision-making as a homogeneous black box, neglecting differentiated transmission pathways2. Two critical research gaps persist: first, the heterogeneous impacts of data valorization across innovation decision types,second, the differential effects of distinct innovation decisions on entrepreneurial quality metrics.

This study decodes the value realization process of the data element to explore the black-box mechanism through which it influences entrepreneurial quality. Specifically, we conceptualize data valorization as the independent variable, innovation decision-making as the mediating variable, entrepreneurial quality as the outcome variable, and data application scenarios as the moderating variable, thereby constructing a mechanistic framework to elucidate impact pathways. The research addresses two pivotal questions: (1) How does data valorization enhance entrepreneurial quality through distinct innovation decision pathways? (2) What catalytic effects do application scenarios exert on the data valorization-innovation-entrepreneurship nexus? Resolving these questions will advance theoretical boundaries of digital-era innovation while providing micro-mechanistic explanations for overcoming the “data abundance paradox”, so as to promote innovation and high-quality sustainable development.

Theoretical background and hypotheses

Data element valorization and entrepreneurial quality

Since data elements were established as strategic emerging production factors in the digital economy era, academic research on their value realization has surged. Current studies primarily focus on conceptualizing the connotation, processes, and characteristics of data element valorization, while identifying data morphologies across different value chain stages and exploring their corresponding value-release mechanisms. This has gradually formed a research framework spanning three analytical dimensions: macroeconomic, meso-systemic, and micro-contextual perspectives. Macro-level investigations emphasize the economic mechanisms through which data elements generate multiplier and amplification effects by interacting with traditional production factors. At the meso-level, predominant scholarship categorizes the valorization process into three phases: resourceization-assetization-capitalization of data elements3, during which their manifestations evolve sequentially from data resources and assets to data products, commodities, and ultimately capital4. Micro-level analyses concretely situate value realization within specific industrial contexts, examining how data elements enable organizational strategic innovation5, drive technological upgrades, and facilitate product development across diverse operational scenarios6.

The valorization of data elements injects systematic dynamism into entrepreneurial activities through a sequential progression of resourceization, assetization, and capitalization, reconfiguring innovation capabilities and high-quality growth trajectories for startups. During the resourceization phase, startups acquire, integrate, and structure fragmented data resources, establishing foundations for precise analytics that enhance decision-making quality while mitigating operational risks. Concurrently, this phase strengthens resource allocation competence, enabling startups to better identify resource potential, optimize utilization patterns, and dynamically adjust resource distribution (CAICT PERI 2021).

The assetization phase institutionalizes data valuation and ownership through standardized processes including rights confirmation, registration, appraisal, and pricing. By overcoming data silo challenges through clarified property rights and transactional mechanisms, startups achieve cross-domain data integration capabilities via market-driven external resource acquisition. This process elevates corporate asset portfolios, enhancing both market competitiveness and financing capacities7. Inter-firm data transactions and collaborative sharing further amplify collective resource efficiency. Fostering open data ecosystems broadens innovation horizons while optimizing resource allocation efficiency through complementary asset utilization.

Capitalization transforms data assets into financial instruments via credit financing and securitization mechanisms8, diversifying funding channels for entrepreneurial ventures and sustaining R&D investments. Through enabling novel products, services, and business models, this phase elevates startups from value transmitters to value creators. The capitalization process simultaneously strengthens endogenous growth drivers and cultivates data network effects, attracting ecosystem partners to establish positive feedback loops that amplify innovation momentum and market dominance. Based on this framework, we propose:

H1

The valorization of data elements is positively related to entrepreneurial quality.

The mediating role of innovative decision-making

Before data element valorization can reshape innovation decisions, data resources must be cognitively processed and strategically interpreted by entrepreneurial management teams. In other words, the influence of data element valorization on innovation strategy is not automatic. Rather, it operates through a data-driven decision-making process in which managers identify salient signals from heterogeneous data, evaluate strategic alternatives, reduce uncertainty, and translate data insights into specific innovation priorities. Recent research suggests that data-driven decision-making strengthens organizational creativity and sustainable competitive advantage by improving how firms transform digital inputs into strategic action. In entrepreneurial settings, this mechanism is especially important because startups often face resource constraints, environmental turbulence, and high levels of strategic ambiguity, making the managerial interpretation of data a critical step between data accumulation and innovation choice.

The effectiveness of this data-driven decision process further depends on digital leadership. Digital leadership provides the managerial foundation for interpreting data, building a shared digital vision, encouraging analytic reasoning, and coordinating cross-functional action around data-informed innovation. Prior research highlights that digital leadership positively strengthens the performance effects of data-driven decision-making, and that leaders are a key success factor in the organizational shift from intuition-based to analytics-based decision-making. In startup contexts, digital leadership is particularly relevant because focused and dispersed innovation strategies both require not only access to data, but also managerial capabilities to prioritize, mobilize, and govern data-based experimentation. Therefore, digital leadership helps explain why similar levels of data element valorization may produce different innovation choices and entrepreneurial outcomes across firms9.

Innovative decision-making constitutes a strategic process characterized by enterprises’ differential emphasis on innovation types based on their resource endowments and market positioning, subsequently shaping resource allocation configurations that align with functional and business-unit strategies10. This process exerts sustained impacts on organizational innovation capabilities and performance outcomes11. Existing scholarship primarily categorizes innovative decision-making along two dimensions: The first dimension classifies decisions by entrepreneurial agency, particularly founders’ behavioral orientations, including radical vs. incremental innovation strategies12, and risk-seeking vs. risk-averse decision paradigms13. The second dimension evaluates decisions through the lens of resource mobilization motives, wherein enterprises strategically balance proportions of incremental, breakthrough, and disruptive innovations when restructuring ventures, optimizing resource utilization, and capturing market opportunities14. Distinct from prior frameworks, this study investigates micro-level mechanisms and heterogeneous effects within startup innovation decision-making. Adopting a product development lifecycle perspective, we operationalize decision types as focused versus dispersed configurations11,15, analyzing how these orientations mediate resource conversion efficiency throughout innovation implementation phases.

The valorization process of data elements facilitates the formation of focused innovation decisions through resource agglomeration effects, but this influence is realized only when management teams are able to transform data inputs into strategic judgments through data-driven decision-making processes. Data resourceization provides cognitive anchors for focused decision-making by transforming raw data into machine-readable, business-interpretable information through cleansing, integration, and structural processing. This infrastructure-building process establishes data-to-knowledge conversion capabilities for startups. In manufacturing ventures, for instance, real-time production line data collection and operational parameter modeling enable precise identification of equipment failure patterns and process optimization spaces, thereby channeling previously scattered trial-and-error innovations into targeted technical improvements. Concurrently, data governance practices during resourceization critically determine decision-focusing precision, where timely data updates enhance the efficiency of capturing innovation window opportunities.

The assetization phase imposes resource allocation constraints through property rights delineation and value quantification, compelling startups to optimize innovation investment structures16. By requiring formal rights confirmation, valuation benchmarking, and balance sheet incorporation of data assets17, this phase exerts a screening effect on decision orientation. Ventures with data tool-dominated asset portfolios demonstrate vertical innovation preferences around core technologies, whereas those emphasizing data services tend toward niche specialization (CAICT & Neusoft 2024). Furthermore, the financial visibility from capitalized data assets heightens managerial sensitivity to innovation ROI, systematically curbing opportunistic innovation behaviors.

Capitalization amplifies innovation scale effects through financial leverage and ecosystem synergies, elevating focused decisions from tactical options to strategic imperatives. The transformation of data assets into tradable financial instruments essentially magnifies marginal returns on innovation investment via liquidity premiums18. This phase optimizes implementation conditions for focused decisions through dual mechanisms: enhanced coupling efficiency between data elements and complementary factors (e.g., talent, technology) accelerates technical barrier formation, while capital market valuation pressures force startups to reduce innovation trial spaces. Based on this analysis, we propose:

H2a

The valorization of data elements is positively related to focused innovation decisions.

The valorization of data elements, as a novel production factor allocation mechanism in the digital economy era, exerts nonlinear impacts on startups’ dispersed innovation decisions. This influence stems from the intrinsic non-rivalrous nature and diminishing marginal costs of data elements, while being modulated by interactive effects among institutional constraints, resource rigidity, and financial leverage.

During the initial resourceization phase, data valorization stimulates nascent growth of dispersed innovation decisions through expanded opportunity identification breadth. The transformation of raw data into structured information assets significantly mitigates startups’ experimentation costs and information asymmetry. By implementing real-time user behavior tracking and multidimensional tagging systems, ventures can concurrently identify latent demands across multiple market segments, thereby incentivizing parallel development of cross-category innovation projects. This phase’s innovation diffusion originates from data’s non-excludable usage characteristics-identical datasets enabling low-marginal-cost exploration through differentiated application scenarios.

However, the assetization phase introduces diminishing marginal returns to dispersed decisions via property rights constraints and value lock-in effects. Institutionalized processes of rights confirmation and valuation benchmarking establish opportunity cost barriers for innovation resources. Furthermore, data morphology in the assetization phase intensifies path dependence in innovation decision-making. Data tool-driven ventures exhibit innovation extension along established technological trajectories, while data service-oriented ventures face convergence pressures from client customization requirements. Both mechanisms constrain the diversification of decision orientations.

The capitalization phase amplifies suppression effects on dispersed innovation decisions through financial leverage and ecosystem dynamics. By converting data assets into tradable financial instruments, the liquidity premium mechanism inherent in capitalization critically restructures innovation incentives. Firstly, while enhanced coupling efficiency between data and complementary production factors could theoretically expand innovation boundaries, capital market participants’ preference for quantifiable short-term returns compels ventures to abandon high-risk long-tail innovation initiatives19. Secondly, although ecosystem networks formed during capitalization might ostensibly promote open innovation, enterprises’ exclusive control over core data assets paradoxically reinforces innovation path locking20. This dual institutional alienation precipitates a steeply ascending marginal cost trajectory for dispersed innovation decisions in advanced stages.

Beyond these structural constraints, the sustainability of dispersed innovation decisions also depends on whether startups can build information-driven resilience. As dispersed innovation broadens experimentation scope, ventures face rising coordination costs, greater exposure to market shocks, and increasing pressure on managerial attention. Under such conditions, the valorization of data elements can contribute not only to opportunity expansion but also to adaptive capacity by improving real-time information processing, early risk sensing, and rapid strategic adjustment. Recent research suggests that information-driven resilience reflects an organization’s capacity to convert AI- or data-enabled information flows into adaptive responses, and that such resilience is a key mechanism linking information capability to entrepreneurial success in volatile environments. This perspective helps explain why some startups can absorb the inflection point at which marginal management costs begin to exceed innovation returns, whereas others become trapped in resource fragmentation and strategic overload21. Based on this analysis, we propose:

H2b

The valorization of data elements demonstrates an inverted U-shaped relationship with dispersed innovation decisions.

Focused innovation decisions enhance entrepreneurial quality through resource agglomeration effects that strengthen core competency development, whereas dispersed innovation decisions dynamically influence quality via opportunity exploration and resource dissipation mechanisms. These divergent pathways manifest differential effects when measuring entrepreneurial quality in growth dimensions and innovativeness metrics.

Drawing on the resource-based view (RBV) and dynamic capability theory, focused innovation enables enterprises to concentrate finite data resources along specific technological trajectories. Through deep knowledge accumulation, this strategic resource orchestration fosters technological exclusivity, simultaneously enhancing product innovativeness while erecting competitive barriers. Empirical evidence confirms that heightened resource focus significantly improves new product development efficiency, validating the amplifying effect of intensive resource allocation on innovation output22. Regarding growth dimensions, focused decision-making accelerates market penetration through specialization advantages. As Barney’s seminal work demonstrates, focused startups rapidly develop domain-specific marketing networks, consequently achieving faster market share growth rates than diversified counterparts23.

Dispersed innovation decisions exhibit complex nonlinear relationships with entrepreneurial quality. Grounded in organizational learning theory, moderate diversification facilitates breakthrough innovation by enabling firms to transcend core rigidities through cross-domain knowledge recombination24. However, excessive dispersion triggers resource fragmentation, resulting in individual project investments falling below innovation threshold levels25 while escalating coordination costs. During initial stages, dispersed decision-making enhances resource heterogeneity through multipoint market engagements. Yet when technological portfolio complexity surpasses organizational absorptive capacity, ventures face precipitous declines in resource integration efficiency as they struggle to manage concurrent R&D initiatives26. At this inflection point, marginal management costs exceed innovation returns. Whether ventures can prevent this inflection point from undermining entrepreneurial quality depends in part on their information-driven resilience, namely, their ability to use data-enabled information flows to absorb shocks, coordinate distributed innovation efforts, and reconfigure decisions under uncertainty. This study proposes the following hypotheses:

H3a

Focused innovation decisions are positively related to entrepreneurial quality.

H3b

Dispersed innovation decisions demonstrate an inverted U-shaped relationship with entrepreneurial quality.

(3) The mediating role of innovative decision-making.

(3) The mediating role of innovative decision-making.

The valorization process of data elements serves as a catalytic mechanism for synergizing and agglomerating data with conventional production factors, enabling both value creation and equitable resource sharing. This constitutes a fundamental prerequisite for startups to achieve neo-productivity characterized by quality-driven growth27. Nevertheless, prevailing research paradigms predominantly examine this phenomenon through macro-level valorization processes while insufficiently addressing the micro-mechanisms through which entrepreneurial firms leverage data to reconfigure innovation decision architectures.

Focused innovation decisions manifest as goal-oriented, resource-concentrated strategic selections that prioritize optimization and deepening of existing technological trajectories11. When data resource accumulation reaches critical mass, startups can rapidly identify technical bottlenecks and achieve localized breakthroughs through focused decision-making, significantly enhancing innovation efficiency. However, excessive reliance on established technologies may induce data redundancy or technological path dependence, thereby eroding marginal returns on entrepreneurial activities. These decisions demand intensive technological investment and organizational coordination. While initial data resource integration substantially improves product innovativeness, escalating data complexity may eventually offset benefits through rising management costs and decision rigidity.

Conversely, dispersed innovation decisions emphasize pluralistic exploration and cross-boundary integration within entrepreneurial processes. Data openness and sharing can stimulate transdisciplinary inspiration, as exemplified by multi-scenario applications of user behavior data in platform economies that catalyze novel business formats. By assimilating heterogeneous data resources, dispersed decisions drive original technological innovation and market boundary expansion. Their effectiveness, however, exhibits significant sensitivity to organizational experience and external market conditions, with startups potentially trapped in “innovation bubbles” due to resource dispersion. The scale economies generated through data valorization may initially amplify positive impacts of dispersed decisions, but overreliance on data-driven experimentation risks resource dissipation, creating a coexistence of entrepreneurial risk and reward28. Based on this analysis, we propose:

H4a

Focused innovation decisions mediate the association between valorization of data elements and entrepreneurial quality.

H4b

Dispersed innovation decisions mediate the association between valorization of data elements and entrepreneurial quality.

The moderating role of data application scenario orientation

The effectiveness of application scenario orientation is not uniform across all industrial environments. Its role becomes particularly salient in specialized and high-stakes contexts in which data use is subject to stronger regulatory scrutiny, tighter operational coupling, higher coordination demands, or clearer sustainability and safety requirements. In such settings, the valorization of data elements depends less on generic data accumulation alone and more on whether firms can embed data resources into clearly defined, scenario-specific strategic priorities. Recent research in the energy industry shows that advanced digital systems such as AI-adaptive supply chains improve innovation and collaboration capabilities, and that their performance effects are strengthened when firms adopt proactive, scenario-specific sustainability strategies29. This suggests that application scenario orientation should be understood not only as a contextual moderator, but also as a boundary condition that becomes more consequential when startups operate in specialized, high-accountability environments.

The effectiveness and impact pathways of data element valorization are influenced by their application scenario30. Application scenario orientation has emerged as a critical external determinant shaping the valorization process during entrepreneurship, with the diversity and variability of application contexts introducing latent challenges and uncertainties for ventures31,32, including data reuse constraints, divergent institutional orientations, and multifaceted operational scenarios. The core mechanism of data valorization lies in realizing economic value conversion through data circulation, integration, and application. This conversion process is systematically modulated by application context orientation through triple mechanisms: demand anchoring, technological adaptation, and organizational synergy, thereby shaping corporate innovation decisions and entrepreneurial quality.

The valorization of data elements enhances corporate innovation decisions by improving data resource usability and enabling externalities spillover, providing both informational foundations and technological support. Its effect intensity is modulated by application context orientation, which determines the allocation efficiency and innovation directionality of data elements. When application scenarios are well-defined, data screening, processing, and integration can be optimized for specific business domains or market demands, reducing information redundancy and trial-and-error costs. High-value data scenarios drive ventures to prioritize context-aligned technological combinations by capturing demand pulses, thereby strengthening the coupling between data elements and innovation objectives to enhance both technological precision and entrepreneurial success rates.

Conversely, ambiguous application scenarios lead to generalized data allocation, diminishing the marginal contribution to innovation decisions. Furthermore, application scenario orientation regulates innovation conversion pathways through organizational dynamic capabilities. In particular, proactive scenario-specific strategies can function as catalysts in these contexts by helping firms align data resources, innovation priorities, and cross-organizational collaboration requirements around concrete use cases, thereby increasing the conversion efficiency from data valorization to innovation and entrepreneurial outcomes. Complex scenarios necessitate integration of heterogeneous multi-source data and technological framework reconfiguration, where high data liquidity stimulates radical and focused innovation. Linear data applications in simple contexts support incremental innovation and dispersed decision-making. Thus, by defining scenario complexity levels, application scenario orientation shapes the decision-making preferences in data valorization processes33.

Entrepreneurial quality is significantly influenced by the interaction between data element valorization and application scenario orientation. Regarding innovativeness, scenario-driven data enhances technological novelty through cross-domain knowledge recombination. For instance, the integration of meteorological data with energy consumption metrics in green low-carbon scenarios can catalyze breakthrough innovations such as carbon emission prediction algorithms. Furthermore, application scenario orientation strengthens commercialization viability by constructing synergistic data-technology-market networks that align innovation outputs with market demands. In growth dimensions, scenario scale effects amplify the increasing returns characteristic of data valorization. When application scenarios demonstrate high scalability, data element reuse reduces entrepreneurial marginal costs while accelerating market penetration, thereby establishing a positive feedback loop where data scale refinement and contextual network expansion mutually reinforce value growth. Based on this framework, we propose:

H5

Application scenario orientation positively moderates the relationship between data element valorization and innovation decisions.

H6

Application scenario orientation positively moderates the relationship between data element valorization and entrepreneurial quality.

Methods

Sample and data collection

We conducted a survey of the Chinese startups to collect the data used in this study from March 2025 to July 2025. A back-translation strategy was used to develop the questionnaire in order to ensure conceptual accuracy and equivalence. To increase the questionnaire’s validity and clarity in the Chinese context, a pre-survey was conducted on the startups in Shaanxi, China. The respondents were required to answer all the questionnaire items and provide feedback on the wording and design of the questionnaire. Based on the feedback, we modified several items and then finalized the questionnaire.

During the formal research phase, we chose the Questionnaire Star platform to distribute online questionnaires to startups in Beijing, Shanghai, Hangzhou and Shenzhen with the introduction and assistance of venture capital organizations in these regions. These four cities were selected because they represent some of the most active entrepreneurial and digital-economy hubs in China. They concentrate a large number of technology-oriented startups, venture capital institutions, and scenario-rich digital application environments, making them suitable settings for observing the mechanisms linking data element valorization, innovation decision-making, and entrepreneurial quality. In addition, venture capital organizations were used as access channels mainly to improve respondent identification and data quality, as they helped us reach senior managers who were familiar with their firms’ data practices, innovation strategies, and entrepreneurial development. At the same time, we acknowledge that this sampling approach does not constitute a random national sample. Startups connected to venture capital networks may be more formalized, growth-oriented, innovation-active, or better resourced than the average startup. Therefore, the sample is better interpreted as representing innovation-intensive startups embedded in leading digital entrepreneurial ecosystems rather than the full population of Chinese startups. All participants provided informed consent through an online research questionnaire. To minimize social desirability bias, participants were also guaranteed that there were no correct or incorrect answers, and were promised strict confidentiality.

After removing invalid responses with missing or incomplete information, a total of 500 questionnaires were issued, 355 valid questionnaires were obtained, the validity rate of questionnaires was 71%. The relevant information of the samples is shown in Table 1. According to the recovered questionnaires, samples from different regions were subjected to the ANOVA test. The results show that the questionnaire data did not have any obvious regional deviation. To reduce potential common method bias at the design stage, several procedural remedies were implemented. We used a back-translation strategy to ensure semantic equivalence, conducted a pre-survey to improve item clarity, assured respondents of anonymity and confidentiality, and explicitly informed them that there were no right or wrong answers. These procedures were intended to reduce evaluation apprehension and social desirability bias. In addition, after data collection, we conducted both Harman’s single-factor test and a more rigorous unmeasured latent method construct (ULMC) test to further assess the potential influence of common method bias.

Measures

The measures of the constructs were adapted from the maturity scales of existing studies. A 5-point Likert scale, from 1, “disagree extremely” to 5, “agree extremely, was used to evaluate the multi-item constructs.

Data element valorization was measured by six items that were adapted from studies by Yin et al.34 and Zhu et al.35. It is measured in terms of data element resourceization, data element assetization and data element capitalization, respectively.

Innovative decision-making was measured by six items that were adapted from studies by Zhu et al.11, Carnabuci and Bruggeman36. Measurements were carried out in terms of focused and dispersed innovation decisions respectively.

Application scenario orientation was measured by five items that were adapted from studies by Cai et al.30 and Jin et al.37.

Entrepreneurial quality was measured by three items that were adapted from studies by Song et al.38 and Peng et al.39. Entrepreneurial quality is operationalized strictly at the firm level, focusing on the innovativeness and growth-related performance of entrepreneurial activity.

Controls. In this study, based on the development characteristics of entrepreneurial enterprises, the age and size of entrepreneurial enterprises and whether they are high-tech enterprises are selected as control variables. In order to eliminate the right-skewed influence of the distribution of quantitative variables, age and size are measured by ordered series. And whether it is a high-tech enterprise is measured by using dummy variables 1 and 0.

Analysis and results

Common method bias assessment

Because all focal constructs were measured using a single questionnaire completed by senior managers, we conducted additional post hoc analyses to assess the potential influence of common method bias. First, Harman’s single-factor test showed that the first unrotated factor accounted for 34.20% of the total variance, suggesting that common method bias was not likely to be severe. However, because Harman’s test alone is insufficient, we further applied an unmeasured latent method construct (ULMC) approach within a confirmatory factor analytic framework.

Specifically, we first estimated a baseline five-factor measurement model including data element valorization, focused innovation decisions, dispersed innovation decisions, application scenario orientation, and entrepreneurial quality. We then estimated an alternative model by adding a latent common method factor that loaded on all measurement items while retaining the substantive factor structure. The baseline five-factor model showed a good fit to the data ( , CFI = 1.000, TLI = 1.000, RMSEA = 0.000, SRMR = 0.028). After including the latent method factor, model fit improved modestly (, , , CFI = 1.000, TLI = 1.000, RMSEA = 0.000, SRMR = 0.019; , , p = 0.037).

Importantly, the substantive measurement structure remained stable after controlling for the latent method factor. The average change in standardized substantive loadings was limited, and the directions of the core relationships among the focal constructs remained unchanged. These results indicate that although some common method variance may exist, it is unlikely to substantially bias the substantive findings of this study.

Discussion

Conclusions

Through empirical tests, this paper finds that the impact of data element valorization on entrepreneurial quality is not a simple linear transmission, and its mechanism of action relies on the moderating effects of the dynamic fitness of innovative decision-making and data application scenarios.

First, data element valorization demonstrates a significant positive promoting effect on entrepreneurial quality. This conclusion corroborates the foundational role of data as a novel production factor in entrepreneurial activities. Data element resourceization, capitalization, and assetization processes effectively mitigate information asymmetry, enhance resource allocation efficiency, and establish the material foundation for building core competitive barriers.

The mediation analysis further reveals complex transmission mechanisms underlying this relationship. The linear promoting effect of data valorization on focused innovation decisions contrasts with its concave nonlinear impact on dispersed innovation decisions. Specifically, although the quadratic term is negative and significant, the turning point lies beyond the observed data range, indicating diminishing marginal returns rather than a fully realized inverted U-shaped relationship within the sample. This dichotomy suggests that overreliance on data-driven short-term competitive advantages may paradoxically constrain exploratory innovation capabilities, inducing path dependency and cognitive lock-in risks. It resonates theoretically with the “resource curse” phenomenon observed in data-abundant ventures.

Second, innovative decision-making exhibit significant differential impacts on entrepreneurial quality. Focused innovation decisions deepen competitive advantages through specialized knowledge accumulation and technological iteration. In contrast, while dispersed innovation decisions initially facilitate market boundary expansion, their eventual negative effects on entrepreneurial quality stem primarily from resource dilution41, escalating organizational coordination costs, and the surpassing of resource integration capacity thresholds. Dispersed decision-making compels ventures to concurrently invest across multiple technological domains, leading to excessive fragmentation of finite data resources. This impedes breakthroughs in technical bottlenecks and entraps firms in low-level repetitive innovation cycles25. Furthermore, dispersed innovation amplifies cross-departmental collaboration demands, pushing nascent ventures beyond their managerial capacity limits. The resulting heterogeneous knowledge streams often exceed organizational absorptive capacity, inducing cognitive overload and strategic defocus, which collectively contribute to diminished entrepreneurial quality.

Third, both innovation decision types demonstrate partial mediating roles in the relationship between data element valorization and entrepreneurial quality. However, as ventures deepen their data valorization capabilities, the negative impacts of dispersed innovation decisions accelerate. This acceleration primarily stems from ventures’ propensity to allocate finite resources toward short-term incremental innovations that promise rapid market share gains, thereby inducing diminishing marginal returns from dispersed innovation42. Concurrently, dispersed decisions necessitate long-term R&D investments and tolerance for experimentation. Market feedback mechanisms driven by data element valorization such as real-time user behavior analysis can reinforce the pursuit of short-term performance, further squeeze the space for exploratory innovation31,32, and ignore the medium- and long-term growth entrepreneurial quality goals of enterprises. Our findings provide micro-level evidence for understanding the innovation paradox in the digital economy era.

Finally, data scenario orientation plays critical moderating and catalytic roles in optimizing innovation decisions and entrepreneurial quality. High-efficacy contextualization mitigates innovation myopia by translating data value into sustainable competitive advantages through contextual adaptation mechanisms. The findings reveal that scenario-driven approaches refine innovation decision logic in the digital economy via “contextual adaptation-value conversion” mechanisms. Furthermore, embedding data elements into operational scenarios reduces informational uncertainty in decision-making, thereby enhancing the feasibility of innovation initiatives and elevating entrepreneurial quality. These conclusions advance the development of scenario-driven theoretical paradigms and provide more granular methodological tools for innovation management in the digital era.

Theoretical contributions

This study makes tripartite theoretical contributions: First, by deconstructing the transmission chains between phased characteristics of data element valorization and innovation decision typologies, it transcends the limitations of the techno-market dichotomy prevalent in classical innovation theories. It reveals data valorization’s dual role as both an innovation catalyst that expands decision spaces and an institutional anchor that constrains experimental boundaries. This duality provides micro-mechanistic explanations for the innovation paradox of coexisting data abundance and innovation scarcity in the digital economy. It also offers a decision-theoretic framework for ventures to calibrate data valorization rhythms and optimize innovation portfolio strategies.

Second, this study empirically demonstrates that deepening data element valorization may trigger a “resource curse” effect in entrepreneurial innovation decision-making. It will provide theoretical grounding for preventing valorization processes from disengaging with the real economy. While extant literature predominantly emphasizes data’s empowering effects, it largely overlooks associated path dependency risks. Our findings reveal that as data valorization accumulates, ventures risk entering a vicious cycle of “data dependency-innovation narrowing-quality deterioration”. Specifically, valuation bubbles during data assetization distort resource allocation signals, while data capitalization may incentivize speculative innovation investments, causing dispersed decisions to deviate from substantive economic needs. This conclusion extends natural “resource curse” theory to digital factor markets, constructing a dynamic analytical framework for understanding the co-evolution of data endowments and innovation capabilities.

Finally, this study posits the moderating role of application scenario orientation in innovation decisions and outcomes, advancing entrepreneurial quality research from static resource-based perspectives toward dynamic fit paradigms. While extant studies predominantly conceptualize entrepreneurial quality as a function of resource endowments, our introduction of scenario-oriented moderation mechanisms reveals its synergistic catalytic effects in the alchemical process of data value conversion. High-efficacy scenario orientation dynamically calibrates data development intensity and innovation portfolio strategies, thereby enabling the transition of entrepreneurial models from factor-driven to scenario-driven paradigms. By organically integrating dynamic capability theory with scenario-driven framework, this research transcends the static analytical confines of the resource-based view, offering novel theoretical lenses for investigating adaptive innovation in the digital economy era.

Practical implications

The findings of this study provide practical guidance for entrepreneurial ventures to optimize data valorization rhythms and innovation decisions toward high-quality entrepreneurship, while offering policymakers evidence to refine data element marketization mechanisms and strengthen context-driven governance.

First, startups should establish a phased pathway for data element valorization. Data standardization system is established through resourceization, then data circulation and data rights are realized through assetization, and finally data securitization is completed by capitalization. If the overload risk of decentralized innovation occurs in practice, the innovation decision should be converged in time, and the focused innovation strategy should be mixed to balance the scale economy effect of data elements and the cost of innovation decision combination. At the same time, it is necessary to force the innovation R&D team to disengage from the established data system to carry out disruptive conceptualization and innovation in a specific cycle to avoid cognitive inertia and path dependence.

Second, it is recommended to adopt a dual-track innovative decision-making structure. Maintain focused innovation investment in core business areas, while setting up independent incubation units to carry out scenario-driven dispersed exploration.

Third, innovation decisions need to be dynamically matched with the data scenarios application orientation. In the technology catching-up phase, a focused innovation strategy should be adopted to rapidly form technical barriers. In the technology disruption phase, the proportion of dispersed innovation should be moderately increased and the commercialization of entrepreneurial activities should be accelerated by relying on highly scalable scenarios.

Fourth, the policy level should focus on fostering a scenario ecology for data application. (1) Establish sector-specific data exchange ecosystems (e.g., trusted medical data circulation platforms) to lower data application barriers for SMEs through scenario specialization. Such platforms provide scenario validation mechanisms, thereby amplifying data elements’ catalytic effects on innovation decisions. (2) Develop cross-regional data collaboration scenarios. Leverage scenario scalability to unleash data’s scale economies while refining data asset valuation frameworks and risk-hedging instruments to prevent innovation bubbles induced by over-capitalization. In addition, startups operating in highly specialized or highly scrutinized sectors should avoid treating application scenarios as generic digitalization labels. Instead, they should formulate proactive scenario-specific strategies that connect data use with concrete regulatory, operational, sustainability, or inter-organizational collaboration requirements.

Limitations and future research

This study has several limitations. First, although we controlled for startup age, startup size, and high-tech enterprise status, we did not incorporate founder- or top-management-team-level human capital characteristics. In entrepreneurial firms, factors such as founders’ educational background, prior entrepreneurial experience, industry tenure, and managerial digital competence may shape how data resources are interpreted, how innovation decisions are organized, and how effectively data element valorization is translated into entrepreneurial quality. Therefore, omitted variable bias cannot be fully ruled out. Future research should incorporate entrepreneur- and team-level controls and, where possible, combine firm-level and managerial-level data to better isolate the independent effect of data element valorization.

Second, although the sample was appropriate for testing the proposed relationships, it was drawn from startups in Beijing, Shanghai, Hangzhou, and Shenzhen and accessed partly through venture capital networks. As a result, the sample is more likely to capture firms that are relatively formalized, innovation-active, and embedded in resource-rich entrepreneurial ecosystems, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to startups in less developed regions, outside major digital clusters, or without venture capital connections. These cohort characteristics may also have influenced the structural relationships observed in this study. Future studies should therefore test the model using broader and more diverse samples, including non-VC-backed firms and firms from non-core regions.

Third, this study relies on cross-sectional data and therefore cannot capture the temporal evolution of the relationships among data valorization, innovation decisions, and entrepreneurial quality. In particular, we are unable to examine whether the negative effects of dispersed innovation may change over time as firms accumulate learning, coordination routines, or dynamic capabilities. Future research should adopt longitudinal or panel designs to investigate the co-evolutionary mechanisms linking data valorization, innovation decisions, and adaptive capability development.

Finally, the focal dependent variable in this study is defined at the firm level. Broader regional outcomes, such as employment creation and local economic contribution, are better understood as downstream socio-economic spillovers of high-quality entrepreneurship rather than as indicators of entrepreneurial quality itself. Future research may explicitly distinguish firm-level entrepreneurial quality from these external spillover outcomes and examine their sequential relationships.

Nanopore sequencing metrics and methodology

To clarify our long-read sequencing protocol and address the “missing pieces” regarding metrics:

•Enrichment & Amplification: We used Exonuclease V (NEB) to degrade linear nuclear DNA, followed by long-range PCR with a single primer pair (F: ccgcttctggccacagcacttaaacac; R: ggaggatggtggtcaagggacccctat) to generate a 16.1 kb amplicon of the entire mitochondrial genome [3].

•Bioinformatics: Sequencing was performed using the MinION platform (R9.4.1 flow cell; Oxford Nanopore Technologies, Oxford, UK). Data were base-called using Guppy (v6.1.5, SUP mode) and aligned to the reference genome using minimap2 (v2.22) [4]. Clinical strategy and turnaround time

In stage 2, our protocol allows for whole mtDNA sequencing with Sanger and/or long-read sequencing. Although Nanopore sequencing provides robust long-read analytical capabilities, its clinical cost-effectiveness often necessitates sample batching (e.g., 12 samples per run). To prioritize a faster turnaround time (TAT) and avoid delays associated with sample pooling, we currently utilize Sanger sequencing for routine stage 2 analysis. However, we recognize that long-read sequencing is uniquely advantageous for identifying complex structural rearrangements and large-scale deletions.

Classification of sequencing technologies

We thank the reader for the clarification on terminology. While the term “Next-Generation Sequencing” (NGS) has historically been used as a broad umbrella, we agree that in the current landscape, it is more precise to distinguish between second-generation (short-read) and third-generation (long-read) technologies [3]. We will adopt this more precise terminology in future communications to provide better clarity.

We believe these clarifications reinforce the technical validity and practical utility of our platform.

Pairing Anderson motives via formal residues in the Frobenius endomorphism
Quentin Gazda, Andreas Maurischat

Abstract
Anderson modules form a generalization of Drinfeld modules and are commonly understood as the counterpart of abelian varieties but with function field coefficients. In an attempt to study their “motivic theory”, two objects of semilinear algebra are attached to an Anderson module: its motive and its dual motive . While the former is better suited to follow the analogy with Grothendieck motives, the latter has proven much useful in the study of transcendence questions in positive characteristic. Despite sharing similar definitions, the relationship between motives and dual motives has remained nebulous. Over perfect fields, it was only proved recently by the second author that the finite generation of the motive is equivalent to the finite generation of the dual motive, answering a long-standing open question in function field arithmetic (the “abelian equals A A -finite” theorem). This work constructs a perfect pairing among the motive and the dual motive of an Anderson module, with values in a module of differentials, thus answering a question raised by Hartl and Juschka. Our construction involves taking the residue of certain formal power series in the Frobenius endomorphism. Although it may seem peculiar, this pairing is natural and compatible with certain base change. It also comes with several new consequences in function field arithmetic; for example, we generalize the “abelian equals A A -finite” theorem to all perfect algebras.

of

admit a unique expansion as

for some

and coefficients

,

. Then, in fact

for all

This is the usual mandatory section where we recall notations and definitions of Anderson’s

,

and

. We allow ourselves to generalize slightly the usual setting to permit general

3.1
Preliminaries on forms of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}

In this minimal subsection, we recall some known facts and warnings on forms of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}
. To start with, let
R
be a ring of characteristic
p>0
and let
G
be a smooth affine
\mathbb{F}
-vector space scheme over
R
. We consider the following two sets of
\mathbb{F}
-vector space schemes homomorphisms over
R
:

M(G):=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}/R}(G,\mathbb{G}_{a}),\quad\text{and}\quad N(G):=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}/R}(\mathbb{G}_{a},G).

The former is a left-
R[\tau]
-module and the latter is a right-
R[\tau]
-module through the identification of
R[\tau]
with
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{G}_{a})
. Because left-
R[\tau]
-modules are better behaved than right ones (
e.g.
footnote
3
),
M
is generally a better invariant than
N
.

Remark 3.1
.

To show how
N
could be ill-behaved, we recall Rosenlicht’s example. Let
R=k
be an imperfect field, choose
a\in k\setminus k^{q}
, and set
k^{\prime}:=k(a^{1/q})
. Let
G
be the
k
-subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{a}\times\mathbb{G}_{a}
consisting of couples
(x,y)
subject to the relation
x^{q}=y+ay^{q}
. Then
G
is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a}
but its base change along the inclusion
k\to k^{\prime}
is, via the map

\mathbb{G}_{a,k^{\prime}}\longrightarrow G_{k^{\prime}},\quad t\longmapsto(t+a^{1/q}t^{q},t^{q}).

As a consequence
N(G)=(0)
–as any non zero quotient of
\mathbb{G}_{a,k}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a}
–but
N(G_{k^{\prime}})\cong k^{\prime}[\tau]
. In particular
N
does not commute with flat base change in general, although
M
does (see Remark 3.3 in
[
H
]
).

We say that
G
is
a form of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}
is there exists a faithfully flat ring homomorphism
f:R\to S
such that the base change
G_{S}
of
G
along
f
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a,S}^{d}
. In the previous remark, Rosentlich’s example is a form of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
. From now on, we assume that
G
is a form of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}
.

In this situation, there is an obvious relation between
M
and
N
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The map
N(G)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{left}-R[\tau]}(M(G),R[\tau])
,
n\mapsto(m\mapsto m\circ n)
is an isomorphism of right-
R[\tau]
-modules.

Proof.

The assignments
S\mapsto N(G_{S}):=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}/S}(\mathbb{G}_{a,S},G_{S})
and
S\mapsto M(G_{S})
are well-known to be sheaves for the flat topology. We claim that

S\longmapsto\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{left}-S[\tau]}(M(G_{S}),S[\tau])

(6)

is one as well. To see this, fix a faithfully flat ring homomorphism
R\to S
and consider the left-exact sequence of
R
-modules
0\to R\to S\to S\otimes_{R}S
. Right-tensoring this sequence along the flat map
R\to R[\tau]
and applying the left-exact functor
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{left}-R[\tau]}(M(G),-)
, we get an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{left}-R[\tau]}(M(G),R[\tau])\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{left}-R[\tau]}(M(G),S[\tau])\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{left}-R[\tau]}(M(G),S\otimes_{R}S[\tau]).

By tensor-hom adjunction and the fact that
M
commutes with flat base change, this sequence coincides with the descent sequence for (
6
) along
R\to S
. Therefore (
6
) is a sheaf for the flat topology.

From this claim we may argue by descent and thus it suffices to prove the statement under the assumption that
G
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}
, which is then clear.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Note however that the map
M(G)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{right}-R[\tau]}(N(G),R[\tau])
is generally not an isomorphism,
e.g.
for Rosentlich’s example. This is because the latter, as opposed to (
6
), does not satisfy descent.

Proposition 3.4
.

M(G)
is of finite presentation and flat as a left-
R[\tau]
-module.

Proof.

Let
R\to S
be a faithfully flat ring homomorphism on which
G
splits. Since
S\otimes_{R}R[\tau]\cong S[\tau]
, we have as right-
S[\tau]
-modules:

S[\tau]\otimes_{R[\tau]}M(G)\cong S\otimes_{R}M(G)\cong M(G_{S})\cong S[\tau]^{d}.

The classical descent arguments carry to the non-commutative situation
[Stack Project: 058Q]
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

The formation of
N
commutes with flat base change among perfect rings. If
R
is perfect, then
N(G)
is finitely generated as a right-
R[\tau]
-module.

Proof.

We have
N(G_{S})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{S[\tau]}(M(G_{S}),S[\tau])\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{R[\tau]}(M(G),S[\tau])
. By
[
B
, X§1 Proposition 8.(b)]
, the latter is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Hom}_{R[\tau]}(M(G),R[\tau])\otimes_{R[\tau]}S[\tau]
if
R[\tau]\to S[\tau]
is right-flat. This happens if
R
and
S
are perfect, as then
S[\tau]\cong R[\tau]\otimes_{R}S
. This proves the first assertion.

For the second assertion, we may use again that
R[\tau]\to S[\tau]
is right-faithfully flat and that
N(G)\otimes_{R[\tau]}S[\tau]\cong S[\tau]^{d}
.
∎

3.2
Motives and comotives

Let

be an
\mathbb{F}
-algebra and let

\mathbb{F}
-algebra homomorphism; we will voluntarily forget

An

-module of constant rank and

-power torsion.

An

-comotive over

(N,\tau_{N})

is a finite projective

-module of constant rank and

-linear map

\tau(\mathfrak{j})
-power torsion.

A large supply of

-motives and (respectively

-comotives) are obtained from
abelian (respectively
A
-finite) Anderson

-modules
whose definition was recalled as Definition
1.1
above. Let

be an Anderson
A
-module over
R
. We consider the following two groups of
\mathbb{F}
-vector space schemes homomorphisms over

3.1
:

Both are naturally

-modules where

acts on

and
R
acts on
\mathbb{G}_{a}
. They also admit an action of the

-Frobenius

acting on
\mathbb{G}_{a}
that we denote by

defines a left

-module,

defines a right

-module. Accordingly, we obtain

In addition, Condition

of Definition

ensures that their cokernel are respectively
\mathfrak{j}
-power torsion and
\tau(\mathfrak{j})
-power torsion. However
(M(E),\tau_{M})
(respectively
(N(E),\tau_{N})

Definition 3.7
.

We say that

is

is finite projective of constant rank over

. We say that

is

(or

is finite projective of constant rank over

.

One goal of this work is to show that these two notions are equivalent whenever

is perfect.

We end this section with well-known result of Anderson.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
E
be an Anderson

-motive over
R
. Then, there is an exact sequence of

-modules

0\longrightarrow N(E)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-\tau}N(E)\longrightarrow E(R)\longrightarrow 0.

Proof.

For the map

, we take

; it is clearly surjective (a preimage of

being
(1\mapsto e)\in N(E)
), and its precomposition with
\operatorname{id}-\tau
on

is zero as

. To prove that the sequence is exact, we use faithfully flat descent to reduce to the case where

which is already treated in
[
HJ
, Proposition 2.5.8]
.
∎

4.1
The
\sigma
-adic topology

We start with the topology on

.

Note that

is finitely generated as a module over

by Lemma

.

Definition 4.1
.

A
R[\![\sigma]\!]
-lattice
in

is a finitely generated
R[\![\sigma]\!]
-submodule
\Lambda
of

that contains a finite generating subset of
M(\!(\sigma)\!)
over
R(\!(\sigma)\!)
.

The notion of lattices allows us to define a linear topology on
M(\!(\sigma)\!)
. Let
\Lambda
be an
R[\![\sigma]\!]
-lattice in
M(\!(\sigma)\!)
; we call
the
\sigma
-topology on
M(\!(\sigma)\!)
the one given by the fundamental system of open submodules
(\sigma^{n}\Lambda)_{n\geq 0}
. This topology does not depend on the choice of the lattice

, as the next lemma shows.

Lemma 4.2
.

Given two
R[\![\sigma]\!]
-lattices
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}
in
M(\!(\sigma)\!)
, there are integers
n_{1}\geq n_{2}
such that

\sigma^{n_{1}}\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma^{n_{2}}\Lambda.

Proof.

Since
\Lambda
contains a finite generating subset
G
of
M(\!(\sigma)\!)
, every element of a generating set for
\Lambda^{\prime}
is an
R(\!(\sigma)\!)
-linear combination of elements of
G
. Choosing
n_{2}
such that all the coefficients of those linear combinations are in
\sigma^{n_{2}}R[\![\sigma]\!]
implies
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma^{n_{2}}\Lambda
. For the other inclusion, switch the roles of
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
in the previous argument to obtain an integer
-n_{1}
with
\Lambda\subseteq\sigma^{-n_{1}}\Lambda^{\prime}
so that
\sigma^{n_{1}}\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}
.

The inequality
n_{1}\geq n_{2}
is a consequence of the inclusion
\sigma^{n_{1}}\Lambda\subseteq\sigma^{n_{2}}\Lambda
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
a\in A
be an element of positive degree. Then, there exists an
R[\![\sigma]\!]
-lattice
\Lambda\subset M(\!(\sigma)\!)
and an integer

such that

\text{for\penalty\ all\penalty\ }n\geq\ell:\quad\tau\Lambda\subset a^{n}\Lambda

Proof.

Let

be a finitely generated
R
-submodule which both generates
M
as an
R[\tau]
-module and an
R[a]
-module, where
R[a]
is understood as a subring of
A\otimes R
. Consider
\Lambda_{0}
the
R[\![\sigma]\!]
-lattice in
M(\!(\sigma)\!)
generated by
S
. Let
(v_{i})_{i\in I}
be a finite
R
-generating subset of

. For

, we write

\tau v_{i}=\sum_{j\in I}{p_{ij}\cdot v_{j}}

(7)

for coefficients

indexed by

. Let also
\ell:=\max_{i,j}\{\deg_{a}(p_{ij})\}
and set

\Lambda=\sum_{\ell>k\geq 0}{a^{k}\Lambda_{0}}.

We claim that
\ell
and
\Lambda
are as sought,

, that
\tau\Lambda\subset a^{n}\Lambda
for all

. Indeed, let

be the matrix

with coefficients in

and indexed by

which we write as

P=P_{0}+P_{1}a+\ldots+P_{\ell}a^{\ell}\quad\text{for}\quad P_{k}\in\mathscr{M}_{I}(R).

From (

(1-P_{0}\sigma)\tau(v_{i})_{i\in I}=(\tau-P_{0})(v_{i})_{i\in I}=\left(\sum_{\ell\geq k>0}{P_{k}a^{k}}\right)(v_{i})_{i\in I}

and then

\tau(v_{i})_{i\in I}=a(1-P_{0}\sigma)^{-1}\left(\sum_{\ell\geq k>0}{P_{k}a^{k-1}}\right)(v_{i})_{i\in I}.

From this expression we deduce

\tau\Lambda_{0}\subseteq a\Lambda

(8)

and then
\Lambda=\sum_{\ell>k\geq 0}{a^{k}\Lambda_{0}}=\Lambda_{0}+\sum_{\ell>k>0}{a^{k}\Lambda_{0}}\subset\tau\Lambda_{0}+a\sum_{\ell>k\geq 0}{a^{k}\Lambda_{0}}

\Lambda\subseteq a\Lambda.

Inductively, for

\Lambda\subseteq a^{k}\Lambda\subseteq a^{\ell}\Lambda

\tau a^{k}\Lambda_{0}=a^{k}\tau\Lambda_{0}\subseteq a^{k+1}\Lambda\subseteq a^{\ell}\Lambda

The

Note the canonical isomorphism

Remark 4.8

The connection between the

\infty
-topology is made through the following lemma.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\Lambda
be an
\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(R)
-lattice of
\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(M)
. Then, there exists an integer
s_{0}>0
such that
\sigma^{s}\Lambda\subset\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}\Lambda
for all
s\geq s_{0}
.

Proof.

Because
M
comes from an Anderson
A
-module over
R
, it is finitely generated as an
R[\tau]
-module by Lemma
3.8
. In particular there exists a finite
R
-submodule
V\subset M
for which

M=V+\tau V+\tau^{2}V+\ldots

(12)

If
\Lambda_{0}\subset\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(M)
denotes a finitely generated
\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(R)
-submodule of
\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(M)
which generates it and contains
V
, (
12
) implies:

\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(M)=\Lambda_{0}+\tau\Lambda_{0}+\tau^{2}\Lambda_{0}+\ldots

In particular, there exists some integer
N_{0}>0
for which we have both

\sigma\Lambda_{0}\subset\Lambda_{0}+\tau\Lambda_{0}+\ldots+\tau^{N_{0}}\Lambda_{0},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}^{-1}\Lambda_{0}\subset\Lambda_{0}+\tau\Lambda_{0}+\ldots+\tau^{N_{0}}\Lambda_{0}.

(13)

By induction, the first inclusion implies
\sigma^{n}\Lambda_{0}\subset\Lambda_{0}+\tau\Lambda_{0}+\ldots+\tau^{N_{0}}\Lambda_{0}
for all
n\geq 0
. Setting

\Lambda^{\mathrm{st}}_{0}:=\sum_{n\geq 0}{\sigma^{n}\Lambda_{0}}

gives a module stable by
\sigma
such that
\Lambda^{\mathrm{st}}_{0}\subset\Lambda_{0}+\tau\Lambda_{0}+\ldots+\tau^{N_{0}}\Lambda_{0}
, and which contains
\Lambda_{0}
. Hence it is generating, and is contained in a finitely generated submodule; note that it is not necessarily a lattice as it may not be of finite type (typically if

is not noetherian). Applying
\sigma^{N_{0}}
to the second inclusion of (
13
) yields
\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}^{-1}\sigma^{N_{0}}\Lambda_{0}\subset\Lambda_{0}+\sigma\Lambda_{0}+\ldots+\sigma^{N_{0}}\Lambda_{0}
. Applying then
\sigma^{n}
and summing over all

gives
\sigma^{N_{0}}\Lambda^{\mathrm{st}}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}\Lambda^{\mathrm{st}}_{0}
.

By construction of
\Lambda^{\mathrm{st}}_{0}
, there exists two integers
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
for which
\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}^{k_{1}}\Lambda^{\mathrm{st}}_{0}\subset\Lambda\subset\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}^{-k_{2}}\Lambda^{\mathrm{st}}_{0}
. Set
s_{0}:=N_{0}(k_{1}+k_{2}+1)
. Then for all

,

\sigma^{s}\Lambda=\sigma^{N_{0}(k_{1}+k_{2}+1)+(s-s_{0})}\Lambda\subset\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}^{-k_{2}}\sigma^{N_{0}(k_{1}+k_{2}+1)}\sigma^{(s-s_{0})}\Lambda^{\mathrm{st}}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}^{-k_{2}}\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}^{k_{1}+k_{2}+1}\Lambda^{\mathrm{st}}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}\Lambda.

In particular, the integer
N_{1}
works as desired.
∎

Note that
\tau
acts bijectively on
\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(M)
as is shown,
e.g.
, in
[
G
, Proposition 3.17]
. In particular,
\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(M)
is canonically an
R[\tau,\tau^{-1}]
-module. With the help of Proposition
4.9
and reasoning as in the proof of Proposition
4.6
, we obtain:

Proposition 4.10
.

The left-action of
R[\tau,\tau^{-1}]
on
\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(M)
extends uniquely to a continuous left-action of
R(\!(\sigma)\!)
.

4.3
Isomorphism of topological spaces

The next theorem is a surprising byproduct of most results of Sections

&
4.2

[

, Proposition 7.8 (a)]
.

Theorem 4.11
.

The topological
R
-modules
\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(M)
and

Proof.

Proposition
4.6
gives a unique continuous action of
\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(R)
on

. Hence, the universal property of the tensor product then gives a unique
\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(R)
-linear map
\iota_{1}
making the following diagram commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(R)\times M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M(\!(\sigma)\!)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(M)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{1}}

Similarly, Proposition
4.10
gives a unique left-
R(\!(\sigma)\!)
-linear map

inserting in a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M(\!(\sigma)\!)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{B}_{\infty}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R(\!(\sigma)\!)\times M}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{2}}

We claim that

and

are continuous. Indeed, let
V\subset M
be a finite

-submodule which is both generating for the

and the

-module actions. Let

-lattice it generates, and
\Lambda_{\sigma}\subset M(\!(\sigma)\!)
the

-lattice it generates. By Proposition

there exists

large enough for which

. We then set

which defines another

which is stable under the action of

. In particular

.

For

implies the existence of

is also

-linear and
\iota_{2}

That

∎

5
The residue-in-
\tau

We again assume that the
A
-algebra

is perfect.
We let

be an Anderson

-module over
R
,
M(E)=\mathrm{Hom}(E,\mathbb{G}_{a})
be its motive and
N(E)=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{G}_{a},E)
its comotive. For better readability, we denote by

the

-module scheme structure of

.

Assuming that

is abelian or coabelian, we reinterpret the pairing of Hartl and Juschka
[

, Theorem 2.5.13]
and construct an
A\otimes R
-linear map

\mathrm{Res}_{\tau}:\tau^{*}M(E)\otimes_{A\otimes R}N(E)\longrightarrow\Omega_{A/\mathbb{F}}\otimes R.

(14)

5.1
Construction of
\mathrm{Res}_{\tau}

The construction of the map
\mathrm{Res}_{\tau}
is done in several steps.

Step 1.

Given
m:E\to\mathbb{G}_{a}
in
M(E)
and
n:\mathbb{G}_{a}\to E
in
N(E)
, we may compose them to obtain
m\circ n\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{G}_{a})=R[\tau]
. Better, we obtain a map

M(E)\times N(E)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(A,R[\tau]),\quad(m,n)\longmapsto(a\mapsto m\circ\Phi(a)\circ n)

(15)

into the
\mathbb{F}
-linear homomorphism from
A
to
R[\tau]
. It is, by design,
A
-bilinear and hence factors uniquely through
M(E)\otimes_{A}N(E)
.

Step 2.

If
E
is abelian we may upgrade the previous construction thanks to Corollary
4.4
and Proposition
4.6
. Indeed, the composition map
M(E)\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}N(E)\to\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{G}_{a})=R[\tau]
promotes, extending scalars on the left along
R[\tau]\to R(\!(\sigma)\!)
, to

M(E)(\!(\sigma)\!)\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}N(E)\longrightarrow R(\!(\sigma)\!).

The same formula (
15
) allows to define a map

M(E)\otimes_{A}N(E)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(K_{\infty},R(\!(\sigma)\!))

(16)

assigning, to a couple
(m,n)
, the map
f\in K_{\infty}\mapsto(m\circ\Phi(f))\circ n\in R(\!(\sigma)\!)
where
\Phi
now denotes the extended action of Proposition
4.6
.

Respectively, if
E
is coabelian, scalars are extended on the right and we rather consider the composition
m\circ(\Phi(f)\circ n)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The map (
16
) lands in
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{cont}}(K_{\infty},R(\!(\sigma)\!))
;
i.e.
the submodule of continuous homomorphisms with respect to the
\infty
-adic topology on
K_{\infty}
and the
\sigma
-adic topology on
R(\!(\sigma)\!)
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the continuous action of
K_{\infty}
on
M
. In details, let
m\in M
,
n\in N
and let
h:K_{\infty}\to R(\!(\sigma)\!)
be the
\mathbb{F}
-linear homomorphism associated to
m\otimes n
via (
16
). We shall show that, for all
D\geq 0
there exists
\delta_{D}
such that if
g\in K_{\infty}
with
v_{\infty}(g)\geq\delta_{D}
then
h(g)\in\sigma^{D}R[\![\sigma]\!]
.

Let
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{d})
be generators of
M
over
R[\tau]
and let
u
be the maximal degree of the polynomials in
\tau
appearing as coefficients in an expression of
m
written in
\kappa
. Let
\Lambda_{\kappa}
be the
R[\![\sigma]\!]
-lattice of
M(\!(\sigma)\!)
generated by
\kappa
(so that
m\in\sigma^{-u}\Lambda_{\kappa}
). Let also
v:=\max_{i}\{\deg_{\tau}(\kappa_{i}\circ n)\}
so that any element of
\Lambda_{\kappa}
composed with
n
belongs to
\sigma^{-v}R[\![\sigma]\!]
.

By Proposition
4.6
, let
\delta_{D}
be such that
g\Lambda_{\kappa}\subset\sigma^{C}\Lambda_{\kappa}
for all
g\in K_{\infty}
with

, where

. Then,

m\circ g\circ n\in\sigma^{-u}\Lambda_{\kappa}\circ g\circ n\subseteq\sigma^{C-u}\Lambda_{\kappa}\circ n\subseteq\sigma^{C-(u+v)}R[\![\sigma]\!]=\sigma^{D}R[\![\sigma]\!].

∎

Step 3.

Still, the morphism (
16
) is not
R
-linear. To address this, for some integer
\ell
, we compose with the continuous map
\mathrm{coeff}_{-\ell}:R(\!(\sigma)\!)\to R
of Definition
2.9
which by Remark
2.10
amounts to

M(E)\otimes_{A}N(E)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{cont}}(K_{\infty},R),\quad m\otimes n\longmapsto(g\mapsto\mathrm{coeff}_{0}(\tau^{\ell}\circ m\circ\Phi(g)\circ n)).

(17)

The resulting map (
17
) is now
R
-sesquilinear with respect to the

-power map on
R
, hence factors through
\tau^{\ell*}M(E)\otimes_{A\otimes R}N(E)
. While it is tempting to take

to have

-linearity, the choice

has the quite pleasant feature that any element in the image of (
17
) vanishes on
A\subset K_{\infty}
. That is, for
\ell=1
, (
17
) becomes:

\widetilde{\operatorname{Res}}_{\tau}:\tau^{*}M(E)\otimes_{A\otimes R}N(E)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{cont}}(K_{\infty}/A,R)

(18)

(the quotient
K_{\infty}/A
is endowed with the quotient topology).

Step 4.

The final step relies on residue-duality (
e.g.

[
P
, Theorem 8]
), which asserts that the pairing

\Omega_{A/\mathbb{F}}\times(K_{\infty}/A)\longrightarrow\mathbb{F},\quad\omega\otimes(g+A)\longmapsto\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{\infty}|\mathbb{F}}(\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}(g\omega)).

identifies
\Omega_{A/\mathbb{F}}
as the continuous dual of
K_{\infty}/A
. Extending scalars along
\mathbb{F}\to R
, we get an
A\otimes R
-linear isomorphism

\Omega_{A/\mathbb{F}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}R\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{cont}}(K_{\infty}/A,R).

(19)

The combination of (
18
) and (
19
) yields the construction of (
14
).

Definition 5.2
.

If
E
is abelian, we define
the residue-in-
\tau
pairing of
E
to be the unique
A\otimes R
-linear map

\mathrm{Res}_{\tau}:\tau^{*}M(E)\otimes_{A\otimes R}N(E)\longrightarrow\Omega_{A/\mathbb{F}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}R

assigning to an elementary tensor
m\otimes n
the unique differential form
\omega
which satisfies, for all
g\in K_{\infty}
,

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{\infty}|\mathbb{F}}(\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}(g\omega))=\mathrm{coeff}_{0}(\tau\circ(m\circ\Phi(g))\circ n).

If
E
is coabelian, we define this map via the same formula, but with a change of parenthesis:

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{\infty}|\mathbb{F}}(\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}(g\omega))=\mathrm{coeff}_{0}(\tau\circ m\circ(\Phi(g)\circ n)).

In the following, we will omit the inner most parenthesis to unify presentation for both case of
E
being abelian or
E
being coabelian.
Indeed, we will see in Corollary
6.1
, that our pairing enables us to show that the properties of being abelian and being coabelian are equivalent.

Remark 5.3
.

If
C
denotes the curve
\mathbb{P}^{1}
over
\mathbb{F}
and
\infty
the point
[0:1]
, then
A
identifies with
\mathbb{F}[t]
for some function

on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
having a simple pole at
\infty
. The field
K_{\infty}
identifies with
\mathbb{F}(\!(\varpi)\!)
where we set
\varpi:=1/t
.

In this context, the isomorphism
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{cont}}(K_{\infty}/A,R)\to\Omega_{A/\mathbb{F}}^{1}\otimes R
is explicitly given by sending a continuous
\mathbb{F}
-linear map

to the differential

-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{f\left(\varpi^{k+1}\right)t^{k}dt}.

The continuity of

ensures that this sum is finite.

The residue pairing is therefore

\operatorname{Res}_{\tau}(m\otimes n)=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{coeff}_{0}\left(\tau\circ m\circ\Phi(\varpi^{k+1})\circ n\right)t^{k}dt.

An immediate corollary of the construction is the following property (which is less obvious to see in
[

]
).

Proposition 5.4

(Commutation with
\tau
)
.

We have
\operatorname{Res}_{\tau}((\tau\circ m)\otimes n)=\tau(\operatorname{Res}_{\tau}(m\otimes(n\circ\tau)))
in

As the residue duality is compatible with the
\tau
-action, we can equivalently show the formula for

.

For

, we write
\tau\circ m\circ\Phi(f)\circ n\in R(\!(\sigma)\!)
as

whose zeroth coefficient is

∎

\Phi(t)=g_{r}(\frac{g_{0}}{g_{r}}\sigma^{r}+\frac{g_{1}}{g_{r}}\sigma^{r-1}+\ldots+\frac{g_{r-1}}{g_{r}}\sigma+1)\tau^{r},

we see that

\Phi(t)^{-1}=\sigma^{r}\left(1+\frac{g_{r-1}}{g_{r}}\sigma+\ldots+\frac{g_{1}}{g_{r}}\sigma^{r-1}+\frac{g_{0}}{g_{r}}\sigma^{r}\right)^{-1}\frac{1}{g_{r}}\in\sigma^{r}R[\![\sigma]\!].

In particular, we deduce that the zeroth coefficient of
\tau^{i+1}\Phi(\varpi^{k})\tau^{j}=\tau^{i+1}\Phi(t)^{-k}\tau^{j}
is zero whenever
i+j+1<kr
. Since we chose
i,j\leq r-1
, this shows that this coefficient is
0
if
k\geq 2
.

Therefore, the computation of the pairing reduces to that of the zeroth coefficient of
\tau^{i+1}\Phi(t)^{-1}\tau^{j}
. We have:

\displaystyle\Phi(t)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{r}\left(1+\frac{g_{r-1}}{g_{r}}\sigma+\ldots+\frac{g_{1}}{g_{r}}\sigma^{r-1}+\frac{g_{0}}{g_{r}}\sigma^{r}\right)^{-1}\frac{1}{g_{r}}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{r}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{g_{r-1}}{g_{r}}\sigma+\ldots+\frac{g_{1}}{g_{r}}\sigma^{r-1}+\frac{g_{0}}{g_{r}}\sigma^{r}\right)^{n}}\right]\frac{1}{g_{r}}

The summand can be expanded as follows:

\left(\frac{g_{r-1}}{g_{r}}\sigma+\ldots+\frac{g_{1}}{g_{r}}\sigma^{r-1}+\frac{g_{0}}{g_{r}}\sigma^{r}\right)^{n}=\sum_{1\leq v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\leq r}{\left(\frac{g_{r-v_{1}}}{g_{r}}\right)\sigma^{v_{1}}\left(\frac{g_{r-v_{2}}}{g_{r}}\right)\sigma^{v_{2}}\cdots\left(\frac{g_{r-v_{n}}}{g_{r}}\right)\sigma^{v_{n}}}.

Commuting all
\sigma^{v_{i}}
to the left, altogether we obtain

\Phi(t)^{-1}=\sum_{m\geq 0}{\sigma^{r+m}\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0},\penalty\ v_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\\
v_{1}+\ldots+v_{n}=m\end{subarray}}{\!(-1)^{n}\!\left(\frac{g_{r-v_{1}}}{g_{r}}\right)^{q^{v_{1}+\ldots+v_{n}}}\!\left(\frac{g_{r-v_{2}}}{g_{r}}\right)^{q^{v_{2}+\ldots+v_{n}}}\!\cdots\!\left(\frac{g_{r-v_{n}}}{g_{r}}\right)^{q^{v_{n}}}\!\frac{1}{g_{r}}}}.

This formula suffices to conclude.
∎

7.2
Tensor powers of the Carlitz module

Recall that the Carlitz module
\mathsf{C}
is the Drinfeld module of rank one which is equal to
\mathbb{G}_{a}
and whose action of
t
is described by
\Phi(t)=\theta+\tau
. Both its motive and comotive are free of rank one over
R
, generated by
\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{G}_{a}}
. According to Proposition
7.1
, we have

\mathrm{Res}_{\tau}(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{G}_{a}}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{G}_{a}})=-dt.

We now undertake the computation of the
d
th tensor power of the Carlitz module, where
d
is a positive integer, and where the tensor product is either
\otimes
or
\otimes^{\mathrm{co}}
of Subsection
6.2
(because
\Omega_{A/\mathbb{F}}\cong\mathbb{F}[t]
, the operations
\otimes
and
\otimes^{\mathrm{co}}
are equivalent).

Recall from e.g.
[
BrP
, Section 1.5.3]

that the
d
-th tensor power
\mathsf{C}^{\otimes d}
of the Carlitz module
is the Anderson module
which, as an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space scheme is
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}
, and whose
t
-action corresponds in canonical coordinates to the matrix

\Phi(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\theta&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&&\ddots&\ddots&1\\
\tau&0&\cdots&0&\theta\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{d\times d}(R[\tau]).

The motive
M(\mathsf{C}^{\otimes d})
is a free
R[t]
-module of rank
1
with basis
\{\kappa_{1}:\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}\}
, where
\kappa_{1}
is the projection to the first coordinate, and the comotive
N(\mathsf{C}^{\otimes d})
is a free
R[t]
-module of rank
1
with basis
\{\check{\kappa}_{d}:\mathbb{G}_{a}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}^{d}\}
, where
\check{\kappa}_{d}
corresponds to the
d
th coordinate. Hence computing the pairing, due to
R[t]
-sesquilinearity, amounts to consider
\kappa_{1}\circ\Phi(f)\circ\check{\kappa}_{d}
for
f=\varpi^{k}=t^{-k}\in K_{\infty}
(with
k\geq 1
). In matrix view, this is just the entry in the upper right corner of the matrix
\Phi(f)
and
\mathrm{coeff}_{0}(\tau\circ\kappa_{1}\circ\Phi(f)\circ\check{\kappa}_{d})
is just the right coefficient in
\sigma
of this entry.

The matrix
\Phi(\varpi)=\Phi(t)^{-1}
can be computed using the Gaussian algorithms, taking into account that row operations correspond to multiplications with matrices from the left,
i.e.
, that we have to multiply scalars in the elementary operations from the left. This results in
\Phi(\varpi)=C_{0}+\sigma D
where

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
1&\ddots&&&\vdots\\
-\theta&\ddots&\ddots&&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(-\theta)^{d-2}&\cdots&-\theta&1&0\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{d\times d}(R),\text{ and }

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\left((-\theta)^{q(i-1)}\left(1-\sigma(-\theta)^{qd}\right)^{-1}(-\theta)^{d-j}\right)_{i,j}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{d\times d}(R[\![\sigma]\!]).

The coefficient of
\sigma
of the upper right entry of
\Phi(\varpi)=C_{0}+\sigma D
is therefore the coefficient of
\sigma^{0}
of the upper right entry of
D
,
i.e.
, equals
1
.

For
k\geq 2
, we have

\Phi(\varpi^{k})=(C_{0}+\sigma D)^{k}\equiv C_{0}^{k}+C_{0}^{k-1}\sigma D+C_{0}^{k-2}\sigma DC_{0}+\cdots+\sigma DC_{0}^{k-1}\mod\sigma^{2}.

Since the first row and the last column of
C_{0}
are zero, the upper right entries of all the products on the right hand side are
0
. Hence,
\mathrm{coeff}_{0}(\tau\circ\kappa_{1}\circ\Phi(\varpi^{k})\circ\check{\kappa}_{d})=0
as soon as
k\geq 2
.

In total, we obtain

\operatorname{Res}_{\tau}(\kappa_{1}\otimes\check{\kappa}_{d})=-dt.

7.3
Maurischat’s example

In
[
Ma1
, Example 6.3]
, the second author provided an example of a simple Anderson
t
-module which nevertheless is not pure (unless the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is
2
).
It is defined over the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta)
, and is of dimension
2
and rank
3
. In canonical coordinates, it is given by

\Phi(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\theta+\tau^{2}&\tau^{3}\\
1+\tau&\theta+\tau^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

In
[
Ma2
, Examples 5.4 & 7.3]
, the second author showed furthermore, that a
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta)[t]
-basis
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})
of the motive
M(E)
is given by
e_{1}=\tau\kappa_{2}
,
e_{2}=\kappa_{2}
,
e_{3}=\kappa_{1}
, and a
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta)[t]
-basis
(\check{e}_{1},\check{e}_{2},\check{e}_{3})
of the comotive
N(E)
is given by
\check{e}_{1}=\check{\kappa}_{1}\tau
,
\check{e}_{2}=\check{\kappa}_{1}
,
\check{e}_{3}=\check{\kappa}_{2}
.
Actually, the computations in
[
Ma2
]
didn’t use any property of the base
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta)
, so they are valid over any
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
-algebra
R
, if we choose
\theta
as the image of
t
in
R
.

A straight forward, but tedious computation along the lines of the previous examples leads to

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{\tau}:\tau^{*}M(E)\otimes_{R[t]}N(E)

\displaystyle\to R^{\mathrm{perf}}[t]dt,

\displaystyle(\sum_{i}a_{i}e_{i})\otimes(\sum_{j}b_{j}\check{e}_{j})

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}^{(1)},&a_{2}^{(1)},&a_{3}^{(1)}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1+g&1&-g\\
1&0&-1\\
-g&-1&g\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}\\
b_{2}\\
b_{3}\end{pmatrix}dt

where
g=\theta^{q}+\theta-2t\in\mathbb{F}(\theta)[t]
.

Surprisingly, the image is even in
\mathbb{F}(\theta)[t]dt
so no perfection is needed here.

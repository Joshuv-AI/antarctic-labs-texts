Multiscale structural engineering enables superior energy storage in tetragonal tungsten bronze relaxor ferroelectrics
Authors: Saifei Wang, Juntao Huang, Guangyao Li, Tao Yang, Haihua Huang, Yajie Han, Wei Li, Zhenxiang Cheng, He Qi, Mahesh Kumar Joshi, Peng Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLead-free relaxor ferroelectric ceramics are promising for pulsed-power systems due to their ultrafast discharge and high-power density, yet their practical use remains limited by low breakdown strength and insufficient energy-storage density. Here, we report Ba2La1−xBixTi2Nb3O15 ceramics with a tetragonal tungsten bronze structure, which achieve a recoverable energy density of 14.39 J/cm3 and an efficiency of 87.69% under an ultrahigh field of 1400 kV/cm–one of the highest recoverable energy density values reported for bulk tetragonal tungsten bronze structured ceramics. By combining experiments, first-principles calculations, and finite-element simulations, we unravel a multiscale structural optimization mechanism. Bi3+ incorporation induces oxygen-octahedral distortion that disrupts long-range order and enhances relaxation behavior. Concurrent grain refinement and band gap widening substantially raise the breakdown strength, leading to superior energy-storage properties. Moreover, the material exhibits excellent stability against variations in temperature, frequency, and fatigue cycles. This work establishes a generally applicable multiscale structural-engineering strategy for tetragonal-tungsten-bronze dielectrics with high energy storage performance.Subject terms: Materials science, Condensed-matter physics, Ferroelectrics and multiferroics

Introduction

With the rapid development of renewable energies and growing global electricity demand, efficient and reliable energy storage materials have become an urgent research focus. Among them, dielectric energy storage materials stand out due to their high-power density and fast charge–discharge capabilities. However, their practical application is hindered by low recoverable energy density and limited efficiency1,2.

Dielectric energy storage ceramics, in particular, have attracted widespread attention in pulsed-power systems owing to their high-power density, good stability, and rapid charge–discharge characteristics2,3. Yet, the relatively low recoverable energy density (Wrec) and efficiency (η) of ceramic capacitors continue to restrict their broader use4. Therefore, improving ESP requires simultaneous enhancement of polarization strength and breakdown field strength8.

To date, research on lead-free energy storage ceramics has largely focused on perovskite-structured ceramics, including Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3-based9,10, BaTiO3-based11, and NaNbO3-based12,13 ceramics. Extensive studies have deepened the understanding of energy storage properties in perovskite ceramics. In comparison, tungsten bronze-structured ceramics have received less attention, despite preliminary reports indicating promising energy storage characteristics. As the second-largest class of ferroelectric materials, tetragonal tungsten bronze (TTB) systems are derived from the perovskite structure. The tungsten bronze framework retains the BO6 octahedral skeleton of perovskites but develops into a more complex, open, and often non-stoichiometric arrangement14. Its general formula can be expressed as (A1)2(A2)4(C)4(B1)2(B2)8O30, where the BO6 octahedra creates three distinct interstices: pentagonal (A2), quadrilateral (A2), and triangular (C) sites. Based on their filling, these structures are classified as (I) fully filled (all A1, A2, and C occupied by cations), (II) partially filled (A1 and A2 filled, C sites vacant), or (III) unfilled (partial A-site occupancy and empty C sites). The flexible chemistry of A- and B-site ions allows precise structural tunning and leads to superior ferroelectric properties, drawing considerable research interest. Nevertheless, the energy storage behavior of TTB ceramics remain less understood compared with that of perovskite systems.

Research on the energy storage in tungsten bronze ceramics has mainly centered on Sr1−xBaxNb5O15 (SBN) and Sr2NaNb5O15 (SNN) systems. For example, Zhang et al.15 introduced CuO into SNN via a dual-defect strategy, achieving a recoverable energy density of 4.17 J/cm3 with an efficiency of 89.5% under 350 kV/cm. By incorporating Sb ions into SNN-based ceramics to tailor relaxor behavior, Xu et al.16 reported a Wrec of 2.27 J/cm3 with an efficiency of 93.3%. In the SBN system, Gao et al.17 successfully promoted polar nano regions (PNRs) by substituting Sm3+ ions at A-sites, obtaining a maximum Wrec of 9 J/cm3 and an efficiency of 84% in a Gd-modified composition. While these results confirm the promise of tungsten bronze ceramics for energy storage, their ESP still lags behind that of perovskite counterparts, and the underlying mechanisms are not fully resolved. However, current research mainly focuses on single-scale structural regulation, while the construction of multi-scale structures and their synergistic regulation mechanisms remain underexplored.

Bi3+ possesses stereochemically active 6 s² lone pair electrons, which can induce local lattice distortion and enhance polarization. This is crucial for designing high energy storage materials9. Guided by this mechanism, we first conducted first-principles calculations on undoped and Bi-doped Ba2LaTi2Nb3O15 (BLTN) ferroelectrics. The results predicted that both Bi substitution and increasing Bi content lead to a progressive increase in the band gap of the BLTN ferroelectric, which is generally favorable for enhancing breakdown strength (Eb) and ultimately improving ESP. Driven by these computational predictions, we designed and synthesized Bi-doped BLTN ceramics with a TTB structure, and systematically studied their ESP and corresponding multiscale structural synergistic regulation mechanism. Ultimately, under an ultrahigh electric field of 1400 kV/cm, a Wrec of 14.39 J/cm3 and a high η of 87.69% were achieved, exceeding most previously reported values for bulk TTB-structured ceramics. These results highlight the potential of these materials for pulse power applications. Furthermore, by combining experiments, calculations, and microstructural characterization, this study elucidates how multiscale structural features from atomic-level lattice distortion to micrometer-scale grain characteristics synergistically govern the ESP, offering new insights into the multiscale structure optimization mechanisms of tungsten bronze-structured ceramics.

Results and discussion

Calculation verification and structural analysis

To assess the feasibility of Bi3+ doping and guide the experimental design, first-principles calculations were performed on the Ba2LaTi2Nb3O15 (BLTN) system, and the results are shown in Fig. 1. The calculated bandgap of undoped BLTN is 1.81 eV. Upon Bi3+ substitution, the calculated bandgap increases to 1.86 eV for x = 0.25 and further to 2.21 eV for x = 0.50, indicating that Bi doping progressively widens the bandgap. This trend can be ascribed to the introduction of wide-bandgap oxides and the structural disorder induced by the Bi3+ ions, both of which are beneficial to enhancing the insulating behavior. As foreign atoms occupy equivalent lattice sites, the differences in electronegativity, mass, and radius cause lattice distortion and atomic disorder, which can seriously hinder the transport of electrons and thereby increase the Eb9.

The bonding strength between atoms was analyzed by calculating the integrated Crystal Orbital Hamiltonian Population (ICOHP), as shown in Fig. 1d. The ICOHP values for the Bi-O bond and La-O bond are −0.08 and −0.05, respectively, indicating that the Bi-O bond is stronger than that of La-O bond. This stronger bond enhances the stability of structure and suppress the carrier mobility. Consequently, the larger band gap and reduced carrier mobility lead to a higher breakdown field. Figure 1d presents the Crystal Orbital Hamiltonian Population (COHP) analysis for the La-O and Bi-O bonds; the Bi-O bond is found to be slightly stronger than the La-O bond. The electronic localization function (ELF) of BLTN and BLBTN shown in Fig. 1e, after Bi replaces La, electrons tend to be localized around Bi atoms, resulting in a decrease in carrier concentration and ultimately an increase in resistivity.

As shown in Fig. S1a, the exponential increase in absorption near the band edge is characteristic of indirect bandgap transitions in complex oxides and primarily reflects intrinsic band-to-band absorption18. To validate the computational results, the experimental bandgap was determined from UV-vis absorption spectra via Tauc-plots (Fig. S1b). the optical bandgap rises gradually from 3.06 eV (x = 0.35) to 3.16 eV (x = 0.50), exhibiting the same trend as the DFT predictions, albeit with larger absolute values-a discrepancy commonly encountered because the generalized gradient approximation with the PBE functional usually underestimates the bandgap5. Nevertheless, this discrepancy does not affect our conclusion, which is based on the trend. This trend confirms that our strategy of increasing the bandgap via Bi doping is effective. Moreover, the PDOS analysis (Fig. 1) reveals that the valence-band maximum near the Fermi level is dominated by O-p orbitals, whereas the conduction-band minimum arises primarily from Ti-d and Nb-d states, consistent with the typical electronic structure of Nb-based TTB oxides.

The phase composition of the BLBTN-x ceramics was examined by XRD, as shown in Fig. 2a. All diffraction peaks align well with the reference patterns JCPDS No. 01-074-8572, confirming that Bi3+-doped BLTN ceramics adopt a pure tungsten bronze structure without detectable secondary phases. This suggests that Bi can readily enter the BLTN lattice, owing to the similar ionic radii of Bi3+ (1.32 Å) and La3+ (1.35 Å). In the enlarged view of the (200)/(620) region, the (002) and (620) peaks progressively merge with increasing Bi content, which is associated with a reduction in tetragonality17. To quantify the structural evolution, Rietveld refinement (Fig. 2b) was performed on all XRD patterns using the P4bm space group for the tetragonal phase. The refined profiles (Fig. S2) and unit-cell parameters (Table S1) show excellent agreement between experimental and calculated data, confirming that all samples maintain P4bm symmetry and exhibit an increase in structural symmetry along with an increased c/a ratio upon Bi substitution. To further verify the ion doping, a separate HAADF-STEM measurement was carried out, and atomic mapping images are shown in Fig. 2e. Bi3+ was observed to have replaced La3+, which is consistent with the XRD results, thus demonstrating the effectiveness of our design strategy.

The influence of Bi3+-ion substitution on the local structure was further probed by Raman spectroscopy (Fig. 2c). In the range of 50–1000 cm-1, the spectra can be divided into three characteristic regions: (I) low wavenumber modes below 200 cm-1, primarily associated with A-site cation vibration; (II) modes between 200 and 400 cm-1, related to tilting and rotation of TiO6 octahedra19; and (III) high-wavenumber modes above 400 cm-1, corresponding to B–O bond stretching vibrations. Additionally, no extra peaks were observed in Raman spectra, indicating the absence of secondary phases in the samples. This is consistent with XRD results, further confirming that the BLBTN-x ceramics exhibit a single TTB structure. The wavenumber v1 peak (above 800 cm-1) is sensitive to BO6 octahedral distortion20. In particular, the distortion of NbO6 octahedra is strongly affected by the local environments at the A1 and A2 sites, with spectral shifts arising from both the compressive stress imposed by A-site ions and intrinsic octahedral distortion21. As shown by the fitting results in Fig. 2d, increasing the Bi content induces a distinct red-shift of the v5 mode together with a marked increase in its full width at half maximum (FWHM), indicating significant peak broadening. As shown in Fig. S3, the low wavenumber peak (less than 200 cm-1) exhibits a blue shift, reflecting the enhanced compression effect of A-site ions on the BO6 octahedron, i.e., an increase in octahedral distortion22. Such octahedral distortion is known to disrupt long-range ferroelectric order and promote the formation of PNRs, a key feature of relaxor ferroelectrics16.

Dielectric properties and energy storage performance

The frequency-dependent relative dielectric constant (ɛr) and loss tangent (tan δ) of the BLBTN-x ceramics are displayed in Fig. 3a–e. All compositions exhibit a single, broad dielectric peak that shifts toward higher temperatures with increasing frequency, along with a gradual increase in dielectric loss. These features are characteristic of a relaxor-type diffuse phase transition19. Relaxor ferroelectrics are particularly attractive for energy-storage applications due to their high Eb and low Pr.

The unipolar I-E curves of BLBTN-x ceramics are shown in Fig. 4e. As the Bi doping content increases, the width of the current plateau first broadens and then narrows, indicating a corresponding change in leakage behavior. Notably, for the BLBTN-0.50 ceramic, the current remains below an extremely low value of 10 μA at 1400 kV/cm, evidencing its excellent insulation under ultrahigh fields. To evaluate the dielectric reliability, breakdown tests were performed on all ceramic samples. Eight regions on each sample were measured, and the results were analyzed using a standard Weibull distribution (Fig. 4f). All compositions exhibit high Weibull modulus β, indicating good breakdown strength uniformity. The average breakdown field strength initially increases with x, peaks at 1244 kV/cm for x = 0.50, and then decreases at higher Bi contents–a trend fully consistent with the P-E loop measurements. It is worth noting that the average breakdown strength obtained from the Weibull distribution (1244 kV/cm) reflects the statistical reliability of the material, while the maximum measured field (1400 kV/cm) represents the performance limit of a single sample. These two values correspond to different concepts, and in dielectric ceramics research, the latter is often higher than the former. To fully demonstrate the potential of the optimal composition (x = 0.50), we report the ESP under the maximum electric field that a sample can withstand before breakdown (1400 kV/cm), which is higher than the Weibull average and highlights the superior breakdown tolerance of this material.

Local structure and mechanism analysis

The microstructure and domain configuration of the BLBTN-0.50 ceramic were investigated by TEM. The selected-area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern taken along the [110] zone axis (Fig. 5a) displays, in addition to the fundamental Bragg reflections of the TTB structure, a set of superlattice spots (marked by yellow arrows), indicative of a structural modulation. Similar weak satellite reflections are observed along the [11−2] zone axis (Fig. 5(b)). Lattice-fringe measurements along the [11−2] direction (Fig. S4e) yield an interplanar spacing of 0.8668 nm, consistent with the XRD data. Atomic-resolution high-angle annular dark-field scanning TEM (HAADF-STEM) was employed to probe the local polarization order at the nanoscale. In TTB ferroelectrics, polarization mainly originates from displacement of B-site ions29. By calculating the offset between a central B-site cation and its surrounding B-site neighbors, the local polarization vectors were mapped, as shown in Fig. 5c, d, where arrow direction and colors represent the polarization orientation and magnitude, respectively. Regions with aligned polarization vectors, typically about 2 nm in size, are clearly identified, confirming the existence of PNRs in the BLBTN-0.50 ceramic. These PNRs disrupt long-range ferroelectric order, weaken inter-region polarization coupling, and thereby enhances relaxor characteristics. The resultant reduction in the energy barrier for domain switching minimize hysteresis losses, contributing directly to the improved ESP. Furthermore, inverse Fourier-transform analysis of the high-resolution TEM image (Fig. S4d) reveals the presence of numerous polar nanoregions (PNRs). Figure S5 shows the high-resolution TEM images of BLTN, BLBTN-0.35, and BLBTN-0.50 ceramics. From BLTN to the Bi‑doped samples, and with increasing Bi content, the sizes of PNRs decrease significantly. Smaller PNRs exhibit weaker inter-region coupling, faster polarization switching, lower Pr, and delayed polarization saturation-all of which are essential for achieving high ESP. While commensurate modulation usually arise from cation displacements and lead to long-range-ordered ferroelectric transitions, incommensurate modulation are typically associated with relaxor behavior17. From the spacing between the weak diffraction spots, the incommensurate modulation vector can be expressed as (1/4+δ) (a-b) + 1/2c, where a, b, and c are reciprocal lattice vectors and δ quantifies the deviation from strict commensurability. These features confirm that an incommensurate structural modulation exists in the BLBTN-0.50 ceramic at room temperature. The parameter δ is determined by δ = (x-y)/(x + y), with x and y representing the distances between adjacent weak reflections17; the measurement procedure is illustrated in Fig. 5e. As shown in Fig. S6, both BLBTN-0.35 and BLBTN-0.50 ceramics exhibit incommensurate modulation. It was also found that the calculated δ value for BLBTN-0.35 (δ0.35 = 0.17) is smaller than that for BLBTN-0.50 (δ0.50 = 0.216). The larger δ is due to the high disorder of A-site cations caused by Bi ion doping, which provides a solid foundation for ESP. Moreover, the average polarization displacement was measured and calculated to be 9.89 pm (Fig. 5f). Under an external electric field, these small-magnitude polarization responses not only effectively mitigate internal stress but also exhibit rapid recovery upon field removal. This behavior is attributed to lower energy barriers for polarization reorientation40.

The dynamic behavior of nanoscale domains was further examined using piezoelectric force microscopy (PFM). Figure 5g, h presents the domain evolution within a 20 × 20 μm2 area under applied voltages of +15, +30, and +60 V. For both BLBTN-0.35 and BLBTN-0.50 ceramics, no significant domain switching is observed at any of these voltages, indicating that the domain structures are associated with highly dynamic PNRs36. The absence of detectable switched domains even under 15 V explains the linear P-E loops exhibited by BLBTN ceramics at low fields. The fast reorientational response of PNRs reduces the Pr and delays polarization saturation, yielding slim P-E loops that are crucial for achieving high energy-storage performance12.

Figure 6a, b show the SEM images (x = 0.35, 0.50) and impedance diagrams of the BLBTN-x ceramics with different compositions. As seen in Fig. S7a–e, all samples show relatively dense microstructures. Rod-shaped grains are clearly observed, consistent with previously reported morphology for TTB-structured ceramics14,29. The formation of such non-equiaxed grains is attributed to the lower surface energy along the [001] direction, which promotes faster growth along the c-axis and lead to elongated grain morphology21.

Finite-element analysis was performed using a two-dimensional microstructure-based model to gain deeper insight into the role of grain morphology on the breakdown behavior. The model geometry was extracted from actual SEM images: a representative 6×8 μm2 region was discretized into 180×240 grid, and a larger 10 × 12 μm2 region (Fig. S8) was meshed into 200×240 nodes. The simulation examined the propagation of electric-tree branches and the corresponding potential distribution under a fixed electric field of 1050 kV/cm (Fig. 6g, h). For BLBTN-0.35 ceramic, the electric branches extend fully from the bottom to the top electrode, resulting in dielectric breakdown. In contrast, under the same field, the branches in BLBTN-0.50 do not completely traverse the thickness, leaving the proportions of the material intact. After breakdown, SEM testing was performed on the sample cross-section. As shown in Fig. S11, a clear breakdown path is observed, consistent with the finite-element simulation. This confirms the validity of finite-element analysis. The equipotential line density reflects the overall field uniformity: twisted lines indicate local field inhomogeneity. Because grains possess a higher dielectric constant than grain boundaries, the electric field concentrates at the boundaries while remaining relatively low inside grains5. This field concentration at grain boundaries significantly retards the advance of electric trees. When a propagating branch encounters a grain boundary, it often splits into multiple sub-branches, dissipating energy from the external electric field. The enhanced breakdown strength at grain boundaries arises mainly from the potential-energy barrier that impedes charge-carrier transport34,49, effectively providing strong resistance to breakdown. Compared with BLBTN-0.35, BLBTN-0.50 exhibits finer and more uniform grains, which reduces localized high-field regions and promotes a more homogenous field distribution. These factors collectively account for the higher Eb and improved ESP observed in BLBTN-0.50, consistent with the experimental trends shown in Fig. 4a.

Cycle stability and charge discharge performance

For practical applications, dielectric capacitors must operate stably under varying environmental and cycling conditions. Therefore, the BLBTN-0.50 ceramic-exhibiting the best energy storage performance-was subjected to systematic stability tests. As shown in Fig. 7a, under a fixed field of 700 kV/cm, the unipolar P-E loops remain slim over a wide temperature range (25 °C–140 °C). The corresponding variations in Wrec and η are less than 10% and 1.1%, respectively (Fig. 7d), indicating excellent temperature stability. Under different testing frequencies, Wrec changes from 5.08 to 4.86 J/cm3 (variation <5%), while η varies by less than 7.5% (Fig. 7b, e). After 105 charge-discharge cycles, the P-E loop retains its slim shape, with Wrec and η varying by less than 5.5 and 8.8% (Fig. 7c, f). These results demonstrate that the multiscale structural design employed here effectively enhances the operational stability of the ceramics under complex working conditions.

In addition to energy-storage density, efficiency, and stability, practical pulse-power applications require excellent charge-discharge kinetics. Key parameters characterizing the charge–discharge behavior include the released energy density (WD), discharge current density (CD), power density (PD) and discharge time (t0.9) Overdamped pulse-discharge current profiles are shown in Fig. 7g, h. The integrated discharged energy (Wdis) yields an ultra-short discharge time t0.9 = 10.2 ns. As shown in Fig. 7i, Underdamped discharge test reveals that both CD and PD increase with the applied electric field. At 500 kV/cm, CD and PD reach maximum values of 1014 A/cm2 and 254 MW/cm3, respectively. Together, the excellent stability and rapid charge–discharge performance confirm that BLBTN-0.50 ceramic is a highly promising candidate for pulsed-power applications.

In summary, this work demonstrates a multiscale structural‑engineering strategy that significantly enhances the ESP of tungsten‑bronze relaxor ferroelectrics. By incorporating Bi3+ into the Ba2La1-xBixTi2Nb3O15 lattice, we successfully introduced dynamic PNRs that disrupt long‑range ferroelectric order and strengthen relaxor behavior. Coupled with concurrent grain refinement and band‑gap widening, this approach yields an ultrahigh Wrec of 14.39 J/cm³ and an η of 87.69% under 1400 kV cm−1-to our knowledge, one of the highest Wrec values reported for bulk TTB-based ceramics. The optimized BLBTN‑0.50 composition also exhibits exceptional operational stability across broad frequency (20–140 Hz), temperature (25–140°C), and fatigue (>105 cycles) ranges, confirming its practicality under real operating conditions. More importantly, we establish clear multiscale structure-property relationships linking atomic‑scale lattice distortion, nanoscale PNRs, microstructural grain features, and macroscopic dielectric performance. This integrated design paradigm, which combines compositional simplification with precise control over electronic structure, domain configuration, and microstructure, provides a generalizable route for developing lead‑free dielectric ceramics with high energy density, high efficiency, robust stability, and fast discharge capability. Our findings validate the feasibility of relaxor‑ferroelectric design in TTB systems and highlight their strong potential for next‑generation high‑power pulsed energy applications.

Methods

Material preparation

Ba2La1−xBixTi2Nb3O15 ceramics (BLBTN-x (x = 0.35, 0.40, 0.45, 0.50, 0.55)) were synthesized by a conventional solid-state reaction route. High-purity starting powders of BaCO3 (99%, Sinopharm), La2O3 (99%, Macklin), TiO2 (98%, Sinopharm), Nb2O5 (99.5%, Sinopharm), and Bi2O3 (99.7%, Sinopharm) were weighed according to the stoichiometric formula of Ba2La1-xBixTi2Nb3O15. The mixed powders were ball-milled in ethanol using zirconia balls for 16 h, dried, and then calcined in air at 1050 °C for 2 h. The calcined powders were again ball milled for 16 h to ensure homogeneity. After drying, 8 wt% polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) binder was added, and the powders were uniaxially pressed into circular pellets 10 mm in diameter. The pressed pellets were heated at 610 °C for 5 h to burn out the binder, followed by sintering in air at 1200 °C for 2 h to obtain dense ceramics.

Structural characterization

The phase composition of the sintered ceramics was examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD, TD-3000, China) with Cu Kα radiation over a 2θ range of 20°–70°. Microstructural morphology and elemental distribution were characterized using field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Zeiss, merlin compact, Germany) coupled with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS, IE250X-Max50, Oxford, UK). For transmission electron microscopy (TEM) studies, samples were thinned by a Gatan 695 ion-polishing system and observed on an FEI Talos F200X microscope operated at 200 kV; bright-field images and selected-area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns were acquired. Raman spectra were collected at room temperature on a HORIBA LabRAM HR Evolution spectrometer using a 532-nm laser over 50–1000 cm−1.

Properties measurement

Dielectric properties were measured as a function of temperature (−150 °C–250 °C) and frequency (0.1–1000 kHz) using an HCT−1821 high-temperature dielectric tester. Ferroelectric polarization-electric field (P-E) hysteresis loops were recorded under unipolar conditions with a ferroelectric analyzer (TF3000, Germany) to evaluate energy-storage performance. Charging-discharge behavior was characterized with a capacitor charging-discharging system (Tongguo Technology, CFD-003, China), which captured both underdamped and overdamped discharge waveforms for energy-release quantification. For ESP measurements, ceramic disks were polished to a thickness of 0.05 ± 0.01 mm and coated with gold electrodes with an area of 3.14 mm2. Breakdown tests were performed in silicone oil at room temperature using an AC voltage ramp rate of 100 V/s.

Computational method

First-principles calculations based on density functional theory (DFT) were carried out to elucidate the influence of Bi3+ doping on the electronic structure of the BLBTN-x system. Two doping configurations were computed, one was to replace a La atom in Ba4La2Ti2O8O30 (BLBTN-0.50), and the other was to construct 2×1×1 supercell (Ba8La4Ti4O16O60), in which a La atom was replaced (BLBTN-0.25). The band structures and projected density of states (PDOS) were computed within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) using the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional. A plane-wave cutoff energy of 550 eV was used and an 8×8×4 Monkhorst-Pack k-point grid was employed for Brillouin-zone sampling, with convergence criteria set to 0.03 eV/atom for total energy and 10-5 eV/Å for forces.

HIRA loss is frequent but not universal in human fumarate hydratase-deficient renal cell carcinoma and defines divergent oncogenic programs
Authors: Xu Shi, Zehua Tan, Guangxi Sun, Hao Zeng, Xingming Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Fumarate hydratase-deficient renal cell carcinoma (FH-dRCC) is a rare and highly aggressive malignancy driven by fumarate accumulation and widespread epigenetic reprogramming. Although Valcarcel-Jimenez et al. have demonstrated that loss of the histone chaperone Hira is a critical and necessary event for the malignant transformation of Fh1-deficient cells through activation of MYC-dependent transcriptional programs based on murine models, by analyzing HIRA protein expression and transcriptomic profiles in a multicenter cohort of human FH-dRCC tumors harboring either germline or somatic FH mutations, we found that HIRA loss was frequent (73.9%, 17/23) but not universal. Although HIRA status was not associated with clinical outcomes, transcriptomic analyses revealed two divergent oncogenic programs: HIRA-negative tumors are characterized by activation of MYC and mTORC1 signaling, whereas HIRA-positive tumors preferentially engage Wnt and Notch pathways. These findings suggest that HIRA loss is not an obligate event in human FH-dRCC tumorigenesis, but instead may direct toward distinct molecular trajectories.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40364-026-00990-4.

Main text

To the editor,

Fumarate hydratase-deficient renal cell carcinoma (FH-dRCC) is a highly aggressive malignancy characterized by early metastasis and marked molecular heterogeneity [1, 2]. The loss of fumarate hydratase (FH) leads to the accumulation of fumarate, driving widespread metabolic and epigenetic rewiring [3–5]. A recent mechanistic study using Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats screening in murine models identified loss of the histone chaperone Hira as a critical event that enables the malignant transformation of Fh1-deficient lesions from premalignant cysts to carcinomas, through non-canonical activation of Myc-dependent transcriptional programs [6]. These findings suggest an indispensable role of Hira in tumorigenesis and implicated the Hira-Myc axis as a potential oncogenic mechanism in Fh1-deficient tumors. However, it remains largely unknown whether this mechanism also applies to human FH-dRCC.

In parallel, human FH-dRCC exhibits profound clinical and morphological heterogeneity [7, 8]. Our group has recently revealed that FH-dRCC exhibits substantial transcriptional heterogeneity, with tumors clustering into three molecular subtypes characterized by distinct oncogenic pathways [1, 8]. Specifically, the C3 subtype displays a strong enrichment of MYC and E2F target signatures, represents a highly proliferative transcriptional state, and correlates with poor response to the combination of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) and anti-angiogenic therapy [8]. Consequently, these observations raise a key question of whether HIRA loss universally drives the MYC-dependent phenotype observed in human FH-dRCC, or whether it instead defines a specific molecular subset of tumors. Therefore, we aimed to assess HIRA expression in FH-dRCC tissue microarrays and, through transcriptomic analyses, to characterize the distinct oncogenic mechanisms associated with its loss versus retention.

To address this question, we evaluated HIRA protein expression in a multicenter cohort of 23 primary FH-dRCC tumors harboring either germline or somatic FH mutations. RNA-seq data were available for 21 (91.3%, 21/23) patients. Clinicopathological analyses revealed no significant association between HIRA status and germline or somatic FH mutations (p = 1.000) (Fig. 1B and Table 1). Among the 23 patients, 20 (87.0%) subsequently developed metastatic disease and received at least one line of systemic treatment. Survival analyses further demonstrated that HIRA expression status did not significantly correlate with disease-free survival (HR: 0.684; 95% CI: 0.083–5.618; p = 0.737) (Fig. 1D). Most patients received the combination of ICB and anti-angiogenic therapy (57.1% in the HIRA-negative group and 66.7% in the HIRA-positive group). Response to first-line systemic therapy showed no significant difference between the two groups (p = 1.000) (Fig. 1C), progression-free survival (HR: 0.922; 95% CI: 0.276–3.085; p = 0.895) (Fig. 1E), or overall survival (HR: 0.637; 95% CI: 0.074–5.454; p = 0.678) (Fig. 1F).

Although HIRA status showed no clear clinical associations, transcriptomic analyses revealed that HIRA expression significantly influences the tumorigenesis of FH-dRCC by directing tumors toward distinct molecular states. Gene set enrichment analysis demonstrated that MYC targets (NES = 1.61; P.adj < 0.001) and mTORC1 (NES = 1.42; P.adj = 0.014) signaling were significantly enriched in HIRA-negative tumors (Fig. 1G). In contrast, Wnt/β-catenin (NES = −1.55; P.adj = 0.050) and Notch (NES = −1.56; P.adj = 0.053) signaling pathways were preferentially enriched in HIRA-positive tumors (Fig. 1G). The above findings were further supported in an independent TCGA-KIRP cohort including 10 cases of confirmed or suspicious FH-dRCC (Supplementary Fig. S1).

Notably, these transcriptional signatures correspond closely with recently described molecular subtypes of FH-dRCC. HIRA-negative tumors resemble the C3 proliferation/stemness subtype, characterized by MYC activation [8]. Conversely, HIRA-positive tumors display features consistent with the C2 subtype, which is enriched for Wnt and Notch signaling [8]. The observation that HIRA-positive tumors appear to retain aggresive clinical behavior despite lacking MYC enrichment indicates that alternative oncogenic mechanisms likely compensate for the absence of HIRA loss. FH-dRCC is characterized by profound genomic instability and recurrent co-mutations in genes regulating chromatin organization and cell signaling [1, 7]. Previous genomic studies have identified NF2 as one of the most frequently co-mutated genes in FH-dRCC, with truncating mutations predicted to activate Hippo-YAP signaling [1, 8, 9]. Mutations in epigenetic regulators such as KMT2D may further promote enhancer reprogramming and transcriptional plasticity [8, 10]. These co-occurring mutations in FH-dRCC may shape tumor behavior through distinct biological mechanisms. Within this framework, HIRA status may function as a molecular switch that determines which transcriptional programs dominate during tumor evolution, leading to either MYC-driven proliferation or alternative signaling pathways such as Wnt and Notch signaling.

In conclusion, our integrated clinical and transcriptomic analyses of human FH-dRCC highlight three key insights. First, HIRA loss is a frequent but not universal event in human FH-dRCC. Second, HIRA loss is not significantly associated with clinical outcomes. Third, HIRA status functions as a pivotal molecular determinant that directs the tumor toward divergent transcriptional states, thereby influencing the tumorigenesis of FH-dRCC. Collectively, these clinical observations contextualize the HIRA-MYC oncogenic axis discovered in murine models as one crucial branch within the multidimensional oncogenic landscape of human FH-dRCC.

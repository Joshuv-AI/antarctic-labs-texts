Torsion and complete dualizable objects in tensor-triangulated categories over a Noetherian ring Thanks: The research was supported by the Czech Science Foundation grant GA ČR 23-05148S
Jun Maillard and Jan Šťovíček

Abstract
. We study categories of dualizable torsion and complete objects for compactly-rigidly generated tensor-triangulated categories 𝖳 \mathsf{T} with a Noetherian central action of a graded commutative Noetherian ring R R . We show that they always admit a natural Noetherian action of the completed graded ring R ∧ R^{\wedge} and that the categories of dualizable torsion and complete objects can be abstractly reconstructed as tensor-triangulated R ∧ R^{\wedge} -linear categories from the category of compact torsions objects with the corresponding structure. If the category of compact objects of 𝖳 \mathsf{T} in addition admits a strong generator g g , we show that the torsion coreflection (resp. complete reflection) of g g is a strong generator for the category of dualizable torsion (resp. dualizable complete) objects. In that case, we also show that the categories of dualizable torsion and compact torsion objects determine each other in terms of Brown-type representability theorems.

Adic completion in tensor-triangular geometry

1.2.
Problems about complete dualizable objects

The main challenge which is treated in the references above is how to understand the complete (or torsion) dualizable objects and how exactly they are related to
\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{T}^{c}
. First of all, one may hope to reconstruct the category of complete dualizable objects
(\Lambda^{\mathcal{V}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
from the category of torsion/complete compact objects
(\Lambda^{\mathcal{V}}\mathsf{T})^{c}=(\Gamma_{\mathcal{V}}\mathsf{T})^{c}\subset\mathsf{T}
by some sort of completion, similarly as the adic completion of a ring
R^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{a}}
is obtained as a limit of its torsion quotients
R/\mathfrak{a}^{n}
. Albeit this might seem naive at first, there are instances of metric completion of triangulated categories initiated by Krause
[
33
]
and perfected by Neeman in
[
47
]
. Unfortunately, these techniques heavily depend on the presence of a non-degenerate
t
-structure and interesting classes of examples, e.g. coming from modular representation theory of finite groups, do not admit any of these (as it often happens that some power of the suspension functor is isomorphic to the identity). However, as we will explain below, one still can achieve complete reconstruction of
(\Lambda^{\mathcal{V}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
from
(\Lambda^{\mathcal{V}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
at the level of tensor-triangulated categories (so without reference to any model such as
\infty

Another previously intensely studied problem is about thick generation.
As mentioned, since the completion functor

\mathsf{T}
are dualizable in
\Lambda^{\mathcal{V}}\mathsf{T}
. We hence have sequence of inclusions of categories

where the first inclusion is typically proper. On the contrary, the second inclusion is known to be an equality in at least the following cases:

(1)

(2)

(3)

(5)

1.3.
The triangulated reconstruction result

Let us first explain our setting, which is inspired by that of
[

We will assume an action of a graded commutative Noetherian (but not necessarily local) ring

so that (graded) Hom-groups for all pairs of compact objects are finitely generated
R
-modules. This encompasses both
\mathsf{T}=\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{Mod}R)

But we can also consider the derived category of any dg algebra or the homotopy category of any ring spectrum whose cohomology (respectively homotopy) ring is Noetherian. For instance, any graded commutative Noetherian algebra viewed as a formal dg algebra with zero differential is an example.

Compared to

In fact, the assumptions from the previous paragraph ensure, using

Let

2.
Preliminaries on the tensor-triangulated setting

A triangulated category
\mathsf{T}
is
tensor-triangulated
if it is endowed with a symmetric monoidal structure
(\otimes,\mathbb{1})
such that
-\otimes-
is exact in both variable (see
[
7
, Definition 3]
or
[
26
, Definition A.2.1]
). We will also assume that
-\otimes-
preserves any existing coproduct in each variable. An internal hom-object
\underline{\Hom}(y,z)
between two objects
y,z\in\mathsf{T}
is defined, when it exists, by the adjunction isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(x,\underline{\Hom}(y,z))\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(x\otimes y,z)

natural in
x
. As a consequence of Brown Representability Theorem
[
44
, Theorem 3.1]
, internal hom-objects exist whenever
\mathsf{T}
is compactly generated.

2.1.
Grading

Throughout this paper, all rings and modules are
graded
, and ideals are
homogeneous
. Recall that a ring
R
is called
graded-commutative
if
yx=(-1)^{|x||y|}xy
for all homogeneous elements
x,y\in R
. Such rings behave analogously to ordinary commutative rings; see
[
16
, §1.5]
for specific aspects of the grading.
If
R
is graded-commutative, we denote by
\Spec R
the homogeneous spectrum, that is, the set of all prime homogeneous ideals. If
\mathsf{T}
is a triangulated category,

\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(x,y)=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}^{i}(x,y)

denotes the
graded
Hom-group, with
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}^{i}(x,y)=\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}^{0}(\Sigma^{-i}x,y)
. Morphisms appearing in our commutative diagrams are always degree
0
morphisms.

2.2.
Compactness and dualizability

Compact and dualizable objects are classical classes of ‘small’ objects definable in a tensor-triangulated category.

A
compact object
of a triangulated category
\mathsf{T}
with arbitrary coproducts is an object
c\in\mathsf{T}
, whose representable functor
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,-)
commutes with arbitrary coproducts in the sense that the following canonical map is an isomorphism:

\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,x_{i})\overset{\sim}{\to}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{I}}x_{i})

A set
\mathsf{G}\subset\mathsf{T}
of compact objects is called
generating
if
\mathsf{T}
is the smallest triangulated subcategory of
\mathsf{T}
closed under arbitrary coproducts and containing
\mathsf{G}
.
A compact object
c
is a
generator
of
\mathsf{T}
if
\mathsf{G}=\{c\}
is generating.
This can equivalently be tested by the following criterion; see
[
44
, Theorem 2.1]
:

2.1
.

A set of compact objects
\mathsf{G}\subset\mathsf{T}^{c}
is generating if and only if, for any object
x
in
\mathsf{T}
,
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,x)=0
for each
c\in\mathsf{G}
implies that
x=0
.

The full subcategory of compact objects is then the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathsf{T}
containing
\mathsf{G}
, also by
[
44
, Theorem 2.1]
. We denote it by
\mathsf{T}^{c}
.
Note also that compactly generated triangulated categories have products since for any family of objects
x_{i}\in\mathsf{T}
,
i\in I
, the functor
\prod_{i\in I}\Hom(-,x_{i})
is representable by the Brown Representability Theorem
[
44
, Theorem 3.1]
.

A
dualizable
(sometimes also called
rigid
) object in a tensor-triangulated category
\mathsf{T}
is an object
d
such that the canonical map

\underline{\Hom}_{\mathsf{T}}(d,\mathbb{1})\otimes x\overset{\sim}{\to}\underline{\Hom}_{\mathsf{T}}(d,x)

is an isomorphism. The object
{d}^{\mathrm{SW}}=\underline{\Hom}_{\mathsf{T}}(d,\mathbb{1})
is then called the
Spanier-Whitehead dual
of
d
, and is dualizable as well, with Spanier-Whitehead dual
d
.
We refer to e.g. to
[
38
, §III.1]
for this and other basic facts about dualizable (there called ‘finite’) objects.
The full subcategory of dualizable objects, denoted by
\mathsf{T}^{d}
, is a thick subcategory of
\mathsf{T}
. We will say that
\mathsf{T}
is
rigidly generated
if
\mathsf{T}
is the smallest triangulated subcategory of
\mathsf{T}
closed under arbitrary coproducts and containing
\mathsf{T}^{d}
.

Basic relations between these notions are recorded in
[
26
, §2.1]
.
Dualizable and compact objects are also related by the following tensor duality.

2.2
.

Let
\mathsf{T}
be a tensor-triangulated category.

(a)

For any compact object
c\in\mathsf{T}^{c}
and any dualizable object
d\in\mathsf{T}^{d}
,
c\otimes d
is compact.

(b)

Assume
\mathsf{T}
is both compactly generated and rigidly generated. Let
d
be an object in
\mathsf{T}
such that
-\otimes d
preserves compactness. Then
d
is dualizable.

(c)

Let
c
be an object in
\mathsf{T}
such that
c\otimes-
maps dualizable objects to compact ones. Then
c
is compact.

Proof.

If
c
is a compact object and
d
is a dualizable object, then

\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c\otimes d,-)\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,{d}^{\mathrm{SW}}\otimes-)

Both functors
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,-)
and
{d}^{\mathrm{SW}}\otimes-
preserve coproducts, hence
c\otimes d
is compact. This proves
(a)
.

Fix an object
d
in
\mathsf{T}
, such that
c\otimes d
is compact for any compact object
c
. Then
\underline{\Hom}(d,-)
preserves coproducts. Indeed, fix a family of objects
(x_{i})_{i}
. Then for any compact object
c
, we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,\underline{\Hom}(d,x_{i}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,\bigoplus_{i}\underline{\Hom}(d,x_{i}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,\underline{\Hom}(d,\bigoplus_{i}x_{i}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c\otimes d,x_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c\otimes d,\bigoplus_{i}x_{i}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

where the vertical isomorphisms are given by the adjunction and the horizontal morphisms are the canonical ones. Two of these are also isomorphisms as indicated, since
c
and
c\otimes d
are compact.
So, if
\phi\colon\bigoplus_{i}\underline{\Hom}(d,x_{i})\to\underline{\Hom}(d,\bigoplus_{i}x_{i})
is the canonical homomorphism, we have proved that
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,\phi)
is an isomorphism for each
c
compact.
Since
\mathsf{T}
is compactly generated, it follows that
\underline{\Hom}(d,-)
preserves coproducts, as claimed.

Now fix a dualizable object
g
of
\mathsf{T}
and consider the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\Hom}(d,\mathbb{1})\otimes g}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\Hom}(d,g)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\Hom}({g}^{\mathrm{SW}},\underline{\Hom}(d,\mathbb{1}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\Hom}({g}^{\mathrm{SW}}\otimes d,\mathbb{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\Hom}(d,\underline{\Hom}({g}^{\mathrm{SW}},\mathbb{1}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

The top arrow must thus be an isomorphism. The natural transformation

\underline{\Hom}(d,\mathbb{1})\otimes-\to\underline{\Hom}(d,-)

is a natural transformation between exact, coproducts-preserving functors and an isomorphism on a generating set of objects, hence it is an isomorphism. This proves
(b)
.

Statement
(c)
is a direct consequence of the fact that
\mathbb{1}
is always dualizable.
∎

All the ‘large’ tensor-triangulated categories considered in this article are compactly generated, and the compact objects are dualizable. In particular, the hypotheses of
Proposition

2.2

(b)
are satisfied.

2.3
.

A tensor-triangulated category
\mathsf{T}
is called
rigidly-compactly generated
if its compact and dualizable objects coincide,
\mathsf{T}^{c}=\mathsf{T}^{d}
, and they generate
\mathsf{T}
.

2.4
.

If the monoidal unit
\mathbb{1}
of
\mathsf{T}
is compact, then
\mathsf{T}^{d}\subset\mathsf{T}^{c}
; see the proof of
[
26
, Theorem 2.1.3(d)]
.
So for tensor-triangulated
\mathsf{T}
to be compactly-rigidly generated, it suffices to verify that it is compactly generated and
\mathbb{1}\in\mathsf{T}^{c}\subset\mathsf{T}^{d}
.

2.3.
Sequential homotopy colimits

If
\mathsf{T}
be a compactly generated triangulated category, we can define the sequential homotopy colimit
\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font holim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font holim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font holim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font holim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i}x_{i}
of a chain morphisms

x_{0}\xrightarrow{\phi_{0}}x_{1}\xrightarrow{\phi_{1}}x_{2}\xrightarrow{\phi_{2}}\cdots

as a cone of the morphism
\id-\phi\colon\bigoplus_{i}x_{i}\to\bigoplus_{i}x_{i}
, see
[
26
, Definition 2.2.3]
or
[
45
, §1.6]
. Of course, one cannot expect this construction to be functorial, but using the notion of minimal weak colimits from
[
26
, Definition 2.2.1]
, we can see that it is determined uniquely up to a non-canonical isomorphism just by the underlying category of
\mathsf{T}
, independently of the triangulation.

2.5
.

If
F\colon I\to\mathsf{T}
is a diagram, we say that a cocone
(f_{i}\colon F(i)\to x)
is a
weak colimit
if for any other cocone
(g_{i}\colon F(i)\to y)
, there exists a (not necessarily unique) morphism
h\colon x\to y
such that
g_{i}=hf_{i}
for each
i
. In other words, we require that the canonical morphism

\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(x,y)\to\varprojlim_{i\in I}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(F(i),y)

is surjective for each
y\in\mathsf{T}
.
A weak colimit
(f_{i}\colon F(i)\to x)
is said to be
minimal
if it induces for each
c\in\mathsf{T}^{c}
an isomorphism of abelian groups

\varinjlim_{i\in I}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,F(i))\to\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,x).

2.6
.

[
26
, Definition 2.2.1]

be an isomorphism for each coproduct preserving homological functor

[
30
, Corollary 2.4]

2.4.
Action of a graded ring and Noetherian objects

Let
\mathsf{T}
be a tensor-triangulated category and
R
a graded-commutative Noetherian ring. A map of graded rings
R\to\emorp(\mathbb{1})
endows every graded Hom-set with a structure of graded
R
-module:

R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(x,y)\to\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbb{1},\mathbb{1})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(x,y)\xrightarrow{\otimes}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(x,y)

Under these actions, the compositions are
R
-linear.
If
\mathsf{T}
is compactly generated, we can follow the idea from
[
13
, §4]
of transferring finiteness properties from
R
-modules to objects of
\mathsf{T}
using homomorphism
R
-modules from the compacts.

2.11
.

If
\mathsf{T}
is a compactly generated tensor-triangulated category with an action of a graded-commutative Noetherian ring
R
, we say that:

•

an object
x\in\mathsf{T}
is
Noetherian
if
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,x)
is a Noetherian
R
-module for each
c\in\mathsf{T}^{c}
,

•

the category
\mathsf{T}
is
Noetherian
if all compact objects are Noetherian, i.e.
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,c^{\prime})
is Noetherian over
R
for any pair of compact objects
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathsf{T}^{c}
;

•

strictly Noetherian
if the classes of compact and Noetherian objects coincide.

2.12
.

If
\mathsf{T}
has a compact generator
g\in\mathsf{T}^{c}
, then
x\in\mathsf{T}
is Noetherian if and only if
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(g,x)
is a Noetherian
R
-module.

2.13
.

It is instructive to consider the
\mathsf{T}=\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{Mod}R)
, where
R
is commutative Noetherian, as an example. Then
\mathsf{T}^{c}
is known to coincide with the class
\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{per}}(R)
of perfect complexes, while the class of Noetherian objects is easily seen to coincide with
\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{b}}(\mathsf{mod}R)
. Hence
\mathsf{T}=\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{Mod}R)
is strictly Noetherian if and only if each finitely generated
R
-modules has finite projective dimension, which is equivalent to

being regular (see e.g.
[
18
, Theorem 20.2.11]
).

2.7.
Compact reflections and cogeneration

In

As one might suspect, the situation is more complicated than in the previous section, but we obtain a criterion which is still useful for some classes of examples. The key observation is the following.

2.24

Let
\mathsf{T}

Proof.

The fact that

It remains to prove that

This isomorphism is induced by precomposition by a map

2.25

Let
\mathsf{T}

The orthogonality condition on

A triangulated category

The obvious question is which tensor-triangulated categories are compactly cogenerated. On the positive side, we have the following classes of examples.

2.27

Let
\mathsf{T}=\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{Mod}R)
, where

(1)

(2)

[

and for the second case in
[

.

On the negative side, there are many natural compactly generated (and often even tensor-triangulated,

2.28

(1)

If

is a complete local commutative Noetherian ring, then

is not compactly cogenerated unless

is Artinian. Indeed, if

is non-maximal, then
\mathbf{R}\!\Hom_{R}(k(\mathfrak{p}),R)=0
(and so

or
[

.

(2)

Suppose

\mathsf{T}=\mathsf{KInj}(R)

-module of infinite projective dimension, then

Indeed, in this case Krause

3.2.
Power-torsion and derived complete modules

The key task we will need to achieve to understand dualizable
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion or
\mathfrak{a}
-complete objects in
\mathsf{T}
is to get a better control of Hom-modules of
\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
. This is achieved via the concept of derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete
R
-module, going back to Greenlees and May
[
21
]
.
Our definition of derived complete modules is very similar to
[
56
]
, while a thorough and much more general discussion of derived completion and related homological conditions is given in
[
54
]
.
Along with this, we will discuss the more familiar theory of
\mathfrak{a}
-power torsion modules, to stress the analogies and point up differences.

3.8
.

Let
M
be a
R
-module.

(a)

M
is
\mathfrak{a}
-
power torsion
if on any element
m\in M
, some power
n
of
\mathfrak{a}
acts trivially:
\mathfrak{a}^{n}m=0
.

(b)

M
is
\mathfrak{a}
-
complete
if the canonical map

M\to\varprojlim_{n}M/\mathfrak{a}^{n}M

is an isomorphism.

(c)

M
is
derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete
if it is a cokernel of a morphism of
\mathfrak{a}
-complete modules.

The following characterization of power torsion modules is well known. We will denote by
R^{\wedge}=\varprojlim_{n}{R/\mathfrak{a}^{n}}
the usual
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion of
R
.

3.9
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

M
is
\mathfrak{a}
-power torsion;

(ii)

for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\backslash\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{a})
,
M_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
;

(iii)

for all
r\in\mathfrak{a}
,
\Tor_{*}^{R}(R[r^{-1}],M)=0
.

(iv)

for all
r\in\mathfrak{a}
,
R[r^{-1}]\otimes_{R}M=0
.

The class of
\mathfrak{a}
-power torsion modules is an hereditary torsion class (i.e. a Serre subcategory closed under coproducts) in
\mathsf{Mod}{R}
.

Moreover, the
R
-module structure of any
\mathfrak{a}
-power torsion
R
-module uniquely extends to an
R^{\wedge}
-module structure, and the restriction functor induces an equivalence between the categories of
\mathfrak{a}R^{\wedge}
-power torsion
R^{\wedge}
-modules and
\mathfrak{a}
-power torsion
R
-modules.

Proof.

See
[
61
,
Tag 0953
]
.
∎

Quite non-trivially, derived complete
R
-modules admit an almost perfectly formally dual characterization.
We mostly rely on
[
52
,
54
]
and explain where to find the claims of the following lemma there (see also
[
50
, Propositions 3.10 and 7.2]
for a similar summary).

3.10
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

M
is derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete;

(ii)

for all
r\in\mathfrak{a}
,
\Ext^{*}_{R}(R[r^{-1}],M)=0
.

(iii)

for all
r\in\mathfrak{a}
,
\Hom_{R}(R[r^{-1}],M)=0=\Ext^{1}_{R}(R[r^{-1}],M)=0
.

The class of derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete modules is an exact Abelian subcategory of
\mathsf{Mod}{R}
which is closed under extensions and products.

Moreover, the
R
-module structure of any derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete
R
-module uniquely extends to an
R^{\wedge}
-module structure, and the restriction functor induces an equivalence between the categories of derived
\mathfrak{a}R^{\wedge}
-complete
R^{\wedge}
-modules and derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete
R
-modules.

Proof.

The equivalence between conditions
(i)
–
(iii)
follows from
[
52
, §2]
, where the modules satisfying condition
(iii)
are called
\mathfrak{a}
-contramodule
R
-modules. Adopting this terminology temporarily, note that the class of
\mathfrak{a}
-contramodule modules contains all
\mathfrak{a}
-complete modules by the discussion at the beginning of
[
52
, §2]
, and is coreflective by
[
52
, Proposition 2.1]
. In particular, this class is closed under taking cokernels and so contains all derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete modules in our sense. On the other hand, given any
\mathfrak{a}
-contramodule module
M
, we can take a projective presentation
P_{1}\to P_{0}\to M\to 0
and apply the
\mathfrak{a}
-contramodule coreflector, which we denote by
\Delta_{\mathfrak{a}}
, to obtain
\Delta_{\mathfrak{a}}(P_{1})\to\Delta_{\mathfrak{a}}(P_{0})\to\Delta_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\cong M\to 0
. Since
\Delta_{\mathfrak{a}}(P)\cong P^{\wedge}
for any projective (or ever flat)
R
-module

by
[
52
, Lemma 2.5]
, this shows the equivalence between
(i)
and
(iii)
. The equivalence between
(ii)
and
(iii)
follows from the fact that the localizations
R[r^{-1}]
have projective dimension at most one over
R
.

The properties of the full subcategory of derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete
R
-modules follows from
[
20
, Proposition 1.1]
, using the fact that
R[r^{-1}]
has projective dimension at most one over
R
for any
r\in R
.

Finally, the moreover part follows from
[
54
, Proposition 1.5]
. Regarding the terminology used there, we only remark that each
\mathfrak{a}
-contramodule
R
-module is quotseparated (i.e. a quotient of an
\mathfrak{a}
-complete
R
-module) in our case since

is Noetherian; cf.
[
54
, Corollary 3.7]
.
∎

Modules annihilated by a fixed power of
\mathfrak{a}
are both
\mathfrak{a}
-power torsion and (derived)
\mathfrak{a}
-complete.

3.11
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module such that
\mathfrak{a}^{n}M=0
, for some
n\geq 1
. Then
M
is
\mathfrak{a}
-power torsion and
\mathfrak{a}
-complete.

Proof.

The module
M
is obviously
\mathfrak{a}
-power torsion.
Furthermore, for any
k\geq n
,
M/\mathfrak{a}^{k}M=M
and the transition map
M/\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}M\to M/\mathfrak{a}^{k}M
is the identity. Hence the infinite linear system
(M/\mathfrak{a}^{k}M)_{k\geq 1}
is equivalent to a finite subsystem, whose limit is
M
by initiality.
∎

A key property of derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete modules is that they satisfy a variation of Nakayama’s lemma. Note that it holds for
all
derived-complete modules, without any assumption of finite generation.

3.12
Nakayama for derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete modules.

Let
M
be a derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete module. Then
M=0
if and only if
M/\mathfrak{a}M=0
.

Proof.

Since any derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete module is a cokernel of completions of free modules, one case reduce the problem to the Nakayama lemma for
\mathfrak{a}
-complete modules; see
[
51
, Corollary 0.3]
for this argument.

Alternatively, one can use that if
M
is derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete,
r\in\mathfrak{a}
and
m_{0},m_{1},\dots
is any sequence of elements of
M
, one can naturally define infinite sums
z_{k}=\sum_{n\geq 0}r^{n}m_{n+k}
for all
k\geq 0
. More precisely, the countable system of equations
z_{k}-rz_{k+1}=m_{k}
,
k\geq 0
, has a unique solution
z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},\cdots\in M
; see
[
53
, Lemma 2.1]
. Now the Nakayama lemma follows easily by
[
53
, Lemmas 3.2, 4.2 and Remark 4.3]
.
∎

3.13
.

Assume
R
is
\mathfrak{a}
-complete. Let
M
be a derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete
R
-module and
r
be an element of
\mathfrak{a}
. If

is Noetherian, so is

.

Proof.

Let
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\in M
be representatives of generators of

, and let

. We see that

, hence
N/\mathfrak{a}N=0
. Since
M
and
R
are derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete,
N
is derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete, thus
N=0
; hence
M
is finitely generated.
∎

Now we can characterize
\mathfrak{a}
-power torsion, but more importantly also
\mathfrak{a}
-complete objects of
\mathsf{T}
as follows.

3.14
.

Let
\mathsf{T}
be a compactly-rigidly generated tensor-triangulated category, Noetherian over a graded commutative ring Noetherian ring

.
Let

\mathsf{T}

(a)

\mathfrak{a}
-power torsion for each

(b)

\mathfrak{a}
-complete for each
c\in\mathsf{T}^{c}
if and only if
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(x,y)

\mathfrak{a}
-complete for each

Proof.

Statement
(a)
is a direct consequence of
Lemma

3.9
.

In order to prove
(b)

and

, and we will prove that
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(x,y)
is derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete. In order to do this, we fix an injective cogenerator

of
\mathsf{Mod}(R)

defined using Brown Representability:

\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(-,t_{c/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}}(I))\cong\Hom_{R}(\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c/\!\!/\mathfrak{a},-),I).

These objects have been studied in
[
14

.
In particular, by an adaptation of
[
14

, the set
\{t_{c/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}}(I)\mid c\in\mathsf{T}^{c}\}

in the sense of
[

[

containing the set

.

Since by
Lemma

3.10
, the category of derived
\mathfrak{a}
-complete modules is an extension closed exact Abelian subcategory of the category of

-modules which is also closed under products, it is sufficient to check that
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c^{\prime},t_{c/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}}(I))

-complete for any compact objects

. The

is annihilated by a power of
\mathfrak{a}
, hence so is

\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c^{\prime},t_{c/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}}(I))\cong\Hom_{R}(\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c/\!\!/\mathfrak{a},c^{\prime}),I)

By
Lemma

3.11

\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c^{\prime},t_{c/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}}(I))
is
\mathfrak{a}
-complete.

Suppose conversely that

is such that

\mathfrak{a}
-complete for each

Since

Let

3.16
.

The adjunction isomorphism, for any

.

3.17

Let

\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}

(i)

(ii)

for any

Assume

Let

(i)

for any

,
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(x,c)

(ii)

for any

3.3.
Finiteness on homomorphisms from dualizability

In this section we collect finiteness properties involving of homomorphism modules involving torsion and complete dualizable objects.
In particular, we will show that the homomorphism groups between torsion or complete dualizable modules are finitely generated over the adic completion

of

.

3.19
.

Let

be a dualizable object in
\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}

\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,d)
and
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(d,c)

Proof.

Note that the compact object

R^{\wedge}
by
Remark

3.16
. If
x,y\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
, then the
R^{\wedge}
-module
\Hom(x,y)
is finitely generated by
Theorem

3.22
, so
\mathfrak{a}
-complete. Hence, the first isomorphism established by the proposition takes the form

\Hom_{(\Gamma\mathsf{T})^{d}}(x,y)\cong\varprojlim_{m}\Hom_{(\Gamma\mathsf{T})^{d}}(\Sigma^{md-1}x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]},y)
, which is none other than the universal property for a honest categorical colimit.
∎

We finish with a technical statement, which will be important in
Section

4
.

3.33
.

Given any pair of dualizable objects
x,y\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
, the inverse system of abelian groups
(\Hom_{(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}}(x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]},y))_{m\geq 1}
is Mittag-Leffler.

Proof.

This is proved by induction on the number of generators of
\mathfrak{a}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
, where the case
k=1
was explicitly established in the proof of
Proposition

3.26
.
For
k>1
, denote
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k-1})
, by
d^{\prime}
the sum of the degrees of the generators for
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
, by
d_{k}
the degree of
r_{k}
, and

M_{m,l}:=\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(\Sigma^{md^{\prime}+ld_{k}-k}(x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{\prime[m]})/\!\!/r_{k}^{l},y).

Since the system in the statement is cofinal in
(M_{m,l})_{m,l\in\mathbb{N}}
, it suffices to prove the Mittag-Leffler property for
(M_{m,l})_{m,l\in\mathbb{N}}
. The inductive hypothesis says that
(M_{m,l})_{m\geq 1}
is Mittag-Leffler for each
l\geq 1
, and also that the inverse system

\Big(\varinjlim_{m}M_{m,l}\Big)_{l\geq 1}\cong\Big(\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}\big((\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}x)/\!\!/r_{k}^{l},y\big)\Big)_{l\geq 1}

is Mittag-Leffler. So given any
m_{0},l_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, there are by
Lemma

3.25
:

•

l_{1}\geq l_{0}
such that image of
\varprojlim_{m,l}M_{m,l}\to\varprojlim_{m}M_{m,l_{0}}
equals the image of
\varprojlim_{m}M_{m,l_{1}}\to\varprojlim_{m}M_{m,l_{0}}
, and

•

m_{1}\geq m_{0}
such that the image of
\varprojlim_{m}M_{m,l_{1}}\to M_{m_{0},l_{1}}
equals the image of
M_{m_{1},l_{1}}\to M_{m_{0},l_{0}}
.

It follows that the image of
\varprojlim_{m,l}M_{m,l}\to M_{m_{0},l_{0}}
coincides with the image of
M_{m_{1},l_{1}}\to M_{m_{0},l_{0}}
, so
(M_{m,l})_{m,l\in\mathbb{N}}
is Mittag-Leffler as required.
∎

3.34
.

One may ask whether
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}d)
and
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,d)
, where the second
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}
stands for the torsion radical
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\colon\mathsf{mod}{R}\to\mathsf{mod}{R}
,
compare in a similar way. Unfortunately, the situation is more complicated here, since unlike the completion functor, the torsion radical is
not
exact on finitely generated modules. If
\mathfrak{a}=(r)
is principal, a similar argument as the one for
Proposition

3.26
only gives us a short exact sequence

0\to H^{1}_{\mathfrak{a}}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(\Sigma^{-1}c,d)\to\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}d)\to\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,d)\to 0,

where
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{a}}\colon\mathsf{mod}{R}\to\mathsf{mod}{R}
is the first local cohomology (i.e. the first right derived functor of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}
). If the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is not principal, the situation gets even more complicated as also higher local cohomologies come into play.

3.5.
Thick generation for dualizable objects

We again fix a tensor-triangulated, compactly-rigidly generated category
\mathsf{T}
, with a Noetherian action of
R
and a homogeneous ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset R
. Our aim here is characterize in terms of torsion compact objects when
x\in\thick(g)
for a pair
x,g\in(\Gamma\mathsf{T})^{d}
.

We will need two preparatory lemmas. The first one allows to detect vanishing of morphisms in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
in terms of their tensors in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
.

3.35
.

Let
f\colon x\to y
be a homomorphism in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

f\otimes c=0
for each
c\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
;

(b)

f\otimes(\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]})=0
for each
m\geq 0
;

(c)

f=0
.

Proof.

The first condition obviously implies the second one.
If
f
is such that
f\otimes(\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]})=0
for each
m\geq 0
, then
3.5
yields a direct system of maps
f\otimes(\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]})\colon x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\to y/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}
in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
with a cocone
f
, and all the compositions
x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\to y/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\to y
vanish by assumption. So the compositions
x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\to x\to y
vanish as well, and so does
f
by Corollary
3.32
.
∎

The second lemma shows how properties of the sequence from
2.18
translate to Koszul complexes.

3.36
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})\subset R
is a homogeneous ideal generated by a sequence of
k
elements of total degree
d
. If we have an object
g
and a sequence of maps

x_{0}\xrightarrow{\phi_{0}}x_{1}\xrightarrow{\phi_{1}}x_{2}\xrightarrow{\phi_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\phi_{2^{2k}-1}}x_{2^{2k}}

in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
such that
\Hom(g,\phi_{i})=0
for each
i
, then also

\Hom(g/\!\!/\mathfrak{b},(\phi_{2^{2k}-1}\circ\cdots\phi_{1}\circ\phi_{0})\otimes\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{b})=0.

Proof.

Since
g/\!\!/\mathfrak{b}\in\thick^{2^{k}-1}(g)
by
Lemma

3.1
,
[
17
, Proposition 3.3]
implies that
\Hom(g/\!\!/\mathfrak{b},\phi_{i+2^{k}-1}\circ\cdots\circ\phi_{i})=0
for each
0\leq i\leq 2^{k}(2^{k}-1)
.
Further, note that
\mathbb{1}/\!\!/r\cong\Sigma^{1-e}{(\mathbb{1}/\!\!/r)}^{\mathrm{SW}}
for each
r\in R
of degree
e
. By induction on
k
, we get that
\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{b}\cong\Sigma^{k-d}{(\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{b})}^{\mathrm{SW}}
and so

\Hom(g/\!\!/\mathfrak{b},\phi_{i+2^{k}-1}\circ\cdots\circ\phi_{i})\cong\Hom(\Sigma^{k-d}g,(\phi_{i+2^{k}-1}\circ\cdots\circ\phi_{i})\otimes\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{b})=0

for each
0\leq i\leq 2^{k}(2^{k}-1)
. The conclusion follows by another application of
[
17
, Proposition 3.3]
, using that
g/\!\!/\mathfrak{b}\in\thick^{2^{k}-1}(\Sigma^{k-d}g)
.
∎

Now we can prove the main result of the section.

3.37
.

Let
\mathsf{T}
be an R-linear, Noetherian, tensor-triangulated, compactly-rigidly generated category and let
\mathfrak{a}\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal. Then the following are equivalent for
x,g\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d})
:

(i)

x\in\thick(g)
in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
;

(ii)

there exist
n\geq 0
such that for each
m>0
, we have
x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\in\thick^{n}(g/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]})
in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
.

If
g
is of the form
g\cong\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}c
for
c\in\mathsf{T}^{c}
, then the conditions are further equivalent to

(iii)

there exist
n\geq 0
such that for each
m>0
,
x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\in\thick^{n}(c)
in
\mathsf{T}^{c}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
y\in\thick(x)
; then
y\in\thick^{n}(x)
for some
n\geq 0
.
Since
-\otimes\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\colon(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}\to(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
is an exact functor for each
m>0
, we get the implication
\ref{item:dual-gen-in-Td}\implies\ref{item:dual-gen-in-Tc}
.

Conversely, assume condition
(ii)
. We will apply
2.18
to the
R^{\wedge}
-linear triangulated category
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
. Clearly, the category
\mathsf{F}
there contains
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
by
Theorem

3.22
and the constructed sequence

x=x_{0}\xrightarrow{\phi_{0}}x_{1}\xrightarrow{\phi_{1}}x_{2}\xrightarrow{\phi_{2}}\cdots

belongs to
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
. If
k
is the length of a chosen generating sequence for
\mathfrak{a}
,
Lemma

3.36
tells us that

\Hom(g/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]},(\phi_{i+2^{2k}-1}\circ\cdots\phi_{i+1}\circ\phi_{i})\otimes\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]})=0

for each

and
m\geq 1
. As
x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\in\thick^{n}(g/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]})
by assumption,
[
17
, Proposition 3.3]
implies that

\Hom(x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]},(\phi_{2^{2k}n-1}\circ\cdots\phi_{1}\circ\phi_{0})\otimes\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]})=0,

or equivalently that
(\phi_{2^{2k}n-1}\circ\cdots\phi_{1}\circ\phi_{0})\otimes\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}=0
for each
m\geq 1
.
Consequently,
\phi_{2^{2k}n-1}\circ\cdots\phi_{1}\circ\phi_{0}=0
by
Lemma

3.35
, and
x\in\thick^{2^{2k}n-1}(g)
by
Lemma

2.19

(2)
. Hence
\ref{item:dual-gen-in-Tc}\implies\ref{item:dual-gen-in-Td}
.

In order to prove
\ref{item:dual-gen-in-Tc}\implies\ref{item:dual-gen-tor-Tc}
, notice that if
g\cong\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}c
and
\mathfrak{a}
has a chosen generating sequence of length
k
, then we get by the same argument as in the proof of

3.36

g/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\cong(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}c)/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\cong\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(c/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]})\cong c/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\in\thick^{2^{k}-1}(c).

It follows that
(ii)
implies that
x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\in\thick^{(n+1)(2^{k}-1)-1}(c)
for each

.

Finally, assume
(iii)
and that

, so that

for each

. Given any particular

is a direct summand of

3.38
.

If

is an ordinary commutative Noetherian ring,
\mathsf{T}=\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{Mod}R)
and

, Balmer and Sanders

for any

. The idea of their proof, rephrased in our terminology, is that they proved condition

of

3.37
for any given

. The choice of

.

4.
Reconstruction of dualizable objects from compact objects

Throughout all of
Section

4
, we fix a tensor-triangulated, compactly-rigidly generated category
\mathsf{T}
, with a Noetherian action of a Noetherian graded ring
R
, and we further fix a homogeneous ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
. We only be interested in the subcategory
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
of
\mathsf{T}
.
We will show that the categories of torsion compact and torsion dualizable objects determine each other as triangulated closed symmetric monoidal categories (with the nuance that the tensor unit is not compact) in a way formally similar to
[
47
]
. As the category of torsion compact objects is a subcategory of the one of torsion dualizable objects, the main problem is to reconstruct the latter category from the former one.

Despite the formal similarity of the results, our techniques are completely different from
[
47
]
. Our categories potentially admit no nontrivial
t
-structures, there cannot be any translation invariant metric associated to our completion in the sense of
[
47
, Heuristic 9]
, and torsion dualizable objects are typically not compactly supported with respect to our “Cauchy sequences” in the sense of
[
47
, Definition 4]
.
So our class of examples seems to be of a rather different nature: we use the monoidal structure in an essential way and where the compactly supported objects would induce eventually stable inverse systems, we only get Mittag-Leffler ones by
Corollary

3.33
.

4.1.
Compacts versus dualizables as additive categories

Following
[
37
, §2.5]
, we will denote by
\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
the category of graded
R
-linear (i.e. enriched over the category of graded modules
\mathsf{Mod}R
) contravariant functors

(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c,\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Mod}R.

Note that since
\id_{x}\in\Hom_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}^{c}}(x,x)
is annihilated by some power of
\mathfrak{a}
for each
x\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
, the value
F(x)
must be an
\mathfrak{a}
-power torsion module for each
F\in\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
.
We also have the associated
R
-linear restricted Yoneda functor

(4.1)

h\colon\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}\to\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c},\quad h(x)=\Hom_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}}(-,x)_{|(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}}.

A key point for the reconstruction of
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
from
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
is the following proposition. The situation is formally similar to the one studied in
[
34
, Proposition 5.2.8]
, where one completes the perfect derived category of a coherent ring to its bounded derived category.

4.2
.

The restriction of the functor
h\colon\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}\to\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
to dualizable objects is fully faithful.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
. By Yoneda’s lemma,
h
is fully faithful on
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
.
Then, by
Corollary

3.32

\displaystyle\Hom(x,y)

\displaystyle\cong\varprojlim_{m}\Hom(\Sigma^{md-k}x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]},y)

\displaystyle\cong\varprojlim_{m}\varinjlim_{l}\Hom(\Sigma^{md-k}x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]},\Sigma^{ld-k}y/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[l]})

\displaystyle\cong\varprojlim_{m}\varinjlim_{l}\Hom(h(\Sigma^{md-k}x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}),h(\Sigma^{ld-k}y/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[l]}))

\displaystyle\cong\varprojlim_{m}\Hom(h(\Sigma^{md-k}x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}),\varinjlim_{l}h(\Sigma^{ld-k}y/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[l]}))

\displaystyle\cong\Hom(\varinjlim_{m}h(\Sigma^{md-k}x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}),\varinjlim_{l}h(\Sigma^{ld-k}y/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[l]}))

\displaystyle\cong\Hom(h(x),h(y)).\qed

While we will devote next subsections to describing the essential image of
h_{|(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}}
, it is easy to recover
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
inside
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
as finitely presentable objects.

4.3
.

The following are equivalent for an object
x\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
:

(1)

x
is compact in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
;

(2)

\Hom(x,-)\colon(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}\to\mathsf{mod}R
preserves all direct limits that exist in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
.

Proof.

If
x
is compact, then
h(x)
is finitely presentable (even finitely generated projective) in
\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
, and so
\Hom(c,-)
preserves all existing direct limits in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
by
Proposition

4.2
.
Conversely, we have
x\cong\varinjlim_{m}\Sigma^{md-k}x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}
in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
by
Corollary

3.32
. So if
\Hom(x,-)
preserves direct limits, then
x
must be a summand of one of the
\Sigma^{md-k}x/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}
and, in particular, compact.
∎

4.2.
Reconstructing the closed monoidal structure

Our next step is to lift the tensor product from
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
to
\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
(cp.
[
2
, §A.13]
).
For that purpose, we will need

4.4
.

Let
F,G
be in
\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
. The
Day convolution
of
F
and
G
is defined as

(F\boxtimes G)(d)=\int^{c_{1},c_{2}\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}}F(c_{1})\otimes_{R}G(c_{2})\otimes_{R}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(d,c_{1}\otimes c_{2})

This assignment extends to a bifunctor over
\mathsf{Mod}R
,

\boxtimes\colon\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}\times\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}\to\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}.

4.5
.

Since a coend is a colimit and the tensor product on
\mathsf{Mod}(R)
preserves small colimits in each variable, the Day convolution bifunctor
\boxtimes
also preserves small colimits in each variable.

In fact, as the target category in which we are taking the coend is
\mathsf{Mod}R
, we have also another explicit formula for
(F\boxtimes G)(d)
as the smallest quotient graded
R
-module of the direct sum

\bigoplus_{c_{1},c_{2}\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}}F(c_{1})\otimes_{R}G(c_{2})\otimes_{R}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(d,c_{1}\otimes c_{2}),

where for each pair of maps
v_{1}\colon c_{1}^{\prime}\to c_{1}
and
v_{2}\colon c_{2}^{\prime}\to c_{2}
, and an element
x\otimes y\otimes u\in F(c_{1})\otimes_{R}G(c_{2})\otimes_{R}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(d,c_{1}^{\prime}\otimes c_{2}^{\prime})
, we identify

F(v_{1})(x)\otimes F(v_{2})(y)\otimes u\sim x\otimes y\otimes((v_{1}\otimes v_{2})\circ u).

A basic property of the co-end is the following “dual” version of the Yoneda lemma. Although we state it in a general form, using elements of enriched category theory from
[
29
]
, we only need it in a much more down-to-the-earth situation where
\mathsf{V}=\mathsf{Mod}R
.

4.6
.

Let
(\mathsf{V},\otimes,\mathbb{1})
be a bicomplete closed symmetric monoidal category and
\mathsf{C}
be a small
\mathsf{V}
-enriched category. For any
\mathsf{V}
-functor
F\colon\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{V}
, there is an isomorphism,
\mathsf{V}
-natural both in
F
and
b\in\mathsf{C}
:

F(b)\cong\int^{a\in\mathsf{C}}\underline{\Hom}_{\mathsf{C}}(a,b)\otimes F(a)

Dually, for any functor
G\colon\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{V}
, there is an isomorphism,
\mathsf{V}
-natural both in
G
and
b\in\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}
:

G(b)\cong\int^{a\in\mathsf{C}}\underline{\Hom}_{\mathsf{C}}(b,a)\otimes G(a)

Proof.

This is standard. A version for
(\mathsf{V},\otimes)=(\mathsf{Set},\times)
is a part of
[
39
, Proposition 2.2.1]
, but the same proof applies in general using the
\mathsf{V}
-enriched Yoneda lemma
[
29
, §2.4]
.
∎

4.7
.

The isomorphisms from the latter proposition can be made explicit. We will discuss only the first case as the second in similar.
For each
a,b\in\mathsf{V}
, the homomorphism of the corresponding Hom-objects
\underline{\Hom}_{\mathsf{C}}(a,b)\to\underline{\Hom}_{\mathsf{V}}(F(a),F(b))
has an adjoint form, the evaluation morphism

\varepsilon_{a,b}\colon\underline{\Hom}_{\mathsf{C}}(a,b)\otimes F(a)\to F(b).

Jointly, the evaluation morphisms form a
\mathsf{V}
-natural cowedge (cf.
[
29
, §1.8]
), inducing a morphism
\int^{a\in\mathsf{C}}\underline{\Hom}_{\mathsf{C}}(a,b)\otimes F(a)\to F(b)
.
The inverse to this map is also explicit. One tensors the unit map
\eta_{b}\colon\mathbb{1}\to\underline{\Hom}_{\mathsf{C}}(b,b)
with
F(b)
, which gives rise to

F(b)\cong\mathbb{1}\otimes F(b)\to\underline{\Hom}_{\mathsf{C}}(b,b)\otimes F(b)\to\int^{a\in\mathsf{C}}\underline{\Hom}_{\mathsf{C}}(a,b)\otimes F(a).

As an immediate corollary, we obtain well-known properties of the Day convolution: it is symmetric and associative up to natural transformations satisfying the usual coherence axioms, and the restricted Yoneda functor (
4.1
) is (non-unitary) strong monoidal. Getting back to our setting, we obtain the following formula.

4.8
.

For any dualizable torsion object
x\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
and any
F\in\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
, we have an isomorphism

h(x)\boxtimes F\cong F({x}^{\mathrm{SW}}\otimes-),

natural (in the
\mathsf{Mod}R
-enriched sense) both in
x
and
F
.

Proof.

If
x\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
is compact, we have for any other compact object
c\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
a chain of natural isomorphisms, where the first and the last one are using
Lemma

4.6
and the symmetry of the tensor product while the second one comes from the dualizablity of
c
:

\displaystyle(h(x)\boxtimes F)(c)

\displaystyle=\int^{c_{1},c_{2}\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c_{1},x)\otimes_{R}F(c_{2})\otimes_{R}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,c_{1}\otimes c_{2})

\displaystyle\cong\int^{c_{2}\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}}F(c_{2})\otimes_{R}\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,x\otimes c_{2})

4.3.
Dualizable functors

In this part, we describe the essential image of the full strongly monoidal embedding

.

Let

(i)

for some

(ii)

(iii)

is finitely generated projective in

.

The restricted Yoneda functor

h_{|(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}}\colon\big((\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d},\otimes,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathbb{1}\big)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\big((\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c})^{d},\boxtimes,\tilde{\mathbb{1}}\big).

4.4.
Reconstructing the triangulated structure

Finally, we will take up the most subtle part, which is reconstructing the triangulated structure of
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
from that of
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
. The shift functor is the obvious one induced by the graded shift in
\mathsf{Mod}R
and it will turn out that the distinguished triangles in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
are colimits of distinguished triangles in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
.
Our arguments will use the following technical lemma.

4.14
.

Let
(M_{0}\to M_{1}\to M_{2}\to\cdots)
be a sequential direct system in an AB5 abelian category
\mathsf{A}
and let
N\in\mathsf{A}
. Then we have a short exact sequence of abelian groups

0\to\varprojlim\nolimits^{(1)}_{i}\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(M_{i},N)\to\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{A}}(\varinjlim_{i}M_{i},N)\to\varprojlim\nolimits_{i}\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{A}}(M_{i},N)\to 0

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence
0\to\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}M_{i}\to\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}M_{i}\to\varinjlim M_{i}\to 0
which defines the direct limit and apply
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(-,N)
. The desired short exact sequence is induced by the resulting long exact sequence around the term
\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{A}}(\varinjlim_{i}M_{i},N)
. One should also note that coproducts are exact in
\mathsf{A}
, and so the contravariant functor
\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{A}}(-,N)
sends coproducts in
\mathsf{A}
to products of abelian groups.
∎

4.15

In view of
Proposition

4.2
, one can extend

\tilde{h}\colon\mathsf{mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c},

where
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
is the category of

[

.

For brevity, we also put

4.16

Let

from
4.15

By adjunction, this is the same as proving that

The Day convolution product

is dualizable in
\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
by
Corollary

4.13
(or directly by

). In particular, for each

, the Day convolution products with

induce a homomorphism of abelian groups

(4.18)

\zeta_{F,G}\colon\Ext^{1}(F,G)\to\prod_{m\geq 0}\Ext^{1}\big(U_{m}\boxtimes F,U_{m}\boxtimes G).

4.19
.

If

are in the essential image of the functor
\tilde{h}\colon\mathsf{mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}\to\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
from

, then the above map

is injective.

Proof.

Suppose that

is such that
U_{m}\boxtimes\varepsilon
splits for each

. Then each
\psi_{m}\colon U_{m}\to\tilde{\mathbb{1}}
yields a morphism of short exact sequences
U_{m}\boxtimes\varepsilon\to\varepsilon
, which in turn gives rise to a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{m}\boxtimes\varepsilon\colon}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{m}\boxtimes G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{m}\boxtimes E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{m}\boxtimes F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{m}\colon}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{m}\boxtimes F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon\colon}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{m}\boxtimes F}

Then also each

for each

, where

by

4.16
.
∎

Finally, we obtain the description of distinguished triangles in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
in terms of those in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
.

4.20
.

Let
x\to y\to z\to\Sigma x
be a diagram in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
. Then it is a distinguished triangle in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
if and only if

(\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]})\otimes x\to(\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]})\otimes y\to(\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]})\otimes z\to(\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]})\otimes\Sigma x\cong\Sigma(\mathbb{1}/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[m]}\otimes x)

is a distinguished triangle in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
for each
m\geq 1
.

Proof.

The ‘only if’ part is clear since
-\otimes-
is assumed to be exact in both variables.

For the ’if’ part, we transport the tensor-triangulated structure from
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
to the category of dualizable objects in
\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
using the monoidal equivalence
Corollary

4.13
and prove the statement there. We can also assume that the shift functor
\Sigma
on
\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
is given simply by a graded shift on
\mathsf{Mod}R
.
Using
4.15
, suppose that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau\colon\quad h(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h(z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma h(x)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w}

is a diagram in

such that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{m}\boxtimes\tau\colon\quad U_{m}\boxtimes h(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{m}\boxtimes h(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{m}\boxtimes h(z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma U_{m}\boxtimes h(x)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{m}\boxtimes u}

is a distinguished triangle for each

(making the identification

implicit). In particular,

for each

. Now, recall that we have a direct system

in

with a colimiting cocone

\mathsf{Mod}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}

is a triangulated category, we can complete

to a distinguished triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau^{\prime}\colon\quad h(x)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w^{\prime}}

giving rise to a similar direct system of distinguished triangles

. For each single

.

be an

5.
Consequences of strong generation

Now we will focus on compactly-rigidly generated Noetherian
R
-linear categories
\mathsf{T}
such that the category of compact objects
\mathsf{T}^{c}
admits a strong generator (recall
Definition

2.17
). As explained in
Example

2.23
, this can be viewed as a regularity condition in the context of commutative Noetherian rings.

5.1.
Strong generators for torsion and complete dualizable objects

First, we are going to prove that if
\mathsf{T}^{c}
has a strong generator, then so have it the categories of dualizable
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion and dualizable
\mathfrak{a}
-complete objects. So the slogan is that if
\mathsf{T}^{c}
is “regular”, so is the small tt-completion
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}\simeq(\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
.
Note that, in contrast, the smaller categories of compact objects
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
and
(\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
are typically
not
strongly generated due to
[
58
, Theorem 4.1]
.

5.1
.

Let
\mathsf{T}
be an
R
-linear, Noetherian, tensor-triangulated, compactly-rigidly generated category. Let
g
be a strong generator of
\mathsf{T}^{c}
. Then
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}g
is a strong generator of
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
and
\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}g
is a strong generator of
(\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
.

Proof.

Since the equivalence of categories
\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\colon\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
maps the subcategories
\thick(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}g)
to
\thick(\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}g)
and
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}g)^{d}
to
(\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}g)^{d}
, it suffices to prove the version for
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}g)^{d}
.
This is then an immediate consequence of
Theorem

3.37
, since condition
(iii)
there is always satisfied by the strong generation assumption.

However, we can also give a more direct and streamlined argument.
Fix
D\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\thick^{D}(g)=\mathsf{T}^{c}
.
Then for any
c\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
, we have
c\in\thick^{D}(g)
and, by applying the exact functor
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}
, also

c\cong\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}c\in\thick^{D}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}g).

Hence
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}\subset\thick^{D}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}g)
.
Now fix
d\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
and recall that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
has a canonical action of
R^{\wedge}
by
Remark

3.16
. Then for any
k\geq 1
,

d/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[k]}\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}\subset\thick^{D}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}g)

Since
d\cong\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}d
is a homotopy colimit of certain shifts of
d/\!\!/\mathfrak{a}^{[k]}
by
Proposition

3.7
, the defining triangle for the homotopy colimit shows that
d\in\Loc^{2D+1}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}g)
. Hence, by
Proposition

2.20
applied to the
R^{\wedge}
-linear category
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
, the thick subcategory
\mathsf{U}=(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
and the object
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}g\in\mathsf{U}
, we obtain that

\thick^{2D+1}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}g)=\thick(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}g)=(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}.

Here we also used that
\Hom_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}g,d^{\prime})
is a finitely generated
R^{\wedge}
-module for any
d^{\prime}\in\mathsf{U}
by
Corollary

3.21
.
∎

5.2.
Characterization of dualizable objects via Hom-groups

Putting all the previous results together, we obtain several characterizations of dualizable objects in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
and
\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
in terms of Hom-groups from and to compacts.
First of all, in a strictly Noetherian category (in particular in a
\mathsf{T}
where the category of compact objects is strongly generated, recall
Proposition

2.21
), the dualizable objects are even characterized by finite generatedness of homomorphism
R
-modules.
This can be viewed as a partial converse to
Proposition

3.19
and
Corollary

3.20
.

5.2
.

Assume that
\mathsf{T}
is strictly Noetherian.
Then:

(a)

Let
x\in\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
be an object such that, for any compact object
c\in(\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
,
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,x)
is Noetherian over
R
. Then
x
is dualizable in
\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
.

(b)

Let
x\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
be an object such that, for any compact object
c\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
,
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c,x)
is Noetherian over
R
. Then
x
is dualizable in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
.

Proof.

We first prove
(a)
. Let
c\in(\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}=(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
. By
Proposition

2.2
, it suffices to show that
c\otimes_{\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}}x
is compact. To this end, suppose that
c^{\prime}\in\mathsf{T}^{c}
. Then

\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c^{\prime},c\otimes_{\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}}x)=\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c^{\prime},\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}(c\otimes x))\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}c^{\prime},c\otimes x),

where the first equality comes from
Proposition

2.15

(b)
and the second isomorphism from the adjunction
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\dashv\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}
. Since
c
is dualizable both in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
and
\mathsf{T}
and the Spanier-Whitehead duals coincide, we further have

\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}c^{\prime},c\otimes x)\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}((\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}c^{\prime})\otimes{c}^{\mathrm{SW}},x)\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c^{\prime}\otimes{c}^{\mathrm{SW}},x),

where the last isomorphism follows from the fact that
{c}^{\mathrm{SW}}\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
.
In particular,
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(c^{\prime},c\otimes_{\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}}x)
is Noetherian over
R
, since
c^{\prime}\otimes{c}^{\mathrm{SW}}
is in
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}=(\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
. As
c^{\prime}
was chosen arbitrarily, our hypothesis on
\mathsf{T}
then implies that
c\otimes_{\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}}x
is compact.

We now turn to
(b)
. Note that
\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}x
satisfies the hypothesis of
(a)
, hence is dualizable in
\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
. Hence
x
is dualizable in the equivalent category
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
.
∎

As for the converse to the other halves of
Proposition

3.19
and
Corollary

3.20
, we can prove it only for some categories
\mathsf{T}
, namely those which are compactly cogenerated in the sense of
Definition

2.26
(see also
Example

2.27
).

5.3
.

Assume that
\mathsf{T}
is compactly cogenerated.
Then:

(a)

Let

be an object such that, for any compact object

,
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(x,c)
is Noetherian over
R
. Then
x
is dualizable in
\Lambda^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
.

(b)

Let
x\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
be an object such that, for any compact object
c\in(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
,
\Hom_{\mathsf{T}}(x,c)
is Noetherian over
R
. Then
x
is dualizable in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T}
.

Proof.

The argument is completely dual to the one for
Proposition

5.2
. Only instead of strict Noetherianity, we use
Corollary

2.25
.
∎

Now we can give a characterization of torsion dualizable objects. If
R
is graded local and
\mathfrak{a}\subset R

(ii)

is a finitely generated

(iii)

If, moreover,

(iv)

for any compact object

-module;

(v)

.

5.3.
Representability theorems

In the presence of strong generator, we also have another way to recover (as
R
-linear categories) torsion dualizable modules from the torsion compact ones and vice versa, complementary to §
3.26
. This is closely related to representability theorems of Bondal and Van den Bergh
[
15
, Theorem 1.3 and Appendix A]
, Rouquier
[
55
, Corollary 7.50]
and Neeman
[
49
]
and makes the relation between
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{c}
and
(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathsf{T})^{d}
looks similar as the one between
\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{per}}(X)
and
\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{b}}(\mathsf{coh}X)
for a nice enough Noetherian scheme. However, our methods are very different as, for example unlike
[
49
, Theorem 0.4]
, we cannot expect the existence of non-trivial
t
-structures since some power of the suspension functor in our setting may well be isomorphic to the identity.

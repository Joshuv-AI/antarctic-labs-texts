Microbial Enzymes in Industrial Biotechnology: Sources, Production, and Significant Applications of Lipases
Authors: Nisha Sharma, Yogesh K Ahlawat, Nattan Stalin, Sajid Mehmood, Sonia Morya, Anurag Malik, Malathi H, Jayshree Nellore, Deepak Bhanot
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract The variety of microorganisms represents the most prevalent sources utilized within diverse industries and research fields. Enzymes with microorganisms are applied in the use of industrial biotechnology. Since the dawn of civilization, there are techniques like extraction and fermentation that use plant or bacterial enzymes as well as other byproducts. Enzymes, the natural catalysts, are intricately involved in many aspects of life. Enzymes pose remarkable specificity for their substrate, which implies that these metabolic cycles in a living cell need to be executed by a team working in collaboration. The major sources of these enzymes are yeast, some fungi and bacteria. Just like all living forms, microbes interact with their environment in which they must live in order to survive. A large number of microorganisms that are capable of producing great varieties of enzymes are important in the production of bread, cheese, yogurt, beer, and many other foods. One of the most widely used lipolytic enzyme is lipase from various sources including food and dairy industry, leather, detergent, pulp and paper, bioenergy and even pharma. With the latest innovation in biotechnology, the need for organisms that produce different commercially important lipases which other strains of lipases do is increasing. Lipases produced from microbial cells have a major industrial significance because of their property of versatility and ease of mass production. This review seeks to clarify the sources of microorganisms, lipase production and purification processes, as well as the environmental and industrial uses of lipase enzymes.One-Sentence SummaryThis manuscript explores the diverse microbial sources of lipase, their production processes and the crucial applications in industries such as food, pharmaceuticals, and biofuels.Keywords: Enzymes, Lipase, Microbial productions, Purification, Industrial applications

Introduction

Ecofriendly enzymes are the best candidates to reduce energy cost because their catalysis reactions are specific to the substrate and result in greater returns. There is an increasing utilization of microbial lipases, thanks to the strong growth in enzyme technology. Lipases are a significant class of natural or man-made biocatalysts. According to Shin et al. (2019), lipases are a group of serine hydrolases and fall within the triacylglycerol acyl hydrolases sub-class, which sets them apart from esterases by virtue of their substrate. The first principle that tried to separate these two kinds of enzymes is interfacial activation, which is an increase of enzyme activity in the presence of an interface. But this attribute by itself does not allow for complete understanding and classification of these enzymes. The canonical sequences for lipases and esterases as applied to the ProSite database are rather close. Thus, lipases are defined as enzymes that hydrolyze the acylglycerol esters of fatty acids with side chains of greater than 10 for carbon atoms while esterase's substrate acrylics with 10 or less carbon atoms. Nevertheless, both types of enzymes have been shown to possess a wide range specificity of substrate which must be taken into account (Zavarise et al., 2024).

Lipolytic enzymes are aiding toward the bioenergy field, which is critically important at present (Erika et al., 2024). Other scientists have advanced these differentiations by adding complex structural bioinformatics and machine learning approaches that forecast enzyme activity as a function of a 3D model and choice of various substrates by the enzymes (Daniele et al., 2024). Furthermore, some metagenomic studies have identified new lipase and esterase-containing genes whose enzymes are more active and stable in extreme conditions that are very useful to industry (Kumar et al., 2023). While cutinases are commonly associated with the cleavage of ester bonds in cutin polymers, they are widely considered as a transitional class between lipases and esterases, since they can hydrolyze long and short-chain triglycerides without the requirement of interfacial activation (Lokha et al., 2020).

At present, active enzymes or microorganisms are used in biotechnology with the objective of obtaining as byproducts chemicals, minerals, and fuels from renewable sources. Industrial biotechnology is often called the third wave of biotechnology, and it is different from the first two, which are medical or red biotechnology and agriculture or green biotechnology. There is great interest in industrial biotechnology because it is thought that industrial biotechnology can improve productivity while decreasing energy usage, greenhouse gas emissions, waste production, and transition from fossil fuels to biobased value-added chemical production. Also called white biotechnology, industrial biotechnology is defined as the branch of biotechnology that deals with the alteration of microorganisms or enzymes to obtain energy, industrial chemicals, and goods from renewable resources, thereby reducing the consumption of fossil fuels and promoting sustainable industrial activities (Kordi et al., 2022; Tiwari et al., 2022). A particular class of lipases with altered microbial solubility has significant electrophoretic and biotechnological importance (Melani et al., 2020). In this review, we focus on various aspects of lipases, with particular emphasis on microbial lipase production, extraction, purification, and wider uses in food, leather, detergents, pharmaceuticals, and even in the environment. The article discusses possible lipase enzyme applications and emphasizes the importance of novel techniques of enzyme engineering in creating sustainable bio-based strategies.

Scope and Significance of Lipase Enzymes

Lipase enzymes are highly versatile and have found extensive applications across various industries due to their remarkable ability to function under diverse environmental conditions, including extreme pH, temperature, and solvent environments. Their broad specificity, high stability, and eco-friendly catalytic properties make them invaluable in sectors such as food processing, pharmaceuticals, biofuels, cosmetics, and biotechnological research (Abdelaziz et al., 2025).

The industrial applications of lipases are vast and varied. In the food industry, lipases play a crucial role in enhancing flavors, improving texture, and modifying fats to meet specific dietary and sensory requirements. In the pharmaceutical industry, lipases are employed for the synthesis of enantiomerically pure compounds, which are critical for the production of chiral drugs. Their stereoselectivity allows for the production of specific drug isomers, enhancing the efficacy and reducing side effects of pharmaceuticals (Girelli et al., 2020). Their role extends to the synthesis of bioactive lipids, which serve as precursors for new therapeutic compounds. The biofuel industry has seen significant advancements with the use of lipase enzymes, particularly in the production of biodiesel through transesterification reactions. Lipase-catalyzed biodiesel production offers several advantages over traditional chemical methods, including operation under milder conditions, reduced energy consumption, and minimized use of harsh chemicals (Ozgen et al., 2020). This results in a more sustainable, cost-effective, and environment friendly approach to biofuel production. The cosmetic and personal care industry benefits from lipase enzymes for their ability to modify oils and fats used in skincare and haircare products. They contribute to the formulation of emulsions, enhancing the texture, stability, and moisturizing properties of creams, lotions, and shampoos. Furthermore, lipases are involved in the production of biolubricants, biodegradable detergents, and biodiesel additives, promoting the development of environmentally friendly and sustainable products. The production of bioplastics and the development of green chemistry applications also highlight the significance of lipases in promoting sustainability (Phulpoto et al., 2020). Their use as catalysts in various chemical reactions reduces the need for toxic reagents and harsh conditions, aligning with the principles of green chemistry and reducing the environmental footprint of industrial processes. The significance of lipase enzymes in industrial applications lies in their unique properties and the wide range of processes they can catalyze. One of their most remarkable features is their ability to function under extreme conditions, including high temperatures, acidic or alkaline environments, and in the presence of organic solvents. This stability makes them ideal candidates for industrial processes that require robust and resilient catalysts (Titus et al., 2018).

Lipases are also highly specific, allowing for selective reactions that are crucial in pharmaceutical synthesis and the production of specialty chemicals. Their stereoselectivity enables the production of chiral compounds, which are essential for the efficacy of many drugs. This specificity reduces the need for extensive purification steps, thereby improving the efficiency and cost-effectiveness of manufacturing processes (Joseph et al., 2007). From an environmental perspective, lipases offer a greener alternative to traditional chemical catalysts. They operate under mild conditions, reducing energy consumption and minimizing the production of hazardous by-products. This makes them integral to sustainable industrial practices, as they help reduce the carbon footprint and environmental impact of manufacturing processes.

Moreover, the economic significance of lipase enzymes cannot be overstated. Their ability to catalyze reactions with high efficiency and specificity reduces the need for expensive reagents, minimizes waste generation, and lowers production costs. This makes them attractive for industries seeking cost-effective and sustainable solutions. Lipase enzymes are indispensable in modern industry, offering eco-friendly, efficient, and cost-effective solutions across diverse sectors. Their broad specificity, high stability, and ability to catalyze reactions under mild conditions underscore their significance in promoting sustainable development and innovative industrial processes. As biotechnological advancements continue, the potential applications of lipases are expected to expand, further enhancing their role in shaping a more sustainable and environmentally conscious future.

Fungal Lipases: Potential Fungal Strains

Fungal lipases find applications from food processing to biodiesel production, pharmaceuticals, and even waste treatment because of the enzyme's effectiveness at extreme pH and temperature conditions with organic solvents. This ability to be made in recombinant systems increases their usefulness further due to the genetic manipulations that can be crafted for targeted industrial uses. Lipase-producing fungi such as Aspergillus, Rhizopus, Penicillium, and Mucor have the capability of extramitochondrial lipid hydrolysis and are capable of lipolytic actions over a broad range of substrates. Because of the simplicity of their production, these enzymes are commonly used in biotechnology and are known to work under different environments. With such remarkable features, lipases are said to possess high specificity, stability, and broad applications in different fields of biotechnology (Mahfoudhi et al., 2022; Morya et al., 2023).

Novo Nordisk is a well-known lipase enzyme manufacturer and marketer of “Lipolase,” which was produced commercially for the first time. This enzyme is produced from the fungus “Thermomyces lanuginosus” and “Aspergillus Oryzae” and is used as a transcriptional fungus. The discovery enabled the industrial application of fungal lipases. Their capacity to produce a wide variety of lipases that can serve multiple functions has already been invested on. In one study, multiple lipases synthesizing fungi were recovered from the soils of the Brazilian Savanna; this serves as a testament to the unexplored, new fungal-rich soils in Brazil (Melo et al., 2017). Cihangir and Sarikaya reported in 2004 that they identified an Aspergillus sp. which had lipase activity of 17 U/ml in soil samples from Turkey (Elemuo et al., 2019). Greater lipolytic yeast species include Yarrowia lipolytica, Rhodotorulaglutinis, Pichia burtonii, Candida rugosa, Tropicalis, and Antarctica (Ahmed et al., 2019). As an instance, Rhodotorula mucilaginosa MTCC 8737 is a strain recovered from a marine soil in the Arabian Sea close to an oil drilling site and was initially identified by picking hydrolytic, halo-forming bacterial colonies on tributyrin agar (Yu et al., 2013).

Kumar and Gupta (2008) uncovered 15 yeast strains from oil and petroleum waste sited in Delhi, India, which underscores the possibility of employing extreme surroundings for new lipase producers. This research captures the importance of extreme environment concerning microbial diversity and enzyme lipase production necessary for bioremediation and industrial processes. Griebeler et al. (2012) work further endorses this view when he studies the isolation and screening of microorganisms with lipase-producing potential and with it the capture of active microbial strains to be incorporated in biotechnology.

Furthermore, the economic feasibility of microbial lipases production is even more pronounced because the lipase producers can be grown on cheap substrates in large quantities, maximizing profit margins (Souza et al., 2015).

Physical and chemical factors that affects the lipase enzyme production.

Lipases are incredibly efficient because of how their catalytic action is active with varying temperatures and pH levels alongside organic solvent. The development of fermentation techniques and genetic engineering have allowed for the finer production processes to amplify the yield of microbial lipases for industrial purposes. Nutritional and physico-chemical factors in the culture like lipid sources, inorganic salts, stirring, and dissolved oxygen content all affect the activity of bacterial lipases. Other than these, carbohydrates such as sugars, sugar alcohols, polysaccharides, whey, and even casamino acids are also very important for lipase production. More recent work suggests that the fermentation processes, particularly the type of carbon source used, may also improve lipase yields. For example, high yields of lipase are dependent on fatty carbon sources because these types of carbon sources affect the enzyme activity and production rate (Alabdalall et al., 2020). In general, other organic nitrogen sources like peptone or yeast extract are more commonly used for lipase production from different Bacillus species (like Bacillus strain A30-1, B. alcalophilus, B. licheniformis), and some species of Pseudomonas (such as Pseudomonas fragi, P. fluorescens). Staphylococcus haemolyticus L62 has used a mixture of yeast extract with tryptone.

Apart from that, specific microbes, when researched, were found to exploit ammonium chloride and diammonium hydrogen phosphate as effective potential sources of inorganic nitrogen. The polysaccharides, glycogen, hyaluronate, laminarin, pectin, and gum Arabic have been reported to further increase exolipase production in Serratia marcescens, while glycogen, hyaluronate, and alginate do so in Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Niyonzima and More, 2014). The application of calcium chloride is known for stimulating lipase production from a range of Bacillus species, which is of great importance for the industrial use of these enzymes. Research suggests that bacterial lipases, in general, work best at neutral to alkaline pH values. More precisely, lipases from species of Bacilli like Bacillus atrophaeus and Bacillus licheniformis are active over a wide range of pH from 3 to 12, though greater activity is noted at alkaline pH, particularly above seven (Sharma et al., 2017). It has been documented that Bacillus subtilis increases the activity of lipase in highly alkaline environments, achieving optimum conditions at pH 9.5 (Haniya et al., 2017). Bacterial lipases do well thermally within the range of 30-60°C. However, some strains such as those of Bacillus and Pseudomonas are very thermostable and are therefore suitable for a number of high-temperature industrial processes (Bhosale et al., 2015).

The function of the enzyme was still active after 150 min at 70°C. This refers to the extinction which accompanied the addition of ethylene glycol, sorbitol, and glycerol that were used because they are known to improve the thermal stability of lipases (Farrokh et al., 2017). The lipases from species of Pseudomonas are among the most stable lipases, with some records reporting stability up to 100°C and in some cases even 150°C (Hagaggi, 2020). More specifically, Bacillus stearothermophilus produces an exceptionally thermotolerant lipase with a half-life of around 100°C of about 15-25 min. This feature is significant in processes that demand the activity of an enzyme at high temperatures. Optimally speaking for the production of lipases, different strains of bacteria differ with regard to the incubation periods. For example, Bacillus sp. RS1 has been described as a strain that produces lipase after an incubation period of 12 hr. On the other hand, Pseudomonas fragi and Pseudomonas fluorescens attained peak lipase production after 72 and 96 hr, respectively (Obeidat & Al-Shomali, 2023). It has been reported that lipase production is dependent upon the organism and the composition of the medium, with some increases reported as high as 50-fold due to the optimization of culture media.

These findings, along with previous records, suggest that lipase production can be observed every 12 hr, where higher values lipase concentration is noted at the peak around 48 hr, after which incubation leads to a decline in value (Kiymaci et al., 2022). In addition, Yarrowia lipolytica has also been noted on account of their ability to produce lipase amounting to 139 ± 2 U/g during 14 hr of SSF (Sipiczki et al., 2024). This indicates various microorganisms’ abilities to synthesize lipases under optimum conditions, which is crucial for industrial biotechnology applications that require high enzyme activity and stability. The advantages of high volumetric productivity and the use of agro-industrial wastes for enzyme production accentuates the cost-effectiveness and green chemistry of using SSF (Costa et al., 2022).

Latest Advancements in Lipase Production Methods

Recent advancements in lipase production have significantly enhanced the efficiency, yield, and cost-effectiveness of these enzymes, broadening their industrial applications. Key developments encompass fermentation strategies, genetic engineering, optimization techniques, purification methods, and immobilization technologies.

Fermentation Techniques

SSF has gained prominence over traditional SmF for lipase production. SSF utilizes solid substrates, often agro-industrial residues, providing a natural habitat for filamentous fungi. This method offers advantages such as reduced energy consumption, lower equipment costs, and decreased contamination risks. Moreover, SSF has been associated with higher lipase activity due to the solid-state environment promoting enzyme production (Daniele et al., 2025).

Genetic Engineering

Genetic modifications have been employed to enhance lipase production. Codon optimization of lipase genes, such as the lip3 gene of Candida rugosa, has resulted in a 50- to 70-fold increase in lipase production. Additionally, promoter optimization techniques have significantly advanced lipase production by modulating gene expression levels. Insertion mutant libraries have also been developed to improve lipase expression in recombinant strains, facilitating higher yields (Albayati et al., 2020).

Optimization Techniques

Response surface methodology (RSM) has been effectively applied to critical and accurate optimization of fermentation conditions for lipase production. RSM evaluates the interactions among various factors influencing lipase production, such as substrate concentration, pH, temperature and incubation time to identify optimal conditions for maximum enzyme yield (Soares et al., 2022).

Purification Methods

Advancements in purification techniques have improved lipase recovery and activity. Reverse micellar systems (RMS) have emerged as an efficient method for lipase purification, offering high recovery rates and reduced processing times. RMS utilize surfactants to create microreactors that protect enzymes during extraction, resulting in enhanced purification efficiency (Kim et al., 2024).

Immobilization Technologies

Immobilization of lipases onto solid supports or within gel matrices has been developed to enhance enzyme stability and reusability. This approach improves resistance to harsh conditions, such as high temperatures and organic solvents, and extends the enzyme's operational lifespan. Recent innovations include the use of nanomaterials and microcapsules for immobilization, which further enhance stability and activity in non-aqueous environments (Mirsalami et al., 2024).

Sustainable Production Practices

Sustainability has become a focus in lipase production, with efforts directed toward green chemistry metrics. Process assessments have been conducted to evaluate the environmental impact of lipase production methods, aiming to reduce mass intensity and improve process efficiency. For instance, sustainable production methods for lipases from Thermomyces lanuginosus have been developed, aligning with green chemistry principles by minimizing waste and energy consumption (Le et al., 2024).

These advancements have not only increased enzyme yields and activity but have also expanded the potential applications of lipases across various industries, including food, pharmaceuticals, biofuels, and cosmetics. Continued research and development in these areas are expected to further enhance the efficiency and sustainability of lipase production processes.

Factors Affecting Lipase Enzyme Production: Process Parameters

A variety of physical as well as chemical components influence the growth and metabolism of microorganisms that synthesize lipase. The variables can be split into media components such as sources of carbon and nitrogen, and physical factors like pH, temperature, moisture levels, degree of aeration, and level of agitation (Shanmugam et al., 2022). The combination of these factors is critical for the optimization of enzyme production both in the industry and in laboratory settings. As mentioned earlier, sources of lipase include some substrate that is considered necessary and often functional as a carbon source. The activity of the enzyme and the particular microorganism's growth is caused mainly by temperature and pH of the medium. Besides, scientists tried to enhance lipase activity and stability using metal ions and other additives or even some aspects of nanotechnology. These assumptions are important for planning fermentation processes which should result in the production of large quantities of lipase with specific desirable features (Mehmood et al., 2024).

Carbon

The availability of monosaccharides and polysaccharides has great potential for lipase synthesis. While using mannitol as a carbon source for Bacillus megaterium AKG-1 lipase production, the optimal enzyme activity was found to be 848.0 U/ml. Moreover, the Staphylococcus pasteuri SNA59 isolate exhibited extracellular lipase activity in MPY medium with 1% olive oil added. Carbon sources such as olive oil have been shown to stimulate lipase production by A. radioresistens and Bacillus stearothermophilus SB-1. There have been many attempts to study lipase production in the presence of different carbon sources ranging from lipids to complex nutrient media. For example, olive and other vegetable oils are important substrates of microbial lipase because of their high value in oleic acid, which promotes lipolytic activity (Sampaio et al., 2022).

As with Candida viswanathii, biomass growth and energy metabolism are possible with several carbon compounds. The same was observed for lipase production by Rhizopus chinensis where enzyme expression is dependent on the presence of lipid substrates. Lipases are a type of inducible enzyme, which means that oil or other fat-containing substances like triacylglycerols, fatty acids, and glycerols must be available in order for there to be maximum activity. A study reported that Acinetobacter sp. has high lipase activity when using tributyrin as the carbon source (Valerio et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2023).

pH

The alteration in microbial population and pH level is known to affect the morphology of microorganisms and also has an effect on the rate of enzyme synthesis. Hence, both pH and morphology may determine the activity of enzymes and, for that reason, they are very important in enzyme studies. Several forms of enzymes are greatly affected by pH values such as acidophilic, alkalophilic or the combination of both. Minimal chances from ideal pH can result in drastic consequences on growth and even the production of the enzyme. For example, the lipase produced by fungi like strains of Rhizopus is best obtained with the pH set at approximately 7 in a basal medium (Safdar et al., 2023). The degree of pH acidity for maximum lipase production in different bacterial species seems to have a wide range, especially with many bacterial species having midrange optimum pH for growth at 7.0 (Akinduyite et al., 2022; Sena et al., 2022). Furthermore, some microorganisms may exhibit more than one peak pH that is ideal for growth due to the nature of nutrients supplied, presence of some metal ions and even temperature (Cesário et al., 2021; Triyaswati and Ilmi, 2020).

The bacterial lipases exist, so P. fluorescens SIK W1 lipase that performs best in lipolytic activity at the acidic range is the only exception. Among others, lipase activity has been noted on Bacillus stearothermophilus SB-1, Bacillus licheniformis, and Bacillus atrophaeus SB-2. Szymczak et al. (2021) report that lipases derived from Bacillus SB-3 have a broad range of activity in pH (from 3 to 12). The initial pH of the growth medium must be verified and set properly prior to lipase fermentation (Szymczak et al., 2021).

The pH setting for maximum growth and lipase enzyme production will be about 7.0, a value known to be favorable for lipolytic Bacillus species such as Bacillus sp., Acinetobacter sp., and Burkholderia sp. (Khudhair, 2022). On the contrary, there are many claims by researchers where high activity was reported at alkaline pH conditions (>7.0). For example, earlier studies using lipases from Bacillus subtilis and B. pumilis suggested very high levels of 9.5 for optimal and stable pH activity (Troncoso et al., 2022). But, as cited earlier, temperature is one of the other important process parameters that affects the enzyme production (Vieira et al., 2021). Beyond the temperature range, the activity of the enzymes decreases as the bonds that hold together the 3D structure of the enzyme are altered, leading to a higher breakdown of amino acids that maintains the bonds. Enzyme activity increases as the temperature approaches the optimum because the rise in kinetic energy enhances the frequency of collisions between the enzyme and substrate, thereby accelerating the hydrolysis reaction (Andualem, 2014).

It has been widely reported that the hydrolysis of bacterial lipases occurs optimally between the temperature range of 30-60°C. However, some bacterial lipases have been noted to operate toward both the lower and higher sides of that range. For example, Bacillus sp. has been shown to be active even after 150 min of incubation at 70°C, clearly demonstrating increased thermostability when stabilized with ethylene glycol, sorbitol, and glycerol. Some Pseudomonas lipases have been reported to resist activities at about 100°C. However, their half-life at this temperature is usually very low. One extremely heat-resistant lipase from B. stearothermophilus had a half-life of 15-25 minutes at 100°C. Lipase production can be impacted by the growth temperature, which usually averages at around 37°C. Interestingly, some studies have shown that no lipase activity above 50°C is recorded. Furthermore, Pseudomonas xinjiangensis has been known to exhibit the highest lipase production at 37°C (Chow et al., 2021).

Divalent Metal Ions

Metal ions are capable of acting as electrophiles via the donation or acceptance of electrons. They form coordinate bonds with enzymes or even substrates. Moreover, metal ions can also orient the reacting groups that are needed in three dimensions and keep them in a catalytically active conformation. The enzyme—calcium ion complex is expected to have greater rigidity and stability supported by research about the influence of different metallic ions on lipase activity that observed an increase in thermostability due to calcium ions. Standardization of Process Parameters for Maximizing Lipase Production

The removal of the stepwise processes that occur in adipose tissues from various sources is significant since it enhances churned output and responsiveness within an industrial system. More generally, under the supervision of Palmer and his associates, one of the ways by which lipolytic enzymes were captured by modifying the cultivation conditions is done for almost all microbes. The optimization focus is put on modifying a variety of factors such as pH, carbon, temperature, composition of the media, duration of incubation, ions, concentration of several substrates, and specific nutrients. In addition, some simple designs of experiments such as Plackett-Burman designs and RSM, plus one-factor-at-a-time (OFAT) designs, are done to evaluate enzyme production based on predetermined values of a number of operational factors. Here, the conditions under which maximum quantities of lipase enzymes can be produced at minimum cost and with the greatest efficiency of the fermentation process can be determined. That lipase of industrial grade is made more readily available is driven primarily by the need to improve enzyme yield and these techniques of optimization are just as important as any of the other engine part assembling works (Kumar et al., 2022).

As a survival strategy for microbes, the excretion of metabolic by-products presents a significant advantage that can be applied in various scenarios. As such, manipulating culture media to maximize enzyme production should be feasible. Lipase-producing microorganisms are influenced by, and in turn influence, culture conditions impacting both their growth and lipase output. Medium optimization is the bottleneck issue in fermentation technology application development. In some experiments, the Plackett-Burman design was used and the response achieved was the highest lipolytic activity. Lipase production was optimally determined by the following medium composition: glucose 0.1%, olive oil 3.0%, NH4Cl 0.5%, yeast extract 0.36%, K2HPO4 0.1%, MgCl2 0.01%, and CaCl2 0.4 mM. Enhancing lipase production from the Burkholderia multivorans V2 strain was achieved by 2.2-fold through statistical optimization by Cesário et al. (2021). A sequential optimization strategy based on statistical design of experiments, consisting of OFAT, was employed in order to enhance lipase production from Candida cylindracea ATCC 14830, which was cultivated on palm oil mill effluent as a basal medium in shake flask cultures. This medium was the most optimal and for the lipase production increased to 20.26 U/ml, 5.19 times when compared with the unoptimized medium. Stenotrophomonas maltophilia, a newly isolated bacterium, offered a solution for achieving improved production of lipase by creatively employing a statistical design methodology for making an extracellular lipase.

Every aspect was pre-described to be optimized via the Box-Behnken approach, which resulted in a cumulative rise in lipase productivity from 500 U/ml to 4559 U/ml, which indicates an increase by 9.1 for the lipase productivity in the optimum levels of effective factors (Ikhwani et al., 2024).

In an effort to optimize the medium for the yeast T. asahii, several statistical methods have been applied, particularly focusing on one variable at a time and applying a Plackett-Burman design to determine the main factors affecting lipase production. The Plackett-Burman design, which consists of several independent variables, was used to study the effect of glucose concentration, olive oil, yeast extract, malt extract, MgCl2, and CaCl2 on the fermentation medium. It is useful for simultaneously screening several factors to evaluate their effect on enzyme production (Nadaf et al., 2023; Shana et al., 2023). At the same time, the composition of the fermentation medium for lipase production from R. chinensis was improved, and the best composition was obtained in the form of peptone, olive oil, maltose, K2HPO4, and MgSO4·7H2O.

This underscores the significance of the combination of certain nutrients for improving lipase activity, which is essential for many biotechnological uses (Elhussieny et al., 2023; Khmaissa et al., 2021). In addition, the Plackett–Burman approach has previously been used to screen components of the fermentation medium for extracellular lipase production from A. carneus and was shown to considerably increase production under these specific conditions: sunflower oil, glucose, peptone, agitation, and extended incubation (Ashraf et al., 2024). He and Tan (2006) applied RSM to select the appropriate culture medium for lipase production by Candida sp. 99-125.

The use of OVAT in optimizing culture conditions for lipase production results in greater yields of lipases by an additional increase in the amount of each constituent added. The combination of several physico-chemical factors such as lipase assay, incubation time, temperature, pH, N, and C sources, metal salts, and other substrates were changed and activities of lipases were determined every 24 hr for 96 hr. After 48 hr is when maximum lipase activity was registered (Aktar and Saha, 2023; Alabdalall et al., 2020).

Extraction and Purification of Microbial Lipase

Industries such as pharmaceuticals, clinical practice, and biotechnology have a persistent need for purified lipase with high specificity for therapeutic and diagnostic purposes. The dedication to the purification processes rests in making certain that no extraneous particles or proteins obstruct the enzymatic activity. Several techniques including ammonium sulfate precipitation, solvent extraction, dialysis, and various forms of chromatography such as ion-exchange, affinity, and gel filtration chromatography are used for lipase purification. All these techniques take advantage of the particular properties of lipase, including the lipase's molecular size, electric charge, solubility, and affinity toward the substrates in complex mixtures. There are multiple indicators that determine the method of choice in the purification process such as the purity level of the samples, the enzyme activity, and even the cost for making bulk production (Ali et al., 2023; Mazhar et al., 2023).

The growing use of lipase enzymes across different industries is remarkable, especially considering their cost-effective and powerful approach to catalyzing lipid hydrolysis. From an industrial perspective, lipases are often utilized in their native form due to the lack of need for enzyme purification, however, in pharmaceutical and clinical contexts, a greater level of purity is needed. This requires multiple rounds of separation and purification, which usually consist of removing the fermentation media and then applying ammonium sulfate precipitation, cooled acetone extraction, or other organic solvent extraction methods (Yassin et al., 2023). Many proteins, including lipases, can be extracted and separated from these complex mixtures because of certain identifiable physical and chemical characteristics. For example, the varying degrees of protein solubility can be taken advantage of when using ammonium sulfate precipitation for selective precipitation of the desired enzyme while the other proteins remain in the supernatant (Contesini et al., 2020).

Salting Out of Proteins/Enzymes

Single-protein precipitation is one of the most common approaches employed in the fields of biochemistry and biotechnology, utilizing processes like changes in pH, temperature, or even salt and organic solvent addition. Of such methods, the salting-out is probably the best known with ammonium sulfate being one of the most frequently used precipitating salts. The salting-out effect is the phenomenon where greater concentrations of salt are shown to decrease the solubility of certain proteins. This is due to the fighting between protein molecules, ions of salt, and water molecules where the added salt concentration leads to the erosion of the hydration sphere encapsulating the proteins and thus aiding in the aggregation and sedimentation of proteins (Krainer et al., 2021; Goktayoglu et al., 2023).

Protein precipitation with ammonium sulfate is a commonly used method for the purification of proteins in biochemistry. Ammonium sulfate addition leads to the reduction of the solvation layer around proteins, allowing their precipitation. It is a preferred method because ammonium sulfate is highly soluble, and unlike other salts, it does not lead to protein denaturation or bacterial contamination at saturation levels, which are around 3.9 M at 0∘C, and 4.04 M at 20∘C (Shayesteh et al., 2021). Different proteins have specific salt concentration requirements for precipitation, thus enabling the selective precipitation and subsequent dissolution of the protein having different solubilities (Ahn et al., 2021). To avoid the protein backbone from bending out, the solution requires careful stirring while introducing ammonium sulfate. Each portion of ammonium sulfate must be allowed to dissolve fully before adding the next portion to avoid undissolved ammonium sulfate.

This systematic method is useful for focusing on the solutions of proteins with low activities that may be present in certain steps of protein purification (Saginova et al., 2021). As an example, Putranto et al. (2021) argue that studies show ammonium sulfate precipitation, which is a method to remove non-protein contaminants, is the first step of loading untagged recombinant proteins into a chromatography column, successfully capturing and separating the intended proteins from contaminants and intact (Putranto et al., 2021).

In-Silico Quantification of Lipase Enzymes

In silico tools are increasingly used for the quantitation and analysis of lipases, offering computational approaches to predict enzyme properties, activity, and structure-function relationships. These tools are crucial in enzyme engineering, drug design and industrial biotechnology. Here are some key in silico tools and methods used for lipase quantitation. GROMACS, AMBER, CHARMM tools predict the stability, flexibility, and conformational changes of lipase enzymes at the molecular level, which can affect their activity and substrate specificity (Lu et al., 2024). SWISS-MODEL, Phyre2, and I-TASSER tools predicts the 3D structure of lipases, helping in understanding active sites and substrate-binding pockets for better quantification of enzyme activity. COPASI, BioSim, MATLAB SimBiology tools used in model enzyme kinetics to quantify reaction rates, substrate affinity (Km) and catalytic efficiency (kcat) in lipase-catalyzed reactions. BLAST, Clustal Omega, MEGA tools analyze lipase gene sequences for evolutionary relationships, conserved motifs, and potential active site regions, which indirectly inform activity quantification. Primer3, Oligo Analyzer designs primers for qPCR to quantify lipase gene expression levels, providing insights into enzyme production levels in different conditions. R, Python (SciPy, NumPy), and GraphPad Prism are used to analyze experimental data from enzyme assays, fitting Michaelis-Menten kinetics and calculating enzyme activity parameters. TensorFlow, Scikit-learn, DeepMind AlphaFold predict lipase enzyme behavior, including activity under different conditions, using large datasets from proteomics and genomics (Kelley et al., 2015).

Differences between Conventional and Modern Techniques

Lipase enzyme production has evolved significantly over the years, transitioning from conventional methods rooted in natural processes to modern techniques leveraging advanced biotechnology. Conventional techniques for lipase production primarily rely on natural sources such as microorganisms (Bacillus subtilis, Candida rugosa) or animal tissues, with cultivation occurring in basic batch, fed-batch, or continuous fermentation systems. These methods often involve simple bioreactors, and the purification process is typically limited to precipitation, centrifugation, and basic chromatographic techniques (Abdelaziz et al., 2025). The process is straightforward, cost-effective, and well-established, making it suitable for small to medium-scale production. However, the yields are generally moderate, and the enzyme's stability, activity, and specificity can be inconsistent. Additionally, the lack of advanced control over environmental parameters like pH, temperature, and dissolved oxygen levels often results in variability in enzyme quality.

In contrast, modern techniques in lipase production utilize cutting-edge biotechnology tools, including genetic engineering and recombinant DNA technology, to enhance enzyme yield and performance. These methods employ genetically modified microorganisms such as Pichia pastoris and Escherichia coli, which are engineered to overexpress lipase genes, thereby significantly increasing enzyme production rates (Majumder et al., 2024). The cultivation process is carried out in sophisticated bioreactors equipped with precise control systems that regulate key parameters like pH, temperature, nutrient concentration, and oxygen levels, optimizing enzyme expression. Moreover, modern purification methods involve advanced chromatographic techniques such as ion exchange, affinity chromatography, and membrane filtration, ensuring high purity and specific activity of the lipase enzymes. These techniques are highly scalable, making them ideal for industrial applications where large volumes of enzyme are required (Ali et al., 2023). The advantages of modern techniques are clear, as they offer higher enzyme yields, faster production cycles, and the ability to tailor enzyme properties for specific industrial applications, such as enhanced thermostability, pH tolerance, and substrate specificity. This has led to the widespread use of lipases in diverse industries, including food processing, pharmaceuticals, biodiesel production, and detergents. However, the implementation of modern techniques comes with challenges, including high initial investment costs, the need for specialized equipment, and regulatory concerns, especially regarding the use of genetically modified organisms.

While conventional methods are cost-effective and simple, they lack the efficiency and precision offered by modern techniques. They are often limited by the natural variability of microbial strains and environmental conditions, which can affect both the yield and quality of the lipase produced. Furthermore, conventional methods are not as adaptable to large-scale industrial demands, where consistency and high productivity are crucial. On the other hand, modern techniques provide the ability to manipulate microbial strains at the genetic level, leading to robust strains with optimized metabolic pathways for maximum lipase production. This level of control over the production process significantly reduces batch-to-batch variability, ensuring consistent product quality (Sajid et al., 2023).

Another key distinction lies in the sustainability aspect. Modern techniques, despite their higher energy and resource demands, can be optimized to reduce waste, improve resource efficiency, and enhance the overall environmental footprint of enzyme production. The use of renewable feedstocks, waste valorization and bioprocess intensification strategies are becoming increasingly common in modern lipase production systems. In contrast, conventional methods often rely on traditional fermentation substrates, which may not be as sustainable in the long term (Mahfoudhi et al., 2022). Conventional techniques for lipase production offer simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and ease of implementation, they fall short in terms of yield, scalability, and enzyme quality. Modern techniques, with their advanced genetic engineering approaches and sophisticated bioprocess controls, revolutionize lipase production by enabling high yields, customized enzyme properties, and industrial scalability. However, they require significant investment and expertise. The choice between conventional and modern techniques largely depends on the specific industrial requirements, economic considerations, and desired enzyme characteristics.

Food and Dairy Industry

According to Mehta and associates in 2021, lipases are essential to flavor development in processed dairy and meat products as well as in vegetables, fruits, baked goods, milk, and beer. Because of the extensive use of lipases in the food sector, many products now have improved flavors as well as better overall quality. For example, Silva and colleagues in 2022 noted that lipases facilitate flavoring by hydrolysis of triglycerides, leucine, and valine in the case of dairy and processed meat products. Foods are further enhanced in flavor due to their ability to hydrolyze fats into free fatty acids and glycerol. Different from the previous case, in the dairy sector, lipases are used to cleave ester bonds in triglyceride lipids for the long-chained fatty acids which add flavors and tastes to cheeses, promotes ripening of cheeses, and contributes to the lipolysis of cream and cheese. Lipase has been studied from microbial sources such as Mucor miehei and Aspergillus niger or oryzae as potential strains for use in the cheese-making industry. They were also used for improving food flavors though the enzymatic reaction of fatty acids, known as esterification (Reyes-Reyes et al., 2022).

As noted by Warra and Prasad (2024), flavoring of cells can occur through the active form of lipases outside a cell via inter or transesterification processes to produce more nutritionally suited forms for parental feeding. Food lipases have also been used in the modification of food taste by adding esters of short-chain fatty acids and alcohols, which are recognized flavoring and fragrant compounds (Ye et al., 2024).

Detergent Industry

Lipases are additives in detergents (laundry and dishwasher detergents). Lipases are used in conjunction with other addenda such as cellulase and proteases in the making of detergents. Lipases cleave the greasy stains and aid in solubilization cleansing as they change the stains into more hydrophilic parts, which can be easily removed than non-hydrolyzed stains (Sales et al., 2021). The lipases’ mechanism of action in the fabrication of detergents is due to their pH and temperature tolerance, which allows their use in a wide variety of washing conditions (Bredai et al., 2021; Safdar et al., 2023). Additionally, they need a wide variety of substrate specificity for hydrolyzing fats with different compositions (Xin et al., 2023). Apart from its application in washing, lipases are employed in dishwashing, cleansing contact lenses, degrading organic solid wastes in the surfaces of exhaust pipes and toilet bowls, and so on (Ardicli et al., 2024).

Leather Industry

In the leather sector, the use of lipase has served as a fat removal step, which has an added value over the conventional processes. Khambhaty and others have argued that the emission of damaging surfactants and organic solvents organic compounds is very detrimental to the environment and supports a sustainable leather industry. However, the development of lipospheric processes for fat utilization in leather processing not only solves the problem of efficient fat removal, but also meets the needs of the industry's effort to be sustainable. Due to their capability to perform under soft conditions and reduce the negative effects of traditional methods, microbial lipases are noted for their versatility and effectiveness in the industrial sector, including leather processing (Kumar, 2023).

Pharmaceutical Industry

In modern pharmaceuticals, specifically drug development, lipases help in obtaining enantiomeric medicine through a biopharmaceutical process called lipase-catalyzed processes esterification. They are important in the imposition of diltiazem, some anti-inflammatory, and some cholesterol-lowering drugs on the molecules of the compounds obtained by lipases catalyzed processes (Kumar et al., 2023).

Lipases have other applications in pharmaceuticals, such as the transesterification and the hydrolysis of fats and oils. A wide variety of microorganisms such as bacteria, yeasts, molds and even protozoa have been discovered that can secrete lipases and utilize lipid material as a source of energy and food. These microorganisms have also been found to be able to exist in extreme environmental conditions. Serratia marcescens has been considered as a candidate organism for lovastatin production, which employs lipase-catalyzed asymmetric hydrolysis of 3-phenylglycidic acid 3-ester in one of the steps in diltiazem hydrochloride synthesis (Najjar et al., 2021). This is called diltiazene hydrochloride. The broad scope of lipases makes them capable of functioning in a wide range of reactions ranging from hydrolysis and esterification to transesterification, which is useful in the pharmaceutical industry as well as in the production of biodiesel.

Pulp and Paper Industry

Lipases are crucial in the pulp and paper industry to control pitch and improve product quality. Lipases neutralize pitch operational problems by hydrolyzing triglycerides into glycerol and fatty acids. In addition, they are of great significance in pulp recycling by removing adhesive and ink contaminants, resulting in high quality paper without harming the environment. The use of lipases in this sector is of great importance because they address the problems associated with pitch deposits that negatively impact the quality of paper products and the productivity of the mills. Studies show that lipases improve the quality of paper, environmental protection by the breakdown of contaminants facilitated during lipases action (Jevtović et al., 2023).

Lipids, acids, waxes, and wood triacylglycerols combine to create a resinous substance known as pitch, which is highly hydrophobic. Pitch causes major concerns for the paper and pulp industries. Among other things, pitch contributes to difficulties in spot formation during the manufacturing of paper, construction equipment fouling, machinery overheating, and many operational challenges. Along with pulp treatment, lipase enzymes pose as effective tools for the environmentally friendly management of pitch. These enzymes undergo hydrolysis which cleaves the lipids esters’ bonds, turning triacylglycerol into glycerol and free fatty acids. Through this mechanism of hydrolysis, the components within the pulp that drive pitch formation undergo chemical degradation. This mechanism hinders the agglomeration of pitch particles through lipids, allowing for the mitigation of pitch buildup on machinery and optimization of the entire system (Chandra et al., 2020). The secretion of lipase leads to a more efficient capture of objectionable matter, achieving a better working environment without the use of chemicals and further attaining sustainability, which the industry is trying to adopt more now (Kvasha et al., 2023). In addition, lipases can work under different conditions, which helps in the rapid processing of pulp and makes these enzymes more adaptable for use in the pulp and paper industry (Kumar et al., 2023).

The application of lipases in treatment processes is more advantageous when compared to the previous approaches that utilized chemicals. With the use of these complex chemicals, processes require higher temperatures and are more energy-consuming. Additionally, these procedures lipases possess selective action, meaning they can remove lipid-based pollutants that are cellulosic fiber and essential pulp components and leave the cellulose fibers as well as important pulp parts undamaged. Moreover, this kind of treatment gives rise to a superior quality of paper by reinforcing the final paper product in comparison to stickies, spots, and rough surfaces which lower the printability and aesthetic features of the paper (Pereira et al., 2022). Besides their remarkable capability to modulate pitch, lipase enzymes can also be applied on the treatment of recycled pulp because stickies assist in lipid contamination is a troublesome factor for deinking and repulping. The use of lipases in the deinking step of the procedure has been thoroughly researched and for the most part easier to use because the breakdown of stickies in lipids for recycled paper products is established.

There are studies that show that lipases can aid in the removal of ink and foreign particles from paper fibers, making the recycled pulp more useful. Not to mention, more so in this study, the method of enzyme deinking is often more sustainable in comparison because it uses less chemicals than is required for traditional methods (Sežun et al., 2020). Aggressive lipases that destroy lipid contaminants guarantee that the recycled pulp obtained is good in quality and is suitable for paper making. All in all, the use of lipase enzymes in the pulp and paper industry has increased the production efficiency and quality of the product, while at the same time taking an environmentally friendly measure by cutting down on chemicals and harming the environment.

Environmental Applications: Bioremediation and Wastewater Treatment

Lipases focus on the elimination of pollutants by integrating bioremediation, wastewater treatment, and plastic disposal. Their functions have expanded to include the cleaning of industrial wastewater as well as the treatment of hydrocarbon contaminated regions (Kumar et al., 2023). In bioremediation, lipases are of great importance due to their ability to break down various lipid pollutants, including oils, fats, and grease within soil and water ecosystems. The byproducts of factory wastes, oil spills, and sewage are highly detrimental to the environment as they contain an overwhelming amount of oxygen that is impossible for the ecosystem to handle. That group of lipases which are responsible for the conversion of pollutants that do not mix with water into simple, friendly fatty acids and gylcerol, which are less complex, are more easily broken down. Such modification of the enzyme renders the breakdown of these substances simpler, and so it renders bioremediation more effective and faster in cleansing the contaminated environment (Tedesco et al., 2024). Lipases break down and digest Fat, Oil, and Grease (FOG), which is one of the leading contaminators in industrial and domestic wastewater.

The build-up of FOGs can result in severe obstruction of pipes, decrease the effectiveness of the treatment processes, as well as produce harmful toxins via anaerobic degradation. By employing lipolytic microorganisms, these lipid pollutants are eliminated by being changed into metabolites that are transformable or removable in the water of the treatment systems’ microbial consortium. Moreover, lipases enhance the reduction of waterborne pollutants, COD and BOD. This makes it possible to release less contaminated effluents after the wastewater treatments to water bodies (Baena et al., 2022). Additionally, polycaprolactone (PCL) and polyhydroxyalkanoates (PHA) are polyesters that undergo hydrolysis and are susceptible to lipases’ biodegradation. Even though these alternatives to conventional plastics have a function, they are not without environmental issues. Lipases cleave the environmentally benign biodegradable polymers by bonds of esters to form monomers that can be utilized by microorganisms. Such plastic enzymatic degradation is part of a broader solution to effective waste management and increased ecological efficiency. It mitigates the severe environmental impacts caused by growing plastic waste.

Bioenergy production: Bioethanol and Biodiesel

The rising demand for energy is placing considerable strain on fossil fuel reserves; thus, new sources of energy need to be developed from renewable resources. Bioenergy is a viable replacement for fossil fuels that can help cut pollution and therefore promote the use of clean and green technologies. The use of lipolytic enzymes like lipases greatly contributes toward solving the critical issue of lipid waste while also addressing energy security needs through the estimated substitution of fossil fuels.

Harnessing agricultural residues and forestry lignocellulosic biomass is particularly appealing because its carbon balance allowing for a sustainable energy cycle cuts across the carbon emissions produced by fossil fuel combustion (Qamar et al. 2024). The major sources of bioenergy include agricultural residues and microalgae, which have been noted due to their high per-unit lipid production and fast growth rates. This bioenergy shift is vital because it contributes to increased greenhouse gas emission mitigation and decreased reliance on fossil fuels (Olabi et al., 2023). The transition toward bioenergy contributes not only to environmental sustainability but to economic development with new jobs in the renewable energy industry and improved energy independence (Chen 2024; Nkordeh et al., 2023).

The information provided is well-known and formed on the base of theories and works set in the years 2023 and 2024, claim Olabi, Nkordeh, Chen, and Xia. The activities they refer to intend to receive and utilize energy sustainably. In addition, they foresee a more profound employment growth effect due to lifts in productivity associated with improved skills and infrastructure. It is well explained that there is a necessity to consider biodiesel production. It is created with the aid of microorganisms as well as certain substrates to foster greater bioethanol and biodiesel yields. Lipases can work at milder conditions than other enzymes, making them ideal for sustainable biodiesel. Increasing methanol concentration increases the reaction's rate, thus improving productivity and the biodiesel's overall yield. The aforementioned is evidence that biodiesel lipases are vital. For this reason, cooking oils and animal fat can easily be turned into biodiesel without any extensive preparations. Amobonye from 2021 argues that specific methods and materials have advanced vastly beyond traditional engineering approaches and techniques.

Plastic Biodegradation

The problem of plastic pollution has become dramatically more serious, and there are many research efforts into solutions. One of the approaches includes the application of lipases in the biodegradation processes of PCL and PHA. These lipases are produced by a wide range of microorganisms, and they can catalyze the hydrolysis of ester bonds in these biodegradable polymers, in effect, breaking the polymers down into less harmful constituents. Some researchers have suggested that enzyme-based biotechnological solutions could be focused not only on polyethylene terephthalate, but also on polyether plastics which are easier to degrade by microbes, such as PCL and PHA (Gambarini et al., 2021; Orlando et al., 2023).

The application of lipases in biodiesel production is reported to be more environmentally sustainable. Further efforts should be directed toward engineering lipases that can withstand extreme conditions, such as high concentrations of methanol, which would increase the overall efficiency of the process (lipase biodegradation of plastics). Currently, there is some attention paid to lipases for their potential application in solving plastic pollution. It has been shown that lipases can hydrolyze PHA, polycaprolactone, PCL, so there is a hope that they can be engineered into other synthetic polymers to be more environmentally friendly (Rahmati et al., 2024).

There are still a host of issues that require additional attention, including overcoming language barrier, improving the kinetics and stability of the generated enzymes, and achieving economic feasibility. However, the advances that have been made in the fields of protein engineering, immobilization, and bioinformatics have definitely put the innovation of lipases on the map (Contesini et al., 2020). It is undeniable that in the future, more sophisticated lipases are possible, but these innovations need to be fully utilized for the advancement of diverse fields including biodiesel production, environmental protection, pharmaceuticals, and the food industry. It is also recommended to focus on the design of lipases that can be incorporated into industrial processes in a way that promotes sustainability and eco-friendliness.

Challenges Their Possible Solutions

Lipases are multifunctional enzymes that are used in making biodiesels, pharmaceuticals, food, and even help in environmental management. Even with a broad range of applications, there are existing challenges regarding the cost of production, stability under industrial conditions, and specific substrates required—most of which prevent these enzymes from being used at a large scale. These problems further aggravate the issues of enzyme recovery and reusability in enzymes that are required for continuous processes. However, immobilizing lipases has been shown to increase their stability and reusability, somewhat accomplishing these objectives. For example, lipases that were immobilized on hierarchical mesoporous silica retained 55% activity recovery after 10 operational cycles, which is significantly higher than other reports with different methods of lipase immobilization (Dong et al., 2024).

Bulk Production At Industrial Level

One of the major problems in industrial lipases production is the economy of scale in operations, which often leads to high production costs compared with the limited activity of the enzyme. The lipase microbial production is made expensive in general because of costly growth media, optimal fermentation conditions, and excessively complicated downstream processing. The scale up of laboratory bioprocesses to the industrial level is also from enzymes and many parameters like oxygen transfer, shear force, and pH have no optimization in large bioreactors (Ali et al., 2023a).

Enzyme Stability and Durability

More greatly inhibiting than this is a limitation, super critically unstable enzymatic reactions in practical directly stressful conditions of high temperature, combinations of extreme pH level stressing mechanical environment, and extreme solvents. Most native lipase enzymes are inactivated by many of these conditions, which mostly restrain their use to biodiesel fuels, detergents, organic synthesis, and other processes uses of them are rather more important, dramatically rise in non-aqueous systems solvent sensitive to the overwhelming majority of industrial processes (Hamdan et al., 2021).

Cost-Effectiveness of Production

The use of lipases in the industry is limited as a result of high production lipases, purification, and stabilization costs. Enzymes activation maintenance, together with the urine storage and urine transport processes are equally expensive (Ali et al. 2023b). Consequently, the potential for industries to adopt enzymatic processes instead of chemical ones is associated with developing affordable and efficient production systems.

Protein Engineering

Protein engineering, especially in the case of enzymes such as lipases, has greatly improved their performance parameters because modification of stability, specificity, and catalytic activity is achieved. Techniques like site-directed mutagenesis, directed evolution, and in silico design are vital in the modification process of lipases for specific industrial needs (Plikat et al., 2022; Vasilescu et al., 2022). For example, site-directed mutagenesis can be utilized to design enzymes with increased tolerance to organic solvents or greater temperature elevations, thus substantially increasing their use in different industrial processes. Rational design is a method that uses structural information to make specific modifications to achieve required functions (Liu et al., 2023; Tiku et al., 2024). Additionally, the engineering of lipases also depends on computational design. With the use of advanced models, researchers can design particular mutations that best improve enzyme stability and activity. As a result, lipases with improved hydrolysis activity to different substrates have been developed successfully, proving that computational techniques are useful in enzyme engineering (Verma et al. 2023). The application of such techniques not only improves the functional properties of lipases, but also meets the increasing needs of the industries, such as in biodiesel production and biocatalysis in organic synthesis (Li et al., 2023).

Immobilization Technologies

Technologies of immobilization improve the stability and reusability of critical lipases in diverse industries. This is accomplished by covalently bonding the enzyme to solid supports such as silica, chitosan, or polymers, or by encapsulating them in gels to improve their performance under environmental stresses. This form of stabilization permits the efficient re-use of lipases in industrial processes, significantly improving their effectiveness economically (Li et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2022; Zou et al., 2022). They are even more useful than free lipases in continuous systems, including bioreactors for biodiesel production and waste treatment plants. The cross-linking of lipases with glutaraldehyde, for instance, forms stable enzyme aggregates, which have retained activity under severe operational conditions. For example, research suggests that the CLEA format of enzymes demonstrates better thermostability and recovery of activity, which makes them amenable for multiple applications (Khiari et al., 2023). In addition, better immobilization of the CLEAs increases the emulsion interfacial area, which leads to better contact of the substrate and consequently increases the catalytic activity (Li et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2022). Modern modification processes, such as the inclusion of novel cross-linking agents and magnetic nanoparticles, have further improved lipases’ performance. These modifications increase the enzyme's operational stability and simplify the separation and recovery from the reaction mixtures, which is critical for effective industrial application (Yan et al., 2021; Zou et al., 2022). These strategies of enzyme immobilization adopting multi-cycling the immobilized enzyme have been proven to be economically feasible for industrial use (Song et al., 2023).

Bioinformatics and Computational Tools

The incorporation of bioinformatics, computational tools, and artificial intelligence has greatly impacted enzyme R & D. The current techniques in the field, including molecular dynamics simulation, docking studies, and structural biology, have further advanced toward the understanding of enzyme-substrate interactions to rationally engineer lipases with particular properties. The docking studies help scientists estimate the action of lipases on substrates, which is vital for enzyme modification to industrial needs (Li et al., 2023). Along with genome sequencing, metagenomic approaches have increased the scope of lipases bling novel enzyme sources from new environments (Hossack et al., 2023). This phenomenon is very significant for increasing the number of enzymes for use in different industries. As well as machine learning and artificial intelligence greatly contribute to the optimization of production processes and enzyme compositions.

These technologies can collect, analyze, and find data across multiple datasets to determine advantages for enzyme efficiency and stability prediction. Such as the application of AI in biocatalysis, engineering enzymes with enhanced catalytic activities has been achieved using deep learning algorithms, showcasing the capabilities of deep AI in biocatalysis (Jiang & Yi, 2024). Moreover, the use of AI in predicting other enzyme parameters aids in the determination of favorable conditions for the enzyme activity, which is essential for industrial needs. The integration of these particular algorithms greatly simplifies the design of the enzyme and both the time and resource conservation needed for development are nullified. Future studies regarding lipases should be oriented toward solutions to the problems of their large-scale production, stability, and cost, in order to broaden their industrial applications.

The application of lipases on an industrial scale often runs into problems like low yields and expenditures that focus on the purification and immobilization aspects (Maghraby et al., 2023). Modern techniques in enzyme engineering, especially directed evolution and rational design, are beneficial for increasing lipase's stability, specificity, and activity in the presence of extreme conditions such as high temperature, pH changes, and organic solvents (Hlima et al., 2021). The application of bioinformatics, machine learning, and artificial intelligence makes it possible to develop lipases customized to the specific requirements of industry, thus increasing the speed of their development and application (Saxena et al., 2024). Immobilization methods that make use of nano-supports and hybrid materials can help increase enzyme recovery and reusability of the lipases in continuous processes (Tan et al., 2023). Other than that, these methods allow lipases to be used multiple times, which makes them more economical for industrial processes (Paul et al., 2022). In addition, new microbial sources and genetic engineering techniques can produce lipases suitable for newer applications, including biogenesis fuels, pharmaceutical products, and bioremediation agents (Hlima et al., 2021). Bioinformatics and machine learning tools will further facilitate the engineering and screening of these enzymes, making them efficient and effective for specific industry needs (Saxena et al., 2024). Creating and optimizing the use of lipases for plastic biodegradation and creating sustainable processes, such as eco-friendly pitch control in the pulp and paper industry, should be further studied.

Razib et al. (2020) claim that lipases are active enzymes which can facilitate the breakdown of complex substrates. This skill can be utilized for environmental purposes, which will aid in achieving industrial and ecological sustainability. Further work in these areas will help broaden the industrial use of lipases and foster sustainability alongside environmentally friendly approaches.

Role of Lipase Enzymes in the Advancement of Sustainable Development Goals

Lipase enzymes play a significant role in advancing several Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) set by the United Nations, due to their versatility in industrial, environmental, and health-related applications (United Nation, 2000). There are following specific SDGs which lipase enzymes fulfill:

SDG 9: Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure

Lipases are crucial in developing sustainable industrial processes. Their use in the production of biofuels (like biodiesel) from renewable sources reduces dependence on fossil fuels. For example, lipase-catalyzed transesterification is used to convert waste oils into biodiesel, supporting cleaner and green energy production with lower carbon emissions.

SDG 12: Responsible Consumption and Production

Lipases contribute to green chemistry and sustainable manufacturing processes. They are used in the production of biodegradable detergents, reducing environmental pollution compared to conventional chemical-based detergents. For instance, in the detergent industry, lipase enzymes break down fats and oils, enabling effective cleaning with less environmental impact.

SDG 13: Climate Action

By promoting the use of bio-based products and reducing the need for harmful chemicals, lipases help lower greenhouse gas emissions. In bioremediation, lipase enzymes degrade oil spills and other environmental pollutants, aiding in the cleanup of contaminated ecosystems, thus supporting climate resilience (Dodds, 2015).

SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being

Lipases play a key role in the pharmaceutical industry for synthesizing active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) through enzyme-catalyzed reaction processes. They are also used in diagnostic kits for detecting lipid-related disorders, contributing to improved healthcare outcomes.

SDG 6: Clean Water and Sanitation

In wastewater treatment, lipase enzymes break down lipid pollutants, helping to treat industrial effluents more efficiently. This supports cleaner water systems, particularly in regions where water pollution is a critical issue.

SDG 15: Life on Land and Below Water

Lipases aid in bioremediation of marine oil spills, reducing the impact of environmental disasters on marine ecosystems. This helps protect biodiversity and supports the restoration of natural habitats affected by pollution (United Nation, 2015). Lipase enzymes are more than just biological catalysts; they are powerful tools for achieving global sustainability goals. Their applications span across energy, environmental protection, healthcare, and sustainable industrial practices, making them integral to a greener, healthier, and more sustainable future.

Future Perspectives

The future of lipase research holds immense potential, driven by advancements in biotechnology, genetic engineering, and industrial applications. As the demand for sustainable and efficient biocatalysts grows across industries such as biofuels, pharmaceuticals, food processing, and environmental remediation, lipase enzymes are emerging as key players due to their versatility, stability, and ability to catalyze a wide range of reactions. Future research will likely focus on enhancing the thermostability, pH tolerance, and substrate specificity of lipases through protein engineering and directed evolution techniques. Additionally, the integration of synthetic biology and metabolic engineering will enable the development of microbial strains with optimized metabolic pathways for higher lipase yields and cost-effective production processes. The exploration of extremophilic microorganisms from harsh environments, such as deep-sea vents and alkaline lakes, is expected to uncover novel lipase variants with unique properties suitable for specialized industrial applications. Moreover, the growing interest in green chemistry and sustainable bioprocesses will drive research toward the use of renewable feedstocks, waste valorization, and eco-friendly production methods. As researchers continue to unlock the full potential of lipases, the enzyme's role will expand beyond traditional applications to include emerging fields like bioremediation of pollutants, bioplastics production, and personalized medicine, solidifying its status as a cornerstone of future biotechnological innovations.

Conclusion

Lipases are critical and efficient biocatalysts for large, fast-growing industries, as well as for environmental sectors, because they can catalyze lipids breakdown and synthesis under moderate conditions. The significant applications of lipases span numerous industries, including food and beverages, pharmaceuticals, biofuels, detergents, and environmental management. Their ability to catalyze esterification, transesterification, and hydrolysis reactions under mild conditions makes them invaluable in green chemistry and sustainable production practices. As research continues to evolve, the development of genetically modified strains and the exploration of novel microbial sources promise to further expand the utility of lipases.

However, their inline mass production, enzyme's industrial stability, and economical effectiveness for complete utility pose problems toward the overall commercialization. Alternative methods in protein engineering, techniques in enzyme immobilization, and other bioinformatics activities aimed at enzyme's production maximization and optimization require up-to date tools and methods for bioinformatics enzyme discovery will have to be created to resolve these problems. New strategies appear to provide an opportunity to enhance lipase catalytic activity, its thermal stability, and its reusability for commercial purposes in food processing, detergent manufacturing, biodiesel production and even in the synthesis of pharmaceuticals. This work is made to stress the importance of lipases toward solving some important environmental issues through bioremediation, wastewater treatment, and degrading plastic wastes. Enzymes, and in particular lipases, will pose more challenge to scientists and engineers in terms of studying them for their process improvement and economical mass production and these activities will demonstrate how beneficial lipases can be to every industry and how inexpensive they can be. In an effort to mitigate the threat posed by industrial waste and over-reliance on chemicals, lipases are considered central to increasing technological sustainability as a response to both environmental scarcity and global economic issues. So, lipase biochemistry is bound to advance in the direction of innovative, efficacious, and effective environmentally sustainable solutions. Ultimately, the ongoing integration of microbial lipases into industrial biotechnology not only boosts economic efficiency but also aligns with environmentally friendly and sustainable practices, marking a progressive step toward a bio-based economy.

355. PPARγ as a predictive biomarker for antidepressant response in major depressive disorder: insights from TNIP1 transcriptional regulation
Authors: Y-W Lin, Y-Y Hung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMajor depressive disorder (MDD) is a debilitating disease possibly linked to immune defense mechanisms. TNIP1, a key anti-inflammatory regulator in NF-κB and TLR pathways, is implicated in MDD, but its transcriptional regulation and role in treatment response are unclear.Aims & ObjectivesElucidating the transcriptional regulation of TNIP1 in depression.MethodWe analyzed mRNA expression of TNIP1 and 13 transcription factors in monocytes from pre- and post-treatment (4-6 weeks) MDD patients and healthy controls. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated, tagged by CD14, and mRNA was extracted and analyzed. Participants were recruited at Kaohsiung Chang Gung Memorial Hospital (2014–2025). ANCOVA, paired t-tests, and multiple linear regression adjusted for age, gender, BMI, and smoking were used to compare groups and predict treatment outcomes.ResultsThis study encompassed 62 MDD patients and 52 healthy controls, mean age was 43.24 ± 10.76 years, with males accounting for 23.3%. Pre-treatment HAMD-17 averaged 23.90 ± 4.60, and post-treatment HAMD-17 averaged 8.43 ± 4.24 (Table 1). Compared to controls, MDD patients had significantly higher PPARγ (F = 6.213, p < 0.001), higher FOS (F = 2.775, p = 0.023) and lower JUN (F = 2.631, p = 0.029) expression. After treatment, TNIP1 significantly increased (p = 0.031), without transcription factors changed (Figure 1A). Lower pre-treatment PPARγ was associated with greater depressive symptom improvement (p = 0.016) (Figure 1B-C).Discussion & ConclusionsThis study highlights the differential expression of PPAR-γ, FOS, and JUN in MDD patients, underscoring their potential roles in immune regulation. The association between lower pre-treatment PPAR-γ expression and improved treatment outcomes suggests its utility as a biomarker for predicting therapeutic response.

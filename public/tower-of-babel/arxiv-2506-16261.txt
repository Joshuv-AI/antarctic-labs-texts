Global well-posedness for 2D compressible radially symmetric Navier-Stokes equations with swirl
Xiangdi Huang, Weili Meng* a. State Key Laboratory of Mathematical Sciences, Academy of MathematicsSystems Science,Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, China;

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the radially symmetric compressible Navier-Stokes equations with swirl in two-dimensional disks, where the shear viscosity coefficient μ = const > 0 \mu=\text{const}>0 , and the bulk one λ = ρ β ​ ( β > 0 ) \lambda=\rho^{\beta}(\beta>0) . When β ≥ 1 \beta\geq 1 , we prove the global existence and asymptotic behavior of the large strong solutions for initial values that allow for vacuum. One of the key ingredients is to show the uniform boundedness of the density independent of the time. When β ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \beta\in(0,1) , we prove the same conclusion holds when the initial value satisfies ‖ ρ 0 ‖ L ∞ ≤ a 0 \left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq a_{0} , where a 0 a_{0} is given by ( 1.12 ) as in Theorem 1.2 . To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result on the global existence of large strong solutions for 2D compressible Navier-Stokes equation with real non-slip (non Navier-slip) boundary conditions when β ≥ 1 \beta\geq 1 and the first result on the global existence of strong solutions when β ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \beta\in(0,1) . Keywords: compressible Navier-Stokes equations; Dirichlet problem; global strong solutions; swirl. Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010): 35D35; 35Q30; 35Q35; 76N10.

\displaystyle\rho(r,0)=\rho_{0}(r),\quad u_{r}(r,0)=u_{r_{0}}(r),\quad u_{\theta}(r,0)=u_{\theta_{0}}(r),

Suppose that

\displaystyle\beta\geq 1,\quad\gamma>1,

\displaystyle-\mu\Delta\mathbf{u_{0}}-\nabla((\mu+\lambda(\rho_{0}))\div\mathbf{u_{0}})+\nabla P(\rho_{0})=\sqrt{\rho_{0}}g,

for some

1
)-(
1.8

\displaystyle\rho\in C([0,T];W^{1,q}),\quad\mathbf{u}\in C([0,T];H^{1}),

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{\gamma}}+\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}\leq C,\\

We now comment on the analysis of this paper. The previous literature
[
10
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
shows that the strong solutions of the system
(
1.1
)

(
1.3
)
blow up in finite time, typically due to the blow up of the

norm of the density. Therefore, obtaining the upper bound for the density is the key to obtaining global solutions. We are interested in understanding the role that swirl plays in preventing density blow-up within our model. Is it positive? The answer is yes. Rewriting the transport equation as

\displaystyle(\theta+\xi)_{t}+u_{r}\partial_{r}(\theta+\xi)+\int_{R}^{r}\frac{\rho}{s}(u_{r}^{2}-u_{\theta}^{2})ds+P-\bar{P}+G(R,t)=0,

(1.14)

where

\displaystyle\theta(\rho)=2\mu\log\rho+\frac{1}{\beta}\rho^{\beta},\quad\xi=\int_{R}^{r}\rho u_{r}ds,\quad G=(2\mu+\lambda)\div\mathbf{u}-(P-\bar{P}).

The sign-preserving property of
u_{\theta}^{2}
indicates that, in some sense, swirl and pressure are similar, as both can prevent finite-time density blow-up. In addition, we also need to estimate the other terms in
(
1.14
)
.

In order to derive the uniform upper bound of the density independent of time, we divide the proof into four cases.

1.

When
\beta>\max\left\{1,\frac{\gamma+2}{4}\right\}
, we adapt the method from
[
10
]
and
[
14
]
. First, by taking advantage of the special structure of equation
\eqref{Equ 2}_{2}
under radial symmetry, we provide the boundary representation of the effective viscous flux
G
(see Lemma
3.2
). Then, to estimate
\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}
, we prove that the growth of
\log(e+\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})
is at most
R_{T}^{1+\kappa+\varepsilon}
, where
R_{T}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+1
and
\kappa=\max\left\{0,\gamma-2\beta,\beta-\gamma-2\right\}
(see Proposition
3.1
). At this point, the lack of boundary conditions for the effective viscous flux
G
and the vorticity
\omega
becomes our biggest challenge. We note that
\nabla G=\partial_{r}G\mathbf{e_{r}}
and
\nabla^{\perp}w=\partial_{r}w\mathbf{e_{\theta}}
, which allows us to directly take the inner product of the momentum equation with
\mathbf{e_{r}}
and
\mathbf{e_{\theta}}
respectively to decouple
G
and
\omega
. Therefore, for any
1\leq p<\infty
, we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla G\right\rVert_{L^{p}}=\left\lVert\nabla G\cdot\frac{\mathbf{x}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}=\left\lVert\rho\dot{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{x}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\left\lVert\rho\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}},

\displaystyle\mu\left\lVert\nabla^{\perp}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}=\mu\left\lVert\nabla^{\perp}\omega\cdot\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}=\left\lVert\rho\dot{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{x^{\perp}}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\left\lVert\rho\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}.

Using the estimate of
\log(e+\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})
, we prove that the growth of
\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}
will not exceed
R_{T}^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}+\varepsilon}
through a logarithmic-type embedding inequality. Therefore, using standard methods, we have proven the uniform upper bound of the density (see Proposition
3.2
).

2.

When
1<\beta\leq\gamma
, we first prove that
\left|\int_{0}^{t}G(R,\tau)d\tau\right|
has a time-independent bound (see Proposition
3.3
). Then, inspired by
[
6
]
, by fully utilizing the damping mechanism of

, we prove the time-independent higher integrability of
\rho
(see Proposition
3.4
). This ensures that the growth of
\log(e+\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})
is at most
R_{T}^{1+\varepsilon}
(see Proposition
3.5
), which improves the estimate of
\xi
. Finally, using the Zlotnik inequality, we obtain the uniform upper bound of the density (see Proposition
3.6
). Note that
\gamma>\frac{\gamma+2}{4}
, so we obtain the asymptotic behavior of the solution when
\beta>1
.

3.

When
\beta=1
, the methods mentioned above become ineffective. Because it seems we can only achieve control of
\log(e+\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})
by superlinear growth of
R_{T}
, rather than sublinear growth. This results in
\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}
being controlled only by the superlinear growth of

. Therefore, We can only obtain

\displaystyle R_{T}\leq CR_{T}^{1+\varepsilon},

which leads to a failure in obtaining the upper bound on the density. Therefore, to overcome this difficulty, we changed the way of estimating
\xi
. First, utilizing the radially symmetric structure and delicate index analysis, we prove that a uniform
L^{p}
estimate for the density still holds in the endpoint case (see Proposition
3.4
). This relaxes the restriction of
\beta>1
when establishing the uniform
L^{p}
estimate for the density in
[
6
]
. Next, we used the idea from
[
6
]
to prove that
\int_{\Omega}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{2+\delta}dx
has a time-independent bound, where
\delta=C(\mu,\gamma)R_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and

(see Proposition

). Then, we estimate
\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\xi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{R}|\rho u_{r}|ds\leq\int_{0}^{R}\left(s\rho|u_{r}|^{2+\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2+\delta}}\rho^{\frac{1+\delta}{2+\delta}}s^{-\frac{1}{2+\delta}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1+\delta}{2+\delta}}\left(\int_{0}^{R}s^{-\frac{1}{1+\delta}}ds\right)^{\frac{1+\delta}{2+\delta}}\leq CR_{T}^{\frac{1+\delta}{2+\delta}}\delta^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2+\delta}}

With the estimate of

When

,
3.15
). Then, we constructed the initial values
(\rho^{\delta}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0}^{\delta})
for the approximating system and proved that the approximating solutions
(\rho^{\delta},\mathbf{u}^{\delta})
are global. At this point, due to the requirements for the initial values of local existence (see Lemma
2.1
), we must close the estimate for
\|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}
. Because of the structure of the transport equation, this necessitates estimating
\|\nabla^{3}\mathbf{u}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}
. To achieve this, following Hoff’s method, we need to estimate
\|\nabla^{2}G^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\nabla^{2}w^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}
. The absence of boundary conditions for the effective viscous flux and vorticity once again presents a challenge. We observe that, under the radially symmetric setting,
\nabla^{2}G^{\delta}
and
\Delta G^{\delta}
have the following representations:

\displaystyle\partial_{ij}G^{\delta}

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}\left(\partial_{r}G^{\delta}\frac{x_{i}}{r}\right)=\partial_{rr}G^{\delta}\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{r^{2}}+\partial_{r}G^{\delta}\frac{\delta_{ij}r^{2}-x_{i}x_{j}}{r^{3}},

and

\displaystyle\Delta G^{\delta}=\partial_{rr}G^{\delta}+\frac{\partial_{r}G^{\delta}}{r}.

This means we can control
\nabla^{2}G^{\delta}
using
\Delta G^{\delta}
and a tail term. Specifically,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}G^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}&\leq\hat{C}\left(\left\lVert\partial_{rr}G^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\frac{\partial_{r}G^{\delta}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)\leq\hat{C}\left(\left\lVert\Delta G^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\frac{\partial_{r}G^{\delta}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq\hat{C}\left(\left\lVert\nabla(\rho^{\delta}\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\frac{\rho^{\delta}\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)\leq\hat{C}\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\end{split}

where we have used
\Delta G^{\delta}=\div(\rho^{\delta}\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta})
and the following simple fact

\displaystyle|\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}|^{2}=(\partial_{r}\left<\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta},\mathbf{e_{r}}\right>)^{2}+(\partial_{r}\left<\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta},\mathbf{e_{\theta}}\right>)^{2}+\frac{|\left<\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta},\mathbf{e_{r}}\right>|^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{|\left<\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta},\mathbf{e_{\theta}}\right>|^{2}}{r^{2}}\geq\left|\frac{\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}}{r}\right|^{2}.

The estimate for
\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\omega^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
is similar. After obtaining the global approximating solutions, we obtain
(\rho,\mathbf{u})
through standard compactness arguments.

We organize the paper as follows: In Section 2, we collect some basic facts and inequalities used in the proof. In Section 3, we derive the necessary a priori estimates. Specifically, in Section 3.1, we derive the uniform upper bound of the density when
\beta>\max\left\{1,\frac{\gamma+2}{4}\right\}

and small initial density. In Section 3.5, we establish the higher-order estimates. In Section 4, we complete the proof of the main theorems.

Next, we present the renormalized transport equation structure first introduced by Kazhikhov and Vaigant

. Define

and the effective viscous flux as

\displaystyle G=(2\mu+\lambda)\div\mathbf{u}-(P-\bar{P}).

Multiplying the transport equation

\displaystyle\theta_{t}+u_{r}\partial_{r}\theta+G+P-\bar{P}=0.

Due to the definition of the effective viscous flux (

\displaystyle(\rho u_{r})_{t}+\partial_{r}(\rho u_{r}^{2})+\frac{\rho}{r}(u^{2}_{r}-u_{\theta}^{2})=\partial_{r}G.

Integrating the above equation from
R
to

\displaystyle\xi_{t}+\rho u_{r}^{2}+\int_{R}^{r}\frac{\rho}{s}(u_{r}^{2}-u_{\theta}^{2})ds=G-G(R,t),

where

. Adding equation (

\displaystyle(\theta+\xi)_{t}+u_{r}\partial_{r}(\theta+\xi)+\int_{R}^{r}\frac{\rho}{s}(u_{r}^{2}-u_{\theta}^{2})ds+P-\bar{P}+G(R,t)=0.

(3.6)

Following the idea in
[
14
]
, we provide the boundary representation of the effective viscous flux.

Lemma 3.2
.

It holds that

\displaystyle G(R,t)=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\left\{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{R}\rho u_{r}r^{2}dr+\int_{0}^{R}2G(r,t)rdr-\int_{0}^{R}\rho(u_{r}^{2}+u_{\theta}^{2})rdr\right\}.

(3.7)

Proof.

Multiplying the momentum equation
\eqref{Equ 2}_{2}
by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(r^{2}\rho u_{r})+\partial_{r}(r^{2}\rho u_{r}^{2})-r(\rho u_{r}^{2}+\rho u_{\theta}^{2})=r^{2}\partial_{r}G.

Integrating the above equation from

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{R}\rho u_{r}r^{2}dr-\int_{0}^{R}\rho(u_{r}^{2}+u_{\theta}^{2})rdr=R^{2}G(R,t)-\int_{0}^{R}2G(r,t)rdr.

Then

\displaystyle G(R,t)=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\left\{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{R}\rho u_{r}r^{2}dr+\int_{0}^{R}2G(r,t)rdr-\int_{0}^{R}\rho(u_{r}^{2}+u_{\theta}^{2})rdr\right\}.

In this subsection, our aim is to establish a time-independent uniform upper bound of the density under assumption

We need the following lemma to obtain

Lemma 3.3

For any

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\lVert\nabla G\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\left\lVert\rho\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}},\\

(3.9)

Proof.

Note that
G
,

and the following simple fact

\displaystyle\nabla G=\partial_{r}G\mathbf{e_{r}},

This completes the proof.

e+A_{1}^{2}
shows

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\log(e+A_{1}^{2})+\frac{A_{2}^{2}}{e+A_{1}^{2}}\leq CR_{T}^{\max\left\{1+\alpha\beta,1,\gamma-2\beta+1,\beta-\gamma-1\right\}}A_{3}^{2}.

Integrating with respect to time gives

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\log(e+A_{1}^{2})+\int_{0}^{T}\frac{A_{2}^{2}}{e+A_{1}^{2}}dt\leq CR_{T}^{1+\kappa+\alpha\beta}.

(3.22)

Therefore, the above equation combined with
(
3.21
)
completes the proof.
∎

Next, we will employ the Zlotnik inequality to obtain the uniform upper bound of the density.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\beta>\max\left\{1,\frac{\gamma+2}{4}\right\}
, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
\mu,\beta,\gamma,R
and initial data, but not on
T
, such that for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C.

(3.23)

Proof.

Reviewing the previous transport structure

\displaystyle\frac{D}{Dt}(\theta+\xi)+\int_{R}^{r}\frac{\rho}{s}(u_{r}^{2}-u_{\theta}^{2})ds+P-\bar{P}+G(R,t)=0.

Let

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=\theta(t,x(t)),\quad g(y)=-P(\theta^{-1}(y)),

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle=-\xi(t,x(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{r}^{R}\frac{\rho}{s}(u_{r}^{2}-u_{\theta}^{2})dsd\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\bar{P}(\tau)d\tau-\int_{0}^{t}G(R,\tau)d\tau.

Simple calculations show that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|h(t_{2})-h(t_{1})|&\leq 2\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{r}^{R}\frac{\rho}{s}(u_{r}^{2}+u_{\theta}^{2})dsd\tau+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\bar{P}d\tau+\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}G(R,\tau)d\tau\right|\\
&:=\sum_{i=1}^{4}J_{i}.\end{split}

(3.24)

Next, we estimate
J_{1}
-
J_{4}
. By Proposition
3.1
, for some
q>2
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\xi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}&\leq C\left\lVert\rho\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(e+\left\lVert\rho\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{q}})+C\left\lVert\rho\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}}+C\\
&\leq CR_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(e+R_{T}+\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})+C\\
&\leq CR_{T}^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}+\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}},\end{split}

which means that

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq CR_{T}^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}+\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}}.

By using the energy equality (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle J_{2}\leq CR_{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}dxdt\leq CR_{T},

where we used the following simple fact

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}=(\partial_{r}u_{r})^{2}+(\partial_{r}u_{\theta})^{2}+\frac{u_{r}^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{u_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{2}}.

The energy equality
(
3.1
)
indicates that

\displaystyle J_{3}\leq C(t_{2}-t_{1}).

Finally, we estimate the boundary term
J_{4}
. Recalling that

\displaystyle G(R,t)=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\left\{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{R}\rho u_{r}r^{2}dr+\int_{0}^{R}2rG(r,t)dr-\int_{0}^{R}\rho(u_{r}^{2}+u_{\theta}^{2})rdr\right\}

Employing the energy equality (
3.1
) again, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\int_{0}^{R}\rho u_{r}r^{2}dr(t_{2})-\int_{0}^{R}\rho u_{r}r^{2}dr(t_{1})\right|+C\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\beta}\div\mathbf{u}dxd\tau\right|

\displaystyle\quad+C\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{0}^{R}\rho(u_{r}^{2}+u_{\theta}^{2})rdrdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq C+CR_{T}^{\beta-\gamma}+C(t_{2}-t_{1}),

where we used

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho^{\beta})+\div(\rho^{\beta}\mathbf{u})+(\beta-1)\rho^{\beta}\div\mathbf{u}=0.

Substituting the above estimates into (
3.24
) gives

\displaystyle|h(t_{2})-h(t_{1})|\leq CR_{T}^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}+\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}}+CR_{T}^{\beta-\gamma}+C(t_{2}-t_{1}).

By using Lemma
2.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\theta(t,x(t))\leq CR_{T}^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}+\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}}+CR_{T}^{\beta-\gamma},

(3.25)

taking the supremum in time and space completes the proof.
∎

3.2
Uniform upper bound of density for
1<\beta\leq\gamma

In this subsection, we prove the uniform upper bound of the density when
1<\beta\leq\gamma
. First, the following lemma shows that the boundary integral of
G
can be independent of time.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
\gamma>1
,
1\leq\beta\leq\gamma
, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
\mu,\beta,\gamma,R
and initial data, but not on
T
, such for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}G(R,\tau)d\tau\right|\leq C.

(3.26)

Proof.

Integrating (
3.7
) over
(0,t)
gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}G(R,\tau)d\tau&=\dfrac{1}{R^{2}}\left[\int_{0}^{R}\rho u_{r}r^{2}dr|_{\tau=t}-\int_{0}^{R}\rho u_{r}r^{2}dr|_{\tau=0}+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{R}G(r,\tau)rdrd\tau\right.\\
&\quad\left.-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{R}\rho(u_{r}^{2}+u_{\theta}^{2})rdrd\tau\right]\\
&:=K_{1}+K_{2}+K_{3}+K_{4}.\end{split}

(3.27)

By using the energy equality (
3.1
), we obtain the estimates for the terms
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
,

\displaystyle|K_{1}|\leq C\int_{0}^{R}(\rho(t)+\rho u_{r}^{2}(t))rdr\leq C,

and

\displaystyle|K_{2}|\leq C\int_{0}^{R}(\rho_{0}+\rho_{0}u_{r_{0}}^{2})rdr\leq C.

Note that when
1<\beta\leq\gamma
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{R}G(r,\tau)rdrd\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\beta}\div\mathbf{u}dxd\tau=\frac{1}{2\pi(1-\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\beta}dxd\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi(1-\beta)}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\beta}(t)dx-\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}^{\beta}dx\right).

and when
\beta=1
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{R}G(r,\tau)rdrd\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\rho\div\mathbf{u}dxd\tau=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho\log\rho dxd\tau

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho\log\rho(t)dx-\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\log\rho_{0}dx\right).

Therefore, we use equality (
3.1
) to obtain the estimate for
K_{3}
,

\displaystyle|K_{3}|\leq C.

Using the energy equality (
3.1
) and (
2.1
) , one has the estimate for
K_{4}
,

\displaystyle|K_{4}|\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{R}\rho rdrd\tau\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\leq C.

Combining the estimates for
K_{1}
to
K_{4}
, we complete the proof.
∎

Next, we use a key idea from
[
6
]
to obtain the time-independent higher integrability of the density field.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that
\gamma>1
,
1\leq\beta\leq\gamma
, for any
k\geq 1
, there exists positive constants
M
and
C
depending only on
\mu,\beta,\gamma,k,R
and initial data, but not on
T
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{k\beta\gamma+1}dx\leq C.

(3.28)

Proof.

Define
H(t)=\int_{0}^{t}G(R,s)ds
, there is also a structure similar to equation (
3.6
):

\displaystyle(\theta+\xi+H(t))_{t}+u_{r}\partial_{r}(\theta+\xi+H(t))+\int_{R}^{r}\frac{\rho}{s}(u_{r}^{2}-u_{\theta}^{2})ds+P(r,t)-\bar{P}(t)=0.

(3.29)

Let
f=(\theta+\xi+H(t)-M)_{+}
, where
M
is a sufficiently large constant to be determined. Multiplying (
3.29
) by
\rho f^{k\gamma-1}
, one obtains

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dfrac{1}{k\gamma}\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx&=\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}\dfrac{D}{Dt}fdx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}\left(\int_{r}^{R}\frac{\rho}{s}(u_{r}^{2}-u_{\theta}^{2})ds-P(r,t)+\bar{P}\right)dx.\end{split}

(3.30)

Next, we claim that under the condition
f>0
, there exists constants
C
such that

\displaystyle f^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}+\left(\frac{M}{2}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\leq C\left(f+\frac{M}{2}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}=C\left(\theta+\xi+H(t)-\frac{M}{2}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\leq C(P+|\xi|^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}),

(3.31)

where we choose
M
sufficiently large such that
|H(t)|\leq\frac{M}{2}
, using (
3.26
). In fact, observing
\theta+\xi
is positive, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\theta+\xi\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}=\left(2\mu\log\rho+\frac{1}{\beta}\rho^{\beta}+\xi\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\leq C(\rho^{\beta}+|\xi|)^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\leq C(P+|\xi|^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}).

Substituting inequality
(
3.31
)
into
(
3.30
)
yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\dfrac{1}{k\gamma}\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}\left(f^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}+\left(\frac{M}{2}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\right)dx\\
&\leq C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}\left(\int_{r}^{R}\frac{\rho}{s}u_{r}^{2}ds\right)dx+C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}\bar{P}dx+C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}|\xi|^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}dx\\
&=N_{1}+N_{2}+N_{3}.\end{split}

(3.32)

By using Hölder inequality and Sobolev inequality, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{1}&\leq C\left\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{k\gamma}}f\right\rVert_{L^{k\gamma}}^{k\gamma-1}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{k\beta\gamma+1}}^{\frac{1}{k\gamma}}\left\lVert\int_{r}^{R}\frac{\rho}{s}u_{r}^{2}ds\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{k\beta\gamma+1}{\beta}}}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{k\gamma}}f\right\rVert_{L^{k\gamma}}^{k\gamma-1}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{k\beta\gamma+1}}^{\frac{1}{k\gamma}}\left\lVert\frac{\rho u_{r}^{2}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2(k\beta\gamma+1)}{k\beta\gamma+1+2\beta}}}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{k\gamma}}f\right\rVert_{L^{k\gamma}}^{k\gamma-1}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{k\beta\gamma+1}}^{\frac{1}{k\gamma}}\left\lVert\rho u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{k\beta\gamma+1}{\beta}}}\left\lVert\frac{u_{r}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(3.33)

Note that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\rho u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{k\beta\gamma+1}{\beta}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{k\beta\gamma+1}{\beta}}}\left\lVert u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{k\beta\gamma+1}}\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{u_{r}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=2\pi\int_{0}^{R}\frac{u_{r}^{2}}{r^{2}}rdr\leq 2\pi\int_{0}^{R}\left(\partial_{r}u_{r}+\frac{u_{r}}{r}\right)^{2}rdr=\int_{\Omega}|\div\mathbf{u}|^{2}dx.

Therefore, we obtain the estimate for
N_{1}
:

\displaystyle N_{1}\leq C\left(1+\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+\int_{\Omega}\rho^{k\beta\gamma+1}dx\right)\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By using the equality
(
3.1
)
, we obtain

\displaystyle N_{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}\bar{P}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}dx.

(3.34)

Finally, we estiamate
N_{3}
in two cases:
\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\leq 2
and
\frac{\gamma}{\beta}>2
.

When
\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\leq 2
, if
\gamma>2
, then by the Sobolev embedding, (
2.1
) and (
3.1
) yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int_{R}^{r}\rho u_{r}ds\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\rho u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\gamma}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\gamma}}^{2}\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Then we estimate
N_{3}
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{3}&\leq C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}(1+|\xi|^{2})dx\\
&\leq C(1+\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2})\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}dx\\
&\leq C(1+\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2})\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}dx.\end{split}

(3.35)

If
\gamma\leq 2
, we choose
\varepsilon
sufficiently small such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int_{R}^{r}\rho u_{r}ds\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\rho u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2+\varepsilon}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\gamma}}^{2-2\theta}\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{k\beta\gamma+1}}^{2\theta}\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where

\displaystyle\theta=\frac{k\beta\gamma+1}{k\beta\gamma+1-\gamma}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{2+\varepsilon}\right).

Therefore, we estimate
N_{3}
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{3}&\leq C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}(1+|\xi|^{2})dx\\
&\leq C(1+\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2})\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}dx\\
&\leq C(1+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{k\beta\gamma+1}}^{2\theta}\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2})\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}dx\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{k\beta\gamma+1}}^{2\theta}\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx\right)^{\frac{k\gamma-1}{k\gamma}}+C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}dx\\
&\leq C\left(1+\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+\int_{\Omega}\rho^{k\beta\gamma+1}dx\right)\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}dx,\end{split}

(3.36)

where we used

\displaystyle\beta\geq 1,\quad\gamma>1,

and

\displaystyle\frac{k\gamma-1}{k\gamma}+\frac{2}{k\beta\gamma+1-\gamma}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{2+\varepsilon}\right)\leq 1,

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\beta\geq-\frac{2\gamma}{2+\varepsilon}+\left(2+\frac{1}{k}\right)-\frac{1}{k\gamma}.

When
\frac{\gamma}{\beta}>2
, we choose
\varepsilon
sufficiently small such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int_{R}^{r}\rho u_{r}ds\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left\lVert\rho u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2+\varepsilon}}\leq C\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1-\eta}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\right\rVert_{L^{2\gamma}}^{1+\eta}\left\lVert u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{\eta},

where

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\left(\gamma+1-\frac{2\gamma}{2+\varepsilon}\right)\in(0,1).

At this point, we estimate
N_{3}
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{3}&\leq C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}|\xi|^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}dx\leq C\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}dx\leq C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{\eta\gamma}{\beta}}\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}dx.\end{split}

(3.37)

Let
\frac{\eta\gamma}{\beta}=2
, then

\displaystyle\frac{2\beta}{\gamma}=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\left(\gamma+1-\frac{2\gamma}{2+\varepsilon}\right),

we point out that such an
\varepsilon
exists, due to our assumptions

\displaystyle\beta\geq 1,\quad\gamma>2.

Substituting the estimates for
N_{1}
to
N_{3}
back into
(
3.32
)
gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{k\gamma}\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}\left(f^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}+\left(\frac{M}{2}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\right)dx\\
&\leq C\left(1+\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+\int_{\Omega}\rho^{k\beta\gamma+1}dx\right)\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}dx.\end{split}

Let
M
be sufficiently large such that

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}\left(\frac{M}{2}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}dx,

therefore, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{k\gamma}\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}\left(f^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}+\left(\frac{M}{2}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\right)dx\\
&\leq C\left(1+\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+\int_{\Omega}\rho^{k\beta\gamma+1}dx\right)\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.38)

Finally, by using
(
3.26
)
, Hölder inequality and Sobolev embedding, we estimate
\int_{\Omega}\rho^{k\beta\gamma+1}dx
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{k\beta\gamma+1}dx&=\int_{\Omega\cap(\rho<2)}\rho^{k\beta\gamma+1}dx+\int_{\Omega\cap(\rho>2)}\rho^{k\beta\gamma+1}dx\\
&\leq C+C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+C\int_{\Omega}\rho|\xi|^{k\gamma}dx\\
&\leq C+C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2(k\beta\gamma+1)}{2k\beta\gamma+2-k\gamma}}}\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{2(k\beta\gamma+1)}}^{k\gamma}\\
&\leq C+C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2(k\beta\gamma+1)}{2k\beta\gamma+2-k\gamma}}}\left\lVert\rho u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2(k\beta\gamma+1)}{k\beta\gamma+2}}}^{k\gamma}\\
&\leq C+C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{k\beta\gamma+1}}\left\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{2(k\beta\gamma+1)}}^{k\gamma}\left\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{k\gamma}\\
&\leq C+C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{k\beta\gamma+1}}^{\frac{k\gamma}{2}+1}.\end{split}

The Young’s inequality shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho^{k\beta\gamma+1}dx\leq C+C\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx.

(3.39)

Substituting (
3.39
) into (
3.38
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{k\gamma}\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}\left(f^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}+\left(\frac{M}{2}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\right)dx\leq C\left(1+\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma}dx\right)\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.40)

We complete the proof by using the Gronwall inequality and (
3.39
).
∎

The Proposition
3.4
essentially establishes a new potential under the condition
1\leq\beta\leq\gamma
. Therefore, naturally, we can obtain the following Proposition similar to Proposition
3.1
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
1\leq\beta\leq\gamma
. For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
\alpha,\mu,\beta,\gamma,R,\left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}},\left\lVert\mathbf{u_{0}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\log(e+A_{1}^{2}(t)+A_{3}^{2}(t))+\int_{0}^{T}\frac{A_{2}^{2}(t)}{e+A_{1}^{2}(t)}dt\leq CR_{T}^{1+\alpha\beta}.

(3.41)

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Proposition
3.1
, but for completeness, we still provide some key steps of the proof.
Multiplying equation (
3.12
) by
\dot{\mathbf{u}}
and integrating by parts gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2}&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{2\mu+\lambda-\beta\lambda}{(2\mu+\lambda)^{2}}G^{2}\div\mathbf{u}dx-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\beta\lambda(P-\bar{P})}{(2\mu+\lambda)^{2}}G\div\mathbf{u}dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\gamma P}{2\mu+\lambda}G\div\mathbf{u}dx\\
&\quad-(\gamma-1)\int_{\Omega}\frac{G}{2\mu+\lambda}dx\int_{\Omega}P\div\mathbf{u}dx-\int_{\Omega}G(\div\mathbf{u})^{2}dx\\
&\quad+2\int_{\Omega}G\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}u_{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\Omega}w^{2}\div\mathbf{u}dx\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{7}I_{i}.\end{split}

(3.42)

The estimates for
I_{1}
,
I_{4}
and
I_{7}
are the same as in Proposition
3.1
.
For
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
, we estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}|I_{2}|+|I_{3}|&\leq C\int_{\Omega}\frac{P+1}{2\mu+\lambda}|G||\div\mathbf{u}|dx\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\frac{P+1}{(2\mu+\lambda)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right\rVert_{L^{2+\varepsilon}}\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2(2+\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}}}\left\lVert\sqrt{2\mu+\lambda}\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{1}}A_{3},\end{split}

(3.43)

where we used (
3.28
). Similarly,

\displaystyle\begin{split}|I_{5}|&\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{G^{2}}{2\mu+\lambda}|\div\mathbf{u}|dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{|P-\bar{P}|}{2\mu+\lambda}|G||\div\mathbf{u}|dx\\
&\leq CA_{1}A_{3}R_{T}^{\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}}\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+C\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{1}}A_{3},\end{split}

(3.44)

and

\displaystyle|I_{6}|\leq C\left\lVert\nabla G\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla G\right\rVert_{L^{2}}A_{3}^{2}.

(3.45)

Substituting (
3.14
),(
3.43
),(
3.16
),(
3.44
),(
3.45
) and (
3.19
) into (
3.42
) gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{d}{dt}A_{1}^{2}+2A_{2}^{2}\\
&\leq C(R_{T}^{\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}}\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{H^{1}})A_{1}A_{3}+C\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{1}}A_{3}+CA_{3}^{2}+C\left\lVert\nabla G\right\rVert_{L^{2}}A_{3}^{2}\\
&\leq\varepsilon R_{T}^{-1}(\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})+CR_{T}^{1+\alpha\beta}A_{1}^{2}A_{3}^{2}+CR_{T}A_{3}^{2}+CR_{T}A_{3}^{4}\\
&\leq A_{2}^{2}+CR_{T}^{1+\alpha\beta}A_{1}^{2}A_{3}^{2}+CR_{T}A_{3}^{2}+CR_{T}A_{3}^{4},\end{split}

(3.46)

where we used

\displaystyle|\bar{G}|=\left|\int_{\Omega}\lambda\div\mathbf{u}dx\right|\leq\left\lVert\sqrt{\lambda}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}A_{3}\leq CA_{3}.

Note the following relationship between
A_{1}
and
A_{3}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{3}^{2}&\leq C\left\lVert\sqrt{2\mu+\lambda}\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\frac{G}{\sqrt{2\mu+\lambda}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\frac{P-\bar{P}}{\sqrt{2\mu+\lambda}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq CA_{1}^{2}+C.\end{split}

(3.47)

Dividing equation (
3.46
) by
e+A_{1}^{2}
implies that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\log(e+A_{1}^{2})+\frac{A_{2}^{2}}{e+A_{1}^{2}}\leq CR_{T}^{1+\alpha\beta}A_{3}^{2}.

Integrating with respect to time gives

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\log(e+A_{1}^{2})+\int_{0}^{T}\frac{A_{2}^{2}}{e+A_{1}^{2}}dt\leq CR_{T}^{1+\alpha\beta}.

(3.48)

Using (
3.47
) again, we complete the proof.
∎

3.2

\kappa

0

3.3
Uniform upper bound of density for
\beta=1

In this subsection, we will obtain a uniform upper bound for the density under the condition
\beta=1
, which is entirely different from the case where
\beta>1
.

We first prove the time-independent spatial higher integrability of the density field, which is a natural corollary of Proposition
3.4
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Suppose that
\beta=1,k\gamma\geq 3
, then there exists a sufficiently large constant
M_{1}
and
C
, depending on
\mu,\beta,\gamma,R,k
and initial data, but not on time
T
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\rho-M_{1})_{+}^{k\gamma-1}dxdt\leq C.

(3.50)

Proof.

Assume that
M
is the constant in (
3.40
). Let
M_{1}\geq\frac{3}{2}M
, then we obtain from
f=(\theta+\xi+H-M)_{+}
that

\displaystyle\rho\leq f-2\mu\log\rho-\xi-H+M\leq f+|\xi|+\frac{3M}{2}\leq f+|\xi|+M_{1},

when
\rho\geq M_{1}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle(\rho-M_{1})_{+}\leq f+|\xi|.

Direct calculations show that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\rho-M_{1})_{+}^{k\gamma-1}dxdt\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho f^{k\gamma-1}dxdt+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\xi|^{k\gamma-1}dxdt,\end{split}

(3.51)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\rho-M_{1})_{+}^{k\gamma-1}dxdt\geq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\rho-M_{1})_{+}^{k\gamma-1}dxdt.

(3.52)

From (
3.40
), we know that the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.51
) is bounded, so we estimate the second term as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\xi|^{k\gamma-1}dxdt&\leq C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{\gamma}}\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{k\gamma-1}L^{\frac{\gamma(k\gamma-1)}{\gamma-1}}}^{k\gamma-1}\leq C\left\lVert\rho u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{k\gamma-1}L^{\frac{2\gamma(k\gamma-1)}{\gamma(k\gamma-1)+2(\gamma-1)}}}^{k\gamma-1}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}u_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{k\gamma-3}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{\frac{\gamma(k\gamma+1)}{2\gamma-1}}}^{k\gamma+1}\leq C,\end{split}

(3.53)

where we used

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma(k\gamma+1)}{2\gamma-1}\leq 2k\gamma.

Thus, we have completed the proof.
∎

We utilize the Lemma
2.5
to prove the following Proposition.

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose that
\beta=1
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
\mu,\beta,\gamma,R
and initial data, but not on time
T
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\rho^{\gamma-1}+1)(\rho-\rho_{s})^{2}dxdt\leq C.

(3.54)

Proof.

Taking the inner product of
\eqref{Equ1}_{2}
with
\mathcal{B}(\rho-\rho_{s})
and integrating by parts yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(\rho^{\gamma}-\rho_{s}^{\gamma})(\rho-\rho_{s})dx&=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathcal{B}(\rho-\rho_{s})dx-\int_{\Omega}\rho\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathcal{B}(\partial_{t}\rho)dx\\
&\quad-\int_{\Omega}\rho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u}:\nabla\mathcal{B}(\rho-\rho_{s})dx+\mu\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathbf{u}:\nabla\mathcal{B}(\rho-\rho_{s})dx\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}(\mu+\lambda)\div\mathbf{u}(\rho-\rho_{s})dx=\sum_{i=0}^{4}V_{i}.\end{split}

(3.55)

The Hölder inequality, (
3.28
) and Lemma
2.5
indicate that

\displaystyle V_{1}\leq C\left\lVert\rho\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

(3.56)

and

\displaystyle V_{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}|\nabla\mathcal{B}(\rho-\rho_{s})|dx\leq C\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{B}(\rho-\rho_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.57)

By Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle V_{3}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{B}(\rho-\rho_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\varepsilon\left\lVert\rho-\rho_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

(3.58)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{4}&\leq C\int_{\Omega}(\rho-M_{1})_{+}|\div\mathbf{u}||\rho-\rho_{s}|+M_{1}|\div\mathbf{u}||\rho-\rho_{s}|dx\\
&\leq\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}|\rho-\rho_{s}|^{\gamma+1}+|\rho-\rho_{s}|^{2}dx+C\int_{\Omega}(\rho-M_{1})_{+}^{\frac{2(\gamma+1)}{\gamma-1}}+|\div\mathbf{u}|^{2}dx.\end{split}

(3.59)

Note the following simple fact:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(\rho^{\gamma}-\rho_{s}^{\gamma})(\rho-\rho_{s})dx\geq C\int_{\Omega}(\rho^{\gamma-1}+1)(\rho-\rho_{s})^{2}dx.\end{split}

Therefore, substituting (
3.56
)-(
3.59
) into equation (
3.55
), we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}C\int_{\Omega}(\rho^{\gamma-1}+1)(\rho-\rho_{s})^{2}dx&\leq\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathcal{B}(\rho-\rho_{s})dx+C\int_{\Omega}(\rho-M_{1})_{+}^{\frac{2(\gamma+1)}{\gamma-1}}dx+C\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.60)

Finally, we use (
3.28
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\rho\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathcal{B}(\rho-\rho_{s})dx\right|\leq\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\mathcal{B}(\rho-\rho_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{4}}\leq C.

Thus, integrating (
3.60
) with respect to time, we complete the proof with (
3.50
).
∎

The following Proposition, similar to Proposition 3.4 in
[
6
]
, shows the higher integrability of the velocity field, which plays an important role in bounding the upper bound of the density.

Proposition 3.9
.

Suppose that
\beta=1
, there exists constants
C(\mu,\gamma)\in\left(0,\max\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{\gamma-1}{2(\gamma+1)}\right\}\right)
depend only on
\mu,\gamma
and
C
depend only on
\mu,\beta,\gamma,R
and initial data, but not on
T
, such that for all
\delta\leq C(\mu,\gamma)R_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{2+\delta}dx\leq C.

(3.61)

Proof.

Multiplying
\eqref{Equ1}_{2}
by
(2+\delta)|\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}\mathbf{u}
, and integrating over
\Omega
we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{2+\delta}dx+\mu(2+\delta)\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}(|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\delta|\nabla|\mathbf{u}||^{2})dx+(2+\delta)\int_{\Omega}(\mu+\lambda)|\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}|\div\mathbf{u}|^{2}dx\\
&\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\rho^{\gamma}-\rho_{s}^{\gamma}||\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|dx+\delta(2+\delta)\int_{\Omega}(\mu+\lambda)|\div\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}|\nabla|\mathbf{u}||dx\\
&\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\rho^{\gamma}-\rho_{s}^{\gamma}||\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|dx+\frac{\mu(2+\delta)}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}|\nabla|\mathbf{u}||^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2\mu}\delta^{2}(2+\delta)\int_{\Omega}(\mu+\lambda)^{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}|\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u}|^{2}dx.\end{split}

Note that

\displaystyle(2+\delta)(\mu+\lambda)>\frac{1}{2\mu}\delta^{2}(2+\delta)(\mu+\lambda)^{2},

due to

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\leq(C(\mu,\gamma))^{2}R_{T}^{-1}<\frac{2\mu}{\mu+R_{T}}\leq\frac{2\mu}{\mu+\lambda}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{2+\delta}dx+\dfrac{\mu(2+\delta)}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}dx+\mu\delta(2+\delta)\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}|\nabla|\mathbf{u}||^{2}dx\\
&\leq C\int_{\Omega}(\rho^{\gamma-1}+1)|\rho-\rho_{s}||\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|dx\\
&\leq C\int_{\Omega}(\rho-M_{1})_{+}^{\gamma-1}|\rho-\rho_{s}||\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|dx+C\int_{\Omega}M_{1}^{\gamma-1}|\rho-\rho_{s}||\mathbf{u}|^{\delta}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|dx\\
&\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\rho-\rho_{s}|^{\gamma+1}+(\rho-M_{1})_{+}^{q}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}dx+C\int_{\Omega}|\rho-\rho_{s}|^{p}+|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}dx,\end{split}

(3.62)

where
q
,
p
satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma-1}{q}+\frac{1}{\gamma+1}+\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=1,\quad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=1.

Due to
0<\delta<\frac{\gamma-1}{2(\gamma+1)}
, one has

\displaystyle q\in\left(2\gamma+2,4\gamma+4\right),\quad p\in\left(2,\frac{4\gamma+4}{\gamma+3}\right).

We calculate

\displaystyle|\rho-\rho_{s}|^{\gamma+1}\leq C(\rho^{\gamma-1}+1)(\rho-\rho_{s})^{2},

and

\displaystyle|\rho-\rho_{s}|^{p}\leq C(\rho^{\gamma-1}+1)(\rho-\rho_{s})^{2}.

Integrating (
3.62
) with respect to time, we complete the proof using (
3.50
) and (
3.54
).
∎

With the above lemmas in hand, we can now derive the uniform upper bound of the density.

Proposition 3.10
.

If
\beta=1
, there exists a constant
C
depend on
\mu,\beta,\gamma,R
and initial data, but not on time
T
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C.

(3.63)

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Proposition
3.2
, we only need to estimate each term in (
3.24
), for any
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}|h(t_{2})-h(t_{1})|&\leq 2\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{r}^{R}\frac{\rho}{s}(u_{r}^{2}+u_{\theta}^{2})dsd\tau+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\bar{P}d\tau+\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}G(R,\tau)d\tau\right|\\
&:=\sum_{i=1}^{4}J_{i}.\end{split}

(3.64)

Let
\delta=C(\mu,\gamma)R_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, where
C(\mu,\gamma)
is the constant from Proposition
3.9
. The Hölder inequality shows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\xi(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{R}|\rho u_{r}|ds\leq\int_{0}^{R}\left(s\rho|u_{r}|^{2+\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2+\delta}}\rho^{\frac{1+\delta}{2+\delta}}s^{-\frac{1}{2+\delta}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\rho(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1+\delta}{2+\delta}}\left(\int_{0}^{R}s^{-\frac{1}{1+\delta}}ds\right)^{\frac{1+\delta}{2+\delta}}\leq CR_{T}^{\frac{1+\delta}{2+\delta}}\delta^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2+\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq CR_{T}^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{1+\delta}{2+\delta}}\leq CR_{T}^{\frac{9}{10}},

where we used
C(\mu,\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Then we get

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq CR_{T}^{\frac{9}{10}}.

(3.65)

Note that

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}=(\partial_{r}u_{r})^{2}+(\partial_{r}u_{\theta})^{2}+\frac{u_{r}^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{u_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{2}}.

Then, from the energy equality (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle J_{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\rho(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau,

(3.66)

and

\displaystyle J_{3}\leq C(t_{2}-t_{1}).

(3.67)

Proposition
3.3
shows

\displaystyle J_{4}\leq C.

(3.68)

Therefore, one has

\displaystyle|h(t_{2})-h(t_{1})|\leq C+CR_{T}^{\frac{9}{10}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\rho(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau+C(t_{2}-t_{1}).

(3.69)

By employing the Zlotnik inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\theta(t,x(t))\leq C+CR_{T}^{\frac{9}{10}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\rho(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau.

Taking the supremum over space yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C+CR_{T}^{\frac{9}{10}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\rho(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau.

By using the Gronwall inequality, we derive

\displaystyle\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C+CR_{T}^{\frac{9}{10}}.

Taking the supremum over time gives

\displaystyle R_{T}\leq C+CR_{T}^{\frac{9}{10}},

which, combined with Young’s inequality, completes the proof.
∎

3.4
Uniform upper bound of small density for
0<\beta<1

In this subsection, we aim to prove that under condition
0<\beta<1
, when the
L^{\infty}
norm of the initial density is sufficiently small, the local strong solution
\rho
has a time-independent uniform upper bound. Moreover, we provide a precise bound of how small the initial density needs to be. We define

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{T}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\rho(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

The following Proposition shows that when
\tilde{R}_{T}\leq C(\mu,\beta)
, the higher integrability of the velocity field has a time-independent bound and it can be controlled by
\rho_{0}
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Suppose that

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{T}\leq(7\mu)^{\frac{1}{\beta}},

(3.70)

then

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{3}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}|\mathbf{u}_{0}|^{3}dx+3\sqrt{2}7^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\mu^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}-1}RE_{0}.

(3.71)

Proof.

Multiplying equation
\eqref{Equ1}_{2}
by
3\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{u}|
gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{3}dx+3\mu\int_{\Omega}\left(|\mathbf{u}||\nabla|\mathbf{u}||^{2}+|\mathbf{u}||\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)dx+3\int_{\Omega}(\mu+\lambda)|\mathbf{u}||\div\mathbf{u}|^{2}dx\\
&\leq 3\int_{\Omega}P\div(|\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{u})dx+3\int_{\Omega}(\mu+\lambda)|\mathbf{u}||\div\mathbf{u}||\nabla|\mathbf{u}||dx\\
&\leq 3\tilde{R}_{T}^{\gamma}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|(|\div\mathbf{u}|+|\nabla|\mathbf{u}||)dx+3\int_{\Omega}(\mu+\lambda)|\mathbf{u}||\div\mathbf{u}|^{2}dx+\frac{3}{4}\int_{\Omega}(\mu+\lambda)|\mathbf{u}||\nabla|\mathbf{u}||^{2}dx.\end{split}

Note that (
3.70
) shows that

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}(\mu+\lambda)\leq 6\mu.

Therefore, direct calculations imply that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{3}dx

\displaystyle\leq 3\tilde{R}_{T}^{\gamma}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|(|\div\mathbf{u}|+|\nabla|\mathbf{u}||)dx

\displaystyle\leq 6(\pi R^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{R}_{T}^{\gamma}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 3\sqrt{2}R\tilde{R}_{T}^{\gamma}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\sqrt{2}(7\mu)^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}R\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used (
2.1
) and the following simple fact

\displaystyle|\nabla|\mathbf{u}||\leq|\nabla\mathbf{u}|,\quad\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Integrating with respect to time, we obtain from the energy equality (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{3}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}|\mathbf{u}_{0}|^{3}dx+3\sqrt{2}7^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\mu^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}-1}RE_{0}.

∎

With the help of the Proposition
3.11
, we have the following conclusion.

Proposition 3.12
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{T}\leq\frac{99}{100}(7\mu)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}.

(3.72)

Proof.

Recall the transport structure

\displaystyle\frac{D}{Dt}(\theta+\xi)+\int_{R}^{r}\frac{\rho}{s}(u_{r}^{2}-u_{\theta}^{2})ds+P-\bar{P}+G(R,t)=0.

(3.73)

Similarly to Proposition
3.2
, we define

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=\theta(t,x(t)),\quad g(y)=-P(\theta^{-1}(y)),

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle=-\xi(t,x(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{r}^{R}\frac{\rho}{s}(u_{r}^{2}-u_{\theta}^{2})dsd\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\bar{P}d\tau-\int_{0}^{t}G(R,\tau)d\tau.

Therefore, for any
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<T
, we have

\displaystyle|h(t_{2})-h(t_{1})|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{r}^{R}\frac{\rho}{s}(u_{r}^{2}+u_{\theta}^{2})dsd\tau+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\bar{P}d\tau+\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}G(R,\tau)d\tau\right|

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{4}L_{i}.

By using (
3.71
), one has

\displaystyle\left\lVert\xi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{R}\rho|u_{r}|ds=\int_{0}^{R}(\rho|u_{r}|^{3}s)^{\frac{1}{3}}\rho^{\frac{2}{3}}s^{-\frac{1}{3}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\int_{0}^{R}\rho|u_{r}|^{3}sds\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\tilde{R}_{T}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\int_{0}^{R}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}ds\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq(2R^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{2}{3}}(2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\tilde{R}_{T}^{\frac{2}{3}}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{3}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq(2R)^{\frac{1}{3}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{3}}(7\mu)^{\frac{2}{3\beta}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}|\mathbf{u}_{0}|^{3}dx+3\sqrt{2}7^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\mu^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}-1}RE_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}.

Therefore, we obtain the estimate for
L_{1}
,

\displaystyle L_{1}\leq 2^{\frac{4}{3}}R^{\frac{1}{3}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{3}}(7\mu)^{\frac{2}{3\beta}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}|\mathbf{u}_{0}|^{3}dx+3\sqrt{2}7^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}}\mu^{\frac{\gamma}{\beta}-1}RE_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}.

(3.74)

By using the energy equality
(
3.1
)
, we obtain

\displaystyle L_{2}\leq\tilde{R}_{T}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{u_{r}^{2}+u_{\theta}^{2}}{s}dsd\tau\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\tilde{R}_{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\leq\frac{1}{2\mu\pi}(7\mu)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}E_{0},

(3.75)

where we used the following simple fact

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}=(\partial_{r}u_{r})^{2}+(\partial_{r}u_{\theta})^{2}+\frac{u_{r}^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{u_{\theta}^{2}}{r^{2}}.

The energy inequality (
3.1
) shows that

\displaystyle L_{3}\leq\frac{(\gamma-1)E_{0}}{\pi R^{2}}(t_{2}-t_{1}).

(3.76)

Finally, we estimate the boundary term of the effective viscous flux,

\displaystyle L_{4}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}G(R,\tau)d\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{R^{2}}\left|\int_{0}^{R}\rho u_{r}r^{2}dr(t_{2})-\int_{0}^{R}\rho u_{r}r^{2}dr(t_{1})\right|+\left|\frac{1}{\pi R^{2}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\beta}\div\mathbf{u}dxd\tau\right|

The Hölder inequality shows that

\displaystyle L_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi R^{2}}\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\beta}\div\mathbf{u}dxd\tau\right|=\frac{1}{\pi R^{2}(1-\beta)}\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\beta}dxd\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{R^{2}}\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{R}\rho rdrdt\right|\leq\frac{M_{0}}{2\pi R^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}dt

(3.77)

Under the assumptions of Proposition

depending only on

(3.80)

Proof.

Operating

to

and summing over

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx\right)_{t}\\
&=-\int_{\Omega}\dot{u}^{j}[\partial_{j}P_{t}+\div(\partial_{j}P\mathbf{u})]dx+\mu\int_{\Omega}\dot{u}^{j}[\partial_{t}\Delta u^{j}+\div(\mathbf{u}\Delta u^{j})]dx\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}\dot{u}^{j}[\partial_{jt}((\mu+\lambda)\div\mathbf{u})+\div(\mathbf{u}\partial_{j}((\mu+\lambda)\div\mathbf{u}))]dx\\
&:=\sum_{i=1}^{3}O_{i}.\end{split}

(3.81)

Direct integration by parts and the Young’s inequality show that

\displaystyle\begin{split}O_{1}&=-\int_{\Omega}\dot{u}^{j}[\partial_{j}P_{t}+\div(\partial_{j}P\mathbf{u})]dx\\

&\leq\frac{\mu}{8}\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

&=-\mu\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}+\partial_{i}\dot{u}^{j}\partial_{k}u^{k}\partial_{i}u^{j}-\partial_{i}\dot{u}^{j}\partial_{i}u^{k}\partial_{k}u^{j}-\partial_{i}u^{j}\partial_{i}u^{k}\partial_{k}\dot{u}^{j})dx\\
&\leq-\dfrac{3\mu}{4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx+C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{4}dx.\end{split}

(3.83)

Finally, integration by parts lead to

\displaystyle\begin{split}O_{3}&=\int_{\Omega}\dot{u}^{j}[\partial_{jt}((\mu+\lambda)\div\mathbf{u})+\div(\mathbf{u}\partial_{j}((\mu+\lambda)\div\mathbf{u}))]dx\\

&\leq C\left\lVert\div\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}+C\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}+C\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\

&\leq\hat{C}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\rho^{\delta}||\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u}^{\delta}||\nabla^{2}\rho^{\delta}|dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\delta}||\nabla^{2}\rho^{\delta}|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\delta}|\nabla^{3}\mathbf{u}^{\delta}||\nabla^{2}\rho^{\delta}|dx\right)\\
&\leq\hat{C}(1+\left\lVert\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}})\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\delta}|^{2}dx+\hat{C}\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{q}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\hat{C}\left\lVert\rho^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla^{3}\mathbf{u}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq\hat{C}(1+\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\delta}|^{2}dx+\hat{C}+\hat{C}\left\lVert\nabla^{3}\mathbf{u}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

(4.12)

where we have used
\eqref{third}.
Next, we estimate
\left\lVert\nabla^{3}\mathbf{u}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Standard elliptic theory indicates that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\nabla^{3}\mathbf{u}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}&\leq\hat{C}+\hat{C}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\div\mathbf{u}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\hat{C}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\omega^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\hat{C}+\hat{C}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\left(\frac{G^{\delta}+P^{\delta}-\bar{P}^{\delta}}{2\mu+\lambda(\rho^{\delta})}\right)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\hat{C}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\omega^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\hat{C}+\hat{C}\left(\left\lVert\nabla^{2}G^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla G^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2}}}\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{q}}+\left\lVert G^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\rho^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right.\\
&\quad\left.+\left\lVert G^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\rho^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\omega^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq\hat{C}\left(1+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}G^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\omega^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)+\hat{C}(1+\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})(1+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\rho^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}).\end{split}

(4.13)

Next, we estimate the most challenging parts,
\left\lVert\nabla^{2}G^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
and
\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\omega^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
. Direct calculations show that

\displaystyle\partial_{ij}G^{\delta}

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}\left(\partial_{r}G^{\delta}\frac{x_{i}}{r}\right)=\partial_{rr}G^{\delta}\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{r^{2}}+\partial_{r}G^{\delta}\frac{\delta_{ij}r^{2}-x_{i}x_{j}}{r^{3}},

and

\displaystyle\Delta G^{\delta}=\partial_{rr}G^{\delta}+\frac{\partial_{r}G^{\delta}}{r}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}G^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}&\leq\hat{C}\left(\left\lVert\partial_{rr}G^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\frac{\partial_{r}G^{\delta}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)\leq\hat{C}\left(\left\lVert\Delta G^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\frac{\partial_{r}G^{\delta}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq\hat{C}\left(\left\lVert\nabla(\rho^{\delta}\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\frac{\rho^{\delta}\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}}{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)\leq\hat{C}\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\end{split}

(4.14)

where we used

\displaystyle|\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}|^{2}=(\partial_{r}\left<\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta},\mathbf{e_{r}}\right>)^{2}+(\partial_{r}\left<\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta},\mathbf{e_{\theta}}\right>)^{2}+\frac{|\left<\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta},\mathbf{e_{r}}\right>|^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{|\left<\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta},\mathbf{e_{\theta}}\right>|^{2}}{r^{2}}\geq\left|\frac{\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}}{r}\right|^{2}.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\omega^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\hat{C}\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, one has

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla^{3}\mathbf{u}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\hat{C}(1+\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\rho^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\hat{C}\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\hat{C}.

(4.15)

Substituting the above estimates into
(
4.12
)
gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\delta}|^{2}dx\leq\hat{C}(1+\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2})\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\delta}|^{2}dx+\hat{C}\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\hat{C}.\end{split}

(4.16)

Abstract
. Let 𝒩 \mathcal{N} be the σ \sigma -ideal of the null sets of reals. We introduce a new property of forcing notions that enable control of the additivity of 𝒩 \mathcal{N} after finite support iterations. This is applied to answer some open questions from the work of Brendle, the first author, and Mejía [ BCM23 ] .

This paper is part of the consistency results concerning the cardinal characteristics associated with the strong measure zero ideal, specifically looking into its additivity number with respect to the bounding number and additivity number of the null ideal. We follow up on the study conducted in

The
\sigma

\mathcal{SN}
of the strong measure zero
subsets of the real line
\mathds{R}
(or of the Cantor space
2^{\omega}

Generally, these cardinals are characterized in the following manner: Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal of subsets of
X

\{x\}\in\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle\mathsf{add}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle=\min\left\{|\mathcal{J}|\;\middle|\;\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I}{,}\ \bigcup\mathcal{J}\notin\mathcal{I}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{cov}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle=\min\left\{|\mathcal{J}|\;\middle|\;\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I}{,}\ \bigcup\mathcal{J}=X\right\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{non}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle=\min\{|A|\mid\allowbreak\,A\subseteq X{,}\ A\notin\mathcal{I}\},\textrm{\ and}

\displaystyle\mathsf{cof}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle=\min\{|\mathcal{J}|\mid\allowbreak\,\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I}{,}\ \forall\,A\in\mathcal{I}\ \exists\,B\in\mathcal{J}\colon A\subseteq B\}.

Most of our notation is standard and compatible with classical textbooks on Set Theory (like
[
Kun80
,
Kec95
,
Jec03
,
Kun11
]
).

We say that
\mathsf{R}=\langle X,Y,{\sqsubset}\rangle
is a
relational system
if it consists of two non-empty sets
X
and
Y
and a relation
\sqsubset
.

(1)

A set
F\subseteq X
is
\mathsf{R}
-bounded
if
\exists y\in Y\,\forall x\in F\,\colon x\sqsubset y
.

(2)

A set
D\subseteq Y
is
\mathsf{R}
-dominating
if
\forall x\in X\,\exists y\in D\,\colon x\sqsubset y
.

We associate two cardinal characteristics with this relational system
R
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}(\mathsf{R})

\displaystyle:=\min\{|F|\mid\allowbreak\,\text{$F\subseteq X$ is $\mathsf{R}$-unbounded}\},

the
unbounding number of
R
, and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}(\mathsf{R})

\displaystyle:=\min\{|D|\mid\allowbreak\,\text{$D\subseteq Y$ is $\mathsf{R}$-dominating}\},

the
dominating number of
R
.

The dual of
\mathsf{R}
is defined by
\mathsf{R}^{\perp}:=\langle Y,X,{\sqsubset^{\perp}}\rangle
where
y\sqsubset^{\perp}x
iff
x\not\sqsubset y
. Note that
\mathfrak{b}(\mathsf{R}^{\perp})=\mathfrak{d}(\mathsf{R})
and
\mathfrak{d}(R^{\perp})=\mathfrak{b}(\mathsf{R})
.

The cardinal
\mathfrak{b}(\mathsf{R})
may be undefined, in which case we write
\mathfrak{b}(\mathsf{R})=\infty
, as well as for
\mathfrak{d}(\mathsf{R})
. Concretely,
\mathfrak{b}(\mathsf{R})=\infty
iff
\mathfrak{d}(\mathsf{R})=1
; and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathsf{R})=\infty
iff
\mathfrak{b}(\mathsf{R})=1
.

As in
[
GKMS22
,
CM22
]
, we also look at relational systems given by directed preorders.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
\langle S,{\leq_{S}}\rangle
is a
directed preorder
if it is a preorder (that is,
\leq_{S}
is a reflexive and transitive relation in
S
) such that

\forall\,x,y\in S\ \exists\,z\in S\colon x\leq_{S}z\text{ and }y\leq_{S}z.

A directed preorder
\langle S,{\leq_{S}}\rangle
is seen as the relational system
S=\langle S,S,{\leq_{S}}\rangle
, and their associated cardinal characteristics are indicated by
\mathfrak{b}(S)
and
\mathfrak{d}(S)
. The cardinal
\mathfrak{d}(S)
is actually the
cofinality of
S
, typically denoted by
\mathsf{cof}(S)
or
\mbox{\rm cf}(S)
.

Note that
\leq^{*}
is a directed preorder on
\omega^{\omega}
, where
x\leq^{*}y
means
\forall^{\infty}n<\omega\colon x(n)\leq y(n)
. We think of
\omega^{\omega}
as the relational system with the relation
\leq^{*}
. Then
\mathfrak{b}:=\mathfrak{b}(\omega^{\omega})
and
\mathfrak{d}:=\mathfrak{d}(\omega^{\omega})
.

The Tukey connection is a useful tool for establishing relationships between cardinal characteristics. Let
\mathsf{R}=\langle X,Y,{\sqsubset}\rangle
and
\mathsf{R}^{\prime}=\langle X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},{\sqsubset^{\prime}}\rangle
be two relational systems. We say that

(\Psi_{-},\Psi_{+})\colon\mathsf{R}\to\mathsf{R}^{\prime}

is a
Tukey connection from
\mathsf{R}
into
\mathsf{R}^{\prime}
if

\Psi_{-}\colon X\to X^{\prime}
and
\Psi_{+}\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
are functions such that

\forall x\in X\,\forall y^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}\colon\Psi_{-}(x)\sqsubset^{\prime}y^{\prime}\Rightarrow x\sqsubset\Psi_{+}(y^{\prime}).

The
Tukey order
between relational systems is defined by

\mathsf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathsf{R}^{\prime}
iff there is a Tukey connection from
\mathsf{R}
into
\mathsf{R}^{\prime}
.
Tukey equivalence
is defined by
\mathsf{R}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathsf{R}^{\prime}
iff
\mathsf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathsf{R}^{\prime}
and
\mathsf{R}^{\prime}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathsf{R}
. Notice that
\mathsf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathsf{R}^{\prime}
implies
(\mathsf{R}^{\prime})^{\perp}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathsf{R}^{\perp}
,
\mathfrak{d}(\mathsf{R})\leq\mathfrak{d}(\mathsf{R}^{\prime})
and
\mathfrak{b}(\mathsf{R}^{\prime})\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathsf{R})
.

Definition 3.1

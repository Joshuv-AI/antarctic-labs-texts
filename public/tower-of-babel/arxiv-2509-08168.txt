Pathological solutions of Navier-Stokes equations on 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2} with gradients in Hardy spaces
Jan Burczak, Antonio Hidalgo-Torné

Abstract
For an arbitrary smooth initial datum, we construct multiple nonzero solutions to the 2 2 d Navier-Stokes equations, with their gradients in the Hardy space ℋ p \mathcal{H}^{p} with any p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) . Thus, in terms of the path space C ⁡ ( ℋ p ) C(\mathcal{H}^{p}) for vorticity, p = 1 p=1 is the threshold value distinguishing between non-uniqueness and uniqueness regimes. In order to obtain our result, we develop the needed theory of Hardy spaces on periodic domains.

Preliminaries

In order to decrease the error
R_{0}

we will modulate a function on

As usual in convex integration schemes, we will invoke fast oscillations to decrease error. Therefore, for

and

, we will consider the fast-oscillating function

The concrete realisations of the concentrated, fast oscillation function will be presented when we introduce our building blocks.

Appendix A
Real Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}

Since we could not find a suitable reference for Hardy spaces on

The properties of

(expected in view of the analogy between

with

From now on, if the domain is not stated explicitly, it is
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, Also, to simplify the exposition, we fix

with periodic boundary conditions.

A.1
Definition of
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})

We will define Hardy spaces using maximal functions. To this end, one introduces a rescaling parameter for a test function, which is more natural in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We understand the convolution between a function
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
as the convolution in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
\Psi
and
{f^{\text{ext}}}
, which is the periodic extension of
f
. To avoid confusion, we denote by
*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
the convolution between periodic functions, by
*_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
the convolution between functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and by
*
the above mentioned convolution of
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, i.e.
\Psi*f:=\Psi*_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{f^{\text{ext}}}
.

Since the resulting function
\Psi*f
inherits the period of

, we treat it as a periodic function (without renaming).

Definition A.1

(Maximal function)
.

Let
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\Psi(x)dx\neq 0
. For each
0<\zeta<\infty
, let
{\Psi}_{\zeta}(x)=\zeta^{-n}\Psi(\frac{x}{\zeta})
. Given
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, we define the maximal function

m_{\Psi}f(x)=\sup_{0<\zeta<\infty}|f*\Psi_{\zeta}(x)|.

Definition A.2

(Hardy space)
.

Let
0<p<\infty
. The real Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{p}
is defined as

\mathcal{H}^{p}=\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n}):m_{\Psi}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\},

and we write

\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}=\left|\left|m_{\Psi}f\right|\right|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}.

Observe that, since in case
p\in(0,1)
one has merely
(a+b)^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(a^{p}+b^{p})
,
\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}
is only a quasinorm.

f_{n}=\tilde{a}_{n+1}-\tilde{a}_{n},

observe that
\sum\left|\left|f_{n}\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}<\infty
. Since the partial sums of this series are Cauchy in
\mathcal{H}^{p}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, the series converges in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
to a distribution
f=a-\tilde{a}_{0}
.
We have

\left(m_{\Psi}f(x)\right)^{p}=\left(m_{\Psi}\sum f_{n}(x)\right)^{p}\leq(\sum m_{\Psi}f_{n}(x))^{p}\leq\sum(m_{\Psi}f_{n}(x))^{p}

because
p\leq 1
,
and integrating it we obtain

\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}\leq\sum\left|\left|f_{n}\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}<\infty,

so
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p}
, and
a=f+\tilde{a}_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{p}
.
∎

A.3
Atomic decomposition

A different way to characterize Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}^{p}
is through atomic decomposition, which allows any function in
\mathcal{H}^{p}
to be expressed as a linear combination of ’atoms’. i.e. functions with well-controlled properties in terms of size, support, and cancellation. This characterization provides an effective tool for analyzing functions in
\mathcal{H}^{p}
, without relying on maximal function definitions.

In the atomic decomposition, the support of an atom plays an important role.
Since we are working simultaneously with periodic and non-periodic functions, we have naturally identified (a function on)
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with its periodic extension, i.e. with (function on)
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with periodicity.
However, in what follows, instead of working with infinite copies (of the same function), it will be sometimes convenient to work with a single representative. This motivates the following notation, which formalises the choosing of the copy closest to the origin, while keeping its support connected.

Definition A.7
.

Let
f
be a periodic function supported on a ball
B_{f}\subsetneq\mathbb{T}^{n}
. We denote by
\overline{B}_{f}
the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
(\cup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\overline{B}_{f})/\mathbb{Z}^{n}\equiv B_{f}\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}
, and such that its origin is closest to
0
. If this choice is not yet unique, we choose the ball whose origin has more coordinates nonnegative. If
\supp f=\mathbb{T}^{n}
, we set
\bar{B}_{f}=B(0,\sqrt{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We identify
f
with its representative supported on
\overline{B}_{f}
. We say that the ball
\overline{B}_{f}
is associated to
f
.

Let us remark that, according to the above definition, we understand that any periodic function
f
is supported on a periodic ball, and we can always choose a representative ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that contains all the information of
f
(see Figure
1
).

Definition A.8
.

We say that
B
is a small ball if
|{B}|<C_{at}
, for a fixed constant
C_{at}
small enough. In the following, we will take
C_{at}=\frac{1}{10}
.

As in the case of
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, cf
[
20
]
, we require that
\mathcal{H}^{p}
-atoms supported in small balls integrate to
0
, when tested with polynomials (up to certain degree). Observe that for periodic functions supported on small balls, testing them with any polynomial
p
is well-defined. Furthermore, in such case, if for
f
supported on
{\bar{B}_{f}}
we have
\int_{\bar{B}_{f}}p(x)f=0
, then
\int_{\bar{B}_{f}+x_{0}}p(x)f=0
, so periodicity is not relevant.

Keeping in mind the above identification, we introduce

Definition A.9

(
\mathcal{H}^{p}
-atoms)
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
be a measurable function, and
\bar{B}_{a}
its representative ball according to Definition
A.7
. We say that
a
is an
\mathcal{H}^{p}
-atom associated to
\bar{B}_{a}
if:

1.

\left|\left|{a}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}}\leq|\bar{B}_{a}|^{-\frac{1}{p}}
.

2.

If
\bar{B}_{a}
is a small ball,
\int_{\overline{B}_{a}}x^{\beta}{a}=0
for all multiindices
\beta
with
|\beta|\leq n(p^{-1}-1)
.

Observe that one of the advantages of choosing the representative as in Definition
A.7
is that we can write the point 2. of Definition
A.9
, while writing
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}x^{\beta}a
is problematic, because
x
is not periodic.

Proposition A.10
.

There is a uniform constant
C
, such that for all
\mathcal{H}^{p}
-atoms
a
it holds
\left|\left|a\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}\leq C
(where the constant may depend on
d,n,\Psi
).

Proof.

To simplify the exposition, we assume without loss of generality that
\Psi
is supported on the unit ball.

Take an arbitrary
\mathcal{H}^{p}
-atom
a
, and let
\bar{B}_{a}
be the associated ball according to Definition
A.7
.
Using the property 1. of Definition
A.9
, and the fact that maximal function is pointwisely bounded by the
L^{\infty}
norm, we have

m_{\Psi}a(x)\leq|\bar{B}_{a}|^{-\frac{1}{p}}.

(29)

(i) Assume first that the ball
\bar{B}_{a}
is not a small ball. Using the bound (
29
) and the fact that
|\bar{B}_{a}|\geq C_{at}
, we have

\left|\left|a\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(m_{\Psi}a(x))^{p}\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\bar{B}_{a}|^{-1}\leq C_{at}^{-{1}}.

(30)

(ii) We assume now that
\bar{B}_{a}
is a small ball, and we denote its center by
\bar{x}
. We define the hypercube
D=[-1/2,1/2]^{n}+\bar{x}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see Figure
1
).

Figure 1:
Picture of our domain. The periodic function
a
is supported on the periodic ball denoted by circles. We select as representative the ball
\bar{B}_{a}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and we work on the domain
D
, whose borders are marked with a dashed line. The vertical lines correspond to
x_{1}=\pm\frac{1}{2}
, and the horizontal lines correspond to
x_{2}=\pm\frac{1}{2}
.

We will use the bound (
29
)
for
x\in\bar{B}_{a}^{*}
, where
\bar{B}_{a}^{*}
has the same center as
\bar{B}_{a}
and twice its radius. To deal with
x\in D\setminus B_{a}^{*}
, we consider two cases.

(ii.1) Firstly, we treat the case
\zeta\leq\frac{1}{4}
. Since
\zeta
and
|\bar{B}_{a}|
are small enough, the support of
\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)
intersects only
\bar{B}_{a}
in the set of balls
\{\bar{B}_{a}+k,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
. Thus

(a*\Psi_{\zeta})(x)=\int_{\bar{B}_{a}}a(y)\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)dy.

We use the cancellation of moments (Property 3. of Definition
A.9
) to write

\int_{\overline{B}_{a}}a(y)\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)dy=\int_{\overline{B}_{a}}a(y)[\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)-P_{x,\Psi_{\zeta},d}(y)]dy,

where
P_{x,\Psi_{\zeta},d}
is the degree
d=\lfloor n(\frac{1}{p}-1)\rfloor
Taylor polynomial of the function
y\mapsto\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)
expanded over
\bar{x}
, the center of
\overline{B}_{a}
. By properties of the Taylor expansion, we have

|\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)-P_{x,\Psi_{\zeta},d}(y)|\lesssim\frac{|y-\bar{x}|^{d+1}}{\zeta^{d+1+n}}.

Let
r
be the radius of
\overline{B}_{a}
.
Recall
y\in\overline{B}_{a},x\not\in\overline{B}_{a}^{*}
.
In case
|x-y|\leq\zeta
, which means in particular that
r\leq\zeta
, we have

|x-\bar{x}|\leq|x-y|+|\bar{x}-y|\leq\zeta+r\leq 2\zeta
. In case
|x-y|>\zeta
we have
\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)=0
.
Therefore we have for any
x\in D\setminus\overline{B}_{a}^{*}
and any
\zeta<\frac{1}{4}

|(a*\Psi_{\zeta})(x)|=\left|\int_{\overline{B}_{a}}a(y)[\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)-P_{x,\Psi_{\zeta},d}(y)]dy\right|\lesssim\left|\left|{a}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\int_{\overline{B}_{a}}\frac{r^{d+1}}{|x-\bar{x}|^{d+1+n}}dy\right|,

which gives via Property 2. of Definition
A.9

\sup_{0<\zeta<\frac{1}{4}}|a*\Psi_{\zeta}(x)|\lesssim r^{-\frac{n}{p}}\frac{r^{d+1+n}}{|x-\bar{x}|^{d+1+n}}.

Altogether, we have, using that
d=\lfloor n(\frac{1}{p}-1)\rfloor>n(\frac{1}{p}-1)-1

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\sup_{0<\zeta<\frac{1}{4}}|a*\Psi_{\zeta}(x)|^{p}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{B}_{a}^{*}}(m_{\Psi}a(x))^{p}dx+\int_{D\setminus\bar{B}_{a}^{*}}\sup_{0<\zeta<\frac{1}{4}}|a*\Psi_{\zeta}(x)|^{p}dx

(31)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{B}_{a}^{*}}|\bar{B}_{a}|^{-1}+\int_{2r}^{\sqrt{n}}r^{-n}\frac{r^{p(d+1+n)}}{z^{p(d+1+n)-(n-1)}}dz\lesssim 1.

(ii.2) We study now the case
x\in D\setminus\bar{B}_{a}^{*}
,
\zeta\geq\frac{1}{4}
. We note that, for any fixed
x
, the support of
\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)
may intersect more than one ball in the set of balls
\{\bar{B}_{a}+k,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
. We denote
K_{x}
as the set of
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
(\supp\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y))\cap(\bar{B}_{a}+k)\neq\emptyset
.
Then
|K_{x}|\lesssim\zeta^{n}
. We write

(a*\Psi_{\zeta})(x)=\sum_{k\in K_{x}}\int_{\bar{B}_{a}+k}a(y)\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)dy=\sum_{k\in K_{x}}\int_{\bar{B}_{a}+k}a(y)[\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)-P^{k}_{x,\Psi_{\zeta},d}(y)]dy,

where
P^{k}_{x,\Psi_{\zeta},d}
is the degree
d=\lfloor n(\frac{1}{p}-1)\rfloor
Taylor polynomial of the function
y\mapsto\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)
expanded over
\bar{x}+k
, the center of
\bar{B}_{a}+k
. By properties of the Taylor expansion, we have

|\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)-P^{k}_{x,\Psi_{\zeta},d}(y)|\lesssim\frac{|y-\bar{x}|^{d+1}}{\zeta^{d+1+n}}.

We bound

|(a*\Psi_{\zeta})(x)|\lesssim\zeta^{n}r^{-\frac{n}{p}}r^{d+1+n}\zeta^{-(d+1+n)}\lesssim 1,

since
r<1,-\frac{n}{p}+d+1+n\geq 0
and
\zeta\geq\frac{1}{4},n-d-1-n\leq 0
. This implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\sup_{\frac{1}{4}<\zeta<\infty}|a*\Psi_{\zeta}(x)|^{p}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{B}_{a}^{*}}(m_{\Psi}a(x))^{p}dx+\int_{D\setminus\bar{B}_{a}^{*}}\sup_{\frac{1}{4}<\zeta<\infty}|a*\Psi_{\zeta}(x)|^{p}dx

(32)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{B}_{a}^{*}}|\bar{B}_{a}|^{-1}+\int_{D\setminus\bar{B}_{a}^{*}}1dx\lesssim 1.

From
\eqref{eq:Hpboundbigatoms},\eqref{eq:Hpboundsmallatomlocalmax}
and
(
32
)
, we conclude

\displaystyle\left|\left|a\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim 1.

∎

Proposition A.11
.

If
\{a_{k}\}
is a collection of
\mathcal{H}^{p}
atoms and
\{\lambda_{k}\}
is a sequence of numbers with
\sum|\lambda_{k}|^{p}<\infty,
then the series

f=\sum a_{k}\lambda_{k}

converges in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, and its sum belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{p}
, with
\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}\leq c(\sum\lambda_{k}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

Proof.

If the sum is finite, we have

m_{\Psi}f\leq\sum|\lambda_{k}|m_{\psi}a_{k},

and

\Big(\sum|\lambda_{k}|m_{\psi}a_{k}\Big)^{p}\leq\sum|\lambda_{k}|^{p}(m_{\Psi}a_{k})^{p},

since
0<p\leq 1
. Integrating these inequalities and using Proposition
A.10
, we obtain

\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}\leq c(\sum\lambda_{k}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This shows the unconditional convergence of the series in the
\mathcal{H}^{p}
metric, thus also in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.
∎

Corollary A.12
.

A function
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
satisfying condition
2
in Definition
A.9
, satisfies the bound

\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}\leq C|\bar{B}_{f}|^{\frac{1}{p}}\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}}.

Proof.

Taking
\lambda=\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}}|\bar{B}_{f}|^{\frac{1}{p}},
it is clear that
\lambda^{-1}f
is an atom. Therefore, we can apply Proposition
A.11
to
f=(\lambda^{-1}f)\lambda
, giving the bound

\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}\lesssim\lambda=\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}}|\bar{B}_{f}|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

∎

We are interested now in bounding the
\mathcal{H}^{p}
norm of a concentrated function not satisfying
2
in Definition
A.9
. One can do it straightforwardly by using the embedding
\mathcal{H}^{p}\subset L^{q}
for any
0<p\leq 1<q
, but the resulting bound turns out to be insufficient for our purposes.

Instead, we will correct locally the moments of the function, without increasing its support, and consequently obtain better bounds.

For this, we want to be able to reproduce any value of a moment with a function.
Let
\alpha
be a multiindex,
|\alpha|\leq N
. For a compactly supported function
f
, we denote its
\alpha
-moment by
m_{\alpha}
, i.e.
m_{\alpha}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}f(x)dx
. Denote
|m|:=\max_{|\alpha|\leq N}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}f(x)dx\right|.
Observe that the set
\{m_{\alpha}|\;|\alpha|\leq N\}=\mathbb{R}^{{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}N+n\\
n\end{smallmatrix}\right)}}
.
Let us recall
[
5
, Lemma 3.11]

Lemma A.13
.

For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a degree
N
polynomial
p_{N}
on
\mathbb{R}
such that the following holds. For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there is a linear operator
m\to L_{\varepsilon,\bar{x},m}
that maps each choice of order
\leq N
moments
m\in\mathbb{R}^{{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}N+n\\
n\end{smallmatrix}\right)}}
to a function
L_{\varepsilon,\bar{x},m}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(\bar{x},\varepsilon))
,
so that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}L_{\varepsilon,\bar{x},m}(x)dx=m_{\alpha}\text{ for any multiindex }|\alpha|\leq N,

and

\left|\left|L_{\varepsilon,\bar{x},m}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{p_{N}(|\bar{x}|)}{\varepsilon^{n+N}}|m|.

Now we can control the
\mathcal{H}^{p}
-norm of concentrated functions not satisfying
2
in Definition
A.9
by using the following proposition. It is a ’periodic version’ of
[
5
, Proposition 3.9]
.

Proposition A.14
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
. Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
be a function, and
\bar{B}_{f}
its representative ball according to Definition
A.7
. Assume that
\bar{B}_{f}
has center
\bar{x}
and radius
\varepsilon
, with
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
. Let
N=\lfloor n(\frac{1}{p}-1)\rfloor
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}\leq

\displaystyle C(p,n)\big(\varepsilon^{n}\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}+|\log\varepsilon|\max_{|\alpha|\leq N}\left|\int_{\bar{B}_{f}}x^{\alpha}f^{\text{ext}}(x)dx\right|^{p}\big).

Proof.

The idea is to add a local corrector
L
to
f
so that it satisfies the condition
2
in Definition
A.9
. We will do it in one period centered at
\bar{x}
, and extend the correction periodically.
We choose a family of shrinking radii

\varepsilon_{i}=2^{-i}\text{ for }1\leq i<K,

where
K=-\lfloor\log_{2}(\varepsilon)\rfloor\lesssim|\log\varepsilon|
, and we set
\varepsilon_{K}=\varepsilon
, so that

\frac{1}{2}=\varepsilon_{1}>\varepsilon_{2}>\dots>\varepsilon_{K}=\varepsilon.

Using the functions from Lemma
A.13
, we decompose

\displaystyle f=f-L_{\varepsilon,\bar{x},m}+\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}L_{\varepsilon_{i+1},\bar{x},m}-L_{\varepsilon_{i},\bar{x},m}+L_{\varepsilon_{1},\bar{x},m},

and we extend this decomposition periodically. We use Corollary
A.12
and Lemma
A.13
to estimate

\left|\left|f-L_{\varepsilon,\bar{x},m}\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim\varepsilon^{n}\left(\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n+N}}|m|\right)^{p}\lesssim\varepsilon^{n}\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}+|m|^{p},

since
\varepsilon<1
and
N\leq n(p^{-1}-1)\Rightarrow p(n+N)\leq n
.
We use again Corollary
A.12
and Lemma
A.13
to estimate

\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\left|\left|L_{\varepsilon_{i+1},\bar{x},m}-L_{\varepsilon_{i},\bar{x},m}\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\frac{2^{-in}}{2^{-(i+1)p(n+N)}}|m|^{p}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}2^{-i(n-p(n+N))}|m|^{p}.

We note that the power of
2
in the previous series is always negative. Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\left|\left|L_{\varepsilon_{i+1},\bar{x},m}-L_{\varepsilon_{i},\bar{x},m}\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim K|m|^{p}\lesssim|\log\varepsilon||m|^{p}.

Finally,

\left|\left|L_{\varepsilon_{1},\bar{x},m}\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim\left|\left|L_{\varepsilon_{1},\bar{x},m}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}\lesssim|m|^{p},

concluding the proof.
∎

Proposition A.15
.

Every
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p}
can be written as
f=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}a_{k}
, where
a_{k}
are
\mathcal{H}^{p}
atoms, and
\lambda_{k}
are real numbers. The sum converges to
f
strongly in
\mathcal{H}^{p}
, and

\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|^{p}\lesssim\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}.

(33)

Proof.

The idea of the proof for the analogous result in
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

[
23
]
is to use the Whitney covering lemma for each set
O^{j}=\{x:\mathcal{M}f>2^{j}\}
, and decompose
f=g_{j}+b_{j}
, with
\left|\left|g_{j}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c2^{j}
, and
b_{j}
supported on
O^{j}
and having the property
2
in Definition
A.9
. Then, it is observed that
\lim_{j\to\infty}g_{j}=f
in
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and an atomic decomposition of
\sum_{j}g_{j+1}-g_{j}
is constructed. Lastly, equation (
33
) is checked.
A complete proof in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be found in
[
23
]
. The proof for
\mathcal{H}^{p}\cap L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
functions can be found in pages
101-105
together with
107-109
. The proof for a general
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
element is completed in pages
110-111
.

To adapt the proof from
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\mathcal{H}^{p}
, we observe that we can decompose
f=g_{j}+b_{j}
with the same procedure if
|O^{j}|<C
, for
C>0
small enough. Since
|O^{j}|
is decreasing and converging to
0
, it is enough to fix
j_{0}
such that
|O^{j_{0}}|<C
. We choose the minimum possible value of
j_{0}
. Then, we can write

f=g_{j_{0}}+\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}g_{j+1}-g_{j}.

To obtain an atomic decomposition of
g_{j_{0}}
, we note that
c^{-1}2^{-j_{0}}g_{j_{0}}
is an atom. To keep the bound (
33
), we need to show that
2^{j_{0}}p\lesssim\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}
. Using that
j_{0}
is minimal, we observe

2^{j_{0}}p\leq|O^{j_{0}-1}|C^{-1}2^{p}2^{(j_{0}-1)p}\leq C^{-1}2^{p}\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}.

We can obain an atomic decomposition of
\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}g_{j+1}-g_{j}
satisfying (
33
) proceeding as in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Combined with the decomposition of
g_{j_{0}}
, we have an atomic decomposition of
f
.

∎

A.4
Proof of Proposition
3.9

Fix any function
\varphi\in L^{2}
with
\left|\left|\varphi\right|\right|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
. It can be written as
\varphi=\curl g_{1}+\nabla g_{2}
for some
g_{1},g_{2}\in H^{1}
, with
\left|\left|g_{1}\right|\right|_{H^{1}},\left|\left|g_{2}\right|\right|_{H^{1}}\lesssim 1
.

In the sense of distributions,

|\langle f,\varphi\rangle|\leq|\langle f,\curl g_{1}\rangle|+|\langle f,\nabla g_{2}\rangle|=|\langle\curl f,g_{1}\rangle|.

In two dimensions
H^{1}\hookrightarrow(\mathcal{H}^{1})^{*}
, so

|\langle f,\varphi\rangle|\lesssim\left|\left|\curl f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}.

Since
\varphi
was arbitrary, this shows that

\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left|\left|\curl f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}.

A.5
Singular Integral Operators

In this subsection, we show that singular integral operators are bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{p}
, as it occurs in
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since we are using simultaneously periodic and non-periodic functions, we begin by clarifying some aspects regarding the Fourier transform.
Given
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and its periodic version
{f^{\text{per}}}(\cdot)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f(\cdot+k)\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-i2\pi\xi\cdot x}f(x)dx,\quad

\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle\hat{{f}}^{\text{per}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}e^{-i2\pi\xi\cdot x}{f^{\text{per}}}(x)dx,\quad

\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Since
e^{-i2\pi\xi\cdot x}
is also periodic, it is direct that
\hat{f}(\xi)=\hat{{f}}^{\text{per}}(\xi)

\forall\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

We consider now a periodic function
g
,
whose periodic extension to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
g^{\text{ext}}
. The
\mathbb{R}^{n}
convolution
f*g^{\text{ext}}
is equal to the
\mathbb{T}^{n}
convolution
{f}^{\text{per}}*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}g
, so we have the identity

\displaystyle f*g^{\text{ext}}(x)={f}^{\text{per}}*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}g(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i2\pi\xi\cdot x}\hat{f}^{\text{per}}(\xi)\hat{{g}}(\xi)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i2\pi\xi\cdot x}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{{g}}(\xi).

(34)

Proposition A.17
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be the Leray projector,
0<p<\infty
, and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p}
. Then,

\left|\left|\mathbb{P}f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}\lesssim\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}.

Proof.

The case
1<p<\infty
is a classic result. We prove the case
0<p\leq 1.
Since
\mathbb{P}
is a linear operator, it is enough to prove that the Leray projector of an atom is bounded in
\mathcal{H}^{p}
.

We first prove the case where
a
is an atom supported on a big ball. Then, there exists
C
independent of
a
such that
a\leq C
, which implies

\displaystyle\left|\left|\mathbb{P}a\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(m_{\psi}\mathbb{P}a)^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(m_{\psi}\mathbb{P}a)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(\mathbb{P}a)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}a^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}a^{p}C^{2-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(m_{\psi}a)^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\left|\left|a\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{\frac{p}{2}}\lesssim 1.

We deal now with the case where
a
is an atom supported on a small ball. We adapt the proof of Theorem 5, case II of
[
18
]
. Using Remark
A.16
, we can assume that
a
satisfies property 2. in Definition
A.9
for any multiindex
|\beta|\leq n(p^{-1}+1)
. Besides, we can assume without loss of generality, that
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, and that its representative ball
\bar{B}_{a}
according to Definition
A.7
is centered at the origin. We denote the Fourier expansion of
a
by

a=-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\alpha_{k}e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}.

Thus, we have

\mathbb{P}a=-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus 0}\left(I-\frac{k\otimes k}{|k|^{2}}\right)\alpha_{k}e^{2\pi ik\cdot x},

where we have used that
\int a=0
.

We let

\Xi(x)\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-4x_{j}^{2})_{+},

where
2x_{+}=x+|x|
.
We denote
\Xi^{\frac{1}{N}}(x)=\Xi(x/N).
We denote by
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
the Leray projector on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We let

\mathcal{E}_{N}(x)=\Xi^{\frac{1}{N}}(x)(\Psi_{\zeta}*\mathbb{P}a)^{\text{ext}}(x)-\Psi_{\zeta}*\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Xi^{\frac{1}{N}}a^{\text{ext}})(x).

By checking the Fourier transform, we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{t}_{N}(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus 0}\alpha^{t}_{k}e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{\Xi}(\xi)

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\Psi}(\zeta(k+\frac{\xi}{N}))\left(I-\frac{(k+\frac{\xi}{N})\otimes(k+\frac{\xi}{N})}{|k+\frac{\xi}{N}|^{2}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle-\left.\hat{\Psi}(\zeta k)\left(I-\frac{k\otimes k}{|k|^{2}}\right)\right]^{t}e^{\frac{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}{N}}d\xi,

where
v^{t}
is the transpose of the vector or matrix
v
.
We fix
R>0
. Since
\alpha_{k}
decays rapidly, and the terms in
[\cdot]
are bounded and converging to
0
if
N\to\infty
,

\mathcal{E}_{N}(x)\to 0\quad\text{uniformly for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\zeta\in(0,R]$ as $N\to\infty.$}

(35)

For large
N
, we have

\displaystyle\|\sup_{0<\zeta\leq R}|\Psi_{\zeta}*\mathbb{P}a|\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{-n}\int_{[-N/2,N/2]^{n}}\sup_{0<\zeta\leq R}|\Xi^{\frac{1}{N}}(\Psi_{\zeta}*\mathbb{P}a(x))^{\text{ext}}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{0<\zeta\leq R}|\Psi_{\zeta}*\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Xi^{\frac{1}{N}}a^{\text{ext}})(x)|^{p}dx+o(1),

where we have used the property (
35
).
Using that
\left|\left|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\sup_{0<\zeta\leq R}|\Psi_{\zeta}*\mathbb{P}a|\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\lesssim N^{-n}\|\Xi^{\frac{1}{N}}a^{\text{ext}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}+o(1).

(36)

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\|\Xi^{\frac{1}{N}}a^{\text{ext}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{0<\zeta<\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-4x_{j}^{2}/N^{2})_{+}a^{\text{ext}}(x)\Psi_{\zeta}(y-x)dx\right|^{p}dy

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{0<\zeta<\infty}\left|\int_{|x_{j}|<N/2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-4x_{j}^{2}/N^{2})_{+}a^{\text{ext}}(x)\Psi_{\zeta}(y-x)dx\right|^{p}dy.

We choose
N=2m+1
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, up to a set of measure
0
, the set
\{|x_{j}|<N/2\}
is the union of the disjoint sets
Q_{k}=\{(-1/2,1/2)^{n}+k\},
where
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},|k_{j}|\leq m
. We can bound (
37
) by

\|\Xi^{\frac{1}{N}}a^{\text{ext}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{|k_{j}|\leq m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{0<\zeta<\infty}\left|\int_{Q_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-4x_{j}^{2}/N^{2})_{+}a^{\text{ext}}(x)\Psi_{\zeta}(y-x)dx\right|^{p}dy.

(38)

On
Q_{k}
,
\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-4x_{j}^{2}/N^{2})_{+}
is a polynomial of degree
2n
which is bounded by
1
. Therefore,
\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-4x_{j}^{2}/N^{2})_{+}a^{\text{ext}}(x)\mathbbm{1}_{Q_{k}}(x)
is an
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-atom, so

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{0<\zeta<\infty}\left|\int_{Q_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-4x_{j}^{2}/N^{2})_{+}a^{\text{ext}}(x)\Psi_{\zeta}(y-x)dx\right|^{p}dy\lesssim 1

for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},|k_{j}|\leq m
. We use (
38
) to bound

\|\Xi^{\frac{1}{N}}a^{\text{ext}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\lesssim N^{n},

showing that

\liminf_{N\to\infty}N^{-n}\|\Xi^{\frac{1}{N}}a^{\text{ext}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\lesssim 1
. We use (
36
) to bound

\|\sup_{0<\zeta\leq R}|\Psi_{\zeta}*\mathbb{P}a|\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\lesssim 1,

uniformly on
R
. We conclude

\left|\left|\mathbb{P}a\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}^{p}=\|\sup_{0<\zeta<\infty}|\Psi_{\zeta}*\mathbb{P}a|\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\lesssim 1.

∎

Remark A.18
.

Proposition
A.17
is the only result from this subsection that we need for the main part. From its proof, one can also deduce the boundedness in
\mathcal{H}^{p}
of other singular operators that are bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, provided their Fourier symbols are well controlled.

We now prove a complementary result, which may be of independent interest: it shows that the boundedness of singular operators can also be established by controlling their singularity in the physical variables.

Lemma A.19
.

Let
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
K
be a periodic function satisfying

\displaystyle|\hat{K}(\xi)|\lesssim

\displaystyle 1,\quad

\displaystyle\forall\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

(39)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{\beta}K(x)|\lesssim

\displaystyle|x|^{-n-|\beta|},\quad

\displaystyle\forall|\beta|\leq M,\quad x\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{n}.

Then, the kernel

K_{\zeta}=\Psi_{\zeta}*K

satisfies estimates (
39
), with bounds that depend on
\Psi
but can be chosen independent of
\zeta
.

Proof.

From the identity (
34
) and the assumption
|\hat{K}(\xi)|\lesssim 1
, it is enough to bound the Fourier coefficients of
\Psi_{\zeta}
. By scaling of the Fourier transform, we have

\widehat{\Psi_{\zeta}}(\xi)=\widehat{\Psi}(\zeta\xi),

so the property
|\widehat{K_{\zeta}}(\xi)|\lesssim 1
follows from the boundedness of
\widehat{\Psi}
.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\supp\Psi\subset B(0,1)
. We fix
x\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{n}
, which covers a whole period. Finally, we fix a multiindex
\beta
with
|\beta|\leq M
.

For
0<\zeta<\frac{|x|}{2}
, we bound

|\Psi_{\zeta}*\nabla^{\beta}K(x)|\leq\left|\left|\nabla^{\beta}K\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(B(x,\frac{|x|}{2}))}\lesssim|x|^{-n-|\beta|}.

For
|x|\lesssim\zeta
, we first observe

\nabla^{\beta}\Psi_{\zeta}*K(x)=\zeta^{-|\beta|}(\nabla^{\beta}\Psi)_{\zeta}*K(x),

and
\zeta^{-\beta}\lesssim|x|^{-\beta}
by assumption, so it is enough to prove the bound for
\beta=0
.
By (
34
), we have

\Psi_{\zeta}*K(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{i2\pi\xi x}\widehat{\Psi_{\zeta}}(\xi)\hat{K}({\xi}),

so

|\Psi_{\zeta}*K(x)|\lesssim\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{\Psi_{\zeta}}(\xi)|=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{\Psi}(\zeta\xi)|.

(40)

Since
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there is a function
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with radial symmetry and strictly decreasing such that

|\widehat{\Psi}(\xi)|\leq g(\xi),

giving

\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{\Psi}(\zeta\xi)|\leq\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}g(\zeta\xi)\lesssim g(0)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(\zeta\xi)d\xi=g(0)+\zeta^{-n}\|g\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim|x|^{-n}.

(41)

The use of the auxiliary function
g
is not strictly necessary, but its properties significantly simplify the proof of inequality
\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}g(\zeta\xi)\lesssim g(0)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(\zeta\xi)d\xi
.
∎

Proposition A.20
.

Let
0<p<1
and
K
be a periodic function satisfying

\displaystyle|\hat{K}(\xi)|\lesssim

\displaystyle 1,\quad

\displaystyle\forall\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

\displaystyle|\nabla^{\beta}K(x)|\lesssim

\displaystyle|x|^{-n-|\beta|},\quad

\displaystyle\forall|\beta|\leq\lfloor n(\frac{1}{p}-1)\rfloor+1,\quad x\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{n}.

Then, it holds

\left|\left|K*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}\lesssim\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}.

Proof.

By Proposition
A.15
, it is enough to show that
\left|\left|K*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}a\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}\lesssim\left|\left|a\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}}
for an atom
a
, with a constant independent of the atom.
We recall that both
m_{\Psi}
and
K*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
are bounded operators from
L^{2}
into itself.

If
a
is an atom supported on a big ball, we use Hölder inequality to bound

\int(m_{\Psi}(K*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}a))^{p}\leq\Big(\int(m_{\Psi}(K*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}a))^{2}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big(\int 1\Big)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\lesssim\Big(\int a^{2}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big(\int 1\Big)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\lesssim 1,

since atoms supported on big balls are bounded from above, and the domain has finite size.

We now assume that the atom
a
has a small ball
\bar{B}_{a}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
as its representative ball according to Definition
A.7
. We denote by
\bar{x}
the center of
\bar{B}_{a}
. As we did in the proof of Proposition
A.10
, we will work in the domain
D=[-1/2,1/2]^{n}+\bar{x}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see Figure
1
). We also denote by
\bar{B}^{*}_{a}
the ball with the same center as
\bar{B}_{a}
and twice its radius.

We split
D=\bar{B}^{*}_{a}\cup D\setminus\bar{B}^{*}_{a}
. In
\bar{B}^{*}_{a}
, we proceed as before to bound

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{B}^{*}_{a}}(m_{\Psi}(K*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}a))^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\bar{B}^{*}_{a}}(m_{\Psi}(K*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}a))^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big(\int_{\bar{B}^{*}_{a}}1\Big)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}

(42)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{\bar{B}^{*}_{a}}a^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big(\int_{\bar{B}^{*}_{a}}1\Big)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\leq(|\bar{B}^{*}_{a}||\bar{B}_{a}|^{-\frac{2}{p}})^{\frac{p}{2}}|\bar{B}^{*}_{a}|^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\lesssim 1.

For any
x\in D\setminus\bar{B}^{*}_{a}
, we have

\displaystyle K*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}a*\Psi_{\zeta}(x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)\Big(\int_{\bar{B}_{a}}K(y-z)a(z)dz\Big)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{B}_{a}}a(z)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)K(y-z)dy\Big)dz=\int_{\bar{B}_{a}}a(z)K_{\zeta}(x-z)dz,

where
K_{\zeta}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Psi_{\zeta}(x-y)K(y)dy
.

We denote
P_{x,\zeta}
the degree
d=\lfloor n(\frac{1}{p}-1)\rfloor
Taylor polynomial of the function
z\mapsto K_{\zeta}(x-z)
expanded at
\bar{x}
. Because of property
2
. in Definition
A.9
,we have

\displaystyle K*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}a*\Psi_{\zeta}(x)=

\displaystyle\int_{{\overline{B}_{a}}}a(z)(K_{\zeta}(x-z)-P_{x,\zeta}(z))dz.

Since
z\in\bar{B}_{a}
and
x\in D\setminus\bar{B}^{*}_{a}
, by properties of the Taylor expansion and Lemma
A.19
, we have

|K_{\zeta}(x-z)-P_{x,\zeta}(z)|\lesssim\frac{|z-\bar{x}|^{d+1}}{|x-\bar{x}|^{d+1+n}}.

Denoting as
r
the radius of
\bar{B}_{a}
and using
|a|\lesssim r^{-\frac{n}{p}}
, we have

\displaystyle|K*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}a*\Psi_{\zeta}(x)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{{\overline{B}_{a}}}r^{-\frac{n}{p}}\frac{|z-\bar{x}|^{d+1}}{|x-\bar{x}|^{d+1+n}}dz\lesssim\int_{{\overline{B}_{a}}}r^{-\frac{n}{p}}\frac{r^{d+1}}{|x|^{d+1+n}}dz\lesssim\frac{r^{-\frac{n}{p}+d+1+n}}{|x|^{d+1+n}}

uniformly on
\zeta
, and therefore

\displaystyle\int_{D\setminus\bar{B}^{*}_{a}}(m_{\Psi}(K*_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}a))^{p}\lesssim r^{p(-\frac{n}{p}+d+1+n)}\int_{r}^{\sqrt{n}}\frac{|x|^{n-1}}{|x|^{p(d+1+n)}}d|x|\lesssim r^{p(-\frac{n}{p}+d+1+n)}\left.\frac{r^{n}}{r^{p(d+1+n)}}\right.\lesssim 1,

(43)

where we used that
n-p(d+1+n)<0
.
Combining (
42
) and (
43
), we conclude the case for atoms supported on small balls.
∎

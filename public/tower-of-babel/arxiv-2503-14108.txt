1.
Introduction and Statement of Main Results

The Falconer distance conjecture (
[
1
]
) says that if the Hausdorff dimension of
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, is greater than
\frac{d}{2}
, then the Lebesgue measure of the distance set
\Delta(E)=\{|x-y|:x,y\in E\}
is positive.

Recent celebrated results
[
8
,
9
,
17
]
show that for every
compact subset
E
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
, the Lebesgue measure of the
distance set
\Delta(E)=\{|x-y|:x,y\in E\}
is positive if the
Hausdorff dimension of
E
satisfies

{\rm dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>{d\over 2}+{1\over 4}
when
d
is even, and
{\rm dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>{d\over 2}+{1\over 4}+{1\over 8d-4}
when
d
is odd.

This improved the well-known result by Wolff
[
2
]
in two dimensions and Erdogan
[
3
]
in higher
dimensions.

Recall that Falconer distance problem on Cartesian product sets
was studied by Iosevich and Liu (
[
5
]
) via Mattila integral,
which states as follows.

Theorem A
(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
E=A\times B
, where
A
and
B

are compact subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}
with positive
s_{A},s_{B}
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, respectively. If
s_{A}+s_{B}+\max(s_{A},s_{B})>2
, the Lebesgue measure of

\Delta(E)
is positive.

Theorem B
(
[
5
]
)
.

Suppose that
E
is a compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of the form
A_{1}\times A_{2}\times\dots\times A_{d}
, where
A_{j}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
has positive
s_{j}
-dimensional Hausdorff measure for all
1\leq j\leq d
. Suppose that
\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}>\frac{d^{2}}{2d-1}
. Then the Lebesgue measure of
\Delta(E)
is positive.

Their result improved Erdogan’s
\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{3}
exponent in higher dimension for Cartesian products. Note also that they studied the pinned version of Falconer distance problem

[
6
]
.

In this paper, we study the pinned version of Falconer distance problem
for Cartesian product sets
by implementing a new observation on Cartesian product sets
associated with a particular parabolic structure.
We improve the threshold for the Falconer distance set of product sets
A_{1}\times A_{2}\times\dots\times A_{d}
in Theorem
B
in certain case.

We now state our main results in details.

In what follows, for any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
x\in E
, we define
\triangle_{x}(E)
to be the pinned version of the distance set, that is

\displaystyle\triangle_{x}(E):=\left\{|x-y|:y\in E\right\}.

Moreover, for a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, we denote
A^{d}=A\times\cdots\times A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}
be compact subsets and
d\geq 3
.
1. If one of the following conditions holds

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)\geq\frac{21d^{2}-71d+21\beta\cdot d+29}{21d(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\frac{16d-16}{3d(d-2)}\cdot\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A\cap B)>1+\frac{3d\beta+4d-4}{3d(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)>1+\frac{\beta d-2d+2}{d(d-2)},\end{cases}

then there is a point
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})\in B^{d}
such that the Hausdorff dimension of pinned distance set of the product
\triangle_{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})}(A^{d})
is no less than
\beta
, where
A^{d}
is the product set of
A
.

2.
If one of the following conditions holds

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)\geq\frac{21d^{2}-29d+29}{21d(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\frac{16d-16}{3d(d-2)}\cdot\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A\cap B)>1+\frac{10d-4}{3d(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)>1+\frac{2}{d(d-2)},\end{cases}

then there is
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})\in B^{d}
such that
\triangle_{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})}(A^{d})
contains an non-empty interval, where
A^{d}
is the product set of
A
.

3.
If one of the following conditions holds

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)\geq\frac{(21d-29)(d-1)}{21d(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\frac{16d-16}{3d(d-2)}\cdot\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A\cap B)>1+\frac{7d-4}{3d(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)>1-\frac{1}{d},\end{cases}

then there is
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})\in B^{d}
such that
\triangle_{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})}(A^{d})
has positive Lebesgue measure, where
A^{d}
is the product set of
A
.

Note that our main theorem implies the following
special case.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact subset and
d\geq 3
.
1. If one of the following conditions holds

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\geq\frac{21d^{2}-71d+21\beta\cdot d+29}{42(d-1)(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{3d(d-2+\beta)}{(d-1)(6d+4)}+\frac{2}{3d+2}\end{cases}

or

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{d^{2}-4d+\beta d+2}{(d-2)(2d-2)},\end{cases}

then there is
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})\in A^{d}
such that the Hausdorff dimension of pinned distance set of the product
\triangle_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d})
is no less than
\beta
, where
A^{d}
is the product set of
A
.

2.
If one of the following conditions holds

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{34d-13}{42(d-1)(d-2)}\end{cases},\text{or}\,\,\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5d-2}{(d-1)(6d+4)}\end{cases}

or

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d}{(d-2)(2d-2)},\end{cases}

then there is
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})\in A^{d}
such that
\triangle_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d})
contains an non-empty interval, where
A^{d}
is the product set of
A
.

3.
If one of the following conditions holds

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{13}{42(d-2)}\end{cases},\text{or}\,\,\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3d+2}\end{cases}

or

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

then there is
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})\in A^{d}
such that
\triangle_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d})
has positive Lebesgue measure, where
A^{d}
is the product set of
A
.

Based on part 3 in the Corollary
1.1
on the pinned version of the distance set, we improve the threshold for the Falconer distance set of product sets
A_{1}\times A_{2}\times\dots\times A_{d}
in
[
5
]
in certain case: the Hausdorff dimensions
s_{d-1}
and
s_{d}

of
A_{d-1}
and
A_{d}
, respectively, satisfying

s_{d-1}+s_{d}>\frac{5}{4}
.
We state this in details in below.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 3
. Suppose that
E
is a compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of the form
A_{1}\times A_{2}\times\dots\times A_{d}
, where
A_{j}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
has positive
s_{j}
-dimensional Hausdorff measure for all
1\leq j\leq d
. Suppose that
\sum_{j=1}^{d-2}s_{j}>\frac{d}{2}-1
and
s_{d-1}+s_{d}>\frac{5}{4}
. Then there is a point
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})\in E
such that the Lebesgue measure of the pinned distance set
\Delta_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(E)
is positive.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that Iosevich and Liu (
[
6
]
)
studied the pinned version of distance set
for general set
E,F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Our results improve the threshold obtained by
[
6
]
when applying their results to
E=A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{d}
and
F=B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{d}
. In fact, one can see from our proofs below that as long as one can prove a variant of distance result for the particular parabolic distance, one can improve the result for the classical distance for product sets.

Besides, there are other variant distance problems related to the classical Falconer distance set. (see for example
[
4
,
11
,
13
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
18
]
) Our technique can be also applied to improve the threshold for those distance problems in the case of Cartesian product sets.

2.
Proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.1
: Parabolic Attack

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function that satisfies
Phong–Stein rotation curvature condition
and
Sogge’s cinematic curvature condition
, that is

\Phi
has a nonzero Monge–Ampere determinant

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}0&\quad\nabla_{x}\Phi\\
\nabla_{y}\Phi&\quad\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial x\partial y}\\[5.0pt]
\end{pmatrix}\neq 0

and for any
t>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\left\{\nabla_{y}\Phi:\Phi(x,y)=t\right\}
has nonzero Gaussian curvature. In particular, the parabolic distance
\Phi(x,y):=(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(x_{d-1}-y_{d-1})^{2}+(x_{d}-y_{d})
satisfies both curvature conditions.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
E,F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that
\Phi
satisfies the Phong–Stein rotation curvature condition and the cinematic curvature condition. Then there is a probability measure
\mu_{F}
on
F
such that for
\mu_{F}
-a.e.
y\in F
,

\displaystyle(1)\,\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E))\geq\beta,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-1}{d+1}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-1+\beta,

\displaystyle(2)\,\,\left|\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)\right|>0,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-1}{d+1}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d,

\displaystyle(3)\,\,\left(\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)\right)^{0}\neq\emptyset,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-1}{d+1}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d+1.

We then have a direct corollary from Theorem
2.1
as follows when applying their
E
and
F
to product sets.

Corollary 2.1
.

In particular, if
E=F:=A\times\cdots\times A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is a compact subset. Then there is a probability measure
\mu_{F}
on
F
such that for
\mu_{F}
-a.e
y\in F
,

\displaystyle(1)\,\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{(d-1+\beta)(d+1)}{2d^{2}}\implies\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E))\geq\beta,

\displaystyle(2)\,\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{d+1}{2d}\implies\,\left|\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)\right|>0,

\displaystyle(3)\,\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{(d+1)^{2}}{2d^{2}}\implies\,\left(\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)\right)^{0}\neq\emptyset.

Remark 2.1
.

The threshold in Corollary
1.1
is better than the threshold in Corollary
2.1
for all large
d
.

Next, we recall the following two auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

For any compact set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)>1
, there exists a point
x\in\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{x}\left(\Omega\right))\geq\min\left\{\frac{4}{3}\cdot\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)-\frac{2}{3},\,1\right\}.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
10
]
)
.

For any compact set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\in(1,\,1.04)
, there exists a point
x\in\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{x}\left(\Omega\right))>\frac{29}{42}.

Remark 2.2
.

When
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
is closed to
1
, then the lower bound
\frac{29}{42}
is bigger than the lower bound
\min\left\{\frac{4}{3}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)-\frac{2}{3},\,1\right\}
. To compare these two lemmas, one can actually have that there is a point
x\in\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{x}\left(\Omega\right))>\frac{29}{42},\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\leq\frac{57}{56};\,\text{and}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{x}\left(\Omega\right))\geq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{4}{3}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)-\frac{2}{3},\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\leq\frac{5}{4},\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle 1,\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega).\end{cases}

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
d\geq 3
and
y_{d-1},y_{d}\in A\cap B
and
x_{0},x_{1}\in B
. Consider the sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

\displaystyle E:=\left(A\times\cdots\times A\right)\times\triangle^{2}_{(y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{2})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{1};\,\text{and}

\displaystyle F:=\left(B\times\cdots\times B\right)\times-\triangle^{2}_{(x_{0},x_{1})}(B^{2})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{1}.

If
\Phi_{d-1}:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the parabolic distance, then there is a probability measure
\mu_{F}
on
F
such that for
\mu_{F}
-a.e.
y\in F
which is of the form

\left(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-2},-\left(|x_{0}-b_{d-1}|^{2}+|x_{1}-b_{d}|^{2}\right)\right),

satisfying

\displaystyle(1)\,\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E))\geq\beta,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-2+\beta,

\displaystyle(2)\,\,\left|\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)\right|>0,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-1,

\displaystyle(3)\,\,\left(\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)\right)^{0}\neq\emptyset,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d.

However note that

\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)=\triangle^{2}_{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-2},y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{d})+|x_{0}-b_{d-1}|^{2}+|x_{1}-b_{d}|^{2},

which says the set
\triangle^{2}_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d})
is a translation of
\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)
so that we have

\displaystyle(1)\,\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E))=\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle^{2}_{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-2},y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{d}))=\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-2},y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{d})),\,\text{and}

\displaystyle(2)\,\,\left|\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)\right|=\left|\triangle^{2}_{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-2},y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{d})\right|\leq C_{A}\cdot\left|\triangle_{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-2},y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{d})\right|,\,\text{and}

\displaystyle(3)\,\,\left(\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)\right)^{0}\neq\emptyset\implies\left(\triangle_{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-2},y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{d})\right)^{0}\neq\emptyset.

Then one has

\displaystyle(1)\,\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-2},y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{d}))\geq\beta,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-2+\beta,

\displaystyle(2)\,\,\left|\triangle_{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-2},y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{d})\right|>0,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-1,

\displaystyle(3)\,\,\left(\triangle_{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-2},y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{d})\right)^{0}\neq\emptyset,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d.

To guarantee the condition
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-2+\beta
holds, we note that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)

\displaystyle\geq(d-2)\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{2}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{d-2}{d}\cdot\left((d-2)\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(x_{0},x_{1})}(B^{2}))\right)

\displaystyle=(d-2)\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{2}))

(2.2)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(x_{0},x_{1})}(B^{2})).

To apply Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
, we need to assume that the Hausdorff dimension of the set
A\cap B
is at least
\frac{1}{2}
, and hence we have the following three cases:

Case
(1)
: Assume
1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}
, then (
2.2
) reveals that if we choose the point
(y_{d-1},y_{d})=(x_{0},x_{1})\in A\cap B
such that Lemma
2.2
holds for the set
(A\cap B)^{2}
, then

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)

\displaystyle\geq(d-2)\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(y_{d-1},y_{d})}((A\cap B)^{2}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(x_{0},x_{1})}((A\cap B)^{2}))

\displaystyle>(d-2)\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\frac{2d-2}{d}\cdot\frac{29}{42}.

Suppose that
(d-2)\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\frac{2d-2}{d}\cdot\frac{29}{42}\geq d-2+\beta
,
then we have

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)\geq\frac{21d^{2}-71d+21\beta\cdot d+29}{21d(d-2)},

whenever
d\geq 3
.

Case
(2)
: Assume
\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}
, then (
2.2
) reveals that if we choose the point
(y_{d-1},y_{d})=(x_{0},x_{1})
such that Lemma
2.1
holds for the set
(A\cap B)^{2}
, then

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)

\displaystyle\geq(d-2)\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2d-2}{d}\cdot\left(\frac{4}{3}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})-\frac{2}{3}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(d-2)\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{16d-16}{3d}\cdot\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A\cap B)-\frac{4d-4}{3d}.

Suppose that

(d-2)\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\frac{16d-16}{3d}\cdot\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A\cap B)-\frac{4d-4}{3d}>d-2+\beta,

then we have

\displaystyle\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\frac{16d-16}{3d(d-2)}\cdot\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A\cap B)>1+\frac{3d\beta+4d-4}{3d(d-2)},

whenever
d\geq 3
.

Case
(3)
: Assume
\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})
, then (
2.2
) reveals that if we choose the point
(y_{d-1},y_{d})=(x_{0},x_{1})
such that Lemma
2.1
holds for the set
(A\cap B)^{2}
, then

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)

\displaystyle\geq(d-2)\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\frac{2d-2}{d}.

Suppose that

(d-2)\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\frac{2d-2}{d}>d-2+\beta,

then we have

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)>1+\frac{\beta d-2d+2}{d(d-2)},

whenever
d\geq 3
. Therefore, we can conclude that for all
d\geq 3
, if

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)\geq\frac{21d^{2}-71d+21\beta\cdot d+29}{21d(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\frac{16d-16}{3d(d-2)}\cdot\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A\cap B)>1+\frac{3d\beta+4d-4}{3d(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)>1+\frac{\beta d-2d+2}{d(d-2)},\end{cases}

then the Hausdorff dimension of pinned distance set of the product
A^{d}
is no less than
\beta
, which improve the threshold in the case of product sets. Similarly, we have
for all
d\geq 3
, if

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)\geq\frac{(21d-29)(d-1)}{21d(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\frac{16d-16}{3d(d-2)}\cdot\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A\cap B)>1+\frac{7d-4}{3d(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)>1-\frac{1}{d},\end{cases}

then the pinned distance set of the product
A^{d}
has positive Lebesgue measure; and if

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)\geq\frac{21d^{2}-29d+29}{21d(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\left[\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\right]+\frac{16d-16}{3d(d-2)}\cdot\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A\cap B)>1+\frac{10d-4}{3d(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}((A\cap B)^{2})\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(B)>1+\frac{2}{d(d-2)},\end{cases}

then the pinned distance set of the product
A^{d}
contains an interval, which also improve the threshold in the case of product sets.

2.2.
Proof of Corollary
1.1

Let
d\geq 3
and
y_{d-1},y_{d},x_{0},x_{1}\in A
. Consider the sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

\displaystyle E:=\left(A\times\cdots\times A\right)\times\triangle^{2}_{(y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{2})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{1};\,\text{and}

\displaystyle F:=\left(A\times\cdots\times A\right)\times-\triangle^{2}_{(x_{0},x_{1})}(A^{2})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{1}.

If
\Phi_{d-1}:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the parabolic distance, then there is a probability measure
\mu_{F}
on
F
such that for
\mu_{F}
-a.e.
y\in F
which is of the form

\left(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d-2},-\left(|x_{0}-a_{0}|^{2}+|x_{1}-a_{1}|^{2}\right)\right),

satisfying

\displaystyle(1)\,\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E))\geq\beta,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-2+\beta,

\displaystyle(2)\,\,\left|\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)\right|>0,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-1,

\displaystyle(3)\,\,\left(\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)\right)^{0}\neq\emptyset,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d.

However note that

\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)=\triangle^{2}_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d})+|x_{0}-a_{0}|^{2}+|x_{1}-a_{1}|^{2},

which says the set
\triangle^{2}_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d})
is a translation of
\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)
so that we have

\displaystyle(1)\,\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E))=\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle^{2}_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d}))=\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d})),\,\text{and}

\displaystyle(2)\,\,\left|\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)\right|=\left|\triangle^{2}_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d})\right|\leq C_{A}\cdot\left|\triangle_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d})\right|,\,\text{and}

\displaystyle(3)\,\,\left(\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(E)\right)^{0}\neq\emptyset\implies\left(\triangle_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d})\right)^{0}\neq\emptyset.

Then one has

\displaystyle(1)\,\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d}))\geq\beta,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-2+\beta,

\displaystyle(2)\,\,\left|\triangle_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d})\right|>0,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-1,

\displaystyle(3)\,\,\left(\triangle_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A^{d})\right)^{0}\neq\emptyset,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d.

To guarantee the condition
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-2+\beta
holds, we note that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)

\displaystyle\geq(d-2)\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{2}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{d-2}{d}\cdot\left((d-2)\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(x_{0},x_{1})}(A^{2}))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2d-2)\cdot(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A^{2}))

(2.3)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\triangle_{(x_{0},x_{1})}(A^{2})).

To apply Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
, we need to assume that the Hausdorff dimension of the set
A
is at least
\frac{1}{2}
, and hence we have the following three cases:

Case
(1)
: Assume
1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}
, then (
2.3
) reveals that if we choose the point
(y_{d-1},y_{d})=(x_{0},x_{1})
such that Lemma
2.2
holds, then

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>\frac{(2d-2)\cdot(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{2d-2}{d}\cdot\frac{29}{42}.

Suppose that
\frac{(2d-2)\cdot(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{2d-2}{d}\cdot\frac{29}{42}\geq d-2+\beta
,
then we have

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\geq\frac{21d^{2}-71d+21\beta\cdot d+29}{42(d-1)(d-2)},

whenever
d\geq 3
.

Case
(2)
: Assume
\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}
, then (
2.3
) reveals that if we choose the point
(y_{d-1},y_{d})=(x_{0},x_{1})
such that Lemma
2.1
holds, then

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(2d-2)\cdot(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{2d-2}{d}\cdot\left(\frac{8}{3}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)-\frac{2}{3}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(d-1)(6d+4)}{3d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)-\frac{4(d-1)}{3d}.

Suppose that
\frac{(d-1)(6d+4)}{3d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)-\frac{4(d-1)}{3d}>d-2+\beta
,
then we have

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{3d(d-2+\beta)}{(d-1)(6d+4)}+\frac{2}{3d+2},

whenever
d\geq 3
.

Case
(3)
: Assume
\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})
, then (
2.3
) reveals that if we choose the point
(y_{d-1},y_{d})=(x_{0},z_{0})
such that Lemma
2.1
holds, then

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(2d-2)\cdot(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{2d-2}{d}.

Suppose that
\frac{(2d-2)\cdot(d-2)}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)+\frac{2d-2}{d}>d-2+\beta
,
then we have

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{d^{2}-4d+\beta d+2}{(d-2)(2d-2)},

whenever
d\geq 3
. Therefore, we can conclude that for all
d\geq 3
, if

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\geq\frac{21d^{2}-71d+21\beta\cdot d+29}{42(d-1)(d-2)}\end{cases}{\rm\ \ or\ \ }\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{3d(d-2+\beta)}{(d-1)(6d+4)}+\frac{2}{3d+2}\end{cases}

or

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{d^{2}-4d+\beta d+2}{(d-2)(2d-2)},\end{cases}

then the Hausdorff dimension of pinned distance set of the product
A^{d}
is no less than
\beta
, which improve the threshold in the case of product sets. Similarly, we have
for all
d\geq 3
, if

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\geq\frac{21d-29}{42(d-2)}\end{cases},\text{or}\,\,\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{3d+4}{6d+4}\end{cases}

or

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

then the pinned distance set of the product
A^{d}
has positive Lebesgue measure; and if

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle 1<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{57}{56}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)\geq\frac{21d^{2}-29d+29}{42(d-1)(d-2)}\end{cases},\text{or}\,\,\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{57}{56}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\leq\frac{5}{4}\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{(3d-2)(d+2)}{(6d+4)(d-1)}\end{cases}

or

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A^{2})\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A)>\frac{d^{2}-2d+2}{(d-2)(2d-2)},\end{cases}

then the pinned distance set of the product
A^{d}
contains an interval, which also improve the threshold in the case of product sets.

2.3.
Proof of Corollary
1.2

Let
d\geq 3
and
y_{d-1}\in A_{d-1},\,y_{d}\in A_{d}
. Consider the sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

\displaystyle G:=\left(A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{d-2}\right)\times\triangle^{2}_{(y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A_{d-1}\times A_{d})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{1};\,\text{and}

\displaystyle F:=\left(A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{d-2}\right)\times-\triangle^{2}_{(y_{d-1},y_{d})}(A_{d-1}\times A_{d})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{1}.

If
\Phi_{d-1}:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the parabolic distance, then there is a probability measure
\mu_{F}
on
F
such that for
\mu_{F}
-a.e.
y\in F
which is of the form

\left(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d-2},-\left(|y_{d-1}-a_{0}|^{2}+|y_{d}-a_{1}|^{2}\right)\right),

satisfying

\displaystyle\left|\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(G)\right|>0,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(G)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-1.

However note that

\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(G)=\triangle^{2}_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{d})+|y_{d-1}-a_{0}|^{2}+|y_{d}-a_{1}|^{2},

which says the set
\triangle^{2}_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{d})
is a translation of
\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(G)
so that we have

\displaystyle\left|\triangle^{\Phi}_{y}(G)\right|=\left|\triangle^{2}_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{d})\right|\leq C_{A}\cdot\left|\triangle_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{d})\right|.

Then one has

\displaystyle\left|\triangle^{2}_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{d})\right|>0,\,\,\text{if}\,\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(G)+\frac{d-2}{d}\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-1,

To guarantee that the Hausdorff dimension of
G
and
F
fit the threshold, we may hope that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{2d-2}{d}\left(\sum^{d-2}_{j=1}s_{j}+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\triangle^{2}_{(y_{d-1},y_{y})}(A_{d-1}\times A_{d})\right)\right)>d-1;

By lemma
2.1
, we have if
s_{d-1}+s_{d}>\frac{5}{4}
, then
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(A_{d-1}\times A_{d})>\frac{5}{4}
and hence

(2.5)

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\triangle^{2}_{(y_{d-1},y_{y})}(A_{d-1}\times A_{d})\right)=\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\triangle_{(y_{d-1},y_{y})}(A_{d-1}\times A_{d})\right)\geq 1.

Combining (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we have if
\sum^{d-2}_{j=1}s_{j}>\frac{d}{2}-1
,
then
\left|\triangle^{2}_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})}(A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{d})\right|>0
.

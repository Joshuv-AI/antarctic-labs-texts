Real and finite field versions of Chebotarëv’s theorem
Tarek Emmrich, Stefan Kunis

Abstract
Chebotarev’s theorem on roots of unity states that all minors of the Fourier matrix of prime size are non-vanishing. This result has been rediscovered several times and proved via different techniques. We follow the proof of Evans and Isaacs and generalize the original result to a real version and a version over finite fields. For the latter, we are able to remove an order condition between the characteristic of the field and the size of the matrix as well as decrease a sufficient lower bound on the characteristic by Zhang considerably. Direct applications include a specific real phase retrieval problem as well as a recent result for Riesz bases of exponentials. Key words and phrases : Discrete Fourier transform, finite fields, minors, Schur polynomials. 2020 AMS Mathematics Subject Classification : 11T06, 15B33, 15B99, 43A25, 65T50.

Prerequisites

An important tool for our proofs are Schur polynomials. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a set
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}\in\binom{\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}}{k}

\displaystyle V_{A}=V_{A}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{a_{1}}&x_{2}^{a_{1}}&x_{3}^{a_{1}}&\ldots&x_{k}^{a_{1}}\\
x_{1}^{a_{2}}&x_{2}^{a_{2}}&x_{3}^{a_{2}}&\ldots&x_{k}^{a_{2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
x_{1}^{a_{k}}&x_{2}^{a_{k}}&x_{3}^{a_{k}}&\ldots&x_{k}^{a_{k}}\end{pmatrix}\in(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}])^{k\times k},

For any matrix

\displaystyle\alpha_{B}(s_{A})(1)=\underbrace{\Phi_{p}(1)}_{=p}\cdot R(1).

3
Real version of Chebotarëv’s theorem

For each prime
p>3
the group
(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}
is the Galois group of the field extension
\mathbb{Q}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}[X]/\Phi_{p}(X)
and has the subgroup
H=\{1,-1\}
. This subgroup
H
is generated by the automorphism on
\mathbb{Q}[X]/\Phi_{p}(X)
which is induced by the complex conjugation
i\mapsto-i
. By the Galois correspondence this induces the subfield

\mathbb{Q}\subsetneq\operatorname{Fix}(H)\subsetneq\mathbb{Q}[X]/\Phi_{p}(X),

of degree
\deg_{\mathbb{Q}}(\operatorname{Fix}(H))=\frac{p-1}{2}
. This field is generated as
\mathbb{Q}(\omega_{p}+\omega_{p}^{-1})=\operatorname{Fix}(H)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and has the cyclic Galois group

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}/H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{\frac{p-1}{2}},

which is generated by the image of any generator
(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}
modulo
H
. It is
\omega_{p}+\omega_{p}^{-1}=2\cos(2\pi/p)
and these elements are mapped to
(\omega_{p}^{j}+\omega_{p}^{-j})=2\cos(2\pi j/p)
by the Galois group of the cyclotomic field. Thus the elements

(\omega_{p}^{j}+\omega_{p}^{-j})=2\cos(2\pi j/p),\quad j=1,\ldots,\frac{p-1}{2},

are conjugate and share the same minimal polynomial

P(X)=\prod_{j=1}^{(p-1)/2}(X-2\cos(2j\pi/p)).

The coefficients of this polynomial are symmetric in the cosine terms and hence, due to the Galois correspondence, must lie in the fixed field of the Galois group, i.e., the coefficients are rational. An explicit formula for the coefficients of this minimal polynomial is given in
[
5
]
by

P(x)=\sum\limits_{0\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-k}{k}X^{n-2k}+\sum\limits_{0\leq k<\frac{n}{2}}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-k-1}{k}X^{n-2k-1},

where
n=\frac{p-1}{2}
. However, this expression does not provide much additional insight into the structure of the minimal polynomial and will not be used later.
In order to use the Schur polynomials, we need to understand the polynomial expression between the roots.

Lemma 5
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
P(X)\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
be the minimal polynomial of
2\cos(2\pi/p)
. The elements
\varphi_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}[X]/P(X)
that are given by the recursion

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(X)

\displaystyle=X,

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(X)

\displaystyle=X^{2}-2,

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}(X)

\displaystyle=X\cdot\varphi_{j-1}(X)-\varphi_{j-2}(X),\qquad j=3,\ldots,\frac{p-1}{2},

are to the roots of
P
. Viewed over the rationals, the polynomials
\varphi_{j}(X)
have the common fixed point at
2
, i.e.,
\varphi_{j}(2)=2
for all
j
.

Proof.

In the field
\mathbb{Q}[X]/P(X)
we can use
X=(\omega_{p}+\omega_{p}^{-1})
and we have

X^{2}-2=\omega_{p}^{2}+\omega_{p}^{-2}.

Induction on
j
proves the first part, where we use

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}(X)

\displaystyle=(\omega_{p}+\omega_{p}^{-1})\cdot(\omega_{p}^{j-1}+\omega_{p}^{1-j})-(\omega_{p}^{j-2}+\omega_{p}^{2-j})=\omega_{p}^{j}+\omega_{p}^{-j}.

Regarding the second part, we see that
\varphi_{j}(2)=2\cdot\varphi_{j-1}(2)-\varphi_{j-2}(2)=2\cdot 2-2=2
.
∎

Now, we evaluate the minimal polynomial
P(X)
at the fixed point
2
of the polynomials
\varphi_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,\frac{p-1}{2}
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
p
be a prime. The minimal polynomial
P(X)
of
2\cos(2\pi/p)
has the evaluation
P(2)=p
.

Proof.

The Chebyshev polynomials of first kind
T_{p}\colon[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
are given by

\displaystyle T_{p}(x)=\cos(p\arccos x).

The roots of

\displaystyle P(X)=\prod_{j=1}^{(p-1)/2}\left(X-2\cos\frac{2j\pi}{p}\right)

coincide with the local maxima of
2T_{p}(X/2)
in
(-2,2)
,
2T_{p}(X/2)=2
, and
2T_{p}(X/2)
is a monic polynomial of degree
p
. Hence we have the identity

\displaystyle 2(T_{p}(X/2)-1)=(P(X))^{2}(X-2)

from which also

\displaystyle(P(2))^{2}=\lim_{y\to 1}\frac{T_{p}(y)-1}{y-1}=T_{p}^{\prime}(1)=p\lim_{\theta\to 0}\frac{\sin p\theta}{\sin\theta}=p^{2}

follows.
∎

We now have all the tools to state and prove the main theorem of this section, which can be seen as a real version of Chebatarëv’s theorem.

Theorem 7
.

Let
p
be a prime. All minors of the Vandermonde matrix

\left(\left(\cos\left(\frac{2\cdot j\cdot\pi}{p}\right)\right)^{m}\right)_{j,m=1,\ldots,\frac{p-1}{2}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{p-1}{2}\times\frac{p-1}{2}}

are nonzero.

Proof.

For notational convenience, we work with the Vandermonde matrix, see Equation (
2.1
),

V\left(2\cdot\cos\left(\frac{2\cdot j\cdot\pi}{p}\right)_{j=1,\ldots,\frac{p-1}{2}}\right),

whose columns are a multiple of the original matrix and hence the minors show the same vanishing behaviour. Let
A,B\in\binom{[\frac{p-1}{2}]}{l}
be the indices of the submatrix that is under consideration. This minor is zero if and only if
s_{A}(2\cos(2b_{1}\pi/p),\ldots,2\cos(2b_{l}\pi/p))=0
. We break down the step from
s_{A}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
to
s_{A}(2\cos(2b_{1}\pi/p),\ldots,2\cos(2b_{l}\pi/p))=0
in two steps. The first step is the substitution

\alpha\colon\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}]\to\mathbb{Q}[X]\text{, }x_{i}\mapsto\varphi_{b_{i}}(X),

with
\varphi_{b_{i}}
from
lemma

5
. The second step is to look at the image of
\alpha_{B}(s_{A})
modulo the minimal polynomial
P
of
2\cos(2\pi/p)
.
The minor is zero if and only if
P|\alpha_{B}(s_{A})
in
\mathbb{Q}[X]
. If this equation holds, it will also hold after another substitution
X\mapsto 2
.
lemma

6
yields
P(2)=p
and
lemma

4
yields

\displaystyle\alpha_{B}(s_{A})(2,\ldots,2)

\displaystyle=s_{A}(2,\ldots,2)

(3.1)

\displaystyle=2^{d}\cdot s_{A}(1,\ldots,1)

(3.2)

\displaystyle=2^{d}\cdot\frac{\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq l}(a_{j}-a_{i})}{\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq l}(j-i)},

(3.3)

which is a contradiction to the divisibility by
p
, hence the minor must be nonzero. Equality
eq.

3.1
follows from the fact that
2
is a fixed point of all
\varphi_{k}
, equality
eq.

3.2
follows from the fact that Schur polynomials are homogeneous of some degree
d
and equality
eq.

3.3
is
lemma

4
.
∎

Remark 9
.

Another real version can be infered from the classical Chebotarëv result.
Let
p
be a prime, set
r=(p-1)/2
, and

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\left(\exp\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}kj}{p}\right)_{k,j=-r,\ldots,r}\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times p},\qquad C=\left(2\cos\frac{2\pi kj}{p}\right)_{k,j=1,\ldots,r}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}.

Now assume
\det(C_{J\times K})=0
for some
J,K\in\binom{[r]}{s}
.
Then there exists a
\hat{f}=(\hat{f}_{-r},\ldots,\hat{f}_{r})^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
,
\hat{f}_{k}=\hat{f}_{-k}
, which fulfils

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{k\in K}2\hat{f}_{k}\cos\frac{2\pi kj}{p}=\sum_{k\in K\cup-K}\hat{f}_{k}\exp\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}kj}{p}

for
j\in J
, as well as for
j\in-J
in the first equation since the cosine is even.
This implies
\det(F_{(J\cup-J)\times(K\cup-K)})=0
which is false.

More general results on uncertainty principles for vectors with symmetry and related Chebotarëv theorems can be found in

4
Chebotarëv’s theorem over finite fields

Now let us once again consider the Fourier matrix
F_{p}
of prime size, but this time over fields of characteristic
q
for
q\in\mathbb{N}
prime.
It is well known that, given two primes
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
, the polynomial

\overline{\Phi_{p}(X)}=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}X^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]

is not even necessarily irreducible. In fact
\overline{\Phi_{p}(X)}
is irreducible if and only if
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(q)=p-1
.
This follows from the fact that, if
\Phi_{p}
is irreducible, the roots of
\overline{\Phi_{p}(X)}
are given by
X^{q^{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]/\Phi_{(}X)
, for
i=0,1,\ldots,p-2
, and the roots fulfill
X^{p}=1
. Thus the roots are given by

X,X^{(q\mod p)},X^{(q^{2}\mod p)},X^{(q^{3}\mod p)},\ldots,X^{(q^{p-2}\mod p)},

and these are repeated, if
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(q)=r<p-1
, which would contradict the separability of
\overline{\Phi_{p}(X)}
. In fact it is known that
\overline{\Phi_{p}(X)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
splits into
\frac{p-1}{r}
many irreducible polynomials of degree
r
. The roots of all all these polynomials are contained in the field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
.

Example 10
.

For
p=7
and
q=11
we have
r=\operatorname{ord}_{7}(11)=3
and the factorization

(X^{3}+7X^{2}+6X+10)(X^{3}+5X^{2}+4X+10)=(X^{6}+X^{5}+X^{4}+X^{3}+X^{2}+X+1)\in\mathbb{F}_{11}[X].

Zhang’s conditions
[
17
]
,
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(q)=p-1
and
q>\Gamma
, for some large
\Gamma
, imply the nonvanishing of the minors, which on polynomial side again can be written as

\overline{\Phi_{p}(X)}\nmid\alpha_{B}(s_{A}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}))\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X],

for all
A,B\in\binom{\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}}{k}
. The sufficient lower bound is given by

\Gamma=\max_{A\subseteq\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}}\frac{\prod_{i<j}(a_{j}-a_{i})}{\prod_{i<j}(j-i)},

and for
q>\Gamma
, the Euclidean division

\alpha_{B}(s_{A}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}))=f(X)\cdot\Phi_{p}(X)+R(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]

in combination with

\alpha_{B}(s_{A}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}))(1)=\frac{\prod_{i<j}(a_{j}-a_{i})}{\prod_{i<j}(j-i)},

yields that
q
is too large to divide all the coefficients of
R(X)

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}\alpha_{(l\cdot B)}(s_{A}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}\alpha_{(l\cdot B)}\left(\sum_{\mu\in T}c_{\mu}x^{\mu}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}\left(\sum_{\mu\in T}c_{\mu}\alpha_{(l\cdot B)}(x^{\mu})\right)

Theorem 16
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
be two distinct primes with
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(q)=r
.
Let
F_{p}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}^{p\times p}
be the Fourier matrix in characteristic
q
, see
eq.

4.1
, and let
A,B\in\binom{\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}}{k}
be two index sets. If

q\nmid\left(m_{A,B}\cdot p-\frac{\prod_{i<j}(a_{j}-a_{i})}{\prod_{i<j}(j-i)}\right),

we have
\det((F_{p})_{A,B})\neq 0\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\det((F_{p})_{A,B})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
, by
lemma

12
we know that

\det((F_{p})_{A,l\cdot B})=0
for all
l\in(\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\times}
. Furthermore let
R_{l}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]/\Phi_{p}(X)
be the remainder of
\alpha_{l\cdot B}(s_{A}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}))
modulo
\Phi_{p}(X)
.
We distinguish two cases, first
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(q)=p-1
and second
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(q)=r<p-1
.
In the first case it is
\det((F_{p})_{A,l\cdot B})=0
if and only if
q
divides every coefficient of
R_{l}
for every
l
. Hence we also know that
q
divides the coefficients of the sum

\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}R_{l}(X)=\left(m\cdot p-\frac{\prod_{i<j}(a_{j}-a_{i})}{\prod_{i<j}(j-i)}\right),

where the equality follows from
lemma

13
. This is a contradiction to our assumption and hence
\det((F_{p})_{A,B})\neq 0
.

In the second case we have
\det((F_{p})_{A,B})=0
if and only if
R_{l}=0\mod\{\langle q,P(X)\}\rangle
and consequently also

\sum_{l=1}^{p-1}R_{l}(X)=\left(m\cdot p-\frac{\prod_{i<j}(a_{j}-a_{i})}{\prod_{i<j}(j-i)}\right)=0\mod\langle q,P(X)\rangle.

Since
|m_{A,B}\cdot p-s_{A}(1,\ldots,1)|
is an integer that clearly is not contained in the ideal
\langle q\rangle
by the condition, it will be mapped to a nonzero element of degree zero in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
. Thus it is not an element of the kernel
\langle\overline{P(X)}\rangle
.
∎

Our main result now follows directly.

Corollary 17
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
be two distinct primes with
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(q)=r
.
Let
F_{p}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}^{p\times p}
be the Fourier matrix in characteristic
q
, see
eq.

4.1
.
If

q>\max_{A,B\subseteq\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\},\#A=\#B}\left|m_{A,B}\cdot p-\frac{\prod_{i<j}(a_{j}-a_{i})}{\prod_{i<j}(j-i)}\right|,

F_{p}

By
theorem

16

for all
A,B\subseteq\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
, with
\#A=\#B
. This is trivially the case under the condition on
q
and the statement follows.
∎

PANDECTS (Lat. pandecta, adapted from Gr. πανδέκτης , all containing), a name given to a compendium or digest of Roman law compiled by order of the emperor Justinian in the 6th century (A.D. 530–533). The pandects were divided into fifty books, each book containing several titles, divided into laws, and the laws into several parts or paragraphs. The number of
jurists from whose works extracts were made is thirty-nine,
but the writings of Ulpian and Paulus make up quite half the
work. The work was declared to be the sole source of non-statute
law: commentaries on the compilation were forbidden,
or even the citing of the original works of the jurists for the
explaining of ambiguities in the text. See Justinian Justinian I.; and
Roman Law.

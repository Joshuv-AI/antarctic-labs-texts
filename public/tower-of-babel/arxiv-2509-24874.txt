The geodesic restriction problem for arithmetic spherical harmonics
Maximiliano Sanchez Garza

Abstract
. Given a Riemannian manifold M M and an L 2 L^{2} -normalized Laplacian eigenfunction ψ \psi on M M with eigenvalue λ 2 \lambda^{2} , a general problem in analysis is to understand how the mass of ψ \psi distributes around M M . There are different ways to attack this problem. One of them is to analyze the L p L^{p} -norm of ψ \psi restricted to a submanifold of M M . Here, we concentrate on the case M = S 2 M=S^{2} , p = 2 p=2 , and we restrict to geodesics of the sphere. Burq, Gérard, and Tzvetkov showed, for γ \gamma a geodesic of S 2 S^{2} (and indeed for more general surfaces), that ‖ ψ | γ ‖ L 2 ≪ λ 1 / 4 ||\psi|_{\gamma}||_{L^{2}}\ll\lambda^{1/4} and that this bound is optimal in general. In this paper, we specialize to the case in which ψ \psi is an eigenfunction of all the Hecke operators on the sphere and consider the set of geodesics 𝒞 D \mathcal{C}_{D} of S 2 S^{2} associated to fundamental discriminants D < 0 D<0 . By combining approaches of Ali and Magee, we improve the previous upper bound to | | ψ | 𝒞 D | | L 2 ≪ D , ε λ ε ||\psi|_{\mathcal{C}_{D}}||_{L^{2}}\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon} for any ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , which is essentially sharp.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold and let
\Delta
be the Laplace operator associated to
g
. Let
\psi
be a Laplacian eigenfunction on
M
satisfying
\Delta\psi=\lambda^{2}\psi
. An interesting problem in analysis is to study how much of the mass of
\psi
concentrates in small subsets of
M
. There are several ways to approach this question; see the introduction of
[
BGT07
]
for some of these approaches and references. One of them is to take
\psi
to be
L^{2}
-normalized and bound
||\psi|_{M^{\prime}}||_{L^{p}}
, where
M^{\prime}
is a submanifold of
M
and
2\leq p\leq\infty
.

This approach was first studied by Reznikov in
[
Rez04
]
in the particular case of
M
being a compact hyperbolic Riemann surface,
p=2
, and restricting
\psi
to a closed geodesic
\gamma
, where he showed that

(1)

||\psi|_{\gamma}||_{L^{2}}\ll\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Later, in
[
BGT07
, Thm. 3]
, Burq, Gérard, and Tzvetkov generalized Reznikov’s results to Riemannian manifolds in general, where the bounds are now dependent on
p
and the dimensions of
M
and
M^{\prime}
, and showed that these are sharp in most cases. Sarnak, in his letter to Reznikov in
[
Sar08
, p. 1]
, calls bound (
1
) and the bounds obtained by Burq, Gérard, and Tzvetkov the
convexity bounds
. The reason for this is that, in the case of
M^{\prime}=M
being an arithmetic hyperbolic surface and
p=\infty
, there is a deep connection between our problem, the Ramanujan conjectures for Maass forms, and the Lindelöf Hypothesis. This connection is described in
[
Sar95
, Section 4]
.

Even though the bound (
1
) is sharp in general, it is expected to be not optimal in the case where
M
has constant curvature
-1
since the geodesic flow is ergodic and quantum ergodicity holds (see
[
Shn74
]
,
[
CdV85
]
,
[
Zel87
]
). The only power-saving progress in this setting is when
M
is an arithmetic quotient of the modular surface, such as
M=\Gamma_{0}(q)\backslash\mathbb{H}
, and
\psi
is arithmetic, i.e. a simultaneous eigenfunction of
\Delta
and all the Hecke operators. The first improvement to the convexity bound in this setting is due to Iwaniec and Sarnak, where they showed in
[
IS95
, Thm. 0.1]
that

||\psi||_{L^{\infty}}\ll_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{5}{24}+\varepsilon}

for any
\varepsilon>0
. More recently, Ali showed in
[
AA22
]
that for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

(2)

||\psi|_{\mathcal{C}_{D}}||_{L^{2}}\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\lambda^{\theta+\varepsilon}

where
D>0
is a fundamental discriminant,
\mathcal{C}_{D}
is the union of all closed geodesics in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}
associated to
D
, and
\theta\geq 0
is any bound towards the Ramanujan Conjecture for Maass forms. One can take
\theta=\frac{7}{64}
by results from Kim and Sarnak in
[
KRS03
, Appendix 2, Prop. 2]
.

We concentrate on the case where
M=S^{2}
is the
2
-sphere, that is,

S^{2}:=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=1\},

which has constant curvature
1
. We give it the round metric and associated measure, which gives it the structure of a Riemannian manifold. Let
\Delta
be its Laplace operator. We know that
L^{2}(S^{2})
has a complete spectral resolution with respect to
\Delta
. Moreover, this spectral resolution is completely explicit in terms of spherical harmonics; see
[
STV18
, Thm. 2.9]
,
[
SW71
, p. 140]
,
[
AH12
, Th. 2.34, Prop. 3.5]
. More explicitly, the
\ell
-th Laplacian eigenvalue is
\lambda_{\ell}:=\ell(\ell+1)
and its eigenspace, denoted by
H_{\ell}
, is the space of harmonic homogeneous polynomials in three variables of degree
\ell
restricted to the sphere
S^{2}
, which are precisely the spherical harmonics.

Let
\psi\in H_{\ell}
and let
\gamma
be a geodesic of
S^{2}
. We know
||\psi|_{\gamma}||_{L^{2}}
satisfies bound (
1
). In fact, for arbitrary
\psi
, the bound (
1
) is sharp; see
[
BGT07
, Thm. 1]
. The reason for this is that, at least for certain
\psi
, there are high concentrations of mass in specific regions of the sphere (see Figure
1
).

Even though
S^{2}
has constant curvature
1
, we expect improvements on the bound (
1
) for specific
\psi
. The reason for this is that a basis of Laplacian eigenfunctions for
S^{2}
can also be diagonalized further with respect to Hecke operators, thus making the basis elements arithmetic. These operators are defined using the action of
B^{\times}(\mathbb{R})
on
S^{2}
via rotations, where
B^{\times}(\mathbb{R})
is the group of units of the Hamiltonian quaternion algebra
B(\mathbb{R})
over
\mathbb{R}
. This action makes
S^{2}
into a homogeneous space.

To be more precise, for any ring
R
, we define

B(R):=\langle 1,i,j,k\>|\>i^{2}=j^{2}=k^{2}=-1,\>ij=-ji=k\rangle_{R},

so that
B(R)=B(\mathbb{Q})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}R
for any
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
R
. We denote by
B^{\times}(R)
the group of units of
B(R)
. Then
B^{\times}
is an affine algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
. For
\alpha=a+bi+cj+dk\in B(R)
, let
\mathrm{Nrd}\left(\alpha\right):=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}\in R
and
\mathrm{Trd}\left(\alpha\right):=2a\in R
be its (reduced) norm and (reduced) trace, respectively. Let
B^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
be the subset of
B(R)
of quaternions of trace zero and norm
1
. The group
B^{\times}(R)\ni\alpha
acts on
B^{0,1}(R)\ni\gamma
by conjugation, i.e.
\alpha\cdot\gamma:=\alpha\gamma\alpha^{-1}
.

We now specialize to the case
R=\mathbb{R}
. Since we have the bijection
1

B^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})

). Hence, there is an induced action on

, which is the left regular representation of

Let
\mathcal{O}\subset B(\mathbb{R})
denote the ring of Hurwitz integers, i.e.

\mathcal{O}=\left\langle 1,i,j,\frac{1+i+j+k}{2}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}},

which is a maximal order in
B(\mathbb{R})
of class number
1
. If
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
denotes the group of units of
\mathcal{O}
, we have that
|\mathcal{O}^{\times}|=24
. For any positive integer
n
, we let

\mathcal{O}(n):=\left\{\alpha\in\mathcal{O}:\mathrm{Nrd}\left(\alpha\right)=n\right\},

and we define the
n
-th Hecke operator
T_{n}:C(S^{2})\rightarrow C(S^{2})
by

(T_{n}f)(z):=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{O}^{\times}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}(n)}(\gamma^{-1}\cdot f)(z)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{O}^{\times}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}(n)}f(\gamma\cdot z),

where
\gamma^{-1}
is interpreted as an element of
B^{\times}(\mathbb{R})
. When
n
is a power of two, the
n
-th Hecke operator degenerates to either
T_{1}
or
T_{2}
depending on the
2
-adic valuation of
n
. Hence, the Hecke operators of interest are the ones corresponding to odd values of
n
.

Since the Laplacian is rotation-invariant, all the Hecke operators commute with
\Delta
. Moreover, one can show that all the Hecke operators commute with each other, are self-adjoint in
C(S^{2})
, and satisfy the recurrence relation

T_{m}T_{n}=\sum_{d\,\mid\,\mathrm{gcd}(m,n)}d\>T_{mn/d^{2}},

where
m
and
n
are odd positive integers. Thus, if
H_{\ell}^{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
denotes the subspace of
H_{\ell}
of functions that are invariant under the action of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
, we can diagonalize a basis of each
H_{\ell}^{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
to obtain an orthonormal basis of spherical harmonics which are eigenfunctions of all the Hecke operators. We call these Hecke eigenfunctions.

These Hecke eigenfunctions have a strong connection with modular forms. Indeed, let
S_{\ell}^{\text{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(2))
denote the space of holomorphic newforms of weight
\ell
and level
2
. We have a map

(3)

\displaystyle H_{\ell}^{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow S_{2\ell+2}^{\text{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(2))

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\longmapsto f_{\psi}

given by

f_{\psi}(z):=\frac{1}{24}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}}\left\langle\psi(\xi),\psi\left(\gamma\xi\overline{\gamma}\right)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S^{2})}e^{2\pi i\mathrm{Nrd}\left(\gamma\right)z},

where
\left\langle\psi(\xi),\psi\left(\gamma\xi\overline{\gamma}\right)\right\rangle
denotes the inner product in
L^{2}(S^{2})
.
One can show that
f_{\psi}
is Hecke normalized, i.e. the first Fourier coefficient is
1
, if
\psi
is
L^{2}(S^{2})
-normalized and that
f_{\psi}
is an eigenform if
\psi
is a Hecke eigenfunction. Moreover, if this is the case and

, then
T_{n}f_{\psi}=\lambda_{\psi}(n)n^{\ell}\psi
. In fact, the map (
3

\mathrm{GL}_{2}
and
B^{\times}
. Moreover, this correspondence tells us that the Hecke eigenspaces in

are one-dimensional.

Now, let
D<0
be a negative fundamental discriminant and assume that

to be the union of (closed) geodesics on

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\ell\geq 1
be an integer and let
\psi\in H_{\ell}^{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
be an
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunction of all the Hecke operators. Let
\mathcal{C}_{D}
be the union of all the geodesics of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}
associated to the negative fundamental discriminant

, as described below. Then, for all
\varepsilon>0
, we have

||\psi|_{\mathcal{C}_{D}}||_{L^{2}}\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{\varepsilon}.

The structure of this paper is the following. In Section
2

1.1

3
, we define the set
\mathcal{C}_{D}
and introduce necessary notation for the proof. Finally, in Section

2.1.
Spherical harmonics and automorphic representations of
B^{\times}(\mathbb{A})

The space

P_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{2^{n}n!}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}x^{n}}(x^{2}-1)^{n}.

For each integer

P_{n}^{m}(x):=(-1)^{m}(1-x^{2})^{m/2}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}x^{m}}P_{n}(x).

The functions

[
Hob31

[
Mac48
, §VII.9]

Proposition 2.1
.

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{H_{\ell}}=2\ell+1

\{P_{\ell}(\cos(\phi))\}\cup\{P_{\ell}^{n}(\cos(\phi))\cos(n\theta)\>|\>1\leq n\leq\ell\}\cup\{P_{\ell}^{n}(\cos(\phi))\sin(n\theta)\>|\>1\leq n\leq\ell\}

-orthogonal basis for

, where

(x,y,z)=(\sin(\phi)\cos(\theta),\sin(\phi)\sin(\theta),\cos(\phi))

for
\theta\in[0,2\pi)

\phi\in[0,\pi]

2.2.
Closed geodesics of the 2-sphere

As we mentioned before, we will work with geodesics on
\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}
. Before this, we first recall what the geodesics of
S^{2}
are. These are the equators of
S^{2}
when we choose a point of
S^{2}
to be the “north pole”.

Theorem 2.2
.

\mathrm{(}
[
GHL90
, Example 2.80(c)]
\mathrm{)}
The nonconstant geodesics of
S^{2}
are the “great circles”, i.e. the circles which result of the intersection of
S^{2}
with any plane of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
containing the origin. In particular, all the geodesics of
S^{2}
are closed.

The group
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
acts properly on
S^{2}
by isometries, but it does not act freely (
-1
and
1
act trivially, and any other element of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
has exactly two fixed points which are antipodal). However, the quotient space
\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}
is a smooth manifold and inherits a Riemannian metric from
S^{2}
. From
[
Mag15
]
, a fundamental domain for
\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}
is given by
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
, where
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are the spherical triangles from Figure
2
.

Figure 2.

In terms of the geodesics of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}

\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}

, using

The nonconstant geodesics of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}
are the projections of the geodesics of
S^{2}
on
\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}

The length of any geodesic of
S^{2}
is

. The length of the corresponding geodesic of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}
need not have the same length. In fact, one can show the following result.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\gamma
be a geodesic of
S^{2}
and
\overline{\gamma}
the corresponding geodesic of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}
. Let
\xi\in S^{2}
be a “north pole” of
S^{2}
with equator
\gamma
, i.e. a point on
S^{2}
such that the geodesic
\gamma
is the intersection of
S^{2}
with the plane
\langle\xi,(x,y,z)\rangle=0
. Denote by
\ell(\gamma)
and
\ell(\overline{\gamma})
the lengths of
\gamma
and
\overline{\gamma}
, respectively, in the corresponding spaces.

(1)

If exactly two entries of
\xi
are zero, then
\ell(\overline{\gamma})=\frac{1}{4}\ell(\gamma)
.

(2)

If exactly one entry of
\xi
is zero, then
\ell(\overline{\gamma})=\frac{1}{2}\ell(\gamma)
.

(3)

If the absolute values of the entries of
\xi

\ell(\overline{\gamma})=\frac{1}{2}\ell(\gamma)
.

(4)

If none of the above occurs, then
\ell(\overline{\gamma})=\ell(\gamma)
.

2.3.
CM modular forms

There is an important construction of modular forms from a given Hecke character of an imaginary quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
. It was introduced by Hecke in
[
Hec27
]
. This construction satisfies that, if
\chi
is the Hecke character and
f_{\chi}
is the corresponding modular form, then
L(s,f_{\chi})=L(s,\chi)
, where
L(s,\chi)
is seen as a degree
1

L
-function over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
. In other words,
f_{\chi}
is the automorphic induction of
\chi
from
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
to
\mathbb{Q}
.

For our exposition, we follow
[
Iwa97
, §12.3]
. Throughout this section, we let
E=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
be an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant
D<0
and
\mathcal{O}_{E}
be its ring of integers.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
a modulus of
E
and
\xi
a primitive Hecke character modulo
\mathfrak{m}
for which there exists a non-negative integer
u
such that

\xi((a))=\left(\frac{a}{|a|}\right)^{u}\text{ if }a\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{m}}.

We define the theta series associated to
\xi
, denoted by
f_{\xi}
, by

f_{\xi}(z):=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\xi(\mathfrak{a})N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{a})^{u/2}e^{2\pi iz\cdot N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{a})},

where the sum is taken over all the nonzero integral ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{E}
. The function
f_{\xi}
is called the
CM modular form
associated to
\xi
.

Theorem 2.6
.

\mathrm{(}
[
Iwa97
, Thm. 12.5]
\mathrm{)}
We have that
f_{\xi}
is a holomorphic newform of weight

, level
N=|D|\cdot N_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{m})
, and character
\chi\pmod{N}
, which is given by

\chi(n)=\chi_{D}(n)\xi((n))\text{ for }n\in\mathbb{Z},

where
\chi_{D}
is the primitive quadratic character modulo
-D
associated to
E
. Moreover, if

, then
f
is a cusp form.

Lemma 4.7

For any
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra homomorphism

; i.e.

is an optimal embedding.

Proof.

Let
\mu=\iota(\sqrt{-n_{D}})
and suppose

for some

. Since
\mathrm{Trd}\left(\mu\right)=0
, it follows that

, and so

. Then

, so that

has integral coordinates. Since

is square-free and the coordinates of
\mu
are integral, it follows that the coordinates of
\mu
are pairwise coprime, from where it follows that

. Moreover, the same argument shows that if

, then
b\in\mathbb{Z}
. For

, the result follows from here. So, assume

, so that

, i.e.
n_{D}\equiv 1,2\pmod{4}
. Now, assume that

, so that

an odd integer. It follows that

with

an odd integer. In this case, we must have that
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}-B(\mathbb{Z})
, so that all the entries of
\alpha
must be of the form

. Since

and

, so that at least one of

must be even. This implies that

∎

B^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})
, where
\mathcal{O}_{2}
is the closure of
\mathcal{O}
in
B(\mathbb{Q}_{2})
and is a maximal order in
B(\mathbb{Q}_{2})
. By
[
GP91
, Prop. 2.6]
, there is a unique vector (up to scalar multiplication) in
\rho_{2}
which satisfies this property, and is precisely the Gross–Prasad test vector for linear forms. By the definition of
f
, we have that
\rho_{2}(f_{2})
is the orthogonal projection onto the span of the local component of
\phi^{*}
at
2
. With this setup, the factor
\tilde{J}_{\pi^{B}}(f_{2})
is computed in
[
MW09
, §2.2, 2.2.1]
for our case (
B
non-split at
2
,
\pi
ramified at
2
), where they obtain that
\tilde{J}_{\pi^{B}}(f_{2})=1
.

We now concentrate on computing
\tilde{J}_{\pi^{B}}(f_{\infty})
. We follow the proof of
[
Mag15
, Lemma 8]
. Now, we assume
|n|\leq\ell
, since the integral
I(\psi,\lambda,\rho,n)
vanishes if
|n|\geq\ell
. By the discussion in Section
2.1
, we can pick as a model for
\rho_{\infty}
the space
H_{\ell}
of homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degree
\ell
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
restricted to the
2
-sphere, which is precisely the
\ell
-th irreducible representation of
\mathbb{R}^{\times}\backslash B^{\times}(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathrm{SO}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
.

Let
Y_{\ell}^{n}
be the associated Legendre polynomial
P_{\ell}^{n}
normalized so that

||Y_{\ell}^{n}(\langle\cdot,k\rangle)||_{L^{2}(S^{2})}=1,

where we use the convention
P_{\ell}^{0}=P_{\ell}
. Let

e_{1}(v):=Y_{\ell}^{n}(\langle v,i\rangle)e^{in\theta(v)}\>\text{ and }\>e_{2}(v):=Y_{\ell}^{n}(\langle v,i\rangle)e^{-in\theta(v)},

where
\theta(v)
is an angle around the axis of
i
such that
\theta(k)=0
and
\theta(j)=\pi/2
. These unit vectors are in
H_{\ell}
by Proposition
2.1
and satisfy that

\rho_{\infty}(t)e_{1}=\overline{\Omega_{n}}(t)e_{1}\>\text{ and }\>\rho_{\infty}(t)e_{2}=\Omega_{n}(t)e_{2}

for
t\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Indeed, recall that
[\rho_{\infty}(t)e_{1}](v)=e_{1}(tvt^{-1})
for
t\in B^{\times}(\mathbb{R})
. By a direct computation, we can verify that if
t\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, then
\langle tvt^{-1},i\rangle=\langle v,i\rangle
. Moreover, we can see that if
t=re^{i\theta_{t}}
and
\theta(v)=\theta
, then
\frac{v-\langle v,i\rangle i}{||v-\langle v,i\rangle i||}=ke^{i\theta}
, so that

t\left(ke^{i\theta}\right)t^{-1}=re^{i\theta_{t}}\cdot ke^{i\theta}\cdot r^{-1}e^{-i\theta_{t}}=ke^{i(\theta-2\theta_{t})},

from where it follows that
\theta(tvt^{-1})=\theta-2\theta_{t}
. Consequently,

[\rho_{\infty}(t)e_{1}](v)=e_{1}(tvt^{-1})=Y_{\ell}^{n}(\langle v,i\rangle)e^{in(\theta-2\theta_{t})}=e^{-2in\theta_{t}}e_{1}(v)=\overline{\Omega_{n}}(t)e_{1}(v),

and similarly with
e_{2}
.

From
[
JN01
, §5.1, §6]
, since
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are elements of an orthonormal basis of
H_{\ell}
, it follows that

\tilde{J}_{\pi^{B}}(f_{\infty})=\langle\rho_{\infty}(f_{\infty})e_{1},e_{2}\rangle.

Now, we chose
f_{\infty}
in such a way that
\rho_{\psi}(f)
is the projection onto
\phi^{*}
, which is the translation by
\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime}
of the unique (up to scalar multiplication)
K^{\infty}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}
-fixed vector of
\rho_{\psi}
. Hence,
\rho_{\infty}(f_{\infty})
is precisely the orthogonal projection onto the rotation by
\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime}
of the (unique)
K^{\infty}
-fixed element of
H_{\ell}
, which is
Y_{\ell}^{0}(\langle k,\cdot\rangle)
. Thus,

\displaystyle[\rho_{\infty}(f_{\infty})e_{1}](y)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{S^{2}}Y_{\ell}^{0}(\langle k,\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime}\cdot x\rangle)Y_{\ell}^{n}(\langle x,i\rangle)e^{-in\theta}\>\mathrm{d}x\right)Y_{\ell}^{0}(\langle k,\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime}\cdot y\rangle)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{S^{2}}Y_{\ell}^{0}(\langle(\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot k,x\rangle)Y_{\ell}^{n}(\langle x,i\rangle)e^{-in\theta}\>\mathrm{d}x\right)Y_{\ell}^{0}(\langle(\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot k,y\rangle)

Since
\sqrt{2\ell+1}Y_{\ell}^{0}(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is the reproducing kernel of
H_{\ell}
in
L^{2}(S^{2})
(see
[
Mor98
, §2.3, Lemma 2.22]
), by the choice of
\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime}
we obtain that

\displaystyle[\rho_{\infty}(f_{\infty})e_{1}](y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\ell+1}}\left(Y_{\ell}^{n}(\langle(\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot k,i\rangle)e^{-in\theta((\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot k)}\right)Y_{\ell}^{0}(\langle(\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot k,y\rangle)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\ell+1}}Y_{\ell}^{n}(0)Y_{\ell}^{0}(\langle(\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot k,y\rangle)e^{-in\theta((\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot k)}.

Using the reproducing property again, we finally obtain

(24)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{\pi^{B}}(f_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\langle\rho_{\infty}(f_{\infty})e_{1},e_{2}\rangle

(25)

\displaystyle=\frac{Y_{\ell}^{n}(0)}{\sqrt{2\ell+1}}e^{-in\theta((\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot k)}\int_{S^{2}}Y_{\ell}^{0}(\langle(\kappa_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]}^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot k,y\rangle)Y_{\ell}^{n}(\langle y,i\rangle)e^{in\theta}\>\mathrm{d}y=\frac{Y_{\ell}^{n}(0)^{2}}{2\ell+1}.

In fact, using
[
Mac48
, Ch. VII, §4, eq. (17)]
, we can determine the value of
Y_{\ell}^{n}(0)
.

Lemma 4.10
.

For
0\leq n\leq\ell
of the same parity, we have

Y_{\ell}^{n}(0)=(-1)^{(\ell+n)/2}\frac{\sqrt{(2\ell+1)(\ell+n+2)(\ell-n+2)C_{(\ell+n)/2}C_{(\ell-n)/2}}}{2^{\ell+2}\sqrt{\pi}},

where
C_{m}:=\frac{1}{m+1}\binom{2m}{m}
is the
m
-th Catalan number. If
0\leq n\leq\ell
have different parity, then
Y_{\ell}^{n}(0)=0
.

As a consequence of this result and Stirling’s estimates of the factorial, we obtain the following.

Corollary 4.11
.

For
0\leq n\leq\ell
of the same parity, we have

|Y_{\ell}^{n}(0)|\asymp\frac{\sqrt{\ell}}{(1+\ell+n)^{1/4}(1+\ell-n)^{1/4}}.

From the observations in the previous section and equations (
22
), (
23
), and (
25
), we can see that
|P^{B}(\phi_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]})|^{2}
does not depend on
[\mathfrak{a}]\in\mathrm{Cl}(E)
and
[b]\in\mathbb{Z}/c_{D}\mathbb{Z}
, so that for
\lambda\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}(E)}
and
\rho\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}(E)}
we have

I(\psi,\lambda,\rho,n)\leq c_{D}^{2}h_{D}^{2}|P^{B}(\phi_{[b],[\mathfrak{a}]})|^{2}.

Consequently,

(26)

I(\psi,\rho,\lambda,n)\ll_{D,E,\mathrm{ram}(\Omega_{n}),\mathrm{ram}(\pi)}\prod_{v\in S_{0}}\tilde{J}_{\pi^{B}}(f_{v})\times\frac{L^{S}(1/2,\pi_{E}\otimes\Omega_{n})}{L^{S_{0}}(1,\mathrm{Ad}(\pi))}=\frac{Y_{\ell}^{n}(0)^{2}}{2\ell+1}\frac{L^{S}(1/2,\pi_{E}\otimes\Omega_{n})}{L^{S_{0}}(1,\mathrm{Ad}(\pi))}.

4.7.
Local nonarchimedean factors of the
L
-functions

Next, we would like to add the local factors at
2
to the
L
-functions in our formulas to be able to use the approximate functional equation later. The background for the following discussion can be found in
[
Tat79
]
,
[
Cog04
, §1]
, and
[
BH06
, §7]
.

First of all, recall that
\pi
has conductor
2
and trivial central character since it corresponds to
f_{\psi}
. Thus, by
[
LW12
, Prop. 2.8]
, we know that
\pi_{2}
is the twist of the Steinberg representation by a (unitary) unramified character
\chi
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
, i.e.
\pi_{2}\simeq\mathrm{St}\otimes\chi
. Consequently,
\pi_{2}
corresponds under the local Langlands correspondence to the Weil–Deligne parameters
(\tau,N)
, where
\tau:W_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is a representation of the Weil group such that

(27)

\tau(\Phi)=\begin{pmatrix}\chi(\Phi)2^{-1/2}&0\\
0&\chi(\Phi)2^{1/2}\end{pmatrix},

where
\Phi
is the geometric Frobenius, and

(28)

N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

is a nilpotent endomorphism of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Now, we have the following.

Lemma 4.12
.

The prime
2
of
\mathbb{Q}
is non-split in
E
.

Proof.

If
D
is even, then
2
ramifies in
E
. Now, assume
D
is odd, so
D\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. We know that
\mathcal{O}_{E}=\mathbb{Z}[(1+\sqrt{D})/2]
, where the minimal polynomial of
(1+\sqrt{D})/2
is
f(x)=x^{2}-x+\frac{1-D}{4}
. Recall that we assumed
n\not\equiv 7\pmod{8}
, so that
D\not\equiv 1\pmod{8}
, hence
\frac{1-D}{4}
is odd. Consequently,
f(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. From here, it follows by Dedekind’s Theorem (see
[
Mil20b
, Thm. 3.41]
) that there is only one prime ideal lying above
2
in
E
and, since
2
is unramified, it must be inert.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

The previous lemma can also be concluded from the fact that
E
can be embedded in
B(\mathbb{Q})
. Indeed, consider an embedding
\iota:E\xhookrightarrow{}B(\mathbb{Q})
as described in Section
4.2
. Tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
, we obtain an embedding
\iota^{\prime}:E\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{2}\xhookrightarrow{}B(\mathbb{Q})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{2}=B(\mathbb{Q}_{2})
. If
2
is split in
E
, then
E\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{2}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{2}
by (
14
). However,
\mathbb{Q}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{2}
contains nontrivial idempotents; thus it cannot embed in
B(\mathbb{Q}_{2})
since the latter is a division algebra. Therefore,
2
must be non-split in
E
.

By Lemma
4.12
, there is only one prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
lying above
2
in
E
. We analyze the behavior of the local
L
-function
L_{\mathfrak{p}}(1/2,\pi_{E}\otimes\Omega_{n})
depending on the factorization of
2
in
E
.

First, assume
2
ramifies in
E
, so that
2\mathcal{O}_{E}=\mathfrak{p}^{2}
. Then
E_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{2}
, which implies
W_{E_{\mathfrak{p}}}\cong W_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}
. It follows that
(\pi_{E}\otimes\Omega_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}}
is also a twist of the Steinberg representation, now twisted by
\chi
and by
\Omega_{n}
. Thus, the Weil–Deligne parameters of
\pi_{E,\mathfrak{p}}\otimes(\Omega_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}}
are
(\tau^{\prime},N)
, where
\tau^{\prime}:W_{E_{\mathfrak{p}}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is a representation of the Weil group such that

\tau^{\prime}(\Psi)=\begin{pmatrix}\chi(\Psi)\alpha_{n}(\Psi)2^{-1/2}&0\\
0&\chi(\Psi)\alpha_{n}(\Psi)2^{1/2}\end{pmatrix},

where
\alpha_{n}:\mathrm{Gal}(E_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathrm{ab}}/E_{\mathfrak{p}})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
corresponds to
(\Omega_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}}:E_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
via local class field theory (see
[
Mil20a
, Ch. III, Thm. 3.1]
) and
\Psi
is the geometric Frobenius. From here, we have that
\mathrm{Ker}(N)=\{(z,0)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\}
, where
\tau^{\prime}(\Psi)
acts by multiplication by
\chi(\Psi)\alpha_{n}(\Psi)2^{-1/2}
. It follows that

L_{\mathfrak{p}}(1/2,\pi_{E}\otimes\Omega_{n})=\mathrm{det}\left(1-\tau^{\prime}(\Psi)|_{\mathrm{Ker}(N)}2^{-1/2}\right)^{-1}=\left(1-\frac{\chi(\Psi)\alpha_{n}(\Psi)}{2}\right)^{-1}.

Since the characters
\chi
and
\alpha_{n}
are unitary, we obtain that
L_{\mathfrak{p}}(1/2,\pi_{E}\otimes\Omega_{n})\asymp 1
uniformly on
n
.

Secondly, assume that
2
is inert in
E
, so that
2\mathcal{O}_{E}=\mathfrak{p}
. In this case,
E_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the unique quadratic unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(by the discussion after Prop. 8.10 in
[
Mil20b
]
) and we have
W_{E_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is an open subgroup of index
2
in
W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. If
\Phi^{\prime}
is the geometric Frobenius of
W_{E_{\mathfrak{p}}}
, by the definitions of the Weil group
W_{E_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and the geometric Frobenius, we have
\Phi^{\prime}=\Phi^{2}
. Additionally, by the definition of base change,
\pi_{E,\mathfrak{p}}
corresponds under the local Langlands correspondence to the Weil–Deligne parameters
(\mathrm{Res}_{W_{E_{\mathfrak{p}}}}(\tau),N)
, where
N
is as defined in (
28
) and

\mathrm{Res}_{W_{E_{\mathfrak{p}}}}(\tau)(\Phi^{\prime})=\tau(\Phi^{2})=\begin{pmatrix}\chi(\Phi)^{2}\cdot 2^{-1}&0\\
0&\chi(\Phi)^{2}\cdot 2\end{pmatrix}.

Hence,

L_{\mathfrak{p}}(1/2,\pi_{E}\otimes\Omega_{n})=\left(1-\frac{\chi(\Phi)^{2}\alpha_{n}(\Phi)}{2^{3/2}}\right)^{-1},

where again
\alpha_{n}
corresponds to
(\Omega_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}}
via local class field theory. Since both
\chi
and
\alpha_{n}
are unitary, it follows that
L_{\mathfrak{p}}(1/2,\pi_{E}\otimes\Omega_{n})\asymp 1
uniformly on
n
.

Finally, recall that the adjoint representation of
\tau:W_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
can be realized as follows. Let
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
be the standard basis for
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and let
\{e_{1}^{*},e_{2}^{*}\}
be its dual basis in
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{*}
. Then the adjoint representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
can be realized as the
3
-dimensional invariant subspace
V
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{*}
that is orthogonal to the subspace generated by
e_{1}\otimes e_{1}^{*}+e_{2}\otimes e_{2}^{*}
. (This
1
-dimensional subspace is a copy of the trivial representation.) Thus, a basis for
V
is given by

\{e_{1}\otimes e_{1}^{*}-e_{2}\otimes e_{2}^{*},e_{1}\otimes e_{2}^{*},e_{2}\otimes e_{1}^{*}\}.

Under this basis, we have that

\mathrm{Ad}(\tau)(\Phi)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&2^{-1}&0\\
0&0&2\end{pmatrix}

and, by
[
BH06
, §31.2]
, we have that the nilpotent endomorphism
N^{\prime}
corresponding to
\mathrm{Ad}(\tau)
is given by the restriction of
N\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{2}^{*}-\mathrm{Id}_{2}\otimes N^{t}
to
V
, where
N^{t}:(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{*}\rightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{*}
is given by
N^{t}(f)=f\circ N
. Under the given basis, we have

N^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
-2&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

It follows that
\mathrm{Ker}(N^{\prime})
is the subspace of
V
generated by
e_{1}\otimes e_{2}^{*}
, in which
\mathrm{Ad}(\tau)(\Phi)
acts by multiplication by
2^{-1}
. Therefore,

L_{2}(1,\mathrm{Ad}(\pi))=\mathrm{det}\left(1-\mathrm{Ad}(\tau)(\Phi)|_{\mathrm{Ker}(N^{\prime})}2^{-1}\right)^{-1}=(1-2^{-2})^{-1}=4/3.

By Proposition
2.7
we have

L\left(\frac{1}{2},\pi_{E}\otimes\Omega_{n}\right)=L\left(\frac{1}{2},\pi\otimes\mathcal{AI}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(\Omega_{n})\right)=L\left(\frac{1}{2},f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}}\right),

where the
L
-function on the right hand side is the Rankin–Selberg
L
-function over
\mathbb{Q}
of
f_{\psi}
and the CM form
f_{\Omega_{n}}
associated to
\Omega_{n}
in Definition
2.5
. From the previous analysis and (
26
), it follows that

I(\psi,\lambda,\rho,n)\ll_{D}\frac{Y_{\ell}^{n}(0)^{2}}{2\ell+1}\frac{L(\frac{1}{2},f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}})}{L(1,\mathrm{Ad}(f_{\psi}))},

where the implied constants are bounded as
n
varies. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, define

G(n):=\begin{cases}Y_{\ell}^{|n|}(0)^{2}/(2\ell+1)&\text{ if }|n|\leq\ell,\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus, by Theorem
2.8
, Corollary
4.5
, and (
26
), we obtain

(29)

||\psi|_{\mathcal{C}_{D}}||_{L^{2}}^{2}\ll_{D}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}/c_{D}\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\rho\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}(E)}}\sum_{n=0}^{\ell}\frac{Y_{\ell}^{n}(0)^{2}}{2\ell+1}\frac{L(\frac{1}{2},f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}})}{L(1,\mathrm{Ad}(f_{\psi}))}\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\ell}G(n)L(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}}).

for any
\varepsilon>0
. We proceed to bound the latter sum.

Remark 4.14
.

We know that the constant
\tilde{J}_{\pi^{B}}(f_{\infty})
is nonnegative for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\psi
has real Hecke eigenvalues, it follows from (

) and the computations in this section that

for all

.

4.8.
Smooth partition of unity

To bound the desired sum, we start by introducing a smooth partition of unity. Let
\widetilde{U}(x)
be a smooth bump function that is equal to
1
on
[0,\infty)
and supported on
[-1/2,\infty)
. Define

U(x):=\widetilde{U}\left(\frac{x}{\ell}\right),

which is equal to
1
on
[0,\infty)
and is supported on
[-\ell/2,\infty)
. We can see that
U(x)+U(-x)\geq 1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. It follows from this and
L(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{-n}})=L(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}})
that

(30)

\sum_{n=0}^{\ell}G(n)L(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}})\leq\sum_{|n|\leq\ell}U(n)G(n)L(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}}).

Now, let
W(x)
be the function obtained from Proposition
2.13
. For each
b\geq 0
, we define the function

W_{b}(x):=W\left(\frac{\ell+1-x}{2^{b}}\right).

By construction,
W_{b}
is supported on
2^{b-1}\leq\ell+1-x\leq 2^{b+1}
. We have

\sum_{b\geq 0}W_{b}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ for }x\leq\ell,\\
0&\text{ for }x\geq\ell+1,\end{array}\right.

and is in
[0,1]
for
\ell<x<\ell+1
. Then, from the definition of
G(n)
, we can see that

\displaystyle\sum_{|n|\leq\ell}U(n)G(n)L(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{b\geq 0}W_{b}(n)\right)U(n)G(n)L(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}})

(31)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\geq 0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{b}(n)U(n)G(n)L(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}}).

Let
\ell^{\prime}=\log_{2}\left(\frac{3\ell}{2}+1\right)+1
. By construction, we have that
\ell+1-2^{b-1}\leq-\frac{\ell}{2}
for
b\geq\ell^{\prime}
, which implies that
W_{b}(n)U(n)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that all terms in (
31
) with
b\geq\ell^{\prime}
vanish. Then, from this analysis and inequalities (
29
), (
30
), and (
31
), it follows that

(32)

||\psi|_{\mathcal{C}_{D}}||_{L^{2}}^{2}\ll_{\varepsilon}\ell^{2\varepsilon}\sup_{0\leq b\leq\ell^{\prime}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{b}(n)U(n)G(n)L(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}}).

For each
0\leq b\leq\ell^{\prime}
, the inner sum on the right hand side of (
32
) has only finitely many terms (by the definition of
G(n)
). For the rest of the terms, the analytic conductor of the
L
-functions involved have similar analytic conductors in size, which will be useful for us later. In more rigorous terms, we have the following.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
0\leq b\leq\ell^{\prime}
and define
T:=2^{b}
. Then

(33)

G(n)\ll(T\ell)^{-1/2}

and

(34)

\mathfrak{q}_{\infty}(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}})\asymp_{D}(T\ell)^{2}

for any
n\geq-\ell/2
such that
T/2\leq\ell+1-n\leq 2T
.

Proof.

Assume
n\geq-\ell/2
such that
T/2\leq\ell+1-n\leq 2T
. Since
f_{\psi}
has level
2
and
f_{\Omega_{n}}
has level
D
, we know that
q(f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}})\asymp_{D}1
, so that

(35)

\mathfrak{q}_{\infty}(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}})\asymp_{D}(4+\ell+n)(4+\ell-n)(5+\ell+n)(5+\ell-n).

Now, from the inequalities we assumed, we have that
\ell-n\asymp T
. Moreover, we have that
\ell+1-2T\leq n\leq\ell+1-T/2
. Since
T\ll\ell
, we have that
n\asymp\ell
, so that
\ell+n\asymp\ell
. From these observations, (
33
) follows from Corollary
4.11
, whereas (
34
) follows from (
35
).
∎

Remark 4.16
.

Indeed, the integers
n
that satisfy
n\geq-\ell/2
and
T/2\leq\ell+1-n\leq 2T
are the only ones of interest, since the support of
U(x)
is
[-\ell/2,\infty)
and the support of
W_{b}(x)
is
T/2\leq\ell+1-x\leq 2T
.

Let
0\leq b\leq\ell^{\prime}
and denote
T=2^{b}
. From Lemma
4.15
, it follows that

(36)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{b}(n)U(n)G(n)L(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}})\ll(T\ell)^{-1/2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{b}(n)U(n)L(1/2,f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}}).

We now proceed to prove that, for any
\varepsilon>0
and

4.10.
Partial derivatives of
V_{1/2}

To proceed, we will need certain estimates on the partial derivatives of
V_{1/2}(y,x)
with respect to
x
. We show these estimates in this subsection. First we estimate the quotient of gamma factors inside the integral in the definition of
V_{1/2}(y,x)
and its derivatives.

Lemma 4.22
.

Let
\delta>0
and let
1\leq T\ll\ell
. Suppose that
T/2\leq\ell+1-x\leq 2T
and
x\geq-\ell/2
. Define
F(u,f_{\psi},x):=\frac{\gamma(u+1/2,f_{\psi},x)}{\gamma(1/2,f_{\psi},x)}
, where

\gamma(s,f_{\psi},x):=\pi^{-2s}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\ell-x+\frac{1}{2}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\ell-x+\frac{3}{2}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\ell+x+\frac{1}{2}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\ell+x+\frac{3}{2}}{2}\right).

Then, for
\mathrm{Re}(u)=\delta
, we have

|F(u,f_{\psi},x)|\ll_{\delta}e^{3\pi|\mathrm{Im}(u)|}\ell^{2\delta}.

Proof.

Recall Stirling’s estimate (see
[
MV06
, Thm. C.1]
)

(40)

|\Gamma(s)|\asymp\left|s^{s-1/2}\right|e^{-\mathrm{Re}(s)},

which holds for
|s|\geq\delta
and
|\mathrm{Arg}(s)|<\pi-\delta
. Note that

\left|s^{s-1/2}\right|=|s|^{\mathrm{Re}(s)-1/2}e^{-\mathrm{Arg}(s)\mathrm{Im}(s)}.

Using this observation and (
40
), we have

(41)

|F(u,f_{\psi},x)|\asymp F_{1}F_{2}F_{3},

where

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\ell-x+1+u|^{(\ell-x)/2}|\ell-x+2+u|^{(\ell-x+1)/2}}{(\ell-x+1)^{(\ell-x)/2}(\ell-x+2)^{(\ell-x+1)/2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{|\ell+x+1+u|^{(\ell+x)/2}|\ell+x+2+u|^{(\ell+x+1)/2}}{(\ell+x+1)^{(\ell+x)/2}(\ell+x+2)^{(\ell+x+1)/2}},

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=|\ell-x+1+u|^{\delta/2}|\ell-x+2+u|^{\delta/2}|\ell+x+1+u|^{\delta/2}|\ell+x+2+u|^{\delta/2},

\displaystyle F_{3}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Im}(u)\left[\mathrm{Arg}((\ell-x+1+u)/2)+\mathrm{Arg}((\ell-x+2+u)/2)+\mathrm{Arg}((\ell+x+1+u)/2)+\mathrm{Arg}((\ell+x+2+u)/2)\right]}.

We proceed to estimate each factor
F_{i}
. We start with
F_{1}
. By the triangle inequality, we have

(42)

F_{1}^{2}\leq\left(1+\frac{|u|}{\ell-x+1}\right)^{\ell-x}\left(1+\frac{|u|}{\ell-x+2}\right)^{\ell-x+1}\left(1+\frac{|u|}{\ell+x+1}\right)^{\ell+x}\left(1+\frac{|u|}{\ell+x+2}\right)^{\ell+x+1}.

For any
C\geq 0
, the function
f(t)=\left(1+\frac{C}{t+1}\right)^{t}
is nondecreasing on the interval
[-1,\infty)
and is bounded from above by
e^{C}
. From this observation and (
42
), we obtain

(43)

F_{1}\leq e^{2|u|}\ll_{\delta}e^{\pi|\mathrm{Im}(u)|}.

We now estimate
F_{2}
. First, by definition of
x
and
T
, we have that
\ell-x+1\ll\ell
and
\ell+x+1\ll\ell
. Since
\ell\geq 1
, we have that
|\ell\pm x+1+u|\ll\ell+|u|=\ell(1+|u|/\ell)\leq\ell(1+|u|)
. It follows, since
\mathrm{Re}(u)=\delta
, that

(44)

F_{2}\ll\ell^{2\delta}(1+|u|)^{2\delta}\ll_{\delta}\ell^{2\delta}e^{\pi|\mathrm{Im}(u)|}.

Finally, we estimate
F_{3}
. Notice that, by definition of
x
and
T
, we have that
\ell-x+1>0
and
\ell+x+1>0
. Hence, each argument in the exponential defining
F_{3}
is between
-\pi/2
and
\pi/2
, so in absolute value they are
\leq\pi/2
. Thus

(45)

F_{3}\leq e^{\frac{1}{2}|\mathrm{Im}(u)|\left[\pi/2+\pi/2+\pi/2+\pi/2\right]}=e^{\pi|\mathrm{Im}(u)|}.

Combining (
41
), (
43
), (
44
), and (
45
) the result follows.
∎

To estimate the derivatives of
F
, we need some preliminary results. These are in
[
AA22
, §3.2.1]
and we reproduce them here for the ease of exposition. The first one of them is a consequence of the mean value theorem.

Lemma 4.23
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be an open subset containing
0
and let
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic function. Let
z_{0}\in\Omega
be such that the line segment joining
0
and
z_{0}
is contained in
\Omega
. Then

|f(z_{0})-f(0)|\leq 2|z_{0}|\sup_{r\in[0,1]}|f^{\prime}(rz_{0})|.

Recall that, for
m\geq 0
an integer, the polygamma function
\psi^{(m)}
is the meromorphic function defined as

\psi^{(m)}(z):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)&\text{ if }m=0,\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}z^{m}}\psi^{(0)}(z)&\text{ if }m\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

Using the series representation for
\psi^{(m)}
shown in
[
AS70
, 6.4.10]
, we have the following estimate.

Proposition 4.24
.

For
\mathrm{Re}(z)>0
and
m\geq 1
, we have

\psi^{(m)}(z)\ll_{m}|z|^{-m-1}+|z|^{-m}.

The polygamma functions allow us to write the derivatives of the gamma function as polynomials in these functions. The same is true for quotients of gamma functions, which is what we have in our case.

Definition 4.25
.

For
m\geq 0
,
s\in\mathbb{C}
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we let
\psi^{(m)}(s,f_{\psi},x)
be the expression

\psi^{(m)}\left(\frac{s+\ell+x+\frac{1}{2}}{2}\right)+\psi^{(m)}\left(\frac{s+\ell+x+\frac{3}{2}}{2}\right)-\psi^{(m)}\left(\frac{s+\ell-x+\frac{1}{2}}{2}\right)-\psi^{(m)}\left(\frac{s+\ell-x+\frac{3}{2}}{2}\right).

Proposition 4.26
.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\gamma(s,f_{\psi},x)=\frac{1}{2}\psi^{(0)}(s,f_{\psi},x)\gamma(s,f_{\psi},x).

Moreover, we have an analogue of Proposition
4.24
for
\psi^{(m)}(s,f_{\psi},x)
.

Proposition 4.27
.

Let
1\leq T\ll\ell
. Assume that
T/2\leq\ell+1-x\leq 2T
and
x\geq-\ell/2
. Then, for any
m\geq 1
and
\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1/2
, we have

\psi^{(m)}(s,f_{\psi},x)\ll_{m}T^{-m}.

With the previous results, we can bound the partial derivatives of
F
with respect to
x
.

Proposition 4.28
.

Let
1\leq T\ll\ell
and
\delta>0
. Suppose that
T/2\leq\ell+1-x\leq 2T
,
x\geq-\ell/2
, and
\mathrm{Re}(u)=\delta
. Then, for any
j\geq 0
we have

\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}F(u,f_{\psi},x)\ll_{\delta,j}|u|^{j}e^{3\pi|\mathrm{Im}(u)|}\ell^{2\delta}T^{-j}.

Proof.

We will show that

(46)

\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}F(u,f_{\psi},x)\ll_{\delta,j}|u|^{j}\>|F(u,f_{\psi},x)|.

Then the result will follow from Lemma
4.22
. First, define

D(u,f_{\psi},x):=\psi^{(0)}(1/2+u,f_{\psi},x)-\psi^{(0)}(1/2,f_{\psi},x).

By Proposition
4.26
, we can see that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}F(u,f_{\psi},x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma(1/2,f_{\psi},x)\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\gamma(1/2+u,f_{\psi},x)-\gamma(1/2+u,f_{\psi},x)\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\gamma(1/2,f_{\psi},x)}{\gamma(1/2,f_{\psi},x)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}D(u,f_{\psi},x)F(u,f_{\psi},x).

We first estimate the partial derivatives of
D
with respect to
x
. First, note that for any
j\geq 0
, we have

\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}D(u,f_{\psi},x)=\frac{1}{2^{j}}\left(\psi^{(j)}(1/2+u,f_{\psi},x)-\psi^{(j)}(1/2,f_{\psi},x)\right).

Hence, by Lemma
4.23
and Proposition
4.27
, we have that

\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}D(u,f_{\psi},x)\ll_{j}|u|\>\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\psi^{(j+1)}(1/2+ru,f_{\psi},x)\right|\ll_{j}|u|T^{-j-1}.

We now come back to estimating the partial derivatives of
F
with respect to
x
. To show (
46
), we proceed by induction on
j
. Notice that (
46
) holds for
j=0
and
j=1
. Now, assume that (
46
) holds for all
m\leq j
. By the induction hypothesis, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{j+1}}{\partial x^{j+1}}F(u,f_{\psi},x)

\displaystyle\ll_{j}\sum_{m=0}^{j}\left|\frac{\partial^{j-m}}{\partial x^{j-m}}F(u,f_{\psi},x)\right|\left|\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial x^{m}}D(u,f_{\psi},x)\right|

\displaystyle\ll_{j}\sum_{m=0}^{j}|u|^{j-m}\>|F(u,f_{\psi},x)|T^{-j+m}\cdot|u|T^{-m-1}

\displaystyle\ll_{j}|u|^{j+1}\>|F(u,f_{\psi},x)|T^{-j-1},

which completes the proof.
∎

From these bounds, we can estimate the partial derivatives of
V_{1/2}(y,x)
with respect to
x
.

Proposition 4.29
.

Let
1\leq T\ll\ell
. Suppose that
T/2\leq\ell+1-x\leq 2T
and
x\geq-\ell/2
. Then, for any
j\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}V_{1/2}(y,x)\ll_{\varepsilon,j}y^{-\varepsilon/2}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-j}.

Proof.

Recall from Definition
4.17
that

(47)

V_{1/2}(y,x):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(u)=3}F(u,f_{\psi},x)y^{-u}e^{u^{2}}\>\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u}

Let
\delta=\varepsilon/2
. Since
e^{u^{2}}
decays like
e^{-\mathrm{Im}(u)^{2}}
on vertical lines, by Stirling’s estimate of the Gamma function and Cauchy’s theorem, we can move the line of integration in (
47
) to the line
\mathrm{Re}(u)=\delta
, so that

V_{1/2}(y,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(u)=\delta}F(u,f_{\psi},x)y^{-u}e^{u^{2}}\>\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u}.

It follows by Proposition
4.28
that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}V_{1/2}(y,x)

\displaystyle\ll\int_{\mathrm{Re}(u)=\delta}\left|\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}F(u,f_{\psi},x)\right|y^{-\mathrm{Re}(u)}e^{\mathrm{Re}(u^{2})}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{|u|}

\displaystyle\ll_{\delta,j}y^{-\delta}e^{\delta^{2}}\ell^{2\delta}T^{-j}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(u)=\delta}|u|^{j-1}e^{3\pi|\mathrm{Im}(u)|}e^{-|\mathrm{Im}(u)|^{2}}\>\mathrm{d}u

4.11.
Poisson summation

Notice that, by the construction of
\Omega_{n}
, for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{E}
, we have that

\Omega_{n}((m))=\left(\frac{m}{\overline{m}}\right)^{n}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega_{n}((\alpha))=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\overline{\alpha}}\right)^{n}=e^{in\cdot\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}.

Let

q_{D}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2|D|&\text{ if }D\text{ is odd},\\
\sqrt{2}|D|&\text{ if }D\text{ is even}.\end{array}\right.

By
[
Li79
, Thm. 2.2]
, we have that
q(f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}})=q_{D}^{2}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore,

\Pi_{\alpha}(m)=(T\ell)^{-1/2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{b}(n)U(n)V_{1/2}\left(\frac{m^{2}N(\alpha)}{q_{D}},n\right)e^{in\cdot\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}.

We now fix
0\leq a\leq a_{T}
and write
A:=2^{a}
. We want to estimate the expression inside the absolute value on the right hand side of (
39
), which we call
S_{a}
. Define the smooth compactly supported function

H_{A}(\alpha,m,x):=\frac{(T\ell)^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{N(\alpha)}}W_{a}^{\prime}(\alpha)U_{T}(\alpha)W_{b}(x)U(x)V_{1/2}\left(\frac{m^{2}N(\alpha)}{q_{D}},x\right).

Thus, we have

S_{a}=\sum_{m=1}^{(T\ell)^{1+\delta}}\frac{\chi_{D}(m)}{m}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}}\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{A}(\alpha,m,n)\,e^{in\cdot\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}.

Consequently, we can use the Poisson summation formula in the
n
-sum to obtain that

S_{a}=\sum_{m=1}^{(T\ell)^{1+\delta}}\frac{\chi_{D}(m)}{m}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}}\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{H_{A}}\left(\alpha,m,\xi-\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}\right),

where
\widehat{H_{A}}
is the Fourier transform of
H_{A}
with respect to
x
.

Remark 4.30
.

For
D=-3
and
D=-4
, one has to split the
n
-sum in the definition of
S_{a}
into several sums depending on the congruence of
n
modulo
3
or
4
, respectively. There are two reasons for this change. The first one is the difference in the definition of
\Omega_{n}
for different values of
n
, as pointed out in Remark
4.8
. The second one is that, along each sum, we want
q(f_{\psi}\otimes f_{\Omega_{n}})
to remain constant, so that we can use the Poisson summation formula. However, each sum can be bounded following the procedure we describe now.

For
\xi\neq 0
, by repeated use of integration by parts, we have

(48)

\widehat{H_{A}}(\alpha,m,\xi)\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi|\xi|)^{j}}\left|\widehat{H_{A}^{(j)}}(\alpha,m,\xi)\right|,

where
H_{A}^{(j)}
is the
j
-th partial derivative of
H_{A}(\alpha,m,x)
with respect to
x
. We estimate
\widehat{H_{A}}
via estimating
H_{A}^{(j)}(\alpha,m,x)
.

Proposition 4.31
.

Let
j\geq 0
. Then

H_{A}^{(j)}(\alpha,m,x)=0\text{ unless }2x\in\left[\ell+1-2T,\ell+1-T/2\right]\cap[-\ell/2,\infty)\text{ and }N(\alpha)\in[A/2,2A].

Moreover, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

H_{A}^{(j)}(\alpha,m,x)\ll_{D,\varepsilon,j}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-j}(\ell TA)^{-1/2}.

Proof.
The first statement follows from the definitions of
W_{a}^{\prime}(\alpha)
and
W_{b}(x)
, namely that
W_{a}^{\prime}
has support on
N(\alpha)\in[A/2,2A]
and that
W_{b}
has support on
[\ell+1-2T,\ell+1-T/2]
. Now, assume that
N(\alpha)\in[A/2,2A]
, so that
N(\alpha)\gg A
. By the product rule for derivatives, we have

\displaystyle H_{A}^{(j)}(\alpha,m,x)

\displaystyle\ll(\ell TA)^{-1/2}\left(W_{b}(x)U(x)V_{1/2}\left(\frac{m^{2}N(\alpha)}{q_{D}},x\right)\right)^{(j)}

(49)

\displaystyle\ll_{j}(\ell TA)^{-1/2}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\left(W_{b}(x)U(x)\right)^{(j-i)}V_{1/2}^{(i)}\left(\frac{n^{2}N(\alpha)}{q_{D}},x\right),

where all the derivatives are with respect to
x
. Since
W_{b}(x)=W\left(\frac{\ell-x}{T}\right)
for

a fixed smooth compactly supported function, by using the chain rule several times we obtain that
W_{b}^{(j-i)}(2x)\ll_{j,i}T^{-j+i}
. Similarly, since

for
\widetilde{U}(x)
a fixed bump function, we have that
U^{(j-i)}(2x)\ll_{j,i}\ell^{-j+i}\ll T^{-j+i}
. It follows that

(50)

\left(W_{b}(2x)U(2x)\right)^{(j-i)}\ll_{j,i}\sum_{h=0}^{j-i}W_{b}^{(h)}(2x)U^{(j-i-h)}(2x)\ll_{j,i}\sum_{h=0}^{j-i}T^{-h}T^{h+i-j}\ll_{j,i}T^{-j+i}.

Additionally, from Proposition
4.29
, we have that

(51)

V_{1/2}^{(i)}\left(\frac{m^{2}N(\alpha)}{q_{D}},x\right)\ll_{\varepsilon,i}q_{D}^{\varepsilon/2}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-i}.

From (
49
), (
50
), and (
51
), we obtain

H_{A}^{(j)}(\alpha,m,x)\ll_{D,\varepsilon,j}(\ell TA)^{-1/2}\sum_{i=0}^{j}T^{-j+i}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-i}\ll_{D,\varepsilon,j}(\ell TA)^{-1/2}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-j}.\qed

As a consequence of (

) and Proposition
4.31
, we have the following.

Corollary 4.32
.

For any
j\geq 0
and
\xi\neq 0
, we have

\widehat{H_{A}}(\alpha,m,\xi)\ll_{D,\varepsilon,j}\frac{T\ell^{\varepsilon}}{|T\xi|^{j}}(\ell TA)^{-1/2}.

Moreover, for
N(\alpha)\notin[A/2,2A]
, we have
\widehat{H_{A}}(\alpha,m,\xi)=0
.

Using this last estimate, we can bound the inner two sums of
S_{a}
.

Proposition 4.33
.

For any
m\geq 1
and
0<\delta\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}}\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{H_{A}}\left(\alpha,m,\xi-\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon,\delta}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{1/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}\sum_{\alpha\in S_{T^{-1+\delta}}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|+\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-3/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}\\
A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|,

where

S_{R}:=\left\{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}:-\pi R\leq\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha)\leq\pi R,\>A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\right\}

for any
R\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

From the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{D}
, for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}
we have that
-\pi/2\leq\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha)<\pi/2
, so that
-\pi\leq\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})<\pi
. This implies that
-\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}<\frac{1}{2}
for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}
. Thus, from Corollary
4.32
with
j=2
and the triangle inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}}\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))\sum_{\xi\geq 1}\widehat{H_{A}}\left(\alpha,m,\xi-\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-3/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}\\
A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|\sum_{\xi\geq 1}\frac{1}{\left|\xi-\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-3/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}\\
A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|\sum_{\xi\geq 1}\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}

(52)

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-3/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}\\
A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|.

Using the same reasoning, we have

(53)

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}}\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))\sum_{\xi\leq-1}\widehat{H_{A}}\left(\alpha,m,\xi-\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}\right)\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-3/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}\\
A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|.

Now, let
\xi=0
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}
. By the definition of
S_{R}
, we have that
\left|-\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}\right|\leq T^{-1+\delta}
if and only if
\alpha\in S_{T^{-1+\delta}}
. If this is the case, from Corollary
4.32
with
j=0
, we get

(54)

\widehat{H_{A}}\left(\alpha,m,-\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}\right)\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{1/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}.

If not, then we have
\left|-\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}\right|>T^{-1+\delta}
. In particular,
\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}\neq 0
. Hence, again by Corollary
4.32
with
j=\left\lceil 2/\delta\right\rceil
we have

\displaystyle\widehat{H_{A}}\left(\alpha,m,-\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon,\delta}\frac{\ell^{\varepsilon}T}{\left|T\left(-\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{\pi}\right)\right|^{j}}(\ell TA)^{-1/2}

(55)

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon,\delta}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{1-\delta j}(\ell TA)^{-1/2}\ll_{D,\varepsilon,\delta}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-3/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}.

Combining (
54
) and (
55
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}}\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))\widehat{H_{A}}\left(\alpha,m,-\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}\\
A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|\cdot\left|\widehat{H_{A}}\left(\alpha,m,-\frac{\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha/\overline{\alpha})}{2\pi}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon,\delta}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{1/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}\sum_{\alpha\in S_{T^{-1+\delta}}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|+\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-3/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}\\
A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\\
\alpha\notin S_{T^{-1+\delta}}\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|

(56)

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon,\delta}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{1/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}\sum_{\alpha\in S_{T^{-1+\delta}}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|+\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-3/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}\\
A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|.

The result follows from (
52
), (
53
), and (
56
).
∎

An important observation is that the right hand side of the bound of Proposition
4.33
does not depend on
m
, so that we can use well-known bounds for the harmonic sum to estimate
S_{a}
; for instance, we have

(57)

\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{1}{m}\ll\log(M).

Now, from Deligne’s proof of the Weil conjectures (see
[
Del74
]
), we have for any
n\geq 1
that
\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(n)\ll_{\varepsilon}n^{\varepsilon}
. If
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}
with
A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A
, we have that

\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))\ll_{\varepsilon}N(\alpha)^{\varepsilon}\ll_{\varepsilon}A^{\varepsilon}.

From this bound, Proposition
4.33
, and (
57
), we have

\displaystyle S_{a}

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon,\delta}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{1/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}\sum_{\alpha\in S_{T^{-1+\delta}}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|\sum_{m=1}^{(\ell T)^{1+\delta}}\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle\qquad+\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-3/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}\\
A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{f_{\psi}}(N(\alpha))|\sum_{m=1}^{(\ell T)^{1+\delta}}\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon,\delta}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{1/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}A^{\varepsilon}\log\left((\ell T)^{1+\delta}\right)\left|S_{T^{-1+\delta}}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-3/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}A^{\varepsilon}\log\left((\ell T)^{1+\delta}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}\\
A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\end{subarray}}1

(58)

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon,\delta}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{1/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}(\ell T)^{\varepsilon}\left|S_{T^{-1+\delta}}\right|+\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-3/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}A^{\varepsilon}(\ell T)^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}\\
A/2\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\end{subarray}}1.

Now, by a careful analysis of the factorization of rational primes in number fields, one can show for any
\varepsilon>0
that

(59)

\sum_{N(\mathfrak{a})=n}1\ll_{\varepsilon}n^{\varepsilon},

where the sum is over all ideals
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathcal{O}_{E}
that have ideal norm equal to
n
. From (
58
) and (
59
) it follows that

\displaystyle S_{a}

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon,\delta}\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{1/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}(\ell T)^{\varepsilon}\left|S_{T^{-1+\delta}}\right|+\ell^{\varepsilon}T^{-3/2}(\ell A)^{-1/2}A^{\varepsilon}(\ell T)^{\varepsilon}A^{1+\varepsilon}

(60)

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon,\delta}\ell^{2\varepsilon-1/2}T^{\varepsilon+1/2}A^{-1/2}\left|S_{T^{-1+\delta}}\right|+\ell^{2\varepsilon-1/2}T^{\varepsilon-3/2}A^{2\varepsilon+1/2}.

Finally, we estimate the cardinality of the set
S_{R}
for
0<R\leq 1
.

4.12.
Final estimates

We will use the Lipschitz principle to estimate
|S_{R}|
. First, since
\mathcal{F}_{D}
is contained in the right half-plane of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have that
S_{R}=S_{R^{\prime}}
for
R,R^{\prime}\geq 1/2
, so we only need to bound
|S_{R}|
for
R\leq 1/2
. Now, consider the set

S_{R}^{*}:=\left\{\alpha\in S_{R}:\mathrm{Im}(\alpha)\geq 0\right\}.

Then, we can see that
|S_{R}|\leq 2|S_{R}^{*}|
, so it is enough to estimate
|S_{R}^{*}|
. For this, we will reduce our problem to counting lattice points in a parallelogram. Define the parallelogram

\mathcal{P}_{R}:=\left\{av_{1}+bv_{2}:a,b\in[0,1]\right\},

where

v_{1}:=\left(\sqrt{2A},0\right)\text{ and }v_{2}:=\left(0,\sin\left(\pi R\right)\sqrt{2A}\right).

The important observation is the following.

Lemma 4.34
.

For
0<R\leq 1/2
, we have

S_{R}^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{R}\cap\mathcal{O}_{E}.

Proof.

First, note that
S_{R}^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{D}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{E}
, so that we only need to show that
S_{R}^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{R}
. Since
S_{R}^{*}
is contained in the first quadrant of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have that

S_{R}^{*}=\left\{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{D}:0\leq\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha)\leq\pi R,\>\frac{A}{2}\leq N(\alpha)\leq 2A\right\}.

Suppose
\alpha=x+iy\in S_{R}^{*}
. We then have
A/2\leq x^{2}+y^{2}\leq 2A
, so that

(61)

0\leq x\leq\sqrt{2A}.

Moreover, since
0\leq\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha)\leq\pi R
and
0<R\leq 1/2
, then

(62)

0\leq y=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\sin(\mathrm{Arg}(\alpha))\leq\sqrt{2A}\sin(\pi R).

The result follows from (
61
) and (
62
).
∎

Hence, to estimate
|S_{R}^{*}|
, we only need to estimate
|\mathcal{P}_{R}\cap\mathcal{O}_{E}|
by Lemma
4.34
. Using Proposition
2.14
with the basis
\{1,i\sqrt{-n}\}
and the well-known inequality
\sin(x)\leq x
for
x\geq 0
, we obtain for
0<R\leq 1
that

(63)

|S_{R}|\ll_{D}\left(\sqrt{2A}+R\sqrt{2A}\right)+\left(\sqrt{2A}\cdot R\sqrt{2A}\right)\ll_{D}\sqrt{A}+RA.

Combining (
60
) and (
63
) with
R=T^{-1+\delta}
, and setting
\delta=\varepsilon
, we get

\displaystyle S_{a}

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{2\varepsilon-1/2}T^{\varepsilon+1/2}A^{-1/2}\left(T^{-1+\varepsilon}A+A^{1/2}\right)+\ell^{2\varepsilon-1/2}T^{\varepsilon-3/2}A^{2\varepsilon+1/2}

(64)

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{2\varepsilon-1/2}T^{\varepsilon}\left(T^{\varepsilon-1/2}A^{1/2}+T^{1/2}+T^{-3/2}A^{2\varepsilon+1/2}\right).

However, from the definition of
A
and
T
, we know that
T\ll\ell
and
A\ll_{\varepsilon}(T\ell)^{1+\varepsilon}
. Using these estimates in (
64
), we obtain

\displaystyle S_{a}

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{2\varepsilon-1/2}T^{\varepsilon}\left(T^{\varepsilon-1/2}(T\ell)^{(1+\varepsilon)/2}+T^{1/2}+T^{-3/2}(T\ell)^{1/2+9\varepsilon/2}\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{2\varepsilon-1/2}T^{\varepsilon}\left(T^{3\varepsilon/2}\ell^{(1+\varepsilon)/2}+T^{1/2}+T^{-1+9\varepsilon/2}\ell^{1/2+9\varepsilon/2}\right)

(65)

\displaystyle\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{2\varepsilon-1/2}\ell^{\varepsilon}\left(\ell^{3\varepsilon/2}\ell^{(1+\varepsilon)/2}+\ell^{1/2}+\ell^{1/2+9\varepsilon/2}\right)\ll_{D,\varepsilon}\ell^{8\varepsilon}.

Combining (
32
), (
36
), (
39
), and (
65
) we obtain the desired result.
